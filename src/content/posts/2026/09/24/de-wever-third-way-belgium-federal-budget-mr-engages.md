# De Wever Seeks 'Third Way' as Coalition Splits on Budget

Belgian Prime Minister Bart De Wever is under mounting pressure to broker a compromise between the two Francophone partners of his governing coalition, as the liberal MR and the centrist Les Engagés trade rival economic plans ahead of a pivotal federal budget conclave. The two parties have each unveiled a competing "third way" to close a budgetary gap, leaving De Wever to find a synthesis that keeps the government's plans to save €10 billion by 2029 on track, as [RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/de-wever-somme-de-chercher-une-troisieme-voie-entre-le-mr-et-les-engages-11789764) reported.

## A Coalition Built on Tension

The standoff reflects the recurring friction inside Belgium's "Arizona" coalition, which De Wever assembled after the 2024 elections. The government spans Flemish nationalists (N-VA), Flemish socialists (Vooruit), Flemish Christian democrats (CD&V), Francophone liberals (MR) and Francophone centrists (Les Engagés). The two Francophone partners sit at opposite ends of the economic debate — and their diverging visions are now colliding directly with the budget arithmetic.

In July 2026, the government committed to an effort of €10 billion by 2029 to bring the deficit onto a sustainable path and meet the EU spending norm two years ahead of the 2031 target, [RTBF reported](https://www.rtbf.be/article/le-gouvernement-federal-s-accorde-sur-un-effort-de-10-milliards-d-euros-d-ici-2029-et-tiendra-un-conclave-budgetaire-fin-septembre-11756074). Belgium's deficit is projected to remain high, around 5% of GDP until 2028.

## MR: Growth Through Tax Cuts

The MR's plan, presented on Wednesday 23 September by deputy prime minister David Clarinval alongside ministers Mathieu Bihet and Eléonore Simonet, was submitted to employer federations before being placed on the budget negotiating table. Titled "Strategy to accelerate growth and strengthen competitiveness 2026-2032," it aims to lift Belgian GDP growth by 0.5 percentage points by 2029, generating an expected €1.5 billion in additional revenues.

According to [RTL Info](https://www.rtl.be/actu/belgique/economie/une-troisieme-voie-le-mr-depose-un-plan-de-relance-avant-le-conclave-budgetaire/2026-09-23/article/801174), the strategy contains several dozen measures, of which the MR highlights 17 — including seven "quick wins" that require no structural reform. Among them: an exemption from professional withholding tax in sectors facing labour shortages (chemistry, pharma, agri-food, defence, digital and space); a cut in VAT on new construction to 12% from 21%; a freeze on VAT and excise rates on energy products; a "shock" of administrative simplification for small businesses; and the extension of flexi-jobs to the self-employed. The plan also projects a €2 billion reduction in employer contributions starting in 2027.

"We propose measures that will have a direct impact on growth, employment and therefore on revenues," Clarinval said. "It is not only the MR pushing them; they are supported by employer representatives."

The core critique is that the plan is essentially a reduction of revenues, resting on hoped-for "return effects" — supply-side gains that, as RTBF's chronicle noted, have been systematically overestimated in recent years.

## Les Engagés: Investment and Transition

Les Engagés want to escape the logic of austerity altogether. Their plan is oriented around the energy transition and investment — public and private — presented as "massive." Federal Climate, Environment and Mobility Minister Jean-Luc Crucke advocates investing in rail and the energy transition, invoking a specific flexibility in EU fiscal rules.

As [RTBF detailed](https://www.rtbf.be/article/ce-n-est-pas-une-crise-energetique-c-est-une-crise-de-l-energie-fossile-declare-jean-luc-crucke-qui-veut-investir-dans-le-rail-et-la-transition-energetique-11789205), Europe permits a temporary deviation of 0.3% per year from the budget trajectory, provided the country invests in the ecological transition — amounting to roughly €2 billion per year in 2027 and 2028, to be repaid later. Crucke proposes directing that money toward strengthening electricity grids (estimated at €200–300 million), rail electrification (around €200 million), and social leasing to help low-income households buy an electric vehicle.

"It's not an energy crisis, it's a fossil energy crisis," Crucke said. "But at the same time, you must redirect our economy toward what we call the future."

He drew a parallel with defence spending, which can be de-budgetised as investment: "All this, Europe authorizes it. Why? Because it made the parallel with what we have in national defence."

## A Francophone Publicity War

Both Francophone partners are waging an intense communication offensive. Les Engagés launched a website on 17 September, meschoixpourlabelgique.be, letting citizens "compose" the €10 billion effort themselves by adjusting revenues, spending and investments. Meanwhile, Les Engagés president Yvan Verougstraete publicly urged De Wever to "step out of the N-VA positioning and rise above the fray," calling for a relaunch and growth plan.

The Flemish side has been notably quieter. Vooruit president Conner Rousseau addressed his silence directly in his newsletter: "Why am I silent? Because I want it to work. Because the situation is very serious. And that requires responsibility."

## Underlying Friction

The tensions predate this week. In early September, MR president Georges-Louis Bouchez issued a categorical refusal of any tax increase, while Les Engagés signaled greater openness — a clash that [RTBF described](https://www.rtbf.be/article/budget-federal-pour-la-premiere-fois-on-a-une-divergence-de-fond-et-de-forme-entre-bart-de-wever-et-georges-louis-bouchez-11781692) as "a divergence of substance and form" between De Wever and Bouchez. MR's "third way" is, in effect, focused on lowering revenues, even as the government is supposed to be finding money — a short-term contradiction at the heart of the talks.

De Wever, for his part, has acknowledged the difficulty. Addressing the Chamber on 16 September, he remarked: "If I were alone, things would be simple."

## What to Watch

The MR plan is due to be formally deposited on the negotiating table as the conclave approaches. Clarinval has said that implementing already-decided but unexecuted measures could yield €4 billion of the €10 billion sought — his proposed starting point before any discussion of growth, structural reform or, "if necessary," taxation.

RTBF's chronicle captured the paradox with a wry turn of phrase: "A third way with two voices — does that make two ways?" The answer will rest on whether De Wever can lend the coalition a single voice of his own — and whether the two Francophone partners can be persuaded to converge before the budget deadline slips.
