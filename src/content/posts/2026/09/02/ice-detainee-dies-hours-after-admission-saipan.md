# ICE Detainee Dies Hours After Admission in Saipan

A 51-year-old Chinese national died just hours after being admitted to an immigration detention facility in the U.S. territory of the Northern Mariana Islands, marking the 57th death of an immigrant detainee held in government custody since the start of President Trump's second term, according to [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/09/02/nx-s1-5953128/ice-detention-death-lianyong-wei).

Lianyong Wei was found unresponsive during morning checks by a corrections officer at the CNMI Department of Corrections facility in Saipan on August 23, just one day after being taken into ICE custody "without incident, pending his removal proceedings," according to an [ICE press release](https://www.ice.gov/news/releases/criminal-illegal-alien-china-passes-away-saipan-ice-facility). He was pronounced dead at approximately 7:30 a.m. local time at the Commonwealth Health Care Corporation Emergency Room after life-sustaining interventions were initiated. His official cause of death remains under investigation.

## A Death Within Hours of Intake

Wei was arrested on August 21 by the Commonwealth of the Northern Mariana Islands Department of Public Safety on charges including assault with a dangerous weapon, disturbing the peace, assault and battery, and aggravated assault and battery, following allegations that he assaulted a family of five at their residence. He had entered the U.S. territory through Saipan in February 2019, paroled by U.S. Customs and Border Protection with authorization to remain temporarily until February 16 of that year, but remained beyond that date without DHS authorization. U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services issued Wei a notice to appear on July 14, 2026, placing him in removal proceedings, with his next hearing scheduled for September.

Wei is at least the [fifth Chinese national](https://apnews.com/article/ice-detainee-death-china-northern-mariana-islands-929473082e7fd716dee794665c725f83) who has died in the custody of ICE or the U.S. Border Patrol since March 2025, according to tracking by the Associated Press. Two of the first four deaths were ruled suicides, and the other two resulted from medical complications.

The Chinese Consulate General in Los Angeles has expressed "serious concerns" to U.S. authorities and demanded a thorough investigation. "We have expressed serious concerns to the relevant U.S. authorities over this incident and required a timely and thorough investigation into the cause of Mr. Wei's death, notification of the findings, measures to prevent any recurrence of similar incidents, and assistance to the family of the deceased in handling the aftermath," the consulate said in a statement reported by the [Associated Press](https://www.ctvnews.ca/world/article/china-presses-the-us-for-answers-after-a-man-dies-hours-after-entering-ice-custody/).

## Questions Over Medical Screening

Under Department of Homeland Security regulations, all immigrant detainees must receive a basic health screening within 12 hours of intake. DHS declined to confirm whether Wei received the required evaluation. Instead, officials offered standard language: "Comprehensive medical care is provided from the moment individuals arrive and throughout the entirety of their stay."

CNMI Corrections Commissioner Anthony Torres said the department is "reviewing relevant procedures and will implement any necessary corrective actions to strengthen prevention and response measures," as reported by [RNZ Pacific](https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/pacific/1123336/ice-detainee-dies-in-cnmi-prison).

It also took ICE more than a week to publicly acknowledge Wei's death, despite the agency's stated policy of issuing a news release on detainee deaths within two business days. The CNMI Department of Corrections announced Wei's death publicly on August 24, but ICE did not issue its official news release until September 1.

## A Record Surge in Detention Deaths

Wei's death is part of a broader crisis. Twenty-four detainee deaths have occurred since January 1, 2026, according to [ICE's detainee death notifications](https://www.ice.gov/detain/detainee-death-reporting). A [Human Rights Watch report](https://www.hrw.org/report/2026/06/25/dying-in-detention/rising-deaths-in-an-expanding-us-immigration-detention-system) published in June found that 52 people died in ICE custody in the first 500 days of Trump's second term, with the mortality rate at its highest level in over a decade. The rate is nearly four times that of the Biden administration and more than two and a half times as high as the first Trump administration, and is now even higher than during the Covid-19 pandemic.

The detention population reached a record high of over 71,000 people in January 2026, and deaths have increased at a rate disproportionate to the growth in the detained population. Thirty-six percent of deaths occurred less than two weeks after entering ICE custody.

## A Broken Oversight System

The Trump administration has also dismantled oversight mechanisms designed to catch problems before they become fatal. The Office of the Immigration Detention Ombudsman was fully shuttered in May 2026, while the Office of Detention Oversight suffered massive budget cuts and layoffs. The Office for Civil Rights and Civil Liberties was slashed from roughly 150 employees to just a handful, leaving more than 500 active civil rights complaints frozen or uninvestigated. According to an [NPR investigation](https://www.npr.org/2026/08/20/nx-s1-5850361/grievance-ice-detention-immigrants), the grievance system in immigration detention has "all but collapsed," with only 8% of 485 grievances reviewed by the ACLU found in favor of detained people.

In June 2026, the administration eliminated a Biden-era policy requiring ICE to report deaths occurring within 30 days of people being released from custody. This means deaths like that of [Prisciliano Trejo](https://www.detentionwatchnetwork.org/pressroom/releases/2026/priciliano-trejo-29-dies-after-being-detained-ice), a 29-year-old who died at a hospital after being released from Stewart Detention Center in Georgia while in a coma from leukemia, are not counted in ICE's official death tally. DHS officials defended the change, stating: "Per ICE policy, when an individual is no longer in ICE custody, then ICE will no longer be responsible for monitoring or reviewing deaths that may occur."

Advocates argue the official numbers understate the true scope of the crisis. "Nobody is safe for any amount of time in ICE custody because immigration detention has always been abusive and deadly by design," Laura Hernández, executive director of Freedom for Immigrants, told [Mother Jones](https://www.motherjones.com/politics/2026/08/the-deaths-ice-doesnt-count/).

## What to Watch For

The investigation into Wei's death is ongoing, and the Chinese government has signaled it will continue pressing for answers. The Saipan lockup, which has held an average of 18 ICE detainees on any given day in 2026 in a territory of roughly 50,000 people, is reviewing its procedures following the incident.

As the number of deaths in ICE custody continues to climb, the case raises urgent questions about medical care, screening protocols, and accountability within the nation's immigration detention system. Whether the administration will address these systemic concerns or continue to face scrutiny from domestic advocates and foreign governments alike remains to be seen.
