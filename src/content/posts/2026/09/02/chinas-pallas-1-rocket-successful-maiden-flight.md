# China's Pallas-1 Rocket Completes Successful Maiden Flight

China's Zhishenxing-1 (Pallas-1) carrier rocket achieved a successful maiden flight on September 1, 2026, lifting off at 10:00 a.m. Beijing Time from the Dongfeng Commercial Space Innovation Pilot Zone at the Jiuquan Satellite Launch Center in northwest China's Gansu Province. The medium-sized, two-stage liquid-propellant rocket, developed by Beijing-based private space company Galactic Energy, performed normally throughout its flight and delivered its payload into the designated orbit, with all test objectives met, according to [Xinhua](https://english.news.cn/20260901/56c30ded52654b1493dedc488796d538/c.html).

## A Milestone for China's Commercial Space Sector

The successful launch marks a significant achievement for both Galactic Energy and China's rapidly expanding commercial space industry. The Pallas-1 is the 50th type of carrier rocket China has developed and flown, and the 12th private rocket built and used in the country, as [China Daily](https://www.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202609/01/WS6a965783e4b06d4aa055ba7f.html) reported. More notably, it represents the first successful medium-lift reusable liquid rocket developed by a Chinese private company to reach orbit.

Galactic Energy, founded in Beijing in February 2018, has established itself as one of China's leading commercial space ventures. The company previously developed the Ceres-1 small solid-propellant rocket, which first launched successfully in November 2020, making it the second private Chinese firm to place a satellite in orbit. Since then, Galactic Energy has conducted 22 orbital launches, successfully placing 89 satellites into various orbits, according to [CCTV News](https://news.cctv.cn/2026/09/02/ARTIqd2EpDHe3lohUhWgp7Dy260902.shtml).

## Technical Specifications and Design Innovations

The Pallas-1 rocket features a two-stage configuration with an optional upper stage. Standing 52 meters tall with a core-stage diameter of 3.35 meters, the rocket has a liftoff mass of approximately 283 metric tons and generates 350 tons of liftoff thrust. Its low Earth orbit (LEO) payload capacity of 5 to 7 tons is designed to meet the demands of commercial satellite operators deploying large-scale low-orbit constellations.

The rocket's first stage is powered by seven CQ-50 liquid oxygen/kerosene engines arranged in parallel, developed in-house by Galactic Energy. This seven-engine configuration provides significant fault redundancy—if one engine fails during flight, the remaining six can still complete the mission. The engines feature deep throttling capability ranging from 32 to 105 percent of rated thrust and support multiple restarts, both essential for the rocket's recovery and reuse design.

According to [IT Home](https://www.ithome.com/0/996/784.htm), the Pallas-1 is designed to be reused at least 25 times. The rocket also incorporates an integrated structural-propulsion design, a bipropellant auxiliary propulsion system, and multidimensional aerodynamic load-alleviation technology. It can be fitted with an EROS-2 upper stage for enhanced mission flexibility.

"The rocket is designed to provide commercial satellite customers with an efficient, cost-friendly 'space express,'" a Galactic Energy executive told Xinhua. The company has described the vehicle as a high-efficiency, low-cost "space train" for commercial satellite launches.

## Strategic Positioning and Industry Context

Galactic Energy's dual-track strategy—maintaining its proven Ceres-1 solid rocket operations while developing the liquid reusable Pallas-1—positions the company to compete for large-scale constellation deployment contracts. China's commercial space sector has entered a period of rapid growth, with government policy explicitly supporting commercial aerospace development. The Dongfeng Commercial Space Innovation Pilot Zone, from which the Pallas-1 launched, was established specifically to support high-density commercial launch demand.

The company now faces the next major test: the Yao-2 mission, which is planned to conduct the first in-orbit first-stage recovery test, involving vertical landing and recovery of the rocket's first stage. According to [Phoenix Tech](https://tech.ifeng.com/c/8w3oH05wIPE), the Yao-2 rocket was transported to the Jiuquan launch site on August 10, setting up a rare scenario where two Pallas-1 rockets would be present at the launch site simultaneously—a first for China's private space sector.

Galactic Energy is among several Chinese commercial space companies, including LandSpace, i-Space, CAS Space, and Space Pioneer, racing to establish reusable launch capabilities. The company's track record and technological progress have also positioned it among at least 10 commercial space firms that have initiated IPO processes in China, as noted by [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galactic_Energy).

## What's Next

The successful maiden flight of the Pallas-1 represents more than a single launch achievement—it signals China's private space industry is maturing from small satellite launchers to medium-lift reusable vehicles capable of supporting the country's growing constellation ambitions. The upcoming Yao-2 recovery test will be a critical validation of the rocket's reuse design and a key indicator of whether Galactic Energy can deliver on its promise of cost-effective, high-frequency launch services.

Industry observers will be watching closely as the company moves from demonstration to operational capability, with the potential to reshape the competitive dynamics of China's commercial launch market and accelerate the deployment of low Earth orbit satellite constellations.
