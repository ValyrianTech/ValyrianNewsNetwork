# China Unveils Green Industrial Blueprint for 15th Five-Year Plan

China's Ministry of Industry and Information Technology (MIIT) has issued the *15th Five-Year Plan for Industrial Green and Low-Carbon Development*, a comprehensive policy roadmap that sets ambitious 2030 targets for reducing industrial energy consumption, cutting carbon emissions, and expanding the circular economy. The plan, formally signed on July 15 and publicly released on July 31, 2026, outlines seven projected targets and seven key tasks designed to guide the country's industrial sector through its green transformation.

The blueprint arrives at a pivotal moment. Industry accounts for approximately two-thirds of China's total social energy consumption and about 70 percent of national carbon emissions, making the sector the most concentrated area of energy use and emissions in the world's second-largest economy. As [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/fortune/20260902/86c5a3a7efa94ae59170b28b45c43813/c.html) reported, the "15th Five-Year Plan" period is being described as "a critical period for green technology transformation, rule competition, and carbon peak efforts."

## Ambitious Targets for 2030

The plan sets out seven projected targets for the industrial sector by 2030, including a reduction of more than 10 percent in energy consumption per unit of added value for industrial enterprises above designated size, a decrease of more than 17 percent in CO2 emissions per unit of added value, and a 10 percent reduction in water consumption per unit of added value. The comprehensive utilization rate of bulk industrial solid waste is targeted to rise from 57 percent in 2025 to 62 percent, while annual recycling of major renewable resources is expected to reach 510 million tons by 2030, up from 380 million tons in 2025.

According to the [official MIIT document](https://www.miit.gov.cn/jgsj/jns/wjfb/art/2026/art_7af1d02a062e473a8aacaa1abb65bd74.html), the plan also calls for the output value of green factories at all levels to account for 45 percent of total output value of industrial enterprises above designated size, up from 30 percent in 2025, and the cultivation of 500 zero-carbon factories.

The overall goal, as stated in the [State Council policy interpretation](https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/202607/content_7077144.htm), is that by 2030, China's industrial structure and energy consumption structure will be further optimized, carbon dioxide emissions in the industrial sector will reach their peak, energy and resource utilization efficiency will steadily improve, and the competitive advantages of green and low-carbon industries will be consolidated and enhanced.

## Seven Key Tasks: '1 Action, 4 Capabilities, 2 Systems'

The plan organizes its implementation around seven key tasks, summarized as "1 Action, 4 Capabilities, 2 Systems." The single action involves the deep implementation of the carbon peak initiative in the industrial sector. The four capabilities focus on enhancing industrial green value creation, expanding green equipment and product supply, strengthening green low-carbon technology innovation, and advancing intelligent-green integrated development. The two systems aim to improve the industrial resource recycling framework and the green manufacturing and service ecosystem.

As [CCTV News](https://news.cctv.com/2026/07/31/ARTIwKanjNTdwnncAZhc2Sxt260731.shtml) reported, the MIIT's Energy Conservation and Comprehensive Utilization Department emphasized that the plan "not only emphasizes the green development of industry itself, but also places greater emphasis on industry's supporting role in the comprehensive green transformation of economic and social development."

## Building on '14th Five-Year Plan' Achievements

The new blueprint builds on significant progress made during the previous planning period. From 2021 to 2025, energy consumption per unit of added value of industrial enterprises above designated size fell by more than 13.5 percent, and China established 8,336 national green factories, with their output value share rising from 9 percent to 22 percent of total manufacturing output value above designated size.

Sun Xingshou, Standing Vice President and Secretary-General of the China Building Materials Federation, noted that during the "14th Five-Year Plan" period, the building materials industry's total energy consumption fell by 19.3 percent and carbon emissions by 24.9 percent, with the industry having already achieved its carbon peak. Sun told People's Daily Overseas Edition that the plan's "dual constraint" indicators for industrial energy consumption and carbon emission intensity "can promote the industry's coordinated development of energy conservation and carbon reduction."

Wang Zhiqin, Vice President of the China Academy of Information and Communications Technology, highlighted the scale of the challenge ahead: "Steel, chemical, nonferrous metals, building materials and other high-energy-consuming industries have large emission reduction space and heavy transformation tasks, making them the main battlefield for achieving the carbon peak and carbon neutrality goals."

## Green and Intelligent Integration

The plan places particular emphasis on the convergence of green development with artificial intelligence and digital technologies. Liu Wenqiang, Party Secretary and Vice President of the China Electronic Information Industry Development Research Institute, pointed out that during the "14th Five-Year Plan" period, the proportion of production capacity at energy efficiency benchmark levels for copper, lead, zinc and other nonferrous metal smelting exceeded 50 percent, and the reuse rate of industrial water above designated size surpassed 94 percent.

The plan calls for promoting AI-enabled green manufacturing, building green low-carbon databases, models, and algorithms, and developing green transformation intelligent agents. It also encourages the production of green intelligent home appliances and accelerates the application of intelligent connected new energy vehicles across all climate and scenario conditions.

Yu Xiuming, Vice President of the China Electronics Standardization Institute, explained that the plan "unifies energy efficiency and carbon emission related standards, regulates industrial energy and carbon measurement and accounting" and "sets clear emission reduction benchmarks for enterprises and industries."

## New Economic Growth Drivers

Beyond emissions reduction, the plan positions green and low-carbon development as a pathway to economic value creation. It calls for consolidating China's competitive advantages in green equipment and new energy products by increasing the proportion of green electricity used in polysilicon and wind turbine manufacturing, and it designates hydrogen energy as a strategic future industry requiring forward deployment.

According to the [State Council news release](https://www.gov.cn/lianbo/202607/content_7077143.htm), the plan also directs the construction of green computing facilities in regions rich in green energy and calls for significantly increasing the proportion of green electricity used in new computing infrastructure at national hub nodes.

Qi Shaozhi, Planning Manager of Harbin Electric Group's Strategic Development Department, said the company will "firmly pursue the direction of high-end, green, intelligent and integrated development" and "actively contribute to the green and low-carbon transformation of global energy consumption with our high-end intelligent, clean and green equipment products."

## Implementation and Forward Look

The MIIT has signaled a robust implementation framework. The ministry will strengthen monitoring and evaluation of implementation effectiveness, coordinate industrial policies with energy, science and technology, fiscal, and financial policies, and complete the revision and formulation of 500 industrial green and low-carbon standards. It will also research and formulate administrative regulations on waste power battery recycling and comprehensive utilization.

Industry observers will be watching several key indicators over the coming years: the pace of carbon peak achievement in the industrial sector, the expansion of green factory output, and the development of recycling infrastructure for new energy vehicle power batteries, photovoltaic modules, and wind turbine blades—all of which face large-scale retirement beginning in the late 2020s.

The plan represents more than a compliance framework; it signals a strategic shift in how China views industrial greening. As the MIIT's Energy Conservation and Comprehensive Utilization Department stated, the plan "positions green and low-carbon development as a pathway to value creation and enhancement, not just compliance," emphasizing the coordinated advancement of industrial greening and green industrialization. With the "15th Five-Year Plan" period now underway, the blueprint provides both a "construction map" and a "command baton" for China's industrial sector—setting the course for one of the most consequential industrial transformations in the world economy.
