# Museum Tour Marks China-Egypt Civilizational Exchange

Chinese President Xi Jinping and Egyptian President Abdel Fattah El-Sisi toured the Grand Egyptian Museum together on September 2, using the occasion to highlight the deep historical and cultural bonds between two of the world's oldest civilizations. The joint visit came during Xi's two-day state visit to Egypt, his first in a decade, and coincided with the 70th anniversary of China-Egypt diplomatic relations.

## A Symbolic Encounter Between Ancient Civilizations

Against the backdrop of the Giza pyramids, the two leaders and their spouses — Peng Liyuan and Entissar El-Sisi — walked through the museum's galleries, viewing prized artifacts including the golden mask and throne of Pharaoh Tutankhamun. The Grand Egyptian Museum, which opened in November 2025, is one of the world's largest displays of ancient relics. Xi had sent a congratulatory letter to Sisi upon its opening, as [reported by the Chinese Foreign Ministry](https://www.fmprc.gov.cn/eng/xw/zyxw/202609/t20260903_12015405.html).

During the tour, Xi emphasized that China and Egypt "each has a time-honored civilization and are both home to a great deal of world heritage," noting that the two great civilizations "have long engaged and connected with each other across time and space." He called on both sides to implement the Global Civilization Initiative and promote inclusiveness, coexistence, and mutual learning between different civilizations.

Sisi echoed these sentiments, stating that the "characteristics of inclusiveness and harmony in diversity shared by the ancient Egyptian civilization and the Chinese civilization are highly aligned with the vision of the Global Civilization Initiative." Egypt stands ready to pursue closer people-to-people exchanges with China, he added.

## Seven Decades of Diplomatic Partnership

The visit marked a significant milestone in bilateral relations. Egypt was the first Arab and African country to establish diplomatic relations with the People's Republic of China in 1956, and the two nations have maintained close ties ever since. In 2014, they elevated their relationship to a comprehensive strategic partnership, and in 2024, Egypt became the first Arab country to include the goal of building a community with a shared future in a bilateral joint statement.

In a signed article published in Egyptian newspapers Al-Ahram, Al-Akhbar, and Al-Gomhuria ahead of the visit, Xi quoted an Egyptian proverb: "Once you drink from the Nile, you are destined to return." He wrote that "the Yangtze River and the Yellow River gave birth to the Chinese civilization, and the Nile was the cradle of the ancient Egyptian civilization," as [noted by Xinhua](https://english.news.cn/20260901/dd6590abc3734211b658cf68c2e625fd/c.html).

## Deepening Economic and Strategic Ties

Beyond the cultural symbolism, the visit produced substantial diplomatic outcomes. Xi and Sisi held formal talks at Al-Ittihadiya Palace in Cairo, where more than 20 cooperation documents were signed covering areas including Belt and Road cooperation, artificial intelligence, economy and trade, and industrial and supply chain cooperation. The two sides also issued a Joint Statement on Deepening the China-Egypt Comprehensive Strategic Partnership.

China has been Egypt's largest trading partner for over a decade, with bilateral trade of approximately US$20 billion annually and Chinese investment exceeding US$10 billion. Key projects include the China-Egypt TEDA Suez Economic and Trade Cooperation Zone, the Central Business District in Egypt's New Administrative Capital, and the first intercity electric light rail in Egypt.

## Regional Security and Geopolitical Context

The visit carried significant geopolitical weight. As [AP News reported](https://apnews.com/article/egypt-china-middle-east-iran-war-us-946c3b90b6251c08fdcb219e24a088a2), China is cultivating an extensive relationship with one of America's key allies in the Middle East as it moves to expand its influence in the rapidly changing region. The visit came amid the ongoing US-Iran war, which has disrupted oil shipments through the Strait of Hormuz, making the Suez Canal an increasingly important alternative route for energy supplies.

During talks with Sisi, Xi stated that "the people in the Middle East should be the masters of their own affairs" and that "interference by external forces should be opposed," according to reports from [Friends of Socialist China](https://socialistchina.org/2026/09/04/xi-jinping-pays-state-visit-to-egypt-the-people-in-the-middle-east-should-be-the-masters-of-their-own-affairs/). He also proposed a framework for regional security architecture encompassing endogenous momentum, comprehensive governance, development foundation, and international coordination.

## Cultural Bridges Between Nations

The civilizational dimension of the visit reflects a broader trend of deepening cultural exchange. The Shanghai Museum's "The Civilization of Ancient Egypt" exhibition (July 2024-August 2025) drew 2.77 million visitors, setting a global attendance record for a paid special exhibition. Chinese-language education has been integrated into more than 40 Egyptian middle schools, and the two countries initiated their first joint archaeological mission in 2018 at the Montu Temple complex in Luxor.

Chinese Foreign Minister Wang Yi described the joint museum visit as "a vivid portrayal of civilizational exchange," noting that "the Yellow River and the Nile respectively gave birth to Chinese civilization and ancient Egyptian civilization," as [documented by the Chinese government](https://www.gov.cn/yaowen/liebiao/202609/content_7080095.htm).

## Looking Ahead

The visit concluded with Xi departing Cairo on September 2 and returning to Beijing the following morning. The joint statement and cooperation agreements signal a deepening partnership that extends beyond economics into cultural and strategic realms. As the two ancient civilizations mark seven decades of diplomatic ties, their leaders have demonstrated that civilizational dialogue can serve as a foundation for modern statecraft — a message with particular resonance at a time of heightened geopolitical tensions.

What remains to be seen is how this strengthened partnership will influence regional dynamics, particularly given Egypt's strategic position at the crossroads of the Suez Canal and its role as a key US ally navigating an increasingly multipolar world.
