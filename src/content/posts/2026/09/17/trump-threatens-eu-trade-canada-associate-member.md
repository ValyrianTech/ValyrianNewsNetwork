# Trump Threatens to Halt EU Trade Over Canada 'Associate Member' Plan

US President Donald Trump threatened on Wednesday to cut off all trade with the European Union in retaliation for a proposal to make Canada the bloc's first-ever "associate member," escalating transatlantic tensions as Ottawa and Brussels moved to deepen their partnership.

Speaking to journalists on 16 September, Trump dismissed the idea as "ridiculous" and warned of severe consequences if he deemed it a hostile act. "If they do this, if I consider it a hostile act, I will impose very heavy tariffs or I will stop all trade with Europe," he said, according to [RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/rapprochement-union-europeenne-canada-donald-trump-menace-d-arreter-les-echanges-commerciaux-avec-l-ue-11786370).

## A Proposal From Strasbourg

The threat came just hours after European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen used her annual State of the Union address in Strasbourg to propose elevating relations with Canada "to the highest possible level." Addressing Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney, who was seated as guest of honor — the first foreign head of government ever to attend the address — she said she wanted to work toward "the possibility for Canada to become the first associate member of the EU." She also proposed that Canada join a future "European Security Council" she is advocating.

The status of "associate member" is not legally defined by the EU treaties, leaving its concrete form unclear. As the [HuffPost](https://www.huffingtonpost.fr/international/article/donald-trump-menace-l-ue-a-cause-de-son-projet-ridicule-de-faire-du-canada-un-membre-associe_326042.html) noted, Germany had recently floated a similar status for Ukraine, suggesting such a country could attend certain ministerial meetings or summits without voting rights.

The following day, Carney addressed the European Parliament and welcomed the initiative. "Canada welcomes this ambition, and I can tell you that polls show an overwhelming majority of Canadians support much closer ties with Europe," he said, adding: "Europe and Canada are stronger together." The [RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/le-canada-accueille-favorablement-la-proposition-d-etre-membre-associe-de-l-union-europeenne-annonce-mark-carney-11786553) reported that he was long applauded by MEPs.

## Defensive, Not Dominant

Carney was careful to frame the project as a bid for resilience rather than the formation of a rival power bloc. "I am not proposing the creation of a third bloc with the goal of becoming a rival great power — simply with better manners," he said. "We do not seek power to dominate others. On the contrary, we seek resilience so that no one can control our open markets, undermine our sovereignty, or compromise our liberties."

In a thinly veiled allusion to Washington, he warned that economic integration is now "being used as a weapon and tariffs as a means of pressure," with financial mechanisms used for coercion and supply chains treated as vulnerabilities to exploit. "We are not allies of circumstance. We are not seeking zero-sum deals," he said, according to [France 24](https://www.france24.com/fr/europe/20260917-en-direct-mark-carney-parlement-européen-trump-menace-canada-ue).

Trump, who has repeatedly said he wants Canada to become the "51st US state," reacted sharply to the proposal, threatening to sever trade ties. His remarks land against the backdrop of an ongoing US-Canada trade war and fraught EU-US relations.

## What Each Side Stands to Gain

Beneath the rhetoric, the proposal reflects a shared drive in Ottawa and Brussels to reduce dependence on Washington. EU-Canada trade has grown by 80% since 2016 thanks to the CETA free-trade agreement, which removed almost all tariffs, and Canada this year became the first non-EU country to join the bloc's defense program SAFE.

Canada's ambassador to the EU, Jonathan Wilkinson, told AFP the two sides would examine areas where they have "complementary capabilities and needs" that are "strategically important" to "guarantee our sovereignty" — including energy, artificial intelligence, and critical raw materials such as rare earths.

The European Commission sought to play down the geopolitical implications. Spokesperson Olof Gill insisted the proposal "is not directed against anyone," a message echoed by EU Enlargement Commissioner Marta Kos, who said from Oslo that "Europe has never been so attractive as today."

In [La Libre](https://www.lalibre.be/international/europe/2026/09/17/alliance-unique-membre-associe-les-contours-de-la-romance-entre-lunion-europeenne-et-le-canada-restent-a-definir-MGBTBQ4JPVA6VNQRMZED3ITMEE/), special correspondent Olivier le Bussy noted that Carney had first floated a "unique relationship" with the EU in an interview with the Wall Street Journal days earlier — the groundwork for the Strasbourg invitation.

## Analysis: A Test of Europe's Reach

The episode crystallizes a broader realignment. Both the EU and Canada are navigating a world in which the United States, their longstanding security guarantor and largest trading partner, is increasingly willing to use economic leverage against allies. Carney's language — resilience against "economic coercion" — frames deeper integration as a defensive move rather than an act of aggression, an effort to insulate open economies from the weaponization of interdependence.

For Brussels, the proposal is also a statement of ambition. By extending a novel status to a developed, like-minded democracy, von der Leyen signals that Europe can attract partners beyond its traditional enlargement track — a counterpoint to perceptions of decline. Yet the ambiguity of "associate member" cuts both ways: it allows both sides to embrace a grand vision without committing to the institutional mechanics that would make it real.

Within Europe, reactions ranged from enthusiasm to caution. French Foreign Minister Jean-Noël Barrot said Paris was destined to build "ever-closer ties with Canada," adding that President Emmanuel Macron will meet Carney in Saint-Pierre-et-Miquelon on Sunday. Junior minister for Europe Benjamin Haddad called the idea "a very good idea," while conceding the institutional form remained undecided. "It is not up to the United States to choose the political and geopolitical orientations of Europeans," he said.

## What to Watch

The coming weeks will test whether the "associate member" concept survives contact with reality. Key questions remain: what precisely the status confers, whether it requires ratification by member states, and how far Canada is willing to go in aligning with EU standards.

Much will hinge on the US response. Trump's threat — to impose "very heavy tariffs" or halt trade entirely — is a high-stakes warning that could escalate if Brussels presses ahead. For now, both the EU and Canada appear to be betting that the benefits of closer ties outweigh the risks of provoking Washington. Whether that bet holds may define the shape of transatlantic relations for years to come.
