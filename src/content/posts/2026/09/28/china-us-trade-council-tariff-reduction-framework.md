# China, US Detail Tariff Cuts in Trade Council Deal

China's Ministry of Commerce on Monday released a detailed interpretation of the outcomes of the eighth round of China-US economic and trade consultations, confirming the creation of a government-level China-US Trade Council and a reciprocal "30 billion to 30 billion" tariff reduction framework. The announcement, issued by the ministry's Department of American and Oceanian Affairs, followed consultations held in New York and Washington from 20 to 23 September 2026, according to [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/fortune/20260928/7469b3cc0bbb478aa147b2b6531576f8/c.html).

## What the Two Sides Agreed

Under the framework, both countries will reduce tariffs on roughly USD 30 billion of each other's imports, benchmarked against 2024 bilateral trade volumes. More than 90 percent of the covered products will have all mutually imposed tariffs removed and will receive most-favored-nation (MFN) treatment, the ministry said.

The product lists reflect each side's priorities. The United States will cut tariffs on about USD 30 billion of Chinese goods including toys, home appliances, baby products, kitchen and bath products, and holiday gifts. China will reduce tariffs on roughly USD 30 billion of US imports including agricultural products, personal care products, medical devices, and coal.

According to the ministry's reading, the two sides agreed to jointly publish their reduction lists and to implement the cuts simultaneously after completing their respective domestic legal procedures. "Both sides agreed to reduce, on a reciprocal basis, tariffs on roughly USD 30 billion of each other's imports, with about 90 percent of covered products dropping to MFN rates," the interpretation stated.

The Trade Council's stated task is to optimize bilateral trade and to discuss arrangements and problems affecting trade. "It will provide an important platform and mechanism for China and the US to continuously lengthen the cooperation list and shorten the problem list," the ministry said, describing it as a foundation for a "constructive and stable strategic relationship."

## Institutional Shift Beyond One-Off Deals

The eighth round marks a shift from ad-hoc concessions toward standing mechanisms. As [21st Century Business Herald](https://www.21jingji.com/article/20260928/herald/34c063728cb121e570ba2a3e9abb04d4.html) summarized the ministry's interpretation, the two sides agreed to establish the Trade Council under the existing economic and trade consultation mechanism, and to create a parallel China-US Investment Council to provide a regular channel for dialogue on investment opportunities and barriers.

China's ministry said the Investment Council will raise policy transparency and predictability and respond to "reasonable concerns" of enterprises, subject to each country's laws and regulatory requirements.

A dedicated agriculture working group will sit under the Trade Council, co-led by China's Ministry of Commerce and the US Trade Representative. It will address two-way market access and regulatory issues for agricultural goods, with a first meeting planned before the end of 2026. The two sides had already agreed in May 2026 to resolve some agricultural non-tariff barriers and to include related products in the tariff framework.

## Coal Becomes a Signature Item

The inclusion of US coal in China's tariff-reduction list is among the most concrete elements of the package. The ministry said tariffs on Chinese imports of US coal will be folded into the framework, supporting annual imports of US coal in 2027 and 2028. "Importing US coal is a useful supplement to China's domestic coal market and will bring stable income and employment to the US coal industry," the ministry argued.

The US side has put a figure on the commitment. As [Lianhe Zaobao](https://www.zaobao.com.sg/news/world/story20260926-9740782) reported, citing a White House statement, China agreed to import at least 10 million tonnes of US coal in 2027 and again in 2028. The White House framed the arrangement as part of a broader deal under the newly launched Trade Council, and said eligible US products for preferential tariffs also include seafood, timber, and cosmetics, while Chinese products include small appliances, holiday decorations, and children's car safety seats.

The coal provision also connects to domestic supply pressures. A separate [Lianhe Zaobao](https://www.zaobao.com.sg/news/china/story20260928-9746419) report noted that an explosion at a Shanxi coal mine had reduced output by 100 million tonnes, adding context to China's import needs.

## Technology, Finance, and a Truce Extension

Beyond goods, the two sides carved out several new tracks. Vice Premier He Lifeng and US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent established an artificial intelligence dialogue and held a first session, exchanging views on AI risks and benefits. A next dialogue is planned before the end of November 2026, and the two sides agreed to set up a communication channel for AI incidents.

In financial services, the ministry said the two sides reached a principle-level consensus. China will lawfully approve applications from financial institutions of all countries—including US institutions—to operate and establish branches in China, and expressed hope that the US will offer a fair, transparent, and stable policy environment for Chinese financial institutions.

The consultation also extended the so-called Kuala Lumpur arrangement. That joint arrangement, reached in October 2025, had suspended certain tariff and non-tariff measures until 10 November 2026. In this round the two sides agreed to extend it to 10 January 2027 while continuing to explore a longer-term solution. The ministry said the combination of a fixed extension and continued discussion provides enterprises with a "relatively stable and predictable policy environment."

## Analysis: A Framework, Not a Final Settlement

The MOFCOM interpretation, widely carried by state and commercial outlets including [China News Service](https://www.chinanews.com.cn/cj/2026/09-28/10704683.shtml) and [People's Daily](http://world.people.com.cn/n1/2026/0928/c1002-40806689.html), emphasizes reciprocity and institutionalization: the numbers on each side are deliberately symmetrical, and the mechanisms—councils and working groups—are designed to outlast any single negotiation.

That symmetry is also a limitation. The roughly USD 30 billion covered on each side is a modest slice of a bilateral trade relationship worth hundreds of billions of dollars, and implementation still depends on each government completing its domestic legal procedures. The two sides also continue to differ on sensitive areas: Washington has pressed concerns about rare earths and critical mineral supply chains, while Beijing has sought relief on agricultural market access.

Still, the direction of travel is notable. After eight rounds of consultations since late 2025, the relationship has moved from crisis management toward structured bargaining, with agriculture, investment, finance, and AI now each assigned a dedicated venue.

## What to Watch

Three dates matter in the near term. Before the end of 2026, the agriculture working group is scheduled to hold its first meeting. Before the end of November 2026, the next AI dialogue is due. And by 10 January 2027, the extended Kuala Lumpur truce lapses unless a further arrangement is reached—an outcome the Chinese ministry said it expects the two sides to pursue "through high-level economic interaction before the end of the year."

Both sides also agreed to keep talking about increasing direct passenger flights, a step that would ease people-to-people and business exchanges. For now, the package stands as the most detailed public accounting yet of how the world's two largest economies intend to manage their trade relationship—turning a truce into a framework, while leaving the hardest questions for later rounds.
