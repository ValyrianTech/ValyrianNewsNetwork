# Xi's State Visit to US Redefines China-US Ties

President Xi Jinping made his first state visit to the United States in 11 years on September 23–25, 2026, capping roughly 43 hours of diplomacy with an eight-point consensus and a new framing of the relationship: a "China-US constructive strategic stability relationship based on respect, fairness, and reciprocity." According to [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/politics/leaders/20260928/331c059be6da4b2e9acc83191cca9a85/c.html), which published a detailed chronicle of the visit, the trip consolidated a positioning first established during President Donald Trump's reciprocal visit to Beijing in May and marked the first time the two powers exchanged head-of-state state visits within a single six-month period.

## An Unprecedented Welcome

Xi arrived at Joint Base Andrews on the evening of September 23, where Trump and First Lady Melania Trump personally greeted him and Peng Liyuan — an honor not extended to a visiting leader at that base since President John F. Kennedy welcomed British Prime Minister Harold Macmillan in 1962. The welcome featured a 21-gun salute, national anthems, and a military flyover.

The symbolism was deliberate. Joint Base Andrews was the departure point for Richard Nixon's historic 1972 trip to Beijing, and where Deng Xiaoping's aircraft landed seven years later. Xinhua framed the choice of venue as connecting the current visit to decades of bilateral history.

"This is a visit that has attracted worldwide attention," Trump said at the arrival, calling Xi "a great leader" and the moment "a great moment." In a written statement, Xi said the two countries "should be partners rather than rivals," adding that "realizing the great rejuvenation of the Chinese nation and making America great again can fully go hand in hand, reinforce each other, and benefit the world."

The following morning, a full White House welcome ceremony ran nearly 90 minutes, with the Marine Corps Band, a silent drill platoon on the South Lawn, and a flyover over the White House. Trump later invited Xi to board his helicopter at a newly built landing pad on the grounds.

## The Eight-Point Consensus

The substantive core of the visit was an eight-point consensus issued by the Chinese government on September 26. Its headline item enriches the relationship framing agreed in Beijing with the language of "respect, fairness, and reciprocity."

On trade, the two sides agreed to establish a Trade Council and an Investment Council, to set up an agriculture working group, and to implement a "$30 billion" reciprocal tariff reduction alongside an extension of outcomes from earlier Kuala Lumpur consultations. On artificial intelligence, they agreed to launch a China-US AI dialogue — with the next round set for November 2026 — and to create a communication channel for AI-related incidents. They also agreed to cooperate on anti-narcotics enforcement, including a joint crackdown on new psychoactive substances and their precursors that has already produced dozens of arrests.

Two militaries are to sign a crisis-communication memorandum of understanding "as soon as possible," and both sides will continue efforts to recover the remains of missing US service members in China. The consensus also noted that the US welcomed two pandas leased to Zoo Atlanta.

On international issues, the document stated that Iran should not develop nuclear weapons and that no country or institution should impose tolls on international waterways — a point with implications for global shipping routes.

## Taiwan, Raised but Unresolved

In the talks, Xi pressed the Taiwan issue directly, saying China's position on safeguarding national unity and territorial integrity "is very clear" and urging the US to "adhere to the correct position of opposing Taiwan independence, handle the Taiwan issue cautiously, and lay a solid foundation for China-US strategic cooperation."

Trump did not respond publicly on Taiwan. Reporting referenced in the research indicates that he delayed a roughly $14 billion arms sale to Taiwan, describing it as a "very good bargaining chip." Xinhua's account made no mention of a US response on the issue — a silence that external analysts flagged as significant.

## Between Pageantry and Substance

The visit drew both praise and skepticism. Chinese officials cast it as a milestone that opens a "new path for major powers" and crosses the so-called Thucydides Trap. Foreign Minister Wang Yi elaborated on the new framing, saying that "respect means respecting each other's sovereignty and territorial integrity," that "fairness means carrying out mutually beneficial cooperation" rather than zero-sum competition, and that "reciprocity means the two countries are sovereign and equal."

Outside observers were more measured. BBC correspondent Laura Bicker noted that despite the red-carpet welcome, "the Chinese leader did not get everything he wanted," adding that "underneath the pageantry, competition between the world's two most powerful countries remains as intense as ever." Other BBC coverage characterized the results as "plenty of pomp, little progress," with a tariff truce extended only from November to January and no clarity on AI safety or Taiwan.

Still, the diplomatic choreography carried weight. A state dinner in the East Room was styled in red for the Mid-Autumn Festival, and the toast wine was drawn from the same region as the vintage used when Zhou Enlai and Nixon toasted "peace" in Beijing in 1972. Business and technology leaders at the dinner included Nvidia's Jensen Huang, Meta's Mark Zuckerberg, Apple's Tim Cook, OpenAI's Sam Altman, Microsoft's Satya Nadella, Amazon's Jeff Bezos, and Tesla's Elon Musk.

## People-to-People Ties

Beyond the negotiating table, Xi announced that China would invite 100,000 American youth to visit over the next five years, building on an earlier "50,000 in five years" initiative that Xinhua said was completed two and a half years ahead of schedule. In a toast, he invoked a story of two people connected by 50,000 saplings planted in the Mu Us Desert, and he referenced the WWII alliance and "ping-pong diplomacy" as foundations of the relationship.

Peng Liyuan, accompanied by Melania Trump, visited the National Museum of Asian Art, where she expressed appreciation for the return of Chinese cultural relics and called for continued cooperation on cultural exchange and heritage protection.

## What's Next

The two leaders confirmed reciprocal attendance at upcoming multilateral events: Trump is expected at the APEC summit in Shenzhen in November 2026, and Xi at the G20 in Miami in December. Xi also extended an invitation for Trump to visit China again. Trump, posting on Truth Social after the visit, called the meeting "a meeting of friendship, strength and success for both China and the United States" and said he looked forward to the next one.

The open questions are substantial. The tariff truce is short, the AI dialogue has yet to begin, and Taiwan remains unresolved despite being raised at the highest level. Xi himself cautioned that building the relationship "is not an expedient measure, nor the work of a single day," calling for consensus to be implemented "with the spirit of hammering nails." Whether the pageantry of a 43-hour visit translates into durable stability will depend on the follow-through — a test that begins with November's AI talks and the leaders' next scheduled meetings.
