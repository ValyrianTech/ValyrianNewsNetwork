# Belgian Teachers Left Reeling by Capped Indexation Pay Slip

Hundreds of teachers in Belgium's French-speaking Federation Wallonie-Bruxelles (FWB) were taken aback this weekend to find the gross annual salary scale on their September pay slips had fallen by roughly 100 to 300 euros, even as their net pay rose. An [RTBF investigation](https://www.rtbf.be/article/indexation-plafonnee-en-septembre-les-enseignants-surpris-par-une-baisse-de-leur-bareme-brut-pourtant-les-calculs-sont-corrects-11791063) using an actual teacher's pay slips has concluded that the calculations behind the apparent contradiction are correct, and that the real failure was one of communication.

The confusion stems from Belgium's new capped indexation system, applied for the first time in September to public-sector salaries, pensions and social benefits. As [RTBF explains](https://www.rtbf.be/article/retraite-allocataire-ou-travailleur-dans-le-secteur-public-voici-comment-l-indexation-plafonnee-va-influencer-votre-revenu-de-fin-septembre-11790940), the automatic 2 percent indexation applies fully up to 4,000 euros gross per month, but above that threshold this round yields a fixed maximum increase of 80 euros. For pensions and benefits, the cap is set at 2,000 euros gross, with a maximum increase of 40 euros.

## A Scale That Looks Smaller, a Salary That Is Not

The alarm spread quickly on social media after teachers received their pay slips on Friday. "We noticed that our gross annual salary had been reduced. Given the index jump, you do not notice it directly. Is it legal to reduce our salary? We are dismayed by these sneaky new practices," one teacher told RTBF. A second warned of "an unplanned and illegal modification of our gross scales," citing fear of impacts on "our future taxes and pensions."

The apparent contradiction became clearer once the mechanism was unpacked. For one teacher, the calculation worked as follows: an August annual scale of 27,285.62 euros multiplied by the August index of 2.1647 produced an annual salary of 59,065.18 euros, or 4,922.10 euros gross per month. To cap the September increase at 80 euros, the administration lowered the base scale to 27,185.38 euros and applied the new index of 2.2080, arriving at 60,025.32 euros annually, or 5,002.10 euros a month. That is exactly 80 euros more than August.

The administration chose this route, modifying the annual scale rather than creating a different index for each worker, because the index is common to all of Belgium and is used to calculate future indexations. The result is that only the base scale figure changed; both monthly and annual gross pay increased. According to RTBF, the operation has no impact on pensions, which are calculated on gross pay, nor any additional tax effect beyond that of a normal indexation.

## Union and Political Fallout

Despite the technical confirmation, the political fallout was swift. Unions, opposition parties and activist collectives denounced the lack of warning. According to a [follow-up RTBF report](https://www.rtbf.be/article/indexation-des-salaires-des-enseignants-malgre-les-explications-la-csc-demande-la-clarte-mars-attacks-appelle-a-un-arret-de-travail-lundi-11791233), the CSC-Enseignement union called on Education Minister Valerie Glatigny (MR) to provide full clarity on Monday, while the activist collective Mars Attacks! urged teachers to stage a work stoppage the same day.

"We demand clear, precise and unambiguous information from the start of the week. We demand that all guarantees be provided as to the possible impacts of the measure on the rest of their careers and their retirement pensions," said CSC-Enseignement secretary-general Roland Lahaye. The union later decried the "ambiguity" of the minister's statements, noting that no communication had been made directly to teaching staff in the FWB.

Sentiment among teachers was sharp. "I note that my gross annual salary has dropped by a hundred euros. We are all in the same situation," one teacher, Sandrine, told [RTL Info](https://www.rtl.be/actu/belgique/societe/jai-ete-indexe-mais-mon-salaire-annuel-brut-ete-diminue-de-300-euros-les/2026-09-26/article/801477). Another, Aline, warned that the modest raise "masks a fake accounting fix by the administration that will cost teachers dearly over the long term."

In the FWB Parliament, PS group leader Martin Casier sent a letter to the chamber's president demanding full answers by Sunday evening, threatening an extraordinary committee session otherwise. Ecolo MP Benedicte Linard said she would question the minister, arguing that touching salary scales normally requires a legal decision from the government.

## The Ministry's Defence

Glatigny's cabinet insisted nothing was lost. "The teachers' scales are not modified," it said. "The administration introduced a fictitious scale [bareme fictif] in order to take non-indexation into account in the calculation of remuneration. This has no impact on the teacher's seniority or pension." The cabinet added that, all else being equal, every teacher would see their salary rise with indexation.

The minister stressed that the method used to apply the federal decision is "identical to that applied in Flemish and German-speaking education, as well as in the FWB public service and the federal public service." The FWB government approved the method on 17 July, unions were informed via the Comite A on 20 August, and a formal explanatory notice was promised to teachers only early this week, after pay slips had already been issued.

The deeper problem, [as La Libre noted](https://www.lalibre.be/belgique/2026/09/27/lindexation-plafonnee-sapplique-aux-fonctionnaires-en-septembre-et-suscite-lemoi-chez-les-enseignants-francophones-34G6P7W4VFEWPD6ZBVPIUCLZFA/), is that the controversy erupted in an already explosive context, following the FWB government's contested education savings plan. RTBF also reported that it could not verify whether modifying annual scales in the public service or education requires a formal government decision, leaving one of the unions' core legal questions unanswered.

## A Deeply Unpopular Measure

The episode has thrown a spotlight on the federal capped indexation itself, which is widely disliked. An [RTBF, VRT and De Standaard national survey](https://www.rtbf.be/article/l-enquete-nationale-2026-les-belges-ressentent-la-crise-economiquee-soutiennent-la-reforme-du-chomage-mais-rejettent-l-indexation-plafonnee-a-4000-euros-bruts-11727399) conducted by the University of Antwerp and ULB found fewer than one in three voters favours the measure, 30 percent in Flanders, 27 percent in Brussels and 25 percent in Wallonia. The survey's authors concluded that while Belgians support targeted measures, they reject broad ones that touch everyone's wallet.

That backdrop helps explain why a payroll technicality metastasised into a political crisis. For teachers, the numbers may ultimately have added up, but the explanation arrived after the anger, not before it.

## What to Watch

The coming days will test whether the ministry's promised explanatory notice can calm the situation. Mars Attacks! has called for a work stoppage on Monday, the CSC is demanding written guarantees on career and pension impacts, and the PS has left open the threat of an extraordinary parliamentary session. The outstanding question, whether the modification of salary scales was legally sound, remains, for now, unresolved.
