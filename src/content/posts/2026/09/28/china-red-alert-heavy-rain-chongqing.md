# China Issues Red Alert as Heavy Rain Hits Chongqing

A red-level geological disaster alert—China's highest risk rating—was issued for two districts in the southwestern municipality of Chongqing as a persistent rain band pounded Chongqing, Hubei and Anhui, with a strong cold front set to sweep across central and eastern China beginning late Monday.

Citing the [Chongqing Meteorological Bureau](https://www.news.cn/local/20260927/9d46208d2bfe46579d5c6339669e725e/c.html), the alert placed Kaizhou and Wuxi districts at Level I (red) for the 24-hour window from 20:00 on Sept. 27 to 20:00 on Sept. 28, warning of very high risks of landslides, collapses and debris flows.

## A Tiered Warning Across the Region

The alert, jointly issued at 17:00 on Sept. 27 by the Chongqing Municipal Planning and Natural Resources Bureau and the Chongqing Meteorological Bureau, cascaded down through four risk levels across the municipality:

- **Level I (red):** Kaizhou, Wuxi — very high risk
- **Level II (orange):** Chengkou, Yunyang — high risk
- **Level III (yellow):** Wanzhou, Fengjie, Wushan — relatively high risk
- **Level IV (blue):** Changshou, Fengdu, Shizhu, Pengshui, Zhongxian — some risk

Authorities urged districts and departments to strengthen coordination and consultation and to implement full-chain geological disaster prevention. Under the "four-tier grid" monitoring system, personnel were instructed to conduct pre-rain inspections, in-rain patrols and post-rain verifications, focusing on hidden hazard points and the peripheries of densely populated sites such as market towns and schools.

Heavy rain easily triggers sudden geological hazards such as landslides, collapses and debris flows, and these carry a lag effect, requiring sustained vigilance even after the rain peak passes, officials cautioned.

## Rainfall Intensifies Across the Region

The rain band has held steady from China's Hua'xi region through the Jianghuai basin, according to the [China Weather Network](https://www.news.cn/politics/20260928/da626d29d201474cbf1f91b06622920a/c.html). On Sept. 27, moderate-to-heavy rain fell across northwestern Hubei, southwestern and northern Chongqing, southeastern Henan, Anhui, southern Jiangsu, Zhejiang and northern Jiangxi, with localized torrential rain of 100–141 millimeters striking Chongqing's Chengkou and Wuxi counties as well as Hangzhou and Ningbo in Zhejiang.

The [Central Meteorological Observatory](https://finance.sina.cn/2026-09-28/detail-initirzi8835654.d.html) forecast moderate-to-heavy rain through Sept. 30 across northeastern Sichuan, Chongqing, southeastern Shaanxi, most of Hubei, central-southern Henan, Anhui, Jiangsu, Shanghai, northern Guizhou, northwestern Hunan and northern Zhejiang. Localized torrential rain of 50–90 millimeters was expected in southeastern Shaanxi, central Anhui, western Hubei, northern Chongqing and central Jiangsu.

By the end of September, cumulative precipitation in northeastern Sichuan, central-northern Chongqing, western and northern Hubei, Anhui, Jiangsu, Shanghai and northern Zhejiang could reach 60–120 millimeters, the observatory said.

## Cold Front to Break Southern Heat

The heavy rain coincides with a strong cold front forecast to move from north to south across central and eastern China from Monday night through Oct. 1. Temperatures in Jiangnan and areas to its north are expected to drop 4–8°C, with parts of Inner Mongolia cooling by as much as 10°C, accompanied by level 4–6 northerly winds.

After the cooling, the 20°C minimum temperature line will reach southern Jiangnan, while the 10°C line pushes to southern North China—bringing a sharp autumn chill to the northeast and north. In the meantime, under the subtropical high, southern Jiangnan and South China continue to swelter, with persistent temperatures above 35°C across central-southern Hunan, Jiangxi, southern Zhejiang, Fujian, Guangdong and northern Guangxi. Because of high humidity, the perceived temperature will exceed 35°C even where maximums stay below the official high-temperature threshold.

Separately, the [Ministry of Natural Resources and the China Meteorological Administration](https://news.qq.com/rain/a/20260927A0BOHD00) issued a yellow geological disaster warning for western Hubei, northern Chongqing, southwestern Tibet and southeastern Shaanxi, while the Ministry of Water Resources and the weather administration flagged elevated flash flood risks in northwestern Hubei, northeastern Sichuan, northern Chongqing and southern Shaanxi.

## Timing Raises the Stakes

The event overlaps with the National Day Golden Week travel surge beginning Oct. 1, raising the stakes for transportation and tourism safety across the region. The northwestern Chongqing belt along the Daba Mountains and the Three Gorges reservoir area is historically among China's most landslide- and debris-flow-prone zones, and the Kaizhou-Wuxi-Chengkou-Yunyang corridor is repeatedly placed under high alerts during heavy-rain episodes.

## What to Watch

With the rain band expected to shift southward and the cold front arriving, officials advised the public to monitor destination and en-route forecasts, plan travel reasonably and watch for traffic safety. The key question in the coming days will be whether the lag effect of saturated soil produces landslides in the red-alert districts—and whether the cold front's arrival ends the southern heat wave without adding further strain to already waterlogged ground.
