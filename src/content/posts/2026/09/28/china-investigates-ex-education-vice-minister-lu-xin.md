# China Investigates Ex-Education Vice Minister Lu Xin

China's top anti-corruption bodies have placed Lu Xin, a former vice minister of education and onetime provincial vice governor, under disciplinary review and supervisory investigation, according to an announcement carried by [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/legal/20260928/092d59a2ae8c4841ab7e377cbfbcef93/c.html).

The one-paragraph notice, issued on 28 September 2026 — the first working day after China's Mid-Autumn Festival holiday — stated that Lu, 71, is suspected of "serious violations of discipline and law" and is being investigated by the Central Commission for Discipline Inspection (CCDI) and the National Supervisory Commission (NSC). That phrase is the standard CCDI formulation used when a senior official is formally placed under investigation and precedes any potential prosecution.

## A Long Career Across Liaoning and Beijing

Lu was born in July 1955 in Fushun, Liaoning Province, and earned a doctorate in economics, according to [Jiemian News](https://www.jiemian.com/article/15144157.html). Her career began in the northeastern rust-belt province she would call home for nearly four decades.

She started as a sent-down youth and teacher, worked in the finance office of a Fushun carbon plant in 1974, and studied finance at Liaoning Finance College — now Dongbei University of Finance and Economics — from 1978 to 1982. She then rose through the Fushun and Liaoning finance bureaus, becoming Fushun's finance chief and then its vice mayor in the early 1990s. In 2003, before turning 48, she was appointed vice governor of Liaoning.

In 2009 she moved to Beijing as a vice minister of education and a member of the ministry's Party Leadership Group, a post she held until January 2016, overseeing development planning, vocational education, ethnic education, private education and continuing education. From December 2015 to December 2018 she concurrently served as a deputy director of the Central Xinjiang Work Coordination Office. After retiring from active ministerial service, she was elected president of the Chinese Vocational and Technical Education Association (CVTEA) in December 2018.

## Signs of a Pending Case

According to [Caixin](https://china.caixin.com/2026-09-28/102489188.html), Lu had been absent from public view for nearly two months before the announcement. Her last major appearance was on 25 July 2026, when she attended a CVTEA committee meeting in Beijing and delivered a report on developing the intelligent economy.

Following the announcement, her name was removed from the "Association Leaders" section of the CVTEA website, Jiemian and [Lianhe Zaobao](https://www.zaobao.com.sg/news/china/story20260928-9746653) reported, leaving the president's slot blank — a standard institutional distancing step that may signal the review touches on her post-retirement roles.

## Second Education Official in the Anti-Graft Wave

Lu is the second senior official tied to the Ministry of Education to be caught up in the campaign. The most prominent prior case is Du Yubo, the ministry's former deputy Party chief and vice minister, who was investigated in September 2024 and sentenced in November 2025 to 15 years in prison for accepting more than 67.93 million yuan in bribes, Jiemian reported. In its citation of the Du case, authorities said he had "lived off education through education," treating education resources as a tool for personal enrichment.

Caixin counted Lu as the ninth female senior official investigated since the 20th Communist Party Congress in 2022, a list that includes figures such as Li Weiwei and Zhao Jinyun. That pattern has drawn attention as a measure of the campaign's reach into previously less-scrutinized ranks.

## A Possible Liaoning Nexus

The timing of Lu's case has also prompted scrutiny of a possible regional connection. Caixin noted that she shares an alma mater — Dongbei University of Finance and Economics — with Dai Yulin, a former Liaoning Provincial Standing Committee member who was announced under investigation on 17 September 2026, eleven days earlier. Lu spent 37 years working in Liaoning before her transfer to Beijing, and the province has historically been a focus of China's anti-corruption efforts.

Investigators have not disclosed the specific alleged violations, and it remains unclear whether the case centers on her Education Ministry tenure, her long career in Liaoning, her Xinjiang coordination role, or her activities after retirement. Chinese authorities rarely detail the substance of a case until it reaches the prosecution stage.

## What to Watch

The announcement, released on the first working day after a public holiday, reflects the CCDI's continued emphasis on the education sector and on officials long past their active service. Whether Lu's case broadens into a Liaoning-linked cluster alongside Dai Yulin, or reinforces the pattern of Education Ministry cases following Du Yubo, will become clearer as the investigation proceeds. For now, the standard one-paragraph notice leaves the substance of the allegations — and the full scope of the review — undisclosed.
