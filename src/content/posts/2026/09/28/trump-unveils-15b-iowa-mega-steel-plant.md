# Trump Unveils $15B Iowa Mega-Steel Plant

President Donald Trump announced Monday that Mesabi Metallics will build a roughly $15 billion steel plant in Iowa, which the White House describes as the largest steel facility ever constructed in the United States and the first new "mega-steel" integrated plant since the 1960s. The project, unveiled from the Oval Office alongside company executives, promises 1,750 permanent jobs in Iowa and between 5,000 and 6,000 construction jobs in its first phase, according to [Fox News](https://www.foxnews.com/politics/largest-ever-us-steel-plant-coming-key-red-state-trump-unveils-15b-investment).

The White House says the plant will produce 7.5 million tons of steel annually in its first phase, eventually scaling to 10 million tons per year — roughly one-tenth of the entire U.S. steel industry's output last year. But the scale of the announcement comes with a long timeline: first steel production is not expected until 2030.

## A Domestic Supply Chain, From Minnesota to Iowa

The Iowa facility is designed to anchor a fully domestic supply chain. It will run on iron ore from Mesabi's newly built mine in Nashwauk, Minnesota, on the Mesabi Iron Range, a $2.5 billion project that is the first new iron ore mine in the United States in 50 years. According to [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/trump-administration/trump-unveil-steel-plant-iowa-tariffs-manufacturing-rcna600241), the Minnesota mine will produce about 7.5 million tons of iron per year and create roughly 350 permanent jobs.

The White House says the first phase of the Iowa plant and the Minnesota mine together represent nearly $18 billion in investment, and estimates the steel plant alone will generate approximately $95 billion in economic activity during construction and its first decade of operation. The facility will also be capable of producing high-grade steel for defense-sector requirements and critical infrastructure, which the administration frames as a national security priority.

"President Trump is delivering on his promise to rebuild American industry, reshore manufacturing, and create new jobs," White House spokeswoman Taylor Rogers said in a statement. "Today's announcement underscores the President's historic efforts to revitalize the U.S. steel industry — supporting local communities, strengthening supply chains, and protecting our national security."

Unlike most newer U.S. steel facilities, which are smaller electric arc furnace operations, the Iowa project will be a large-scale integrated steel complex, the administration says.

## A Two-Decade Saga on the Iron Range

The project's history is far from straightforward. As [ABC News](https://abcnews.com/Politics/trump-announce-new-15b-steel-plant-white-house/story?id=136821451) noted, the venture traces back roughly 20 years to an effort by the Essar Group, an Indian multinational conglomerate that first proposed the mine and mill project and ultimately failed. Essar Steel Minnesota filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy in 2016 after accumulating some $1 billion in debt, and the project later emerged as Mesabi Metallics.

The [Star Tribune](https://www.startribune.com/mesabi-metallics-essar-group-taconite-iron-ore-mine-weeks-from-opening/601859251) reported that the Minnesota mine is now weeks from opening, concluding a 20-year saga that included a bankruptcy, lost leases and spurned state investments. About 1,500 contractors are on site to meet the deadline, and the company has begun accepting applications for operational jobs. Commercial pellet production has not yet begun; initial direct-reduction grade output is targeted for the fourth quarter of 2026.

Unease lingers for some who lived through the first attempt. Casey Venema, an Itasca County commissioner and a former Essar Steel Minnesota worker, told the Star Tribune: "Are we a little cautious? Of course. It's been a long road. But every day it gets better." Nashwauk Mayor Greg Heyblom, who attended a 2007 groundbreaking for the mine, said the company's approach has changed, adding, "This time around, the attitude and respect and consideration is completely different."

The project also received a major federal boost. The Export-Import Bank of the United States said in a [September release](https://www.exim.gov/news/exim-chairman-visits-americas-first-new-iron-ore-mine-50-years-operations-begin-northern) that Chairman John Jovanovic toured the mine and that the agency will issue up to $10 billion in financing for Mesabi's expansion. "Make no mistake: supply chain security is national security," Jovanovic said, describing the mine as "the first of its kind opening in America in over 50 years."

## Tariffs Underpin the Push

The announcement is closely tied to Trump's trade policy. Trump doubled tariffs on steel and aluminum imports to 50% last year, arguing that protecting domestic producers is essential for economic and national security. Although the Supreme Court earlier this year struck down many of Trump's tariffs, it left duties on imported steel and aluminum in place, and the U.S. currently levies a 50% tariff on most steel imports.

The policy has drawn support from domestic steelmakers. Steel industry groups sent a letter to Trump last week urging him to "stand firm against efforts to weaken the tariffs." "Weakening the tariffs now would put that progress at risk, threatening American jobs and the economic and national security benefits the policy was designed to protect," they warned.

At the same time, the tariffs have raised costs for companies consuming steel. S&P Global reported that federal data showed prices for iron and steel rose 10.4% between April 2025 and April 2026, while the steel mill products index climbed 13.3%, even as domestic steel production increased.

## A Political Backdrop

The timing is unmistakably political. The announcement lands just over a month before the Nov. 3 midterm elections. According to the [New York Post](https://nypost.com/2026/09/28/us-news/trump-to-announce-largest-steel-plant-in-american-history-to-be-built-in-iowa/), Republicans are defending all four of Iowa's House seats and the Senate seat vacated by retiring Sen. Joni Ernst. Polls show all five congressional races competitive, and Democrat Rob Sand is favored to flip the governor's mansion for the first time in 20 years. The White House has dispatched Vice President JD Vance to Iowa repeatedly.

The steel plant is slated for eastern Iowa, in the Lee County area, where the Des Moines Register reported that a commercial real estate agent had approached local residents about selling land in recent weeks — indicating that site acquisition was still underway.

## Questions Ahead

The project's scale and ambition are matched by significant uncertainty. The two-decade history of missed deadlines and a bankruptcy raises questions about whether the promised jobs and output will materialize on schedule. The roughly $15 billion investment against 1,750 permanent jobs has also drawn scrutiny over its cost per position. And the facility's first steel is not expected until 2030, well beyond the current electoral horizon.

Mesabi is also seeking federal "critical mineral" status for high-grade iron ore, which is not currently on the U.S. Geological Survey's critical minerals list. The company has hired the lobbying firm Akin Gump and longtime Trump adviser Jason Miller to press the case, arguing that steel — and thus ore — is central to both the economy and national security.

For now, the administration is presenting the Iowa plant as evidence that its economic agenda is restoring American heavy manufacturing. Whether it delivers on that promise will play out over years, not months — beginning with the Minnesota mine's long-awaited startup and the Iowa plant's first pour of steel, now targeted for 2030.
