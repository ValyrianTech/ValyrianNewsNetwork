# F-16 Crashes in Northern Michigan; Pilot Ejects Safely

An F-16 Fighting Falcon from the Texas Air National Guard crashed in a residential area of Grand Traverse County in northern Michigan on Thursday afternoon, prompting an emergency response and a temporary evacuation of nearby homes. The jet's single pilot ejected safely and was reported to be in stable condition, and officials said no civilians were injured.

The aircraft, assigned to the 149th Fighter Wing, was on a routine training mission from the Air National Guard's Combat Readiness Training Center in Alpena, Michigan, when it "experienced an incident," a spokesperson for the Texas Military Department said, according to [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/us-news/f-16-fighter-jet-crashes-residential-area-northern-michigan-rcna598451). Michael Kroll, a spokesperson for the Michigan Department of Military and Veterans Affairs, confirmed the jet was an F-16 from the Texas Air National Guard.

## Pilot Safe, No Civilian Injuries

Michigan State Police said the pilot was stable and had been transferred to a local hospital. Grand Traverse County Sheriff Michael Shea told reporters at an afternoon press conference that the pilot was "conscious and speaking with law enforcement."

Blair Township Supervisor Nicole Blonshine said there were no reports of civilian injuries. She said the aircraft came down into a pond between two homes, and local imagery later showed the jet had impacted a clear grass area rather than a structure.

The crash occurred near County Road 633 and Blair Townhall Road, an intersection that drew a rapid response from Grand Traverse 911 dispatchers, who closed nearby roads and urged drivers to stay away.

## Evacuation Over Toxic Hazard

A hazardous chemical spill tied to the crash led authorities to order the evacuation of all business and residential properties within a one-mile radius, state police said. The order was lifted as of 9:44 p.m.

The precaution stemmed from the F-16's emergency power unit, which uses highly toxic hydrazine monopropellant as fuel and requires special handling, as [The Aviationist](https://theaviationist.com/2026/09/17/f-16-crashes-in-northern-michigan/) reported. Residents near County Road 633 and Jeffrey Place in Green Lake Township were among those told to leave the area.

## Possible Bird Strike Under Investigation

Air traffic control audio obtained by NBC News describes the declaration of a possible mayday after the plane "may have taken a bird." The recording goes on to describe "a lot of black smoke" after a report that "it appears that they may have lost an engine and ejected."

The two F-16s were flying in formation under visual flight rules along the low-level route designated VR634, using the callsigns BUCK 11 and BUCK 12, The Aviationist reported. According to radio traffic, BUCK 11 declared the mayday on behalf of its stricken wingman and alerted controllers that only one pilot was aboard the downed jet.

The Texas Military Department spokesperson said the incident is under investigation. The preliminary bird-strike indication has not been confirmed by officials.

## Witness Accounts

Witnesses described a sudden explosion and a dramatic parachute descent. Nicole Conrad told NBC News that her children spotted the military jet first.

"I hear a big boom, and then I see a colorful parachute and this guy floating down from the sky," she said.

Another witness, Ed Wilson, said he saw the plane go down, followed by an explosion and black smoke. "The jet just went straight in with a nose up and disappeared," he said.

Wendy Szatkowski Sobeck, who lives near the crash site, wrote on Facebook that she and her husband heard the explosion and ran outside, adding that "stuff continued to explode" afterward, as [Michigan News Source](https://www.michigannewssource.com/2026/09/update-f-16-crashes-in-grand-traverse-county-area-evacuated/) reported. Early witness accounts had suggested two people might have been aboard, but officials confirmed that only one pilot ejected.

## Training Exercise and What's Next

According to [Stars and Stripes](https://www.stripes.com/branches/air_force/2026-09-17/f-16-crash-michigan-22883398.html), the 149th Fighter Wing is based at Kelly Field in San Antonio and was deployed to Michigan for Operation Northern Cactus, a joint exercise testing the unit's ability to deploy forces and operate from an expeditionary location. The unit had also been scheduled to conduct a flyover Friday night at a football game for POW/MIA Recognition Day, Michigan News Source reported.

The F-16 Fighting Falcon is a single-engine, multi-role fighter known for its maneuverability, and the Air Force describes it as proven in both air-to-air combat and air-to-surface attack.

The crash adds to a broader record of military aviation mishaps involving training flights over populated areas, a recurring concern for communities near active low-level training routes. As investigators work to determine what brought the jet down, the episode's most striking fact remains that a fighter jet fell between two houses—and no one on the ground was hurt.
