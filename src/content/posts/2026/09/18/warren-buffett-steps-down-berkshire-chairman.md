# Warren Buffett Steps Down as Berkshire Chairman

Warren Buffett, the 96-year-old investor known worldwide as the "Oracle of Omaha," stepped down as chairman of Berkshire Hathaway on Friday, effective immediately, ending a 56-year run at the helm of the conglomerate he built into a roughly $1 trillion powerhouse. Buffett will remain on the board as chairman emeritus, while his son Howard G. "Howie" Buffett, 71, was elected chairman of the board. Greg Abel, who took over as chief executive at the start of 2026, remains CEO.

Buffett announced the move in a letter to shareholders, writing simply, "Father Time always wins." According to [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/09/18/nx-s1-5973852/warren-buffett-steps-down), the transition marks the close of one of the most celebrated chapters in American business history.

## A Deliberate, Staged Handoff

The announcement completes a succession plan that Buffett and Berkshire's board have been building for years. He first announced his decision to step back from the CEO role in May 2025, surprising shareholders at the company's annual meeting in Omaha. Abel formally became CEO on January 1, 2026.

Friday's move completes the second half of that transition. [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/18/buffett-stepping-down-as-berkshire-chairman.html) reported that Buffett framed the split of authority deliberately: the CEO runs the company, while the chairman guards its culture and values. "Greg runs the company; Howard will guard its culture and values — both worth more than anything on our balance sheet," Buffett wrote. "Think of Howard as a policy the shareholders own and hope never to claim against."

Howard Buffett, one of the investor's three children, has served on Berkshire's board since 1993 — 33 years. He has led the Howard G. Buffett Foundation, focused on global food security and conflict mitigation, since 1999, and has held board seats at companies including Archer Daniels Midland, ConAgra Foods, and The Coca-Cola Company. Buffett had signaled for years that his son was the intended chairman successor. "He is getting it because he's his son," Buffett told The Wall Street Journal in January 2025. "I'm very, very, very lucky in the fact that I trust all three of my children."

In a statement on behalf of the board, Abel praised his predecessor. "Warren's impact on Berkshire and its owners is without parallel in the history of American business," he said. "The culture Warren built and the values he championed will remain at the heart of Berkshire, and Howard will be their guardian." Susan L. Decker continues as lead independent director.

## From a Failing Textile Mill to a $1 Trillion Conglomerate

Buffett took control of Berkshire Hathaway in 1965, when it was a struggling New England textile manufacturer, and became chairman in 1970. Over six decades he transformed it into a sprawling holding company spanning insurance (GEICO, General Re), railroads (BNSF), energy, manufacturing, and consumer goods, alongside major equity stakes in Apple, American Express, Coca-Cola, and Bank of America.

The results were extraordinary. As [24/7 Wall St.](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/09/18/warren-buffett-is-stepping-down-as-chairman-heres-how-much-youd-have-if-you-invested-1000-in-1970-when-he-first-took-the-role/) noted, a $1,000 investment in Berkshire's Class A stock at the $39 share price in 1970 would be worth roughly $19.6 million today. Berkshire delivered a 19.7% compounded annual return to shareholders over Buffett's tenure — nearly double that of the S&P 500. The company has never split its Class A stock.

Berkshire now employs nearly 400,000 people and reported $44.5 billion in operating earnings last year. Buffett's personal net worth stands at about $145 billion, making him the 10th richest person in the world, according to [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-18/buffett-replaced-by-son-howard-as-berkshire-hathaway-chairman).

## A Tough Act to Follow

The handoff comes as Berkshire faces a challenging market moment. Its shares are up only about 1% year to date in 2026, while the S&P 500 has rallied more than 11%, as investors have favored growth stocks and rising oil prices have weighed on some holdings. Berkshire's Class A shares closed at $763,935.95 on September 17.

The company is sitting on a $365.5 billion cash hoard, and Abel has already shown signs of putting it to work, stepping up buybacks to $4.5 billion in the second quarter of 2026. As [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/business/2026/sep/18/warren-buffett-steps-down-berkshire-hathaway) reported, shareholders are watching closely to see whether Abel can deploy that capital as adeptly as Buffett did.

In his letter, Buffett expressed confidence in his successor. "My expectations for him were sky high from the start, and he has exceeded them," Buffett wrote of Abel. "He has been making the decisions that matter for some time now, and I have not had to think twice about any of them."

Buffett's note also carried his characteristic wit and warmth. Reflecting on his 96th birthday, he wrote that he had celebrated with family, including a great-grandchild who had "just turned one." "He's moving a bit faster than I am these days," Buffett joked. He closed by telling shareholders: "The company is in excellent hands, and I look forward to remaining a shareholder alongside you."

## What's Next

The transition is designed to preserve Berkshire's famously decentralized operating model, in which subsidiary managers operate with unusual autonomy. Whether that culture — long personified by Buffett's annual letters and folksy shareholder meetings — can be sustained without him at the helm is the central question facing the company.

As [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/retail-consumer/berkshire-hathaway-names-warren-buffett-chairman-emeritus-2026-09-18/) reported, the company confirmed the appointments in a separate board announcement, noting that Buffett "will continue to offer his valued judgment and perspective." Buffett also remains a major shareholder and a director — a reminder that while his formal titles have changed, his influence may not disappear overnight.

Investors will be watching the next few quarters for signals: how Abel deploys the cash pile, how the newly separated chairman and CEO roles function in practice, and whether Berkshire's stock can close the performance gap with the broader market. For now, the man who spent more than half a century building the company has handed off the last of his formal leadership roles — on his own terms, and with a plan long in the making.
