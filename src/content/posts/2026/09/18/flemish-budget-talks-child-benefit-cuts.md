# Flemish Budget Talks Open Door to Child Benefit Cuts

Flanders' regional government began its autumn budget negotiations on Friday with a striking political signal: the two coalition parties that once made child benefit indexation a condition of their governing deal now say they have no red lines against savings on the Groeipakket, the Flemish family allowance system. The softening comes as the government hunts for an estimated EUR 1.5 billion to EUR 2 billion to balance its budget.

Hilde Crevits, the CD&V vice-minister-president, called it "very bizarre" that the N-VA-led government had suddenly placed child benefit first on the table, though she declined to set a veto. "As CD&V we are ready to work seriously on the budget, without making big declarations in advance," she told [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/liveblog/wil-vlaamse-regering-besparen-op-kinderbijslag-bij-vooruit-en-c~1780024580839/), adding that "setting vetoes is wrong in these times."

Caroline Gennez, the Vooruit minister for welfare and poverty reduction, echoed the willingness to negotiate. "We will not draw red lines," she said. At the same time she stressed the stakes: "A healthy budget also means investing in people, in children and young people, in families. And the Groeipakket is quite essential in that."

## A Budget Exercise of Unprecedented Scale

The opening of talks caps weeks of signals that this would be Flanders' most demanding budget round in the current legislature. Budget Minister Ben Weyts (N-VA) has described it as "the biggest budget exercise we've ever had to do," pointing to a figure near EUR 2 billion and warning that "the bigger the budget effort, the less room for vetoes, red lines and all kinds of demands." Earlier in September the government's core already removed one contentious item — the West-Flemish governor appointment — from the budget agenda in an effort to narrow the field.

The Groeipakket is not the only target. According to [Redactie24](https://www.redactie24.be/politiek/vlaamse-regering-wil-besparen-op-kinderbijslag-dit-kan-veranderen-voor-gezinnen-met-kinderen-193719), the government is also examining investment budgets and planned tax reductions, weighing competing policy choices to close the gap. Reports suggest the government is studying a technical reform of the Groeipakket's parameters rather than a cut to the base amount.

## What Could Change — and How

The Groeipakket consists of a monthly base amount — currently EUR 184.62 for children born from 1 January 2019 — plus supplementary allowances such as the social toeslag and zorgtoeslag, and a family-composition component. Rather than reducing the base payment, the government is reportedly looking at adjusting income thresholds and calculation rules. As Redactie24 notes, a change to income limits or calculation formulas can alter what families ultimately receive even when the headline rate is untouched.

The discussion plays out against a specific political backdrop. During coalition formation, Vooruit and CD&V insisted the Groeipakket remain linked to the health index. The Redactie24 report points to the 2024-2029 Flemish government declaration, which contains a commitment to index the Groeipakket to support families' purchasing power — making any technical change to supplements politically sensitive even if the base amount is left alone. Notably, indexation and a parameter reform are not the same thing: one adjusts amounts over time, the other changes the conditions under which a given allowance is granted.

That distinction has already drawn fire. Flanders has trimmed the Groeipakket repeatedly in recent years. As [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/08/04/1-1-miljoen-vlaamse-kinderen-krijgen-dit-jaar-geen-schoolbonus-m/) reported in August, the schoolbonus was abolished from 2026, affecting roughly 1.1 million Flemish children, while the kleutertoeslag (toddler supplement) is being fully phased out — a structural saving of about EUR 19.5 million a year. Lower-income families receiving the social toeslag retain the schoolbonus.

## Families and Opposition Push Back

Kathleen Beeusaert, director-general of the Gezinsbond, the country's largest family association, rejected the direction of travel outright. "After indexation we could finally speak of a Groeipakket again. Now it's back on the table to turn it into a shrink-package, while more than a billion euros has already been saved on it in recent years. That really cannot happen for us," she said. "That budget challenge was not caused by families."

Opposition parties seized on the proposal. PVDA MP Lise Vandecasteele said that if the government saves again on the Groeipakket it should be renamed a "krimppakket" — a shrink-package — and called on CD&V and Vooruit to resist and on Gennez to show a backbone. Vlaams Belang MP Immanuel De Reuse argued that formally preserving indexation while tinkering with the parameters "changes nothing to the result if families thereby get less," describing the shift of the bill onto families as "a political choice, not an economic law."

## Analysis: No Red Lines Is Not a Yes

The softened rhetoric is best read as negotiation posture rather than agreement. As governing parties, CD&V and Vooruit cannot simply oppose every measure: with a EUR 2 billion exercise on the table, they have an incentive to keep their objections in reserve rather than spend them early. For N-VA, placing child benefit first is a strategic test of appetite for a politically charged cut before positions harden.

The technical-reform route carries real distributional questions. The key uncertainty is which groups might lose out — particularly the vulnerable households that receive the social toeslag. The Gezinsbond frames the Groeipakket as an unconditional right anchored in the Belgian Constitution and warns that hollowing it out risks discouraging people from raising children. A proposed saving on child benefit triggered a government crisis for CD&V in the previous legislature, so a repeat would carry genuine political risk.

None of this is settled. No final decision has been made on the Groeipakket, and only an option is under study. The broader fiscal context adds pressure: Belgium's federal government is simultaneously seeking roughly EUR 10 billion in savings, with an October deadline. As [Knack](https://www.knack.be/nieuws/belgie/politiek/vlaamse-regering-heeft-akkoord-over-begroting-2026-2/) reported last year, closing the Flemish budget has repeatedly required politically painful trade-offs, from cuts to household subsidies to higher healthcare premiums.

## What's Next

The intensive phase of negotiations begins this weekend, with a budget agreement expected by the end of September. The Septemberverklaring — the government's traditional autumn policy statement — will be the key moment for clarity. Until then, families will be watching for three things: whether the Groeipakket parameters are actually changed, whether the most vulnerable recipients are shielded, and whether the total savings effort lands closer to EUR 1.5 billion or EUR 2 billion. Whatever the outcome, this week's opening statements have made one thing clear — nothing, not even the family allowance the coalition once protected, is off the table.
