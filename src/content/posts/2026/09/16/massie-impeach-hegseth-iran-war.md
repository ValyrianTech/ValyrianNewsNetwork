# Massie Moves to Impeach Hegseth Over Iran War

Republican Rep. Thomas Massie of Kentucky moved Tuesday to impeach Secretary of Defense Pete Hegseth, filing a privileged resolution with eight articles of impeachment that accuse the Pentagon chief of illegally waging war against Iran and abusing his office. The surprise move, announced from the House floor, forces the Republican-controlled chamber to act within two legislative days before lawmakers leave Washington for an extended recess that ends after the November midterms.

## The Core Charge

Massie, a member of the House Judiciary Committee and a longtime critic of the administration, read the articles aloud on Tuesday afternoon. The central allegation is that Hegseth "engaged in hostilities in Iran for more than 90 days without congressional authorization," violating the War Powers Resolution of 1973.

"By engaging in hostilities in Iran for more than 90 days without congressional authorization, Secretary Hegseth is breaking the law and must be held accountable," Massie said in a [statement](https://massie.house.gov/news/documentsingle.aspx?DocumentID=395853). "Secretary Hegseth's constitutional violations extend beyond the illegal war in Iran. He is abusing the power of his office to ignore congressional war powers resolutions, to kidnap foreign leaders, and to intimidate critics of the Trump administration by retaliating against them for exercising free speech."

The resolution's first three articles focus squarely on the Iran conflict, citing three separate provisions of the War Powers Resolution: Section 2(c), which limits the president's authority to introduce forces into hostilities; Section 5(b), which sets a 60-day withdrawal clock; and Section 5(c), which allows Congress to direct the removal of forces through a concurrent resolution. According to [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/09/15/g-s1-143407/massie-impeach-hegseth-iran), Massie alleges Hegseth initiated the conflict unlawfully, defied Congress's order to withdraw, and incurred unnecessary civilian casualties.

## Beyond Iran: The Other Articles

The remaining five articles broaden the case considerably. They accuse Hegseth of ignoring laws meant to minimize civilian casualties; of replacing maritime law enforcement with "unauthorized lethal military targeting of suspected drug traffickers, resulting in at least 221 deaths"; of suppressing free speech by retaliating against Sen. Mark Kelly; of directing an operation to seize Venezuelan President Nicolás Maduro and Cilia Flores without legal authority; and of initiating an unlawful military operation in Yemen.

The full text of the charges is available in the [resolution Massie filed](https://massie.house.gov/UploadedFiles/Articles_of_Impeachment_Sec._Hegseth.pdf).

## A Political Headache for Both Parties

The effort is widely expected to fail. Republicans hold the House majority and could move to table the measure, which requires only a simple majority. Yet the privileged status of the resolution guarantees a politically difficult on-the-record vote for members in competitive districts, given the war's deep unpopularity.

In a sign of the broader political stakes, the House voted hours earlier to table a separate Democratic impeachment resolution against President Trump. As reported by [Fox 13 / Scripps News](https://www.fox13now.com/us-news/thomas-massie-pushes-surprise-impeachment-vote-of-pete-hegseth), that vote created headaches for Democratic leaders, only for Massie to hand Republicans a similar problem. "Just when you think Al Green screws us, Massie turns around and gives it right back to the Rs," one Democratic aide quipped.

Massie told reporters he deliberately kept his plans secret from party leaders. "I didn't give anybody heads-up," he said. "When they found out I was doing the Epstein discharge petition, they canceled two weeks of Congress, and I think if they found out I was going to do this, they might have canceled even more days."

According to [The New York Post](https://nypost.com/2026/09/15/us-news/rep-thomas-massie-moves-to-impeach-pete-hegseth-over-iran-war-will-force-house-vote-on-resolution/), the lame-duck congressman lost his primary in May to a Trump-backed opponent and is reigniting the war powers debate just weeks before the midterm elections.

## A Conflict Entering Its Seventh Month

The war with Iran began on Feb. 28, 2026, when the United States and Israel launched attacks on Iran. By mid-year, reported costs had exceeded $37 billion. The conflict has disrupted oil shipments and pushed fuel prices toward record highs, contributing to the war's unpopularity — a factor complicating the vote for Republicans in swing districts.

The administration maintains that the conflict was necessary to prevent Iran from obtaining a nuclear weapon, though experts say the country was not close to developing one when the fighting began. The White House has also argued the conflict ended after an initial phase, resetting the War Powers clock.

On the same day Massie introduced his resolution, the House rejected a separate war powers measure. According to the [House Clerk's record](https://clerk.house.gov/evs/2026/roll307.xml), H. Con. Res. 93 — which would have directed the president to remove U.S. forces from hostilities with Iran — failed 220–204, with only seven Republicans voting in favor.

## Is the Effort Unlikely to Succeed?

The practical path to removal is narrow. Even if the House approved articles of impeachment, the matter would move to the Senate, where conviction and removal require a two-thirds vote of senators present — a threshold that appears far out of reach.

Historically, courts have declined to order presidents to end conflicts that exceed the War Powers Resolution's timeline, instead holding that Congress can enforce the cutoff itself through its spending power or by disciplining executive branch officials.

The administration has dismissed the allegations. Pentagon press secretary Kingsley Wilson said Hegseth "has been a transformative leader for the Department of War" and that the department is "unified behind the Secretary's vision." Attorney General Todd Blanche told reporters that Hegseth "follows the law" and "is not doing anything wrong." President Trump has repeatedly praised Hegseth, saying he is doing a "fantastic job."

## What's Next

The House must act on Massie's resolution within two legislative days, with a vote possible this week. As [USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/2026/09/16/will-pete-hegseth-be-impeached-house-vote-explained-what-are-massie-articles-of-impeachment/91788975007/) noted, the resolution guarantees fresh scrutiny of Hegseth's role in the administration's military actions even if it is ultimately defeated.

[Stars and Stripes](https://www.stripes.com/theaters/us/2026-09-15/republican-massie-moves-to-impeach-hegseth-22864402.html) reported that the 34-page resolution argues Hegseth "abused the powers entrusted to him as Secretary of Defense by subordinating the constitutional limits on the use of military force and the laws governing the conduct of hostilities to his own policy objectives."

The episode marks the first time a member of Congress has sought to impeach a Cabinet official of the same party, and it underscores the deepening intraparty tensions over executive war powers. Whether the House votes to table the measure or takes it up directly, the war in Iran — and the constitutional questions it raises — will remain central to the political debate heading into November.
