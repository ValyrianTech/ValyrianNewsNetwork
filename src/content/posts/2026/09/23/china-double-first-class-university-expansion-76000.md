# China to Add 76,000 Seats at Top Universities

China will expand undergraduate enrollment at its most prestigious universities by an additional 76,000 seats between 2027 and 2030, the Ministry of Education announced on September 23, extending a multi-year push to widen access to elite higher education and cultivate top-tier talent.

Guo Peng, director of the ministry's Development Planning Department, said at a State Council Information Office press conference that the new places at "Double First-Class" institutions would build on earlier expansion rounds. Xinhua News [reported](https://education.news.cn/20260923/ba144bfb3d6640969f6f6a2ccd104506/c.html) that the additional seats are part of implementing the education measures of China's 15th Five-Year Plan.

## Context

The expansion is the latest phase of a policy direction set at the December 2024 Central Economic Work Conference, which called for "solidly advancing the expansion of high-quality undergraduate education." At the 2025 National People's Congress, Zheng Shanjie, director of the National Development and Reform Commission, said 16,000 undergraduate seats were added in 2024, with another 20,000 targeted for 2025.

Those earlier rounds form part of the broader goal set out in the Education Development 15th Five-Year Plan, issued by the State Council in mid-2026. As the government's policy interpretation [explained](https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/202607/content_7074182.htm), the plan calls for increasing undergraduate enrollment at Double First-Class universities by more than 100,000 students over the plan period, supporting new campuses, and building roughly 200 high-level applied undergraduate universities.

The 76,000 new places announced this week represent the 2027–2030 portion of that larger commitment.

## Why the elite tier matters

China's higher education system has expanded dramatically over the past two decades, with gross enrollment now exceeding 60 percent. But seats at the 147 Double First-Class institutions remain far scarcer. According to [China Youth Daily](https://news.cyol.com/gb/articles/2026-01/31/content_bmRG5qULjv.html), students at those universities make up only about 10 percent of the country's higher-education population.

That gap has made quality seats a persistent source of anxiety for families — and a central concern for policymakers.

## A demographic deadline

The expansion is unfolding against a demographic clock. Education Minister Huai Jinpeng has said primary school enrollment peaked in 2023, junior high is expected to peak around 2026, senior high around 2029, and the higher-education school-age population in 2032.

"Providing a certain number of seats depends, to a large extent, on the total school-age population — but that is not the only factor," Li Jianmin, an associate researcher at the National Institute of Education Sciences, told China Youth Daily, noting that high school education is still climbing toward universal access.

Analysts frame the policy as a shift from what officials call a "population dividend" to a "talent dividend" — investing in people as the labor force shrinks. Zhao Wen, a researcher at the Chinese Academy of Social Sciences, described the expansion as a "key move" in the strategy of "investing in people," intended to convert demographic pressure into human-capital advantage.

## Quality, not just quantity

The expansion is not designed to be uniform. The 15th Five-Year Plan directs new capacity toward national strategic and emerging fields, and officials have said new campuses will support the growth. The plan also calls for expanding the Double First-Class designation itself, adding institutions and disciplines in what it calls "strategic must-win areas."

Experts caution that numbers alone are not the goal. "The expansion of quality undergraduate education will change the direction of education quality, not just the quantity," Ding Yan, an associate researcher at Fudan University's Higher Education Research Institute, told China Youth Daily. She said faculty development and the disciplines themselves would need to evolve, particularly as artificial intelligence reshapes the workplace.

Ren Zengyuan, deputy director of Jilin University's Higher Education Research Institute, argued that higher education should not chase short-term industry trends but instead build students' fundamental capabilities. Tsinghua University professor Li Liguo has proposed a longer-term benchmark: by 2035, Double First-Class students should account for 20 percent of all higher-education students and 20 percent of all undergraduate admissions, with their enrollment doubling from 2023 levels.

## What's next

The expansion now moves into its largest phase. China Youth Daily reported that multiple 985 universities already enrolled more than 20,000 freshmen this year, and the 2027–2030 tranche will add further pressure on campuses, faculty, and infrastructure — considerations the plan addresses through new campus construction and teacher-pipeline measures.

Whether the coming seats translate into genuine opportunity will depend on execution: whether resources reach the universities, fields, and regions that need them, and whether quality keeps pace with scale. For now, the direction is set — and the numbers keep growing.
