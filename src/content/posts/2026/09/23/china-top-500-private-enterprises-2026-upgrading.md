# China's Top 500 Private Firms Turn to Advanced Industries

China's largest private companies are pivoting decisively toward advanced and high-value industries, according to the 2026 ranking of the country's Top 500 Private Enterprises, released on 22 September by the All-China Federation of Industry and Commerce (ACFIC). The annual survey, now in its 28th edition, portrayed a private sector deepening its role in strategic emerging and future industries even as it navigates a challenging economic environment.

The ranking was unveiled in Tianjin alongside the 2026 China Private Enterprise Investment and Financing Fair, with Xinhua [reporting](https://www.news.cn/fortune/20260922/efb3d28531614cd7abae573ea1e7ffa4/c.html) that JD Group, Alibaba (China) Co., Ltd., and Hengli Group took the top three spots.

## Scale and Profitability

The 500 companies together posted total revenue of 44.93 trillion yuan (about $6.3 trillion) in 2025, an average of 898.51 billion yuan per firm and a rise of 4.35% year on year, according to the ACFIC's official [survey and analysis report](https://www.acfic.org.cn/ztzlhz/500q2026/hyfb_500q2026/202609/t20260922_331485.html). Total assets reached 53.74 trillion yuan, up 5.05%, while combined net profit rose 1.31% to 1.83 trillion yuan.

The report counted 110 firms with revenue above 100 billion yuan, five more than the previous year, and 12 with revenue exceeding 500 billion yuan. Twenty-nine of the Top 500 also earned places on the 2026 Fortune Global 500 list. Average net margin stood at 4.06%, and average return on net assets at 9.86%.

## The Structural Upgrading Story

The central finding was what the ACFIC calls structural upgrading. Secondary-industry firms made up 74.40% of the list, and manufacturing companies 70.60%. Manufacturing firms alone generated 32.22 trillion yuan in revenue, up 8.73% and accounting for 71.72% of the total—outpacing overall growth and underscoring a shift toward higher-value industrial activity.

Manufacturing profit and investment climbed even faster: net profit rose 13.43% to 1.09 trillion yuan, assets grew 11.13%, and research and development spending increased 11.20% to 0.89 trillion yuan.

Xinhua's feature [analysis](https://www.news.cn/fortune/20260922/beed5b78d5f548708b5eeee9d8301ead/c.html) highlighted the push into new frontiers. Of the companies that reported detailed data, 311 had invested in 679 strategic emerging industry projects spanning new materials, new energy, next-generation information technology and high-end equipment manufacturing. A further 82 firms had deployed 112 future-industry projects, including embodied artificial intelligence, hydrogen and nuclear fusion energy, and bio-manufacturing.

"We are driving the traditional steel industry to iterate toward green, intelligent, and diversified operations, and actively deploying in strategic emerging and future industries," said Zhang Ronghua, chairwoman of Tianjin Rockcheck Xiangtai Investment Holding Group, a former steelmaker that has grown into a conglomerate spanning steel, hydrogen energy and digital technology. The company ranked 72nd on the list.

## Innovation, Digitalization and Green Transition

Innovation emerged as a defining characteristic of the group. The reporting companies spent a combined 1.26 trillion yuan on R&D and employed 1.3449 million R&D staff, with an average R&D intensity of 2.95%. They held 848,700 valid patents, including 735,500 domestic ones, and led or co-drafted 11,904 national standards and 8,004 industry standards.

Digital and green transformation were broadly embedded in corporate strategy. The ACFIC report found that 60.60% of the companies had formulated strategies for digital transformation and AI applications, 53.20% were pursuing intelligent manufacturing, and 50.00% were developing digital products and services. On sustainability, 83.40% reported advancing a green, low-carbon transition through clean technologies, carbon accounting and green-intelligent retrofits.

## Global Reach and Social Contribution

Internationalization continued to expand. Export revenue among the reporting firms reached 1.97 trillion yuan, up 11.25% and accounting for 7.31% of China's total exports—a gain of 0.34 percentage points. Overseas revenue rose 11.23% to 3.70 trillion yuan, while overseas assets climbed 17.61% to 2.86 trillion yuan. A total of 209 firms reported overseas investment.

The group's social footprint remained substantial. The Top 500 paid 1.30 trillion yuan in taxes, with 254 firms paying more than 1 billion yuan each. They employed nearly 12 million people, or about 23,900 per company, with computer, communications and electronics manufacturing accounting for the largest share. Roughly two-thirds participated in rural revitalization efforts, and 69.40% engaged in charitable giving totaling 9.939 billion yuan.

## Why It Matters

The ranking lands in a pivotal year for China's private economy. 2026 marks the opening of the 15th Five-Year Plan, whose outline devotes a standalone section to building a modern industrial system and names the cultivation of emerging and future industries as a key measure. China's first basic law devoted specifically to private economic development, the Private Economy Promotion Law, also took effect this year, a shift the ACFIC report explicitly credited with creating a better environment for private-sector growth.

Lin Zeyan, ACFIC secretary-general and director of its economic services department, framed the upgrading as strategically important. "The structural upgrading of the Top 500 private enterprises is conducive to cultivating new economic growth points and strengthening the resilience and global competitiveness of industrial and supply chains," he said.

The ACFIC report acknowledged that 2025 unfolded against "increased external pressure and more internal difficulties"—an apparent reference to trade tensions and domestic demand headwinds. Against that backdrop, the emphasis on R&D investment, advanced manufacturing and future industries positions the private sector as a stabilizing, counter-cyclical force in Beijing's broader push to develop "new quality productive forces."

## What to Watch

The headline numbers show a private sector that is growing steadily and becoming more capital- and knowledge-intensive. Whether that structural momentum translates into sustained profitability—average net margin remains modest at just over 4%—will depend on how these firms convert R&D and advanced-industry bets into earnings amid uneven domestic demand and external trade risks. The coming year's report, and the trajectory of strategic emerging industry investment in particular, will be the clearest signal of whether the upgrading narrative holds.
