# Liquid Cocaine Found Hidden in Soap at Antwerp Port

Belgian police and customs officers intercepted a shipping container at the port of Antwerp carrying cocaine that had been liquefied and concealed inside bottles of liquid soap, the Antwerp prosecutor's office announced on 20 September 2026. The shipment, which arrived from Colombia and was destined by road for Croatia, was stopped on 24 August at quay 730 — a find officials described as a "unique catch."

According to the [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/09/20/unieke-vangst-in-haven-van-antwerpen-vloeibaar-gemaakte-cocai/) report, the cocaine was discovered after the Federal Judicial Police in Antwerp gathered intelligence through international cooperation about a possibly suspicious container. Further investigation located the shipment, allowing customs to seize it for a thorough inspection.

## A Distinctive Chemical Odor

When officers checked several bottles from the shipment of liquid soap, a very strong chemical odor immediately spread, according to the official [press release](https://www.om-mp.be/nl/artikel/persbericht-antwerpen-cocaine-aangetroffen-vloeibare-zeep) from the Antwerp prosecutor's office. Initial analyses immediately indicated the presence of cocaine in liquid form.

The contaminated liquid was reportedly barely distinguishable from the genuine liquid soap, though it crystallizes at lower temperatures. The total cargo consisted of 450 boxes of 12 kilograms of soap each — roughly 5,400 kilograms of soap in total. The exact quantity of cocaine concealed inside the shipment remains under investigation.

Authorities also noted that the container numbers appear to have been tampered with, a sign of deliberate efforts to evade detection.

## A Growing Challenge for European Ports

The seizure underscores the persistent challenge facing Antwerp, one of the largest cocaine entry points into Europe. In January 2026, VRT reported that Belgian customs seized nearly [55 tons of cocaine](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/01/21/douane-onderschepte-vorig-jaar-55-ton-cocaine-in-antwerpse-haven/) in the port in 2025 — up from 44 tons in 2024 but still less than half of the record 120 tons recorded in 2023.

Liquid cocaine presents particular difficulties for detection because its characteristics differ significantly from powdered cocaine, making it harder for standard security methods to identify. The method is part of a broader trend of concealing drugs within legitimate commercial products. In February 2026, a Belgian court handed down sentences of up to [15 years](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/02/25/celstraffen-tot-15-jaar-voor-bende-die-cocaine-smokkelde-in-blik/) to 23 defendants who smuggled cocaine by impregnating it into cement, roofing, and glue, or hiding it in tins of tuna and fruit.

As [NOS](https://nos.nl/artikel/2631750-zeepflessen-met-vloeibare-cocaine-onderschept-in-antwerpen) noted, the authorities published the finding only now, roughly a month after the inspection, suggesting an ongoing investigation. No suspects or arrests have been publicly named.

## Broader Implications

The find comes amid sustained Belgian efforts to disrupt drug trafficking through its ports. The federal police have indicated that criminal networks are increasingly adapting their routes and methods, sometimes avoiding Antwerp in favor of smaller ports, even as stricter controls appear to yield results. In 2025, police conducted a record 207 drug-related arrests, the majority of them so-called "extractors" who retrieve drugs from containers — including 41 minors.

Experts have warned that traffickers are also adopting new technologies, including artificial intelligence, to evade detection. Officials have proposed measures such as a dedicated port prosecutor to strengthen the legal response, while drug commissioner Ine Van Wymersch has advocated prevention and alternatives for young people drawn into the trade.

The Antwerp prosecutor's office has opened an investigation into the soap shipment. The exact quantity of cocaine, the origin of the intelligence, and any connection to wider smuggling networks remain under review.

## What to Watch

As Belgian authorities continue their investigation, several questions remain unanswered: how much cocaine the shipment contained, whether the container numbers' tampering can be traced to a specific network, and whether the Colombia-to-Croatia route signals a broader shift in trafficking patterns. Each such seizure adds to an accumulating picture of a trade that is fluid, inventive, and deeply embedded in the economics of European ports.
