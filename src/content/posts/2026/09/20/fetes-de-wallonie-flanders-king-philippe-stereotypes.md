# Fetes de Wallonie: Flanders Attends in Peace as King Philippe Presides Over Call to Move Beyond Stereotypes

Flanders attended the Fetes de Wallonie in Namur as guest of honour for the first time in the festival's 103-year history on Saturday, an unprecedented symbolic gesture that coincided with King Philippe's first visit to the event since acceding to the throne. At the official ceremony in the Theatre Royal de Namur, the leaders of Belgium's two largest regions traded humour and pointed barbs while urging their publics to look beyond long-entrenched stereotypes.

The Flemish delegation was led by Minister-President Matthias Diependaele of the nationalist N-VA, who opened with a line that set the tone for the afternoon: "Fear not, I come in peace." According to [RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/fetes-de-wallonie-la-flandre-venue-en-paix-invitee-a-depasser-les-cliches-en-presence-du-roi-philippe-11787648), the ceremony was held against the backdrop of a tense federal budget conclave and the aftermath of the August wildfires that ravaged the Hautes Fagnes.

## A First in More Than a Century

The invitation of Flanders as guest of honour marked a striking departure for an event that celebrates Walloon identity through folklore, music and heritage. The 2025 edition had honoured Vietnam. Walloon Minister-President Adrien Dolimont (MR) acknowledged the significance with wry understatement, noting that "nobody asked us why" Vietnam had been chosen the year before.

The last royal visit to the "Wallos" dated back to 2005, when King Albert II and Queen Paola came to Namur to mark 25 years of Belgian federalism. King Philippe was initially scheduled to leave after the first speech, but stayed for all three — an unplanned extension widely read as a gesture of interest. He arrived around 15:15 at the Confluence, welcomed by Dolimont and joined by Deputy Prime Minister Maxime Prevot (Les Engages), Deputy Prime Minister David Clarinval (MR) and Walloon Parliament President Willy Borsus (MR), as [RTL Info](https://www.rtl.be/actu/belgique/famille-royale/une-visite-inattendue-namur-le-roi-philippe-participe-pour-la-premiere-fois-aux/2026-09-19/article/800733) reported.

Before the speeches, the King met the public, visited stalls and watched performances by the Alfers namurois, the Masuis et Cotelis jambois, the Molons de la Societe royale Moncrabeau and a Flemish folk group, before briefly attending the joust for the Echasse de Diamant.

## A Nationalist Who Came in Peace

Diependaele made no attempt to soften his political identity. "I am a deeply convinced Flemish nationalist. It remains my political compass. But fear not: I come in peace," he said, describing his presence as a challenge — to come and "throw myself into the wolf's den," or the lion's den, as the Flemish expression has it. "I am here to convey a message of friendship and sympathy from the north of the country."

He praised Walloon conviviality, saying many Flemings are "secretly jealous" of it, and cited the Walloon anthem alongside Johnny Hallyday and a Claude Francois gag. But he also delivered a pointed, ironic remark: the Wallonia he sees "must have confidence in itself, a confidence that will perhaps one day lead it to declare its independence." He concluded that "a strong Flanders needs a strong Wallonia."

Dolimont answered with humour and firmness, quoting Diependaele's own 2025 remark that a "Belgium a la flamande" might suit Wallonia — a line the Flemish leader had first floated on the Flemish Community holiday, as [RTBF reported at the time](https://www.rtbf.be/article/une-belgique-a-la-flamande-est-bien-pour-la-wallonie-declare-matthias-diependaele-le-jour-de-la-fete-de-la-communaute-flamande-11574805). "I know carbonnades a la flamande; a Belgium a la flamande, I admit, is less familiar to me," Dolimont joked.

More seriously, he argued for mutual inspiration over imitation: "If a reform works in Flanders, let us see what we can learn from it. If a reform works in Wallonia — I have our long list at your disposal, dear Matthias — I hope it can inspire you." He drew on cycling metaphors to praise those who "dare," and accepted in principle an invitation to celebrate 11 July, the Flemish Community holiday.

Walloon Parliament President Willy Borsus called for "fewer structures, fewer elected officials" and for rationalising institutions that are "abundant, over-abundant or obsolete," stressing that recovery requires deep cooperation with the federal level, Flanders, Brussels and European partners.

## Budget and Climate Loom Over the Festivities

The ceremony unfolded just ahead of a difficult federal budget conclave, where the government is racing to avoid a "snowball effect" of debt, as [RTBF detailed](https://www.rtbf.be/article/budget-federal-le-gouvernement-au-pied-du-mur-pour-eviter-la-catastrophe-de-l-effet-boule-de-neige-11778462). Dolimont used the stage to defend fiscal discipline: "There are several ways to reduce a deficit: spend less, some will say tax more, or hope very, very hard. Secrecy is easy. But governing a region requires holding firm, and therefore returning to serious public accounts."

He also invoked the August wildfire that devastated the Hautes Fagnes — a disaster still not fully extinguished at the time, as [RTBF reported](https://www.rtbf.be/article/l-incendie-dans-les-fagnes-est-desormais-maitrise-mais-pas-encore-eteint-11784953) days earlier. "Our mandates last five years. The climate does not vote. We must judge a proposal for what it is worth, not for the camp it comes from."

## Opposition: 'Disconnected' and 'Indelicate'

The speeches drew sharp criticism from the opposition. PS group leader Christie Morreale called them "totally disconnected" from people's concerns, telling [La DH](https://www.dhnet.be/dernieres-depeches/2026/09/19/fetes-de-wallonie-un-discours-deconnecte-des-inquietudes-de-la-population-regrette-lopposition-P3EA5Z764ZF6RO2UUARSUC4OOI/) that "not a word was heard about all the citizens struggling to make ends meet." She called the invitation of a self-declared Flemish nationalist "truly indelicate" and criticised the omission of any mention of the Brussels Region.

PTB group leader Germain Mugemangango accused the government of "a certain hypocrisy" for applauding firefighters and public servants while having cut funding to rescue zones and the Department of Nature and Forests, and pointed to a planned 900 million euros in additional savings — three times the effort borne by citizens so far.

## What to Watch For

The Fetes de Wallonie offered a rare, mostly good-natured snapshot of Belgian federalism at a moment of genuine strain. The warmth on stage did not erase the underlying questions: how deep regional reform should go, who pays for fiscal consolidation, and whether symbolic gestures can outlast political cycles.

King Philippe's decision to remain for the full slate of speeches will be read by many as a quiet affirmation of the monarchy's unifying role in a divided country — a role that will be tested again as the federal budget deadline approaches in mid-October. As [L'Avenir](https://www.lavenir.net/actu/belgique/politique/2026/09/19/fetes-de-wallonie-quand-un-nationaliste-flamand-se-jette-dans-la-gueule-du-loup-MYRC5N37YBFLTABX2V4KUUI2EY/) put it, making people laugh during political speeches is a feat; whether the goodwill translates into cooperation is another matter entirely.
