# Trump: D.C. Arch Will Be Military Complex With Snipers

President Donald Trump said Sunday that his proposed 250-foot triumphal arch in Washington, D.C., will double as a "top grade Military Complex" outfitted with snipers, drones and large quantities of ammunition — a striking expansion of a monument originally pitched as a celebration of the nation's 250th anniversary.

In a post on Truth Social, Trump said he agreed to the change "at the strong request of the United States Military, and for National Security purposes." He did not identify any specific threats, and neither the White House nor the Pentagon immediately responded to requests for comment, according to [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/09/20/nx-s1-5975449/trump-washington-dc-arch-military-complex-snipers-drones).

## A Monument Repurposed

Trump wrote that the arch will "house, store, and have the rapid ability to use large numbers of drones, plus Snipers, on both the roof and plaza areas, and additionally have and hold large quantities of sniper ammunition in storage." He added: "There will be no facility like this anywhere in the World."

The arch is planned to stand at Memorial Circle in Arlington, Virginia, on the traffic island linking the Lincoln Memorial and Arlington National Cemetery. At 76 meters (250 feet), it would rise more than twice the height of the Lincoln Memorial, exceeding the district's usual height restrictions for new construction.

Trump first proposed the arch last year to mark 250 years of U.S. independence, comparing it to the Arc de Triomphe in Paris but vowing it would be "the biggest one of all." [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/20/trump-says-monumental-arch-in-washington-dc-will-also-be-military-complex) noted that by law, building height in the city is generally restricted, and Trump provided no details Sunday on how the new military design would alter the structure.

In April 2026, the Trump-appointed U.S. Commission of Fine Arts [approved a design](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/4/16/us-panel-approves-trumps-design-for-massive-arch-in-washington-dc) featuring golden lions at the base, golden eagles, and a winged angel atop a baroque arch resembling the Paris landmark. The commission approved the design despite internal objections and thousands of overwhelmingly negative public comments — roughly three in four commenters opposed the project, many citing its size.

## Legal and Aviation Hurdles

The arch has faced sustained opposition on legal, aesthetic and preservation grounds. Three Vietnam War veterans and retired architectural historian Calder Loth sued in February 2026, arguing the arch would desecrate the sightline between the Lincoln Memorial and Arlington National Cemetery and that Congress must approve any major monument on federal parkland.

Earlier this month, U.S. District Court Judge Tanya Chutkan ordered the government to give 48 hours' notice before any activity on Memorial Circle, warning that construction or demolition before the National Park Service issues final authorization would violate the court, [NPR reported](https://www.npr.org/2026/09/04/nx-s1-5957459/trump-arch-excavation-lawsuit-veterans). The order followed Interior Secretary Doug Burgum's statement on X that the administration planned to break ground within two weeks, even as final approval from the National Capital Planning Commission and other federal reviews remained pending.

Separately, the Federal Aviation Administration said last week that the arch would not pose a hazard to aircraft near Reagan Washington National Airport, clearing a key aviation hurdle, [Reuters reported via Military Times](https://www.militarytimes.com/news/pentagon-congress/2026/09/20/triumphal-arch-in-washington-to-serve-as-military-complex-trump-says/). The agency said the structure would nevertheless require a warning beacon or "eternal flame" on top.

More than 100,000 people submitted public comments to the National Park Service as part of its review of the proposal.

## Echoes of the White House Ballroom

The national security rationale mirrors the justification Trump has offered for the roughly $400 million ballroom complex under construction at the White House, where the historic East Wing was demolished to make way for the project. In August 2026, the U.S. Supreme Court [allowed that construction to continue](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/31/white-house-construction-on-400m-ballroom-can-go-on-says-us-supreme-court) while litigation proceeds, with the majority finding a preservation group likely lacked standing. Chief Justice John Roberts dissented, calling the project "likely unlawful" without congressional approval.

The administration has similarly referred to the ballroom as a "military complex" and cited national security in pressing for its completion — a framing that now extends to the arch. The two projects, alongside Trump's threats to demolish or rename the Kennedy Center, form part of a broader effort to reshape the capital's physical landscape, which has drawn pushback from preservationists and veterans' groups alike.

## What to Watch

Trump's announcement significantly broadens the stated purpose of a monument that had already weathered lawsuits and a contested approval process. Critics have argued the military framing may be intended to strengthen the administration's legal position by invoking national security — an argument that resonated with the Supreme Court in the ballroom case.

For now, the arch's fate rests with a tangle of pending reviews, a federal lawsuit and the courts. The National Capital Planning Commission does not meet again until early October, and the veterans' suit remains unresolved. Whether the arch becomes a military installation, a celebration of the nation's semiquincentennial, or is halted altogether, the coming weeks are likely to bring the dispute to a head.
