# China Fiscal Revenue Slows, Spending Shrinks in August

China's fiscal revenue growth slowed sharply in August while government spending unexpectedly contracted, according to data released Sept. 18 by the Ministry of Finance, underscoring the mounting strain on Beijing's public finances as soft domestic demand and a prolonged property downturn erode revenue sources.

General public budget revenue totaled about 1.29 trillion yuan ($194 billion) in August, up 4.7% year-on-year — a slowdown of 7 percentage points from July, [Caixin Global reported](https://www.caixinglobal.com/2026-09-19/china-fiscal-revenue-growth-slows-as-government-spending-contracts-102486362.html). Meanwhile, government spending fell 0.1% year-on-year, reversing July's increase.

## Revenue Pressure Builds

Tax receipts bore the brunt of the slowdown. August tax revenue growth tumbled by 8.2 percentage points to 5.6%, while non-tax revenue swung from a decline to a marginal 0.3% increase. The softer tax take reflects a cooling economy: August retail sales barely grew at 0.4%, a slump in auto sales deepened, and the unemployment rate for the 16–24 age group climbed to 18.9%.

For the first eight months of 2026, total general public budget revenue reached 15.66 trillion yuan, up 5.7% year-on-year. Tax revenue rose 6.6% to 12.91 trillion yuan, while non-tax revenue edged up 1.5% to 2.75 trillion yuan. Central government revenue grew 9.1%, outpacing the 3.1% growth recorded by local governments.

"With no obvious adjustment to major tax rates, the recovery in tax revenue this year mainly reflects improvement in nominal economic activity and an expanding VAT base," Gao Hongyu, an assistant professor at the Central University of Finance and Economics, [told Securities Times](https://m.21jingji.com/article/20260919/herald/dffafd0f1cc9789ef608bbdfbfe804ff.html).

The composition of tax revenue has shifted notably. Personal income tax jumped 14.5% in the January–August period, and securities transaction stamp duty surged 82%, driven by active stock market trading. Corporate income tax rose 7.3% and domestic value-added tax 5.9%. Those three major taxes accounted for about 74% of total national tax revenue, according to the Securities Times report.

Yet property-linked levies continued to drag. Deed tax fell 14.2% and land value-added tax declined 13.6% over the eight-month period, reflecting persistent weakness in the housing market.

## Spending Pullback

August's spending contraction deepened concerns about the government's capacity to support growth. First-eight-month general public budget spending rose just 1.2% year-on-year to 18.15 trillion yuan — well below the full-year budget target of 4.4%. The cumulative spending progress reached only 60.5% of the annual plan, the second-lowest rate in five years.

The divergence between central and local spending was stark. Central government expenditure rose 6.0% over the eight months, while local spending grew just 0.3%. In August alone, local spending fell 1.1%, and central government's increase of 15.1 billion yuan did not fully offset local cuts of 17.6 billion yuan.

Spending priorities reflected the government's "investment in people" push. Health expenditure rose 9.2% — the fastest of any category — boosted by child-care subsidies, while social security and employment grew 6.5% and debt interest payments climbed 5.4%. By contrast, infrastructure-related categories contracted: agriculture, forestry and water fell 6%, and energy conservation and environment dropped 12.3%.

## Land Sales Add to the Strain

The property downturn continued to weigh heavily through land-sale revenue, a key funding source for local governments. Land-sale revenue fell 12.6% in August, narrowing from a 27.1% drop in July, but remained deeply negative. For the first eight months, government fund revenue fell 19% to 2.14 trillion yuan, dragged down by a 28.6% decline in state-owned land-use-right sale revenue, which totaled 1.375 trillion yuan.

"Weak local spending may be constrained by land-sale revenue and local debt resolution," Wu Qiying, a macro analyst at GF Securities, noted in comments carried by Securities Times. Land-sale revenue has now declined for four consecutive years.

Local government fund budget revenue fell 22.9% over the eight months, while government fund spending dropped 17% — a reflection of both constrained resources and stricter financial discipline.

## Policy Response and Outlook

Analysts at Shenwan Hongyuan struck a more forward-looking tone, arguing that multiple policy levers are now converging. In a [research note](https://news.qq.com/rain/a/20260920A0011600), the team led by chief economist Zhao Wei said August's fiscal adjustment was "a phased adjustment" rather than a reversal of direction, pointing to the delayed effects of accelerated special-purpose bond issuance since June.

The firm highlighted four policy pillars: the lagged impact of faster special bond issuance, financial-fiscal coordination to boost domestic demand through interest subsidies, the rollout of 800 billion yuan in new policy-based financial instruments, and the government's stated readiness to introduce "pragmatic and effective" incremental measures in the second half of the year.

Auguring for recovery, new special-purpose bond issuance rose to 518.8 billion yuan in August, the second-highest level of the year, and the first batch of funds from the 800 billion yuan policy-based instruments was disbursed in early September. Analysts expect physical construction activity to recover in the fourth quarter as financing conditions improve.

The starkest challenge remains local government finances. With land-sale revenue collapsing and implicit-debt resolution imposing rigid constraints, local authorities have limited room to expand spending. In September, the Central Commission for Discipline Inspection and the Ministry of Finance jointly published six cases of accountability for hidden-debt violations, signaling continued strict oversight.

## What to Watch

For the remainder of 2026, the key question is whether Beijing's central fiscal firepower and policy-based financing tools can offset local-level weakness. Year-to-date revenue growth of 5.7% already exceeds the full-year budget target of 2.2%, but spending growth of 1.2% lags well behind the 4.4% target — leaving room for an acceleration in disbursement.

Whether the government deploys additional stimulus, and how quickly the new financing instruments translate into on-the-ground investment, will determine whether China can regain fiscal momentum in the final quarter of the year.
