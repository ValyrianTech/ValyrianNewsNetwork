# Trump Bars CNN, MS NOW and Politico From White House as Democrats Cry Foul

President Donald Trump barred reporters from three news organizations—CNN, MS NOW and Politico—from the White House, a move that took physical effect on Saturday when journalists were turned away and had their press credentials deactivated or confiscated. Democratic lawmakers and press-freedom advocates accused the president of attacking the media to divert attention from mounting political and economic troubles.

## The Ban

Trump announced the ban on Friday, September 18, in a post on his Truth Social platform, accusing the outlets of "constant 'reporting' FAKE NEWS" and warning that "other Fake News Media Outlets" would follow, as [Al Jazeera reported](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/18/trump-bans-news-outlets-cnn-ms-now-and-politico-from-white-house).

Speaking to reporters at the White House later that day, Trump offered no specific article or broadcast to justify the decision. "No, no reason," he said. "It's really just cumulative stories over the last few years. You get sick of it." Questioned about whether the move was meant to intimidate the press, he dismissed the suggestion, saying, "I dislike dishonest press."

He also named The New York Times and The Washington Post as what he called "fake news," hinting they could be added to the list.

## Credentials Confiscated

On Saturday morning, the ban became physical. According to [Al Jazeera's account](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/19/a-ban-on-the-free-press-white-house-bars-journalists-from-three-outlets) of the day's events, CNN Senior White House reporter Betsy Klein and MS NOW White House correspondent Akayla Gardner were both denied entry. Gardner said an officer confiscated her badge after telling her it had been disabled and that the decision was "above him." An MS NOW photographer also had a badge disabled, she said.

Politico White House reporter Cheyenne Haslett was subsequently turned away, with the outlet saying the Secret Service denied her entry and confiscated her White House access pass.

"A few minutes ago, our colleague Cheyenne Haslett attempted to enter the White House to do her job as a POLITICO reporter," the news organization said. "Secret Service denied her entry to the complex and confiscated the pass that allows her access to the White House."

Klein said she had covered the White House in some capacity since the Obama administration and noted that a Secret Service agent took her badge when she arrived Saturday morning, per [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/09/19/nx-s1-5974854/trump-cnn-msnow-politico-ban).

## Outlets Push Back

The three organizations issued defiant statements, each vowing to defend their First Amendment rights.

"The White House belongs to the American people and the decisions made inside are funded by our tax dollars," MS NOW said. "MS NOW intends to take any and all steps necessary to defend our First Amendment rights and the essential role of independent journalism in our democracy."

CNN said it "stands fully behind our White House team and their fair and accurate reporting," adding that it has "a right under the U.S. Constitution to do that reporting without hindrance or interference from the government."

Politico said it would "continue to fairly report on this White House and future ones," and would "vigorously defend our First Amendment rights against any attempt to restrict them." The outlet also rejected a Trump claim that it had received an $8 million government subscription, calling the assertion "false" and "thoroughly debunked."

CNN media analyst Brian Stelter framed the stakes broadly. "It's so much bigger than CNN," he said. "This is a free speech test in America."

## First Amendment Storm

The decision drew sharp condemnation from journalism organizations and legal experts.

White House Correspondents' Association President Jacqui Heinrich said Trump had described "his own planned action as a 'ban on the free press.'" The Constitution's protection, she added, "does not depend on whether the president likes a news organization's coverage, agrees with its reporting, or approves of the questions its journalists ask."

Jameel Jaffer, executive director of the Knight First Amendment Institute at Columbia University, argued the move is "doubly unconstitutional" because the White House press pool functions as a "public forum" under the First Amendment, meaning the president cannot exclude journalists based on their viewpoints.

"It's difficult to imagine a more blatant violation of the First Amendment than Trump banning news outlets from the People's House for criticizing the government," said Seth Stern, chief of advocacy at the Freedom of the Press Foundation in [Al Jazeera's explainer](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/19/can-trump-ban-cnn-news-outlets-he-doesnt-like-from-the-white-house) on the ban's legality.

Mark Schoeff Jr., president of the National Press Club, called the ban "a clear attempt to control which voices are allowed to witness and report on the exercise of presidential power."

Notably, the criticism also came from within conservative media. Fox News contributor Ari Fleischer argued that conservatives should challenge liberal reporters rather than bar them. "Debate them and beat them," he said. "Don't ban them, otherwise one day they'll ban us too."

## A Pattern of Conflict

The ban is the most sweeping yet in a long-running confrontation between Trump and the press. A key precedent is the 1977 federal appeals ruling in *Sherrill v. Knight*, which held that once the White House establishes press facilities for credentialed journalists, access cannot be denied arbitrarily or for less than compelling reasons, and that denied journalists must receive notice, a chance to respond and a final written explanation.

The administration has tested those boundaries before. During Trump's first term, the White House revoked CNN reporter Jim Acosta's press pass; a federal judge ordered it restored. In 2025, the administration restricted Associated Press access to the Oval Office and presidential travel after AP declined to rename the Gulf of Mexico the "Gulf of America." AP sued and won, a ruling now under appeal, as [Deadline detailed](https://deadline.com/2026/09/trump-bans-cnn-ms-now-politico-1237107905/).

The White House did not announce any specific enforcement mechanism beyond the Secret Service's role in turning reporters away and reclaiming credentials.

## Political Undercurrents

The ban lands in the middle of the 2026 midterm election season, with Democrats holding a polling edge. Trump has faced mounting pressure over an unpopular war with Iran, an affordability and inflation squeeze, and controversies including threats to demolish the John F. Kennedy Center.

Democrats framed the media ban as an effort to change the subject. Representative Jamie Raskin (D-MD) of the House Judiciary Committee called on news organizations to collectively boycott the White House if Trump "continues to violate press freedom."

It remains unclear whether CNN, MS NOW or Politico will sue, and how courts might apply *Sherrill* to the revocation of existing credentials rather than the denial of new ones. Press-freedom groups said they were exploring legal avenues.

## What to Watch

Several questions now loom over the capital. Will the three outlets pursue a coordinated legal challenge, and will a court order access restored as it did for Acosta and AP? Can the president lawfully exclude journalists based on the content of their reporting—and where exactly is the line between a president's control of his own house and the constitutional guarantee of a free press?

For now, the ban stands, the credentials are in government hands, and the press is being made to test its rights from outside the gates.
