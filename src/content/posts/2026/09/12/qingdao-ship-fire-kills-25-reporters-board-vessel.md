# Qingdao Ship Fire Kills 25 as Reporters Board Vessel

A fire aboard a foreign-flagged cargo ship docked for repairs at a Qingdao shipyard killed 25 people and injured five others, Chinese authorities said, in one of the deadliest shipyard accidents in China in recent years. On September 11, journalists [boarded the vessel](https://www.news.cn/politics/20260912/c5b15480148945aaa17a3b7998cb9020/c.html) for an on-scene inspection, offering the first close-up look at the charred bulk carrier at the center of the investigation.

The blaze broke out at about 11:15 a.m. on September 10 aboard the OCEAN MELODY, a bulk carrier undergoing maintenance at the China State Shipbuilding Corporation (CSSC) Qingdao Beihai Shipbuilding Co., Ltd. in Shandong Province. Of the 42 people on board at the time, 12 escaped safely, five were hospitalized with stable vital signs, and 25 were initially reported missing.

## A Rapid and Deadly Sequence

The main fire was brought under control by roughly 2:30 p.m., [according to CCTV News](https://www.cqnews.net/web/content_1547706204587130880.html). Rescue teams then worked through the vessel's interior. By 5:30 p.m., 20 of the missing had been recovered, none showing vital signs, as [21 Caijing reported](https://m.21jingji.com/article/20260910/herald/ac874bc299b50e22aa5694a2c9877c20.html). By 7:24 p.m., authorities confirmed that all 25 missing people had been found dead, fixing the death toll.

Shandong provincial and Qingdao municipal emergency mechanisms were activated immediately, mobilizing emergency management, firefighting, police, health, and industry authorities. The Ministry of Emergency Management dispatched a working group to the site to guide the response.

Rescue conditions were grim. Crews had to descend through a narrow hatch into the ship's bottom hold, where the interior was smoky, hot — around 85°C (185°F) — and slippery. Some victims were trapped in crevices or in water, complicating recovery and transport.

## The Vessel and Its Repairs

The OCEAN MELODY is a roughly 20-year-old, Supramax-type dry bulk carrier built in February 2006 by Mitsui E&S, flying the flag of Liberia with a port of registry in Monrovia, [according to Jiemian News](https://m.jiemian.com/article/15081088.html), which cited public maritime registration records. The ship measures about 190 meters long with a beam of about 32 meters, a deadweight tonnage of roughly 55,700 tonnes, and a gross tonnage of 31,279. It is classed by Nippon Kaiji Kyokai (ClassNK).

Maritime records show the vessel's registered owner is Huili Shipping Co Ltd, with Yuyangkunpeng Shanghai Ship Mgm as its ISM management company. The ship previously operated under the name STRANGE ATTRACTOR between roughly 2022 and 2025 and once flew the Marshall Islands flag, according to the same registration data.

Tracking data places the vessel at Qingdao Beihai Shipbuilding from August 31, having arrived from Taicang in Jiangsu Province. It entered the dry dock on September 9 — less than 24 hours before the fire. Planned work included hull rust removal and repainting, structural non-destructive testing, and equipment disassembly and overhaul. At the time of the fire, its AIS status was recorded as "moored," with a draft of about 6.1 meters.

## Why Ship Repair Is So Dangerous

Ship repair is a chronically high-risk operation, and Chinese regulators have repeatedly flagged it. In August 2026, the Ministry of Emergency Management issued a dedicated safety-risk advisory for ship repair and maintenance, warning that vessels contain numerous enclosed compartments and that repair work involves hot work and confined-space operations prone to fire, explosion, and toxic asphyxiation — with fatality rates that tend to run high.

According to Jiemian's review of official accident investigations, unauthorized hot work is the leading ignition source. Arc sparks and molten slag from welding and cutting, at temperatures reaching over a thousand degrees, can drop through deck gaps and pipeline openings into lower compartments, quietly igniting residual fuel, paint, or wood shavings. Inadequate ventilation and accumulated oil residues can push flammable vapors into explosive concentration, while enclosed-space combustion rapidly depletes oxygen and produces carbon monoxide and hydrogen cyanide. Most shipyard fire victims, the reporting notes, die of toxic asphyxiation rather than direct burns — a key reason such incidents are so lethal.

## Official Framing and Open Questions

[BBC Chinese](https://www.bbc.com/zhongwen/articles/czjzvdxw8gvo/simp) noted that under China's emergency response law, incidents with 10 to 30 deaths are classified as "major" accidents. The Qingdao fire falls into that category, triggering a response structure in which provincial authorities take the lead while the central government's emergency management department intervenes — a mid-tier mechanism compared with the top-level central response reserved for incidents killing 30 or more.

Chinese state media consistently described the vessel as "a foreign cargo ship" (外籍货轮) and did not name the Chinese-linked owner or operator. International observers have pointed to the phased, incremental nature of official disclosures and the absence of victim nationality or identity details.

Central leadership issued instructions to make all-out rescue efforts, handle the aftermath properly, determine the cause quickly, and hold those responsible accountable under law, while comprehensively rectifying fire hazards in the shipbuilding and repair sector. As of September 12, no official cause had been announced.

The Qingdao fire is the latest in a string of serious industrial accidents in China over the past year. [VOA Chinese](https://www.voachinese.com/a/china-cargo-ship-fire-kills-25-20260910/8196492.html) noted a shipyard explosion in Fujian Province a month earlier that killed one person and injured 12, a shoe factory fire in July that killed at least 28, and a fireworks plant explosion in May that killed 37. BBC Chinese also referenced the July Fujian shoe factory fire, underscoring a broader pattern of deadly workplace incidents.

## What to Watch

The investigation now turns on two central questions: what ignited the fire, and whether safety procedures were followed during the hot-work operations that put the vessel into dry dock on September 9. The presence of a welding torch at the fire's core, reported in the research trail, will be a focal point for investigators.

Beyond the immediate cause, the case is likely to renew scrutiny of safety enforcement across China's vast shipbuilding and repair industry, an arena where compressed schedules and subcontracting can strain oversight. For the families of the 25 dead and the five still recovering, the answers cannot come soon enough.
