# Tucson Gay Bar Shooting Confirmed as Hate Crime by Police

Police in Arizona have officially confirmed that the shooting outside a Tucson gay bar that left two men dead was a hate crime, according to the [Tucson Police Department](https://apnews.com/article/arizona-gay-bar-shooting-killed-hate-crime-2884382f7e35f55590bd953ceb8d11d1). The gunman, 44-year-old Ousman Ceesay, was new to the Tucson area and had no prior connection to the victims before confronting and shooting them in the parking lot of Venture-N bar early Monday morning.

## The Attack

Vincent Anthony Siqueiros, 42, and Cameron Davis Capara, 33, were in the parking lot of Venture-N at approximately 12:30 a.m. on August 31 when Ceesay confronted and shot them, police said. Both victims died at the scene. Ceesay then shot himself with a 9mm handgun and was pronounced dead at a local hospital. Video evidence confirmed Ceesay never attempted to enter the bar.

During a search of Ceesay's home, detectives found a note that "indicated he acted alone and that this shooting was a targeted attack motivated by hate toward the LGBTQIA+ community," the department said in a news release. Ceesay was also carrying a note listing several LGBTQIA+-associated businesses in the region, suggesting he may have intended further attacks. Police have contacted each business on the list and said there is no known active threat to the community.

Ceesay had no prior contacts with the Tucson Police Department and was not prohibited from possessing firearms, according to [police](https://apnews.com/article/gay-bar-shooting-hate-crime-tucson-3ce3178d5d17a5ec92b7943568502882).

## The Victims

Siqueiros, known as "Vinny" to friends, was a fixture at Venture-N and was described as funny, caring, and sweet. His sister Kate Marquez remembered him at a vigil as "a proud gay man" who was "brave enough to live authentically in a world that sometimes told him his authentic self was unacceptable."

Capara was a photographer and activist who documented protests and social justice movements throughout Tucson, often wearing a flak jacket with "Press" written across it. His close friend Maria Sohn Hasman said he "dedicated hours of his life every single week for years, documenting everything that was going on in the community and fighting for a better world."

Brian King, a regular at Venture-N who knew both men, said, "They were genuinely who they were. They didn't try to be anybody else, they didn't conform, they didn't kiss ass. They liked who they were and that's why people liked them, because they were comfortable in their skins," as [LOOKOUT reported](https://www.lookoutnews.org/tucson-gay-bar-shooting-cameron-capara-anthony-siqueiros/).

## Community Response and Vigil

Thousands gathered at Catalina Park in Tucson on Wednesday evening for a candlelight vigil honoring Siqueiros and Capara, organized by community groups including Tucson Queerstory, Southern Arizona Senior Pride, and the Southern Arizona AIDS Foundation, as [KGUN9 reported](https://www.kgun9.com/news/community-inspired-journalism/midtown-news/community-vigil-held-for-victims-of-lgbt-targeted-hate-crime).

Sam Cloud of Tucson Queerstory told the crowd: "Two amazing humans from our community are gone. And they are not a statistic. They are not a national headline. They were human beings. They were people that this community loved, and they loved people in this community."

The shooting has stirred traumatic memories for many older LGBTQ+ adults, said Mary O'Donoghue, executive director of Southern Arizona Senior Pride. "We're talking about folks that are in their 70s and 80s, having lived through Stonewall and other experiences where there has been discrimination, aggression and violence," she told the AP. "It really has a rippling effect - they're reliving a lifetime of violence and discrimination on their community."

Venture-N, which opened in 1991, has been described as a "cozy neighborhood bar" and "a steadfast safe place for Tucson's queer community" for decades. Owner David Wallis said the bar remained closed following the shooting but plans to reopen eventually.

## Political Response and Legal Questions

Tucson Mayor Regina Romero called the shooting a "direct and devastating attack on our LGBTQ+ community, another senseless act of gun violence," adding that it "serves as another painful reminder that homophobia is still rampant in our country."

U.S. Rep. Adelita Grijalva, a Tucson Democrat, spoke on the House floor about the attack, saying: "This horrific attack is a painful reminder that even in welcoming communities like Tucson, hate and violence against the LGBTQIA+ community still exist." She called for "common-sense gun safety reforms" and noted that "when that hate is combined with easy access to firearms, the consequences can be deadly."

However, the case exposes significant gaps in Arizona law, as [LOOKOUT reported](https://www.lookoutnews.org/venture-n-shooting-hate-crime-arizona-law-hate-enhancement/). Arizona does not have a standalone hate crime statute. State law allows a judge to consider bias-related conduct when imposing a sentence for an underlying felony, but that provision only applies after a conviction. Since Ceesay died by suicide, there can be no prosecution or sentencing hearing in this case.

Pima County Attorney Laura Conover acknowledged this reality: "Because all known suspects are deceased, there will likely be no prosecution to follow." The state's sentencing enhancement also does not expressly list gender identity alongside sexual orientation as a protected category.

## The Shooter's Background

Ceesay was born in The Gambia, a small West African nation south of Senegal, and had been living in Oklahoma City since December before recently moving to Tucson, according to the [Arizona Daily Star](https://tucson.com/news/local/crime-courts/article_c3b0c588-5578-4ce5-b22c-5b593e803889.html). He and his wife filed for Chapter 7 bankruptcy in 2018 after accumulating more than $76,000 in debt. He had no serious criminal record in the U.S. other than a 2012 traffic citation and several small-claims complaints from loan agencies.

Police Chief Monica Prieto condemned the attack, saying: "Bias-based crimes are especially heinous and have no place in our community." The department has increased police presence around LGBTQIA+ establishments in the region.

## What's Next

The Tucson community continues to mourn the loss of Siqueiros and Capara while demanding action to prevent future attacks. The shooting has reignited conversations about gun safety legislation and the need for stronger hate crime protections in Arizona. As the memorial outside Venture-N continues to grow, community members have emphasized that the two men should be remembered for how they lived, not how they died.

"I hope we remember Cameron," Capara's father said at the vigil. "When we remember Cameron, we don't remember how we lost him, but I hope we remember how he lived and wanted the same for all of us."
