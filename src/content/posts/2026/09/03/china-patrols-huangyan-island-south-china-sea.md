# China Patrols Huangyan Island Amid Escalating South China Sea Tensions

Naval and air units of China's People's Liberation Army (PLA) Southern Theater Command conducted combat readiness patrols in the territorial sea and airspace of Huangyan Island (Scarborough Shoal) and its surrounding areas on September 2, according to a [statement](https://www.news.cn/politics/20260902/2cee82c5c39342ec8de53145bcc2b62d/c.html) from the theater command. The patrols mark the latest in a series of increasingly frequent military operations around the disputed South China Sea feature.

## Context: A Long-Running Territorial Dispute

Huangyan Island, a triangular coral reef formation approximately 124 nautical miles off the Philippine coast, is claimed by both China and the Philippines. Beijing took de facto control of the shoal in 2012 following a standoff with Manila, and the area has since become one of the most closely watched flashpoints in the South China Sea dispute. The Philippines claims the feature as Bajo de Masinloc or Panatag Shoal.

## Escalating Military Activity in 2026

The September 2 patrols came amid a significant escalation in tensions between Beijing and Manila over the island. According to the Southern Theater Command, forces have strengthened readiness patrols in the area since August, with operations aimed at "further enhancing control of relevant sea areas and airspace" and serving as "an effective countermeasure to cope with all sorts of rights-violation and provocative acts."

Footage released by the Southern Theater Command showed multiple PLA assets involved in the operation, including the JL-10 advanced trainer jet carrying live missiles, H-6K bombers, a Type 052C destroyer, a Type 054A frigate, and a Type 071 amphibious dock landing ship, as [reported](https://www.globaltimes.cn/page/202609/1369664.shtml?id=12) by the Global Times. In the video, Chinese military pilots can be heard warning approaching Philippine aircraft: "You have approached China's Huangyan Dao territorial airspace. To avoid misunderstandings and miscalculations, leave immediately."

## A Pattern of Escalation

The latest patrols follow months of intensifying confrontation. On August 21, three Philippine aircraft—an Islander, a C-208, and a PA-31T—illegally entered China's territorial airspace over Huangyan Island, prompting the PLA Southern Theater Command to track, monitor, and issue verbal warnings, according to [Global Times](https://www.globaltimes.cn/page/202608/1368704.shtml?id=11) reporting. Senior Colonel Li Jianjian, spokesperson for the PLA STC Air Force, sternly warned the Philippine side to "stop rights-violation and provocative acts immediately."

The Philippines has increasingly drawn on external military support, organizing so-called joint patrols with allied forces in the region. The PLA has maintained full-time tracking and surveillance of these operations, according to [military sources](https://www.globaltimes.cn/page/202608/1368751.shtml?id=11) cited by the Global Times. In late August, a US aircraft carrier entered the South China Sea, and Philippine vessels again approached Huangyan Island before being intercepted by the China Coast Guard.

## Analysis: Strategic Restraint or Calculated Response?

Chinese military affairs experts suggest the choice of the JL-10 trainer jet—rather than more advanced fighter aircraft—reflects deliberate strategic calculation. An anonymous expert told the Global Times that the seemingly "downgraded" aircraft choice "precisely reflects China's strategic restraint and sense of responsibility as a responsible major power." The JL-10 excels at low-altitude, low-speed operations, making it particularly suited to intercepting the slow-moving turboprop aircraft that Philippine forces have deployed for what Beijing characterizes as provocative incursions.

The broader pattern of escalation has been building throughout 2026. In late July, the Philippines notified the United Nations of so-called "baselines and maritime zones" surrounding Huangyan Island, a move China's Foreign Ministry rejected as "illegal, null and void." The Chinese Foreign Ministry [stated](https://www.scmp.com/news/china/military/article/3362700/beijing-warns-manila-powerful-south-china-sea-toolbox-ready-head-blow) that Beijing had a "rich and powerful" toolbox of options for those who "make waves" in the South China Sea.

A rare naval standoff in June, when four Chinese warships confronted the Philippine Navy frigate BRP Diego Silang near the shoal, underscored the risks of miscalculation, as [reported](https://www.scmp.com/news/china/military/article/3358063/china-and-philippines-have-rare-naval-stand-near-disputed-scarborough-shoal) by the South China Morning Post. The incident coincided with the conclusion of Salaknib 2026, a joint military exercise involving more than 7,000 troops from the Philippines, the United States, Japan, New Zealand, and Australia.

## Regional Implications and the Path Forward

Analysts note that China Coast Guard ship-days at Scarborough Shoal have already reached levels equivalent to the total for all of 2025, with activity on track to double. The Philippines, backed by growing security cooperation with the US and Japan, has shown greater willingness to stage provocations, while Chinese forces have responded with increasingly visible shows of military presence.

The Southern Theater Command has stated that its forces "will resolutely safeguard China's sovereignty and security and firmly uphold peace and stability in the South China Sea." As both sides continue to escalate their presence around Huangyan Island, the risk of unintended confrontation remains a persistent concern for regional stability. ASEAN members have expressed interest in concluding a Code of Conduct for the South China Sea, but progress has been complicated by the ongoing tensions between Beijing and Manila.

With patrols now occurring on a near-monthly basis since March 2026, the standoff over Huangyan Island shows no signs of abating. The international community will be watching closely for whether diplomatic channels can ease the confrontation or whether the two nations continue down a path of escalating military posturing in one of Asia's most contested maritime regions.
