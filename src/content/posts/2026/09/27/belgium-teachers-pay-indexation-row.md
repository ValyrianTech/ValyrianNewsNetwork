# Belgium: Teachers' Pay Slips Spark Indexation Row

A dispute over the indexation of teachers' salaries in Belgium's French-speaking education system deepened on Sunday, as the CSC-Enseignement union demanded full clarity from Education Minister Valérie Glatigny while the grassroots collective Mars Attacks called a work stoppage for Monday. At issue is an apparent drop in the "annual gross scale" line on September pay slips, which hundreds of teachers discovered over the weekend despite a wage indexation that raised their net pay.

The CSC (Confédération des syndicats chrétiens) says the explanations offered so far are unsatisfactory, and the socialist opposition has threatened an extraordinary parliamentary session if answers are not provided. The row has reignited tensions over pay and budget cuts in the Fédération Wallonie-Bruxelles (FWB), the body that governs French-speaking education in Belgium.

## A fictitious scale, and a communication gap

According to [RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/indexation-des-salaires-des-enseignants-malgre-les-explications-la-csc-demande-la-clarte-mars-attacks-appelle-a-un-arret-de-travail-lundi-11791233), the confusion stems from the federal government's decision to cap wage indexation at 4,000 euros gross per month. To apply the cap, the FWB administration introduced a so-called "barème fictif" (fictitious scale) that lowers the annual gross scale shown on pay slips.

Multiplied by the new, higher index, that lower scale still produces the correct gross annual and monthly salary — which rose by about 80 euros gross per month for the teachers concerned, RTBF reported, citing a teacher's pay slip. Because the actual gross salary is correct, the operation has no impact on pensions or taxes beyond the ordinary effect of indexation, the minister's office said. The minister added that the method is identical to that used in Flemish and German-speaking education, in the FWB public service and in the federal public service.

That reassurance has not satisfied the unions. "We demand clear, precise and unambiguous information from the start of the week," CSC-Enseignement secretary-general Roland Lahaye said in a statement. "We demand that all guarantees be provided as to the possible impacts of the measure on the rest of their careers and their pensions."

The union also complained that no direct communication was made to teaching staff about their specific situation in the FWB, describing the minister's remarks as "ambiguous." "Given the current climate of crisis and tension, direct communication to staff members would have helped to ease the legitimate anger now being expressed," Lahaye said.

## Why the pay slips changed

The dispute is not about whether salaries went up — net pay did rise slightly — but about the appearance of a lowered annual gross scale and the method used to achieve it. [RTL Info](https://www.rtl.be/actu/belgique/societe/jai-ete-indexe-mais-mon-salaire-annuel-brut-ete-diminue-de-300-euros-les/2026-09-26/article/801477) gathered testimony from several angry teachers. "A teacher appointed for years, I note that we have been indexed. However, my annual gross salary has been reduced by 300 euros. How is this possible?" one wrote. Another, Aline, warned that the small net increase "masks a fake accounting fix by the administration that will cost teachers dearly in the long term."

A detailed [RTBF explainer](https://www.rtbf.be/article/indexation-plafonnee-en-septembre-les-enseignants-surpris-par-une-baisse-de-leur-bareme-brut-pourtant-les-calculs-sont-corrects-11791063) walked through one teacher's figures to show the math is correct: the annual scale fell from 27,285.62 euros in August to 27,185.38 euros in September, while the index rose from 2.1647 to 2.2080. Applied together, the numbers yield a monthly gross of 5,002.10 euros — exactly 80 euros more than the previous 4,922.10 euros. In other words, the capped indexation still delivered a raise; only the base scale was adjusted.

Mars Attacks, a horizontal collective of teachers known for its playful "alien" branding, has challenged the legality of the maneuver. It argues the salary scale is fixed by royal decree and can only be modified by a decree of the French Community government. "You noticed it when reading your pay slip: your annual gross was modified," the group said, per RTL Info. "The salary scale is fixed by a royal decree. Only a decree of the French Community government can modify the scale." The collective also warned that teachers could face higher taxation later and that pension contributions might be affected — concerns the minister's office denies.

## Political pressure mounts

On the political front, the PS group in the FWB Parliament sent a letter to the Parliament president demanding clarity, threatening an extraordinary committee session if complete answers were not received by Sunday evening. "If we do not obtain complete answers to our letter by Sunday at 18:00, we will demand an extraordinary committee session next week so that full transparency can be established," said [La Libre](https://www.lalibre.be/belgique/enseignement/2026/09/27/baremes-des-enseignants-la-csc-exige-des-explications-de-valerie-glatigny-mars-attacks-annonce-un-arret-de-travail-ce-lundi-WZPWX2NHG5GCJMREFNXVBVLCBU/) quoting PS group leader Martin Casier. Ecolo also announced it would question the minister.

Glatigny has insisted the scales themselves are unchanged. "The administration introduced a fictitious scale in order to take non-indexation into account in the calculation of pay," her office said, per [La Libre](https://www.lalibre.be/belgique/politique-belge/2026/09/26/salaires-des-enseignants-les-baremes-ne-sont-pas-modifies-assure-valerie-glatigny-OWFCQVMPJVF37K7AMK5KPZP7JY/). "This has no impact on seniority or on the teacher's pension." The minister said the unions had been informed of the calculation method on 20 August via the comité A, the public-services negotiating body, and promised a formal explanatory communication to teachers early in the week.

## A broader fight over austerity

The episode is the latest flashpoint in a longer confrontation over education savings in the FWB. The capped indexation at the heart of the dispute was part of the federal "Arizona" coalition's loi-programme, adopted in a night vote on 29 May 2026, which limits indexation to salary portions below 4,000 euros gross, [RTBF reported](https://www.rtbf.be/article/la-loi-programme-de-l-arizona-adoptee-a-la-chambre-lors-d-un-vote-nocturne-11731612).

Mars Attacks emerged from that austerity context. Founded as a self-organized movement of teachers in early 2026, the collective has claimed more than 240 schools and uses tactics ranging from work stoppages to boycotts of course hours and media stunts. A portrait by the [Ligue de l'Enseignement](https://ligue-enseignement.be/education-enseignement/articles/actualites/mars-attacks-un-ovni-face-glatigny) described it as complementary to the unions rather than in competition, with demands limited to refinancing the FWB and withdrawing the reforms voted. Lahaye, the CSC leader, compared it to the "gilets jaunes" for its agility and lack of structure.

## What to watch

The immediate test comes Monday, when Mars Attacks' work stoppage is due to take place and the minister has promised a formal explanation to teachers. Whether that communication calms the anger — or, as the CSC warns, merely confirms a sense of contempt — may shape the next phase of mobilization. Two questions remain open: whether the change to the annual scale required a formal government decision, and whether teachers' longer-term pension and tax positions are truly unaffected. With the PS threatening parliamentary action and Ecolo preparing questions, the minister is likely to face pressure to answer both.

For now, the row illustrates how a technical adjustment, applied without prior explanation, can become a political flashpoint — particularly when it lands on pay slips during a period of sustained austerity.
