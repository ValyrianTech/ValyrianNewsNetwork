# Giant Pandas Pingping and Fushuang Depart for the US

Two giant pandas, male Pingping and female Fushuang, departed Chengdu Shuangliu Airport early on September 27 aboard a charter flight to Zoo Atlanta, beginning a 10-year residency in the United States. According to [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/world/20260927/53aaa9e48e5148aca6c411626165516f/c.html), the pair left at 2:55 a.m. Beijing time following a farewell ceremony at the Chengdu Research Base of Giant Panda Breeding. The transfer arrives just two days after President Xi Jinping's state visit to the United States, where the pandas were formally enshrined in the two countries' diplomatic outcomes.

## Context: A Diplomatic Signal

The departure is the latest chapter in "panda diplomacy," the decades-long practice of loaning the animals to foreign zoos to build goodwill. It lands at a notably warmer moment in China-US relations. During Xi's September 23-25 state visit, the two governments reached an eight-point outcomes consensus. The eighth and final point states simply: "The US side welcomes the arrival in the US of a pair of giant pandas loaned by China to Zoo Atlanta," as recorded by the [State Council](https://www.gov.cn/yaowen/liebiao/202609/content_7082158.htm).

That a panda transfer was elevated to the same document as agreements on strategic stability, trade mechanisms, and a new AI dialogue underscores how closely the animals are tied to the broader diplomatic relationship. Few items of wildlife policy carry comparable symbolic weight.

## Key Developments

Pingping, a male born on March 17, 2020, is described as distinctively large with dark, prominent eye patches. Stocky but agile, he is an especially skilled tree climber who often rests among branches. Fushuang, a female born on October 18, 2020, has a tuft of fluffy fur on her head and a full, slightly protruding forehead. She is quick and alert, with a strong ability to locate enrichment food hidden by keepers.

Both were born at the Chengdu Research Base. The China Wildlife Conservation Association signed the new International Cooperative Research Agreement on Giant Panda Conservation with [Zoo Atlanta](https://zooatlanta.org/two-new-giant-pandas-are-expected-to-arrive-at-zoo-atlanta/) in 2025. "Zoo Atlanta is delighted and honored to yet again be trusted as stewards of this treasured species," President and CEO Raymond B. King said when the agreement and the pandas' names were announced in April 2026. "We can't wait to meet Ping Ping and Fu Shuang and to welcome our Members, guests, city, and community back to the wonder and joy of giant pandas."

China prepared a detailed transport plan for the trans-Pacific journey, stocking fresh bamboo shoots, bamboo, apples, and emergency medicine. The pandas underwent adaptive transport training beforehand to reduce stress, and cabin temperature and humidity were held within their comfort range. Senior US veterinarians and keepers accompanied them on the flight. Two batches of Chinese veterinarians and keepers were dispatched to the zoo in advance; the pandas will undergo quarantine on arrival, with Chinese staff staying on site through the quarantine period.

## Enclosure Upgrades and a Track Record

Since December 2025, Chinese experts and Zoo Atlanta have held repeated online and offline meetings on renovating the panda enclosure. In mid-September 2026, Chinese experts conducted an on-site acceptance inspection. The upgrades significantly expand indoor display space and add climbing structures, rock platforms, and pools, along with an upgraded monitoring system. Atlanta's climate resembles Chengdu's, allowing year-round sourcing of fresh bamboo.

The partnership rests on a substantial scientific record. Over more than 20 years of cooperation, the two sides have carried out more than 30 joint research projects and published over 50 academic papers, with advances in breeding, behavioral research, and disease control. During that period, five litters totaling seven cubs were successfully bred and survived, described by the China Wildlife Conservation Association as the best result of China's international giant panda breeding cooperation with Europe and the Americas.

The previous Atlanta pair, Lun Lun and Yang Yang, arrived in 1999 and produced seven offspring between 2006 and 2016, including two sets of twins. They departed for Chengdu in October 2024 alongside their two youngest cubs, ending Zoo Atlanta's original 25-year panda partnership, a pause that the new agreement now reverses.

## Analysis: Why the Pandas Matter

Panda loans have long functioned as a barometer of China's relations with partner countries, and the timing here is pointed. The last several years saw a broad withdrawal of pandas from US zoos amid heightened tensions. Some returned to the United States in 2024 and 2025, to the San Diego Zoo and the Smithsonian National Zoo, but the Atlanta transfer is unusual in that it was written directly into a presidential-level outcomes document.

The framing from China's state media leans heavily on conservation science and people-to-people friendship rather than geopolitics. Beijing retains ownership of the pandas and any cubs born abroad, and the 10-year term signals an expectation of durable cooperation. For Zoo Atlanta, the return of pandas restores a signature attraction and a research program with a long publication history. For both governments, the animals offer a low-cost, high-visibility symbol of a relationship that remains, at the strategic level, deeply contested.

## What's Next

Pingping and Fushuang will spend their first weeks in quarantine, gradually adapting to their new surroundings under the care of Chinese and American staff. Zoo Atlanta has said it will make further announcements about the pair in due course. Whether the pandas become a lasting emblem of warming ties, or merely a pleasant footnote to an eight-point agreement whose other items are far harder to implement, will depend on a relationship that no amount of goodwill between two pandas can, by itself, settle.
