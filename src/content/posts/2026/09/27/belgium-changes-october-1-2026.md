# Belgium: What Changes on October 1, 2026

A broad package of federal and regional measures takes effect in Belgium on October 1, 2026, reshaping how households finance home renovations, sign energy contracts, visit the dentist, and register their vehicles. The changes, spanning consumer policy and civil administration, will be felt most acutely in Wallonia and Brussels.

According to [RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/primes-a-la-renovation-en-wallonie-contrats-d-energie-dentiste-tout-ce-qui-change-le-1er-octobre-11791175), the most consequential item is Wallonia's wholesale replacement of its traditional renovation grants with a new loan-based system, alongside new rules on energy pricing, dental care, personalized license plates, prison overcrowding, student-entrepreneur status, and a cystic fibrosis drug.

## Wallonia ends its renovation grant patchwork

The flagship reform replaces Wallonia's fragmented grant system — reportedly more than 120 overlapping subsidies — with a single loan-based support scheme whose conditions are tied to both household income and achieved energy-performance results. The current grants regime applies until September 30, 2026.

The new support targets the least energy-efficient homes and requires a "label jump." Homes with an energy label of PEB F or G must reach at least label D after works, while homes rated E must reach at least label C.

Two products form the backbone of the system, as detailed by [ImmoPass](https://www.immopass.eu/blog/2026-07-reforme-des-aides-a-la-renovation-en-wallonie-ce-qui-change-au-1er-octobre-2026): the Renopack, a zero-interest loan with a reduction of the amount to be repaid that varies by household income, and the Renopret, a zero-interest or preferential-rate loan for higher-income households, owner-landlords, and co-ownership associations.

The maximum borrowable amount rises to EUR 75,000 for a single-family home, up from EUR 60,000 currently, with apartments eligible for up to EUR 60,000 per dwelling. Financing is now calculated globally across all eligible works rather than item by item. A mandatory prior housing audit becomes the gateway to the scheme, and its cost can be included in the financing.

## Energy contracts: an end to misleading welcome bonuses

From October 1, energy suppliers may no longer grant "misleading reductions" on contracts, according to the CREG, Belgium's federal energy regulator. Suppliers also may not change a product's tariff conditions mid-month without substantively renaming the product.

These misleading reductions are large welcome bonuses counted by price comparison tools, making offers appear cheaper but typically conditioned on staying with the same supplier for one or two years. From October, discounts must apply from the first kilowatt-hour across the whole contract duration. In case of a mid-month tariff change, the product name must change substantially — not merely by adding a digit.

As the reform's architects, Federal Energy Minister Mathieu Bihet and Consumer Protection Minister Rob Beenders, framed it, the law adopted by Parliament on July 3, 2026, pursues one goal in three words: "understand, compare and save," per a [government statement](https://www.mr.be/un-marche-de-lenergie-plus-favorable-aux-consommateurs/). Bihet argued that consumers are being put back at the center of the market. "More transparency means more competition and, ultimately, more purchasing power," he said. The broader transparency law otherwise enters into force on January 1, 2027; only the misleading-discount ban was fast-tracked to October.

Social energy tariffs, meanwhile, are rising for the fourth quarter. Natural gas and heat tariffs increase 11.4%, while electricity rises about 7% on average for night-rate categories, per the CREG. Those electricity tariffs were capped at 10%; without the cap, they would have climbed 15.1% and 17.4%.

## Dentists must check ID for third-party payment

From October 1, dentists must verify patients' identity to apply the electronic third-party payment scheme, according to INAMI, the national health insurance institute. The system lets patients pay only their personal share on the spot, with their health insurer paying the practitioner the reimbursed remainder directly.

Patients are urged to bring their ID card — the Belgian eID for adults, or the Kids-ID or ISI+ card for children. If a patient lacks their ID, the dentist may verify identity by other means, such as a health-insurer vignette. Nurses and hospitals have long been required to verify identity when using third-party payment.

## A legal basis to revoke offensive plates

From October 1, the Vehicle Registration Directorate (DIV) can revoke personalized plates bearing an offensive message, per a Royal Decree published in late September. Until now, the administration had no legal basis to remove such plates and relied on motorists' voluntary agreement.

According to the [SPF Mobilité et Transports](https://mobilit.belgium.be/fr/node/6830), officials analyze flagged combinations using common usage and artificial-intelligence tools to detect offensive references. If a plate is deemed problematic, the DIV contacts the owner, who has 30 days to make observations and request a free replacement. If there is no response or a refusal, the DIV revokes the plate as a last resort.

Federal Mobility Minister Jean-Luc Crucke said a transparent procedure was needed. "Even against payment, hate and inappropriate messages have no place on our roads," he said. Since early 2025, the DIV has refused 1,050 problematic combinations.

## Wider measures: prisons, students, charging, and healthcare

The package also includes emergency measures to ease prison overcrowding, proposed by Justice Minister Annelies Verlinden. Prisoners sentenced to up to 18 months will automatically receive an electronic bracelet until the end of 2028, with higher-risk categories excluded. Sentencing tribunals have already voiced concerns, describing the plan as an unprecedented attack on their independence.

In Brussels, a "rotation" pricing system for public EV charging points takes effect across the 19 communes. Parking remains free during actual charging, but any car exceeding six hours at a charging point faces a rotation tariff of EUR 0.06 per minute, applied only between 9:00 and 22:00.

The student-entrepreneur status is also reformed to be more accessible, removing the age limit of 25 and several administrative obligations. Per INASTI figures, the number of student-entrepreneurs in Belgium reached a record 9,155 in 2025.

Finally, the drug Alyftrek is reimbursed for cystic fibrosis patients aged six and over. For the first time, people with rare genetic mutations of the disease get the same coverage. About 1,400 people in Belgium live with cystic fibrosis.

## Analysis: a structural shift, not just a seasonal reset

Roundups of measures taking effect on the first of a month are routine in Belgium, where federal and regional rules often align to the calendar. But this package reflects a deeper shift in how the government approaches household costs.

The Walloon renovation overhaul is the clearest example. By converting subsidies into income-tested loans and tying support to measurable energy outcomes, the region is abandoning a system that had become politically and fiscally unsustainable — more than 120 overlapping subsidies, processing delays of up to two years, and a budget overrun of about EUR 1 billion. The trade-off is that households must now clear a mandatory audit and achieve a label jump, raising the bar for access even as the maximum financing grows.

The energy measures point in a similar direction: fewer illusions, more responsibility. The misleading-discount ban targets a specific practice that has flattered comparison tools and obscured real prices, while the social-tariff increase — capped but still steep — reminds vulnerable households that market volatility has not disappeared.

Together, the changes suggest a government betting that clearer rules and stricter targeting will deliver better outcomes than broad, opaque support. Whether households experience that as protection or added complexity may depend on how quickly the implementing decrees arrive.

## What to watch

Several practical details remain to be finalized. The exact calculation of Renopack and Renopret aid and the administrative procedure still require execution decrees, and the Walloon Housing Agency will not pilot the target system until January 2028. The full federal energy market transparency reform, meanwhile, does not take effect until January 1, 2027, leaving the October discount ban as a first test of enforcement.

For Belgian consumers, the immediate takeaway is simple: check your energy contract terms, prepare your ID for dental visits, and if you own an energy-inefficient home in Wallonia, understand the new audit-first pathway before committing to renovation.
