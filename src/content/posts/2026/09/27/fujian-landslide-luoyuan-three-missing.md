# Fujian Landslide at Luoyuan Site Leaves Three Missing

A landslide and mudslide struck a land-leveling project in Bili Township, Luoyuan County, in China's southeastern Fujian province, leaving three people unaccounted for and damaging two houses, local authorities said. The hazard occurred at 17:03 local time on Saturday, September 26, and triggered an immediate search-and-rescue operation.

According to [CCTV News](https://news.cctv.com/2026/09/26/ARTIi64NSOjwa1TbBElm3jA1260926.shtml), the incident was reported by the Luoyuan County emergency command. Citing the on-site command center, the broadcaster said the slide damaged two houses and that three people had not yet been reached. The road blocked by the debris had been cleared by the evening of the same day.

## Emergency Response Mobilized

Rescuers were deployed within hours of the collapse. The county organized six teams totaling 116 professional personnel to carry out search-and-rescue, emergency response, site clearing, road clearance, and the resettlement of affected residents, according to the command center cited by [China News Service](https://www.chinanews.com.cn/sh/2026/09-27/10704353.shtml).

China News Service reported that the county government issued an official update on the night of September 26, confirming the damage to two structures and the clearing of the blocked road while search efforts continued.

Fujian is a mountainous, typhoon-exposed province where heavy seasonal rainfall frequently raises the risk of geological disasters. The slide occurred at a "land-leveling" construction site, where earthworks can destabilize slopes — a risk that compounds during the region's rainy season. China's Ministry of Natural Resources routinely monitors Fujian and neighboring Zhejiang for landslide and mudslide hazards, and it launched a Level III geological disaster defense response for the two provinces in July 2026 during Typhoon Bawei.

## Official Priorities

Local officials outlined four priorities going forward: continuing the search for the missing, guarding against secondary disasters, determining the cause of the slide as quickly as possible, and managing the aftermath and relief work, according to China News Service.

The Xinhua News Agency, in a report republished by [China Economic Net](http://www.ce.cn/xwzx/gnsz/gdxw/202609/t20260927_3236793.shtml), said the cause of the incident was under investigation and that search operations were intensifying. Xinhua reporters at the scene overnight confirmed that the blocked road had been reopened.

No confirmed deaths had been reported as of the initial official statements. Chinese authorities typically use the term "unaccounted for" or "not yet contacted" in the early hours of such incidents, before the status of missing individuals is confirmed.

## Why It Matters

Landslides and mudslides are a recurring hazard across China's mountainous interior and coastal provinces, particularly during the summer and autumn rainy and typhoon seasons. Fujian, which sits on the country's southeastern coast opposite Taiwan, combines steep terrain with heavy rainfall, making it one of the most disaster-prone provinces in the country.

The Luoyuan incident underscores the risks associated with construction and land-development projects in geologically sensitive areas. When earthworks alter natural slopes, the resulting instability can turn ordinary rainfall into a catastrophic failure of the hillside.

## What's Next

Rescue teams remain at the site, and the county has said it will continue search-and-rescue operations while investigating the cause. A key question is whether all three missing people will be located, and whether the land-leveling project will be scrutinized for its role in the collapse.

As of the latest official statements, authorities have not identified the missing individuals or provided an estimate of total economic losses. Further updates are expected as the investigation proceeds and rescuers continue to work through the debris, with the county's emergency command serving as the primary source of official information.
