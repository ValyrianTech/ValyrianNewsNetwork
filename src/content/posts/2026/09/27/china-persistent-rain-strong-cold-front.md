# China Braces for Persistent Rain and a Strong Cold Front

Persistent overcast and rainy weather continued across a broad band stretching from western China to the Jianghuai region on Sunday, prompting warnings about cumulative rainfall and disaster risk, while forecasters tracked a powerful cold air mass expected to sweep south around the National Day holiday.

According to [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/politics/20260927/3bd2a3de6e454dd094044157486a5ac3/c.html), citing the China Meteorological Administration's public service portal China Weather Web, the rain-prone pattern would persist from September 27 to 30 across Sichuan, Chongqing, Shaanxi, Hubei, Henan, Anhui, Jiangsu, Shanghai and Zhejiang.

## A Familiar Autumnal Pattern

The rainfall is a manifestation of the West China autumn rain (华西秋雨), a recurring climatological feature that typically runs from September to October and can settle over the Sichuan Basin and the upper-middle Yangtze for extended periods. Because the rain often falls steadily over many days rather than in short bursts, cumulative totals can climb quickly and raise the risk of secondary disasters such as landslides and mountain torrents.

That risk was already evident earlier in the month. On September 19, the Ministry of Water Resources and the China Meteorological Administration jointly issued a blue-level mountain torrent warning for parts of northeastern Sichuan, northern Chongqing and southern Shaanxi, as [China News](https://www.chinanews.com.cn/sh/2026/09-19/10699591.shtml) reported. The new forecast warns of the same hazard returning as fresh rain falls on already saturated ground.

## Rainfall Totals and Regional Risk

Over the coming days, the heaviest accumulations are expected in southern Shaanxi, the northeastern Sichuan Basin, western Hubei, southern Anhui and southern Jiangsu, where 80 to 120 millimeters (3.1 to 4.7 inches) are forecast. Locally, the northeastern Sichuan Basin could exceed 200 millimeters (7.9 inches).

Sunday's forecast called for moderate-to-heavy rain across Jilin, Liaoning, Qinghai, Gansu, Shaanxi, Sichuan, Chongqing, Henan, Hubei, Anhui, Jiangsu, Shanghai, Zhejiang and Hainan, with localized rainstorms in northeastern Chongqing, northern and western Hubei, central Anhui, southern Jiangsu, northern Zhejiang and central Hainan. The rain is expected to intensify on Monday, when rainstorm-to-torrential rain is forecast for southern Shaanxi, northern Chongqing, western Hubei, western Anhui, northern Zhejiang and Hainan.

The preceding day offered a preview of the system's intensity. Parts of Jilin, Liaoning, Hebei, Beijing, Tianjin, Hubei, Chongqing, Henan, Anhui, Jiangsu, Zhejiang and Hainan saw heavy to torrential rain, with localized downpours in Zhenjiang, Yangzhou, Taizhou, Nanjing and Nantong in Jiangsu. Thunderstorm gales of force 8 to 10, locally above force 11, struck parts of Shanxi, Hebei, Beijing and Tianjin.

## Southern Heat Persists Before the Shift

While the north and center contend with rain, the subtropical high has kept southern China hot and humid. Zhejiang, Fujian, Jiangxi, southeastern Hunan, northern Guangxi and central and eastern Guangdong may record four to five high-temperature days, [Sina Finance](https://finance.sina.com.cn/jjxw/2026-09-27/doc-initfpai9767277.shtml) reported, citing the same China Weather Web forecast. Fuzhou is forecast to reach highs of 37 to 38 degrees Celsius (99 to 100 Fahrenheit) for four consecutive days, with lows of 26 to 27 degrees Celsius (79 to 81 Fahrenheit).

## The Cold Front on the Horizon

The more consequential shift is expected late this month. A large cold vortex is forecast to form near Lake Baikal and move east and south, potentially delivering China's strongest cold air mass of the second half of the year. Around National Day, northeastern China, northern China and the middle-lower Yangtze are expected to set new post-summer low temperatures.

Harbin's high on September 30 is forecast to fall to 14 degrees Celsius (57 Fahrenheit), with a low of just 4 degrees Celsius (39 Fahrenheit). Eastern Inner Mongolia and Heilongjiang may briefly experience early-winter conditions, while northern areas will feel a deeper autumn chill.

## Analysis: A Season in Transition

The juxtaposition of persistent southern heat and an incoming northern cold surge reflects a classic late-September meteorological shift. As the subtropical high gradually retreats, cold air from Siberia and the Lake Baikal region pushes south, remaking the temperature map within days.

The timing is significant. The cold front's arrival around the National Day holiday — one of China's busiest travel periods — could affect road conditions and outdoor plans across the north, while the continued rain poses a direct challenge to the autumn harvest. Prolonged overcast rain (连阴雨) during this critical period can waterlog fields and complicate the drying and collection of crops, a risk agricultural authorities have flagged in similar years.

For now, the guidance from forecasters is straightforward: residents in the rain belt should avoid mountainous and disaster-prone areas, and those in the path of the cold front should watch short-term forecasts and prepare for a sharp drop in temperature.

## What's Next

The rain is expected to persist across West China and the Jianghuai region through September 30, with authorities monitoring for overlapping rainfall and flood risk. All eyes will then turn north and east as the cold front arrives, bringing what may be the most pronounced autumn chill of the year and, in some areas, a first taste of winter.
