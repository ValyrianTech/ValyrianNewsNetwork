# White House Leaves CNN Off Air Force One in Media Curbs

The White House signaled a new media restriction late Friday by leaving CNN off Air Force One for President Donald Trump's Saturday trip to Knoxville, Tennessee, replacing the network in the travel pool with the conservative outlet Real America's Voice News, according to [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/09/26/g-s1-145142/cnn-left-off-air-force-one). The exclusion is the latest escalation in an ongoing confrontation between the administration and the press corps over access and pool coverage, carrying significant First Amendment implications.

The move came just days after journalists from CNN, MS NOW and Politico returned to White House grounds with cameras and microphones in hand following a federal court ruling that temporarily blocked their removal.

## A Reprieve, Then a New Restriction

Trump is scheduled to attend a college football game between the University of Tennessee and the University of Texas at midday Saturday. The White House confirmed CNN's exclusion by not listing the network on the guidance sent to the press corps regarding the president's schedule, as [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/sep/25/white-house-cnn-air-force-one) reported. The network's exclusion was first reported by the Washington Post.

The seat meant for CNN on the flight has long been reserved for a member of the U.S. network video pool, of which Real America's Voice News is not a part. The pool consists of five major U.S. broadcast outlets that take turns shooting and sharing video coverage of the president. When the White House banned CNN earlier, the other four pool providers declined to participate in a show of solidarity.

That solidarity briefly fractured into routine on Friday, when the network video pool resumed operation for the first time since the ban. CNN aired live video of Trump and Chinese President Xi Jinping touring the National Archives alongside Melania Trump and Peng Liyuan, Xi's wife.

"As of now, all systems are go," said a person familiar with the pool, speaking on condition of anonymity because of the fluidity of the situation.

## A Judge's Narrow Ruling

U.S. District Judge Timothy Kelly temporarily struck down the White House ban early Thursday, ordering the administration to immediately restore access for CNN, MS NOW and Politico. According to [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/24/us-judge-blocks-trumps-white-house-ban-on-cnn-ms-now-and-politico), Kelly ruled that the outlets were likely to prevail in their legal challenge on several grounds, including that the ban violated their due process rights, and that they would suffer "irreparable harm" without the temporary order.

Kelly, who was nominated by Trump during his first term in 2017, also found a public interest in blocking the ban "given the importance of the free press." He imposed a 14-day injunction pending further proceedings. Rejecting the administration's claim that the outlets were barred to protect national security, Kelly said the government's argument lacked "factual support."

"Defendants offer no declarations or other support – classified or unclassified – explaining how any of the reporting they identified in their letters to Plaintiffs damaged the national security," Kelly wrote.

However, Kelly's order addressed only "hard passes" to White House grounds and said nothing directly about pool duties or Air Force One. Lawyers for the outlets argue the ruling reaches further. Theodore Boutrous Jr., the attorney representing the networks, said on "The Lead with Jake Tapper" that the plaintiffs believe the decision also covers the issue of pool access.

"We greatly appreciate the court's swift action," Boutrous said in a statement welcoming the ruling as a vindication of the "freedom of the press, due process and the rule of law."

The White House did not immediately respond to requests for comment from multiple outlets.

## Rising Tensions and a Broader Pattern

The press pool was created decades ago by the White House Correspondents' Association to ensure a rotating roster of journalists always travels with the president, distributing reports and imagery to the broader press corps to hold the chief executive accountable. Since the start of Trump's second term in 2025, the White House has decided which outlets cover the president via the pool each day — a task previously coordinated by the association.

Trump has increasingly targeted outlets whose coverage irritates him. Early last year he banished AP reporters from White House events over the organization's refusal to fully adopt his renaming of the Gulf of Mexico; a federal district judge ruled in AP's favor, and that case is on appeal. In joint filings, CNN, MS NOW and Politico argued the White House ban violated the First Amendment.

Days after the ban was announced, the White House television pool issued a joint statement: "The public has a vital interest in receiving accurate, independent information about its government. No administration should restrict a news organization because it objects to its reporting."

CNN media analyst Brian Stelter noted that CNN had a pool assignment at the National Archives event and that the White House had approved the network's credentials — underscoring the fluidity of the situation.

Early Saturday, Trump posted on Truth Social, as [the New York Post](https://nypost.com/2026/09/25/us-news/cnn-left-off-air-force-one-for-trumps-trip-to-tennessee-as-white-house-installs-new-media-restriction/) reported: "Why should perpetrators of FAKE NEWS ... be allowed access to a very sacred place, the White House? Despite my big Election Win, almost 100% of 'TRUMP' coverage is negative, and has been for years!" The post singled out CNN and MS NOW, referring to the latter as "MSDNC."

## What to Watch

The Air Force One exclusion raises an unresolved legal question: whether a court order restoring access to White House grounds also compels the administration to preserve pool travel duties. That ambiguity is now the central battleground, with Kelly's 14-day injunction set to expire absent further proceedings.

With a recent Reuters/Ipsos poll placing Trump's approval at an all-time low of 32%, the administration's posture toward the press shows no sign of softening. The AP's separate exclusion case remains on appeal, and the outcome of both disputes could set lasting precedent on how much control a president may exert over who covers him — and how.

For now, the network pool's participation remains uncertain, and CNN's absence from Air Force One stands as a visible marker of how far the dispute has traveled beyond the briefing room.
