# Yangtze Delta Rail Hits Return Peak: 3.32 Million

Railways across China's Yangtze River Delta entered their return-trip peak on Sunday, with an estimated 3.32 million passengers expected to travel as the three-day Mid-Autumn Festival short holiday drew to a close, according to the regional operator China Railway Shanghai Group.

The figure marks the culmination of a busy holiday travel period for one of the country's most densely used high-speed rail corridors, which spans Shanghai and the provinces of Jiangsu, Zhejiang and Anhui. On the previous day, 26 September, the group dispatched 2.808 million passengers — a rise of 27.8% compared with the same day a year earlier, [The Paper](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_34159137) reported, citing the operator.

## A Holiday Wave Built on Visiting, Tourism and Students

The surge reflects the familiar rhythm of China's short-holiday travel calendar, in which outbound crowds early in the break give way to a concentrated homeward rush on the final day. This year's Mid-Autumn Festival short holiday ran from 25 to 27 September, with the festival day itself falling on 25 September and the return peak landing on the closing day, when travellers headed home.

Demand was spread across the region's core cities. According to The Paper, Shanghai, Nanjing, Hangzhou and Hefei saw strong demand for trips to secondary hubs including Suzhou, Yangzhou, Xuzhou, Lianyungang, Nantong, Ningbo, Jinhua, Huangshan, Wuhu and Fuyang. Ticket pre-sale data as of 8 a.m. on Sunday showed tight capacity on longer routes from the delta toward Beijing, Wuhan, Shenyang, Changsha, Nanchang and Xi'an, while some off-peak time slots still had seats available.

## Operators Add Capacity to Match Demand

To absorb the return wave, China Railway Shanghai Group activated its peak operating timetable and moved to expand capacity further. It planned to operate 281 electric multiple-unit (EMU) trainsets in re-coupled formation — linking two shorter units into a single longer train to double seating — and to attach 94 additional conventional passenger cars, The Paper reported.

The operator also added 11 extra train pairs, concentrating on short-haul intra-regional routes. These included two conventional pairs each between Fuyang and Hefei and between Xuzhou and Nanjing, plus four EMU pairs on the busy Hangzhou–Shanghai Hongqiao corridor and single pairs connecting Shengzhou Xinchang, Hengdian and Huai'an East with Shanghai Hongqiao.

## A New Line Opens the Day After the Peak

Just as the holiday rush eases, the region's network is set to expand. The Xiong'an–Shangqiu section of the Beijing–Hong Kong High-Speed Railway was scheduled to open on 28 September, the day after the peak. According to The Paper, the new corridor is expected to strengthen the network effect of high-speed rail and boost Beijing-bound capacity from several Anhui localities — with Jiuhuashan, Lujiang, Shou County, Tongcheng and Bozhou potentially receiving direct high-speed service to the capital for the first time.

## Broader Picture: A Network Under Steady Pressure

The return peak is the latest in a series of high-volume days for the delta network. Holiday-period transport opened on 23 September, when the network dispatched 2.239 million passengers, up 22.1% year-on-year, according to [Huanqiu](https://hqtime.huanqiu.com/article/4TL4o54xd9K), citing CCTV News. That same day, the operator announced the first-ever dedicated "tide-watching" trains to Haining, running through 7 October.

Volume climbed quickly from there. On 24 September the network was expected to dispatch about 3.38 million passengers, Huanqiu reported. Citing CCTV, [21st Century Business Herald](https://m.21jingji.com/article/20260925/herald/1a789de1275b43a7efc3f6692ad6b227_zaker.html) noted that 24 September drew 3.244 million passengers in practice, and that the network was expected to dispatch roughly 4.18 million on 25 September — the first day of the mini-holiday and Mid-Autumn Festival day itself — a jump of 116% from a year earlier.

The scale is consistent with the network's steady growth trajectory. Comparable return peaks in recent years have hovered in the 3.5 million range for major holiday periods, underscoring the Yangtze Delta's role as the busiest node in China's high-speed rail system.

## What to Watch

With the Mid-Autumn break over, attention turns to the next major travel window — the forthcoming National Day holiday season, when the region's railways typically face sustained multi-day pressure. The opening of the Xiong'an–Shangqiu line, and three other new high-speed sections announced by China State Railway Group, may help relieve some of that load by adding fresh capacity to the national grid.

For travelers, the operator's advisory remains straightforward: prepare valid identification in advance, confirm the correct station name on tickets — many cities have more than one — arrive early, and follow staff guidance to keep the return flow orderly.
