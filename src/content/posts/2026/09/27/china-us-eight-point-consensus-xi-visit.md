# China, US Seal Eight-Point Consensus After Xi Visit

China and the United States reached an eight-point consensus on outcomes during President Xi Jinping's state visit to Washington, capping a three-day trip that Chinese state media framed as a historic step toward stabilizing the world's most consequential bilateral relationship.

The visit, which ran from September 23 to 25, was made at the invitation of US President Donald Trump. During the trip, the two heads of state held what officials described as in-depth exchanges on the China-US constructive strategic stability relationship and major international and regional issues, according to [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/world/20260926/2f8689144e21400f8545c99db05f0d12/c.html), which published the full text of the consensus.

## The Eight Points

The consensus, also carried by the Chinese government's official portal [gov.cn](https://www.gov.cn/yaowen/liebiao/202609/content_7082158.htm), spans diplomacy, trade, security and technology.

At its core, both sides agreed to build a "China-US constructive strategic stability relationship" based on respect, fairness and reciprocity — language first introduced during Trump's reciprocal visit to China in May 2026. The two presidents also agreed to support each other in hosting the APEC and G20 leaders' summits, with each intending to attend the meetings hosted by the other.

On trade, the two sides recognized the positive role of the China-US trade and economic consultation mechanism, including the establishment of a Trade Council, a USD 30 billion reciprocal tariff reduction arrangement, and an extension of the Kuala Lumpur trade consultation outcomes — and instructed that these be implemented.

The remaining points covered cooperation on counter-narcotics enforcement, in which recent joint operations cracked multiple cases involving new psychoactive substances and precursor chemicals, resulting in dozens of arrests in both countries; the establishment of a China-US AI dialogue, with the next round set for November and a communication channel for AI incidents; and a shared position that Iran should fulfill its commitment not to develop nuclear weapons, and that no country or institution may impose transit fees on international waterways.

Two of the points carried symbolic weight. The two presidents recalled that China and the US were allies in World War II, and the US welcomed the arrival of a pair of giant pandas leased by China to Zoo Atlanta. In addition, the two militaries agreed to sign as soon as possible a memorandum of understanding on strengthening crisis communication and prevention, and to continue cooperating in the search for the remains of missing US servicemen in China.

## Six Moments of a Historic Visit

Chinese state media gave extensive coverage to the pageantry surrounding the visit. In a "First Look" feature, [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/politics/leaders/20260927/0bdc8e1bb4614dcba20f743243602497/c.html) highlighted six memorable moments, led by Trump's decision to greet Xi personally at Andrews Air Force Base — the first time a US president has met a Chinese head of state at the airport.

Xi and his wife, Peng Liyuan, arrived on the afternoon of September 23. A military band played both national anthems and a 21-gun salute rang out. The two heads of state realized mutual visits within half a year, which Xinhua described as unprecedented in the history of China-US relations.

The following morning, Trump held a grand welcome ceremony at the White House, where Xi announced that China would invite 100,000 American youth to China over the next five years for exchanges and study, and that giant pandas Pingping and Fushuang would soon settle in Zoo Atlanta. The two leaders then held talks in the Oval Office, followed by a state dinner in the East Room decorated in Chinese red, where Xi recounted stories of people-to-people friendship between the two nations.

On September 25, the two presidents and their wives held a tea meeting at the White House, agreeing to further enrich the positioning of the relationship, before jointly visiting the US National Archives — an arrangement Xinhua compared to Xi's invitation to Trump to visit the Temple of Heaven in May, describing both as reflecting mutual respect for each other's history and culture.

## Beyond the Pageantry

Xi's remarks emphasized a balance between cooperation and competition. "We must turn this vision into action, strive toward a direction of cooperation as the mainstay, competition within bounds, controllable differences, and peace in prospect, and walk out a correct way for two major countries to get along," he said, according to Xinhua. He added that "realizing the great rejuvenation of the Chinese nation and making America great again are not incompatible."

Trump, for his part, called the visit "constructive and very successful," saying both sides conducted in-depth communication and achieved "many positive results that benefit both countries."

Yet analysts cautioned that the headline consensus papered over unresolved differences. In an analysis for [BBC Chinese](https://www.bbc.com/zhongwen/articles/cq2dwkw2j0r4o/simp), scholars said pressing issues such as artificial intelligence, rare earths and Taiwan saw no clear progress.

Yang Haowei, a senior Northeast Asia analyst at the International Crisis Group, told BBC Chinese that the meeting's most important achievement was that both sides jointly created a "positive and friendly atmosphere and image," while noting a "slight gap" between the messages the two leaders conveyed. He argued that Xi is gradually gaining the upper hand in the relationship, with the high-specification reception allowing Xi to show audiences at home and abroad that China and the US are essentially on an equal footing.

Dr. Huang Yuxun, an assistant professor of philosophy at the University of Hong Kong, said the focus of the meeting was not policy breakthroughs but the personal relationship between the two leaders, adding that rare earths, AI governance and trade involve territorial and great-power strategic issues that require both teams to negotiate later.

Weng Luzhong, an associate professor of political science at Sam Houston State University in Texas, said that with China controlling important resources such as rare earths, the high-specification US reception was intended to create a good atmosphere. He described the trade truce extension — announced by US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, who said the one-year truce would be extended by another two months — as "already a good result," with details left for future meetings.

## What's Next

The consensus and the warm atmosphere point toward a busy diplomatic calendar. The two presidents are expected to meet again at the APEC summit in Shenzhen in November and the G20 summit in December, where the harder details of trade, technology and security cooperation may be tackled.

The AI dialogue is slated to convene in November, alongside the establishment of a channel to communicate about AI incidents — a notable development given the intense competition between the two countries in the sector.

Still, Xi himself acknowledged the road ahead. "The future of China-US relations is bright; this is the historical trend," he said. "At the same time, the road to brightness is often tortuous; there will always be various difficulties and challenges." Whether the eight points translate into durable progress — or remain a well-choreographed diplomatic moment — will be tested in the months ahead.
