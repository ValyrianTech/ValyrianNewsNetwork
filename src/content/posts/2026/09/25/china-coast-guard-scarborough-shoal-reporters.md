# China Coast Guard Patrols Scarborough Shoal With Reporters Aboard

The China Coast Guard conducted a comprehensive law enforcement patrol inside the lagoon of Scarborough Shoal on September 25, bringing state television journalists into the atoll's so-called "core waters" for the first time - a move analysts read as a deliberate escalation of Beijing's messaging in the disputed South China Sea.

According to [The Paper](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_34148723), which carried the report credited to CCTV News, a flotilla including the coast guard vessels Wanshan and Chuanshan operated inside the Huangyan Island National Nature Reserve, the marine protected area China designated in September 2025.

## What the Report Said

CCTV correspondent Wu Bing broadcast from the lagoon itself, describing it as "the water body naturally enclosed by the outer reef ring of Huangyan Dao - this is also the core waters of Huangyan Island."

Wu reported that enforcement boats were patrolling for vessel activity and investigating illegal acts that damage coral reefs and rare-species habitats. Officers were also tasked with observing coral growth to supply ecological baseline data to government departments, he said.

Coast guard officer Chen Chen said the operation would target "activities such as harvesting coral and entering the protected area without approval." A second officer aboard the Wanshan, Huang Minghai, was quoted directing the flotilla to "strengthen maritime observation and lookout."

The report closed with a statement that the coast guard would "resolutely safeguard national territorial sovereignty and maritime rights and interests, and protect the Huangyan Island National Nature Reserve."

## A Multi-Layered Campaign

The patrol is the latest step in a sustained Chinese campaign at Scarborough Shoal - known in the Philippines as Bajo de Masinloc and Panatag Shoal - that has combined legal designation, administrative rulemaking, military signaling, and now media access.

The shoal is a coral atoll with an inner lagoon, roughly 220 kilometers west of the Philippine island of Luzon. China, Taiwan, and the Philippines all claim the feature, and Beijing's coast guard has maintained a persistent presence around it in recent years, with patrols regulating access at the lagoon entrance.

In 2016, an UNCLOS tribunal ruled that China's historic-rights claims within the nine-dash line had no lawful effect and held that Scarborough Shoal is a "rock" generating no exclusive economic zone, while affirming traditional fishing rights for both Filipino and Chinese artisanal fishers. It did not rule on sovereignty.

## Escalating Presence

Chinese activity at the shoal has intensified sharply over the past two years. Data compiled by the [CSIS Asia Maritime Transparency Initiative](https://amti.csis.org/occupational-risk-chinese-presence-surges-at-scarborough-shoal/) describes the coast guard as "the backbone of Beijing's efforts to exercise control over the shoal," and shows its patrols reached 933 ship-days in the first half of 2026 alone - nearly matching the full-year 2025 total of 1,099 ship-days, which had itself doubled from 516 in 2024. Monthly patrol volume rose to an average of 156 ship-days, peaking at 216 in May.

That tracking also documented 112 days in the first half of 2026 on which interactions were observed between Chinese coast guard vessels and Philippine coast guard or fisheries ships, an average of 19 days per month.

On August 1, 2026, the multi-department "Huangyan Island National Nature Reserve Management Measures" took effect. The same day, the PLA Southern Theatre Command conducted joint air and naval combat drills around the shoal, and a coast guard spokesperson announced a rights-protection drill focused on boarding, intercepting intrusions, and forced towing, as [The Straits Times](https://www.straitstimes.com/asia/se-asia/china-conducts-naval-air-patrols-around-disputed-shoal-in-south-china-sea) reported. [GMA Network](https://www.gmanetwork.com/news/topstories/nation/996999/china-conducts-naval-air-patrols-around-scarborough-shoal/story/) carried a parallel account noting that unauthorized fishing, mining, and coral extraction are now banned within the reserve.

## The Nature Reserve Dispute

China's September 2025 designation of the nature reserve drew an immediate Philippine protest. Manila's Department of Foreign Affairs filed what it called a "strong, unequivocal, and formal articulation" of its objections, arguing that it holds sovereignty and jurisdiction over the feature, according to the [Philippine Daily Inquirer](https://www.inquirer.net/455037/dfa-files-unequivocal-protest-vs-chinas-nature-reserve-in-west-ph-sea/).

The United States backed the Philippine position. US Secretary of State Marco Rubio rejected Beijing's plan, calling it "yet another coercive attempt to advance sweeping territorial and maritime claims in the South China Sea at the expense of its neighbors" and urging China to abide by the 2016 arbitral ruling.

## Analysis: Conservation as Jurisdiction

Framing the patrol as ecological enforcement within a protected area is a legal-communications strategy: it recasts Chinese activity as conservation administration rather than a sovereignty assertion, making international criticism harder to frame.

The decision to place a state journalist physically inside the lagoon for the first time is the more consequential signal. It visually projects Chinese jurisdiction over the shoal's innermost waters and normalizes the nature-reserve framing for a domestic and international audience - without crossing the traditional red lines of land reclamation or permanent construction.

That distinction matters. CSIS analysts noted that Beijing is "employing new methods to enhance its physical presence and establish new patterns of (nominally) civilian activity at the shoal - all without crossing the traditional red lines of building a permanent structure or conducting land reclamation."

## What to Watch

All primary content on the September 25 patrol originates from Chinese state media, with no Philippine or independent confirmation of the specific operation as of publication. Manila has not issued a statement specific to this patrol.

The key open question is whether the lagoon entry signals preparation for more permanent activities at the shoal, or remains within China's stated boundary of no reclamation and no permanent structures. The tempo of coast guard patrols, the persistence of floating objects installed by China, and the Philippines' next diplomatic response will all indicate which direction Beijing is taking.
