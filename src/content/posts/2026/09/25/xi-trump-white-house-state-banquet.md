# Xi, Trump Hail 'Unprecedented' Ties at White House Banquet

U.S. President Donald Trump and First Lady Melania Trump hosted a welcome state banquet for Chinese President Xi Jinping and his wife, Peng Liyuan, at the White House on the evening of September 24, 2026 — the ceremonial high point of a state visit that both leaders framed as a historic milestone in China-U.S. relations.

According to [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/politics/leaders/20260925/c59121f4090d4f8ca3ff85ed311984fd/c.html), the two presidents delivered toasts, with Xi hailing the reciprocal visits of the two heads of state within less than half a year as an event "unprecedented in the history of China-U.S. relations."

## A Milestone of Reciprocal Visits

The banquet followed Trump's visit to China in May 2026, when the two leaders agreed to build what they called a "China-U.S. constructive strategic stability relationship." Xi's September 23-24 state visit to Washington completed a rare exchange of top-level visits in under six months.

Upon arrival at the White House, Xi and Peng were warmly received by Trump and the First Lady. The two couples posed for photographs and met with banquet guests before the dinner began. Senior Chinese officials Cai Qi, Wang Yi, and He Lifeng also attended.

In his toast, Xi reviewed the new consensus reached during the visit, saying it had "enriched the substance of the China-U.S. constructive strategic stability relationship and provided new strategic guidance." The full text of his remarks was published by [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/politics/leaders/20260925/72dc67dd400e414fbf048afc362aeeff/c.html), and the state visit was also covered by China's [State Council](https://www.gov.cn/yaowen/liebiao/202609/content_7082063.htm).

Xi extended Mid-Autumn Festival greetings to guests, noting that the festival — celebrated by Chinese communities around the world — is traditionally a time for reunion. Earlier in the day, he had held formal talks with Trump at the White House, where China's [Ministry of Foreign Affairs](https://www.mfa.gov.cn/web/wjdt_674879/gjldrhd_674881/202609/t20260925_12031134.shtml) said the two sides reached a new joint arrangement on trade and agreed to continue dialogue on artificial intelligence.

## Echoes of History in a Single Toast

Xi's banquet remarks drew deliberately on a series of historical touchstones to frame the relationship. He recalled that more than 200 years ago, at the dawn of American independence, the United States "turned its gaze toward China," and that President George Washington purchased and collected large quantities of Chinese porcelain. More than 80 years ago, he said, the Chinese and American peoples fought "shoulder to shoulder" against Japanese militarism, opening the famous Hump airlift route and forging a friendship "tempered in blood and fire."

The Chinese leader also invoked ping-pong diplomacy of more than 50 years ago, crediting Mao Zedong, Richard Nixon, and statesmen of that era with the political wisdom of "a small ball turning the big ball" — a reference to how table-tennis exchanges helped open the long-closed door to normalized relations. He noted that a special state gift from President Nixon, a redwood sequoia, was planted in Zhejiang Province, where Xi once worked.

More recently, Xi recounted the story of an American named S. who, more than 20 years ago, funded Chinese desert-control worker Yin Yuzhen to plant trees — a project the American later returned to see transformed into a lush forest. Xi called the story "a fine example of friendly exchanges between the Chinese and American peoples."

In his own toast, Trump said the warmth and friendliness of the Chinese people during his May visit had left a deep impression on him. "Although our systems differ, the friendship between the United States and China has endured," he said, according to Xinhua's account of his remarks. Trump added that he was willing to work with Xi, uphold mutual respect, and "bring lasting prosperity and security to future generations." In remarks reported by [Guancha](https://www.guancha.cn/GuoJi·ZhanLue/2026_09_25_902150.shtml), Trump had earlier thanked Xi and Peng for making "this historic visit" and welcomed them "to America."

## A Banquet That Bridged Two Culinary Traditions

The White House arranged the dinner, held in the East Room and organized by the First Lady's office, to blend American ingredients with Chinese elements. Citing the White House, [Guancha reported](https://www.guancha.cn/GuoJi·ZhanLue/2026_09_25_902139.shtml) that guests were first offered a sparkling wine from California's Schramsberg Winery, a deliberate tribute to the historic "Toast to Peace" made during President Nixon's 1972 visit to China with Premier Zhou Enlai.

The three-course menu featured a yellow squash velouté with wild mushrooms and scallion oil; sesame-crusted sea bass with braised baby bok choy and roasted eggplant — the dish most clearly reflecting Chinese influences — and a vanilla cream dessert with White House honey ice cream and sour cherry jam. Red tablecloths and red flowers, including dahlias and ranunculus, dominated the decor, echoing the significance of red in Chinese culture.

American artists performed during the evening, including tenor Christopher Macchio and ensembles of the U.S. Marine Corps Band and Army Chorus. Guests numbered more than 130, according to the New York Times as cited by [Guancha](https://www.guancha.cn/GuoJi·ZhanLue/2026_09_25_902147.shtml), and included a striking roster of U.S. technology and business leaders: Amazon's Jeff Bezos, Tesla's Elon Musk, Meta's Mark Zuckerberg, Google's Sundar Pichai, Nvidia's Jensen Huang, AMD's Lisa Su, and OpenAI's Sam Altman. Executives from Pfizer, LVMH, Goldman Sachs, General Motors, and Boeing were also present.

## Analysis: Signals Beyond Ceremony

The spectacle of the banquet carried more than symbolic weight. The rapid sequence of visits — Trump to Beijing in May, Xi to Washington in September — reflects an effort by both capitals to keep high-level channels open amid persistent friction over trade, technology, and Taiwan.

Xi's framing of the relationship as one that can manage competition while expanding cooperation — "lengthening the cooperation list and shortening the problem list," as he put it in the day's talks — suggests Beijing's preference for stability and predictability. The emphasis on people-to-people ties and shared history served to soften a relationship still defined by strategic rivalry.

The presence of so many leading American technology executives underscores the economic stakes. Both leaders acknowledged that AI cooperation would be a defining theme, with Xi saying the two countries "have competition but even more reason to cooperate" and should jointly guard against the misuse of artificial intelligence.

The leaders also confirmed they would support each other's hosting of the 2026 APEC Economic Leaders' Meeting and the G20 Summit — a signal that, despite differences, both sides see value in continued engagement on the global stage.

## What to Watch

Whether the warmth on display in the East Room translates into durable progress remains the central question. The trade arrangement announced during the visit and the agreed AI dialogue offer concrete threads to follow. Xi's public call for the United States to uphold the position of opposing "Taiwan independence" will also be watched closely as a barometer of the relationship's stability.

For now, the two leaders have set a deliberately forward-looking tone, casting more than a century of shared history as groundwork for a relationship they say can still "achieve each other." The task ahead will be converting that rhetoric into results.
