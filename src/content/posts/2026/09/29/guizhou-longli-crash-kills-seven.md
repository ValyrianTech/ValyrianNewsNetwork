# Guizhou Crash Kills 7; Driver Was Drinking, Overloaded

A pre-dawn traffic accident in Longli County, Guizhou Province, killed seven people early on 29 September, according to [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/legal/20260929/f65fc740fd4143a89a0e134483de8566/c.html). A preliminary police investigation found that the driver had been drinking and was carrying more passengers than the vehicle was rated for, authorities said.

The collision occurred at 01:42 on Longxi Avenue in Longli County, part of the Qiannan Buyei and Miao Autonomous Prefecture, the county's public security bureau said in a police bulletin. After receiving the report, the bureau activated its emergency response plan and coordinated emergency and medical teams to the scene.

## What the Investigation Found

According to the police bulletin, the driver - identified only as Wang XX, a 19-year-old man holding a C1 licence - was operating a small passenger car rated for five people but carrying seven. The car rear-ended a heavy semi-trailer tractor that was parked on the right side of the road. All seven occupants of the small car died.

Testing determined Wang's blood alcohol content to be 66mg/100ml, as reported by [Jiemian News via NetEase](https://www.163.com/dy/article/L804O7VP0534A4SC.html). Accident investigation and arrangements for the victims' families were still underway, Xinhua said.

## A Key Distinction in Chinese Law

While English-language summaries have described the case as "drunk driving," the blood alcohol figure places it in a distinct legal category under Chinese law.

Under the thresholds used in China, drink-driving covers a blood alcohol content of at least 20mg/100ml but below 80mg/100ml and is handled as an administrative offence. The more serious "drunk driving" category begins at 80mg/100ml and can carry criminal liability.

At 66mg/100ml, Wang fell into the administrative drink-driving band - a nuance that matters for how the case is legally characterised, though the consequences were tragically severe regardless. The 66mg/100ml figure, along with the time, location and overloading details, comes from the police bulletin published by [Phoenix News](https://news.ifeng.com/c/8woIg9W5EjV).

## Early Reports Differed on Details

The first wire reports on the crash carried slightly different details. An early Xinhua dispatch published by [Huanqiu](https://3w.huanqiu.com/a/78fa3c/4TP3hxetsk9) at 08:02 described the accident as occurring at "around 01:30" in the "Longshan Town area" and said the cause was under investigation. The later police bulletin provided the more specific time of 01:42 and identified the location as Longxi Avenue.

The police also issued a public appeal for road safety, urging drivers to strictly observe traffic rules and avoid speeding, overloading, fatigued driving, and drink-driving. To spare the victims' families further distress, authorities asked the public not to circulate images, video, or unverified information about the scene.

## Caught at the Start of a Holiday Travel Surge

The tragedy unfolded at the outset of China's 2026 Mid-Autumn and National Day combined holiday, a period of intense road travel. Just days earlier, on 23 September, the Guizhou Provincial Public Security Traffic Management Corps [announced nine convenience measures](https://news.qq.com/rain/a/20260923A06RL300) to smooth holiday travel - including congestion forecasting through Amap, Baidu, and Tencent maps, the flexible opening of highway emergency lanes, and lenient, warning-first handling for minor offences by non-commercial small passenger vehicles.

Crucially, the same package explicitly stated that serious violations - including drink and drunk driving, overloading, street racing, and illegal commercial operation - would continue to be strictly investigated. The Longli crash underscores why that enforcement focus persists.

## Why Overloading Matters

Overloading a small car is a recurring factor in severe Chinese road accidents. Squeezing seven people into a vehicle rated for five reduces or eliminates the protection of seatbelts for rear passengers and raises the likelihood that a single collision becomes a mass-casualty event. Combined with alcohol impairment - which slows reaction time and impairs judgment - it makes a serious outcome more probable.

## What to Watch

Investigators have not yet said whether any charges will follow or when a fuller account of the crash will be released. The case is also likely to feed into the broader holiday-period messaging on road safety, as authorities balance travel convenience against enforcement of the rules most likely to prevent fatalities.

For now, the immediate facts are stark: seven people died in a single vehicle that was both too full and driven by someone who had been drinking - a combination that Chinese traffic authorities have repeatedly identified as among the deadliest on the road.
