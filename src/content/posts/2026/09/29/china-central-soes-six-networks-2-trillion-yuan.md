# China SOEs to Invest 2 Trillion Yuan in Six Networks

China's central state-owned enterprises will arrange roughly 2 trillion yuan (about US$280 billion) in related investment this year under the annual construction plan for its flagship "Six Networks" infrastructure initiative, a senior official announced on Monday.

Pang Xiaogang, spokesperson and deputy director of the State-owned Assets Supervision and Administration Commission ([SASAC](http://www.news.cn/tech/20260929/1174acd3cd2c4dfe9ad51a7effc2821a/c.html)), told a press conference hosted by the State Council Information Office that central enterprises had been tasked with planning ahead and accelerating a series of major and landmark projects around the program, with work now "progressing smoothly."

## What the 'Six Networks' Are

The Six Networks is a strategic infrastructure program spanning six domains: water networks, a new-type power grid, a computing power network, next-generation communications networks, urban underground pipeline networks, and logistics networks.

The initiative has been elevated by both the Communist Party's Politburo and the State Council Executive Meeting. The Politburo's April 28 meeting called for strengthening the planning and construction of the Six Networks and for breaking ground on mature major projects, while a May 9 State Council meeting renewed the emphasis as part of efforts to strengthen China's domestic economic circulation, [Jiemian News](https://www.jiemian.com/article/15146716.html) reported.

## A 2 Trillion Yuan Figure — and a Larger, 7 Trillion Yuan Backdrop

SASAC's figure covers central enterprises only and is a subset of the broader national program. Separately, the National Development and Reform Commission provisionally estimates that total investment in the Six Networks and related key areas will exceed 7 trillion yuan (about US$1 trillion) in 2026 alone, according to [China Daily](https://en.qstheory.cn/2026-06/05/c_1188397.htm), citing NDRC head Zheng Shanjie. Over the full 15th Five-Year Plan period (2026–2030), the broader program is expected to concentrate tens of trillions of yuan in spending.

According to [state broadcaster-linked coverage](https://www.stnn.cc/detail/6abaf61d0b05bc616281956e.html), the sub-targets are equally large: China expects to invest more than 5 trillion yuan on its upgraded power grid during the 15th Five-Year Plan alone, and roughly 5 trillion yuan to build and renovate about 770,000 kilometers of gas, water supply, drainage and heating pipelines.

## Progress Across the Six Networks

Pang detailed the state of each network. On water, the main backbone is accelerating, with the South-to-North Water Transfer middle-route project and the Yellow River Guxian water conservancy hub advancing; central enterprises are deeply involved in 40 backbone water conveyance and drainage corridors.

On the new-type power grid, China has built what the official described as the world's largest, highest-voltage AC/DC hybrid grid with the largest new-energy integration, achieving a full-scope average power supply reliability of 99.948 percent. On computing, central enterprises have cumulatively built and put into operation six domestic 10,000-card intelligent computing clusters, and are piloting "compute-electricity coordination" projects.

In communications, the country now operates 4.83 million 5G base stations, with 5G-Advanced commercial networks covering more than 330 cities, and satellite internet operating capability initially in place. On urban underground pipelines, landmark projects such as the Xiong'an New Area integrated utility corridor are being built and operated. On logistics, central enterprises are helping establish a national-level logistics big-data platform; the Three Gorges Water Transport New Channel has broken ground, and the Pinglu Canal fully opened to navigation on September 16.

## Healthier Balance Sheets Behind the Push

The investment push comes as central enterprises report improving fundamentals. Wang Shaofei, director of SASAC's Financial Supervision and Operations Evaluation Bureau, said that in the first eight months of 2026 central enterprises recorded cumulative added value of 7.2 trillion yuan and total profit of 1.8 trillion yuan, paying nearly 1.8 trillion yuan in taxes and fees — up 4.4 percent year on year. Total assets reached 99.3 trillion yuan as of end-August, up 4.2 percent, a figure [Lianhe Zaobao](https://www.zaobao.com.sg/news/china/story20260928-9749420) also highlighted.

Operating cash flow net reached 2.2 trillion yuan, up 22.1 percent, while the operating revenue profit margin rose 0.2 percentage points to 7.5 percent. Research and development spending reached 591.23 billion yuan, up 2.7 percent, on track to exceed 1 trillion yuan for a fifth consecutive year.

The Six Networks investment is paired with the central enterprises' newly issued 15th Five-Year Plan, which sets a goal of raising the added-value share of strategic emerging industries by another 5 percentage points by 2030. The plan de-emphasizes revenue and scale indicators in favor of added value and includes 106 industrial development actions.

SASAC is also driving an "AI+" agenda. It has published 100 benchmark strategic high-value scenarios across 20 industry categories and pooled more than 1,000 high-quality datasets through four data industry communities in energy, transportation, low-carbon and finance, [Yicai](https://www.yicai.com/news/103380399.html) reported. A "Scenario Myriad" special action launched in July 2026 aims to accelerate the cultivation and opening of application scenarios.

## Four Priorities Going Forward

Pang outlined four priority work areas. First, on investment traction, the Six Networks will be used to orderly expand infrastructure investment. Second, on coordinated networking, SASAC will strengthen the "three-network integration" of the computing network with the new-type power grid and next-generation communications network, while raising the digital and intelligent level of the water, underground pipeline and logistics networks. Third, on safety support, the concept of safe development will be embedded across the full lifecycle of project planning, construction and operation. Fourth, on co-building and sharing, central enterprises will act as operators, service providers and system integrators, working with all types of enterprises.

## Analysis: Strategic Upgrade — and Real Risks

Analysts describe the Six Networks as a strategic upgrade over traditional infrastructure. Guo Yingfeng, an associate researcher at the China Center for International Economic Exchanges, wrote in the [21st Century Business Herald](https://www.21jingji.com/article/20260513/789c5bed86ba69be37fa2c64a1168214.html) that the program expands coverage from single transport or water projects to all-factor domains, shifts strategic focus from pure growth to coordinating security and development, and moves investment value from physical scale to system resilience.

With infrastructure investment up 8.9 percent year on year in the first quarter of 2026, Guo said the Six Networks became the main driver of a V-shaped rebound. But he flagged three challenges: funding constraints as local-government debt management tightens, weak network coordination across fragmented domains, and extensive operations that still lag in digital and intelligent sophistication — along with structural problems such as "valuing above-ground over underground" and "valuing construction over operation."

On the corporate front, the investment wave is expected to ripple across sectors. Zhou Wei, managing director of China Investment Consulting Co., cited demand for steel, pipes and construction machinery from upgrades, while Wang Xiaojie, chief domestic policy analyst at Western Securities, said that in the short term hardware and some software investments carry "the certainty of incremental investment."

## What to Watch

Several questions remain. SASAC did not say how much of the 2 trillion yuan is new versus previously budgeted, nor what economic multiplier it expects. How social capital will be mobilized and how cross-regional and cross-industry coordination mechanisms will be completed are open issues that could shape how quickly the Six Networks take physical form. For now, the program stands as one of the clearest expressions of China's state-led effort to steady investment and build a technology base for its next phase of growth.
