# Belgian Brewers Voice Concern Over Stricter Advertising Rules During Brussels Beer Weekend

As the 26th edition of the Belgian Beer Weekend opened Friday evening at the Grand-Place in Brussels, the festive atmosphere was tinged with unease. Approximately 50 brewers are pouring around 500 varieties of beer at the three-day event, which also marks the 10th anniversary of UNESCO's recognition of Belgian beer culture as intangible cultural heritage. Yet behind the celebration, industry leaders are voicing significant concern about the federal government's plans to tighten alcohol advertising regulations, according to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/09/05/ongerustheid-bij-brouwers-tijdens-het-belgian-beer-weekend-bruss/).

## A Festival of Heritage and Concern

This year's edition is notably expanded. For the first time, a [Belgian Beer Culture Bar](https://www.belgianbeerculturedays.be/belgian-beer-culture-bar) opened for 10 days at the nearby Beursplein as part of the Belgian Beer Culture Days running from September 4 to 13. The celebration extends beyond the Grand-Place to pubs nationwide, marking a decade since UNESCO inscribed Belgian beer culture on its Representative List of Intangible Cultural Heritage.

"We are incredibly proud to welcome tens of thousands of beer lovers here every year," said Krishan Maudgal, CEO of the Belgian Brewers federation. "They come to enjoy the cozy atmosphere and especially to experience the beer culture, which is truly special." Last year's event attracted approximately 70,000 visitors to the capital.

## The Advertising Dispute

Despite the festivities, Maudgal acknowledged that the brewing industry is "particularly restless and uncertain" over the government's plan to change the mandatory health warning on alcohol advertising from "Alcohol abuse harms health" to "Alcohol harms health." The change, recommended by the High Health Council based on recent scientific research showing that any alcohol consumption carries some health risk, was agreed by the federal Council of Ministers on March 27.

On August 26, 100 brewers and approximately 1,000 other sector stakeholders published a full-page open letter in Belgian newspapers opposing the new slogan. The [Belgian Brewers federation](https://belgianbrewers.be/kenniscentrum-publiek/actualiteit/over-nieuwe-gezondheidsmededeling-alcoholreclame/) argues that the new wording shifts the focus from combating abuse to stigmatizing all alcohol consumption.

"With the new text, the Minister of Public Health shifts the focus from combating misuse to problematizing and stigmatizing every form of alcohol use," Maudgal said. "We do not believe this unnuanced message will contribute to consumer awareness or correct information." He warned that the change "creates a kind of slippery slope," adding: "If this ideological paradigm shift is written into Belgian legislation, we know what awaits us. We risk throwing the baby out with the bathwater."

Bart Cuypers of Brouwerij Het Nest in Oud-Turnhout was more blunt, telling [RTV](https://www.rtv.be/actualiteit-en-nieuws/8-brouwerijen-uit-kempen-en-rivierenland-steunen-open-brief-tegen) that "with the new rules, we are exactly being treated like drug dealers." Eight breweries from the Kempen and Rivierenland regions, including Duvel Moortgat and Het Anker, signed the open letter.

## The Health Perspective

Public health officials defend the measure. "Alcohol is harmful. And we all actually know that," said Minister of Public Health Frank Vandenbroucke. "It increases the risk of serious cancers and can lead to severe addictions, with much family suffering as a result." The new rules also include a ban on free alcohol distribution with magazines and a ban on influencers and media with large young audiences advertising alcoholic beverages.

Frieda Matthys, chair of the High Health Council's Permanent Working Group on Mental Health, rejected the brewers' characterization. "From more recent scientific research we know that any use of alcohol carries some risk," she said in an interview with [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/08/26/slogans-discussie-alcohol-gezondheid/). "Abuse is always harmful, even abuse of water. So saying that alcohol abuse is harmful is redundant." Matthys suggested the industry's strong reaction indicates the new message will have impact: "Otherwise they wouldn't react so panickedly and mobilize their entire base."

Katleen Peleman, director of the Flemish Expertise Centre on Alcohol and Other Drugs (VAD), added that "alcohol is not a harmless product. It causes a lot of damage. The broad societal problem lies precisely in normalization, in how self-evident alcohol is in daily life."

## Economic Stakes and Industry Challenges

The brewing sector generates nearly 4 billion euros in revenue, contributes about 1 percent to Belgium's GDP, directly employs 6,605 people, and supports nearly 50,000 indirect jobs. But the industry faces significant headwinds: beer consumption fell 3.2 percent in 2025, a nearly 20 percent decline over a decade, and the number of breweries dropped from 411 to 395 last year, as [Horeca Magazine](https://horecamagazine.be/belgische-brouwers-luiden-de-alarmbel/) reported.

Notably, alcohol-free and low-alcohol beers now account for about 120 references on the Belgian market, with consumption rising 17 percent last year to 6.5 percent of total domestic beer consumption. The sector invested 213 million euros in 2025, nearly 20 percent more than the previous year.

## A Broader Debate

The controversy has also drawn critical voices. Opinion writer Tijs Synaeve, a former alcohol addict who spent 25 years drinking, challenged the brewers' campaign in [De Wereld Morgen](https://www.dewereldmorgen.be/artikel/2026/09/02/volgens-brouwers-staat-onze-manier-van-leven-op-het-spel-ik-dronk-25-jaar-en-weet-wat-er-wel-op-het). "Turning a truthful warning into an attack on our civilization is misleading," he wrote. "It seems more like the panic reaction of a sector that reads a declining curve and then decides it's the patronizing government's fault."

## What's Next

The new rules must still go through a European Union procedure before taking effect in Belgium. The health warning would apply to all alcohol advertising but would not be mandatory on labels and packaging. As the Beer Weekend continues through Sunday, the brewing industry faces a pivotal moment: balancing its cultural heritage and economic significance against evolving public health policy and changing consumer habits. The coming months will determine whether a compromise can be found between the government's health objectives and the brewers' concerns about stigmatization.
