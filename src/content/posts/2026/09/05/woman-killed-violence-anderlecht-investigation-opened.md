# Woman Killed by Violence in Anderlecht; Investigation Opened

A 28-year-old woman whose body was discovered in a home in Anderlecht, Brussels, was killed by physical violence, the Brussels public prosecutor's office has confirmed. A judicial investigation has been opened to determine the full circumstances surrounding her death.

## Discovery and Initial Response

Police from the Brussels South zone (covering Anderlecht, Sint-Gillis, and Vorst) received a call on the morning of Friday, 28 August, reporting a possibly deceased person at a residence on Duivenmelkersstraat in the "Het Rad" neighborhood of Anderlecht, according to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/09/05/vrouw-in-anderlecht-met-geweld-om-het-leven-gebracht/).

Upon entering the property, officers found the body of a woman and immediately established a judicial perimeter. Emergency medical services were called to the scene, and the Brussels public prosecutor's office was notified.

The prosecutor's office dispatched an investigating judge to the location, while the federal police lab conducted forensic evidence collection at the scene. A forensic doctor was also appointed to examine the body.

## Confirmation of Violent Death

"A forensic doctor was also appointed, who confirmed that this is a suspicious death caused by physical violence," said Veerle Byloos, spokesperson for the Brussels public prosecutor's office. "The judicial investigation is still ongoing to investigate all circumstances of the death."

The confirmation that the woman died from physical violence was reported by [BRUZZ](https://www.bruzz.be/actua/samenleving/vrouw-met-geweld-om-het-leven-gebracht-anderlecht-2026-09-04) on 4 September, following initial reports on 30 August that the death was being treated as potentially suspicious.

Earlier reporting from [The Brussels Times](https://www.brusselstimes.com/belgium/2294721/woman-dies-in-suspicious-circumstances-in-brussels) noted that prosecutor's office spokesperson Colombe de Callatay had confirmed police were called to the address after receiving a report of a possibly deceased person.

## Investigation Status

The judicial investigation remains open as of 5 September, with no arrests reported. Authorities have not publicly disclosed the identity of the victim, the specific nature of the violence that caused her death, or whether any suspects have been identified.

The Duivenmelkersstraat, whose name dates from 1890 and refers to pigeon racing, a traditional Belgian hobby, is located in the Het Rad neighborhood near the Duivenmelkerspark, in the southwest of the Brussels-Capital Region. The area sits close to the Veeweidekaai and is part of one of the 19 municipalities that make up the Brussels-Capital Region.

While the investigation is in its early stages, the case raises significant questions about public safety in the Belgian capital. The violent death of a woman in her own home is particularly concerning for residents, though authorities have been careful not to speculate about the motive or circumstances surrounding the killing.

## Broader Context of Violence in Brussels

The death occurs against a backdrop of heightened concerns about violent crime in Brussels. According to [BBC News](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/c9w4r7e7z4ko), there were over 100 shootings in Brussels in 2025 alone, and more than 65 shootings in 2026 up to late August. Anderlecht, particularly the Cureghem area, has been a hotspot for drug-related violence.

Jurgen de Landsheer, chief of Brussels South Police, has noted that organized crime in the capital has become "significantly more violent," with more than 200 drug-dealing points across the city, some generating up to €50,000 per day. In August 2026, a police station in the area came under attack with automatic weapons for the first time.

However, authorities have not established any connection between this woman's death and drug-related violence. The investigation will determine whether this was a domestic incident, connected to broader criminal activity, or another form of violence.

## What to Watch For

As the judicial investigation progresses, key questions remain unanswered: What was the specific nature of the physical violence that caused the woman's death? Was the victim known to authorities? Have any suspects been identified? And was this death connected to the broader pattern of violent crime affecting the Brussels region?

The Brussels public prosecutor's office has indicated that the investigation is ongoing to examine all circumstances of the death. The case also highlights broader concerns about violence in Brussels, where residents and officials alike have called for more effective responses to the wave of criminal activity that has shaken the capital in recent years.

Further updates are expected as forensic analysis continues and investigators piece together the events leading to the woman's death. The outcome of this investigation will be closely watched not only by the local community in Anderlecht but also by those concerned about the wider trajectory of violent crime in Brussels.
