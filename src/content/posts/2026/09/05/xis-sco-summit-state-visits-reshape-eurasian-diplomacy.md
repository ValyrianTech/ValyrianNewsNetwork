# Xi's Diplomatic Marathon: SCO Summit and State Visits Reshape Eurasian Ties

Chinese President Xi Jinping returned to Beijing on September 3 after a five-day diplomatic tour that took him from the Shanghai Cooperation Organization (SCO) summit in Bishkek to state visits in Kyrgyzstan and Egypt, marking one of China's most consequential overseas trips of 2026. Over the course of the visit, Xi attended more than 20 events, signed landmark treaties with both host nations, and put forward new proposals on regional security and global governance, according to [Xinhua](https://english.news.cn/20260901/cc4884e4dfa549c092ea467fd53e53bc/c.html).

## A Milestone for the SCO at 25

The centerpiece of the trip was the 26th Meeting of the Council of Heads of State of the SCO, held September 1 at the Happiness Palace in Bishkek with 21 heads of state in attendance. The summit marked the organization's 25th founding anniversary, a milestone that comes as the SCO has grown from six founding members to 27 countries across Asia, Europe, and Africa, representing nearly half of the world's population and roughly a quarter of global economic output.

In his address, Xi framed the organization's journey in starkly positive terms, identifying what he called the SCO's three defining achievements: becoming the world's largest regional cooperation organization by territory and population, upholding the "Shanghai Spirit" of mutual trust and consultation, and charting a path of non-alliance, non-confrontation, and non-targeting of third parties. "Standing at the crossroads concerning the future of humanity," Xi said, "the SCO should shoulder its historic responsibility and take the initiative in steering the course of history."

The summit produced the Bishkek Declaration, which incorporates the SCO's 10-year development strategy for 2026-2035, building on outcomes from the 2025 Tianjin Summit. Leaders also approved revisions to the SCO Charter and adopted 28 outcome documents covering security, economic cooperation, people-to-people exchanges, and organizational development. Pakistan was confirmed to assume the rotating SCO presidency for 2026-2027.

## Treaty Solidifies China-Kyrgyzstan Friendship

The Kyrgyzstan leg of the visit produced what Chinese Foreign Minister Wang Yi described as the most significant political outcome: the signing of the Treaty on Permanent Good-Neighborliness, Friendship and Cooperation between Xi and Kyrgyz President Sadyr Japarov. The treaty codifies the two nations' friendship in legal form, providing what Xi called "an important legal foundation" for relations between the countries "from generation to generation."

The leaders also signed a joint declaration on the high-quality development of the China-Kyrgyzstan comprehensive strategic partnership for a new era, alongside more than 30 cooperation documents covering trade, transportation, science and technology, education, and cultural exchanges, as reported by [China Daily](https://global.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202609/01/WS6a95ba1ee4b06d4aa055b7f5.html).

Japarov, who hosted Xi on the 35th anniversary of Kyrgyzstan's independence, was effusive in his praise. "China has always been a priority direction of Kyrgyzstan's foreign policy," he told Xi, describing China as "Kyrgyzstan's closest friend and most reliable partner." The Kyrgyz leader called the bilateral relationship "a golden period, at its highest level in history."

Central to the economic agenda was the China-Kyrgyzstan-Uzbekistan railway, a flagship infrastructure project that Xi emphasized should be built to the highest quality standards. Japarov described the railway as "as indispensable to Kyrgyzstan as air and water," noting that it would transform the landlocked nation into a regional transit hub.

## Egypt Visit Marks 70 Years of Diplomatic Ties

From Bishkek, Xi traveled to Cairo for his first state visit to Egypt in a decade, coinciding with the 70th anniversary of China-Egypt diplomatic relations. Egypt was the first Arab and African nation to establish diplomatic ties with the People's Republic of China in 1956.

During talks with Egyptian President Abdel Fattah El-Sisi at the Sun Palace, Xi called for deepening what he characterized as a relationship built on "equality and mutual trust, friendship and mutual learning, solidarity and mutual support, and win-win cooperation." The two leaders issued a joint statement on further deepening their comprehensive strategic partnership and witnessed the signing of more than 20 cooperation documents covering Belt and Road cooperation, artificial intelligence, digital economy, and industrial supply chains, according to [Xinhua](https://english.news.cn/20260902/7237b096f4a64c479c027e835b1bb470/c.html).

China has been Egypt's largest trading partner for 14 consecutive years, with bilateral trade reaching nearly $20.7 billion by the end of 2025. The two sides also announced plans to significantly expand local currency swap arrangements.

## A New Security Architecture for the Middle East

Beyond bilateral cooperation, Xi used the Egypt visit to articulate a four-point proposal for building a new security architecture in the Middle East. The proposal calls for fostering internal momentum for common security, pursuing comprehensive solutions to the region's interconnected conflicts, strengthening the development foundation for lasting peace, and enhancing international coordination.

Xi emphasized that "people in the Middle East should remain masters of their own affairs," and that external interference should be rejected. He also stressed that the Palestinian issue "remains at the core of the Middle East question and should never be overlooked or marginalized."

Sisi welcomed the initiative, saying Egypt "highly appreciates China's consistent, objective, and impartial stance on the Middle East question." He added that Egypt is committed to strengthening coordination with China to ease regional tensions and explore a regional security architecture.

## Strategic Implications

The trip reflects a broader pattern in China's foreign policy under Xi, who has now made six consecutive overseas visits to neighboring countries since last year. This sustained engagement follows China's first-ever Central Conference on Neighborhood Work in April 2025 and signals Beijing's intent to deepen its role as a diplomatic and economic anchor in Eurasia.

Analysts note the visit also underscores China's positioning within the SCO as a counterweight to Western-led governance structures. [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/31/putin-and-xi-affirm-strategic-alliance-amid-unpredictable-world) reported that Xi and Russian President Vladimir Putin met on the sidelines of the summit, with both leaders reaffirming their strategic alliance amid what Xi described as "increased uncertainty and unpredictability in the international order." Putin told Xi that cooperation between the two countries is "successfully developing on all fronts."

The SCO summit unfolded against a backdrop of significant global turbulence, including the ongoing Russia-Ukraine war now in its fifth year, a US-Israel conflict with Iran six months in, and intensifying great power competition. In this context, the Bishkek gathering provided a platform for Eurasian leaders to project a vision of multipolarity and multilateral cooperation.

## What to Watch

As Pakistan prepares to assume the SCO presidency, the organization faces the challenge of translating its ambitious declarations into concrete results. Key tests will include the implementation of the 2026-2035 development strategy, the operationalization of cooperation platforms established at the Tianjin Summit, and the continued construction of flagship connectivity projects like the China-Kyrgyzstan-Uzbekistan railway.

For China-Egypt relations, the focus will be on how the expanded partnership translates into tangible outcomes, particularly in AI cooperation, digital economy initiatives, and efforts to address Middle East security challenges. Xi's proposal for a new regional security architecture, while still nascent, signals Beijing's growing willingness to engage on issues traditionally dominated by Western powers.

As [The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/xi-putin-and-modi-looking-in-different-directions-as-shanghai-cooperation-organisation-marks-25th-anniversary-291058) noted, the SCO's three dominant leaders - Xi, Putin, and Modi - each used the Bishkek forum to pursue distinct agendas. Whether the organization can evolve from a venue for parallel bilateral engagements into a cohesive multilateral force remains an open question as it enters its second quarter-century.

For now, Xi's diplomatic marathon has reinforced China's position as an indispensable actor in Eurasian affairs - from the steppes of Central Asia to the banks of the Nile.
