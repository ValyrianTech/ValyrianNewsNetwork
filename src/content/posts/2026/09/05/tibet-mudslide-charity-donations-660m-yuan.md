# Tibet Mudslide Charity Donations Top 660M Yuan

Charity donations for the Gyirong mudslide disaster in Tibet have surpassed 660 million yuan (approximately $92 million USD), according to [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/local/20260905/2fef4d5e3afc443db44b4c0f221d5b0c/c.html). The announcement came on September 5, China's 11th "China Charity Day," as the Ministry of Civil Affairs confirmed that over 1.47 million people donated 50.16 million yuan through internet public fundraising platforms to support relief efforts in the affected region.

## A Disaster Born of Climate Change

The donations support relief efforts following one of the deadliest glacial disasters in recent Himalayan history. On August 26, a massive glacier collapse on the north slope of Mount Langtang Lirung in Nepal triggered a debris flow that traveled approximately 22 kilometers in just 6-7 minutes before striking Gyirong Port in Gyirong County, Shigatse, Tibet. The impact flattened roughly 0.7 square kilometers of the port area, destroying 27 buildings including the border inspection facility.

As of September 4, the disaster had caused 31 deaths and 531 missing persons on the Chinese side, while Nepal reported 1,287 deaths and 5,083 missing persons, according to [21st Century Business Herald](https://m.21jingji.com/article/20260904/herald/9c175e1b8b45c2bddcd59cc0db927fc8.html). Combined casualties across both countries exceed 1,300 deaths and 5,600 missing.

Scientists attribute the disaster to glacial instability driven by long-term climate warming. Su Pengcheng, a researcher at the Institute of Mountain Hazards and Environment at the Chinese Academy of Sciences, told a [press conference](https://www.news.cn/local/20260830/5bf1df3baf7342ec918d3383bebd24fa/c.html) that the event represents "a new and significant feature of cryosphere disasters in the Qinghai-Tibet Plateau and surrounding areas," characterized by sudden onset, massive peak flows, and extreme destructive power.

## Corporate and Public Response

The charity response has been swift and substantial. [CCTV News](https://news.cctv.com/2026/09/05/ARTI2EuQg04EQYCNoDfQmIZC260905.shtml) reported that the Ministry of Civil Affairs guided 29 internet public fundraising service platforms and relevant charity organizations to conduct disaster relief donations in an orderly manner following the disaster.

Major technology and industrial companies have contributed significant sums. Tencent Charity Foundation and ByteDance Public Welfare Foundation each donated 20 million yuan, while BYD, Xiaomi, Alibaba, Anta Group, Weiqiao Group, Pinduoduo, Zijin Mining, NetEase, and CATL each contributed 10 million yuan, according to [Tencent News](https://news.qq.com/rain/a/20260829A06NR100). By August 28, 24 private enterprises had donated over 110 million yuan in funds plus large quantities of relief materials, as [reported](https://news.qq.com/rain/a/20260829A0BNE500).

The public has also rallied behind relief efforts. Waterdrop, a designated internet public fundraising platform, launched a "Support Tibet Gyirong" special initiative on its homepage, partnering with multiple charity foundations to channel donations toward rescue, resettlement, and reconstruction efforts, according to [21st Century Business Herald](https://www.21jingji.com/article/20260827/herald/ccdee77c88e7b95937fc4f304f5c390d.html).

## A Growing Charity Sector

The donation milestone coincides with broader growth in China's charitable sector. Since the 14th Five-Year Plan period, society has cumulatively donated over 20 billion yuan to support emergency rescue and recovery for major natural disasters, CCTV reported. The number of registered charity organizations nationwide has grown from 9,400 at the end of the 13th Five-Year Plan to 15,900 today—a 69 percent increase.

China's revised Charity Law, which took effect on September 5, 2024, has strengthened the legal framework for charitable activities. The 2026 China Charity Day theme, "Promoting charity culture, practicing charity for the people," reflects the government's push to expand community-based charity and encourage broader public participation.

## Looking Ahead

As rescue operations continue in the disaster zone, the substantial charity donations will support emergency relief, resettlement of displaced residents, and long-term reconstruction. The disaster has highlighted both the growing capacity of China's charity sector to mobilize resources in times of crisis and the increasing vulnerability of Himalayan communities to climate-driven glacial hazards. Authorities continue to monitor the Puru Puzangbu barrier lake formed above Gyirong Port, which poses ongoing risks to rescue operations in the region.
