# China's MIIT Unveils AI Entrepreneurship Plan for SMEs

China's Ministry of Industry and Information Technology (MIIT) has officially released the "AI SME Entrepreneurship Support Plan (2026-2028)" on September 4, 2026, marking a significant policy push to accelerate artificial intelligence innovation at the grassroots business level. The three-year initiative aims to cultivate more than 10,000 new technology and innovation-oriented small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) in key AI sectors including industry applications, data services, and intelligent computing power, according to [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/tech/20260904/18370ebb0ae443579fdf619626c8cda5/c.html).

## Policy Targets and Framework

The plan, formally signed on August 29 and issued as document number 工信厅企业〔2026〕31号 by the MIIT General Office, sets ambitious benchmarks for China's AI startup ecosystem. Beyond the 10,000 new technology-oriented SMEs, the initiative targets breaking through 2,000 specialized and sophisticated "little giant" enterprises and nurturing a batch of gazelle and unicorn companies. It also calls for the high-standard construction of 10 technology enterprise incubators, 10 national SME public service demonstration platforms, and 10 new national-level SME characteristic industry clusters focused on AI, as detailed in the [official MIIT document](https://www.miit.gov.cn/jgsj/qyj/wjfb/art/2026/art_86c400b4473849818629663a94a6d44b.html).

The plan deploys 15 key tasks across four strategic areas: strengthening entrepreneurial factor supply, strengthening entrepreneurial entity cultivation, deepening open-source ecosystem empowerment, and improving entrepreneurial service guarantees.

## Key Measures and Notable Provisions

Among the most notable measures is the explicit encouragement for localities to provide inclusive support for "one-person companies" and "super individuals" who conduct agile entrepreneurship using intelligent tools. This recognition of AI-enabled micro-entrepreneurship represents a notable policy shift acknowledging how modern AI tools allow small teams to compete with much larger organizations.

The plan also emphasizes building "intelligence-native" enterprises and supporting the growth of the national-level AI open-source community, AtomGit. According to [ITHome](https://www.ithome.com/0/998/403.htm), the initiative encourages AI startup enterprises to actively participate in open-source ecosystem development, contributing open-source models, tools, and datasets to create a collaborative and shared ecosystem.

## Financial and Infrastructure Support

On the funding front, the plan leverages the guiding role of the National SME Development Fund, the National AI Industry Investment Fund, and the National Integrated Circuit Industry Investment Fund. As reported by [China News Service](https://www.chinanews.com.cn/cj/2026/09-04/10690492.shtml), these funds are intended to drive social capital toward establishing early-stage venture capital and startup investment funds focused on AI, building a "patient capital" support system covering seed, startup, and growth stages.

The plan also addresses infrastructure needs, calling for affordable computing power through the China Computing Power Platform and quality data resources through industrial data initiatives. It promotes the establishment of data cooperation consortiums involving industry leaders, platform companies, and SMEs to build high-quality industry datasets for AI applications in areas such as intelligent manufacturing, materials research, and biomedicine.

## Strategic Policy Context

The AI SME Entrepreneurship Support Plan is part of a broader, coordinated policy framework. It operationalizes the State Council's "AI+" Action Opinion (国发〔2025〕11号), issued in August 2025, which established targets of over 70% AI application penetration in key areas by 2027 and over 90% by 2030. The foundational document, available on the [Chinese government's official website](https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/content/202508/content_7037861.htm), called for supporting AI open-source communities, strengthening computing power infrastructure, and fostering AI-enabled enterprises.

Significantly, the plan was released just one day after ten government departments jointly issued the "15th Five-Year Plan for SME Development" on September 3, 2026. According to [Global Times](https://www.globaltimes.cn/page/202609/1369812.shtml), that broader plan targets per capita operating revenue growth of approximately 15 percent for major SMEs by 2030 and the expansion of "little giant" enterprises to 22,000, with SMEs contributing roughly 70 percent of China's technological innovation and 80 percent of urban employment.

## Analysis and Implications

This initiative signals China's continued commitment to maintaining AI development momentum through bottom-up entrepreneurship support, complementing top-down state investment in major AI infrastructure. The plan's emphasis on open-source development through AtomGit reflects a strategic choice to build China's own AI development infrastructure rather than relying on foreign platforms.

For AI startups, the plan could meaningfully lower barriers to entry through subsidized computing power, data access, and incubator support. The venture capital ecosystem may see redirected investment toward early-stage AI companies as patient capital structures take shape. Regional development could also benefit, with the emphasis on local implementation and characteristic industry clusters potentially leading to differentiated AI ecosystems across Chinese provinces.

## What to Watch

As implementation begins, key questions remain about how the plan will be funded at the local level, what specific metrics will measure success beyond the headline targets, and how "one-person company" support will interact with existing corporate registration and tax frameworks. International observers will likely view this as part of China's broader strategy to achieve technological self-reliance in AI, potentially intensifying global competition in the sector.

The coming months will reveal how effectively these policy measures translate into tangible support for China's AI entrepreneurs and whether the ambitious targets set for 2028 can be achieved.
