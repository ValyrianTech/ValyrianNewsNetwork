# Tyméo Case: Bryan Brigou's Phone Found, Drug Traces Detected

The Namur public prosecutor's office announced on Tuesday that DNA analysis has formally confirmed the two bodies found in the Walloon municipality of Onhaye are those of five-year-old Tyméo Brigou and his 29-year-old father Bryan, that traces of drug use were detected in the father's samples, and that Bryan's mobile phone has been recovered and is now being analysed by investigators.

The three findings mark the most significant developments since the father and son's remains were discovered in late August, nearly two months after they disappeared on 27 June 2026. The prosecutor stressed that many questions remain unanswered and that no further details would be released to protect the investigation and the family.

## DNA Confirms Identities

According to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/09/15/onderzoek-tymeo-telefoon-van-vader-bryan-gevonden/), the two bodies recovered along the N97 road in Onhaye on 24 and 25 August are definitively those of Tyméo and Bryan Brigou. The Namur prosecutor's office said this identification had not been established during the autopsies.

Toxicology results released the same day revealed a further detail: traces of drug consumption were detected in samples taken from Bryan Brigou, while the analyses carried out on Tyméo were negative. "Bij Bryan Brigou werden sporen van druggebruik aangetroffen, bij Tyméo waren de resultaten negatief," the prosecutor's office said.

## The Phone: A Potentially Decisive Lead

Perhaps the most consequential element of Tuesday's announcement is the recovery of Bryan Brigou's mobile phone. "Die is momenteel in handen van rechercheurs die de gegevens analyseren," the prosecutor's office said, confirming the device is in the hands of investigators who are analysing its data.

The prosecutor did not specify where or under what circumstances the phone was found. As [RTL Info](https://www.rtl.be/actu/belgique/faits-divers/affaire-tymeo-les-tests-adn-et-toxicologiques-ont-livre-leurs-resultats-le-gsm/2026-09-15/article/800236) reported, the device could interest investigators because it may contain communications or location data shedding light on what happened after the pair were last seen. The phone's data could prove pivotal in a case where, as the prosecutor noted, "une multitude de devoirs" — a multitude of investigative steps — remain ongoing on both the judicial and scientific fronts.

## A Search That Took Nearly Two Months

Understanding why it took nearly two months to find the pair, despite their bodies lying barely one kilometre from where Bryan's car was discovered, requires looking at how the investigation unfolded. According to a [VRT NWS background explainer](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/08/26/omstandigheden-zoektocht/), the search was hampered by a combination of open hypotheses and a lack of concrete leads.

On 27 June, Bryan and Tyméo were captured on camera at a local petrol station, where Bryan bought two bottles of soft drink. The following day, Bryan's car was found in the car park of the local football club in Onhaye. After that, nothing. "Wat de politie zeker wist" was little more than this, leaving every scenario open: a voluntary disappearance, an accident, a criminal act, or harm to the child. Investigators carried out more than 100 investigative steps — telephony analysis, interviews and field searches — without success.

"In het merendeel van de gevallen heb je meerdere hypotheses, maar wordt het snel duidelijk dat je er een aantal kan schrappen. En dat is in dit dossier niet het geval," said Gerry Van Loock of Belgium's Missing Persons Unit, explaining that the case defied the usual process of elimination.

The breakthrough came when an investigator phoned local farmers and contractors asking who was working where. One reported seeing a child's shoe, which led to the discovery of Tyméo's body — and the following day, a large police deployment uncovered a second body across the road. The terrain itself was a serious obstacle: the discovery sites were densely overgrown and poorly accessible, defeating infrared drones and heat cameras, while a heatwave during the initial disappearance hampered scent-tracking dogs. "De vindplaatsen zijn plekken waar amper wandelaars komen," said prosecutor Etienne Gaublomme.

## Questions Over Electronic Monitoring

Separately, the case has raised questions about the supervision of Bryan Brigou, who was wearing an electronic ankle bracelet at the time of his disappearance. Unlike some monitoring devices, his tag was not equipped with GPS tracking — a distinction explained by his legal status: he was serving a sentence, not in pre-trial detention.

Tyméo's mother, Tina Bernard, publicly asked why the probation officer had not contacted her after the disappearance. "Pourquoi l'agent de probation ne m'a-t-il pas contactée ?" she said. According to [RTL Info's account of the procedure](https://www.rtl.be/actu/belgique/justice/la-maman-de-tymeo-soupconne-une-erreur-lagent-de-probation-de-bryan-brigou/2026-09-08/article/799574), the standard first step when a person under electronic monitoring fails to return is to contact that person directly. Contacting relatives is not part of the procedure. If the individual remains unreachable for four hours, police can be called to attend the scene — which they did, without finding Bryan. The Maison de Justice of Namur stated that the prescribed procedure was followed.

## What Remains Unknown

Tuesday's findings answer the question of identity and add a forensic data point, but they do not establish the circumstances of the deaths. The prosecutor's office was explicit that the toxicology result on Bryan Brigou does not by itself explain what happened. "Er zijn nog veel mogelijkheden die onderzocht moeten worden," it said, adding that all hypotheses continue to be examined.

For a case that gripped Belgium and drew significant public attention, the announcement is both progress and a reminder of how much remains unresolved. Whether the phone's data, the ongoing scientific analyses, or further investigative steps can ultimately explain the deaths of a father and his young son is the question the coming weeks may finally begin to answer.
