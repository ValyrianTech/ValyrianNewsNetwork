# China Opens 29th Putonghua Week in Yanbian, Jilin

The 29th National Putonghua Promotion Week opened on September 14, 2026, in the city of Yanji, in the Yanbian Korean Autonomous Prefecture of northeastern Jilin Province. Organized by the Ministry of Education and the National Language Commission, the annual campaign promotes the use of Standard Mandarin, or Putonghua, across the country — with a particular focus this year on minority and border regions. According to [People's Daily](http://society.people.com.cn/n1/2026/0915/c1008-40798663.html), this is the first Putonghua Week held since the newly revised Law of the People's Republic of China on the National Common Language and Script came into effect.

## The Headline Number: 87.72%

At the center of this year's campaign is a milestone statistic: China's national Putonghua popularization rate reached 87.72% in 2025. Both People's Daily and Xinhua, reporting via [China Daily](https://cn.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202609/14/WS6aa7f003e4b09a165c789ff7.html), cited the figure, underscoring how far the country's language-unification effort has advanced since the campaign began in the late 1990s.

The rate — which measures the share of the population able to use Standard Mandarin — has become a benchmark for Beijing's broader push to establish a shared national language as a foundation for education, mobility, and national cohesion.

## A Campaign Built Around a New Law

The 2026 theme, "Promoting and Popularizing the National Common Language and Script in Accordance with the Law, Striving on the New Journey of Building a Strong Nation," explicitly ties the week's activities to the revised legal framework. The original National Common Language and Script Law was passed by the National People's Congress Standing Committee on October 31, 2000, and took effect on January 1, 2001, establishing Putonghua and standardized Chinese characters as the country's legal common language. A revision draft was first submitted to the NPC Standing Committee for deliberation in September 2025, and by August 2026 Beijing's municipal language authority was already referring to the "implementation path" of the revised law.

That timing gives this year's campaign unusual weight. Organizers framed the activities around demonstrating "the role of the National Common Language and Script in serving the fundamental task of fostering virtue through education and promoting the forging of a strong sense of community for the Chinese nation" — language that places language policy squarely within the Communist Party's ethnic-unity agenda.

## Activities and the "1+7+N" Model

The Ministry of Education and the National Language Commission staged a photo exhibition titled "Nourishing Ten Thousand Homes, Light and Shadow of the Era," showcasing achievements in language work under Party leadership. The program also included the "Pomegranate Mailbox" letter-collection activity — the pomegranate being a long-standing official metaphor for ethnic unity — and a mass participatory event called "Test Your Putonghua."

This year introduced an innovative "1+7+N" activity model: on top of a single national anchor program, organizers launched seven thematic day activities, including "Legal Putonghua Promotion, Love My China," "Exploring China through Idioms," "Putonghua Promotion Supporting Rural Revitalization," and "Putonghua Promotion Intelligent+." Across the country, member units of the Putonghua Week leading group, National Language Commission committees, and local and industry systems arranged their own events.

## Why Yanbian

The choice of venue is itself a message. Yanbian is China's only Korean autonomous prefecture and the largest Korean ethnic concentration area, sitting at the junction of China, Russia, and North Korea — a strategically sensitive border region. Holding the national opening ceremony there signals the campaign's emphasis on bringing Standard Mandarin to minority and frontier communities.

The ethnic framing echoes official doctrine on "forging a strong sense of community for the Chinese nation," the guiding principle of the Party's ethnic work. As a [QSTheory](https://www.qstheory.cn/20250314/60da14a3433f4adfb1a6bda269daaff9/c.html) analysis of the doctrine notes, the promotion of the national common language is treated as a means of strengthening the basis for interaction and integration among ethnic groups — described in official discourse as helping the Chinese nation "embrace more closely like pomegranate seeds."

## What to Watch

With the revised law now in force and the popularization rate already near 88%, the next phase of language policy will likely focus less on raw reach and more on quality, regional gaps, and the sensitive balance between promoting a common language and preserving minority linguistic heritage. The [People's Daily print edition](http://paper.people.com.cn/rmrb/pc/content/202609/15/content_30181093.html) published the coverage on page 4 of its September 15 issue, placing the campaign within the country's mainstream domestic news agenda.

How smoothly the revised legal framework is implemented in border and minority regions — and whether it is paired with meaningful protections for local languages — will be the key test in the months ahead.
