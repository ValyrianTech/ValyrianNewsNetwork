# Shanghai Unveils Sweeping AI Plans for 2026-2030

Shanghai has issued a cluster of artificial intelligence-focused plans under China's 15th Five-Year Plan framework, laying out an ambitious 2026-2030 roadmap to make the city a global AI powerhouse. The plans, detailed in an August industry briefing published by the [People's Daily Shanghai Channel](http://sh.people.com.cn/n2/2026/0914/c134768-41695924.html) in partnership with the Shanghai Artificial Intelligence Industry Research Institute (AIII), span computing infrastructure, model exports, industrial applications, and international governance.

At the center of the push is the "Hundred-Thousand-Ten Thousand" intelligent computing cluster project, which aims to build a tiered supply system of ultra-large, mid-sized, and edge computing centers across the city.

## A Tiered Computing Backbone

According to the [Shanghai Municipal Commission of Economy and Informatization](https://www.sheitc.sh.gov.cn/cyfz/20260811/7ff732b1c470421c86e46d14c0270ead.html), which issued the Software and Information Services Development 15th Five-Year Plan on August 11, Shanghai will build 100,000-card-class ultra-large intelligent computing clusters in Songjiang, Lingang, and Qingpu, alongside 1,000-card-class clusters in Baoshan, Pudong, and Jiading. Traditional data centers and telecom rooms will be guided toward 100-card-class edge computing centers.

The plan sets hard targets for 2030: the software and information services industry is to reach a scale of 4 trillion yuan, with industry added value exceeding 1.1 trillion yuan. The number of firms with revenue above 10 billion yuan is to rise to 35. By 2030, Shanghai aims to make its software sector an economic growth "power source," a "main front" for AI-empowered applications, and a "bridgehead" for global competition.

Three other municipal plans reinforce the strategy. The "Digital Shanghai" plan proposes a "general + intelligent + supercomputing" trinity service system. The Zhangjiang High-Tech Zone plan targets breakthroughs in optical computing and brain-like chip architectures. And the new industrialization plan calls for upgrading intelligent computing capability across chips, large models, and embodied intelligence, with high-performance computing chips, processors, storage chips, interconnect chips, and smart sensors to reach international advanced levels.

## Export Ambitions and Global Governance

Shanghai is also reaching outward. A service trade plan issued by the Shanghai Commerce Commission and six other departments promotes expanded overseas invocation of computing power and foundation models, and supports the export of agent and multimodal-generation model products.

The plan outlines support for humanoid and embodied intelligence firms, AI empowerment of healthcare, education, and professional services, and the construction of the headquarters of the World AI Cooperation Organization. It also backs Pudong as an AI innovation application pilot zone and Xuhui's "Model Speed Space" large-model ecosystem community, while exploring the establishment of a global AI governance system.

Domestically, the Shanghai Development and Reform Commission is offering computing power subsidies to support private enterprises renting intelligent computing resources for large-model R&D and training. A late-August meeting of the Shanghai Municipal Party Committee Central Group study session emphasized expanding "AI + manufacturing" and "AI + services," cultivating benchmark enterprises, and developing new-type consumer terminal products.

Together, the plans signal a strategy that intertwines domestic industrial upgrading with global outreach, an approach the [China Securities Journal](https://field.10jqka.com.cn/20260812/c678871684.shtml) described as pairing the software sector's role as an economic "power source" with its ambition to compete globally.

## National and International Context

The municipal push fits within a broader national effort. China's National Data Administration reported that, as of August, the country had built more than 126,000 high-quality datasets totaling over 1,815 petabytes, up more than 89% from the end of the first quarter. On August 31, the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology issued a notice launching a special action to cultivate AI application service providers.

The international backdrop is equally active. The European Union's AI Act transparency rules and general-purpose AI model provider rules took effect on August 2, marking the first comprehensive AI law entering substantive enforcement. In the United States, the White House met with AI giants including Anthropic and OpenAI on August 4 to discuss a developing regulatory framework that would apply to frontier closed-source models.

Shanghai's global positioning received high-level recognition on September 14, when the [BRICS New Delhi Declaration](https://www.news.cn/world/20260914/239b101817364377836ea88b09df442e/c.html) acknowledged the Shanghai World AI Conference and the High-Level Meeting on Global AI Governance, recognizing their contribution to strengthening international AI cooperation.

## Enterprise Momentum

Activity on the ground is keeping pace. In August, Shanghai AI firms closed a wave of financings: brain-like vision chip developer Shanghai Xijian Technology raised nearly 500 million yuan in an angel round; embodied world-model startup Shunshi Technology closed about $10 million in seed funding; AI agent infrastructure firm Yitai Zhixin raised tens of millions of yuan; and open-source platform OpenCSG completed a Pre-A round reaching unicorn valuation.

Model releases also intensified. On August 26, Alibaba's Tongyi Qianwen released and open-sourced Qwen3.8-Flash, a preview of its next-generation Qwen4 architecture with 125 billion total MoE parameters activating only 6 billion per token, while Zhipu AI launched GLM-5.3-Flash. Both cut training costs dramatically.

Beyond the lab, AI is moving into daily life. On August 29, a robot restaurant billed as the world's first "zero-modification, fully autonomous, year-round" venue opened at a DQ store on Shanghai's Wujiang Road. In Xuhui District, two humanoid robots took up posts as "AI volunteers" at community service centers.

## What's Next

The breadth of the plans, spanning chips, models, datasets, and applications, reflects Shanghai's intent to compete on every layer of the AI stack. The 2030 targets are ambitious, and execution will depend on whether the city can translate computing infrastructure and subsidies into sustained commercial momentum.

One early signal of that ambition arrived on the international stage: the BRICS declaration's recognition of Shanghai's AI governance efforts suggests the city's plans extend beyond domestic industry to shaping global rules. Whether the 15th Five-Year Plan documents translate into a decisive competitive edge will be measured over the remainder of the decade.
