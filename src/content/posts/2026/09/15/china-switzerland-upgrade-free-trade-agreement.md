# China, Switzerland Upgrade Free Trade Agreement

China and Switzerland have concluded negotiations to upgrade their bilateral free trade agreement (FTA), signing a memorandum of understanding in Bern on August 20, 2026, and pledging to make more than 99 percent of bilateral imports tariff-free. The deal, struck by Chinese Commerce Minister Wang Wentao and Swiss President Guy Parmelin, expands cooperation into services, investment, digital trade, artificial intelligence, and sustainability, and is being framed by both sides as a signal in support of open trade at a moment of rising global protectionism.

## A First-Mover Agreement, Modernized

The original China-Switzerland FTA was signed in July 2013 and took effect a year later, making it China's first comprehensive free trade agreement with a country in continental Europe. At the time, most Western economies took a wait-and-see approach toward such deals, and Switzerland's early move quickly activated bilateral trade. China rose to become Switzerland's third-largest trading partner after the European Union and the United States.

Negotiations to upgrade the agreement formally began in September 2024 and concluded after five rounds of talks. According to a statement from [China's Ministry of Commerce](https://news.cgtn.com/news/2026-08-21/China-Switzerland-reach-agreement-on-an-upgraded-free-trade-deal-1PMqpQQly0w/p.html), both sides committed to higher-standard two-way opening in goods, services, investment, and rules, creating new opportunities for bilateral economic and trade cooperation. China and Switzerland will now complete their respective domestic procedures ahead of the formal signing of the upgrade protocol.

Parmelin said the move sends a positive signal for both sides to promote open cooperation and free trade and uphold a rules-based international economic and trade environment. Wang said that amid growing global uncertainty, the commitment also demonstrates the two countries' support for free trade and their choice to pursue greater certainty.

## What the Upgrade Delivers

The most tangible gains are in market access. According to [China Daily](https://www.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202608/22/WS6a88891aa3106bc57421cd60.html), the upgrade will make over 99 percent of imports tariff-free. For Swiss exporters, the change is substantial: the Swiss tech-industry association [Swissmem](https://www.swissmem.ch/de/engagement/politik/modernisiertes-freihandelsabkommen-mit-china-substanziell-besserer-marktzugang-fuer-die-tech-industrie.html) reports that 99.8 percent of Swiss exports to China are to become duty-free in the medium term, with 77.5 percent duty-free immediately upon entry into force, up from just 53.6 percent today. The remaining tariff lines will be phased in over a five- to ten-year transition. Citing SECO, Swissmem estimates the additional tariff savings at roughly CHF 244 million.

For years the relationship was asymmetric: about half of Swiss exports to China were tariff-free, while almost all Chinese exports entered Switzerland duty-free. The upgrade narrows that gap and extends the agreement into new territory, including services, investment rules, digital trade, green development, competition policy, intellectual property, government procurement, labor standards, rules of origin, trade facilitation, standards cooperation, and a most-favored-nation clause, along with new dispute settlement bodies.

The economic stakes have grown steadily. Bilateral trade rose 44 percent between 2014 and 2024, from $43.57 billion to $62.77 billion, according to [CGTN](https://news.cgtn.com/news/2026-08-26/China-Switzerland-FTA-upgrade-opens-new-chapter-for-bilateral-trade-1PURK5gM7SM/p.html), which also notes that more than 1,000 Swiss companies operate in China, with actual investment exceeding $11 billion. The momentum has accelerated sharply this year: the news service [ECNS](https://www.ecns.cn/cns-wire/2026-08-28/detail-ihfirzsm0920185.shtml), citing Chinese customs data, reports that bilateral trade surged 159.8 percent year-on-year to $72.77 billion in the first seven months of 2026, driven by a 191.6 percent jump in China's imports from Switzerland.

Chinese Commerce Ministry spokesperson Huang Ling said the upgrade also covers rules of origin, trade facilitation, and cooperation on standards and conformity assessment.

## Industry Hails the Deal

Swiss business groups welcomed the modernization. Martin Hirzel, president of Swissmem, called China both a key market and a demanding competitor for the Swiss tech industry, saying the modernization "eliminates existing tariff disadvantages on high-tech products and updates trade rules." He described it as "exactly the pragmatic foreign economic policy an export-oriented small state needs."

Swissmem noted that the last 5 percent of tariff lines still not duty-free account for 14 percent of Swiss tech-industry exports to China, and stressed that Chinese firms increasingly compete on technology and speed rather than price alone, making speed, customer proximity, service, and efficient processes decisive. The association urged rapid signing and ratification.

Eric Bernard, CEO of the Swiss hearing-care company Sonova, said the group will step up localization, expand its application of artificial intelligence, and accelerate digital innovation in China.

## A Signal to Europe, and to Washington

Analysts read the deal as carrying significance well beyond its economic size. Liu Zhiqin, a researcher at Renmin University of China, said the conclusion of talks shows China and European economies can deepen mutually beneficial cooperation through dialogue and accommodate each other's concerns despite growing strains in the global trading system.

A commentary in the [People's Daily](http://world.people.com.cn/n1/2026/0915/c1002-40798688.html) framed the agreement as a message to Europe as a whole, arguing that closer, long-term stable China-EU ties are achievable if both sides respect each other and accommodate concerns. The paper cited Bloomberg's assessment that, amid uncertain global trade and continued US trade-policy pressure, the China deal is seen by Switzerland as an important "pressure relief valve."

The geopolitical subtext was explored at length by the German daily [junge Welt](https://www.jungewelt.de/artikel/529299.freihandelsabkommen-china-schweiz-eidgenossenschaft-trotzt-dem-westlichen-trend.html), which described the deal as a relevant signal in international politics. It noted the divergence between US and EU approaches to China, with Washington treating Beijing as a declared rival and acting protectionistically, while the EU sees China as both competitor and market. It reported that at a recent G20 meeting, German Finance Minister Lars Klingbeil pushed back against US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent's call for the bloc to shield more strongly against Chinese imports. According to junge Welt, the Swiss side has been careful to characterize the agreement as only an update of existing relations rather than a new strategic partnership, citing NZZ.

## Human Rights Concerns Loom

Not everyone has welcomed the deal. Throughout the negotiations, China reportedly resisted anchoring human rights provisions. According to SECO, the final text contains only a symbolic preamble reference to the Universal Declaration of Human Rights and provisions on the fundamental principles of the International Labor Organization, without enforcement or sanctions mechanisms.

In a joint statement, [Amnesty International Switzerland, Public Eye, and Voices](https://www.voices-ngo.ch/de/medien/medienmitteilungen/neues-freihandelsabkommen-schweiz-china-ungenuegende-absicherung-der-menschenrechte/) called the agreement "not acceptable as long as human and labor rights are inadequately protected and products from state-organized forced labor can enter Switzerland." They described the human rights provisions as "little more than an empty commitment," citing a lack of effective sanctions. The groups are demanding a forced-labor import ban and sanctions that could extend to suspension of the agreement, and said they will seriously consider a referendum if Parliament approves the deal without such instruments.

The Swiss team, however, has stressed that China made concessions not made in any prior FTA, including a ban on forced and child labor and a reference to human rights and environmental standards.

## What's Next

The upgrade now faces domestic ratification in both countries. In Switzerland, Parliament will debate the agreement in the coming months, where the NGO campaign over labor and human rights safeguards is likely to intensify. Both governments have said they will move quickly to complete procedures and formally sign the upgrade protocol.

For China, the deal offers a demonstration that high-level, mutually beneficial trade with European economies remains achievable despite frictions. For Switzerland, a small, export-oriented economy navigating an increasingly fragmented global order, it offers a pragmatic expansion of market access for its watches, machinery, precision instruments, and pharmaceuticals, even as it raises unresolved questions about the values its trade relationships are meant to uphold.

Whether the upgrade ultimately enters into force, and on what terms, now rests with Swiss lawmakers, and possibly Swiss voters.
