# China Manufacturing PMI Returns to Expansion at 50.1%

China's official manufacturing Purchasing Managers' Index (PMI) rose to 50.1% in September 2026, climbing back above the 50-point mark that separates expansion from contraction and ending two consecutive months of decline, according to data jointly released by the [National Bureau of Statistics](https://www.news.cn/fortune/20260930/b97119ab134c49dfa44b62c341906887/c.html) (NBS) and the China Federation of Logistics & Purchasing (CFLP) on September 30.

The reading represents a 0.3 percentage point increase from August's 49.8%, marking the first return to expansion since June. The rebound was mirrored across a broader set of indicators: the non-manufacturing business activity index rose 1.2 points to 50.2%, and the composite PMI output index climbed 1.2 points to 50.7%, both back above the threshold.

## A Broad-Based Recovery

The improvement was not confined to headline numbers. Of the 21 industries surveyed, 12 reported PMI readings above the 50-point mark—an increase of four from the previous month—signaling a widening recovery across the manufacturing sector.

Production activity accelerated notably, with the production index rising 1.3 points to 51.7%. The new orders index held steady at 50.5%, a marginal 0.1-point decline that nonetheless kept demand in expansion territory. The new export orders index remained exactly at the 50.0 line, indicating that export demand has stabilized rather than contracted.

Three key industries drove much of the expansion, as [noted by Guangming](https://m.gmw.cn/2026-09/30/content_1304570187.htm), which published the official NBS interpretation by chief statistician Huo Lihui. Equipment manufacturing registered 51.0%, high-tech manufacturing reached 52.5%, and consumer goods hit 50.7%—all comfortably above the expansion threshold. High energy-consuming industries remained below 50 at 48.0%, though this was a slight improvement of 0.1 points.

"The manufacturing production index and new orders index were both above the critical point, and manufacturing enterprise production and market demand continued to improve," Huo said in the official interpretation.

## Prices and Small Business in Focus

One of the more striking developments was a sharp rise in input prices. The main raw material purchasing price index surged 4.2 points to 60.8%, while the ex-factory price index rose 3.6 points to 54.0%—the second consecutive monthly increase for both.

According to the [China News Service](https://www.chinanews.com.cn/cj/2026/09-30/10706023.shtml), which carried the CFLP's industry analysis, the increase was largely driven by geopolitical factors pushing up international crude oil and natural gas prices, which in turn lifted coal prices as a substitute energy source. Purchasing and ex-factory price indices for basic raw material industries jumped by more than 10 points, exceeding 70% and 60% respectively. The cost pass-through has pushed purchasing price indices above 54% across equipment manufacturing, high-tech, and consumer goods sectors.

Company size data painted a mixed but improving picture. Large enterprises maintained stable expansion at 50.6%, unchanged from August. Medium enterprises rose 0.3 points to 49.7%, and small enterprises improved by a full point to 48.9%—still in contraction, but with the decline in demand noticeably narrowing.

## Quarterly Context and Outlook

The September reading fits into a broader pattern of gradual stabilization. According to the China News Service, citing CFLP data, China's manufacturing PMI averaged 49.7% in the third quarter of 2026—0.5 points below the second-quarter average but 0.2 points above the same period last year. Both August and September showed consecutive improvement, suggesting the trough may have passed.

The CFLP attributed the third-quarter stabilization to three factors: the concentrated rollout of pro-growth policies, the continued expansion of new growth drivers such as high-tech and equipment manufacturing, and resilient exports. The average new export orders index for the quarter was 49.9%, rising for a second consecutive quarter, with AI-related exports highlighted as a bright spot.

The construction sector also returned to expansion, with its business activity index jumping 3.4 points to 50.3%—the highest level of the year. Services rebounded to 50.2%, up 0.9 points, with telecommunications, broadcasting, monetary and financial services, and insurance all registering above 55.0%.

## What's Next

Looking ahead, the CFLP projected that China's manufacturing sector will maintain a "stable operation, moderate expansion" trajectory in the fourth quarter. Policy support remains a key variable: the State-owned Assets Supervision and Administration Commission has indicated that central state-owned enterprises are planning approximately 2 trillion yuan in annual investment around major "six networks" infrastructure projects, according to the China News Service.

October is traditionally a peak season for construction and several manufacturing subsectors, and the year-end period is a critical window for enterprises aiming to meet annual targets. The production and operating activity expectation index held steady at 53.8%, indicating that businesses remain cautiously optimistic about the months ahead.

The key question for the fourth quarter will be whether the recovery can broaden beyond policy-driven and seasonal factors—and whether rising input costs begin to erode margins across the industrial base.
