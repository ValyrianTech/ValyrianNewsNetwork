# China Charges Ex-Securities Chief Yi Huiman With Bribery

Chinese prosecutors have filed a public prosecution against Yi Huiman, the former chairman of the China Securities Regulatory Commission (CSRC), on charges of accepting bribes, according to [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/legal/20260930/fef2b2f0792c4c4fac330f3af205787e/c.html). The case, announced on September 30, 2026, marks a significant escalation of China's anti-corruption campaign into the upper ranks of the financial regulatory system.

The indictment was submitted to the Qingdao Intermediate People's Court in Shandong Province, following an investigation by the National Supervisory Commission. In a statement, the Supreme People's Procuratorate said Yi abused a series of senior positions at both the Industrial and Commercial Bank of China (ICBC) and the CSRC to secure benefits for others while illegally accepting property of an "especially huge" amount.

## The Charges

According to the indictment reported by Xinhua, Yi is accused of using the conveniences of his posts to seek advantages for others — spanning roles as Deputy Party Secretary and Vice President of ICBC's Jiangsu Branch, President of that branch, Vice President and President of ICBC, Chairman of ICBC, and finally Party Secretary and Chairman of the CSRC. The prosecution said the illicit property he accepted was of an "especially huge" magnitude, warranting criminal liability for bribery.

The procuratorate stated that during the review-and-prosecution stage it informed Yi of his litigation rights, interrogated him, and heard the opinions of his defense lawyer — a standard procedure intended to demonstrate that due process was observed.

## A Fall From Grace

Yi's case is the latest chapter in a sweeping anti-corruption drive that has increasingly targeted the financial sector. On April 30, 2026, the Central Commission for Discipline Inspection and the National Supervisory Commission announced that Yi had been expelled from the Communist Party and dismissed from public office, per the [Procuratorial Daily](https://news.ifeng.com/c/8wpueMxJ5zP).

The disciplinary report alleged that Yi "lost his ideals and beliefs, betrayed his original aspiration and mission," engaged in trading power and money for sex, and used his position to seek benefits for others in job promotions, IPO approvals, and financing and loans. It further stated that he failed to restrain himself after the 18th Party Congress — a standard formulation signaling an aggravating factor.

Yi's removal from the CSRC had come earlier. He was relieved of his posts as Party Secretary and Chairman in February 2024 and replaced by Wu Qing, a move widely linked to the prolonged stock market slump. In June 2024, he was appointed Deputy Director of the Economic Committee of the 14th National Committee of the Chinese People's Political Consultative Conference — at the time read by some observers as a signal of a "safe landing." That impression was shattered on September 6, 2025, when authorities announced he was under disciplinary review and supervisory investigation.

## A Career Spanning ICBC and the CSRC

Born in 1964 in Cangnan County, Zhejiang Province, Yi rose from modest origins to lead the world's largest bank by assets, according to his [biographical profile](https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/易会满). A graduate of the Zhejiang Bank School, he joined ICBC in 1985 and spent more than three decades at the institution, becoming Vice President in 2008, President in 2013, and Chairman in 2016.

In January 2019, Yi was appointed CSRC Chairman, becoming the first person from ICBC to head the securities regulator. He was promoted to full ministerial level and later elected a member of the 20th CPC Central Committee.

His five-year tenure at the CSRC oversaw major reforms, including the STAR Market registration-system pilot in 2019, ChiNext reform in 2020, the establishment of the Beijing Stock Exchange in 2021, and the nationwide rollout of the full registration system in 2023. During his tenure, more than 1,800 new companies listed on the A-share market, raising over 2 trillion yuan.

Yet the market was volatile throughout. As [Time Weekly](https://m.163.com/dy/article/K8P9PR3H0519APGA.html) noted in its analysis, the Shanghai Composite Index fell below the 3,000-point mark 20 times during his tenure, and rose only about 190 points — from 2,597.78 to 2,788.55 — over the period. The CSRC chairmanship, the publication observed, is viewed by the market as a "crater" position.

## Analysis: A Signal to the Financial Sector

The prosecution of a full-ministerial-level official and Central Committee member represents one of the most high-profile cases in China's recent financial anti-corruption campaign. Notably, Yi's predecessor as CSRC Chairman, Liu Shiyu, was also investigated — making the top securities regulatory post, and the finance sector more broadly, a sustained focus of discipline inspections.

The case carries symbolic weight given Yi's own public record on financial integrity. During his CSRC tenure, he repeatedly warned about corruption risks in the capital market, calling in 2021 for stronger oversight of the registration and approval process and for cracking down on "hunting" and "counter-hunting" in the markets. He once said the CSRC chairmanship "touches the hearts of hundreds of millions of investors."

The choice of Qingdao, rather than Beijing, as the venue for prosecution is consistent with the common practice of moving major cases outside the capital to underscore impartiality.

Yi's case also unfolds against a backdrop of widening probes into ICBC. In May 2025, Shen Rongqin, former Party Secretary and President of ICBC's Zhejiang Branch — a native of the same province and a fellow alumnus of Yi's — was placed under investigation, an example of the cascade of cases reaching Yi's former colleagues and subordinates.

## What's Next

The case now enters the judicial phase, with the Qingdao Intermediate People's Court expected to schedule a trial. The severity of the alleged conduct — described as involving an "especially huge" sum and occurring after the 18th Party Congress — suggests a potentially lengthy sentence if Yi is convicted.

For China's financial sector, the prosecution reinforces the message that no official, however senior, is beyond the reach of the anti-corruption apparatus. What remains to be seen is whether the case prompts further institutional scrutiny of the CSRC and ICBC, and whether it will be followed by additional disclosures about the broader network of cases connected to Yi's long career.
