# Jiangxi Mudslide Kills 13 as County Toll Reaches 16

Rescue workers have recovered the body of a final missing person at the site of a mudslide in Gaoping Town, Suichuan County, in China's Jiangxi Province, bringing the death toll from the September 5 disaster to 13, [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/local/20260910/12adaab3221146e49b4a4f4be38a26b3/c.html) reported on Thursday. Combined with other rain-triggered geological disasters across the county, the total death toll in Suichuan has reached 16.

The mudslide struck Shixia Group in Mingkeng Village at roughly 4:00 a.m. on September 5, following days of heavy rain brought by Typhoon "Sadel." According to Xinhua, the Gaoping Town site alone accounted for 13 deaths as of 9:00 a.m. on September 10. One additional victim was found in Tanghu Town and two in Zuo'an Town, [state media reported](https://www.sohu.com/a/1074156057_267106).

## A Remote Mountain Community

Mingkeng Village sits at the junction of Shangyou County in Ganzhou, Guidong County in Hunan, and Suichuan County, about 80 kilometers from the county seat. It is home to more than 300 households and roughly 1,200 residents, according to [Jiemian News](https://m.jiemian.com/article/15060217.html).

The mudslide damaged 12 houses — eight of them completely destroyed and four partially damaged — and initially left 11 people missing. The deposit zone was long and deep, with a large volume of collapsed material that buried floor slabs and brick-concrete components layer by layer, blocking rescue channels, Jiangxi's fire and rescue service said.

## Rescue Hampered by Terrain and Secondary Slides

The remote, mountainous setting and continuing instability severely complicated the search. Jiangxi Fire and Rescue reported that crews used toothless saws and hydraulic breaking tools to cut through buried areas, deploying life detectors and search dogs to locate signs of life. Multiple secondary mudslides and collapses occurred during the operation, and soft soil further hampered progress.

Initial response involved 41 vehicles and 224 personnel from county-level public security, armed police, fire, and emergency services. Because roads were blocked by landslides, 49 rescuers reached Mingkeng Village on foot, and 39 trapped villagers were safely relocated. By 10:00 p.m. on September 5, 641 rescuers had arrived on site, [according to Guangming Net](https://m.gmw.cn/2026-09/06/content_1304559726.htm). China Aneng Second Engineering Bureau mobilized 80 rescue personnel and 45 sets of equipment to help open access routes.

By 4:00 p.m. on September 6, rescuers had built 100 meters of main road and cleared more than 20,000 cubic meters of mud and debris, [China.com reported](https://news.china.com/socialgd/10000169/20260910/49732339.html).

## A Wider Disaster Across Suichuan County

The Gaoping Town mudslide was the deadliest of several geological disasters that struck Suichuan during the same period of heavy rain. In neighboring Tanghu Town, a former village party secretary named Zhang Xinshi went missing on September 4 after a secondary collapse while he and three villagers were inspecting hazards; the other three escaped, [Lianhe Zaobao](https://www.zaobao.com.sg/news/china/story20260906-9634518) reported. In Yangfen Village, Zuo'an Town, repeated landslides on September 4 and 5 left two people missing.

Displaced residents were moved to temporary shelters. A teacher at Gaoping Middle School told Jiemian News that the school received 400 affected residents, using idle classrooms and dormitories after classes were suspended. County-wide, 5,339 people were evacuated as a precaution, according to Guangming Net.

## National Response and Early Warning

China's Ministry of Emergency Management activated a Level IV national natural disaster relief response and a Level IV national geological disaster response, dispatching a work group to the site. The Ministry of Natural Resources raised its geological disaster defense response to Level III, and Suichuan County launched a Level I disaster relief response at 10:00 a.m. on September 5, [NetEase reported](https://www.163.com/dy/article/L637E59505563VTN.html).

Authorities had issued a geological disaster meteorological risk warning covering 8:00 a.m. September 3 to 8:00 a.m. September 5, flagging a high risk of collapse, landslide, and mudslide in the high-susceptibility zone of southwestern Ji'an. On September 6, the Ji'an Natural Resources Bureau and Meteorological Bureau issued a fresh yellow warning for southwestern Suichuan, [Nanfang Dushi reported](https://m.mp.oeeee.com/a/BAAFRD0000202609061660532.html).

## Analysis: A Familiar Pattern in China's Mountainous South

The Suichuan disaster reflects a recurring vulnerability in southern China's mountainous terrain, where intense typhoon-season rainfall can rapidly saturate slopes and trigger cascading geological hazards. The early warning issued two days before the event underscores a persistent challenge: even when risk is identified, the speed and scale of a mudslide in a remote, road-dependent community can overwhelm the window for evacuation.

The event also unfolded alongside a far larger mudslide catastrophe in Nepal, which has killed more than 1,300 people, though the two are separate disasters. Two central government departments allocated 240 million yuan (about $33 million) to support relief across five provinces affected by Typhoon "Sadel."

## What's Next

By September 7-8, water, power, roads, and communications had been reported fully restored in Gaoping Town, and search-and-recovery operations continued through the week. As of the September 10 update, all previously missing people at the Gaoping Town site had been accounted for. Attention now turns to recovery and resettlement for the hundreds of displaced residents, and to whether the region's warning and evacuation systems can be strengthened before the next typhoon season.
