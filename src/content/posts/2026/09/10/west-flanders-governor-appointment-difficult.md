# Why Naming a West Flanders Governor Is So Hard

West Flanders governor Carl Decaluwé retires on 30 September 2026, yet the Flemish government still has not named a successor. Under the proportional D'Hondt system that governs how the majority divides mandates, it is now the socialist party Vooruit's turn to propose a candidate. But the Christian-democratic CD&V, which supplied the outgoing governor and still holds most mayoral posts in the province, wants to keep the job. As of 10 September, the post remains vacant — and the question is threatening to collide with the Flemish budget negotiations.

## A neutral post, a political game

On paper, the appointment should be simple. A provincial governor is a government commissioner who represents both the federal and the regional governments, oversees local authorities and public welfare centres, is responsible for public order and disaster relief, and accompanies the royal family on provincial visits. It is an administrative role, not a political one, and the appointment is meant to be apolitical.

In practice, it rarely works that way. "For a politically neutral mandate, you very quickly end up in a political game," said Ivan De Vadder, a political journalist at [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/09/10/waarom-is-de-verkiezing-van-een-nieuwe-west-vlaamse-provinciegou/), which first reported the standoff. De Vadder pointed to the paradox at the heart of the affair: "It's always former politicians who become governor."

That pattern is on full display now. CD&V argues that because it still holds many mayoral offices, especially in West Flanders, the governorship is effectively its due. The party is reportedly considering current Flemish Minister Hilde Crevits for the role. Decaluwé, a former Flemish MP, took the post in 2012 — the last time CD&V designated the candidate.

## The D'Hondt trap

This time, however, the arithmetic points elsewhere. Under the D'Hondt system, mandates are distributed within the majority according to each party's size, and by that rule the nomination now belongs to Vooruit. "According to that arrangement, it is now Vooruit's turn. Full stop," De Vadder said. "Only, that full stop is not made so simple."

The complication is a web of compensation demands. Vooruit has been slow to put a name forward and appears to be waiting for CD&V to make its move. If CD&V wants to keep the post, it would have to compensate Vooruit. But Vooruit is said to prefer the East Flanders governorship — a position that, under D'Hondt, N-VA has the right to decide. That, in turn, would require compensating N-VA.

The most frequently mentioned Vooruit candidate for West Flanders is Jurgen Vanlerberghe, a provincial deputy in the province since December 2018. It is party chairman Conner Rousseau who would formally have to submit the nomination. N-VA MP and Koksijde mayor Sander Loones told KW that it is Rousseau's responsibility to come forward, calling the delay "shameful."

The two provinces have become entangled in what analysts describe as a likely package deal. [W16](https://www.w16.be/p/vooruit-en-n-va-verdelen-gouverneurs-in-oost-en-west-vlaanderen-als-cd-v-denkt-dat-ze-nu-nog-veel-ca) reported in late June that Vooruit and N-VA were positioning to divide the East and West Flanders governorships, with the scenario of replacing Crevits in the Flemish government with CD&V heavyweight Kris Declercq looking increasingly unlikely.

The mood between the parties has soured. Asked whether CD&V could still expect gifts, Vooruit's response, as relayed by W16, was blunt: "We are not Jesus, are we." N-VA dismissed as "nonsense" the suggestion that the socialists had a first pick between the two provinces.

## The budget entanglement

What worries Flemish Minister-President Matthias Diependaele (N-VA) is not only the delay but the risk that the governor question becomes a bargaining chip in the budget talks. On 9 September he pressed CD&V at the government's inner cabinet to make its position clear, insisting the matter should not drag on or be mixed with the budget file.

De Vadder warned of exactly that outcome. "Imagine such a mandate being linked to something in the budget, then you get a complete carpet-trading," he said — invoking the image of a market haggling over goods. That, he added, is precisely why Diependaele wants the issue kept separate.

All parties now publicly agree that the governor appointment must not be tied to the budget debate. The difficulty is that agreement in principle does not resolve who gets the post.

## What to watch

With no decision this week, De Vadder expects none until after the budget negotiations conclude. If no successor is named by 30 September, Decaluwé's mandate is expected to be extended — described by De Vadder as "the easiest solution."

Several questions remain open. Will Vooruit ultimately nominate Vanlerberghe, or will it trade West Flanders for East Flanders? Will CD&V formally push for Crevits, triggering a reshuffle that could bring Declercq into the Flemish government? And will the two governorships be settled as a single package — with N-VA, Vooruit and CD&V each needing to be compensated?

For now, a mandate designed to sit above politics is serving as a reminder of how thoroughly politics can reach into it. The province has a deadline, a front-runner, and a standoff — and the clock is running down.
