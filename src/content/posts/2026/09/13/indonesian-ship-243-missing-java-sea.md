# Indonesian Ship with 243 Aboard Missing in Java Sea

An Indonesian passenger ship carrying 243 people went missing in the Java Sea in bad weather early Sunday, triggering a large sea-and-air search operation. As of Sunday evening, rescuers had evacuated 103 people, 97 survivors and six dead, leaving about 130 unaccounted for, according to Indonesian authorities.

The vessel, the KM Virgo Transport 8, had departed Surabaya in East Java province on Saturday bound for Banjarmasin in South Kalimantan, on the island of Borneo. It lost contact after encountering rough conditions, and officials later said the ship had capsized. The cause of the incident remains under investigation.

## What Happened

According to [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/world/20260913/2574f96ae5464d7d9a189aa8f9be20c2/c.html), the Indonesian passenger ship disappeared with 243 people aboard. The vessel's operator, PT Virgo, reported the loss of contact to authorities after learning the ship had run into bad weather.

Indonesia's Transport Minister, Dudy Purwagandhi, said at a Sunday press conference that the ship had flipped but had not sunk, citing observations from a helicopter crew, and that the cause of the incident awaited further investigation. The search-and-rescue agency had earlier said the ship had sunk.

The [ABC](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-09-13/search-underway-for-indonesian-passenger-ship/107148298), citing agency reports, said the vessel capsized after encountering bad weather. Rescuers battled waves of up to 2.5 metres to reach the ship.

## Timeline and Rescue Effort

A chronology published by [Kompas.com](https://www.kompas.com/tren/read/2026/09/13/141500965/kronologi-kapal-virgo-transport-8-hilang-kontak-berangkat-dari-surabaya) traced the sequence of events: the KM Virgo Transport 8 departed Surabaya on Saturday at roughly 10 a.m. local time and was reported out of contact around 2 a.m. Sunday. The Banjarmasin Search and Rescue Office received the report at 4:10 a.m. from PT Virgo general manager Yoel Rihi.

Within twenty minutes, according to the chronology, SAR personnel set out aboard the rescue vessel KN SAR Laksamana 241 toward the ship's estimated last position. I Putu Sudayana, head of the Banjarmasin Search and Rescue Office and the operation's SAR mission coordinator, said the last received position of the vessel was about 80 nautical miles from the Basirih SAR dock.

Ship-tracking data gave investigators a detailed record of the voyage. According to [ANTARA News](https://www.antaranews.com/berita/5738684/sistem-aisits-ungkap-jejak-terakhir-km-virgo-di-selat-madura), Indonesia's AISITS monitoring system recorded the vessel leaving Terminal Jamrud Selatan at about 10 a.m. and accelerating from port speeds to 12 to 14 knots as it entered the West Surabaya shipping channel. The final signal was logged at 10:33 p.m. Saturday at 12.9 knots, after which the ship's data stopped reaching the system. Ship-systems expert Dhimas Widhi Handani of the Institut Teknologi Sepuluh Nopember said the gap could mean the vessel had moved out of range or that its AIS transponder had stopped transmitting.

## Casualties and Evacuation

The evacuation was carried out by several vessels passing through the area. ANTARA News [reported](https://www.antaranews.com/berita/5738679/basarnas-enam-meninggal-dalam-kecelakaan-kapal-virgo-transport-8) that the TB Mauhaw IX evacuated 16 people, 15 survivors and one who died, while the TB TCP took 38 people, of whom 33 survived and five died. The MV Haida evacuated 49 people, all of whom survived. Together, that accounted for 103 evacuated, with 97 survivors and six dead.

Sixteen of those rescued, including one body, were to be taken to Tuban port, while the remaining 87 were headed for Trisakti port in Banjarmasin. Families gathered at Trisakti port awaiting news of relatives.

With 130 people still unaccounted for, officials said the search remained the top priority. "The priority of the operation is directed at the search and rescue of the 130 people still being searched for," Sudayana said, as reported by ANTARA News [here](https://www.antaranews.com/berita/5738807/basarnas-prioritaskan-pencarian-130-orang-dalam-kecelakaan-kapal-virgo).

## Growing Questions About Maritime Safety

The disaster has renewed scrutiny of maritime safety oversight in Indonesia, an archipelago of some 17,000 islands where ferries and passenger ships are a primary mode of transport and sea routes are often more affordable and accessible than air travel. Safety standards are not always rigorously enforced, and the country has a comparatively high rate of maritime accidents.

M Aulia Rahman, chairman of the South Kalimantan branch of the Indonesian Transportation Society, said the incident should prompt a review of how sea travel is supervised, particularly the risks posed by bad weather. "We focus on the field of oversight and also providing input for the transportation sector, so that what happened can be material for evaluation and solutions going forward," he said, according to ANTARA News [here](https://www.antaranews.com/berita/5738687/mti-kalsel-soroti-keselamatan-pelayaran-di-tengah-evakuasi-virgo-8).

Indonesia's National Search and Rescue Agency, Basarnas, led an integrated sea-and-air operation. The Ministry of Transportation set up an emergency command post, the port operator Pelindo prepared facilities and a victim registration desk, and the regional RSUD Ulin hospital readied medical teams. Thirty volunteer ambulances were staged at Trisakti port, and the state insurer Jasa Raharja reported it was processing claims for victims.

## What's Next

Search teams widened their sweep using the vessel's last known position, the estimated accident site, and the locations where survivors were found, authorities said. The fate of the roughly 130 people still missing, and the precise cause of the capsize, remain the central questions as the operation continues.

Weather may complicate the effort: Indonesia's meteorological agency, BMKG, had warned of heavy rain and strong winds for Monday. For the families waiting at Banjarmasin's Trisakti port, the coming hours will determine whether the search ends in rescue or in grief.
