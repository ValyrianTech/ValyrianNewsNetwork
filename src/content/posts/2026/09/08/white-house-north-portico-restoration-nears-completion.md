# White House North Portico Restoration Nears Completion

Restoration work on the White House North Portico appears to be nearing completion, with crews removing scaffolding Tuesday after workers took down the shroud that had obscured the iconic entrance since July. The project, ordered by President Donald Trump, began in June and is finishing ahead of Chinese President Xi Jinping's state visit to Washington later this month, according to [AP News](https://apnews.com/article/white-house-north-portico-columns-restoration-161db72ffd1fcd13314d06755b279b88).

## A Historic Entrance Restored

The North Portico — the columned entrance facing Pennsylvania Avenue where presidents greet visiting heads of state — was first completed in 1830 during Andrew Jackson's presidency. Designed by architect Benjamin Henry Latrobe, the structure features large Ionic columns that have served as the backdrop for countless diplomatic ceremonies over nearly two centuries.

Administration officials have said the work involves refurbishing stonework and plaster on the structure due to routine damage from age and weather exposure. The project also includes "security enhancements and upgrades" to the entrance doors, as [ABC News](https://6abc.com/post/white-house-entrance-undergoing-security-enhancements-upgrades/19486229/) reported in July.

Workers placed a photorealistic tarp over the scaffolding on July 9 to conceal the restoration from public view. The shroud was removed over the Labor Day weekend, leaving only the scaffolding visible before its removal began this week, as [KYMA/NBC](https://kyma.com/news/top-stories/2026/09/06/white-house-north-portico-tarp-removed/) reported.

## Trump's Broader White House Overhaul

The North Portico restoration is just one element of Trump's sweeping renovation program at the executive mansion and federal properties across Washington. Interior Secretary Doug Burgum discussed the project in July, explaining that Trump noticed the condition of the columns while greeting world leaders.

"President Trump comes out to greet a world leader, he sees door dings in the pillars and says, 'Look at all this stuff that needs to be repaired,'" Burgum said on The Katie Miller Podcast, as [USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/politics/2026/07/09/trump-white-house-renovations-makeover-columns/90864395007/) reported. "It's all historic renovation work," with plaster being restored "from the door level all the way up to the crowns of those towers."

Trump has defended the project on Truth Social, describing the White House's condition as "deplorable" and promising to return it to what he called "the Glory of 100 years ago." The president's broader makeover agenda has included demolishing the East Wing to build a massive ballroom and underground bunker, constructing a new granite helipad on the South Lawn, paving over the Rose Garden, and installing 88-foot-tall flagpoles on the lawns.

## Timing Ahead of Xi's State Visit

The restoration is being completed ahead of Xi Jinping's state visit to Washington on September 24 — his first to the White House in more than a decade. Trump formally invited Xi during his own state visit to Beijing in May, and confirmed the date in July, as [The Independent](https://www.independent.co.uk/news/world/americas/us-politics/trump-china-xi-visit-ai-date-b3020554.html) reported. The visit is expected to focus significantly on artificial intelligence and trade relations.

Trump announced Sunday that construction of the new South Lawn helipad was also complete, another project timed to be ready for the Chinese leader's arrival, according to [Newsmax](https://www.newsmax.com/us/trump-white-house-helipad/2026/09/07/id/1268589/).

## Legal Challenges and Controversy

The broader renovation program has drawn significant legal opposition. The National Trust for Historic Preservation sued in December 2025 to halt the ballroom construction, arguing it proceeded without legally required reviews by federal planning commissions and without congressional authorization. The Supreme Court allowed construction to continue on August 31 in a 5-4 decision, finding the plaintiff likely lacks standing, as [SCOTUSblog](https://www.scotusblog.com/2026/08/supreme-court-allows-construction-on-white-house-ballroom-to-continue/) reported.

Chief Justice John Roberts dissented, writing: "We shape our buildings, and afterwards our buildings shape us. All the more reason to ensure that those responsible follow the rules in deciding what to tear down and what to build up at the People's House."

## What's Next

With scaffolding coming down and the project expected to be fully complete by mid-September, the North Portico will be ready to serve as the ceremonial backdrop for Xi's arrival. Trump must host the state dinner in existing spaces and amid ongoing ballroom construction — a circumstance that will give the president a new opportunity to showcase his renovation vision to a visiting world leader.

Historic preservationists and legal critics will be watching closely as the broader White House overhaul continues to unfold, with questions about regulatory oversight and congressional authority likely to persist as Trump's most ambitious projects move forward.
