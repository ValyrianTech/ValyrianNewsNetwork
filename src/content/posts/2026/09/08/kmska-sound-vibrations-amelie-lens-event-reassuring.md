# KMSKA: Amelie Lens Event Vibrations 'Very Reassuring'

The Royal Museum of Fine Arts Antwerp (KMSKA) has announced that sound and vibration measurements conducted during last weekend's techno event featuring Belgian DJ Amelie Lens returned "very reassuring" results, confirming no threat to the museum's artworks or its historic building. Museum director Carmen Willems confirmed the findings on Tuesday, following the sold-out open-air concert held at the Leopold De Waelplaats on September 5.

## Scientific Monitoring of a Landmark Event

An external company, working alongside a dedicated KMSKA team, set up measurement points primarily along the museum's front facade throughout the nine-hour event, which drew 14,000 attendees. The measurements were conducted as standard procedure whenever events take place in or near the museum, or during construction works.

"At no point did an alarm from the measurement equipment go off," Willems told [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/09/08/kmska-antwerpen-geluidstrillingen-event-amelie-lens-metingen/). "We saw that the difference with the situation without the event was truly negligible."

Willems compared potential risks to the effect of an earthquake on artworks, noting that "we were very far from that." The measurements were conducted to scientifically substantiate that vibrations from the event posed no danger to paintings, sculptures, or the building itself.

## Preventive Measures and Building Resilience

Before the concert, the museum coordinated with organizers on measures to minimize potential impact. Large bass speakers were deliberately directed toward the audience rather than the museum facade, and bass levels were slightly adjusted at the request of both the museum and the artist herself, who is currently pregnant.

The museum building, which dates from 1890, proved particularly resilient. "It has a very sturdy structure," Willems explained. "It actually keeps the sound out very well. Inside, visitors noticed some decibels, but at a level that wasn't disturbing, and only in the front rooms."

The event marked the first time a concert of this scale was held at the KMSKA, one of Belgium's most important cultural institutions. Organizers [Chaos in the City](https://www.lalibre.be/dernieres-depeches/2026/09/05/amelie-lens-en-tete-daffiche-du-chaos-in-the-city-a-anvers-HQKHLDTKSFAOHFNGIF67TSUYHY/) staged the event in partnership with the City of Antwerp, branding it "Amelie Lens presents EXHALE" to celebrate the release of her debut album "AURA."

## Community Response and Limited Complaints

Despite initial concerns from some neighborhood residents—including a petition signed by more than 400 people—the event passed with remarkably few complaints. The city received only 28 complaints, 18 of which came from a single individual. Mayor Els van Doesburg praised the event's success, noting that "most Antwerp residents are simply proud that we brought a world-class event here."

According to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/09/07/amelie-lens-kmska-antwerpen-evenement/), even local businesses that had initially closed in anticipation of disruption expressed regret afterward. Alain Van Poeyer of café Chatleroi, who had signed the petition against the event, admitted: "I think we could have perfectly opened our doors. Too bad we didn't."

## A Cultural Milestone

For Amelie Lens, performing at the KMSKA fulfilled a personal dream. "It's truly a dream come true that we get to do this on one of the most iconic spots in the city," she said when the event was [announced in May](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/05/27/amelie-lens-antwerpen-set-kmska/). "The KMSKA is not only a beautiful building, it's also my favorite museum."

The event, which ran from 14:00 to 23:00, featured supporting acts from Lens's label and included a surprise appearance by Belgian singer Angèle. After dark, the museum's facade became part of the show through video projections.

Willems, who attended the concert with her team to monitor the situation, acknowledged the event's cultural significance while emphasizing the museum's priorities. "Amelie Lens gave a cracking show," she said, "but we mainly want our artworks to still be able to crack afterwards."

## Looking Ahead

The city has indicated it does not rule out similar large-scale events at the museum in the future, though officials say they will first conduct a thorough evaluation. The successful outcome of the vibration measurements, combined with the overwhelmingly positive community response, may pave the way for more events of this kind at cultural heritage sites across Belgium.

As [The Brussels Times](https://www.brusselstimes.com/2304369/over-10000-attend-nine-hour-techno-fest-in-antwerp) reported, the event was the first of its scale at the KMSKA, bringing together music, art, architecture, and technology in a unique open-air setting. The museum's evidence-based approach to heritage protection demonstrates that cultural institutions can embrace contemporary events while maintaining rigorous conservation standards.
