# Xi Concludes US State Visit, Trade Truce Extended

Chinese President Xi Jinping concluded a three-day state visit to the United States on Friday, returning to Beijing after an unusually high-protocol engagement with President Donald Trump that produced a two-month extension of the US-China trade truce but no major breakthroughs on the core disputes dividing the world's two largest economies.

Xi's aircraft departed Washington on the afternoon of September 25 after senior US officials saw him off and a 21-gun salute was fired, according to [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/politics/leaders/20260926/d46b5583fed24347bb465184678cdc1d/c.html), the Chinese state news agency. At Joint Base Andrews, overseas Chinese and Chinese students lined the route waving the flags of both countries.

## A Reciprocal, and Symbolic, Encounter

The visit was a reciprocal return trip following Trump's own journey to China in May 2026, meaning the two leaders exchanged state visits within roughly six months — a frequency both sides framed as historic. It was Xi's first visit to the White House in more than a decade.

The symbolism was deliberate from the outset. Trump personally greeted Xi at Andrews Air Force Base on September 23, the first time a US president had received a foreign leader at the base since John F. Kennedy welcomed British Prime Minister Harold Macmillan in 1962. The welcome included a 21-gun salute and a fighter-jet flyover.

The following day brought a full White House arrival ceremony, remarks from both leaders, a Marine Corps Silent Drill Platoon performance and a Rose Garden military parade, followed by an Oval Office meeting, then a state dinner in the East Room that drew an extraordinary roster of American business leaders — including Nvidia's Jensen Huang, Apple's Tim Cook, Microsoft's Satya Nadella, OpenAI's Sam Altman and Tesla's Elon Musk.

On Friday, the final day, the two couples held tea at the White House before visiting the US National Archives and bidding farewell.

## A Truce Extended, Not Resolved

The most concrete outcome was the extension of the US-China trade truce, first reached in October 2025, by an additional two months to January 10, 2027. US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent announced the move as Xi arrived. Beyond that, the two sides announced no broader agreement.

China's trade leverage helps explain the dynamics. According to [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/xi-rolls-into-trump-summit-with-chinas-trade-engine-roaring-2026-09-21/), China entered the talks with its trade engine "roaring" and little urgent need to concede — a position that lowered expectations of a dramatic deal.

Speaking at the White House welcoming ceremony, Xi sought to cast the relationship in cooperative terms. "Although US-China cooperation cannot solve all the world's problems, without cooperation between the two countries, many of the world's problems will be hard to solve," he said, according to the [full text of his remarks](https://www.news.cn/politics/leaders/20260924/bfdbb11ef5044d96a910673a7396e29d/c.html) published by Xinhua.

Xi also invoked the language of strategic stability, describing competition as something that "should be benign and bounded" — "a race of catching up, not a win-lose gladiatorial contest" — and asserting that the so-called "Thucydides' Trap" of great-power conflict "can be overcome." He further announced that China would invite 100,000 American young people to study and exchange in China over the next five years, and confirmed that two giant pandas, Pingping and Fushuang, would soon arrive at Zoo Atlanta.

## Divergent Framings

The two sides' public messaging revealed subtle but real gaps. Xi characterized the relationship, according to [VOA](https://www.voachinese.com/a/xi-wrapping-up-trump-visit-with-tea-at-the-white-house-touting-very-successful-20260925/8204356.html), as "a constructive strategic relationship of stability based on mutual respect, fairness, and reciprocity." But US officials have stressed an additional clause. Randall Schriver and Chris Slevin, the Republican and Democratic leaders of the US-China Economic and Security Review Commission, told VOA before the visit that Washington adds "based on fairness and reciprocity" — language China's framing omits.

There was no joint press conference, and the opening remarks of the leaders' meeting were not made public, leaving Chinese state broadcaster CCTV footage as the primary public record of the sit-down. Xi pressed the US on Taiwan during the Oval Office meeting, asking Washington to "oppose Taiwan independence," though no shift in US policy was announced.

## Experts: Atmosphere Over Breakthrough

Analysts broadly agreed that the visit's principal achievement was atmospherics and image rather than policy.

Brian Wong, a scholar at the University of Hong Kong, told the [BBC's Chinese service](https://www.bbc.com/zhongwen/articles/cq2dwkw2j0r4o/simp) that the focus "was not a policy breakthrough, but the personal relationship between the two leaders." Judged by the protocol — the tarmac greeting, the White House meeting, the state dinner — he called the progress in the relationship "quite successful."

Yang Haowei of the International Crisis Group argued that the most important outcome was both sides projecting "a positive, friendly atmosphere and image." But he noted the two leaders' messages showed "a bit of a gap" on artificial intelligence — a field where the two countries are in intense competition. Xi devoted explicit attention to AI in his welcoming remarks, saying both nations "have the capability and the responsibility to develop and manage artificial intelligence well, ensuring that its development remains controlled by humans and benefits the people."

Yang further suggested that Xi appears to be "gradually gaining the upper hand," arguing that the high-protocol reception allowed Xi to demonstrate to both domestic and international audiences that China stands on a "footing of equals" with the United States.

Weng Lichung of Sam Houston State University told the BBC that China's command of critical resources such as rare earths gave it leverage, and that the US extended its elaborate reception precisely to "set a good atmosphere." In his view, the trade-truce extension was "already a good outcome," with harder issues left to future meetings.

## No Breakthrough on AI, Rare Earths or Taiwan

Notably absent from the visit's outcomes were concrete advances on the issues that most concern policymakers: artificial intelligence governance, rare-earth supply chains and Taiwan. Before the trip, Trump had said on his Truth Social platform that "Super Intelligence" would be a central topic, though he added he "hoped to put a stop to that."

In the background, broader structural pressures remained. China's trade surplus with the United States from January to August 2026 stayed near US$200 billion, up roughly US$14 billion year-on-year, underlining why Beijing felt little urgency to make concessions. Meanwhile, the White House's initial decision to bar CNN and three other outlets from covering the visit prompted a US media boycott, and while credentials were later restored, CNN remained excluded from the pooled filming mechanism.

## What to Watch

Both leaders confirmed that they will meet again before the end of 2026: at the APEC summit in Shenzhen, China, in November, and at the G20 summit in Miami, Florida, in December. Trump wrote on Truth Social that the Washington encounter was "one of friendship, strength, and success for both China and the US," adding, "We will meet again in China in November, and then at the G20 in Miami, Florida in December."

For now, the visit's significance may rest less in what it produced than in what it preserved. With both economies deeply intertwined and their competition widening across technology, resources and geopolitics, the meeting kept a fragile stability in place — buying time, and setting the stage for the next round of talks later this year.
