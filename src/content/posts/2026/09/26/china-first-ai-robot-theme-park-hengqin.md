# China Opens First Large-Scale AI Robot Theme Park

China has opened what it bills as the world's first large-scale "embodied intelligence" theme park, deploying hundreds of humanoid and AI robots across a major attraction in Hengqin, Zhuhai. The upgraded Hengqin Chimelong Spaceship Park reopened on September 24, 2026, after a partnership between cultural-tourism operator Chimelong Group and Shanghai-based robotics firm AGIBOT Innovation — marking China's first normalized, large-scale deployment of embodied AI robots in a live tourism setting.

According to [Xinhua News](http://www.news.cn/tech/20260926/9518dae58c1b4497ba0d4f0c47ab066e/c.html), the park has deployed embodied-intelligence robots covering six core scenarios. The robots are no longer confined to fixed programmed performances: they can autonomously perceive their surroundings to perform coordinated light-and-shadow dances, host interactive science classes, guide visitors, deliver supplies, and act as interactive "park companions."

## Context: A Bet on Technology and Tourism

The project sits at the intersection of two of Guangdong's strengths — robotics manufacturing and a vast cultural-tourism market. Per [Yangcheng Pai](https://m.21jingji.com/article/20260824/herald/8af517cbe32885b0e72f2f187f3d5e91.html), the collaboration pairs an embodied-intelligence industry searching for large-scale deployment scenarios with a tourism sector hunting for new experiences. Theme parks, hotels, and performances — with dense foot traffic and rich interaction needs — are increasingly seen as prime testing grounds.

For Chimelong, a 37-year-old operator that transformed Hengqin's tidal flats into a world-class resort, the park represents a pivot from traditional attractions toward "tech-immersive" tourism. "Technological innovation is the core secret to high-quality development in cultural tourism," Chimelong Group chairman Su Zhigang said, according to the [company's official release](https://www.chimelong.com/news/3198.html). "Embodied intelligence is driving the industry from simple sightseeing to a deeply immersive era."

## The Scale and the Scenarios

Early planning materials from [ITHome](https://www.ithome.com/1/004/731.htm) projected more than 300 robots and over 100 interaction points spanning performances, science popularization, guidance, retail, hotels, and sports. The [Global Times](https://www.globaltimes.cn/page/202609/1370949.shtml) reported the park features more than 100 robot interaction points across seven core scenarios, including entertainment, science education, tour guidance, service and retail, and sports competitions.

Robots of the same models that topped both the gold-medal and overall standings at the World Humanoid Robot Games are expected to appear regularly at the park, showcasing their medal-winning performances. On opening day, AGIBOT delivered its 20,000th embodied-intelligence robot, which participated in a large-scale human-robot opening performance.

Chimelong's release cites three industry-first achievements: the world's first normalized deployment of embodied intelligence in a cultural-tourism scenic area, the first one-stop operations-and-maintenance platform for such a site, and the first large-scale 5G-A embodied-intelligence application in a tourism setting. Three bionic robots, including "Coral Diva" and "Cosmic Diva," also made their global debut — alongside the unveiling of a joint laboratory for cultural-tourism embodied intelligence.

## From Display Piece to Companion

The shift the partners are emphasizing is qualitative, not just quantitative. A [China Daily](https://global.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202609/24/WS6ab4fa5ee4b06d4aa05600e2.html) report described the park as helping "promote the deep integration of AI technology into real-world cultural and tourism scenarios."

AGIBOT founder Deng Taihua framed the ambition in broader terms. "Robots are not just work assistants — they will also become life companions," he said, expressing hope that the park shapes the technology awareness of a new generation. The joint laboratory will use Chimelong's multiple parks as real-world proving grounds for algorithm R&D and scenario validation, with both sides aiming to move humanoid robotics in tourism "from single-point demonstrations to scalable replication."

## Challenges on the Road to Scale

Not everyone assumes the transition will be seamless. A [Southern Metropolis Daily](https://m.mp.oeeee.com/a/BAAFRD0000202608241650634.html) analysis flagged several hurdles that could determine whether the project becomes a replicable model or a temporary novelty.

The first is stability in open public scenes. In a theme park, visitors touch robots freely, crowds surge unpredictably, floors are uneven, and lighting shifts — conditions far harsher than a rehearsal. "The same algorithm can perform perfectly in a rehearsal environment, yet easily suffer navigation failures and abnormal movements in a noisy real-world setting," the outlet noted.

The second is content-hardware coordination: circus shows, parades, and interactive performances require deep integration of robot capabilities with creative scripts, demanding long-term collaboration between robotics engineers and entertainment directors. The third is long-duration operation — component wear, fault repair, and rapid on-site maintenance. And with many ordinary visitors, especially children, human-robot interaction safety redundancy and emergency response mechanisms remain unavoidable prerequisites.

## A Test Field, Not Just a Showcase

The broader significance may lie in what the park proves industrially. Xiang Ligang, a veteran telecom and technology policy observer, told the Global Times that the park's value goes beyond "bringing robots into theme parks" — it provides embodied AI with large-scale, complex, and high-frequency human-robot interaction scenarios that can feed continuous data accumulation and technological validation.

That framing reflects a national push. China's embodied intelligence market grew from roughly 213.3 billion yuan ($31.85 billion) in 2018 to an estimated 1.09 trillion yuan in 2026, a compound annual growth rate of 22 to 23 percent, according to the China Embodied Intelligence Industry Development Report (2026), as cited by the Global Times. The project is also tied to China's "AI + Consumption" initiative and the development of "new quality productive forces," as well as Hengqin's role as an international leisure tourism island supporting Macao's economic diversification.

Chimelong's release argued that China holds a first-mover advantage, noting that overseas theme-park robots "have mostly remained localized pilots, making normalized commercial operations difficult."

## What to Watch

The next test is whether the economics and reliability hold up. A [Tonghuashun](https://stock.10jqka.com.cn/20260920/c680089313.shtml) financial report noted that more than 300 of AGIBOT's full robot lineup went "on duty" at the park's opening.

If the park can sustain a closed loop of "hardware, content, operations, and commercial returns," analysts say it could offer a replicable template for deploying humanoid robots in consumer scenarios across China and beyond. If not, it may remain what the Southern Metropolis Daily called a phased technology highlight — impressive in the moment, but hard to scale.

For now, the Hengqin park stands as a high-profile bet that the road to commercializing embodied intelligence may run not only through factories, but right past the ticket gate.
