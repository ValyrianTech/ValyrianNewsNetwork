# China Opens Four High-Speed Rail Lines at Once

China National Railway Group announced that four new high-speed rail lines will begin commercial operation on September 28, 2026, spanning the country's northeast, north, central and northwest regions — a simultaneous opening the state operator described as a milestone for the national network. The centerpiece is the Xiong'an-Shangqiu section of the Beijing-Hong Kong high-speed railway, which closes the key northern gap in China's flagship north-south corridor.

According to [People's Daily](http://finance.people.com.cn/n1/2026/0926/c1004-40805730.html), the operator said all four lines have completed integrated testing, inspection and acceptance, and safety assessment, with track, communication and signaling, and train-control systems verified as stable and ready for safe operation.

## The Beijing-Hong Kong Corridor's Missing Link

The Xiong'an-Shangqiu section is the most consequential of the four openings. Running 552 kilometers at a design speed of 350 km/h, it links Xiong'an in Hebei province to Shangqiu in Henan, passing through parts of Hebei, Shandong and Henan. Sixteen stations serve the route, and up to 64 trains per day will run initially, all as cross-line services.

The line cuts the fastest travel time between Xiong'an and Shangqiu to 2 hours 27 minutes and, for the first time, brings high-speed rail to counties including Renqiu, Suning, Hejian and Shenzhou. It also enables new direct services, including the first-ever Xiong'an-Shanghai connection via Shanghai Hongqiao.

"After the Xiongshang section opens, the Xiong'an New Area, the southern Hebei plain, southwest Shandong and eastern Henan will be more closely connected," said Han Yong, deputy director of the Beijing Railway Bureau Passenger Transport Center, as [reported](https://m.ithome.com/html/1007094.htm) by IT Home.

The opening fills a long-standing gap in the Beijing-Hong Kong corridor. Most other sections are already operating, from Shangqiu south through Hefei, Nanchang, Ganzhou, Shenzhen and into Hong Kong. Yet the corridor is not yet fully continuous: the Nanchang-Jiujiang segment remains under construction, and the Beijing Fengtai-Xiong'an link is still in preliminary work.

## Westward: Xi'an-Ankang and Yichang-Xingshan

The 171-kilometer Xi'an-Ankang section of the Xi'an-Chongqing high-speed railway opens at 350 km/h, serving five stations and cutting the fastest Xi'an East-Ankang West journey to 45 minutes. Up to 20 trains per day will run, including 10 cross-line services.

The line cuts through the Qinling Mountains, an area of complex geology and high ecological sensitivity where construction began in June 2021. Its completion strengthens the Xi'an hub and links the Guanzhong Plain to the Qinba mountain region of southern Shaanxi, an area official communications frame as a beneficiary of rural revitalization, according to [IT Home](https://m.ithome.com/html/1007103.htm).

In central China, the 101-kilometer Yichang-Xingshan line connects the Shanghai-Chongqing-Chengdu corridor with the Zhengzhou-Chongqing corridor, [Cover News reported](https://www.thecover.cn/news/S3IFTvAiYo2H90qSdq8Jkw==). With a bridge-tunnel ratio exceeding 90%, the route runs almost entirely through mountainous terrain.

"The bridge-tunnel ratio exceeds 90% for the whole line; the vast majority runs through high mountains and steep ridges," said Du Hailiang, a construction management section chief at the Wuhan Railway Bureau.

The line's signature engineering challenge was the Xingshan East Tunnel, which broke through in January 2026 after roughly 1,900 days of work. Its completion is expected to reduce the fastest Wuhan-Chongqing journey from 4 hours 56 minutes to 3 hours 54 minutes.

## North to the Cold Frontier

The Harbin-Yichun line becomes China's northernmost high-speed railway. Running 318 kilometers at 250 km/h through Heilongjiang province, it serves nine stations, with Yichun West standing at latitude 47°N — the highest-latitude high-speed rail station in the country.

The line brings high-speed rail to Suihua and Yichun for the first time, cutting the fastest Harbin-Yichun journey to 1 hour 46 minutes. Up to 38 trains per day will run initially. Officials emphasize its significance for Northeast revitalization, tourism and the region's ecological resources.

The engineering conditions are extreme. Yichun West is China's first high-speed rail station house built on permafrost in a severe-cold region, where winter temperatures can fall to −40°C.

"Yichun West station sits at 47°N; it is China's first high-speed rail station house built on permafrost in a severe-cold region, and the largest newly built station house on the entire line," said Shi Qinwei, project head for China Railway Construction, according to [Xinhua/China Economic Net](http://www.ce.cn/xwzx/gnsz/gdxw/202609/t20260925_3235664.shtml). The station can accommodate 1,500 waiting passengers at once. Notably, the line will apply a market-based, flexible pricing mechanism.

## Why It Matters

The four openings advance China's "Eight Vertical and Eight Horizontal" master plan, the framework defining the country's high-speed rail grid. Each line belongs to or connects key corridors: the Beijing-Hong Kong vertical, the Shanghai-Chongqing-Chengdu and Zhengzhou-Chongqing horizontals, and the northeast network.

Taken together, the openings reflect a deliberate strategy of simultaneously densifying the core network while extending service to previously unconnected counties and remote regions. Official framing consistently ties the projects to rural revitalization, Northeast revitalization and high-quality development of the Yangtze Economic Belt.

The scale of a single-day, four-line opening is unusual and signals both the maturity of China's construction pipeline and its political priority on connectivity as a driver of regional economic integration.

## What to Watch

The immediate focus will be on ridership and whether the new cross-line services — such as Xiong'an-Shanghai and Beijing West-Hefei — draw the traffic officials expect. Full realization of the Beijing-Hong Kong corridor still depends on completing the Nanchang-Jiujiang segment and the Beijing Fengtai-Xiong'an link.

For the northeast, the Harbin-Yichun line will be watched as a test of high-speed rail's ability to spur development in sparsely populated, severe-cold regions. And with the market-based pricing mechanism on that route, observers will track whether flexible fares become a wider model for newer lines in lower-density markets.
