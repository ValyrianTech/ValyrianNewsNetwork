# Flemish Cultural Sector Warns Against Fresh Budget Cuts

Belgium's broad cultural sector has warned that further budget cuts being weighed by the Flemish government would have a "huge impact" on employment and public accessibility, as the region's coalition races to find between €1.5 billion and €2 billion in savings before a Monday deadline.

Sector umbrella bodies say a possible 10 percent reduction in culture subsidies — reported in the press but not yet confirmed by the government — would be disproportionate for a policy area that now accounts for just 0.72 percent of the Flemish budget. The warning, issued in a joint press release and amplified across the sector this week, comes days before Minister-President Matthias Diependaele (N-VA) is due to deliver his September declaration on 28 September.

## A sector 'at a lower limit'

The cultural sector's objections were voiced most directly by Leen Laconte of the arts umbrella OKO (Overleg Kunstenorganisaties), who told [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/liveblog/cultuursector-vreest-verdere-besparingen-enorme-impact-op-tewe~1780024580839/) that years of repeated savings had already stretched the sector thin.

"The sector has been suffering from cuts for several years," Laconte said. "In 2026 we have already saved disproportionately compared with other sectors. Now another 10 percent blow... would have a huge impact on employment but also on accessibility."

She stressed that Flemish subsidies are the foundation on which cultural organisations sustain their operations. "Without those Flemish subsidies it is very difficult to maintain operations structurally. And if you can't, you also can't seek extra resources in the form of ticket income, sponsorship, cake sales, whatever you name. And in many of our sectors we are at a kind of lower limit."

The joint press release was issued by three sector umbrella bodies — De Federatie (amateur arts and socio-cultural work), OKO (the arts sector) and OCE (cultural heritage) — on 21 September, and has since been recirculated by cultural organisations and the artist association NICC, which launched a petition against the cuts.

## A shrinking share of the budget

At the heart of the sector's argument is a simple ratio: culture's slice of the Flemish budget has been steadily shrinking. According to the umbrella bodies, the culture budget stood at 1.99 percent of the total Flemish budget in 2004 and has since fallen to 0.72 percent.

In the previous year's savings round, the cultural sector contributed 13.5 percent — or €23 million — of the total savings found, a share the sector describes as disproportionate relative to its small footprint in the budget. Years of partial or non-indexation of subsidies, combined with inflation, have further eroded the sector's financial resilience, the bodies argue.

"With barely 0.72 percent of the entire Flemish budget... Therefore, saving on culture is saving on each of us," the [press release](https://www.overlegkunsten.org/nieuws/mogelijke-besparing-van-10-op-cultuur-help-het-persbericht-mee-verspreiden) states.

Hanne Geukens of De Federatie made a related point about the arithmetic of the budget exercise itself. "What goes to culture now is a small bite out of the whole budget. So saving on culture will not help to close that budget," she said, as reported by [De Wereld Morgen](https://www.dewereldmorgen.be/artikel/2026/09/23/mogelijke-besparingen-op-cultuur-stuiten-op-kritiek-cultuur-is-geen-extraatje).

## 'Culture is not an extra'

Beyond the numbers, the sector has sought to frame culture as a public good that touches everyday life, spanning museums, libraries, amateur arts, heritage and the performing arts.

Nele Provoost of OCE put it plainly. "People sometimes think culture is niche, but it touches on many things people deal with daily... It therefore affects not just a small group of people from the cultural sector, but society as a whole. Culture is not an extra," she said.

The sector's figures underline that reach: 2 million active amateur artists in Belgium, 10 million unique visitors a year to socio-cultural organisations, and 5.2 million annual museum visitors. According to the [NICC petition page](https://nicc.be/engagement/zware-besparingen-bij-cultuur-teken-mee-de-petitie/), 81 percent of Flemish people visit a monument or heritage site each year and 89 percent attend a concert or performance. Two in three Flemish people say art contributes to social connection, and 46 percent consider it crucial for a healthy society.

The umbrella bodies also point to the sector's broader value, citing an OECD report from 2023 and research from Flanders' Kenniscentrum Cultuuronderzoek on the social, economic, health and cognitive effects of culture.

## The budget puzzle

The cultural sector's warning lands in the middle of a tense Flemish budget negotiation between the governing coalition of N-VA, Vooruit and CD&V, which must balance the budget toward equilibrium from 2027.

Negotiations have stretched on for days. By Friday evening, the amount still to be found had narrowed to a range of €1.6 billion to €1.9 billion, according to VRT NWS, with one minister telling the broadcaster that an agreement would likely only come "before Sunday night." Other measures on the table include a potential increase in the water bill, higher registration duties on homes above €500,000 and possible changes to child benefits — proposals that have drawn objections from coalition partners.

For the sector, the timing and framing matter. The cultural umbrella bodies argue that cuts undermine the legal certainty and stability promised under the culture decrees, which set out the quality frameworks and objectives organisations work within.

Cultural organisations across Flanders have amplified the message. The Antwerp arts centre [hetpaleis](https://www.hetpaleis.be/nl/nieuws/besparingen-op-cultuur) shared the press release with its audience, noting that the sector "substantially contributes to the realisation of Article 23 of the Constitution," which enshrines every citizen's right to cultural development.

## What to watch

Everything now hinges on the 28 September deadline, when Diependaele delivers his September declaration and the coalition aims to have a budget agreement in hand. Until then, the precise fate of the culture budget remains uncertain: the 10 percent figure is based on press reports, and the government has not officially confirmed a decision on culture cuts. The final savings target has been reported variously as €1.5 billion to €2 billion and, more recently, €1.6 billion to €1.9 billion.

What is clear is that the sector has already organised to push back — through a joint front of umbrella bodies, a petition, and organisations relaying the message to their own audiences. Whether that pressure changes the arithmetic in a negotiation dominated by a much larger deficit figure is the question that Monday will answer.

As the press release states: "Culture is not a cost item limited to the resources the government invests in it." The coming days will test whether the government agrees.
