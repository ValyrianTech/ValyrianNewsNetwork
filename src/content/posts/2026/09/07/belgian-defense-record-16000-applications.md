# Belgian Defense Hits Record 16,000 Applications as New Recruitment Year Begins

Belgium's Defense department received 16,000 applications last year — an absolute record — as Defense Minister Theo Francken gave the starting signal for the new recruitment year on Monday morning in the port of Brussels. The new recruitment cycle opens with a record 5,600 available positions, signaling a surge in interest in military careers amid ongoing European security concerns.

According to [Het Laatste Nieuws](https://www.hln.be/binnenland/francken-zeer-trots-na-16-000-sollicitaties-bij-defensie-nieuw-rekruteringsjaar-van-start-met-recordaantal-functies~ac1406b0/), the 5,600 open positions include more than 3,000 active military personnel, 1,500 reservists, and nearly 1,000 civilian staff roles. Nearly 3,000 new employees were recruited last year — also a record — and Francken expects to exceed that threshold in 2026.

## A Turning Point for Military Careers

"Five to ten years ago, such figures were not predictable," Francken told reporters, expressing pride in the recruitment numbers. "But we also need them if we want to reach our goal of 34,500 military personnel by 2035. We are now at just over 25,000."

The minister's confidence reflects a broader transformation in how young Belgians view military service. "Defense is an attractive employer," he said, pointing to the record application numbers as evidence of a cultural shift.

About 1,800 military personnel who could retire at age 56 have indicated they will continue serving longer, providing additional stability to the force as it works toward its expansion targets.

## Addressing the Dropout Challenge

One of the most significant hurdles to meeting recruitment goals has been the high dropout rate during initial training — a phenomenon known as the "choc militaire." The rate, which previously stood at 40 percent, has dropped to just over 30 percent, and an action plan aims to reduce it further.

Notably, the dropout rate later in military careers is remarkably low at just 2-3 percent, suggesting that those who complete initial training tend to stay committed long-term.

To help meet its targets, Defense has implemented several adjustments, including relaxed medical selection criteria and, for reservists, a raised maximum age. New training opportunities have also been introduced, including a master's degree in cybersecurity and a master's in pharmacy, expanding the range of educational pathways available through the military.

## Military Service Year Gains Momentum

A key component of the recruitment strategy is the voluntary military service year (militair dienstjaar), which allows young people aged 18 to 25 to serve as full-time reservists for one year without being deployed on missions. Last week, the first 500 young people began their service year, and Francken noted with satisfaction that they reported for duty at 7:00 AM on Monday morning.

For next year, 625 places will be opened for the program. At the end of October, everyone who turns 18 in 2027 will again receive a letter in the mail inviting them to consider the opportunity.

The program has proven highly competitive — only one in seven candidates was selected — reflecting growing interest among young Belgians in exploring military life. As [Het Laatste Nieuws reported](https://www.hln.be/binnenland/torre-18-daan-18-en-nena-18-beginnen-in-september-aan-hun-vrijwillig-dienstjaar-in-het-leger-leren-schieten-is-mijn-jongensdroom~af9bf5c8/) last month, young participants are drawn by a mix of personal challenge, family tradition, and a desire to contribute to society.

## Strategic Expansion Amid Geopolitical Pressures

The recruitment push comes as Belgium pursues a major military expansion under its Strategic Vision, targeting 34,500 active military personnel, 8,500 civilian staff, and 12,800 reservists. The government has budgeted approximately 50 billion euros over nine years for personnel, including pensions.

The expansion is part of broader NATO efforts to strengthen European defense capabilities amid heightened geopolitical tensions following Russia's invasion of Ukraine. Belgium has been increasing defense spending and acquiring new equipment, including F-35 fighter jets.

According to the [official Belgian Defense website](https://www.mil.be/nl/news/rekrutering-2026-defensie-zoekt-4800-extra-collega-s-waarvan-500-via-militair-dienstjaar/), last year's recruitment campaign opened 4,800 vacancies, making Defense the country's largest employer recruiter. The increase to 5,600 positions this year reflects the accelerating pace of the expansion program.

## The Road Ahead

Despite the record numbers, Francken acknowledged that a "substantial catch-up operation" is still needed to meet the 2035 targets. The aging of the military workforce remains a key challenge — the minister has previously described the army as "a very old army" — and Defense must recruit 5,000-6,000 people annually to both expand and replace retiring personnel.

French-language coverage from [Belafrika Media TV](https://www.belafrika.tv/defense-5600-postes-a-pourvoir-un-recrutement-record/) confirmed the same figures, noting that the minister remains confident the momentum can be sustained.

As the new recruitment year gets underway, all eyes will be on whether the record application numbers translate into the sustained growth needed to meet Belgium's ambitious defense targets by 2035. With new training programs, relaxed selection criteria, and an expanding voluntary service year, Defense is positioning itself as a career destination for a new generation of Belgians.
