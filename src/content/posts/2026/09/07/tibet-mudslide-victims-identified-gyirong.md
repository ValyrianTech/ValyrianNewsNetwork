# Tibet Mudslide: Some Victims Identified in Gyirong Disaster

Authorities in Tibet have confirmed the identities of some victims from the devastating mudslide that struck Gyirong Port on August 26, as forensic teams intensify efforts to identify the dead amid an international relief operation, according to [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/local/20260907/e0e5e2f0021f4fecbd4c97abfea91ae0/c.html).

The Gyirong County "8-26" Mudslide Disaster Emergency Rescue Command has established an expert team comprising more than 60 forensic technicians and activated three specialized laboratories equipped with advanced instruments. Officials stated that identification work is being conducted in strict accordance with Chinese laws and regulations while fully referencing international practices, maintaining the dignity of the deceased and respecting cultural customs.

## A Catastrophic Glacial Collapse

The disaster began on the morning of August 26 when a massive glacial ice-rock avalanche occurred on the southern slope of Langtang Lirung peak in Nepal at approximately 5,200 meters elevation. The collapse rapidly descended, scouring the mountainside and forming a giant mudslide that traveled roughly 22 kilometers to impact the Gyirong Port area at approximately 1,800 meters elevation in just six to seven minutes.

According to [China News Service](https://www.chinanews.com.cn/gn/2026/09-06/10691481.shtml), as of September 5 at 18:00, the disaster had caused 43 confirmed deaths and 519 people missing on the Chinese side. Two people were rescued alive, and 993 personal belongings were recovered. The mudslide flattened approximately 0.7 square kilometers of the port area, destroying 27 buildings including the border inspection facility and the Resuo Bridge.

On the Nepalese side, the toll has been far higher, with 1,344 deaths and 4,898 missing persons reported, according to data compiled from multiple sources. The disparity stems largely from population density differences along the disaster path, as experts have noted.

## The Challenge of Victim Identification

The identification work carries significant international weight. Among the missing on the Chinese side are 261 foreign nationals from 23 countries, including 104 from Nepal, 49 from India, 33 from Latvia, 18 from the United States, and 15 from the United Kingdom. The extreme force of the mudslide means many victims may require DNA analysis for identification, underscoring the scale of the forensic challenge.

Rescue workers have been conducting fine-grained, partitioned searches in the core disaster area, as [Guangming Daily](https://m.gmw.cn/2026-09/07/content_38987548.htm) reported. The mudslide deposits in the affected zone average four meters in thickness and reach up to 16 meters in some areas, complicating recovery efforts.

## Understanding the Casualty Disparity

Experts attribute the vastly different casualty figures between China and Nepal to several factors. Yang Yabo, director of the South Asia Research Institute at the Tibet Academy of Social Sciences, told [Beijing Youth Daily via NetEase](https://m.163.com/news/article/L66C0VBI0001899O.html) that casualties from geological disasters largely depend on the scale of population exposure along the disaster path.

On the Chinese side, the affected area around the port had a daily population of only 200-300 people, primarily border inspection officers, customs officials, nearby merchants, and construction workers. In contrast, the Nepalese side had densely populated towns along the Bhotekoshi River valley, including popular mountain trekking routes frequented by international tourists.

Ouyang Chaojun, deputy director of the Key Laboratory of Mountain Hazards and Earth Surface Processes at the Chinese Academy of Sciences, noted that because the mudslide reached Gyirong Port in only about seven minutes, people had no time to evacuate, contributing to the severity of casualties.

## Climate Change and Glacial Instability

The disaster was officially attributed to glacier instability under long-term climate warming. The Tibetan Plateau has warmed at approximately 0.3-0.4 degrees Celsius per decade over the past 50 years — twice the global average — and glacial retreat and ice lake expansion are at record highs.

A barrier lake that formed above the Gyirong Port core area after the disaster has now fully drained, with the river channel resuming flow and the danger essentially resolved, as [Guangming Daily](https://m.gmw.cn/2026-09/06/content_38987007.htm) reported. The dam was approximately 60 meters high, 1,100 meters long, and 300 meters wide.

## Ongoing Recovery and International Cooperation

China has sent 10 forensic experts to Nepal to assist with victim identification there, and has provided emergency material assistance including 320 tons of Bailey bridge components. The G216 national road to Gyirong Port was reopened on September 2, enabling deeper search operations.

On September 1, Tibet held a memorial ceremony for the victims, as [Xinhua](https://www1.xinhuanet.com/local/20260901/18c1a0d3bba6416b8434317be279f777/c.html) reported. Officials have indicated they will conduct in-depth disaster risk assessments before deciding on the future functionality of Gyirong Port, a historically significant border crossing that has served as a key trade route between China and Nepal for centuries.

As forensic identification work continues, the full human toll of this disaster — and its implications for glacial hazard management in a warming world — will become clearer in the weeks ahead.
