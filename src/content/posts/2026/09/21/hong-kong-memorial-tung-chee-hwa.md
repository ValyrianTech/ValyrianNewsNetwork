# Hong Kong Holds State-Level Memorial for Tung Chee-hwa

A public memorial ceremony for Tung Chee-hwa, the first Chief Executive of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region, was held on the morning of 20 September at the Hong Kong Funeral Home in North Point, according to [People's Daily](http://politics.people.com.cn/n1/2026/0921/c461001-40802263.html). Tung, who led Hong Kong through its 1997 handover and served two terms as its top official, died of illness at 9:57 p.m. on 8 September at the age of 89. His coffin was draped in the national flag of the People's Republic of China, and the arrangements were widely compared to a state funeral.

## A Ceremony at the Highest Level

The mourning hall, set up in the Fuhai Hall, was presided over by Leung Chun-ying, a Vice Chairman of the Chinese People's Political Consultative Conference (CPPCC) and a former Hong Kong Chief Executive, serving as deputy director of the funeral committee. Shi Taifeng — a member of the Communist Party of China (CPC) Political Bureau, a CPPCC Vice Chairman, and head of the CPC Central Organization Department — delivered the eulogy as director of the committee.

Shi's eulogy reviewed Tung's life and his contributions to the country, Hong Kong and society, describing him in the official framing as an "outstanding contributor to One Country, Two Systems," a "renowned patriotic personage," an "outstanding social activist," and a "close friend of the Communist Party of China," the [China News Service](https://www.chinanews.com.cn/gn/2026/09-20/10700067.shtml) reported.

A banner reading "Deeply Mourning Mr. Tung Chee-hwa" hung above his portrait, with wreaths offered by his family. Attendees bowed three times before the portrait and paid final respects before his body. Also present were Wang Dongfeng, CPPCC Vice Chairman and Secretary-General; Xia Baolong, director of the CPC Central Hong Kong and Macao Work Office and the State Council Hong Kong and Macao Affairs Office; and HKSAR Chief Executive John Lee Ka-chiu, alongside heads of central government offices in Hong Kong, principal HKSAR officials, local deputies to the National People's Congress, and CPPCC National Committee members.

## Funeral Arrangements and Protocol

The lying-in-state was held from 3 p.m. to 10 p.m. on Saturday 19 September. The public memorial and coffin-sealing ceremony began at 9 a.m. on Sunday, and the cortege departed at 11 a.m., according to [Lianhe Zaobao](https://www.zaobao.com.sg/news/china/story20260920-9704510). The funeral committee comprised 87 members drawn from central government organs and across Hong Kong's political and business circles. Its four deputy directors were Leung Chun-ying, Wang Dongfeng, Xia Baolong and John Lee.

As a further mark of respect, the Hong Kong Jockey Club cancelled the race meeting scheduled for 20 September at Sha Tin Racecourse and closed all off-course betting branches for the day. Condolence counters were opened by the Home Affairs Department at locations in Kai Tak, Causeway Bay and Tsuen Wan.

## A Level Above Precedent

Since the handover, only a small number of Hong Kong leaders have received the highest funeral honours. The closest precedents are An Zijie, who died in 2000, and Henry Fok, who died in 2006 — both deceased CPPCC Vice Chairmen. For those three earlier figures, the funeral committee director role was filled by the First Vice Chairman of the CPPCC. In Tung's case, the role went to Shi Taifeng, who holds both Political Bureau membership and the CPPCC First Vice Chairmanship. As [Lianhe Zaobao](https://www.zaobao.com.sg/news/china/story20260916-9682476) noted, this places the arrangements at a level higher than precedent.

Both Tung's and Fok's funerals featured national flag-draped coffins and CPPCC-led funeral arrangements. But two decades on, Beijing's positioning of Tung extends beyond that of a patriotic businessman or CPPCC leader to include his historical role as Hong Kong's first post-handover Chief Executive.

As [BBC News Chinese](https://www.bbc.com/zhongwen/articles/cky9z175jgdlo/simp) observed, no member of the CPC Politburo Standing Committee attended in person; condolences were conveyed through wreaths, telegrams and statements. Yet the seniority of the officiating officials and the wording of the eulogy place Tung's honours on a par with Fok's 2006 funeral. For Hong Kong's first post-handover leader, the arrangements carry indicator significance — setting a template both as the verdict on his political career and as a reference for the honours due to future Chief Executives.

## A Contested Legacy

Born in Shanghai in 1937 to shipping magnate Tung Ho-yun, Tung moved with his family to Hong Kong in the 1940s. He studied engineering at the University of Liverpool, worked in the United States, then returned to Hong Kong to steer the family shipping business through the 1980s downturn. In 1996 he won election by the Selection Committee to become the first Chief Executive, taking office on 1 July 1997.

His two terms spanned some of Hong Kong's most turbulent years — the Asian financial crisis, a prolonged property-market decline, and the 2003 SARS outbreak. As [Initium Media](https://theinitium.com/20260909-hongkong-passing-of-tung-chee-hwa-zh-hans/) framed it, Tung's era embodied Hong Kong's "borrowed time" under One Country, Two Systems. Supporters credit him with shouldering historical responsibility at a critical period and laying the foundations for the SAR system. Critics say he failed to respond effectively to democratic aspirations, and his push to legislate under Article 23 of the Basic Law triggered the first mass protest after the handover. He resigned in 2005.

Tributes crossed the political spectrum. Chief Executive John Lee said Tung "took serving the country and serving Hong Kong as his mission throughout his life," adding that his contributions "will remain forever in our hearts." Former Chief Executive Donald Tsang recalled Tung's "selfless work ethic, working year-round without rest." The British Consulate-General in Hong Kong offered "sincere condolences," noting Tung studied marine engineering at the University of Liverpool and was a lifelong Liverpool FC supporter.

## What's Next

With the coffin-sealing and cortege complete, attention turns to how the ceremony sets a template. Tung's funeral is the first for a former Hong Kong Chief Executive since the handover, and the honours paid to him are likely to shape expectations for the treatment of his successors. The pallbearer list had not been fully detailed at the time of the ceremony, and no wreath bearing the personal name of a top Party leader was visible — details that analysts will weigh as signals of how Beijing chooses to commemorate the architects of One Country, Two Systems.
