# Belgian Diesel Price Hits Record 2.501 Euros per Liter

The maximum price of diesel (B7) in Belgium rose to a new all-time record of 2.501 euros per liter, the Federal Public Service for Economy (FOD Economie) announced, marking the highest level ever recorded for the fuel and deepening pressure on households and businesses already contending with surging energy costs.

The increase adds 3.3 eurocents to the previous price and surpasses the prior record of 2.489 euros per liter set on 9 April 2026. According to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/liveblog/dieselprijs-stijgt-naar-nieuw-record-2501-euro-per-liter~1774343778229/), the record follows a rapid September escalation: diesel crossed 2.4 euros per liter for the first time since April on 9 September, climbed 6.1 cents to 2.468 euros on 15 September, and then rose the final 3.3 cents to its record level.

Belgium regulates the maximum retail price of petroleum products, meaning the record is not merely a market event but an official national threshold published by the FOD Economie and effective the following day.

## Why Diesel Matters

Diesel is the backbone fuel of freight transport, logistics, agriculture, construction machinery and shipping. Because it powers the movement of goods, a diesel price shock transmits quickly into the real economy and, eventually, into consumer prices.

The immediate cause is the war in the Middle East. Price spikes in refined oil products are a consequence of the US–Iran conflict and repeated disruption to shipping through the Strait of Hormuz, the key chokepoint for Gulf oil and liquefied natural gas. Brent crude traded above 107 dollars per barrel in mid-September, while European gas prices on the Dutch TTF benchmark exceeded 83 euros per megawatt-hour, the highest since December 2022.

## Economic Pain

The sustained high price poses a direct threat to sectors that cannot quickly reduce their fuel dependence. Credit insurer Allianz Trade warned that high diesel prices are causing "perhaps the most direct economic pain" for many companies.

"A haulier cannot switch to electric long-distance transport overnight," said Johan Geeroms, Director Risk Underwriting Benelux at Allianz Trade, in an analysis [reported by Transport Online](https://www.transport-online.nl/139051/allianz-trade-hoge-dieselprijs-veroorzaakt-directe-economische-pijn-bij-transportbedrijven/). "A farmer needs fuel for machines and at the same time faces more expensive fertiliser. In construction, costs for diesel, materials and financing are rising."

The pressure reflects a broader refining bottleneck. The European diesel refining margin reached 74 euros per barrel in August, nearly double the five-year average, as Middle East refineries and Russian facilities damaged in the conflict constrained supplies of finished fuel.

## Households Feel the Strain

The diesel record is the sharpest edge of a wider energy squeeze. In September, the estimated annual electricity bill for an average Flemish household signing a new contract rose to 1.378 euros, the highest since May 2023, while the estimated annual gas bill climbed 118 euros to 1.898 euros, the highest since February 2023, according to the Flemish energy regulator and [reported by V-Nieuws](https://www.v-nieuws.be/energiefactuur-blijft-exploderen-vlaamse-gezinnen-betalen-de-rekening/).

From 1 October, social energy tariffs for vulnerable households will also rise. The federal regulator [CREG](https://www.creg.be/nl/publicaties/nota-z3308) confirmed increases of 7 percent for electricity and 11.4 percent for gas, after applying quarterly caps that limited what would otherwise have been steeper increases.

The cost squeeze has spilled into politics. "This government leaves our families out in the cold," said Kurt Ravyts, a member of parliament for Vlaams Belang. "Affordable energy should be a basic right, not a luxury product."

## A Wider Backdrop

Belgian inflation in the second quarter of 2026 stood at 3.8 percent, above its neighbours — the Netherlands at 2.8 percent, Germany at 2.6 percent and France at 2.4 percent — largely driven by energy prices, which rose 16 percent over three months, according to the FOD Economie's Price Observatory. French pump diesel also hit a record, averaging 2.41 euros per liter, showing the spike is regional rather than purely Belgian.

Allianz Trade noted that Europe is better prepared on physical supply security than during the 2022 energy crisis, with expanded LNG import capacity, more renewables and more energy-conscious consumers. But governments have less fiscal room to compensate households and businesses, and consumer demand is weaker.

"The European economy can absorb a period of high gas prices, but the room to absorb new setbacks is small," Geeroms said. "When gas stays expensive for a long time, that bill works through into purchasing power, production and ultimately economic growth."

## What to Watch

The record diesel price is unlikely to fall quickly while the Middle East conflict and shipping disruptions persist. The key questions are whether refinery capacity recovers, whether the Strait of Hormuz reopens to normal traffic, and whether Belgian and European policymakers can shield vulnerable households as winter approaches. With gas storage unusually low and the heating season ahead, energy prices remain the central economic risk facing the country.
