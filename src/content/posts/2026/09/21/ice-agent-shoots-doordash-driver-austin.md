# ICE Agent Shoots DoorDash Driver in Austin, Spurring Outcry

A federal immigration agent shot and wounded a 28-year-old Venezuelan man in North Austin on Sunday, an incident that triggered protests, drew sharp condemnation from city and county officials, and renewed national scrutiny of force used by U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement.

The man, identified by his attorney as Wilber Rafael Garces Perez, was shot just before 1 p.m. local time near the intersection of Anderson Square and Research Boulevard (U.S. 183). According to [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/09/20/nx-s1-5975517/ice-shooting-austin-texas), he was reportedly working as a DoorDash delivery driver at the time. He was struck in the torso and taken to Dell Seton Medical Center in serious but stable condition.

Later that day, he was released from the hospital into ICE custody and moved to an undisclosed facility, according to his attorney. The Department of Homeland Security said in a statement that his condition was stable and that he was "in federal custody pending removal," as [KUT News](https://www.kut.org/austin/2026-09-20/man-injured-in-shooting-by-ice-officer-in-north-austin) reported.

## What Happened

Accounts of the moments leading up to the shooting remain contested and incomplete. Austin-Travis County EMS Chief Robert Luckritz confirmed medics received a call from the Austin Police Department at about 12:56 p.m. and arrived to find one man shot in the torso, according to [MySA](https://www.mysanantonio.com/news/austin/article/austin-texas-ice-shooting-22441527.php).

Austin Police Chief Lisa Davis said preliminary information indicated there may have been a brief foot pursuit, and [USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/state/texas/2026/09/21/austin-texas-ice-shooting-update/91870931007/) reported that she described the man as having been shot after a "small foot pursuit." Police confirmed that Austin officers were not involved in the shooting itself and assisted only with traffic control.

A dark blue Toyota Corolla was left at the scene with damage to its passenger-side door and at least two apparent bullet holes on the passenger side, according to [CBS Austin](https://cbsaustin.com/news/local/ice-shooting-in-north-austin-sparks-protest). A bystander's Facebook livestream captured the sound of tire screeches and gunfire, though the exact sequence of events remains unclear.

## A Pattern of Concern

The shooting is the latest in a series of violent encounters involving federal immigration agents. According to a CNN analysis cited in the research, at least 20 people have been shot by on-duty ICE and Customs and Border Protection agents during the second Trump administration, eight of them fatally.

In July, federal agents in Houston shot and killed Lorenzo Salgado Araujo, a 52-year-old Mexican national and father of three, during a traffic stop; he was not the target of the warrant agents were executing. The first person killed by immigration agents since Trump's second term began was Ruben Ray Martinez, in South Padre Island, Texas, on March 15, 2025.

"This is a pattern — it's a disturbing pattern — and it's got to stop," Rep. Greg Casar, a Democrat who represents Austin, said in a statement quoted by NPR.

## Officials Demand Independence

The response from local and state officials was swift and pointed, centering on a single demand: that the investigation not be led by the agency whose officer fired the shot. DHS has said Homeland Security Investigations, its investigative branch, is leading the probe with FBI assistance.

Mayor Kirk Watson said Austinites "have a right to know what happened," adding, "It's their city." He said he was angry but "not surprised at all," given how ICE has operated in other cities.

Travis County District Attorney Jose Garza called for an independent investigation and requested federal cooperation in providing access to evidence, noting that "it is possible that this incident included violations of state law." State Rep. Vikki Goodwin framed the issue bluntly: "It needs to be somebody other than the federal government because when you're investigating yourself, that's not ideal."

Casar sent a letter to DHS demanding an immediate independent investigation, the suspension of the agent involved, and a congressional briefing by Sept. 28. He also raised a pointed concern about the victim's status as a witness: "If ICE deports him, the key witness to their shooting disappears. He must not be removed from this country, and he must receive proper medical care."

## Protests and Community Response

Within hours, demonstrators gathered near the scene, lining sidewalks, waving Mexican flags and chanting "Abolish ICE," CBS Austin reported. Law enforcement used pepper balls to clear protesters from the street, and the crowd later blocked the southbound U.S. 183 frontage road. Organizers with Hands Off Central Texas helped mobilize the response.

"There is no reason to shoot somebody unless your life is in danger and their life was not in danger," Austin protester Rebecca Cruz told CBS Austin. "This is something that is becoming an everyday norm in our community, in the immigrant community."

## Dispute Over Legal Status

A significant dispute has emerged over Garces Perez's immigration history. DHS said he "illegally entered our country under the Biden administration and had a final order of removal." His attorney, Kate Lincoln-Goldfinch, disputed that characterization, saying he previously held a valid work permit and, based on available information, entered the United States lawfully.

Speaking to NPR, Lincoln-Goldfinch described being turned away from the hospital and said the family had received no updates on his condition or location. "We do not want to live in a country where ICE kills people for delivering DoorDash," she said.

## Analysis: A Familiar Flashpoint

The Austin shooting lands at the intersection of several intensifying trends. The Trump administration has surged immigration agents into Democrat-run cities including Los Angeles, Chicago and Minneapolis, and Texas has cooperated closely with federal enforcement. The question of who investigates officer-involved shootings — a federal agency or an independent body — has become a recurring battle, echoing disputes in Houston and Minneapolis.

Politically, the incident carries particular weight. A July 2025 Pew poll found about seven in ten Latinos believed the administration was doing too much to deport immigrants, and roughly a third of Hispanic voters who backed Trump in 2024 now describe the policies as harmful to Hispanics. With midterm elections approaching, each new shooting risks deepening that erosion.

## What to Watch

Several questions remain unanswered. It is still unclear what exactly preceded the shooting, whether Garces Perez was armed or posed a threat, and whether body-camera or dashcam footage will be released. Where he is being held — and whether he could be deported before an investigation concludes — remains a central concern for his attorneys and for officials who argue his presence is essential to establishing the facts.

Whether the investigation remains under federal control or shifts to an independent authority could prove the defining controversy of the case. For now, Austin officials say they will keep pressing for answers about what happened on their streets — and why.
