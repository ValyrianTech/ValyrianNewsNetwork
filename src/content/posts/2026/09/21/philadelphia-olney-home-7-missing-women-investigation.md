# Philadelphia Case Links 7 Missing Women to One Home

Months before Amy McHale vanished in 2016, she confided in a friend that she was frightened of her ex-husband, Raymond "R.C." Horsch. She was last seen at his home in Philadelphia's Olney neighborhood. Now her disappearance is one of seven connected to that same West Chew Avenue property — a sprawling death investigation in which police say five of the women are believed to be deceased.

Investigators have recovered more than one million digital images and videos from the home, and have reviewed only about 30 percent of the material. No bodies or human remains tied to the missing women have been found, and no one has been charged in any of the disappearances.

## How the Case Began

The investigation opened by chance. On June 19, 2026, a U.S. Park Police officer approached an illegally parked BMW near Independence Mall in Old City and encountered Eugene Horsch, 44, R.C. Horsch's son, along with a woman who has not been publicly identified, according to [NBC10 Philadelphia](https://www.nbcphiladelphia.com/investigators/philadelphia-olney-home-mystery-house-missing-women/4464043/). The officer reported hearing the woman say something to the effect of, "You're going to hurt me!"

Inside the vehicle, authorities found two handguns, roughly $8,000 worth of illegal drugs and credentials that claimed Eugene Horsch was a federal law enforcement officer and a DEA agent. Those credentials were later determined to be false. The woman with him carried an identification card bearing the name of Blair Tonzelli, a woman reported missing in 2023 — but displaying the bearer's own photograph.

Detectives later learned that Eugene Horsch allegedly made a statement suggesting Tonzelli would "never be seen again," according to court documents cited by NBC10. He remains in federal custody on charges of felony possession of a firearm and possession of an unlawfully produced document. Five state charges against him were withdrawn in August 2026, and his attorney, Jerry Brown, has said he plans to plead not guilty. A jury trial is scheduled for January 2027. He has not been charged with any violent crime or any disappearance.

## A House Under Scrutiny

Following the arrest, authorities searched the Chew Avenue property and found at least one additional illegal firearm, a marijuana grow operation, another fake FBI badge and five urns containing cremated remains. A second search on June 25 by the Philadelphia Police Homicide Unit, ATF agents and a bomb disposal unit uncovered computer parts and what appeared to be a home laboratory, including containers of an unknown oily substance.

On June 26 and 27, the FBI Evidence Response Team and Technical Hazards Response Unit conducted a two-day operation, removing more than 30 boxes and bags of evidence. In total, investigators seized more than 600,000 images, 70,000 videos and over 10,000 pages of writing, according to [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/us-news/philadelphia-raymond-horsch-house-victims-rcna598408).

R.C. Horsch, who died in 2025 at age 82, produced violent fetish films and erotic books with sadomasochistic themes and appears in some of the recovered material. He had a criminal history involving counterfeiting, drug manufacturing, fraudulent identities and firearms, but was never charged with a violent offense before his death. Investigators say he recruited women experiencing addiction by offering money and drugs in exchange for sexual activity and appearances in his films and photographs.

Among the items seized was a piece of writing about serial killer Ted Bundy that referenced zip ties and a drum and mentioned killings and "trash that needs to go," according to [NBC New York](https://www.nbcnewyork.com/news/local/what-we-know-about-the-7-missing-women-connected-to-philadelphia-mystery-house/6549163/). It is unclear who wrote it; Eugene Horsch's attorney has suggested R.C. Horsch was the author.

## The Seven Women

Among those tied to the home is McHale, R.C. Horsch's ex-wife, who disappeared in June 2016. Her daughter told NBC10 that McHale was actively using heroin and receiving methadone treatment when she vanished.

Gabrielle Amarando disappeared in 2012 in her early 20s, and Maribel Fresses was reported missing in 2018, also in her early 20s. In August 2026, police said video evidence recovered from a hard drive led them to believe both women are deceased. Deputy Commissioner Frank Vanore said the images depict both women lying unconscious with injuries to their bodies; the files were dated to 2017, and no DNA connected to either woman has been found.

Nicole Fusaro disappeared in 2018 at age 27. Investigators say they found evidence they believe shows R.C. Horsch strangling her. Family members told the [Philadelphia Inquirer](https://www.inquirer.com/news/raymond-horsch-philadelphia-missing-women-dead-20260916.html) that they had been frustrated by the pace of the inquiry and by how they learned details of the case.

Tonzelli disappeared in 2023 at age 35. A sixth woman, who appeared about 25 years old in recordings and is believed deceased, has not been identified at her family's request. A seventh woman, appearing deceased and estimated to be in her 20s, remains unidentified and is being treated as a Jane Doe.

In a statement, the Philadelphia Medical Examiner's Office said it has "reviewed the images involving the three additional women and advised that, if the images are authentic and have not been altered, the women depicted appear to be deceased."

Investigators have identified 58 people in the digital material. Five women appear in videos as lifeless or unconscious. Ten people later overdosed and were found elsewhere. Sixteen were located but were "not very cooperative," 25 others are being interviewed, and three have refused to speak with investigators.

## Families Demand Answers

One video dated to 2017 shows R.C. Horsch with a nude woman who uses narcotics before he hands her money, cuffs her to a bed and places a zip tie around her neck; she appears to lose consciousness. A second stationary camera was also recording. Detectives do not know whether the woman died, and investigators have not revealed whether they believe she is Fusaro. Authorities are still determining whether the footage depicts a real assault or a staged adult film.

Dawson Fresses, Maribel's brother, described his reaction to learning his sister's case was connected to the investigation. "My first reaction was pure shock," he told NBC10. "It's been eight years since I've seen her. Hearing that, I just couldn't believe it. I had gotten to the point where I thought I might never find out what happened to her."

Jonathan Halloran-Koren, a cousin of Nicole Fusaro, issued a statement criticizing how police communicated with the family and said he is assembling an independent investigation. He plans to submit findings to the U.S. Department of Justice Civil Rights Division and pursue civil litigation. Philadelphia police responded that their "deepest sympathy remains with every family whose loved one may be connected to this extraordinarily complex and deeply troubling investigation."

## Broader Questions

The case has renewed scrutiny of how earlier reports were handled. Narcotics officers had searched the property after a fire in May 2025, finding marijuana plants, firearms, ammunition and chemical containers — but the police report from that search did not mention missing women. Friends and relatives of some of the missing women had previously directed authorities to the property, which some advocates say raises questions about whether warning signs were missed.

Philadelphia Police Commissioner Kevin Bethel said the department remains committed to the families. "We have a duty," he said. "We have a duty to those families who are all concerned about whether their loved one lost their lives in that house or maybe somewhere else."

The investigation has also highlighted the vulnerability of women struggling with addiction and homelessness — a population that advocates say is often underserved in missing-persons cases. According to the National Missing and Unidentified Persons System, 58 women older than 14 are currently listed as missing in Philadelphia, a statistic that has given the case additional resonance in a city already contending with a large number of open missing-person files.

Councilmember Anthony Phillips, whose district includes Olney, said neighbors are shaken. "To live next to a house of horrors, a lot of the neighbors are concerned about how this impacts their quality of life," he told WHYY's [Billy Penn](https://billypenn.com/2026/09/18/philadelphia-house-of-horrors-police-investigation-olney-update/). "They're also concerned about the victims and their families."

As the digital review continues, a forensic laboratory in Chicago is analyzing chemicals recovered from the basement. Sources familiar with the investigation have told NBC10 that authorities are trying to determine whether any of the chemicals could have been used to destroy evidence or dissolve human remains. Police have not publicly discussed the results of that testing.

## What's Next

Investigators say digital evidence alone is not enough to prove a homicide. Deputy Commissioner Vanore stressed the need for corroboration. "There is thousands and thousands of videos that need to be gone through," he said. "We need to corroborate what we see."

With about 70 percent of the recovered material still to be examined, and chemical testing pending, the case remains open on multiple fronts. Key questions linger: whether human remains will ever be recovered, who wrote the Ted Bundy note, and whether anyone will face charges in the disappearances, given that R.C. Horsch is dead and his son faces only firearms and credential counts.

For now, police say they will continue working through the files, one by one, in search of answers for families who have waited years to learn what happened to the women they love. Anyone with information is asked to call Philadelphia police at 215-686-TIPS.
