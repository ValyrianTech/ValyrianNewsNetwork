# China Holds LPR Steady for 16th Straight Month

China's benchmark lending rate was left unchanged for a 16th consecutive month, with the one-year Loan Prime Rate (LPR) held at 3.0% and the five-year-and-above LPR at 3.5%, as announced on September 20 by the National Interbank Funding Center on behalf of the People's Bank of China (PBOC). The decision, [reported](https://www.news.cn/fortune/20260921/b13ec3848bd14acf903ff4e58c8f4238/c.html) by Xinhua News and sourced from the Economic Information Daily, was broadly in line with market expectations and marks the longest stretch of stability since the last adjustment in May 2025, when both tenors were cut by 10 basis points.

## Why the Rate Held Steady

Analysts point to three main factors keeping the LPR anchored. First, the pricing base has not moved. The PBOC's seven-day reverse repo rate, the main policy rate, has remained at 1.4% since the previous LPR announcement.

"Since the previous LPR announcement, the main policy rate — the PBOC's seven-day reverse repo rate — has remained at 1.4%. This means the pricing base for the September LPR was stable, which largely signaled that the LPR would continue to hold steady this month," said Wang Qing, chief macro analyst at Orient Jincheng.

Second, quoting banks have little incentive to lower their quoted spreads. According to Xinhua, the average overnight interbank rate (DR001) edged up slightly in September from August, and the yield on one-year commercial bank negotiable certificates of deposit also rose modestly, indicating higher wholesale funding costs for lenders.

Third, bank net interest margins remain under pressure. Zhang Xu, chief fixed-income analyst at Everbright Securities, noted that the weighted average rate on newly issued corporate loans in August was slightly below 3%, while the rate on newly issued personal housing loans stood at about 3.1%.

"With commercial banks' net interest margins still at low levels, the 'cost-effectiveness' of guiding the LPR lower in September is low," Zhang said.

He added that policy remains in a period of observing effects: "The necessity of guiding the LPR lower right now is not strong. It is better to let previously introduced measures fully take effect rather than rush to add more."

## External Tightening Seen as Limited

The decision comes as the US Federal Reserve has raised rates by 25 basis points, with the European Central Bank and the Bank of Japan also moving to tighten policy, raising questions about whether external conditions constrain China's domestic policy environment.

According to Orient Jincheng, China's monetary policy can afford to adhere to a "self-centered" approach. Because domestic and foreign price trends differ fundamentally, the analysts argue, external tightening has limited impact on China and the moderately loose policy tone is unchanged.

That view was echoed in [commentary](https://m.163.com/dy/article/L7B969FM0519DM16.html) published by the financial account Xiangshuai's Financial World on NetEase, which noted that the PBOC-affiliated Financial Times cited analysts as saying "the necessity of directly cutting policy rates is not high," and that rates on newly issued August loans had already declined marginally.

## Policy Stance and Outlook

The PBOC said it will continue to implement a moderately loose monetary policy, according to its Q2 2026 China Monetary Policy Implementation Report. The central bank aims to keep liquidity ample and social financing conditions relatively loose, guiding money supply and aggregate financing growth to match economic expansion and price targets.

The hold comes amid a mixed macroeconomic backdrop. Recent August data showed "production busy, domestic demand still weak" conditions, with high-tech exports in chips, shipbuilding, high-end chemicals, and the AI supply chain serving as a bright spot since early 2026, while housing investment remains soft.

On the credit side, the deployment of 800 billion yuan (about $112 billion) in new policy-based financial instruments, combined with faster net government bond financing, may help social financing and M2 growth stabilize temporarily in October, Zhang said.

## What to Watch

Attention now turns to whether the PBOC adjusts its policy rate in the coming months, and whether a further Fed tightening cycle pressures the yuan or capital flows. For now, analysts expect the central bank to keep watching how earlier stimulus measures play out before deciding whether additional easing is warranted — a sign that Beijing intends to hold its monetary course even as major economies tighten.
