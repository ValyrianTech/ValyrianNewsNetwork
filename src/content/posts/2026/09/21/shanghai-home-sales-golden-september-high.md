# Shanghai Home Sales Surge as 'Golden September' Peak Hits

Shanghai's second-hand housing market reached a new high for the second half of 2026, with single-day online-signed transactions hitting 1,495 units on September 19, according to [The Paper](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_34111567). Cumulative September transactions through that date totaled 15,597 units, up 28% year-on-year, as the traditional 'Golden September' peak season combined with two rounds of policy easing to lift a market that had been slow to recover.

Multiple new-home projects in the city also triggered points-based subscription thresholds, signaling strong demand at the higher end of the market. The data points to a property sector finding its footing, though analysts caution that gains remain uneven across projects and districts.

## A Market Lifting Off

The surge builds on momentum from earlier in the month. By September 13, accumulated second-hand online signings had already broken the 10,000-unit mark at 10,407, up 27% year-on-year, the [21st Century Business Herald](https://m.21jingji.com/article/20260914/herald/c869589d9df1ff6dea94a56922093578.html) reported. Transactions had spiked over the preceding weekend, with September 12 alone logging 1,319 units—a roughly 77-day high—and the two weekend days combined reaching 2,481 units.

The recovery is visible in front-end indicators as well. Data from Shanghai Lianjia showed that from September 1 to 19, second-hand property viewings rose 13.4% compared with the same period in August, while customer inquiries increased 5.4%. At one Lianjia transaction service center in Jing'an district, signings climbed 47% over the earlier August period, and the signing success rate rose to 62%.

"The center's signing volume since September is up 47% versus the same period in August, and the signing success rate has also risen," Li Siyao, who manages the signing center, told The Paper. Buyers and sellers, she said, have strong willingness to transact and relatively rational price expectations.

Pacific Housing, another brokerage, recorded 554 second-hand transactions through September 19—higher than July and August levels and up 31.9% year-on-year. New listings rebounded notably month-on-month, and new viewings rose 16.4% month-on-month and 40% year-on-year.

Prices have firmed alongside volumes. According to the National Bureau of Statistics, Shanghai's second-hand price index has risen month-on-month for seven consecutive months since February 2026, and brokerage data show average transaction prices recovering further in September versus August.

## New Homes and the Points Trigger

The new-home market is warming in tandem. China Index Academy data show August new-home transaction area reached 447,000 square meters, up 12.2% month-on-month and higher year-on-year. Sellable new-home inventory continued to fall, pushing the clearance cycle down to 18.47 months.

Several projects drew enough demand to trigger points-based subscription thresholds in September. Among them was Kaiyun Aishangli in Pudong's Beicai area, whose fourth phase launched subscriptions on September 18 with 102 units. By 19:00 on September 20, its subscription rate had reached 206%, meeting the points trigger. Other projects in Sunland, Jiading, and Qingpu districts also triggered the mechanism, as did the Yuexiu Tianyu and Greentown projects.

At the Pudong Jinqiao TOD project, subscriptions closed on September 20 with a ratio near 1:1.3 for its 60 units, priced at an average filing price of 60,000 yuan per square meter, with unit selection scheduled for September 27.

Yet analysts stress that differentiation, not uniform strength, defines the market. "Even within the same district, different new-home projects have different market feel," The Paper noted, adding that only projects combining product strength and cost-effectiveness capture heat. Some developers have resorted to discounts, free parking spaces, and high channel commissions to draw traffic.

## Two Rounds of Policy Easing

The recovery follows an aggressive policy push by Shanghai authorities in 2026. In February, five departments issued the 'New Seven Measures' (Hu Qi Tiao), effective February 26, which eased purchase restrictions and optimized provident fund loans. The most notable change cut the social-security or tax payment requirement for non-Shanghai residents buying inside the outer ring to one year, down from three years, as the [National Business Daily](https://m.nbd.com.cn/articles/2026-02-25/4268552.html) reported.

Then in August, six departments rolled out the 'Hu Ba Tiao' (Shanghai Eight Measures), effective August 21. The package introduced new channels for provident fund withdrawals for down payments, cut the minimum commercial-loan down payment for outer-ring second homes to 15%, and created a staged replacement subsidy of up to 80,000 yuan per unit for residents who buy a new home outside the outer ring and sell a second-hand home within a year, according to the [21st Century Business Herald](https://m.21jingji.com/article/20260820/herald/068c62379f7a33ab13d76683255928c7.html).

"The new policies target first-time buyers and upgraders precisely, releasing potential demand," Lu Wenxi, a market analyst at Shanghai Centaline Property, said. The time-limited subsidy, he argued, may break the stalemate in second-hand price negotiations and accelerate the 'sell old, buy new' replacement chain.

Yan Yuejin, deputy director of the Shanghai E-House Real Estate Research Institute, told The Paper that the relaxation of social-security requirements has released genuine demand, and the Golden September-October window is amplifying the policy effect. "As related positive news continues to be released, the mindset of buyers and sellers will keep changing," he said.

## An Uneven Recovery

Beneath the headline numbers, structural divergence persists. According to data cited by the 21st Century Business Herald, Shanghai's real-estate development investment fell 6.8% year-on-year in the first seven months of 2026, while residential sales area dropped 25.7% even as overall commodity housing sales rose 2.4%. The market has been described as "hot at both ends, warm in the middle," with policy aimed at the 5-6 million yuan mid-tier improvement segment.

The longer arc shows steady inventory clearing. Shanghai's second-hand inventory fell from 336,000 units at the start of 2026 to just over 300,000 by end-May, and Anjuke Shanghai data showed listings dropping to 309,205 by May 31—the first time below the 310,000 mark, down nearly 20% year-on-year. Sellers have shifted from "panic selling" to "reluctant holding," with over 70% of delistings in the second quarter coming from owners voluntarily withdrawing, according to the [21st Century Business Herald](https://m.21jingji.com/article/20260610/herald/c8780470499f5388d7c8925d94dbeb5c.html).

The replacement chain is a key driver. Brokerage reports put single-unit replacement-related transactions at nearly 55% of deals, and Lianjia data show 5-8 million yuan improvement-home transactions rose 9.3% month-on-month in May while sub-3-million-yuan pure first-home deals fell 2.6%.

Not everyone is convinced the rebound is durable. Dissenting economists, including Zhu Ning, have argued for further price declines of 20-30% over three to five years, suggesting the recovery narrative remains contested even as transaction data strengthen.

## What to Watch

The immediate test comes in the second half of September. Zhang Bo, president of the 58 Anjuke Research Institute, warned that while Golden September volumes will beat last year, increased travel during the Mid-Autumn and National Day holidays may curb in-person transaction volumes. Lu Wenxi similarly flagged the traditional June-August and post-holiday lulls as periods when supply could loosen.

Investors and policymakers will watch whether the points-triggered new-home demand translates into sustained price recovery, and whether the replacement subsidy—capped and set to expire March 31, 2027—permanently unlocks the 'sell old, buy new' chain. For now, Shanghai's property market is sending its strongest signal in months: volume has stabilized, good products are moving, and the foundation for confidence, as Yan Yuejin put it, is being rebuilt.
