# China, Egypt Set New Blueprint for Strategic Partnership

Chinese President Xi Jinping and Egyptian President Abdel Fattah El-Sisi outlined a new blueprint to deepen their countries' comprehensive strategic partnership during a state visit to Cairo on 1-2 September, signing a raft of cooperation agreements and issuing a joint statement that commits both sides to building a "China-Egypt community with a shared future."

The visit — Xi's first to Egypt in 10 years — was timed to coincide with the 70th anniversary of diplomatic relations between the two countries. Egypt became the first Arab and African nation to establish ties with the People's Republic of China in 1956.

## A Landmark Anniversary Visit

According to the Chinese state readout carried by [Guangming Online](https://en.gmw.cn/2026-09/03/content_38983062.htm) and [China Daily](https://www.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202609/02/WS6a9833b0e4b06d4aa055c02b.html), Xi told Sisi that the two nations "should support each other, deepen cooperation, and continue advancing the building of a China-Egypt community with a shared future amid a turbulent international and regional landscape."

Egypt hosted the visit with elaborate ceremony: Cairo Tower was lit in "China red" on the day of Xi's arrival, and the two heads of state jointly visited the Grand Egyptian Museum.

Xi framed the 70-year relationship with the phrase "equality and mutual trust, friendship and mutual learning, solidarity and mutual support, win-win cooperation" — a characterization that Egyptian commentators said resonated strongly on the Egyptian side, according to an [official government readout](https://www.gov.cn/yaowen/liebiao/202609/content_7080333.htm) of Egyptian reactions published by Xinhua.

"The close interaction and profound friendship between the Egyptian and Chinese leaders continue to elevate bilateral relations to a higher level," said Izzat Saad, director of the Egyptian Council for Foreign Affairs.

## More Than 30 Cooperation Documents

The two heads of state witnessed the signing of multiple cooperation documents covering Belt and Road cooperation, artificial intelligence, economic and trade ties, and industrial and supply chain cooperation. The two sides also issued a joint statement on further deepening the comprehensive strategic partnership.

Accounts of the number of documents differ. The research compiled for this story counts more than 30 cooperation documents, while the wire readout carried by Guangming Online states the two sides "signed more than 20 cooperation documents covering such areas as digital economy, science and technology, education, and transportation." Both figures point to a substantial expansion of the bilateral agenda.

Beyond traditional fields such as infrastructure, power, agriculture and communications technology, Xi said the two sides should "foster new highlights of cooperation in green energy, electric vehicles, aerospace, the digital economy and modern agricultural technology."

Economically, China has been Egypt's largest trading partner for 14 consecutive years. Bilateral trade reached a record high of US$20.79 billion in 2025, according to the [SCIO readout](http://english.scio.gov.cn/topnews/2026-09/03/content_118678297.html) of the talks.

## Suez Zone at the Center

The China-Egypt TEDA Suez Economic and Trade Cooperation Zone — the flagship demonstration project linking China's Belt and Road Initiative with Egypt's Suez Canal Corridor Economic Zone — has attracted more than 200 enterprises, with cumulative investment exceeding US$4.7 billion.

Nahla Emad, CEO of the Egypt TEDA Special Zone Development Company, said the joint statement's support for the cooperation zone was significant "for creating a stable, open and attractive business environment," adding that the next stage would accelerate project implementation and turn agreements "into more tangible results."

Independent reporting by Kazakhstan's [Qazinform](https://qazinform.com/news/egypt-china-agree-to-broaden-security-and-technology-cooperation-b86aa5) news agency added further detail: Sisi announced a third phase of expansion at the Egyptian-Chinese industrial zone within the Suez Canal Economic Zone, covering renewable energy, vehicle manufacturing, textiles and chemical fibers. Separately, the Suez Canal Economic Zone authority and China's ZC Rubber Group signed a preliminary agreement to study a tire manufacturing complex in the Sokhna Industrial Zone, with an estimated investment of about US$500 million.

## A New Middle East Security Architecture

In the talks, Xi raised a four-point proposal on building a new security architecture in the Middle East — his most comprehensive elaboration of a concept he first raised in 2018. The four points call for fostering the internal driving force for common security, advancing comprehensive governance for common security, strengthening the development foundation for common security, and enhancing international coordination for common security.

"People in the Middle East should remain masters of their own affairs, and external interference should be rejected," Xi said, adding that China supports regional countries in exploring a new security architecture and "taking their future into their own hands."

Xi also stated that the Palestinian issue "remains at the core of the Middle East question and should never be overlooked or marginalized," and called on the international community — especially external powers — to abandon double standards in the region.

Sisi said Egypt "highly appreciates China's consistent, objective, and impartial stance on the Middle East question" and is ready to strengthen coordination with China to ease regional tensions. According to Qazinform, Sisi emphasized the protection of civilians, freedom of navigation and respect for the sovereignty and security of Gulf states and Jordan.

The two sides also agreed to enhance coordination within the China-Arab States Cooperation Forum, the Forum on China-Africa Cooperation, the Shanghai Cooperation Organization, the BRICS mechanism and the Global South framework.

## Analysis and Implications

Xi's four-point proposal builds on earlier Chinese initiatives — the four-point proposition on maintaining Middle East peace and stability, and the four principles on resolving the Palestinian question. Ahmed Kandil, deputy director of the Al-Ahram Center for Political and Strategic Studies, said the initiative "directly addresses the root causes of regional conflict and fully reflects China's responsible role as a major country."

Abdel-Mohdi Mutawae, executive director of the Egyptian Forum for Middle East Strategic Studies and National Security, described Xi's approach as a step-by-step path — gradually building mutual trust, promoting development, and encouraging countries to bridge differences through dialogue.

The economic dimension is equally strategic. Both sides are targeting sectors such as green energy, electric vehicles and the digital economy, with an aim to position Egypt as a new regional manufacturing hub linking Europe, Asia and Africa. Beijing sees Cairo as a pivotal partner in the Global South; Cairo views Chinese investment as central to its "Egypt Vision 2030" development strategy.

## What's Next

The visit consolidates a relationship that has become a model of South-South cooperation, spanning trade, infrastructure, technology and now regional security. The open questions are practical: how quickly the newly signed agreements translate into projects, whether the Suez industrial zone's third phase proceeds as announced, and how Beijing's Middle East security vision is received by other regional capitals and external powers.

For now, both governments present the visit as a milestone. As Tarek Khoury, a member of the Egyptian House of Representatives, put it, the joint statement issued on the 70th anniversary "points the way for advancing the building of an Egypt-China community with a shared future and reflects that Egypt-China relations have reached a new height."
