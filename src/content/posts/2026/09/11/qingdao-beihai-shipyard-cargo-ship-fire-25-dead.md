# Qingdao Shipyard Fire Kills 25; Xi Demands Accountability

A fire that broke out aboard a foreign-flagged cargo ship undergoing repairs at a major Chinese shipyard in Qingdao killed 25 people, authorities said, prompting President Xi Jinping to issue instructions calling for a swift investigation and strict legal accountability.

The blaze erupted at about 11:15 a.m. local time on 10 September aboard the Liberia-flagged bulk carrier OCEAN MELODY, which was in for maintenance at the Qingdao Beihai Shipbuilding Co. yard in Shandong Province, according to [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/politics/leaders/20260910/acf0818e7c18495ebd5cfc4dfc51d982/c.html). Of the 42 people aboard, 12 evacuated safely, five were injured and hospitalized in stable condition, and 25 died.

## The Response

The fire triggered a large-scale emergency mobilization. According to [Jiemian News](https://m.jiemian.com/article/15081088.html), Shandong provincial and Qingdao municipal authorities activated their emergency response mechanism, deploying firefighting, police, health, and industry teams to the scene. Open flames were largely extinguished by about 2:30 p.m., leaving 25 people unaccounted for. Rescue teams recovered 20 victims by 5:30 p.m., all without vital signs, and found the remaining five by 7:24 p.m., confirming a total of 25 dead.

Xi issued "important instructions" directing that search and rescue be intensified, that follow-up and consolation work be handled properly, that the cause be determined as quickly as possible, and that those responsible be held strictly accountable in accordance with the law. He also urged all regions and departments to draw deep lessons, thoroughly identify and rectify fire hazards, and "resolutely curb the occurrence of major accidents" to safeguard people's lives and property. Premier Li Qiang issued a parallel directive, emphasizing rescue of trapped personnel, treatment of the injured, prevention of secondary disasters, and a broader crackdown on risks across all sectors. The Ministry of Emergency Management dispatched a work group to guide on-site handling.

## The Vessel and the Yard

The ship involved was a 2006-built supramax bulk carrier, about 190 meters long with a beam of roughly 32 meters, a gross tonnage of about 31,279 and a deadweight of about 55,742 tonnes, according to shipping registries cited by [The Paper](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_34046695), which aggregates reporting from Red Star News. It is flagged in Liberia with Monrovia as its home port — a typical "flag of convenience" arrangement in international shipping — and is classed by Japan's Nippon Kaiji Kyokai (ClassNK).

Vessel-tracking data show the OCEAN MELODY left Taicang Port in Jiangsu Province on 28 August and arrived in Qingdao on 31 August, remaining moored there. At the time of the fire it was undergoing routine maintenance, though the specific repair operations have not been publicly disclosed.

Jiemian News reported that the vessel is now 20 years old, placing it in the "aging ship" category in an industry where bulk carriers are typically designed for about 25 years of service. Older hulls accumulate corroded structures, degraded coatings, and worn equipment, often requiring extensive dry-dock overhauls with workloads and complexity well above those of newer vessels. The ship had previously operated under the name STRANGE ATTRACTOR between roughly 2022 and 2025 and had changed flags and ownership over its two-decade life, complicating questions of liability.

The Qingdao Beihai Shipbuilding Co., founded in 1898, is a directly supervised second-tier unit of the state-owned China State Shipbuilding Corporation (CSSC), one of the world's largest shipbuilding groups. Its repair business reaches more than 20 countries and regions and ranks first domestically in repair volume, with long-standing partnerships involving Maersk, CMA CGM, Hapag-Lloyd, MOL, and Oldendorff.

## Why Ship-Repair Fires Are So Deadly

The incident underscores a well-known hazard in ship maintenance. In August 2026, the Ministry of Emergency Management issued a special safety advisory on ship-repair operations, noting that vessels contain many enclosed compartments and that repair work involves hot work and confined-space operations that can easily trigger fires, explosions, and toxic-gas accidents with high fatality rates.

Jiemian News reported that in past official investigations, unauthorized hot work ranks as the leading ignition source. Welding and cutting generate molten slag and arc sparks at temperatures exceeding 1,000 degrees Celsius, which can drop through deck gaps and pipe penetrations to ignite residual fuel, paint, or wood debris in compartments below. Bulk carriers' cargo holds, engine rooms, pump rooms, and ballast tanks are confined spaces where accumulated oil residue and volatile paint or solvent vapors can quickly reach explosive concentrations. More critically, combustion in a sealed space rapidly depletes oxygen and produces highly toxic gases including carbon monoxide and hydrogen cyanide, incapacitating people within seconds — most victims die of toxic asphyxiation rather than direct burns.

Scheduling pressure during peak repair seasons can further erode safety management, with hot-work approvals becoming a formality, uncoordinated multi-trade work, and worker fatigue compounding the risk.

## What to Watch

Officials have not yet released the cause of the fire, the nationalities of the deceased, or the total crew composition — questions of particular sensitivity given the vessel's foreign flag. Also unresolved is how Xi's call for "strict accountability" will translate into enforcement, and whether the ship's age and the scope of its repairs will be examined as contributing factors. The Emergency Management Ministry's work group and Shandong authorities remain on site as the investigation continues.
