# Qingdao Shipyard Fire Kills 25 as Xi Orders Inquiry

A fire aboard a foreign-flagged bulk carrier undergoing repairs at a shipyard in eastern China killed 25 people, prompting President Xi Jinping to demand a swift investigation and "serious accountability according to law." The blaze broke out around 11:15 a.m. local time on 10 September at the Qingdao Beihai Shipbuilding Co. plant in Shandong province, according to [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/politics/leaders/20260910/acf0818e7c18495ebd5cfc4dfc51d982/c.html), the official state news agency.

Of the 42 people aboard, 12 evacuated safely and five were hospitalized in stable condition. The remaining 25 were initially reported missing before all were recovered and confirmed dead by 7:24 p.m. the same day, [CCTV reported via CQ News](https://www.cqnews.net/web/content_1547706204587130880.html).

## What Happened

The vessel had arrived at Qingdao on 31 August and entered dry dock on 9 September, according to AIS tracking records cited by [Lianhe Zaobao](https://www.zaobao.cn/news/202609/7984.html) — less than 24 hours before flames erupted during routine repair work. Open flames were extinguished at roughly 2:30 p.m., but rescue teams continued searching the hull for hours.

Rescuers faced punishing conditions. "We had to descend through narrow cabin doors into the bottom hold of the cargo ship to work; the cabin interior was hot and slippery, making transfer difficult," one rescue worker told CCTV. Hao Xiang, a commander's assistant with the Qingdao fire rescue brigade, described victims trapped in crevices and water, adding that "the stairs inside the cabin were very slippery and the trapped people were hard to move."

By early 11 September, CCTV reporters had entered the burned cabin and found a welding torch inside; firefighters described temperatures reaching 85°C during the blaze. The cause of the fire remains officially under investigation.

## A High-Level Response

The incident triggered an unusually senior official response. Xi issued "important instructions" calling for search-and-rescue, proper handling of the aftermath, a prompt investigation, and strict legal accountability. He also urged authorities nationwide to "thoroughly investigate and rectify all kinds of fire hazards" and "resolutely curb the occurrence of major and especially major accidents."

Premier Li Qiang issued parallel instructions, directing that trapped workers be rescued quickly, the injured treated, and secondary disasters prevented. He called on the State Council Safety Committee to "draw inferences" and consolidate safety-production responsibilities across all regions and industries. The Ministry of Emergency Management dispatched a work team to the scene, while Shandong provincial and Qingdao municipal authorities activated emergency-response mechanisms.

China Shipbuilding Industry Corporation (SH 600150), the listed parent whose subsidiary operates the yard, issued a formal announcement on 11 September confirming the casualty toll. "The company expresses deep mourning for those who tragically lost their lives in this accident, sincere condolences to the injured and the families of the deceased, and deep apology for the social impact caused," the company said, according to [National Business Daily](https://www.nbd.com.cn/articles/2026-09-11/4578636.html).

## Questions Over the Vessel's Ownership

Officials have described the ship only as "a foreign-flagged cargo ship." But international coverage identified the vessel as the OCEAN MELODY, a 20-year-old, Liberia-flagged bulk carrier of about 55,742 deadweight tons. According to [The New York Times Chinese edition](https://cn.nytimes.com/china/20260911/china-cargo-ship-fire-qingdao/), a Liberian-flagged ship of the same name had been docked in Qingdao for at least 10 days, according to the vessel-tracking platform Marine Traffic.

Tracking data complicate the "foreign" framing. Hong Kong's Ming Pao reported that the ship's registered owner, Huili Shipping Co Ltd, and its manager, Shanghai Yuyang Kunpeng Ship Management, are both Chinese companies — a finding corroborated by [The Epoch Times](https://www.epochtimes.com/gb/26/9/11/n14847648.htm), which cited LSEG ship-tracking data. The vessel also reportedly sailed under the name STRANGE ATTRACTOR between roughly 2022 and 2025, and previously flew the Marshall Islands flag, indicating ownership or flag changes over its service life.

Those discrepancies fueled online speculation — unverified — that the ship belonged to a so-called "shadow fleet" used to move sanctioned cargo. Chinese state media have continued to describe it only as a foreign bulk carrier, omitting the operator's identity. The nationalities of the deceased have not been officially released.

## Why It Matters

The tragedy has renewed scrutiny of safety practices in China's ship-repair industry, a sector officials acknowledge is a perennial high-risk setting. In August 2026, the Ministry of Emergency Management issued a safety advisory warning that ship maintenance involves confined spaces and hot-work such as welding and cutting — the leading ignition source in past accidents. Sparks and molten slag can fall through deck gaps into lower holds and ignite residual oil, paint, or debris, while toxic fumes in sealed compartments can incapacitate workers within seconds.

Experts note that blast-furnace-hot confined-space fires rarely kill by burns alone; most victims die of smoke inhalation and poisoning, explaining the high fatality rates when such accidents occur. The Qingdao disaster — 25 dead from a single repair-dock fire — fits that grim pattern.

## What's Next

The investigation into the ignition source is ongoing, with a welding torch recovered from the cabin likely to be central to the inquiry. Xi's call for "serious accountability" signals that criminal or regulatory consequences may follow, though no individuals have been named. The shipyard — founded in 1898 and now a key modern shipbuilding and repair base under China Shipbuilding Group — could face heightened regulatory oversight. For now, the identities of the 25 dead and the precise cause of the fire remain the central unanswered questions.
