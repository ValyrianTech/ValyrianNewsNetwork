# Jury Convicts Man in 1996 Killing of Tupac Shakur

A Las Vegas jury has convicted Duane "Keffe D" Davis, 63, of first-degree murder with a deadly weapon in the 1996 killing of rap icon Tupac Shakur, bringing a long-awaited resolution to one of the most notorious unsolved murders in music history. The verdict, delivered Monday after less than three hours of deliberation, marks the first conviction in a case that has captivated hip-hop fans and true crime enthusiasts for nearly three decades.

According to [AP News](https://apnews.com/article/tupac-shakur-trial-las-vegas-duane-davis-0b0b080d73f91cd122b6dd438e5bbb80), Davis could be sentenced to life in prison. After the verdict was read, Shakur's sister, Sekyiwa "Set" Shakur, cried and hugged prosecutors, while Davis's son covered his face with one hand. Sentencing is scheduled for October 13 before Judge Carli Kierny.

## The Night of the Shooting

Shakur was 25 years old when he was fatally shot on September 7, 1996, at the intersection of East Flamingo Road and Koval Lane in Las Vegas. He had attended a Mike Tyson boxing match at the MGM Grand earlier that evening, where an altercation broke out between his entourage and Orlando Anderson, Davis's nephew and a member of the South Side Compton Crips.

As [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2026/aug/31/tupac-shakur-killing-duane-keith-davis) reported, the confrontation was captured on MGM Grand surveillance video. Hours later, while Shakur and Death Row Records co-founder Marion "Suge" Knight were stopped at a red light, a white Cadillac pulled alongside their BMW and a shooter in the back seat opened fire with a .40-caliber Glock 22, striking Shakur four times. He died six days later on September 13, 1996.

## The Case Against Davis

Prosecutors argued that Davis, a former leader of the South Side Compton Crips, orchestrated the shooting as retaliation for the beating of his nephew. Chief Deputy District Attorney Binu Palal told jurors in closing arguments that Davis acquired a gun and "went hunting" for Shakur and Knight.

"He is responsible of the murder of Tupac Shakur," Palal said. "Tell him you hear him. Find him guilty."

Davis's own words proved central to the prosecution's case. He repeatedly placed himself at the scene in interviews, in his 2019 memoir "Compton Street Legend," and in a secretly recorded 2008 police interview. As [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/09/01/g-s1-141341/conviction-tupac-shakur-murder) noted, prosecutors argued that while Davis did not pull the trigger, evidence showed he called for the gunfire as a powerful gang leader whose orders younger men in the car were bound to follow.

Under Nevada law, someone who helps another person commit murder can be convicted of the crime. Davis is the only person still alive among the four men prosecutors say were in the Cadillac from which the shots were fired.

## The Defense's Argument

Defense attorney Michael Sanft argued there was no physical evidence connecting Davis to the shooting, and that his client's public statements were dramatized bravado designed to sell books.

"They have nothing in this case that says that man was here in Las Vegas on Sept. 7, 1996," Sanft said during closing arguments.

Sanft also characterized Davis's memoir as fiction, telling jurors, "You're reading a book that is fiction and not fact. But the state wants you to believe that this is somehow a confession, that he was there and present at the time of the shooting."

In a rebuttal argument, prosecutor Marc DiGiacomo pointed to a scrapbook Davis kept containing articles about Shakur, telling jurors, "This guy's scrapbooking his murder, for God's sake."

## A Decades-Long Investigation

The investigation into Shakur's murder sputtered for years due to a lack of cooperation from witnesses and limited physical evidence. As [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/1/ex-gang-leader-keffe-d-found-guilty-of-killing-rap-legend-tupac-shakur) reported, the case was revived when Davis's memoir was published in 2019, in which he described his role in the killing.

Davis was arrested on September 29, 2023, after being indicted by a Clark County grand jury. The trial began on August 17, 2026, with a panel of 16 jurors hearing testimony from 24 prosecution witnesses and three defense witnesses over nine days. The prosecution dismissed a gang enhancement charge due to witness logistics.

## Cultural Significance

Shakur's murder occurred at the height of the East Coast-West Coast hip-hop rivalry, a feud that also claimed the life of the Notorious B.I.G. six months later. As [Time Magazine](https://time.com/article/2026/09/01/duane-davis-tupac-shakur-1996-killing-guilty-verdict/) noted, Shakur's killing became a defining moment of hip-hop culture's "gangsta" rap era, which reflected the violence pervasive in American inner cities.

Known for hits including "California Love" and "All Eyez on Me," Shakur was the son of a Black Panther and was regarded among the greatest rappers of all time. His music addressed systemic racism, police brutality, and the struggles of marginalized communities.

Zayd Akinyela, Shakur's cousin, told reporters during the trial, "We've been waiting 30 years to grieve properly. A lot of people don't get to grieve until they understand what happened to their loved one and how it happened."

## What's Next

Davis faces a maximum sentence of life in prison without the possibility of parole. He has indicated he plans to appeal the verdict.

For Shakur's family, fans, and the broader hip-hop community, the conviction closes a chapter that has lingered in the public imagination for three decades. As Shakur fan Jessica Martin told reporters outside the courtroom, "It was kinda hard for me to feel so happy at the same time knowing someone is going to jail for so long. But then I looked over at Tupac's family and it reminded me of everything they've gone through. It's really emotional."

While the verdict brings legal resolution, questions remain about the broader conspiracy surrounding Shakur's death, including allegations of involvement by figures in the music industry. The case of the Notorious B.I.G.'s killing remains unsolved.
