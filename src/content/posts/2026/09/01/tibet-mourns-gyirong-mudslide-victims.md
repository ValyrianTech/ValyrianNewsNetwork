# Tibet Mourns Gyirong Mudslide Victims as Toll Reaches 7

Tibet held a solemn mourning ceremony on Tuesday morning for the victims of the devastating August 26 mudslide that struck the Gyirong Port area, as the confirmed death toll on the Chinese side reached 7 with 554 people still missing. The ceremony, held at the Gyirong Port Branch of the Rikaze City Public Security Bureau, was mirrored simultaneously at the disaster rescue site and the road repair site, with rescue teams, local officials, and residents participating in tribute, according to [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/politics/20260901/6ac9abec981444608243613457292f4b/c.html).

## A Catastrophe Unfolds in Minutes

The disaster began on the morning of August 26 when an ice-rock avalanche broke off from the southern slope of Langtang Lirung peak in Nepal at approximately 5,200 meters elevation. The massive debris flow, traveling at roughly 50 meters per second, scoured the mountainside and covered about 22 kilometers in just 6 to 7 minutes before slamming into Gyirong Port at approximately 1,800 meters elevation.

According to [Xinhua's geological analysis](https://www.news.cn/politics/20260830/c203f16b08074b46899be71b2c159f30/c.html), the ice-rock avalanche was triggered by a combination of factors rooted in global climate change. Ge Daqing, director of the Remote Sensing Application Research Institute at China's Natural Resources Aero Geophysical Survey and Remote Sensing Center, explained that rising temperatures are causing previously frozen slope rock masses to melt, fracture, and lose stability. The high-altitude avalanche descended rapidly, picking up debris and transforming into a devastating mudslide that leveled buildings across approximately 0.7 square kilometers of the port area.

Su Pengcheng, a researcher at the Chengdu Institute of Mountain Hazards and Environment, noted at a Tibet Autonomous Region government press conference that the ice-rock avalanche's journey from onset to impact on Gyirong Port took only about 6 to 7 minutes—leaving virtually no time for warning or evacuation.

## Rising Casualty Figures

The human cost of the disaster has been staggering. As of August 29, [Xinhua reported](https://www.news.cn/politics/20260829/e20310f9f89744da82a6c343cf81834b/c.html) that 7 people had been confirmed dead and 554 were missing on the Chinese side, with 2 individuals rescued from Resuo village. Among the missing are 260 foreign nationals. The initial casualty figures reported by [CCTV News](https://news.cctv.com/2026/08/26/ARTInvgBn8vIr71KM3ITeCnL260826.shtml) on the day of the disaster stood at 3 dead and 265 missing, with the numbers climbing as rescue teams reached the affected areas.

The disaster's impact extends far beyond China's border. On the Nepalese side, [Xinhua reported](https://www.news.cn/world/20260831/43f89024ddd649408e2276456c4f72f1/c.html) that 903 people had been confirmed dead and 4,247 remained missing as of August 31, reflecting the disaster's origin in Nepal's high mountain terrain.

## Rescue Operations Under Extreme Conditions

Rescue efforts have been unprecedented in scale and difficulty. More than 2,141 professional and volunteer rescue personnel have been deployed to the disaster zone, according to the Tibet Autonomous Region Emergency Management Department. The China Rescue Team, dispatched by the Ministry of Emergency Management and the National Fire and Rescue Administration, arrived at the core disaster area after a grueling 9-hour forced march on August 28.

Liu Maolin, squad leader of the China Rescue Team's third action group, described the scene in stark terms. As reported by [Guancha.cn](https://www.guancha.cn/politics/2026_08_29_829100.shtml), Liu said: "The disaster situation at the scene is extremely severe. The original buildings within the mudslide impact zone have been basically leveled to the ground, completely unrecognizable as any building outline. The ground is covered with thick mud and gravel, with deep and wide deposits."

Despite the grim conditions, Liu emphasized the team's determination: "Although no trapped persons have been found so far, as long as there is a glimmer of hope, we will never give up. We will continue to race against time to carry out scientific rescue operations and resolutely complete the search and rescue mission."

The [Tibet Autonomous Region government](https://www.xizang.gov.cn/xwzx_406/bmkx/202608/t20260830_555704.html) reported that an 18-member commando team of immigration police advanced toward the disaster core area on August 29, while military medical teams provided care to more than 150 displaced residents at temporary shelters. Road restoration efforts have made progress, with 1.02 kilometers of road restored as of August 29.

## A Valley Marked by Geological Risk

The Gyirong Valley, a deep canyon on the southern slope of the Himalayas, has a long history of geological instability. As [China Daily's analysis](https://cn.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202608/26/WS6a8ef8e9e4b09a165c7863c2.html) detailed, the valley spans approximately 93 kilometers with an elevation drop of over 2,400 meters, sitting on a geological fault zone where Himalayan mudslides primarily develop within 8 kilometers of fault lines.

The region's vulnerability was dramatically amplified by the devastating 8.1-magnitude Nepal earthquake of April 2015, which struck just 43 kilometers from Gyirong town. The earthquake severely destabilized mountain slopes, creating long-term geological instability that continues to manifest years later. Academic research has identified 55 glacial lakes in the upper watershed of the Friendship Bridge area, covering approximately 3.39 square kilometers—a reservoir of potential disaster that experts describe as a "powder keg" hanging over the port.

This is not the first time the valley has been struck in recent memory. On July 8, 2025, a glacial lake outburst flood triggered a massive mudslide that destroyed the Friendship Bridge and caused 17 Chinese nationals to go missing. Port operations were suspended for nearly half a year until a temporary steel bridge was completed on January 1, 2026. The August 2026 disaster occurred just 1 year and 49 days after the previous one.

## Government Response and Forward Outlook

Premier Li Qiang visited the disaster area on August 27 on behalf of General Secretary Xi Jinping to direct rescue and emergency response operations. The central government activated a National Geological Disaster Level II emergency response and allocated 120 million yuan for emergency relief, while the Tibet Autonomous Region activated a Level I emergency response.

As the mourning ceremony concluded, the search and rescue operation continues. Experts warn that the geological factors that made this disaster possible—global warming accelerating glacier retreat, the destabilizing legacy of the 2015 earthquake, and the region's inherent topographic vulnerability—remain unchanged. Liu Qiao, a researcher at the Chengdu Institute of Mountain Hazards and Environment, cautioned that "there were no obvious abnormal signals on the ice surface or ground surface in the early stage. Without special monitoring, it is very difficult to predict in advance."

For the families of the missing and the communities along this vital trade corridor, the coming weeks will be critical. The Gyirong Port, which handled 4.248 billion RMB in import/export trade in 2024—nearly 30 percent of China-Nepal trade—faces an uncertain path to recovery as the region grapples with the aftermath of one of the most devastating natural disasters in recent Himalayan history.
