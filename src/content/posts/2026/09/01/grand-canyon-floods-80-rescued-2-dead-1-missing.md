# Grand Canyon Floods: Rescuers Save 80, Two Dead, One Missing

Rescuers saved approximately 80 people after a devastating flash flood swept through the Grand Canyon's Bright Angel Creek area on Saturday, August 29, according to [AP News](https://apnews.com/article/grand-canyon-flash-flood-bright-angel-arizona-b50ddf9b2f058cb23ad12087f0d938e4). The flood left two people dead and one person still missing, officials confirmed Monday evening.

The bodies of Dr. John Giusti, 46, a chiropractor from Granbury, Texas, and Brent Rosenkranz, 42, a martial arts studio owner also from Granbury, were recovered from the Colorado River. A third friend, 53-year-old Timothy Allen Smith, a pilot from Star Valley Ranch, Wyoming, remains unaccounted for.

## The Rescue Operation

The flooding began around 2:30 p.m. when torrential rain hit the Bright Angel Creek area, which drops roughly 6,000 feet from the North Rim to the Colorado River. By sunset Saturday, 45 people had been rescued, and 37 more were evacuated Sunday, bringing the total to approximately 80.

Incident Commander Dave Black said searchers used night vision and other equipment to locate people during the deluge. Rangers referred to the park's permit system and checked vehicle license plates to track missing persons. "As of right now, they have no reason to believe, from the tips we've been through so far, that there's anybody besides the one missing person," Black said during a news conference Monday evening.

The [National Park Service](https://www.nps.gov/grca/learn/news/significant-flash-flooding-closes-phantom-ranch-and-north-kaibab-trail.htm) reported that 62 people were airlifted from Phantom Ranch and the lower North Kaibab Trail by Sunday morning, with additional evacuations coordinated with the Arizona Department of Public Safety.

## Survivors Describe Terror

Hikers caught in the flood described harrowing escapes. Erica Viola, rescued from the Phantom Ranch area with her partner, recalled the moment floodwater rushed toward them: "He's yelling at me, 'Run, run, run, run.' I can't run any faster. I'm going to just die. I mean, I was scared for my life for sure."

David Gregory, another rescued hiker, said: "I definitely think we could have gotten caught up in that side creek, and if we would have fallen in or the water carried us in, I think it would have been game over. Because that creek was moving so quickly, there was so many rocks and tree branches."

Parth Desai, hiking with fellow physicians and a park ranger, described the scene: "We saw the creek flood, waterfalls coming up out of nowhere, boulders, mud, landslides happening everywhere." A colleague later shouted "Up, Up, Up, Up!" as a 4-foot debris wall headed toward them.

## Infrastructure Devastation

The flood caused extensive damage to park infrastructure. Nearly all footbridges spanning Bright Angel Creek were destroyed, and the Colorado River was temporarily closed to river traffic due to metal structures and debris entering the water.

The Transcanyon Waterline, the sole 12.5-mile pipeline serving the park, suffered approximately 40% damage, according to [AP News](https://apnews.com/article/grand-canyon-water-pipeline-repairs-flooding-305a0474e6833237d57c7287b4e27e39). "We anticipate miles of the pipeline being destroyed," Grand Canyon Deputy Superintendent Brian Drapeaux said, noting that a full survey won't be completed until Tuesday at the earliest.

The pipeline, which has broken more than 85 times since 2010, was already undergoing a $208 million rehabilitation project expected to be completed next year. That timeline is now uncertain. "Heaven help us if the whole thing down there at the bottom has to be reconstructed," said Jack Schmidt, director of the Center for Colorado River Studies at Utah State University. "Where are they going to get the money?"

## Water Restrictions and Closures

Effective Monday, August 31, the [National Park Service](https://www.nps.gov/grca/learn/news/grand-canyon-national-park-to-close-overnight-hotel-accommodations-beginning-august-31.htm) implemented Stage 4 water restrictions on the South Rim and halted all overnight hotel accommodations at the park, including El Tovar, Bright Angel Lodge, Maswik Lodge, Phantom Ranch, Yavapai Lodge, and Trailer Village. Only dry camping is permitted, and all wood burning and charcoal fires are prohibited.

Phantom Ranch, Bright Angel Campground, the North Kaibab Trail, and the Black and Silver Bridges remain closed until further notice. The park is asking residents and visitors to conserve water by limiting showers to five minutes or less, turning off faucets while brushing teeth, and flushing toilets selectively.

## The Dragon Bravo Fire Connection

Rain fell on part of the Dragon Bravo Fire scar, a burn area left by a lightning-caused wildfire in July 2025 that consumed nearly 150,000 acres and destroyed the historic Grand Canyon Lodge. According to [The Arizona Republic](https://www.azcentral.com/story/news/local/arizona-environment/2026/08/30/federal-report-flagged-risk-before-grand-canyon-flooding/91541198007/), federal scientists had warned after the fire that runoff risks in the Bright Angel Creek watershed would be 2-8 times greater, and the area was flagged as particularly vulnerable to debris flows.

Without vegetation, a barren burn scar allows runoff to flow more quickly. Earlier storms had already washed ash, soil, and burned material into streams feeding Bright Angel Creek.

## Climate Factors at Play

This year's monsoon is especially intense due to a strong El Niño, which increases water vapor in the atmosphere, said A. Park Williams, a hydroclimatologist at UCLA. The link to human-caused climate change and the heavy Arizona rain is "pretty strong," according to climate scientist Daniel Swain of the California Institute for Water Resources, as reported by [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/ng-interactive/2026/aug/31/grand-canyon-flash-flood-causes).

A study in the journal Weather and Climate Extremes found that short-term intense downpours in the West have become 10% stronger since 2000. The steep, rocky nature of the canyon itself funnels water rapidly, turning what might be a normal summer downpour elsewhere into a charging wall of water.

## The Victims

The three Texas friends spoke to their families Thursday and found their campsite Friday at the bottom of the Grand Canyon. They planned "several days of hiking, camping, fishing and exploring together," according to a joint family statement reported by [KWTX](https://www.kwtx.com/2026/09/01/men-with-texas-ties-among-15-missing-grand-canyon-flash-flooding/).

Janet Giusti, wife of Dr. John Giusti, said: "To the community, he was a gifted healer. To his friends, a powerhouse of life. To his daughters, he was their ultimate hero. To me, he was my whole world."

The families said: "Our families are profoundly grateful to the search-and-rescue personnel working to bring our loved ones home and to everyone who has prayed for us, reached out, offered help and surrounded our families with love."

## What's Next

More search teams are planned on the ground Tuesday, including four teams with dogs for body recoveries. Officials have not yet fully surveyed the damage, and the timeline for repairs and reopening remains unclear.

Phantom Ranch, established over a century ago with connections to Teddy Roosevelt, has long been a refuge for hikers at the bottom of the canyon, as [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/aug/31/grand-canyon-flash-flood-phantom-ranch) noted. Its closure marks a significant disruption to one of America's most visited national parks, which hosts approximately 4.4 million visitors annually.

Additional thunderstorms and heavy rainfall remain possible, which could result in further flash flooding, debris flows, and rockfall in the already devastated area.
