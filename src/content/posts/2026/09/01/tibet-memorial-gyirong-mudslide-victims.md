# Tibet Holds Memorial for Gyirong Mudslide Victims

Emergency workers, officials and residents in Tibet gathered Tuesday morning to mourn the victims of the devastating '8·26' mudslide that struck the Gyirong Port border crossing on the China-Nepal frontier, according to [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/politics/20260901/6ac9abec981444608243613457292f4b/c.html). The memorial ceremony, held at 9:55 AM at the Gyirong Port Branch of the Shigatse Public Security Bureau, was accompanied by simultaneous observances at the disaster's core rescue site and road clearance operations, with rescue teams, local cadres and residents participating.

The ceremony comes as the death toll from the disaster continues to climb. Official figures put the number of confirmed dead in Tibet at 16, with 546 people still missing—including 261 foreign nationals from 23 countries. In Nepal, the toll has reached at least 1,066 confirmed dead with 3,916 missing, according to data compiled by [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Nepal_floods), bringing the combined death toll past 1,000.

![Surveillance camera screenshot showing flood hitting the Gyirong Port crossing on the China-Nepal border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/File:Mudslide_at_Gyirong_Port_2_(screenshot).png)

## The Disaster

On the morning of August 26, a massive mudslide triggered by a glacier collapse devastated the Gyirong Port area. Scientists from the Chinese Academy of Sciences confirmed that a glacier on the south face of Langtang Lirung mountain in Nepal's Langtang National Park broke off at approximately 5,200 meters elevation, falling to about 4,000 meters before forming a massive debris flow that traveled roughly 22 kilometers to reach the border crossing.

The ice-rock avalanche moved at approximately 50 meters per second, reaching Gyirong Port in just 6-7 minutes from onset to impact. The impact zone covered approximately 0.7 square kilometers, with 27 buildings and associated facilities flattened. A seismic event measuring Ms 5.2 was detected at 10:52 AM Beijing time, with USGS analysis confirming it was consistent with a glacier collapse rather than a tectonic earthquake.

Guo Zhaocheng, director of the Geological Disaster Survey and Monitoring Center at the China Aero Geophysical Survey, explained the disaster mechanism: "This disaster was caused by ice avalanche-triggered debris flow. Specifically, high-altitude ice avalanche fell from the mountain slope, and during high-speed movement it scraped moraine material in the channel, forming a high-speed debris flow... The movement speed was about 50 meters per second, extremely fast, giving people almost no reaction time."

## Rescue and Relief Operations

Tibet activated a Level I emergency response for natural disasters immediately after the event. According to the [Dutenews](https://dutenews.com/n/article/60070826) live coverage, 2,141 rescue personnel have been mobilized, along with 269 rescue vehicles and 3,922 sets of rescue equipment. The Ministry of Finance and Ministry of Emergency Management allocated 120 million yuan, while the National Development and Reform Commission allocated an additional 100 million yuan.

China dispatched its 111-member China Rescue Team with detection, rescue, demolition, communication and support equipment, along with 158 professional rescue personnel from China Anneng deployed to the G216 national highway. More than 680 fire rescue personnel participated in on-site rescue operations, using water, land and air routes to reach the affected core area.

Rescue efforts have been hampered by the destruction of the G216 national highway, which was severely damaged by the mudslide. As of September 1, crews had cleared approximately 700 meters of the remaining damaged section, with rescue personnel working around the clock to reopen the vital supply corridor. Communication signals were restored to Gyirong Port by August 30, and the Purepu Qiangzang barrier lake was completely drained by September 1, though authorities warned of continued glacier collapse risk near the Chocen River impact crater in Nepal.

China has also provided emergency humanitarian aid to Nepal, including cash and supplies, with a second batch of relief resources—including drones, DNA testing equipment, and water purification systems—being dispatched, as reported by [The Straits Times](https://www.straitstimes.com/asia/east-asia/tibet-holds-memorial-services-for-mudslide-victims-chinese-state-media).

## International Impact and Controversy

The disaster has had far-reaching international consequences. Foreign nationals from 38 countries are among the missing in Nepal, including 288 Indian nationals, 80 Americans, 53 Ukrainians, 51 Malaysians, 35 Australians, and 33 UK nationals. At least 28 police officers, 13 Armed Police Force personnel, 44 Nepali Army personnel, and 15 customs employees are also reported missing. Approximately 93,000 people have been impacted by the floods, according to IFRC estimates.

However, the disaster has also sparked controversy over the scale of casualties and the flow of information. The [Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/aug/31/china-concealing-scale-nepal-flood-disaster-say-tibet-campaigners) reported that the International Campaign for Tibet claims the number of casualties is significantly higher than official figures, with approximately 300 homes reportedly destroyed in four villages—Sale, Serchung, Labi and Rizi—on the Tibetan side of the border.

Tencho Gyatso, president of the campaign group, told the Guardian: "What is visible in Nepal exposes what is being concealed in Tibet. Four villages—Sale, Serchung, Labi and Rizi—were among the hardest hit, with approximately 300 homes reportedly destroyed and many people still missing."

Chinese authorities have penalized at least 10 people for sharing what they called "false disaster information," and two Tibetans were detained for posting rumors that more than 1,000 people had been swept away. Maya Wang of Human Rights Watch said: "The instinct of the Chinese government is always to kind of control and to ensure the narrative which is to portray itself as a very efficient government."

Chinese Foreign Ministry spokesperson Guo Jiakun rejected the allegations, saying that "malicious smear campaigns and hype using the disaster situation does not align with popular sentiment," and insisted that information release had been transparent and timely.

## Climate Change and the Cryosphere

The disaster has been attributed to long-term glacier destabilization from climate warming, representing what scientists describe as a new and significant characteristic of cryosphere disasters on the Qinghai-Tibet Plateau. Alton C. Byers, a senior research associate at the Institute of Arctic and Alpine Research at the University of Colorado, noted: "Permafrost has been the cryospheric glue that's held the rock and the ice and the glaciers together for millennia, and now we're getting increasing evidence that it's being weakened by warming trends."

Nepal's foreign minister called for increased global response to climate change, stating that Nepal was bearing a disproportionate share of its consequences. The disaster has also raised questions about the effectiveness of early warning systems, which were optimized for monsoon floods rather than glacier collapse events—upstream monitoring stations in Nepal were swept away before alerts could be sent.

## What's Next

As rescue operations continue and the G216 highway corridor edges closer to full connection, authorities face the grim task of identifying victims. Only 4% of roughly 900 bodies found in Nepal have been identified so far, according to the Ministry of Health. The Water Ministry has warned of continued glacier collapse risk near the Chocen River impact crater, and experts warn that the Gyirong area may face significant rainfall in the coming days, potentially complicating rescue efforts.

The memorial ceremony in Gyirong serves as a somber reminder of the human cost of this unprecedented disaster—and of the broader challenges posed by a warming climate to the fragile cryosphere of the Himalayas. As rescue teams continue their painstaking work, the international community watches closely, awaiting answers about the full scale of the tragedy and the lessons it holds for disaster preparedness in an era of accelerating climate change.
