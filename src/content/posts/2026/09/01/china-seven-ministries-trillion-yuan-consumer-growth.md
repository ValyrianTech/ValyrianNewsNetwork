# China's Seven Ministries Target Trillion-Yuan Growth

Seven Chinese government ministries jointly issued a comprehensive policy document on August 31 aimed at expanding and upgrading commodity consumption, setting an ambitious target of approximately 60 trillion yuan (about $8.4 trillion) in total retail sales of consumer goods by 2030. The "Implementation Opinions on Promoting the Expansion and Upgrading of Commodity Consumption" outlines 20 specific measures designed to cultivate green, smart, and health consumption as trillion-yuan markets while strengthening China's position as the world's largest consumer market, according to [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/fortune/20260901/ae79c956b2e54dfc8c51b95337663310/c.html).

The policy, signed by the Ministry of Commerce, National Development and Reform Commission, Ministry of Industry and Information Technology, Ministry of Finance, Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Affairs, Ministry of Culture and Tourism, and State Administration for Market Regulation, builds on China's existing consumption momentum. In 2025, total retail sales of consumer goods exceeded 50 trillion yuan, with passenger vehicle retail reaching 23.744 million units and retail sales of home appliances and communication equipment each surpassing 1 trillion yuan.

## Policy Framework and Targets

The Implementation Opinions serve as a direct follow-up to the "Expansion of Consumption '15th Five-Year Plan'" approved by the State Council on July 13, 2026. The new document identifies four key task areas: promoting durable goods consumption, improving daily necessities consumption, supporting specialty product consumption, and cultivating upgraded product consumption. It also includes five supporting measures covering infrastructure, standards, branding, fiscal support, and market regulation, as detailed in the [official government interpretation](https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/202608/content_7079711.htm).

Hong Yong, associate researcher at the Ministry of Commerce Research Institute, said the targets are grounded in solid data and industrial foundations. "From the trend perspective, green, smart, and health consumption are on the eve of explosive growth," Hong told Economic Information Daily. "The trillion-yuan market scale is a forward-looking judgment based on consumption structure upgrading and industrial technological breakthroughs."

## Durable Goods: The Foundation of Consumer Growth

Automobiles, home appliances, and home furnishing form the "basic plate and stabilizer" of China's commodity consumption, according to Yi Shaohua, director of the Market Circulation and Consumption Research Office at the Chinese Academy of Social Sciences. The policy calls for deepening auto circulation and consumption reform pilots, removing unreasonable restrictions, and optimizing vehicle purchase restrictions in relevant regions. It also promotes the development of the auto aftermarket, including modification services, maintenance, motorsports, and RV camping.

For smart homes, the policy supports enterprises in establishing smart home experience centers, whole-home smart renovations, and accelerated development of smart home interconnection standards. Home appliance companies are encouraged to cooperate with production and recycling enterprises for promotional activities, with a focus on "deliver new + collect old" services to facilitate trade-in programs.

## Daily Necessities and Specialty Products

The policy addresses everyday consumption through quality improvements in food, beverages, textiles, and cultural/sports goods. It calls for strengthening agricultural product supply-demand connections, improving cold-chain logistics, raising tea and beverage standards to cultivate world-class brands, and promoting traditional Chinese textile heritage through designer and regional brands.

Special attention is given to "one old, one young" demographics. For elderly consumers, the policy promotes exoskeleton robots, electric wheelchairs, and smart mattresses, expanding brain-computer interface and AI applications in elderly-adaptive products. For children, it encourages development of green, smart, and safe infant and child products, including smart monitoring devices and educational toys. The policy also supports national trend ("guochao") products, helping time-honored brands expand globally and developing "must-buy" city gifts.

## Green, Smart, and Health: The Trillion-Yuan Frontier

The centerpiece of the policy is the explicit designation of green, smart, and health consumption as trillion-yuan markets to cultivate. Fu Yifu, special analyst at Su Shang Bank, described this as a "navigation map" for enterprises and investors. "The policy for the first time explicitly identifies green, smart, and health consumption as trillion-yuan cultivation directions," Fu said. "This means subsequent fiscal, tax, and standards supporting policies will all focus on these three directions."

On smart consumption, the policy proposes building national AI application pilot bases in the consumption sector, promoting AI phones, computers, smart wearables, smart robots, and smart home products. Fu noted that the deployment aims to unblock two major bottlenecks: the pilot bases provide an "accelerator runway" for AI consumer products from laboratory to market, while interconnection standards break brand ecosystem fragmentation.

Health consumption measures include nutrition education, functional food innovation, streamlined approval for special medical foods, and the creation of "health stations" at retail pharmacies. Green consumption focuses on promoting new energy vehicles, energy-efficient appliances, water-saving fixtures, green building materials, and green food products.

## Local Implementation and Market Impact

Local governments are already moving to implement the policy. Jiangsu Province began expanding its trade-in subsidy categories on August 28, covering smart home, digital products, and health appliances across ten major categories, according to [China Daily](https://cn.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202609/01/WS6a9632cce4b09a165c7872b4.html).

Yang Mu, director of the Market Operation and Consumption Promotion Department at the Ministry of Commerce, expressed confidence in the policy's impact. "We believe that as the series of policies to expand domestic demand and promote consumption continue to take effect, commodity and service supply will continue to optimize, new consumption growth points and scenarios will accelerate, and China's super-large market advantages will be further highlighted," Yang said.

## Analysis: A Strategic Shift Toward Consumption-Led Growth

The policy represents a significant step in China's economic transformation from investment-led to consumption-led growth. The 60 trillion yuan target for 2030 implies adding roughly 10 trillion yuan in retail sales over the next five years, a substantial increase from the 50-plus trillion yuan achieved in 2025.

The "15th Five-Year Plan" period (2026-2030) marks a critical phase for this transition. As [21st Century Business Herald](https://m.21jingji.com/article/20260713/herald/179235f36766ee18109e7543c6f85dd2.html) reported in its analysis of the broader consumption plan, the significance of the target "lies not only in the leap in scale, but also in forming synergistic effects with consumption rate improvement, structural optimization, and capacity enhancement."

For companies, the policy signals clear investment directions. Fu Yifu's analysis suggests that new energy vehicle companies like BYD and CATL will benefit from optimized purchase restrictions and improved charging infrastructure, while smart home leaders including Haier Smart Home and Midea Group stand to gain from whole-home smart renovation initiatives. AI terminal makers such as Xiaomi and Huawei supply chain companies, along with health and silver economy enterprises, are also positioned for growth.

## What to Watch Next

As the policy moves from announcement to implementation, several key questions remain: How quickly will supporting fiscal and financial measures materialize? Will local governments follow Jiangsu's lead with expanded trade-in programs? And can the smart consumption sector overcome ecosystem fragmentation to achieve the scale envisioned by policymakers?

The coming months will reveal how effectively these 20 measures translate into tangible consumer benefits and whether China can sustain the momentum needed to reach its ambitious 2030 target. For now, the policy provides the clearest roadmap yet for the country's consumption-led growth strategy in the next five years.
