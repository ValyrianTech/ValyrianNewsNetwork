# China's Zhishenxing-1 Rocket Succeeds in Maiden Flight, Advancing Commercial Space Ambitions

China's Zhishenxing-1 carrier rocket successfully completed its maiden flight on September 1, lifting off from the Dongfeng Commercial Aerospace Innovation Test Zone at the Jiuquan Satellite Launch Center at 10:00 AM Beijing time. The rocket, developed by Beijing-based commercial space company Galactic Energy, entered its planned orbit with all flight systems operating normally, according to [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/20260901/a09f4dfb31b14f28b957d2fe8d8ea083/c.html).

## A New Era for Medium-Lift Launch Capability

The Zhishenxing-1 (also known as Pallas-1) represents a significant leap for Galactic Energy, transitioning the company from small solid-fuel rockets to medium/large liquid-fuel reusable launch vehicles. The rocket features a two-stage configuration measuring 52 meters in total length with a 3.35-meter core diameter, approximately 283 tons of takeoff mass, and 350 tons of takeoff thrust. Its low Earth orbit (LEO) payload capacity ranges from 5 to 7 tons, positioning it squarely in the growing satellite constellation market.

The most distinctive feature of the rocket is its first stage, which employs seven CQ-50 liquid oxygen/kerosene engines in a parallel configuration. These engines, self-developed by Galactic Energy, support thrust throttling from 32% to 105% of rated thrust—a critical capability for precise landing control during recovery operations. As [CCTV News](https://news.cctv.com/2026/09/01/ARTIp87kRq3aYEw8HRi6e5Qg260901.shtml) reported, the rocket was launched from the dedicated Zhishenxing series launch pad, which was completed and put into operation in May 2026.

"The rocket's design goal is to provide commercial satellite customers with a high-efficiency, low-cost 'space express train'," a Galactic Energy spokesperson told Xinhua.

## Strategic Decision: No Recovery on First Flight

Perhaps the most notable aspect of this maiden flight is what it deliberately did not attempt: a first-stage recovery. Unlike competitor LandSpace's Zhuque-3, which achieved China's first land-based rocket recovery on August 19, 2026, the Zhishenxing-1's debut mission focused solely on orbital insertion and full-system flight verification.

Galactic Energy's phased approach has been summarized as "first orbit, then recovery; first verify basic capabilities, then iterate reuse capabilities," according to analysis from the [21st Century Business Herald](https://m.21jingji.com/article/20260901/herald/2272953ca568f76b038a75fbbebd192d.html). The reasoning reflects a deliberate risk-management strategy: separating maiden flight risks from recovery risks, prioritizing the most commercially critical objective of successful orbital insertion, and collecting essential data on structural response, engine performance, and flight dynamics.

This approach mirrors international precedent. SpaceX's Falcon 9 first flew in 2010 but didn't achieve its first-stage landing until December 2015. The Zhishenxing-1's Yao-2 mission is planned to conduct the first orbital first-stage recovery test, with subsequent missions progressively testing reentry control, grid fin operation, braking deceleration, and landing.

## Technical Innovation and Design Philosophy

The CQ-50 engine represents a significant technical achievement for China's commercial space sector. As detailed by [Forward-looking Industry Research](https://www.163.com/dy/article/KENMRQOD051480KF.html), the engine is the first 50-ton class kerolox engine in China to employ pintle injector technology, achieving a thrust-to-weight ratio greater than 120 with combustion efficiency above 96%. Key components use metal 3D printing, reducing manufacturing costs to one-third of traditional methods.

The seven-engine parallel configuration provides fault tolerance and redundancy—a critical requirement for reusable launch vehicles. The rocket integrates multiple advanced technologies including structural-dynamic integrated design, dual-component auxiliary propulsion systems, and multi-dimensional aerodynamic load reduction technology. It can optionally be equipped with the Eros-2 upper stage to enhance mission adaptability.

The rocket is designed for reuse, with a design life of no fewer than 25 reuses. The maiden flight did not carry a payload, serving as a demonstration mission to validate the complete launch system.

## Positioning in China's Commercial Space Race

This launch marks China's 61st space launch of 2026 and the 207th globally. It is also the 6th new rocket type to debut in China this year, following GuShenXing-2, Lijian-2, Tianlong-3, Long March 12B, and Long March 10B.

Galactic Energy has now completed 22 orbital launches and delivered 89 commercial satellites to orbit. The company's founder and chairman, Liu Baiqi, has stated that launch orders are booked through 2028. As [Tencent News](https://news.qq.com/rain/a/20260901A04J7J00) analysis notes, the successful maiden flight marks Galactic Energy's transition from small solid rockets to medium/large liquid rockets, positioning the company to compete in the medium-lift segment alongside LandSpace and CAS Space.

The competitive landscape is intensifying. LandSpace's Zhuque-3 achieved China's first land-based rocket recovery in August 2026, while CAS Space's Lijian-2 has successfully launched. The [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pallas-1) entry for Pallas-1 documents the rocket's development trajectory and specifications, noting its planned 5-tonne LEO payload capacity.

## IPO Prospects and Regulatory Momentum

The successful maiden flight is expected to accelerate Galactic Energy's path to a public listing. The company initiated A-share listing preparation with Huatai United Securities in October 2025. In December 2025, the Shanghai Stock Exchange issued rules requiring commercial rocket companies to achieve "first successful orbital launch of a medium/large reusable rocket" for STAR Market listing under its fifth standard—a threshold the Zhishenxing-1 has now met.

Commercial space has been written into China's Government Work Report in both 2024 and 2025, with over 20 provinces issuing 40+ policy plans supporting the sector. Multiple companies are pursuing IPOs, including LandSpace and CAS Space, reflecting the industry's maturation from research projects to investable businesses.

## What's Next for Zhishenxing

The immediate roadmap for Galactic Energy includes:

- **Yao-2 mission**: First orbital first-stage recovery test
- **Progressive recovery validation**: Reentry control, grid fin operation, braking deceleration, and landing tests
- **Zhishenxing-2**: A larger variant with 20-ton LEO capacity, planned for maiden flight by end of 2026
- **Full reuse capability**: Establishing inspection, maintenance, and re-flight standards for recovered stages

As China's commercial space industry moves from "single-point breakthroughs" to "systematic competition," the Zhishenxing-1's successful debut represents more than just another launch milestone. It demonstrates that Chinese commercial companies can develop medium-lift reusable rockets with advanced engine technology, positioning them to compete in the rapidly growing global satellite launch market. The next chapter—proving recovery and reuse—will determine whether Galactic Energy can translate this initial success into sustained competitive advantage.
