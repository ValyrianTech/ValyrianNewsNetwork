# Bruce Campbell Reveals Five-Year Cancer Prognosis

Actor Bruce Campbell, best known for starring as Ash Williams in Sam Raimi's *Evil Dead* horror franchise, has revealed that he has been given approximately five years to live following his cancer diagnosis. The 68-year-old shared the news during an appearance on [The Adam Carolla Show](https://adamcarolla.com/podcast-episode/bruce-campbell-talks-evil-dead-making-films-with-sam-raimi-and-gives-cancer-diagnosis-update/) podcast, which was released September 3.

"I'm in a five-year prognosis," Campbell said on the podcast. "With the type of cancer that I have, which I don't want to get into, the current thing is a five-year deal." When host Adam Carolla asked if that meant Campbell had five years to live, the actor replied, "Give or take."

## A Diagnosis Made Public

Campbell first announced his cancer diagnosis in March 2026 via a post on X, describing it as a type of cancer that is "treatable" but not "curable." [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/us-news/actor-bruce-campbell-says-was-diagnosed-treatable-not-curable-cancer-rcna261491) reported that the actor said he planned to "get as well as I possibly can over the summer" so he could tour with his new film. He has consistently declined to disclose the specific type of cancer.

"These days, when someone is having a health issue, it's referred to as an opportunity, so let's go with that — I'm having one of those," Campbell wrote at the time. "I'm not trying to enlist sympathy — or advice — I just want to get ahead of this information in case false information gets out."

## A Defiant Outlook

During the podcast conversation, Campbell elaborated on his approach to living with the disease, describing a philosophy rooted in determination rather than despair. "I've decided though, pretty specifically, to live with it, not die from it," he said, as [Variety](https://variety.com/2026/film/news/bruce-campbell-cancer-5-years-live-1236852722/) reported.

He explained that he refuses to engage with online cancer communities, saying, "The cancer rabbit hole is enormous. You can go into focus groups of 10,000 guys who have exactly what you have. I won't touch it with a 10-foot pole."

Campbell described the experience of living with cancer as "a whack-a-mole life," noting that "you don't die from cancer, you die from what cancer does to you." He also acknowledged the psychological toll, saying, "It's a fascinating thing that takes place because [cancer] messes with your head, too."

## Continuing to Work

Despite his diagnosis, Campbell has remained professionally active. He told Men's Journal in August that he is "in the middle of treatment" and "responding to treatment, to the point where I'm able to tour." He is currently on an 18-date tour of Alamo Drafthouse locations promoting *Ernie & Emma*, an independent comedy-drama he wrote, directed, produced, and stars in about a widowed pear salesman scattering his late wife's ashes.

Campbell acknowledged the poignant parallel between his personal circumstances and the film's themes of grief and mortality. "It's a very fascinating time of having a movie about a guy who's spreading his wife's ashes, who died, made by a guy who's dying," he told Carolla.

As [Deadline](https://deadline.com/2026/09/bruce-campbell-cancer-prognosis-1237068998/) noted, Campbell said his outlook of "appreciation and gratitude" has "come out of nowhere," something he finds "very hard to describe." He remains an executive producer on the *Evil Dead* franchise, including the upcoming *Evil Dead Wrath*, scheduled for 2028.

## A Wave of Support

Following the news breaking publicly on September 4, an outpouring of support flooded social media from fans and colleagues alike. Campbell responded in the early hours of September 5 with a post on X: "Cancer is all the rage, but I've said plenty and I need to get back to the business of living."

[The Detroit News](https://www.detroitnews.com/story/entertainment/people/2026/09/05/bruce-campbell-cancer-five-years-back-to-business-of-living/91628908007/), Campbell's hometown paper, reported on the flood of fan messages, with one user writing, "The day this man dies, the entire nation will mourn. Bruce Campbell is one of the best celebrities in Hollywood and it will be a sad day in history when he passes."

## What's Next

Campbell's *Ernie & Emma* tour continues through November 14 in Littleton, Colorado, and he remains committed to his creative work despite his health challenges. "I'm moving on," he said on the podcast. "I got a movie to promote. And when I promote this movie, I have nothing. I'm free and clear when I promote this movie mentally."

As the actor navigates this difficult chapter, his characteristic humor and resilience have resonated deeply with the fans who have followed his decades-long career. "You have to make this decision of, 'Well, OK, but do I have to stop everything that I'm doing because of this?'" Campbell said. "Well, no."
