# House Democrats Plot Trump Investigation Blitz if They Retake Congress

With less than two months until the November midterm elections, House Democrats are preparing a sweeping investigative blitz targeting President Donald Trump, his Cabinet, and his family should they reclaim the majority in Congress. Top Democratic lawmakers have outlined a multi-committee strategy that would deploy subpoena power, hearings, and document requests across a range of alleged corruption and mismanagement issues, according to [Fox News](https://www.foxnews.com/politics/house-dems-plot-trump-investigation-blitz-seize-majority).

## A Narrow Majority at Stake

Republicans currently hold a razor-thin House majority of approximately three seats, and historical trends suggest the party in power typically loses ground in midterm elections. Recent polls show Democrats with an edge of between three and eight percentage points on the generic ballot, according to [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/sep/06/midterm-elections-control-of-congress).

Trump himself has acknowledged the stakes. At a Republican House retreat in January, he told lawmakers: "You gotta win the midterms, because if we do not win the midterms, it is just gonna be... I mean, they will find a reason to impeach me."

## Key Investigation Targets

Rep. Robert Garcia (D-CA), poised to chair the House Oversight Committee if the chamber flips, has identified five priority areas: Trump family corruption, the Jeffrey Epstein investigation, corruption at ICE and DHS, corporate misconduct, and the handling of vaccination programs under HHS Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr.

"We've got a team on Epstein, we have a team on Trump family corruption, we have a team on DHS and ICE," Garcia said, according to [MS NOW](https://www.ms.now/news/democrats-preparing-trump-investigations-retake-house). "Those teams are actively working on preparation, letters, research."

Rep. Jamie Raskin (D-MD), expected to chair the House Judiciary Committee, has already sent requests for information targeting the business dealings of Jared Kushner, Donald Trump Jr., and FBI Director Kash Patel. "The Trump family has turned the entire executive branch into a money-making enterprise," Raskin told Fox News, adding that "there's more than enough corruption and lawlessness to keep Democrats across the House busy for years."

Rep. Jason Crow (D-CO), a former impeachment manager and co-chair of the House Anti-Corruption Caucus, plans to focus on the Department of Defense. "Our service members are being put at tremendous risk. There is corruption, there's incompetence, there is nepotism at the DoD," Crow said. He cited "very disturbing reports about ties between defense money and appropriations programs, and requests by the White House to funnel defense spending to certain companies linked to the Trump family."

## Impeachment: Not the First Move

Despite Republican warnings, impeachment is not expected to be Democrats' initial strategy. A senior Democratic aide told [MEAWW](https://news.meaww.com/dems-may-not-immediately-impeach-trump-if-they-win-house-heres-what-theyre-planning-instead) that "we are not going into this to impeach. No one wants a failed vote on day one. The focus is on building a body of evidence through investigations that can hold Trump accountable."

This approach reflects lessons from Trump's first term, when House Democrats impeached him twice but the Senate acquitted him both times. The Democratic establishment also joined Republicans in killing an impeachment resolution on the House floor in June 2025.

However, Democrats have not ruled out impeachment entirely. Crow called it "one of several tools that Congress has to pursue accountability," adding, "I won't hesitate to use that tool if that's the right one." Raskin similarly told MS NOW that "none of us is afraid of impeachment," calling it "a powerful tool in our constitutional toolbox," though he argued that removing Trump is "a far too limited a goal."

## Legal Experts See Wide-Ranging Concerns

Legal analysts and former prosecutors have catalogued what they describe as unprecedented corruption concerns. Ty Cobb, who served as White House special counsel during Trump's first term, said "Trump's corruption is one of a kind," comparing it to "Fort Knox to a child's piggy bank," as [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/jul/03/trump-investigations-democrats-midterms) reported.

Barbara McQuade, a former US Attorney, cited Trump's acceptance of emoluments, including a reported plane from Qatar, his solicitation of donations for a White House ballroom from government contractors, and "the apparent cottage industry that has developed around the pardon power" as all "worthy of investigation."

Former FEC General Counsel Larry Noble described the breadth of alleged corruption as "unrivaled in our history," saying Trump "appears to combine the mindset of a petty crime boss looking for ways to use his office to make as much money as possible with the ambition of a would-be autocrat looking to undermine the pillars of our democracy."

## Republican Response

House Majority Leader Steve Scalise (R-LA) has warned that Democrats would impeach Trump "day one" if they win the majority, as [Fox News](https://www.foxnews.com/politics/trump-could-face-impeachment-democrats-seize-house-majority-gop-leader-warns) reported. He has also argued that socialist insurgents who toppled Democratic incumbents in primaries are pulling the party further left.

"These are tough members," Scalise said of his Republican colleagues. "These are members who have been working with President Trump to make life more affordable for families all across America, running against socialists. Socialists who wanna raise taxes, open the border back up again, bring criminals back in."

Trump has maintained his innocence amid the accusations, and the White House has said it is prepared to respond to oversight requests regardless of which party controls Congress.

## What to Watch

With the November 3 election approaching, Democrats are laying groundwork for what could become one of the most aggressive congressional oversight campaigns in modern history. Potential corporate targets in preliminary discussions include Apple, Alphabet, Palantir, Blackstone, BlackRock, and companies owned by Elon Musk, according to [MEAWW](https://news.meaww.com/impeachment-isnt-the-first-tool-in-dems-arsenal-after-midterms-win-reveal-target-list).

The outcome of the midterms will determine whether these plans move from preparation to execution. If Democrats take the House, the party's ability to balance aggressive oversight with legislative priorities will shape both the political landscape and public perception in the final two years of Trump's presidency.
