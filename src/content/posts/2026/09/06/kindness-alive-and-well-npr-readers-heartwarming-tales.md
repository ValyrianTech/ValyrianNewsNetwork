# Kindness Alive and Well: NPR Readers Share Stories

In a world often dominated by grim headlines and divisive discourse, [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/09/06/g-s1-141564/kindness-respect-feelings) has published a heartwarming counterpoint: a collection of reader-submitted stories proving that kindness and human connection remain vibrant across communities in the United States and beyond. The feature, part of NPR's Following Up series, invited readers to share moments of unexpected connection with strangers — and the responses poured in from Missouri to Kenya.

## The Science Behind Small Gestures

The latest installment traces back to NPR's [original 2023 story](https://www.npr.org/sections/goatsandsoda/2023/08/23/1193148718/why-a-strangers-hello-can-do-more-than-just-brighten-your-day) about psychologist Gillian Sandstrom, whose research on "weak ties" — casual encounters with strangers and acquaintances — grew from her experience as a lonely graduate student in Toronto who exchanged daily waves with a hot dog vendor on campus. Sandstrom, now a senior lecturer in psychology at the University of Sussex, found that even brief interactions can boost mood, ease isolation, and strengthen community bonds.

In a [May 2026 follow-up](https://www.npr.org/2026/05/09/g-s1-119761/hello-greeting-strangers-benefit-social-ties), NPR reported on Kristin Jenkins, a global health professor at Cornerstone University in Grand Rapids, Michigan, who assigned her students to intentionally engage with strangers. The students' overwhelmingly positive experiences inspired NPR to ask readers for their own stories of connection.

## Readers Share Their Moments

The responses paint a vivid picture of everyday kindness. One reader from California described her mother, who survived polio as a child and has spent two decades making weekly visits to nursing homes and hospitals with her dogs, delivering handwritten cards and goody bags to residents who rarely receive visitors.

Michelle Sarnosky of Missouri, who works in corporate events, recounted comforting a woman in tears at a registration desk who felt invisible as an older professional in a male-dominated tech environment. "Out of everyone she could have walked up to, she chose me," Sarnosky wrote. "I gave her a mini pack of tissues and listened." She added: "I try to remind myself that we never know someone's full story, and a little grace — a smile, a quick hello, or a moment of listening — can really go a long way."

Cordelia Biddle of Pennsylvania shared a deeply moving account of visiting a UPS store in Philadelphia the day after her older brother died unexpectedly. When the woman working there learned of their loss, she came around the counter and hugged both Biddle and her father. "There was something incredibly healing and meaningful about that moment," Biddle wrote. "To feel seen by a stranger who cared enough to step forward — it mattered."

Other stories were lighter but no less meaningful. Anita Finley Derr of Florida discovered that walking her puppy in her new small town opened doors to neighborly connections, with her husband amazed at how many new friends she made within a month. Donald Pederson of Maryland offered a simple philosophy: "I always chat with strangers, in elevators, walking along the street, waiting in lines. If you pay attention and listen, everybody does have a story to tell."

A reader in Kenya described how a quick question about an acronym on a man's T-shirt while waiting in line at a bakery turned into a lively 15-minute conversation. And one open-hearted reader extends kindness beyond humans: "Even a simple greeting like 'Hello, brother' to a hawk or 'You are so beautiful' to a flower brightens my day."

## Why Kindness Matters

The stories align with a growing body of research on social connection. The Harvard Study of Adult Development, which has followed individuals for decades, has found that relationships are the strongest predictor of well-being. A 2022 study on "relational diversity" from Harvard Business School, [covered by NPR](https://www.npr.org/2022/10/29/1132078900/talking-strangers-happiness-relational-diversity-harvard-business-study), found that the richer and more even the mix of relationship types in daily conversations, the happier people feel.

This latest collection of reader stories echoes those findings — and extends NPR's ongoing exploration of kindness, which previously included a [November 2025 feature](https://www.npr.org/sections/goats-and-soda/2025/11/26/g-s1-98523/kindness-thankful-sympathy-goodness) in response to World Kindness Day. NPR also regularly highlights such moments through its "My Unsung Hero" segment from Hidden Brain, which shares stories of people whose kindness left a lasting impression.

## A Two-Sided Gift

As Anita Finley Derr put it, kindness is "truly a two-sided gift" — benefiting both giver and receiver. The stories collected by NPR serve as a reminder that in an era of increasing social isolation, small gestures of connection remain powerful tools for building community and lifting spirits.

For readers inspired to share their own encounters, NPR continues to welcome submissions. As the collection demonstrates, kindness doesn't have to be grand to be meaningful — a smile, a listening ear, or a moment of grace can ripple far beyond the moment it's given.
