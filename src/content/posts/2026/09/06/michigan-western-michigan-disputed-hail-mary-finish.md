# Michigan Beats Western Michigan on Disputed Hail Mary Finish

No. 16 Michigan defeated Western Michigan 13-12 on Saturday in a finish that became the signature moment of Week 1 college football, after officials added one second back to the game clock and Bryce Underwood connected with JJ Buchanan on a 47-yard Hail Mary as time expired. The controversial ending at Michigan Stadium, witnessed by 110,879 fans, has sparked intense debate about the officiating decision that gave the Wolverines a second chance at the end zone.

## The Controversial Sequence

With Michigan trailing 12-7 and 35 seconds remaining, Underwood drove the Wolverines to midfield before his first Hail Mary attempt fell incomplete as the broadcast clock showed zero. Western Michigan players and coaches began celebrating what they believed was a massive upset, but Big Ten replay officials reviewed the play and determined that Western Michigan safety Micah Davis had started his jump from an established out-of-bounds position and touched the ball with one second remaining on the clock, per [AP News](https://apnews.com/article/college-football-91aa6e2595a61b0206c8be6889e26578).

On the second attempt, Underwood's 47-yard pass found Buchanan in the end zone for the game-winning touchdown, sending the Michigan Stadium crowd storming onto the field. Buchanan finished with career highs of 6 catches for 126 yards.

## Big Ten's Explanation

The Big Ten released a statement late Saturday night defending the decision, citing NCAA Football rule 4-2-3-a, which states that a ball not in player control is out of bounds when it touches a player who is out of bounds, thus stopping the clock. The conference said replay officials used a side-by-side view of the play and the game clock, with cameras synced via a strobe system 85 minutes before kickoff to ensure alignment. The Big Ten also released raw video of the replay process showing officials discussing the decision.

However, NBC rules analyst Terry McAulay questioned the ruling during the broadcast, stating: "The ball isn't even close to touching the player when it goes to zero... I don't see how that happened, assuming our clock is correct. It is clearly not touching the player. The ball is clearly still in the air when the clock goes to zero on our screen." The discrepancy was later attributed to a difference between the NBC broadcast clock and the official game clock used by the Big Ten's replay center in Rosemont, Illinois, which employs Hawk-Eye's Synchronised Multi-Angle Replay Technology. The Big Ten introduced the advanced replay center in 2024.

## Western Michigan's Response

Despite the heartbreak, Western Michigan coach Lance Taylor took a measured approach. "We can't control whether there was a second left on the clock or not," Taylor said. "Ultimately, the refs did not cost this game." Western Michigan president Russ Kavalhuna, a former federal prosecutor, also accepted the Big Ten's explanation, saying he did not discern "anything unfair or untoward in this process," while acknowledging that "what the viewing public see and the officials see are different."

MAC Commissioner Jon Steinbrecher likewise accepted the Big Ten's clarification, commending Western Michigan for "an outstanding effort and a well-played game."

The Broncos, who entered as a 27.5-point underdog, dominated the contest in nearly every statistical category. They held the ball for 40:08 of the game's 60 minutes, racked up 132 rushing yards compared to Michigan's 106, and converted 8 of 13 third downs compared to Michigan's 3 of 10. Kicker Dieter Kelly connected on four field goals, including a 52-yarder in the first quarter.

## A Debut to Remember

The game marked Kyle Whittingham's debut as Michigan's head coach after 21 seasons at Utah, where he won a school-record 177 games and posted three 10-win seasons in his last five years. Whittingham, 66, was hired in December 2025 on a five-year contract to stabilize a program rocked by the firing and arrest of Sherrone Moore, who lasted just two seasons after the tumultuous tenure of Jim Harbaugh that included a 2023 national title, a spying scandal, and NCAA sanctions for recruiting violations.

Whittingham spoke on multiple occasions this offseason about ending the off-field drama at Michigan. On the field, however, it appears he still has work to do. The Wolverines committed three turnovers without forcing any, and Underwood - who arrived at Michigan as the nation's No. 1 prospect in his class but had an inconsistent freshman season - went just 6 of 16 for 62 yards between the opening drive and the final play. He finished 12 of 21 for 170 yards with one touchdown and one interception, also running for a score in the first quarter.

## What This Means Going Forward

The win masks deeper concerns for Michigan, which faces a brutal schedule ahead including No. 10 Oklahoma next week at the Big House, followed by matchups against No. 22 Iowa, No. 17 Penn State, No. 6 Indiana, No. 2 Oregon, and No. 1 Ohio State. Michigan's all-time record against Western Michigan improved to 9-0, but the Wolverines were outplayed by a MAC opponent for most of the game, and the controversy may follow Whittingham's program throughout the season.

For Western Michigan, the near-upset demonstrated the Broncos' competitiveness as defending MAC champions coming off a 10-4 season. They will host Boise State on September 26 in what could be a significant Group of Six matchup with playoff implications.

The finish has also reignited calls for greater transparency in replay decisions. ESPN's Paul Finebaum sarcastically called the Big Ten explanation "an Emmy award, maybe an Oscar, maybe a lifetime achievement award," while social media exploded with skepticism and conspiracy theories. Some have suggested the ACC/UFL approach of broadcasting replay conversations in real time as a potential model for the sport. The Big Ten's decision to release raw video of the replay process was praised as a transparency measure, even as questions about clock synchronization between broadcast and official feeds persist.
