# Trump Bans CNN, MS NOW and Politico From White House

President Donald Trump said Friday that he was banning CNN, MS NOW and Politico from the White House over what he called unfavorable coverage, and by Saturday morning the order was being enforced — reporters from all three outlets were turned away at the gates and had their press passes confiscated by the Secret Service.

The move, announced on Trump's Truth Social platform, marks the most sweeping restriction on White House press access of his presidency and threatens to upend a century-old arrangement that gives independent journalists regular access to the president.

## How the Ban Unfolded

According to [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/09/18/g-s1-144112/trump-ban-cnn-msnow-politico-white-house-media), Trump wrote in the Friday afternoon post that effective immediately he was banning the three outlets "as a result of their constant 'reporting' FAKE NEWS!" He added that "Media Outlets shouldn't be able to constantly write or report FICTION and LIES when they're covering the President of the United States, the Trump Administration, or the United States of America" and warned that "Other Fake News Media Outlets" could follow.

At the time, Trump offered no details on how the ban would be implemented. Enforcement became concrete the next morning. [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/sep/19/ms-now-reporters-denied-access-white-house-trump-ban) reported that MS NOW correspondent Akayla Gardner and an MS NOW photographer were denied entry at a security gate outside the West Wing and had their White House press passes confiscated by the US Secret Service. CNN's Betsy Klein and a CNN photographer were also barred, and Politico's Cheyenne Haslett received the same treatment.

Gardner described the encounter on air, saying her badge triggered a red warning light and a "disabled" notification when she tried to scan in. "I asked why I could not get inside," she said. "He said it was above him." Klein recounted a nearly identical experience, telling CNN's Kasie Hunt that White House security referred her questions to the White House press office.

Notably, an MS NOW producer was reportedly still able to enter the grounds — a sign the ban may not be applied uniformly.

## The Outlets Respond

All three news organizations issued statements vowing to fight the restrictions. "The White House belongs to the American people and the decisions made inside are funded by our tax dollars," MS NOW said. "MS NOW intends to take any and all steps necessary to defend our First Amendment rights and the essential role of independent journalism in our democracy."

CNN called the ban "an illegal assault" on the constitutional right to report. "CNN's mission to report on the U.S. government will continue regardless of any attempts to restrict physical access to the White House and other government buildings, or any other attempts to impede our journalism," the network said.

Politico editor-in-chief Jonathan Greenberger told his newsroom that the outlet stands by Haslett and its White House team. "We will vigorously defend our First Amendment rights," he said, according to [Deadline](https://deadline.com/2026/09/ms-now-journalists-denied-white-house-entry-following-donald-trumps-announced-ban-1237108359/).

The White House Correspondents' Association, which represents the press corps, condemned the move. "The constitution protects the freedom of the press from government interference," the association said. "That protection does not depend on whether the president likes a news organization's coverage, agrees with its reporting or approves of the questions its journalists ask."

Press-freedom group PEN America was blunter. "Someone needs to explain to President Trump how the first amendment works," it said, arguing the administration's hostility toward the press "is never about the accuracy of news stories, but about its inability to withstand scrutiny of its actions."

## Why CNN's Exclusion Matters

Banning CNN from White House access is especially consequential because the network is part of the traditional five-network television pool that travels with the president, providing independent coverage of his movements and interactions for the public. As NPR noted, removing CNN from that arrangement could reshape how the presidency is documented.

Trump did not cite a single specific story or factual error to justify the ban. Asked about his rationale, he described it as "really just cumulative stories over the last two years. You get sick of it." He also told reporters, "There's something wrong with a country that can allow people to write purposefully negative stories. If they want to write them, that's fine, but I do not have to let them into my, into the people's house."

Former CNN reporter Jim Acosta, who now works at MS NOW, accused Trump of "taking a meat axe to the free press in this country." He called the bans "a slippery slope" and warned, "We are on the path to having a state media-like system in this country." Acosta urged the affected outlets to sue and other Washington newsrooms to band together in solidarity.

## A Familiar Legal Battle Looms

The ban follows a pattern of escalating confrontations between the Trump administration and the press — and a track record in which the courts have repeatedly sided with journalists.

In February 2025, the White House barred Associated Press journalists from the Oval Office, Air Force One and smaller events after the wire service declined to adopt Trump's renaming of the Gulf of Mexico as the "Gulf of America." AP sued, and US District Judge Trevor McFadden — a Trump appointee — ruled that the restriction violated the First Amendment. "If the Government opens its doors to some journalists ... it cannot then shut those doors to other journalists because of their viewpoints," McFadden wrote. That decision is on appeal. In Trump's first term, the White House revoked the credentials of CNN's Jim Acosta; CNN sued, and a federal judge, also a Trump appointee, ruled in the network's favor.

Given those precedents, legal action is widely anticipated. All three affected outlets have signaled they intend to defend their rights in court, and the dispute is unfolding just weeks ahead of November's midterm elections.

## What to Watch

The immediate question is whether the ban will expand. Trump has explicitly warned that other outlets could be next, and the selective treatment of MS NOW's producer suggests the scope remains unsettled. For now, the three networks say they will keep reporting on the administration regardless of access — as Klein put it, "We can do our job anywhere."

What remains to be tested is whether the courts will again intervene to force the White House to open its doors — and whether that ruling would come before or after the political moment it is meant to cover.
