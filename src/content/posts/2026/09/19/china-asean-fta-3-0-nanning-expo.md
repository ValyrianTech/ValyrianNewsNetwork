# China-ASEAN FTA 3.0 Takes Center Stage at Nanning Expo

The 23rd China-ASEAN Expo (CAEXPO) and China-ASEAN Business and Investment Summit opened in Nanning, Guangxi, on September 17, spotlighting the next phase of the China-ASEAN Free Trade Area (FTA) 3.0 and marking the first gathering of all 11 ASEAN member states after the bloc's historic expansion. Under the theme "Sharing 3.0 Opportunities, Building a Better Life," the five-day event drew more than 3,400 companies across a 170,000-square-meter exhibition area, according to [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/world/20260918/65d6ed85a30d4328bef37cc66c19ac37/c.html).

![The 23rd China-ASEAN Expo opens at the Nanning International Convention and Exhibition Center in Guangxi. Photo by Chen Guanyan, China News Service](https://i2.chinanews.com.cn/simg/hd/2026/09/17/366fd3c1c44849e79337b2ff90a7fa4c.jpg)

## A Historic Gathering

The Expo's 2026 edition carries symbolic weight: Timor-Leste participated for the first time as a full ASEAN member state and CAEXPO co-organizer, producing the first "family photo" of all 11 ASEAN members in Nanning following the bloc's first enlargement in 26 years. The [China News Service](https://www.chinanews.com.cn/gj/2026/09-18/10699104.shtml) reported that Malaysia's Bernama news agency described the milestone as a "historic moment" for the Expo's circle of friends.

The Philippines, serving as ASEAN Chair, was the event's Country of Honor. Trade and Industry Secretary Cristina Roque said the Expo had become an important platform for trade, investment, and shared prosperity over more than two decades, adding that Manila seeks expanded investment in renewable energy, infrastructure, and digital technology.

## From Tariffs to Rules

At the core of this year's Expo is the FTA 3.0 Upgrade Protocol, signed on October 28, 2025, in Kuala Lumpur by Chinese Commerce Minister Wang Wentao and Malaysia's Minister of Investment, Trade and Industry Tengku Zafrul Aziz, witnessed by Premier Li Qiang and Malaysian Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim, as documented by [China's Ministry of Commerce](https://fairtrade.ccpitjs.org/art/2025/10/29/art_2608_61235.html).

The protocol expands the trading bloc into nine areas: the digital economy, green economy, supply chain connectivity, standards and technical regulations, sanitary and phytosanitary measures, customs procedures, competition and consumer protection, micro, small and medium enterprises, and economic and technical cooperation. Analysts describe the shift as a move from tariff reduction toward deeper "institutional opening"—aligning rules and mutually recognizing standards in emerging sectors.

"The most central change in version 3.0 is that the focus has shifted from the tariff reductions typical of traditional free trade agreements toward higher-level institutional opening," said Xu Liping, director of the Center for Southeast Asian Studies at the Chinese Academy of Social Sciences, in remarks cited by Xinhua.

The FTA has evolved considerably since its launch in 2002, with version 1.0 completed in 2010 and version 2.0 in 2015. Talks for the 3.0 upgrade began in November 2022 and concluded in May 2025.

## Trade Crosses a Trillion

The Expo convened amid robust growth in bilateral commerce. Bilateral trade exceeded US$1 trillion for the first time in 2025, reaching US$1.05 trillion, up 7.4 percent year on year. China has been ASEAN's largest trading partner for 17 consecutive years, while ASEAN has held the same position for China for six consecutive years. In the first seven months of 2026, trade totaled US$744.41 billion, up 24.7 percent year on year and accounting for 21.8 percent of China's total foreign trade.

Perhaps more significant is the structural shift. Intermediate goods now represent roughly two-thirds of bilateral trade, signaling a move "from buy-and-sell to industrial co-production," as CCTV described it, noting the changing nature of the commercial relationship.

Lin Honghong, vice chairman of the China Council for the Promotion of International Trade, said that as FTA 3.0 advances, enterprises on both sides are broadening "from traditional trade and investment facilitation toward deep expansion into new fields such as the digital economy, green economy, and supply chain connectivity."

## AI, Green Energy, and a New Canal

The Expo reflected these emerging priorities on the show floor. A dedicated "AI Grand Marketplace" gathered more than 70 companies and over 500 consumer AI products, while a new ASEAN demand exhibition shifted focus from what China can sell to what ASEAN countries want. The venue featured AI glasses, multilingual livestreaming, and service robots alongside traditional agricultural and handicraft displays.

Guangxi is positioning itself as a bridge for Chinese AI firms under a model of "R&D in Beijing, Shanghai, and Guangzhou, integration in Guangxi, application in ASEAN." Deng Caidie, president of Guangxi-based Maiyue Technology, said FTA 3.0 will ease cross-border collection and processing of multilingual translation data, enabling her firm to move "from purely product exports to building intelligent application scenarios with ASEAN partners."

The Expo also featured a dedicated zone for the Pinglu Canal, a 134.2-kilometer river-to-sea waterway—New China's first nationally coordinated project of its kind—that opened to navigation around September 2026. Bensak Panichakarn, a logistics expert at Thailand's Naresuan University, said the canal helps reduce the comprehensive logistics costs of supply chains linking ASEAN and southwestern China.

## What to Watch

ASEAN Secretary-General Kao Kim Hourn, addressing the opening ceremony, outlined three areas for deepening the economic partnership: cultivating growth in digitization, AI, green industry, and renewables using FTA 3.0's new sectors; strengthening coordination between FTA 3.0 and the Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership (RCEP) ahead of the latter's 2027 comprehensive review; and ensuring that all businesses, especially MSMEs, see tangible benefits, per the [China News Service](https://m.youth.cn/qwtx/xxl/202609/t20260917_16875040.htm).

Chinese Vice Minister of Commerce Yan Dong said the protocol's signing reflects both sides' commitment to multilateralism and free trade, and that China will work with ASEAN members to push for its early entry into force. The agreement now awaits each side's domestic ratification.

He Min, an associate professor at China Foreign Affairs University, framed the stakes in broad terms. "The China-ASEAN FTA has become the institutional cornerstone of China-ASEAN economic and trade relations," He said. "Version 3.0 will further broaden and deepen cooperation, unlock regional growth potential, and inject new momentum into regional and global economies."

With ratification pending and the RCEP review on the horizon, the coming year will test whether the Expo's showcase of "3.0 opportunities" translates into binding, on-the-ground integration.
