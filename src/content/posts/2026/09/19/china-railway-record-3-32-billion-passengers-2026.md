# China's Railways Hit Record 3.32 Billion Passengers

China's national railway network transported 3.32 billion passengers in the first eight months of 2026, a record high for the period and a 3.9% increase year-on-year, according to figures released by the state operator and carried by [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/politics/20260919/2b46080a1e0448e8b332ac169112a5fc/c.html). The milestone underscores the continued expansion of rail travel demand across the world's second-largest economy.

The data, released by China State Railway Group (国铁集团) and reported by Xinhua on Saturday, reflect steady growth in both domestic and international passenger traffic. The national network averaged 11,508 passenger trains per day during the January-to-August period, up 5.0% from a year earlier.

## Record-Setting Year

The eight-month total extends a pattern of consecutive period records through 2026. In the first half of the year alone, the railway carried 2.348 billion passengers, up 5.0% year-on-year, another all-time high for the period, according to figures [reported](https://k.sina.com.cn/article_1887344341_707e96d502001s3yq.html) at the time. The spring holiday season produced a striking single-day peak: on May 1, the network carried 24.844 million passengers, the highest daily figure in its history, [Xinhua reported](https://stock.10jqka.com.cn/20260502/c676442467.shtml).

By July, the railway had carried roughly 2.8 billion passengers, a 4.1% increase and another record, [China News](https://www.chinanews.com.cn/gn/2026/08-15/10678259.shtml) noted. While the cumulative passenger count has climbed to new heights each month, the year-on-year growth rate has moderated as 2026 progresses — from 5.0% at midyear to 3.9% by August — a trend analysts attribute to seasonal effects and a higher base of comparison.

## Cross-Border and Foreign Travel Surge

Two segments of the network are growing far faster than the overall average. Cross-border traffic on the Guangzhou–Shenzhen–Hong Kong High-Speed Rail reached 23.408 million passengers, up 10.7% year-on-year, while the China–Laos Railway carried 245,000 cross-border passengers, up 39.1%.

The China–Laos link, a flagship Belt-and-Road project that opened in December 2021, has become an increasingly popular route for regional travellers. [Lianhe Zaobao](https://www.zaobao.com.sg/news/china/story20260909-9651179) reported that the route operated 972 international passenger trains over the first eight months, and that Kunming–Vientiane services have expanded from two to four daily departures since launch, with border clearance at Mohan station taking about 50 minutes.

The sharpest growth, however, came from foreign passengers. The national railway carried 16.683 million foreign travellers from January to August, a 33.3% jump from the same period last year. The increase is closely tied to China's expanded transit visa-free policies, which have made it easier for international visitors to enter the country and travel by rail.

## Rail Plus Tourism

The figures also highlight China's growing "rail plus culture and tourism" strategy. In the first eight months of 2026, the network operated 2,501 tourism trains — comprising 1,387 tourist special trains and 1,114 tourist line trains — designed in coordination with local cultural and tourism authorities to serve themed routes and the holiday economy.

The tourism-train programme reflects a broader effort to link rail capacity with domestic consumption, offering travellers everything from family and study tours to red-tourism and wellness-focused itineraries. Railway operators have also rolled out customised services for concerts and sporting events, turning trains into a component of the wider experience economy.

## What to Watch

The continued growth in both passenger volume and capacity points to rail's central role in China's transport and tourism sectors. Ongoing investment in high-speed and regular-speed networks — including roughly 504.1 billion yuan (about $71 billion) in fixed-asset investment in the first eight months, up 5.6% year-on-year — suggests the network will keep expanding.

The key questions ahead are whether the moderating year-on-year growth rate continues through the final months of 2026, and how far the surge in foreign passengers can be sustained as visa-free policies remain in place. Either way, the first eight months of the year have set a bar that the fourth quarter will be measured against.
