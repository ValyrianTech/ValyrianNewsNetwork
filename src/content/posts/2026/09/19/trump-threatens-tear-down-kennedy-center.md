# Trump Threatens to Tear Down Kennedy Center

President Donald Trump has threatened to tear down the John F. Kennedy Center for the Performing Arts, escalating a months-long legal battle over the Washington, D.C. landmark and prompting a federal judge to impose new restrictions on any demolition. The threat, delivered to reporters on Wednesday, came just hours after a court again blocked the Trump-appointed board's effort to add the president's name to the building.

According to [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/09/17/nx-s1-5973036/trump-threat-tear-down-kennedy-center), Trump said the complex may be "ripped down" if his name does not appear on its main building. "For the Trump administration, for me, to get involved, and to take on long term, and to carry it, or to raise money to carry it, I think that the Trump administration should certainly have recognition," he said. "Because frankly, if we don't do that, it's going to close, it will end up being ripped down."

## A Landmark Under Siege

The Kennedy Center opened in 1971 on the banks of the Potomac River as a living memorial to President John F. Kennedy, established by Congress. Over more than half a century it has presented ballet, theatre, comedy, dance, hip-hop, jazz, and educational programmes, and hosts the annual Kennedy Center Honors. It was born with the world premiere of Leonard Bernstein's *Mass*.

Trump seized control of the facility in February 2025, fired board members who did not align with his "vision," and replaced president Deborah Rutter with Ric Grenell, a Trump loyalist with no experience in arts management. According to [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/sep/19/trump-kennedy-center-future), an anti-racism and community outreach initiative was shut down, its ten-person staff dismissed, and programming perceived as "woke" was purged. Audiences, performers, and philanthropists subsequently deserted the venue.

In December 2025, the board voted to rename the venue the "Trump-Kennedy Center," and Trump's name was added to the exterior marble facade. Rep. Joyce Beatty, an Ohio Democrat and ex-officio board member, sued to stop the change. In May 2026, U.S. District Judge Christopher Cooper ruled that only Congress has the authority to rename the memorial and ordered the name removed from public view. Scaffolding and tarpaulins now conceal the spot where it appeared.

## The Week That Changed Everything

The latest chapter unfolded over four days. On Tuesday, September 15, Judge Cooper again ruled against the renaming. Hours later, the board voted to close the facility for up to two years, citing $257 million in needed repairs, and the Center's executive director, Matt Floca, ordered an immediate seven-day emergency closure.

The next day, Trump made his "ripped down" remarks. That same day, an Agence France-Presse photographer, Brendan Smialowski, captured an image of the president aboard Air Force One examining a large printout bearing the partial red text "...dy Center DEMOLISH," with a picture below of a construction vehicle scooping rubble. [Snopes](https://www.snopes.com/fact-check/trump-demolish-kennedy-center/) rated as true claims that the images authentically showed Trump viewing the printout, while noting it remained unclear what the placard represented or whether the administration intended to act on it.

On Thursday, Judge Cooper ruled that the board cannot demolish the building or construct any new structure without 30 days' advance notice, and must inform plaintiffs and the court of any change in the Center's position that the building will not be demolished. He instructed the board to file a detailed status report the following Wednesday. The judge is overseeing two related cases: the *Beatty v. Trump* lawsuit and a separate suit filed in May by eight architecture and culture groups.

## "Like a Child Who Can't Play With a Toy"

The dispute has drawn sharp reactions. Kerry Kennedy, the former president's niece, told The Guardian: "Like a child who can't play with a toy, he wants to stomp on the toy. But this is not a toy. This is the living memorial to a slain president." She added: "It would be like adding the Trump name to the Trump-Lincoln Memorial or the Trump-Jefferson Memorial or Trump-Tomb of the Unknown Soldier."

Senator Sheldon Whitehouse, the top Democrat on the environment and public works committee and an ex-officio board trustee, described three phases of what he called Trump's assault on the complex — an attempted takeover that damaged its business, the "renaming fiasco" that struck "a lethal blow to the centre's business model," and now the threat of "actual destruction."

In a statement to NPR, the eight preservation and architecture groups, among them the American Institute of Architects and the National Trust for Historic Preservation, argued that Trump and the reconstituted board are failing their fiduciary duties. "A trustee cannot sit still while the institution fails," they said. "Nor can a trustee withhold the exercise of fiduciary responsibilities to obtain a personal honor." The groups have asked Judge Cooper to appoint an independent monitor to protect the building.

## On the Ground

On Friday evening, thousands of protesters formed a human chain around the building in an event billed "Hands Around the Kennedy Center." [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/19/demonstrators-in-us-form-human-chain-to-protest-kennedy-center-closure-vote) reported that the crowd filled streets in the Foggy Bottom neighbourhood, bringing traffic to a standstill as demonstrators carried signs and donned costumes. Chris Raleigh, co-founder of the group Hands Off the Arts, told the Associated Press that the turnout demonstrated public resolve. "In no way at all is he winning," he said. "His name is not going up on the building."

Also on Friday, the Center laid off about a dozen staff, according to the Washington Post. And Trump announced that the media outlets CNN, MS Now, and Politico would be banned from the White House.

## Analysis: A Test of Institutional Limits

The episode raises a legal question with little modern precedent: whether a sitting president can dismantle a federally chartered memorial established by Congress. Judge Cooper's May ruling established that only Congress may rename the memorial, and his subsequent order places a procedural hurdle — not an absolute bar — in front of demolition. Critics note that Trump previously ordered the demolition of the White House East Wing for a ballroom without congressional approval, suggesting a willingness to test such limits.

Trump's claim that the Center has lost "hundreds of millions" of dollars remains unsubstantiated, and the White House has not responded to requests for comment on the demolition printout. Whether the two-year closure is driven by genuine safety and financial concerns or serves as what Beatty and Whitehouse call a "pretext" is disputed.

Former Kennedy Center president Michael Kaiser warned that even a temporary closure carries lasting costs. "When you don't have audiences and you don't have donors participating for two years, those individuals go elsewhere," he said. "When you have no staff... it's not going to be so simple to reopen a building of this size and complexity."

## What's Next

The Kennedy Center board must file its detailed status report with Judge Cooper by Wednesday, and any plan to demolish or substantially alter the building now triggers a mandatory 30-day notice to plaintiffs and the court. The preservation groups' request for an independent monitor remains pending.

For now, the marble facade sits behind scaffolding and tarpaulins — a memorial to a slain president, its future hinging on a contest between a president's demand for recognition and the courts' insistence that Congress, not the White House, decides what the Kennedy Center is called.
