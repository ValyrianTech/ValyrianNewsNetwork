# Two Explosions Rock Antwerp-North, Home Twice Targeted

Two explosions struck the Antwerp-North (Antwerpen-Noord) district of Antwerp within minutes of each other in the early hours of Tuesday, damaging two homes and a car but leaving no one injured. Police say the same message was scrawled on the facades of both targeted houses, a detail that investigators believe links the attacks — and one of the addresses, in the Osystraat, had already been hit in a major bombing more than three years earlier.

## What Happened

According to Antwerp police spokesperson Kim Bastiaens, cited by [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/09/22/twee-explosies-op-korte-tijd-in-antwerpen-noord-huizen-beschad/), the first explosive detonated at the front door of a home in the Hollandstraat around 1:30 a.m. A second device went off moments later at a house in the nearby Osystraat.

"Around 1:30 last night, two bangs echoed in Antwerp-North, a few minutes apart," Bastiaens said. "First an explosive detonated at the front door of a home in the Hollandstraat, followed by an explosive that detonated at a house in the nearby Osystraat."

Two homes and a car were damaged. No injuries were reported. The judicial lab and DOVO, the Belgian army's explosive ordnance disposal service, were deployed to the scene to examine the devices.

## A Pattern Investigators Recognize

Bastiaens said there are "indications to assume that both attacks are linked to each other," pointing to a shared message applied to the facade of each targeted house. Belgian outlet [De Standaard](https://www.standaard.be/binnenland/twee-explosies-in-antwerpen-noord-mogelijk-link-met-drugsmilieu/161839911.html) reported that authorities are examining a possible link to the drug milieu, a line of inquiry police have not ruled out. As of the initial reporting, no suspect had been arrested.

The graffiti reportedly included the Dutch word "hoer" ("whore") alongside what appeared to be a name — a form of signaling and intimidation consistent with the settling of scores in drug-related disputes.

## The Osystraat Address, Targeted Before

The recurrence at the Osystraat address is significant. On 2 April 2023, a heavy explosion at the same house blew out its front door and damaged roughly 20 homes and 6 cars across four surrounding streets, according to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2023/04/02/vijftien-woningen-beschadigd-bij-explosie-in-het-centrum-van-ant/). One resident sustained minor injuries from broken glass.

At the time, investigators treated the drug milieu as the most plausible line of inquiry, and the home was reported to be the parental residence of a suspect in a drugs case. A Dutch national was later convicted and sentenced to four years in prison, though an appeal was filed against that ruling. The fact that the same address was struck again in 2026 strongly suggests an unresolved feud rather than a random incident.

## A City Familiar With This Violence

Antwerp-North, a dense, largely 19th-century working-class district north of the city center, has repeatedly featured in Belgium's drug-related crime wave. The city has recorded dozens of tit-for-tat bombings, grenade attacks, and shootings in recent years, most involving explosives or gunfire aimed at homes believed connected to the drug trade. In June 2025, VRT NWS [documented](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2025/06/30/antwerpen-14-incidenten-van-explosies-of-schoten-aan-woningen/) a "new wave of violence" in the city — the 14th incident of its kind at that point.

The unrest is tied to Antwerp's role as a major European gateway for cocaine. The port of Antwerp-Bruges handles enormous volumes of the drug trade, and the resulting conflicts between rival networks have spilled into residential streets. Dutch public broadcaster [NOS](https://nos.nl/artikel/2450904-twintigers-bij-breda-aangehouden-voor-explosie-antwerpen) has reported on the cross-border nature of these disputes, with suspects arrested in the Netherlands for attacks carried out in Antwerp. City and federal authorities, including Mayor Bart De Wever, have faced sustained political pressure over the violence.

## What to Watch

Several questions remain open. Investigators have not named a target or confirmed a drug-milieu connection, and it is unclear whether the 2026 attacks are tied to the same conflict behind the 2023 Osystraat bombing. No arrests have been announced.

What is clear is the pattern: explosive devices deployed late at night, a shared threatening message, and a home struck for a second time. For residents of Antwerp-North, the twin blasts are the latest reminder that the city's drug war continues to reach ordinary streets — and that some addresses are not left alone.

Authorities have asked anyone with information to come forward as the investigation continues.
