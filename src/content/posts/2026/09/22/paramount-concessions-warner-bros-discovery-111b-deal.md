# Paramount Cedes Ground to Salvage $111B Warner Deal

Paramount Skydance reached a settlement with a coalition of 12 Democratic state attorneys general and, separately, the Writers Guild of America, clearing the final legal obstacle to its $111 billion acquisition of Warner Bros. Discovery. The proposed five-year consent decree, announced Monday by California Attorney General Rob Bonta, requires no major divestitures, instead imposing a series of operating commitments on film production, cable-carriage negotiations, and newsroom independence.

The deal — the largest merger in Hollywood history — would combine two iconic film studios, the CBS and CNN news networks, and the Paramount+ and HBO Max streaming platforms under a single corporate roof.

## The Terms of the Truce

Under the agreement, the combined company must release at least 30 films a year in years one and two, rising to 32 annually in years three through five, according to [Deadline](https://deadline.com/2026/09/paramount-deal-36-film-movie-slate-1237109549/). At least 21 must be wide releases, with a guaranteed 45-day theatrical window and no streaming window for qualifying films until at least 90 days after release. The merged entity must spend at least $300 million more annually on U.S. production than it did in 2025 — $1.5 billion above baseline over five years — lifting the domestic share of production from roughly 5% to 20%, or 40% if California or New York enacts uncapped state tax incentives.

Failure to meet the film quota carries a $30 million penalty per film, with half directed to Hollywood union health and retirement trusts and the remainder split between California's film fund and a national antitrust-enforcement fund. Persistent shortfalls could trigger forced divestiture of Paramount's stake in Miramax.

On distribution, Paramount and Warner Bros. must negotiate basic-cable carriage deals separately for five years, effectively acting as two companies to prevent economies of scale. A breach could lead a court to order the sale of channels including BET, VH-1, Comedy Central, Smithsonian, Destination America, and Science, [Deadline reported](https://deadline.com/2026/09/paramount-settlement-pluto-tv-bet-comedy-central-divesture-1237109340/). Premium channels such as CNN and HBO, along with the TV studios, are not on the divestiture table, and the free streamer Pluto TV is protected.

The decree also establishes a five-member News Editorial Independence Board to set journalism principles for CBS News and CNN. Its members — five established journalists with at least a decade of experience — are selected by Paramount's board of directors, with no more than two affiliated with the same political party. Paramount also committed to keeping its headquarters in Los Angeles and to keeping both studio lots open and operating throughout the decree's term, per the [consent decree](https://deadline.com/2026/09/paramount-warner-bros-discovery-consent-decree-1237109397/).

## A Separate Deal With Writers

The Writers Guild of America, which filed its own antitrust suit in July, settled separately. Paramount agreed to prohibit writer layoffs at CBS News for five years and to pay $17.5 million into the WGA health fund plus attorneys' fees, [according to Deadline](https://deadline.com/2026/09/wga-settlement-lawsuit-paramount-wbd-merger-concessions-1237109441/).

"We continue to believe the merger will cause damage to writers and the industry at large," the WGA said in a statement. "Now that the Attorneys General have settled with Paramount, however, as a nonprofit, the WGA must contend with the reality of forging ahead alone, with no backing from government enforcers, with a complex antitrust lawsuit that would cost millions of dollars to pursue through trial."

## Bonta's Reluctant Approval

The states had repeatedly demanded structural remedies — divestitures — but the final agreement contains none as a condition of closing. Bonta framed the outcome as a hard-fought compromise rather than an endorsement.

"The settlement is not a vote of support for this merger," he said at a Monday press conference in Los Angeles. "It is not a blessing for the broader merger ... which doesn't serve the economy or consumers well." He added: "I don't think these companies should merge."

Bonta argued the terms lock in protections rather than merely guard against harm, pointing to the guaranteed production volumes. "This agreement is the opposite of Disney-Fox," he said, referring to the 2018 merger that led to a steep decline in film output. "More production means more work here at home."

His position drew immediate criticism. Connecticut Attorney General William Tong, who had demanded full divestiture of CNN and CBS News, said he was "deeply disappointed that we could not do more." Jessica Gonzales of the advocacy group Free Press called the deal "a new low," adding that it "contains nothing but unenforceable, empty Paramount promises." Alvaro Bedoya, a former FTC commissioner now with the American Economic Liberties Project, was blunter: "Obviously the billionaires won here. These guys censored, bribed, and bullied their way to the top."

## Why California Folded

Paramount had pledged a "ticking fee" of $7 million for each day past October 1, 2026 that the deal failed to close, making a rapid resolution essential. The Hollywood Reporter [attributed the settlement](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/business/business-news/california-paramount-warner-bros-settle-why-fold-1236707326/) to a combination of top-down political pressure, litigation risk, and cracks in the state coalition.

Governor Gavin Newsom, mindful of employment and California's reputation, inserted himself into negotiations, while Los Angeles Mayor Karen Bass and gubernatorial candidate Xavier Becerra also urged a compromise. Paramount CEO David Ellison had been in talks with Tennessee and Texas about relocating headquarters, with a decision expected at the start of October — a threat to a state already suffering historically low filming levels. A trial, meanwhile, was seen as a coin flip against elite defense counsel, and the federal government had already signed off.

In June 2026, the Justice Department's Antitrust Division cleared the deal with no divestitures or behavioral remedies. The FCC followed on September 18, approving a structure that would leave the merged company up to 49.5% foreign-owned — including stakes from the sovereign wealth funds of Saudi Arabia, Qatar, and Abu Dhabi — with Paramount pledging the shares would be silent and non-voting. That decision drew scrutiny over the national-security implications of foreign ownership of major U.S. news outlets.

## Ellison's Victorious Memo

Ellison, the son of Oracle co-founder Larry Ellison, celebrated the resolution in a memo to staff, [reported by Deadline](https://deadline.com/2026/09/paramount-ceo-david-ellison-settlement-wbd-deal-1237109485/), saying the deal had been "validated by unanimous approval from competition authorities in nearly 70 jurisdictions worldwide, who agreed this deal is pro-competitive, pro-consumer and pro-worker."

"Having now addressed the State AGs' and WGA's concerns, we have complete clearance for this merger," he wrote. "We're confident this agreement does exactly that, memorializing a series of commitments that include 30+ films annually and expanded U.S. film production to help revitalize our industry here at home."

Ellison has argued that Paramount must bulk up to compete against Netflix, Amazon, Apple, and Disney. The combined company is expected to generate $6 billion in cost savings — a figure that analysts and unions expect to translate into significant layoffs. An LA County Economic Development Corp. study estimated at least 4,000 jobs could be lost, with a separate report commissioned by county supervisors warning of up to 4,500 more.

## The Political Subtext

The Ellison family's close ties to President Trump hover over the transaction. Given the recent upheaval at CBS News under editor-in-chief Bari Weiss and the administration's public hostility toward CNN, the AGs voiced concern about the integrity of the merged company's news coverage. The same day the settlement was announced, CNN joined MS NOW and Politico in suing the White House after being barred from press credentials.

Bedoya said the timing risked galvanizing public concern: "This is coming at a moment when people already perceive media freedom to be at risk. They watch CNN, for example, to be barred from the White House doors, and then two or three days later, hear that one of the president's closest allies now officially owns CNN."

## What's Next

The proposed consent decree does not take effect until a federal judge approves it. Judge Araceli Martinez-Olguin, who had set a March 2, 2027 antitrust trial date and ordered mediation for mid-October, will now weigh the agreement. The deal is expected to close within roughly ten days to two weeks, [Xinhua reported](https://english.news.cn/20260922/bef907723c40472da11ce667a77a3293/c.html), letting Paramount avoid the ticking fee.

Even with clearance, the settlement leaves open questions: whether the editorial independence board, whose members Paramount selects, can meaningfully protect CNN and CBS News; how deep the layoffs will run; and whether the merger survives future political change within the decree's five-year term. The actors' and writers' unions have signaled that structural separation for the streaming era — a modern version of the old financial-interest-and-syndication rules — will be their next battleground.

Whatever the outcome, the agreement reshapes the U.S. media landscape at a moment of acute anxiety about press freedom, industry consolidation, and the cost of Hollywood's largest-ever corporate marriage.
