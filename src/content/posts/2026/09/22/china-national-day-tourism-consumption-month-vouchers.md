# China Launches Holiday Tourism Push With ¥310M in Vouchers

China officially launched its 2026 National Day Culture and Tourism Consumption Month on 22 September, unveiling more than 310 million yuan (about $44 million) in consumption vouchers and subsidies alongside over 20,000 planned cultural and tourism events nationwide. The initiative is designed to spur holiday spending during a combined eight-day Mid-Autumn Festival and National Day break.

The Ministry of Culture and Tourism (MCT) held the main-venue launch event in Mile City, Honghe Prefecture, Yunnan Province, according to [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/politics/20260922/88e7ccb611e44c91b8c0bf6ea656ebe9/c.html). The consumption month will run from late September through the end of October, spanning both holidays.

## A Strategic Push Around the 'Golden Week'

National Day, observed on 1 October, commemorates the founding of the People's Republic of China. The resulting 1–7 October holiday — known as "Golden Week" — is one of the country's most important seasonal drivers of consumer spending, and it will merge with the Mid-Autumn Festival in 2026 to create a combined eight-day break.

During the consumption month, localities will focus on seasonal demand themes including mountain-and-river travel, Mid-Autumn reunions, autumn leisure, red tourism, educational travel, the nighttime economy, and intangible-heritage cuisine. The MCT said the effort is intended to "enrich the supply of seasonal cultural and tourism products and services" and better meet residents' and tourists' consumption needs during the two holidays and the autumn season.

## Vouchers, Discounts, and Regional Offers

Under the program, the MCT is coordinating with relevant departments and supporting units to distribute consumption vouchers and other subsidies and to offer discounts and inbound-tourism facilitation measures. The [China Tourism News](https://www.ctnews.com.cn/dongtai/content/2026-09/22/content_192258.html) reported that the main-venue event formally opened the consumption month under the theme of bringing cultural and tourism benefits to the public.

Regional examples of expanded cultural and tourism consumption cited by officials include Shandong's tourism discount packages, Henan's "one-code pass" annual ticket, Zhejiang's cultural-tourism benefit cards, Guangdong's 20 million yuan voucher program, and Qinghai's themed vouchers.

The MCT also pointed to new cultural-tourism scenarios, such as the Chinese Farmers' Harvest Festival autumn consumption season, Beijing-Tianjin-Hebei regional coordinated activities, and Sichuan-Chongqing "travel with the performances" and "art-museum night" offerings built around the 14th China Art Festival. Online travel platforms are rolling out film-inspired travel routes and intangible-heritage food itineraries.

The launch was also carried by [Zijing](https://zijing.com.cn/web/article/1551896339673198592/web/content_1551896339673198592.html) and [Guangming Online](https://politics.gmw.cn/2026-09/22/content_39014585.htm), which reported the same 20,000-plus activities and over 310 million yuan in vouchers.

## Analysis: Stimulus With a Seasonal Deadline

The scale of the campaign underscores how central holiday consumption has become to China's broader economic strategy. Cultural and tourism spending sits at the intersection of services, retail, and transportation — sectors that officials have increasingly relied on to offset softer growth elsewhere.

At the same time, the mid-autumn-to-October window gives the push a built-in deadline. Unlike broad-based fiscal stimulus, voucher programs are time-bound and geographically dispersed, which can sharpen their seasonal effect but tends to pull forward purchases that might have happened anyway. Their net effect on total consumption is often debated.

A second variable is capacity. An eight-day combined holiday is expected to strain transport, accommodation, and major attractions. The MCT has said it will strengthen tourism market supervision to maintain order and create a "reassuring, safe, and welcoming" consumption environment.

## What's Next

With the consumption month running through the end of October, the first meaningful signal will arrive after the Golden Week period, when holiday spending data offer an early read on whether the seasonal push met its goals.

The immediate test will be whether the vouchers translate into spending beyond the subsidized transactions — and whether the eight-day break delivers the travel surge officials are counting on.
