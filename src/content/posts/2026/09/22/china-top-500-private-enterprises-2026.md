# China's Top 500 Private Firms Post Record Revenue

The All-China Federation of Industry and Commerce (ACFIC) released its "2026 Top 500 Chinese Private Enterprises" list on September 22 in Tianjin, with JD.com, Alibaba (China) Co. Ltd. and Hengli Group Co. Ltd. taking the top three spots by revenue, according to [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/fortune/20260922/efb3d28531614cd7abae573ea1e7ffa4/c.html). The combined revenue of the 500 largest private companies reached 44.93 trillion yuan (about 6.2 trillion U.S. dollars), up 4.35 percent year-on-year — a closely watched gauge of the scale and health of China's private economy.

The annual ranking, now in its 28th edition, is widely regarded as the most authoritative benchmark for the private sector, which accounts for a dominant share of Chinese employment, tax revenue and innovation. This year's release conference was held in Tianjin for the first time, co-hosted by ACFIC and the Tianjin Municipal People's Government from September 21 to 23.

## Context: A Benchmark for the Private Economy

ACFIC's survey of large-scale private enterprises forms the basis of the list. This year, 6,350 companies with 2025 revenues above 1 billion yuan (10亿元) participated in the survey, and the top 500 by revenue constitute the ranking, Xinhua reported.

The methodology has tightened over time: the entry threshold has climbed steadily, reflecting the growing scale of China's leading private firms. The list is published alongside companion rankings for private manufacturers (Top 500) and private service enterprises (Top 100), along with special survey and analysis reports.

## Key Developments: The Rankings and the Numbers

The results confirm a stable leadership tier at the top. [Sina News, citing Beijing News (新京报)](https://news.sina.com.cn/c/2026-09-22/doc-inissitp2197789.shtml), reported that the top five by revenue were JD.com, Alibaba, Hengli Group, Huawei Investment Holding and BYD.

According to the full ranking table [published by Baidu Baike](https://baike.baidu.com/item/2026中国民营企业500强/69058988), which aligns with official reporting, the country's ten largest private enterprises by revenue were:

The aggregate figures underscore the sector's fiscal weight. [CCTV News](https://news.cctv.com/2026/09/22/ARTIlxhuBinSJwOPck2QLpdo260922.shtml) reported that the top 500 companies posted combined net profit of 1.83 trillion yuan, averaging 36.52 billion yuan each, up 1.31 percent year-on-year. Their total tax contribution reached 1.30 trillion yuan, with 254 companies paying more than 1 billion yuan in tax — accounting for 50.8 percent of the list.

## Analysis: Scale Grows, But Margins Stay Tight

The headline growth masks a more nuanced picture. Revenue rose a solid 4.35 percent, but profit growth lagged at just 1.31 percent — a gap that suggests the largest private firms are expanding their top line faster than their bottom line. That pattern is consistent with a business environment where competition remains intense and pricing power is limited, even for market leaders.

The composition of the list also reveals policy direction. Organizers deliberately invited more than 30 emerging companies from new-energy, intelligent robotics, intelligent connected vehicles and quantum chip sectors to the Tianjin event for matchmaking and site visits, Baidu Baike noted. The gesture signals an official emphasis on steering private capital toward high-tech and "future industries" as part of China's broader push to upgrade its economic model.

The geographic concentration of the top ranks is telling: Beijing, Guangdong and Zhejiang dominate, reflecting the clustering of platform economy, advanced manufacturing and consumer technology in a handful of coastal and capital regions. At the same time, the list's reach extends well beyond these hubs. For the host city, seven Tianjin-based companies made the cut, including Delong Steel Industry Group at No. 28 and Tianjin Rockcheck Xiangtai Investment Holding at No. 72, [according to Sohu](https://www.sohu.com/a/1079372987_121117452) — a reminder that heavy industry and manufacturing remain central to the private economy's backbone.

## What's Next

The 2026 list confirms that China's largest private enterprises continue to grow, even as profit growth trails revenue and broader economic headwinds persist. The modest profit expansion — barely above 1 percent — will be a key indicator to watch in next year's survey, as will the pace at which newer, innovation-driven firms climb the rankings.

With the release conference now rotating to Tianjin for the first time, the event's growing focus on emerging industries suggests the ranking is being positioned not just as a scorecard of the past year, but as a platform to shape the private sector's next phase of growth.
