# Xi Begins Historic State Visit to the United States

Chinese President Xi Jinping will make a state visit to the United States from September 23 to 25 at the invitation of U.S. President Donald Trump, a trip Beijing and Washington are framing as a historic milestone in a relationship both sides now describe as a constructive strategic stability relationship. Announced by China's Foreign Ministry on September 21, the visit is a reciprocal one: it follows Trump's state visit to Beijing in May 2026, making it the first time in Xi's tenure that the two heads of state have exchanged visits within a single six-month period.

## A Visit Framed in Historic Terms

According to [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/politics/leaders/20260922/6cb1b42a4291419db781ad6bad6f0e82/c.html), the exchange of visits carries historic and milestone significance. The state-run agency published a commentary on the eve of Xi's departure invoking more than 50 years of bilateral relations, from the 1971 ping-pong diplomacy that helped open ties to the handshake across the Pacific.

At a regular press briefing, Foreign Ministry spokesperson Guo Jiakun said head-of-state diplomacy plays an irreplaceable strategic guiding role in China-U.S. relations, adding that Beijing is ready to work with Washington to enrich the connotation of the constructive strategic stability relationship, strengthen dialogue and cooperation, and properly manage differences, as [reported](https://www.gov.cn/lianbo/202609/content_7081659.htm) by the Chinese government's official portal.

## Itinerary: Three Days, Carefully Choreographed

Per the White House itinerary, Xi and his wife, Peng Liyuan, will arrive on September 23 at Joint Base Andrews outside Washington, where Trump is expected to personally greet them, an unusually rare honor. The [BBC](https://www.bbc.com/zhongwen/articles/cx980mjemv73o/simp) noted that, excluding the special case of papal visits, a U.S. president has not greeted a foreign head of state at the airport in more than 60 years, drawing comparisons to President Eisenhower's 1959 welcome for Soviet leader Nikita Khrushchev.

On September 24, Trump will host Xi at the White House for a military honor-guard review in the Rose Garden and leaders' talks, followed by a state dinner in the East Room attended by executives from major U.S. technology firms. On September 25, the two leaders and their spouses will hold a tea gathering and visit the U.S. National Archives to view the country's founding documents before Xi departs.

## Building on the Beijing Summit

The visit is the culmination of a diplomatic arc that began with a bilateral meeting in Busan, South Korea, in October 2025, where Trump invited Xi to the United States. During Trump's May 2026 state visit to Beijing, the two leaders agreed to build a China-U.S. constructive strategic stability relationship as the new positioning for bilateral ties, a framework intended to guide the relationship for three years or more.

Xi has articulated the framework along four dimensions: cooperation-driven positive stability, competition-with-limits benign stability, controllable-divergence normal stability, and lasting stability with peace in prospect. A third 1.5-track dialogue in Beijing on August 25 brought together political, strategic, and business figures from both sides to advance implementation of the leaders' consensus.

Ahead of the summit, U.S. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent met Chinese Vice Premier He Lifeng in New York on September 20 to prepare the ground for the talks.

## Economic Resilience and Common Ground

Chinese state media has emphasized the commercial dimension of the relationship. Xinhua cited data showing that bilateral goods trade rose 13.7 percent year-on-year in the second quarter of 2026, and that American cumulative foreign-invested enterprises in China exceeded 84,000 as of July. Harley Seyedin, president of the American Chamber of Commerce in South China, said 75 percent of surveyed U.S. firms plan to reinvest in China in 2026, adding that American companies have not withdrawn from China but are shifting from scale expansion to deep value creation.

Craig Allen, president of the U.S.-China Business Council, described the two economies as the world's two largest, most dynamic, and most technologically advanced, calling their economic relationship essential and indispensable. Both sides have signaled potential cooperation in green energy, the digital economy, artificial intelligence, and biomedicine.

## A Different Kind of Summit

Western analysts frame the meeting differently. Writing in [The New York Times](https://cn.nytimes.com/opinion/20260921/trump-china-ai/), columnist Nicholas Kristof argued that this may be the first China-U.S. summit led by China, citing what he described as a weakened U.S. position under Trump. Julian Gewirtz, a Columbia University China scholar and former Biden White House official, told the paper that it used to be that U.S. leaders drove the relationship, but now Xi holds the initiative, adding that Trump views Xi as an equal, sees China as a superpower, and is often quite deferential to him.

Kristof also pointed to AI safety as a potential area of modest progress. Both leaders are expected to discuss artificial intelligence, with the possibility of cooperation on narrow issues such as reducing the risk of bioweapon-enabled attacks and establishing a hotline to manage AI-related incidents. Trump has previously expressed skepticism about AI safety concerns, while Beijing tends to frame the risk as the technology's potential misuse against the Chinese state.

## What's Next

The summit's core agenda is broad: trade and economic relations, AI competition and safety, Iran and global security, rare earths, and people-to-people exchanges among the likely topics. Taiwan remains a sensitive undercurrent; BBC reporting has noted debate over whether U.S.-Taiwan arms sales could become a bargaining chip in the talks.

For both governments, the immediate objective is stability. As Beijing's commentary put it, the Pacific Ocean is vast enough to accommodate the common development and respective prosperity of both China and the United States. Whether the visit consolidates the new framework into concrete cooperation, or merely underscores the widening gap between the two powers' worldviews, will be the measure by which it is ultimately judged.
