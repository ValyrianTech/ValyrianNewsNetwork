# Pennsylvania Woman Dies of Measles Amid Record U.S. Outbreak

A 40-year-old unvaccinated woman from Jefferson County, Pennsylvania, has died from complications associated with measles, the county coroner's office confirmed — the state's third measles-related death of 2026 and a stark marker of the largest U.S. measles resurgence in decades.

The woman died on Saturday, September 12, and her death was announced the following day, according to [CBS News Pittsburgh](https://www.cbsnews.com/pittsburgh/news/jefferson-county-measles-related-death-report/). Coroner Greg Furlong said the woman, a Jefferson County resident whose identity has not been released, was unvaccinated, as [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/09/13/nx-s1-5968093/pennsylvania-says-an-unvaccinated-woman-has-died-from-measles-related-complications) reported. It is the county's first measles-related death.

"This is a heartbreaking loss for the family and an unfortunate reminder that measles can be a serious and potentially life-threatening disease," Furlong said in a statement. "Our thoughts and prayers are with the family during this difficult time. We ask that the public and media respect their privacy as they mourn the loss of their loved one."

## A Statewide Outbreak of Unusual Scale

The death comes as Pennsylvania contends with one of the country's most severe measles outbreaks. As of September 11, the Pennsylvania Department of Health [reported](https://www.pa.gov/agencies/health/newsroom/department-of-health-provides-statewide-update-091126) 676 confirmed measles cases across 37 counties, with 124 hospitalizations and two cumulative measles-associated deaths — figures that did not yet reflect the Jefferson County death.

Only a tiny fraction of cases have occurred in vaccinated people: four individuals, three adults and one child, representing less than 1 percent of the state's total. None were hospitalized, the department said.

"Many people associate measles with a childhood illness, but it can lead to severe complications in individuals of any age," said Jefferson County Chief Deputy Coroner Jason Stiver, per CBS News. "Measles is highly contagious and can result in serious respiratory illness, hospitalization, and in rare cases like this, death."

The earlier two deaths were infants in Lancaster County, reported in August. In one case, the child was born with a rare genetic condition called Amish lethal microcephaly; in the other, the infant died of a ruptured spleen. The [Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/sep/13/pennsylvania-third-measles-death-outbreak) noted that in one of those cases the infant was born with a genetic defect that made her vulnerable to infection.

## National Context: A Record Resurgence

The death underscores a broader national problem. According to the [U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention](https://www.cdc.gov/measles/data-research/index.html), 3,294 confirmed measles cases were reported nationwide as of September 10, 2026 — the highest count in decades and up sharply from 2,289 cases in all of 2025. About 95 percent of those cases involved people who were unvaccinated or whose vaccination status was unknown.

The resurgence reflects falling vaccination rates. The CDC reports that MMR coverage among U.S. kindergartners declined from 95.2 percent in the 2019–2020 school year to 92.4 percent in 2025–2026 — below the roughly 95 percent threshold needed to sustain community immunity. Two doses of the MMR vaccine are about 97 percent effective at preventing measles.

Measles is among the most infectious diseases known to medicine. The CDC estimates that nearly one in five unvaccinated people who contract it are hospitalized, and that death occurs in roughly one to three patients per 1,000 cases.

Pennsylvania has mounted an aggressive response. State health officials have administered nearly 4,300 MMR vaccinations at 130 pop-up clinics since the outbreak began in late April, and providers delivered more than 46,000 MMR doses in August 2026 — far above the roughly 25,000 typical for a month.

## A Politicized Toll

The Pennsylvania deaths have also become a flashpoint between the state and the federal government. After Gov. Josh Shapiro announced the first two deaths, U.S. Health and Human Services Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. said on X that the announcement appeared "premature" and "may even have been altogether fabricated by one of the Governor's hopeful staffers," NPR reported.

The CDC has not listed any 2026 deaths on its measles data page. Instead, an asterisk states that the National Center for Health Statistics "does not currently have any death records from 2026 that indicate measles is the underlying cause." NPR noted that the agency typically defers to states' determinations in such outbreaks so it can alert the public, doctors and health officials around the country.

Public health experts have raised concerns about the agency's reliability. Dr. Georges Benjamin, executive director of the American Public Health Association, told the Associated Press that the CDC is not to be trusted in sharing information and that people are questioning its motives. Four senior CDC officials resigned in protest in the summer of 2025.

"I've been practicing in Pennsylvania for 26 years," Dr. Jennifer Janco, chair of St. Luke Pediatrics, told CBS News Philadelphia. "This is the worst it's ever been. I have a front row seat to a show I don't want."

## What to Watch

The Jefferson County coroner's office is working with the Pennsylvania Department of Health on the case. Whether the CDC will officially count the Jefferson County death — and the two Lancaster infant deaths — as measles-associated remains unresolved.

A key milestone looms in November 2026, when international health officials are expected to meet to determine whether the United States and Mexico have lost their measles-elimination status. The U.S. eliminated domestic measles spread in 2000 by sustaining high vaccination rates — an achievement that experts warn is now at risk.

For residents, officials repeated a straightforward message: two doses of the MMR vaccine remain the single best protection against a disease that, decades after it was declared eliminated, is once again claiming lives.
