# China Localities Ramp Up Housing Support Measures

Multiple Chinese localities have rolled out targeted measures to support housing consumption since the start of September, tailoring subsidies, trade-in programs and looser purchase rules to local conditions in a renewed effort to stabilize the property market, [People's Daily Finance](http://finance.people.com.cn/n1/2026/0914/c1004-40797997.html) reported.

The steps span provincial and municipal levels, from a 15-point package issued by Henan Province to home-purchase subsidies in Wuhan and relaxed eligibility rules in Haikou, according to Securities Daily, whose report People's Daily Finance republished.

## Context

The wave of local measures follows a national push set at the National Housing and Urban-Rural Development Work Conference in late December 2025, which called on cities to "control increments, reduce inventory and optimize supply" through city-specific policies. [Guangming, citing CCTV](https://m.gmw.cn/2025-12/23/content_1304275886.htm), reported that the 2026 priorities also include acquiring existing commercial housing for subsidized and resettlement housing, expanding the real estate project "whitelist" financing system, and advancing completed-home sales to prevent delivery risks.

September and October mark China's traditional peak home-buying season. The previous year's peak was notably weak, and the current measures represent a renewed attempt to drive a phased recovery in core-city transaction volumes.

## Key Developments

At the provincial level, Henan's Department of Housing and Urban-Rural Development and eight other departments issued the "Several Measures on Coordinating Efforts to Control Increments, Destock and Optimize Supply to Stabilize the Real Estate Market" on September 8, according to [Guandian](https://www.guandian.cn/article/20260908/598319.html).

The 15 measures, divided into four parts, cover housing supply management, consumption support, diversified upgrading demand and broader use of the provident fund. On the demand side, a home within the intended county, city or district can now be counted as a first home if the buyer owns no other property there, while multi-child families that already own a home may deduct one unit when calculating a subsequent purchase.

The package encourages city-specific home-purchase subsidies and loan interest subsidies, along with "real estate plus" promotional campaigns tied to local culture and tourism brands. It also calls for accelerating the acquisition of existing commercial housing as subsidized housing, dormitories and talent housing, continuing trade-in programs and expanding provident fund usage.

Wuhan's Municipal Housing and Urban Renewal Bureau issued its own notice on September 4. From its effective date through December 31, 2026, families buying newly built commercial housing in seven new urban districts — Changjiang New Area, Dongxihu, Huangpi, Jiangxia, Caidian, Xinzhou and Hannan — receive a subsidy equal to 1% of the total contract price. The total pool is capped at 100 million yuan, allocated first-come, first-served.

In Haikou, the Municipal Housing and Urban-Rural Development Bureau issued a notice on September 5 encouraging trade-ins. Anyone who lists a home owned by themselves or a family member for sale in Hainan Province may purchase one commercial home in Haikou, and employees of enterprises registered in Hainan who actually work and live there may also buy one commercial home in the city.

Provident fund support is another focus. [Yicai](https://www.yicai.com/brief/103355620.html) reported that Henan's package calls for advancing a housing provident fund system for flexible-employment workers and for allowing withdrawals for self-owned housing-related spending such as installing elevators in older residential buildings.

## Analysis

Analysts described the measures as a shift from broad, universal stimulus toward precise, targeted support. Yan Yuejin, vice president of the Shanghai E-House Real Estate Research Institute, said the Henan package forms "a full-chain policy system from land supply to housing consumption, from incremental control to inventory revitalization, and from quality improvement to risk prevention and control."

Li Yujia, chief researcher at the Guangdong Provincial Housing Policy Research Center, said support measures have shifted "from universal benefits to precise incentives and targeted support," aligning with talent and upgrading demand. Trade-in subsidies and lower credit thresholds, he added, directly connect the chain of swapping second-hand homes for new ones and are expected to drive a phased recovery in core-city transactions during the "Golden September and Silver October" period.

At the same time, Li cautioned that the policy provides a floor for reasonable housing consumption rather than comprehensively stimulating prices. Under the constraints of increment control and completed-home sales, the market is likely to differentiate further: liquidity in high-quality assets in core cities should recover, while regions lacking population and industrial support will still face inventory pressure.

## What's Next

The effectiveness of the measures will depend on execution. Yan said attention should focus on the actual implementation of local home-purchase and loan subsidies, the pace of acquiring existing commercial housing, and how expanding the completed-home sales pilot affects developers' capital chains.

A separate [21st Century Business Herald](https://m.21jingji.com/article/20260106/7f71c8f4e6456fd46232151627beb924.html) analysis noted that more than 100 cities introduced or refined housing support policies for multi-child families in 2025, spanning purchase subsidies, higher provident fund loan ceilings and priority access to subsidized housing — a trend that continues to shape the current round of local measures. Whether the September package can convert policy intent into sustained sales momentum through the rest of the year remains the central question for China's property market.
