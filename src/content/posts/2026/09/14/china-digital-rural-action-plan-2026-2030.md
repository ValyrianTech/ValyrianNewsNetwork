# China Sets Digital Rural Roadmap Through 2030

China's top internet, agriculture and industry authorities have jointly issued a sweeping action plan to accelerate the digital transformation of the countryside over the next five years, setting eight measurable targets and rolling out 24 work measures across five priority areas for the 2026-2030 period.

The Digital Rural High-Quality Development Action Plan (2026-2030) was released by the Cyberspace Administration of China (CAC), the Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Affairs, and the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology, according to an [official announcement](https://www.cac.gov.cn/2026-09/11/c_1790876575314396.htm) published on the CAC website on September 11. [People's Daily](http://society.people.com.cn/n1/2026/0914/c1008-40797942.html) carried a report on the plan in its September 14 print edition.

## A Roadmap for the 15th Five-Year Plan

The plan serves as the operational blueprint for rural digitalization during China's 15th Five-Year Plan period (2026-2030). It implements the national Five-Year Plan Outline and builds on the 2019 Digital Rural Development Strategy Outline, responding directly to a call in the 2026 Central Document No. 1 to "implement the digital rural high-quality development action."

According to a [government Q&A](https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/202609/content_7080847.htm) with a CAC official, the policy reflects three converging pressures and opportunities: rapid innovation in artificial intelligence, big data and the Internet of Things reshaping agricultural production; the broader push for "four modernizations" in which agricultural and rural modernization remains the weakest link; and the emergence of new groups such as digital nomads who can help channel capital, talent and resources into rural areas.

By 2030, the plan states, rural digital development should achieve "significant progress," with optimized new-type information infrastructure, markedly improved digitalization of rural industry, informatization of rural construction and smart governance, a sounder sustainable development mechanism, and a stronger sense of gain, happiness and security among farmers.

## Eight Indicators, Twenty-Four Measures

The plan sets eight headline indicators for 2030, covering rural internet penetration, the contribution rate of agricultural scientific and technological progress, national rural online retail sales, the share of administrative villages with integrated delivery-logistics service stations, smart integrated management service coverage, the share of township health centers covered by medical imaging centers of tightly-knit county medical communities, the number of online cultural activities by county-level cultural centers, and the share of rural adults with primary-level or above digital literacy and skills.

The 24 work measures are organized into five deployment areas: consolidating the digital rural foundation; raising rural industry digitalization; raising rural construction informatization; raising rural governance smartification; and stimulating endogenous drivers. The [full text](https://www.cac.gov.cn/2026-09/11/c_1790876575309197.htm) details measures ranging from 5G and gigabit optical network deployment and BeiDou satellite applications to smart agriculture, rural e-commerce, telemedicine and AI-assisted governance.

## Building on a Decade of Progress

Officials framed the plan as an escalation of gains already made. According to the government Q&A, China has achieved "gigabit connectivity in every county, 5G in every township and broadband in every village," with administrative village 5G coverage exceeding 95 percent. Rural internet penetration has risen to 69.5 percent, narrowing the urban-rural gap by 8.3 percentage points since the end of the 13th Five-Year Plan, and more than half of rural adults now possess primary-level or above digital literacy and skills.

The economic picture is similarly strong. Rural online retail sales surpassed 3 trillion yuan (about $420 billion) in 2025, a 67.6 percent increase from the end of the 13th Five-Year Plan, while the informatization rate of agricultural production has grown by roughly 2 percentage points annually. New professions such as agricultural digitalization technicians and AI data annotators have emerged as channels for rural employment.

The plan also builds on public-service gains: the national smart education platform now hosts more than 130,000 teaching resources, remote medical service networks cover all cities and counties, and the national e-government extranet reaches 99.4 percent of townships.

## What the Plan Emphasizes

Several measures signal the plan's priorities. On infrastructure, it calls for deepening universal telecommunications service and "Broadband Frontier" construction, deploying 5G and gigabit optical networks in rural areas as needed, and exploring high-throughput satellites and low-orbit satellite internet to reach remote regions. It also promotes blockchain for agricultural product traceability and rural collective asset management, and expands "low-altitude economy" applications in agriculture.

On industry, the plan targets breakthroughs in agricultural sensors and specialized chips, core algorithms, robots and intelligent breeding tools, and calls for piloting unmanned agricultural machinery. It promotes rural e-commerce through the "Digital Commerce for Agriculture" initiative and the orderly adoption of low-altitude logistics, while supporting new energy vehicles and green smart appliances in rural markets.

On public services, the plan emphasizes AI integration in rural education and healthcare, financial inclusion through digital credit and insurance products, and protections for the elderly, children and people with disabilities. On governance, it advances village-level digital administration, the crackdown on rural telecom fraud and online gambling, and smart emergency management systems.

The final section stresses implementation: the CAC will coordinate with the agriculture and industry ministries, conduct regular monitoring, and promote international exchange. It also warns against formalism, urging localities to "do their best within their means" and adapt measures to local conditions.

## Market and Strategic Implications

Financial media framed the plan as supportive of a broad range of sectors, from smart agriculture and sensors to robotics, satellite internet, broadcasting networks, cold-chain logistics, rural e-commerce and electric vehicles, as [Xinhua Finance reported](https://m.21jingji.com/article/20260911/herald/fc0b58b10433a774e7b7f8b13bef9702.html).

The plan's emphasis on "new quality productive forces for agriculture" underscores a strategic shift: digitalization is no longer framed merely as a service to rural residents but as a driver of agricultural modernization and national competitiveness. With agricultural and rural modernization identified as the weakest link in China's 2035 modernization goals, the countryside is becoming a frontier for deploying the country's AI, satellite and robotics capabilities.

## What to Watch

Much will depend on implementation. The plan assigns coordination to the CAC but leaves detailed targets for the eight indicators largely unspecified in public materials, and the gap between ambitious measures and local capacity is a recurring challenge in Chinese rural policy. Analysts will watch whether the plan's anti-formalism language translates into flexible, differentiated rollout across China's vastly diverse countryside—from wealthy suburban villages to remote, depopulated ones. The coming year's provincial implementation plans and the regular monitoring reports will offer the first real test of whether the roadmap's ambitions match its execution.
