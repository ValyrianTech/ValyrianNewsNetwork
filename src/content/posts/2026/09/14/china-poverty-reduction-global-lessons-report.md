# China Touts Global Lessons From Poverty Reduction

China is promoting its anti-poverty record as a model for the developing world, citing a new state-backed study that found overseas media published more than 3,200 English-language reports on the country's poverty reduction efforts over the past five years. The report, produced by the Academy of Contemporary China and World Studies (ACCWS), was summarized on September 14 in a [People's Daily Overseas Edition](http://world.people.com.cn/n1/2026/0914/c1002-40798303.html) article by reporter Peng Xunwen.

The ACCWS, a think tank under the China International Publishing Group (CIPG), examined how international media, think tanks, and academia perceived China's poverty reduction practices from roughly 2021 to 2025. Its findings form the basis of Beijing's latest push to cast its development experience as a template for other nations.

## Counting the Coverage

According to the report, professional database searches identified 3,265 English-language reports on China's poverty reduction published abroad over the five-year period. A further 1,731 English-language reports addressed "comprehensive rural revitalization," Beijing's follow-on program for the countryside.

Attention was unevenly distributed by region. The report found that Asia and Europe showed the strongest interest in China's anti-poverty agenda, with North America and Africa trailing. The [print edition](http://paper.people.com.cn/rmrbhwb/pc/content/202609/14/content_30180939.html) of the People's Daily Overseas Edition carried the same analysis on page 10 under its "Foreign Media Look at China" feature, and the [Global Times](https://3w.huanqiu.com/a/54dc9a/4TCiOinWRZo) reprinted the piece.

The report also highlighted domestic economic data: from 2021 to 2025, per capita disposable income among rural residents in formerly poverty-stricken counties grew faster than the national rural average for five consecutive years. That figure is independently corroborated by China's National Bureau of Statistics, which tracks income trends in designated former poverty counties.

## What the World Is Saying

The study assembled a roster of sympathetic foreign voices. Russia's RIA Novosti cited Andrei Karneev, dean of the Oriental College at the Higher School of Economics, who called lifting so large a population out of poverty so quickly "a unique achievement in world history." Then-World Bank country director Martin Raiser said the institution was "very confident that China's actions to eradicate absolute poverty in rural areas have been successful," according to the report.

Nigeria's The Guardian argued that "it is impossible to truly talk about Chinese-style modernization and its great achievements in poverty reduction without mentioning the impressive, development-oriented reforms promoted by the Communist Party of China." Pakistan's The News International credited a defined "responsibility system" for tightly linking planning, funding, and execution. Zimbabwe Independent described China's success as "a beacon for Global South countries."

Chinese researchers drew their own conclusions. ACCWS president Li Yafang said the international community broadly views China's system and governance capacity as the fundamental support for its poverty reduction achievements, with Communist Party leadership as the "core driving force." Assistant research fellow Hu Shaohua noted that international attention is shifting "from poverty governance to development transformation, and from experience borrowing to poverty reduction cooperation."

## A Shifting Focus

That shift reflects a change in China's posture. After declaring a "complete victory" in its poverty alleviation campaign in 2021, Beijing established a five-year transition period during which formerly poor counties continued to receive the same policy support as before. Singapore's Straits Times was among the outlets that noted the continued backing.

The report points to the 2026 No. 1 Central Document, [issued](https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/2026/issue_12566/material/gwygb202605.pdf) early this year, which anchors agricultural and rural modernization and introduces the term "low-carbon agriculture" for the first time. The document frames rural policy not as charity but as an effort to fold the rural economy into China's high-tech, digital future.

ACCWS has issued similar studies before. A 2021 report, released at a forum in Yunnan's Nujiang prefecture, distilled five core concepts of China's approach, [according](https://yn.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202105/19/WS60a46b74a3101e7ce97503bd.html) to China Daily. A 2023 follow-up laid out a "DREAM" framework covering dynamic poverty prevention, rural industry support, east-west paired assistance, resettlement integration, and rural governance modernization, [according](http://www.cicg.org.cn/2023-03/20/content_42300030.htm) to CIPG.

China has also channeled its Global Development Initiative into sharing agricultural know-how abroad, including modern shrimp-farming techniques with Cambodia and improved crop varieties and water-conservancy technology with Ethiopia and Zimbabwe. In May 2026, China, 53 countries, and 9 international organizations jointly launched a Global Poverty Reduction and Development Partnership Alliance at a high-level forum.

## Analysis and Outlook

This is less a hard news event than an exercise in narrative building. The People's Daily piece is a state media outlet reporting on a state think tank's summary of foreign opinion, and it selects voices that affirm Beijing's preferred framing. That does not make the underlying data false, but readers should treat the report as advocacy as much as analysis.

Still, the underlying trend is real. China's success in lifting hundreds of millions out of extreme poverty is widely acknowledged, including by institutions such as the World Bank. The more contested question is how transferable its model is, given the scale of state mobilization and central planning it relies on.

ACCWS vice president Fan Daqi called for using multilateral platforms and regional cooperation mechanisms to deepen exchange on poverty reduction, while researcher Zhang Danping urged China to release richer, first-hand materials to tell its story abroad. Whether such efforts persuade skeptics may depend less on volume of coverage than on the results of the next phase: turning rural revitalization into a durable engine of growth.
