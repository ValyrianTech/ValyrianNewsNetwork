# Jingdezhen Porcelain Added to UNESCO Heritage List

Jingdezhen, the centuries-old ceramic capital of China, has achieved a historic milestone. The "Jingdezhen Handicraft Porcelain Industry Sites" were inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List on July 25, 2026, at the 48th session of the World Heritage Committee in Busan, South Korea, according to [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/20260725/08df13ab8e6a4611a1e76cb134957c9c/c.html). The inscription makes the site China's 61st World Heritage property and, notably, the first ever on the list to be themed around porcelain.

## Context: The Ceramic Capital

For over two millennia, Jingdezhen has stood as China's unrivaled ceramic capital. Situated in northeastern Jiangxi Province, the city's abundant deposits of kaolin — the pure white clay essential for porcelain — combined with its access to the Changjiang River waterway created the perfect conditions for a ceramics industry that would transform global material culture.

The British scholar Joseph Needham, in his monumental work "Science and Civilisation in China," described Jingdezhen as "the earliest industrial city in the world." By the mid-Ming Dynasty, the city's population exceeded 100,000, and over the course of three centuries following the 16th century, an estimated 300 million pieces of Jingdezhen porcelain reached Europe alone. The city's celebrated blue-and-white wares became synonymous with Chinese artistry worldwide, influencing painting, furniture, architecture, and decorative arts across the European continent.

## What the Heritage Site Includes

The newly inscribed serial property comprises five component parts with 15 groups of heritage elements and 45 individual heritage points, spanning a total area of 7,524.7 hectares. The components include the Town Area Porcelain Production Center, the Hutian Ancient Kiln Site, the Gaoling Porcelain Clay Mining Site, the Changling Porcelain Stone Mining Site, and the Jiaotan Firewood Production Area.

These sites collectively represent the complete industrial chain of Jingdezhen's handcraft porcelain industry from the 10th to the 19th centuries — from raw material extraction through to finished product. UNESCO's World Heritage Committee recognized the property's integrity and authenticity, noting that it showcases the fusion and innovation of world ceramic technology and art during this period.

The committee evaluated the site against four of UNESCO's ten selection criteria — Standards 2, 3, 4, and 6 — reflecting its significance in demonstrating exchanges of human values, providing a unique testimony to a cultural tradition, and representing an outstanding example of a technological ensemble.

## Decades in the Making

Jingdezhen's path to World Heritage status spans more than four decades. The Hutian Ancient Kiln Site was listed as a National Key Cultural Relic as early as 1982, and the Imperial Kiln site was confirmed through archaeology in 1984. By 2006, the Imperial Kiln site had been designated as a National Key Cultural Relic, and by 2017 it was placed on China's tentative World Heritage list.

The campaign gained significant momentum in 2023 when President Xi Jinping inspected Jingdezhen, followed by the official confirmation in January 2025 that the site would be China's 2026 World Heritage nomination. A pivotal moment came in May 2026 when ICOMOS, the UNESCO advisory body, recommended "direct inscription" — the highest possible recommendation — followed by a draft resolution from the World Heritage Center in June. On July 25, 2026, the site was formally approved.

## Voices of Recognition

Speaking at the World Heritage Committee session, Rao Quan, China's Vice Minister of Culture and Tourism, pledged that China would "earnestly fulfill the World Heritage Convention, continuously strengthen the holistic and systematic protection, inheritance and utilization of heritage."

Wang Tongmao, Director of the Jingdezhen World Heritage Office, emphasized that the successful inscription "fills the gap in the World Heritage system with 'porcelain' as the theme." The recognition places porcelain alongside silk and tea as the third pillar of China's cultural heritage recognized by UNESCO — forming what analysts describe as a complete triad of ancient Chinese trade and cultural exports.

International experts welcomed the decision. Oriol Calvo Vergés, President of the International Academy of Ceramics, remarked that "inheritance is everywhere and innovation is ubiquitous in Jingdezhen." Patty Wouters, the Academy's representative to UNESCO, said the site's recognition would ensure that "ceramics continue to connect people, preserve traditions and inspire future generations." Takeshi Yasuda, an 83-year-old Japanese ceramic artist, observed that "tradition and innovation in Jingdezhen are not contradictory but complementary."

## Significance and Implications

The inscription carries both symbolic and practical weight. For Jingdezhen, it represents the highest global validation of its cultural importance and is expected to drive a significant increase in tourism. The World Heritage designation also brings binding preservation commitments under the United Nations convention, requiring China to enhance protections for the site.

Today, Jingdezhen's ceramic industry chain employs over 100,000 people, supported by more than 3,200 inheritors of intangible cultural heritage in ceramic craftsmanship. Chen Kelong, Party Chief of Jingdezhen, described the inscription as "a new starting point" for the city's ongoing cultural and economic development.

The recognition also sets a precedent as the first industrial heritage nomination from China to achieve World Heritage status, potentially paving the way for other industrial heritage sites globally. A 2026-2030 archaeological plan is expected to yield new discoveries about the site's extensive history.

## Looking Ahead

As the oldest continually operating ceramic production center in the world, Jingdezhen's UNESCO recognition cements its place in global cultural heritage. The inscription honors centuries of craftsmanship while committing China to preserving this living heritage for future generations. With partnerships underway including a "Digital Jingdezhen" initiative that leverages AI and gaming technology, the ancient ceramic capital is poised to bridge its illustrious past with an innovative future.
