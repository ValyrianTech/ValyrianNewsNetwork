# Berlin Pride Attack: One Dead, 16 Wounded, Manhunt Underway

A white van plowed into a crowd celebrating Berlin's Christopher Street Day (CSD) Pride parade on Saturday evening, killing one woman and injuring at least 16 others in what German authorities are investigating as a terrorist attack. The suspect, identified as 21-year-old Abdul B., a German national of Lebanese origin with alleged links to Islamist circles, remains at large as a massive city-wide manhunt unfolds.

## The Attack

The incident occurred on the evening of Saturday, 25 July 2026, on the Ahornsteig pedestrian path in Berlin's Tiergarten park, near the iconic Brandenburg Gate. According to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/07/25/auto-rijdt-in-op-menigte-pride-duitsland/), the white van drove at high speed into the crowd before colliding with a tree. The driver then fled on foot.

Emergency services rushed to the scene as hundreds of thousands of Pride attendees were asked to evacuate calmly. The event was officially cancelled shortly after the attack, with organizers using speakers, screens, and social media to instruct the crowd to leave the area. [BBC News](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cevmdxz4872o) reported that a performance was abruptly halted, with the word "Evacuation" projected onto a large screen on the stage as emergency vehicles raced to the scene.

Three of the injured are in life-threatening condition, while eight others sustained severe injuries. Later reports from [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Berlin_Pride_van_attack), citing multiple sources, revised the injury count to 25. Police are also investigating reports that several people were stabbed during or after the vehicle ramming, though it remains unclear whether the stab wounds were part of the same attack or a separate incident.

## The Suspect

Berlin police identified the primary suspect as Abdul B., a 21-year-old man with Lebanese roots residing in Berlin. In a joint statement with the General Public Prosecutor's Office, police described the suspect as having connections to Islamist circles and being known to authorities for prior violent robberies and possible links to terrorist organizations, including the Islamic State (IS).

According to [DW News](https://www.dw.com/en/germany-launches-manhunt-after-berlin-pride-van-ramming/live-78117180), the suspect was released from juvenile detention in May 2026, just two months before the attack. He is described as having a slim build, standing approximately 1.90 meters (6'3"), with black hair. He was last seen wearing a black hoodie and white trousers.

Police have warned the public not to approach the suspect, whom they believe may be armed and dangerous.

## The Manhunt

A massive police operation is underway across Berlin, with helicopters equipped with thermal cameras scouring the city and additional checkpoints set up at train stations. A search of a residence in the Schöneberg district yielded no arrest. Authorities have not ruled out the possibility of multiple perpetrators, and police are investigating whether more than one person fled the vehicle.

The manhunt has been extended to Germany's borders, with alerts issued to neighboring countries including the Netherlands, Belgium, France, Austria, Switzerland, the Czech Republic, Poland, and Denmark.

## Political Reaction

German leaders swiftly condemned the attack. Berlin's Governing Mayor Kai Wegner called it "an attack on our free and open society," stating that "the parade for a tolerant and peaceful Berlin was attacked in the most brutal way."

Chancellor Friedrich Merz described the ramming as a "heinous act" and promised that authorities "will work with particular determination to ensure that the perpetrators are punished." Interior Minister Alexander Dobrindt said he was "deeply shaken" by the attack.

Alfonso Pantisano, Berlin's Queer Affairs Commissioner, described the day as "a day of great mourning for the queer community."

## Broader Context

Berlin Pride, officially known as Christopher Street Day, is one of Europe's largest LGBTQ+ events, drawing approximately one million attendees annually. The 2026 edition featured a parade through central Berlin culminating in a closing concert near the Brandenburg Gate.

Vehicle-ramming attacks have become a recurring tactic among extremists due to their low technical barrier and high potential for casualties. Germany experienced its deadliest Islamist attack in 2016, when a truck was driven into a Berlin Christmas market, killing 12 people.

The attack on Berlin Pride echoes a broader pattern of violence targeting LGBTQ+ events and represents the latest in a series of vehicle-ramming incidents that have challenged European security services. As the manhunt continues, questions remain about how the suspect was able to bypass road blockades and security measures that were in place for the event.

## What's Next

The immediate priority for German authorities remains the capture of Abdul B., with a city-wide and cross-border search operation in full effect. Police are expected to provide further updates as the investigation develops, including whether additional suspects were involved and whether the reported stabbings are linked to the vehicle attack. The LGBTQ+ community in Berlin and across Germany mourns what should have been a celebration of tolerance and diversity, now marked by tragedy.
