# Baby of 1.5 Months Dies in Ichtegem; Investigation Launched

A 1.5-month-old baby boy has been found dead in a villa in Ichtegem, West Flanders, prompting the Belgian public prosecutor's office to launch an investigation into the unclear circumstances of the infant's death. Emergency services responded to a 112 call shortly after noon on Saturday, July 25, but the baby was already deceased when the attending doctor arrived at the scene.

The infant was discovered at a residence on the Keibergstraat, a residential street in the municipality of Ichtegem, approximately 20 kilometers southwest of Bruges. According to [Het Laatste Nieuws](https://www.hln.be/binnenland/baby-van-1-5-maand-oud-overleden-in-villa-in-ichtegem-parket-start-onderzoek-naar-onduidelijk-overlijden~a021fb42/), the emergency call originated from the grandmother's home, where the baby and both parents had been living. A MUG (Mobile Emergency Group) helicopter was dispatched alongside an ambulance, but resuscitation efforts were not possible as the infant had already passed away.

## Investigation Classified as Unclear Death

The West Flanders public prosecutor's office has classified the death as an "onduidelijk overlijden" (unclear death), a standard legal designation in Belgium used when the cause of death cannot be immediately determined. Prosecutor spokesperson Tobias Valcke confirmed that a forensic doctor and a CSI team were dispatched to the scene to investigate.

"Emergency services were called on Saturday morning for the death of a 1.5-month-old baby in Ichtegem," Valcke told [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/07/25/onderzoek-naar-overleden-baby-ichtegem/). "The lab and a forensic doctor were appointed to investigate the circumstances. They must determine the circumstances in which the baby died."

Both parents were taken in for questioning, which is standard protocol in infant death investigations. Valcke emphasized that no further details would be communicated at this stage of the inquiry.

## Witness Account of Handcuffs

Neighbors reported hearing a loud scream coming from the villa shortly before emergency services arrived, suggesting a moment of discovery or distress. In a detail that may indicate a more serious turn in the investigation, VRT NWS quoted a witness who claimed that one person was taken away from the scene in handcuffs.

The prosecutor's office has neither confirmed nor denied this account. According to the prosecutor, "alle pistes liggen nog open" — all possibilities remain under investigation.

Regional broadcaster [Focus-WTV](https://focus-wtv.be/nieuws/baby-van-anderhalve-maand-overleden-in-ichtegem-parket-onderzoekt-omstandigheden) reported that no outward signs of violence were visible on the infant's body at the time of discovery. The body has been sent for a full forensic autopsy, with results pending.

## Belgian Infant Death Protocol

In Belgium, the classification of "onduidelijk overlijden" serves as a neutral procedural step. It neither confirms nor rules out criminal involvement, but rather allows forensic investigators to gather evidence before reaching a conclusion. This designation is distinct from a "natuurlijk overlijden" (natural death) or a "verdacht overlijden" (suspicious death), though an unclear death can escalate to a criminal investigation if evidence of foul play emerges.

If the autopsy reveals natural causes such as Sudden Infant Death Syndrome (SIDS) or Sudden Unexpected Death in Infancy (SUDI), the case is likely to be closed. If signs of trauma, neglect, or other suspicious findings emerge, the investigation could escalate into a formal criminal inquiry with potential charges.

The family had been living in a multi-generational household with the baby's grandmother, who also resides at the Keibergstraat property. The presence of multiple adult caregivers in the home adds layers of complexity to the investigation, as authorities must account for all individuals who had access to the infant in the hours leading up to the death.

## What's Next in the Investigation

The case now hinges on the autopsy results, which will determine the direction and severity of the investigation. Authorities have not disclosed a timeline for when those results will be available, but forensic analyses of this nature typically require several days to complete.

The tragedy has drawn significant media attention across Flanders, ranking among the most-read stories on HLN on the day of the incident. For the community of Ichtegem and the broader West Flanders region, the death of such a young child has raised profound questions that only the ongoing forensic work can answer.

As the investigation continues, authorities have urged patience and declined to speculate on potential causes. The prosecutor's office has indicated that further information will be released to the public only when the investigation permits, balancing the need for transparency with the integrity of the forensic process.

The case serves as a somber reminder of the vulnerability of infants and the meticulous investigative work required when a young life ends under uncertain circumstances.
