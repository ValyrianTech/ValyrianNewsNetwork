# Madison Cancels Signature Events After Fatal Police Shooting

MADISON, Wis. — A cascade of event cancellations and disruptions has swept through Wisconsin's capital city after a police officer fatally shot 38-year-old Corey Ruiz on July 22, forcing Madison to cancel or curtail several of its signature summer gatherings as protests continue to roil the community.

The Wisconsin Chamber Orchestra concert, the Madison Public Market grand opening, AtwoodFest, and the Dane County Farmers Market have all been suspended or shuttered early, marking an extraordinary response to what is the third police killing in Madison's near-east side neighborhood since 2012, according to the [Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/madison-police-shooting-farmers-market-d7372475c1823a5b10580c6481bbfbe7).

## The Shooting

Police responded to reports of someone attempting to enter parked cars in the city's Marquette neighborhood on Wednesday afternoon, according to [AP News](https://apnews.com/article/madison-wisconsin-police-shooting-7755ab2ec68bda1a7d7890394f4b1267). Ruiz, who was riding a bike, fled when officers arrived. During a struggle with four officers, police say Ruiz pulled a knife and injured one officer. An officer deployed a taser unsuccessfully, and the same injured officer then fired his weapon, striking Ruiz multiple times including in the head.

Cellphone video of the shooting circulated widely on social media. No body-worn camera footage exists because Madison, unlike nearly every other similarly sized city in the United States, does not require its officers to wear body cameras.

Ruiz is survived by his 13-year-old daughter, Kah'liyah Aliomar, who described learning of her father's death in an emotional news conference. "My heart was broken and hurt so much knowing I can't see my father anymore," she said, as reported by [AP News](https://apnews.com/article/corey-ruiz-death-madison-wisconsin-police-7ea2c187227243cb1c7988514d1091ac).

## Events Suspended or Disrupted

Protesters took to the streets hours after the shooting, disrupting a popular Wisconsin Chamber Orchestra outdoor concert at the state Capitol that attracts approximately 30,000 attendees each week. Concert organizers subsequently canceled the event.

On Thursday, threats of a protest led the city to postpone the grand opening of the Madison Public Market, located just blocks from the shooting. AtwoodFest, a popular neighborhood festival scheduled for Saturday, was also canceled.

"Our community and our Marquette neighbors witnessed another horrific officer-involved shooting just blocks from our festival grounds, leaving us in deep pain and justified anger," AtwoodFest organizers wrote in their cancellation announcement, per the Associated Press. "In acknowledgment of what has happened far too often in our community and our country as a whole, this year's AtwoodFest, themed 'The Heartbeat of the Neighborhood,' is canceled."

The Dane County Farmers Market, which draws about 20,000 people weekly around the Capitol building, shut down nearly four hours early on Saturday in solidarity with protesters.

"We mourn with our community," market organizers posted on social media. "And we agree — business cannot proceed as usual."

## Community Response and Protests

Hundreds have marched to the state Capitol demanding accountability. Protesters overtook a police news conference on Thursday, and a vigil was held at the shooting site with flowers and candles. On Sunday, dozens of vehicles joined a march through State Street with chants of "Arrest the cops!"

"Our community has spoken loud and clear: this is NOT business as usual," Black activist Clyde Mayberry posted on Facebook, according to AP. "Your decisions have helped ensure that this city continues to acknowledge the pain, the loss, and the call for accountability."

Civil rights attorney Ben Crump, representing the Ruiz family, called for criminal charges against the officer who fired the fatal shots. "They not only tackled him, not only tased him, not only kicked him, but you saw they shot him three times in his head and other parts of his body," Crump said at a news conference on Friday.

## Body Camera Debate Reignited

The absence of body-worn camera footage has become a central flashpoint in the controversy. Unlike nearly every other city of its size, Madison has not adopted body cameras for its nearly 500 officers, a failure that experts say is startling for a city with a progressive reputation.

According to [AP News](https://apnews.com/article/bodycam-footage-madison-wisconsin-police-shooting-4885071da142ae0b225873dbb36aa10b), a decades-long debate over the cameras has been stymied by budget concerns, political inertia, and opposition from some community groups who feared the cameras would be used to surveil marginalized groups. A 90-day pilot program in summer 2024 showed little impact, though the researcher noted the timeframe was too short to draw meaningful conclusions.

Madison Mayor Satya Rhodes-Conway said she expects the new budget taking effect in January 2027 to include funding for body cameras. Police Chief John Patterson has described the devices as "essential tools for the modern police officer."

## Investigations and What's Next

The Wisconsin Division of Criminal Investigation is conducting an independent probe into the shooting, while the city's independent police monitor has launched a separate investigation. The four responding officers remain on administrative leave, and the officer who fired the fatal shots has not been publicly identified.

The shooting has laid bare deep racial divisions in Madison, a city that is 70% white but has a history of racial inequality. The pattern of police shootings in the same near-east side neighborhood — in 2012, 2015, and now 2026 — has raised systemic questions about policing practices in the area.

As the community continues to demand answers, the Common Council is expected to consider a $414,800 proposal for a three-year body camera rollout. Whether this latest tragedy will finally break the political logjam remains an open question — but for now, Madison's summer has been interrupted by grief, protest, and a determination that business cannot proceed as usual.
