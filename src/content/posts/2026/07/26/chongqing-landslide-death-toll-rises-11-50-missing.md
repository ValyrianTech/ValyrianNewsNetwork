# Chongqing Landslide Death Toll Rises to 11, 50 Still Missing

The death toll from a catastrophic mountain collapse in southwestern China has risen to 11, with 50 people still unaccounted for, as rescue operations transitioned to a deep recovery phase after authorities confirmed no signs of life remained at the site. The disaster, which also left 10 people injured, struck Pengshui Miao and Tujia Autonomous County in Chongqing Municipality on the morning of July 17, according to [Caixin Global](https://www.caixinglobal.com/2026-07-24/china-landslide-death-toll-rises-to-11-with-50-still-missing-102467508.html).

## The Disaster

At approximately 9:08 a.m. local time, an estimated 18,000 cubic meters of rock sheared off a steep mountainside in Hanjia Subdistrict, near the Third Wujiang Bridge. The collapse — classified by geologists as a "large-scale" event — destroyed 13 residential buildings instantly, with debris and building fragments plunging into the Wujiang River below. The largest single rock mass measured roughly 3,000 cubic meters.

Thirteen houses and apartment blocks were struck, with multiple structures collapsing entirely. According to the [Beijing Time](https://item.btime.com/f1s9p4vgr4t8jgr52urm9kugs1t) official statement from the On-Site Rescue Command, the collapse volume was so immense that rescue operations proved exceptionally difficult, requiring directional blasting and heavy machinery just to access the site.

## Eyewitness Accounts and Narrow Escape

Local resident Hou Kaiyun, who has lived in the area for 48 years, became an unlikely hero in the hours before the disaster. As [Phoenix News](https://news.ifeng.com/c/8ur7Bc50n3F) detailed in a comprehensive eyewitness reconstruction, Hou heard rocks falling throughout the night of July 16–17 and could not sleep.

At dawn, Hou and his neighbor Xu Zhaoquan ventured up the mountain to investigate. They discovered rock cracks measuring 10 to 20 centimeters wide. "It didn't fall or roll down — it felt like it burst out under force," Hou recalled.

Hou immediately alerted community group leader He Guangxue at 6:34 a.m. The warning chain escalated rapidly: by 7:48 a.m., community Party Secretary Zhang Weiling had dispatched grid workers to the site. Evacuation calls went out by phone and WeChat between 8:35 and 8:44 a.m.

More than 60 residents were evacuated from the core zone before the collapse, and over 1,100 people were ultimately relocated from the broader area. But not everyone heeded the warnings. "The shop owner said it wasn't that dangerous. Her family of three and two employees were all inside. Now none can be reached," Hou told reporters.

"Those rocks were too huge; the houses were like tofu — it happened in an instant," Hou said, describing the moment the mountainside gave way.

## Rescue and Recovery

China's Ministry of Emergency Management activated a Level-II national emergency response within hours, dispatching Minister Zhang Chengzhong to oversee operations. A total of 872 rescuers from 13 specialized teams were deployed, operating 1,100 pieces of equipment including life detection devices, drones, and search dogs.

Search operations were conducted across three zones: the left and right sides of the collapse center and the river surface. However, the site's treacherous conditions — a narrow workspace, unstable slopes, and massive boulders — forced rescue teams to use controlled blasting to advance. By the night of July 17, a heavy rainstorm had temporarily halted operations, and experts warned that 5,000 to 6,000 cubic meters of loose rock remained perched above the site.

On July 23, the On-Site Rescue Command confirmed that after multiple rounds of life detection, no signs of life had been found, and the mission shifted to body recovery.

## Geological Vulnerability

Pengshui County lies within the Wuling Mountain system and is one of Chongqing's most disaster-prone areas. According to [Caixin Global](https://www.caixinglobal.com/2026-07-20/massive-landslide-in-southwestern-china-leaves-8-dead-34-missing-102466176.html), the county had identified 461 geological hazard sites — including 363 landslides and 69 collapses — threatening approximately 29,000 people and 2.35 billion yuan in property. In 2025 alone, 106 geological hazard incidents occurred.

Strikingly, the collapse site was **not** in the pre-existing hazard register, underscoring the challenge of predicting such events in steep, geologically complex terrain. County officials noted that the site's steep topography made the collapse "highly concealed, sudden, and unpredictable."

## What's Next

As recovery efforts continue, authorities face the immediate challenge of stabilizing the remaining loose rock above the disaster zone, which poses a continued threat under extreme weather conditions. The broader questions — why the site was not in the hazard register, whether faster evacuation could have saved more lives, and what compensation will be provided to the families of the deceased and missing — will likely drive investigations in the weeks ahead.

Pengshui County has announced a comprehensive re-inspection of all geological hazard sites and pledged to strengthen early warning systems, emergency response protocols, and community-based disaster prevention measures. For the families of the 50 still missing, the wait for answers — and for closure — continues.
