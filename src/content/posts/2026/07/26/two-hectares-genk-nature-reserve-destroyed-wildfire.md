# Two Hectares of Genk Nature Reserve Destroyed by Wildfire

A fast-moving wildfire tore through approximately two hectares of heathland in the Schemmersberg nature reserve in Genk, Limburg, on Saturday evening, fire officials have confirmed. The blaze, which [VRT NWS reported](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/07/26/tweetal-hectare-natuurgebied-in-genk-door-brand-vernield/) broke out around 6:00 PM local time, was brought under control within an hour, though it left a significant scar on the protected natural area.

Firefighters from the Genk post of Hulpverleningszone Oost-Limburg responded with three forest fire trucks, supported by an additional unit from Hasselt. Multiple water tankers were deployed to navigate the difficult terrain. By 7:00 PM, the fire was under control, and crews remained on site until approximately 9:30 PM, soaking the burned area to prevent any re-ignition.

No injuries were reported, and no structures were threatened. However, residents of the nearby Winterslag neighborhood experienced smoke and odor nuisance as the fire burned through the dry vegetation.

## A Landscape Under Pressure

The Schemmersberg is a heath and dune area covering approximately 35 hectares on the edge of the Kempens Plateau in Genk. Together with the adjacent Zonhoverheide forest, it forms a 311-hectare natural corridor connecting several protected areas across Limburg. The reserve, managed by the city of Genk through the Heempark organization, is popular with hikers and features sand flats, forests, and reclaimed quarry land.

The fire destroyed roughly 5.7 percent of the reserve's heathland — an ecosystem already under pressure in Flanders. Heathlands provide critical habitat for specialized plant and animal species, though some native species can benefit from periodic fires, as certain seeds require exposure to heat to germinate.

## Drought Fueled Rapid Spread

The wildfire's intensity and speed were exacerbated by a prolonged drought that has gripped Flanders throughout July 2026. According to reports, the fire spread very rapidly due to the parched conditions, though limited wind helped firefighters establish control.

Belgium's Agency for Nature and Forests had already upgraded the fire risk to code orange — "very dangerous" — for all Flemish nature areas on July 13, placing watchtowers on alert and urging the public not to smoke or light fires in natural areas. Three days later, the Drought Commission raised the drought management level to code orange, declaring a "phase of effective water scarcity."

The Genk fire is part of a broader pattern of wildfires striking Flanders during the summer of 2026. Earlier in July, a dune fire in Wenduine destroyed 6,000 square meters of coastal habitat, while a separate fire on the Mechelse Heide in Limburg burned approximately eight hectares. Across Europe, massive forest fires in France and Spain forced 80,000 evacuations and consumed 55,000 hectares that same week.

## Investigation and Forward Look

The cause of the Schemmersberg fire remains unknown and is under investigation. Authorities have not ruled out any possibilities, including accidental human activity, lightning, or arson.

The fire department has reiterated its warning to the public: do not make fires and do not smoke in nature reserves, particularly during the current drought conditions. With code orange fire warnings still in effect and no significant rainfall forecast, the risk of further wildfires across Flanders remains elevated.

The city of Genk and the fire department may release a follow-up report with a full ecological damage assessment and any restoration plans for the affected area. For now, the Schemmersberg reserve — while scarred by this weekend's fire — remains part of a larger natural landscape that continues to offer refuge for the region's wildlife.
