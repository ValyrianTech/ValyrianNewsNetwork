# 11-Year-Old DJ Knox Wows Tomorrowland with Step Stool Set

When an 11-year-old boy needs a step stool just to reach the DJ mixer, you know it is going to be a memorable set. Knox Kind, a young prodigy from Amsterdam, became the youngest DJ of the 2026 edition of Tomorrowland on Saturday, July 25, performing a high-energy set on the Elixir stage at the iconic Belgian festival. With over 360,000 Instagram followers and endorsements from electronic music legends like Tiësto and David Guetta, Knox is proving that age is just a number in the world of dance music.

## A Rising Star from Amsterdam

Knox Kind first caught the attention of the music world in early 2026, when his booking for Tomorrowland was announced. At just 10 years old at the time, he was already building a significant social media presence, sharing videos of his DJ skills with a rapidly growing audience. "That talent comes from myself," he told the press with characteristic confidence.

His rise accelerated when established superstars took notice. World-famous DJs Tiësto and David Guetta both shared Knox's videos with their millions of followers, giving the young talent an audience far beyond his years. By July 2026, his Instagram following had grown to over 363,000, according to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/07/25/11-jarige-dj-knox-kind-jongste-dj-tomorrowland/).

## The Tomorrowland Academy Connection

Knox performed at Tomorrowland as a protégé of the [Tomorrowland Academy](https://tomorrowlandbelgium.press.tomorrowland.com/tomorrowland-launches-the-online-tomorrowland-dj--producing-academy), a DJ and producer school launched by the festival organization. The Academy, with physical locations in Antwerp and Hasselt as well as an online platform launched in February 2024, serves as a talent incubator offering courses for children, youngsters, and adults while scouting promising new artists.

The Academy's mission is to "inspire and empower enthusiastic people from every corner of the world to produce, release, create and perform music." Knox represents one of its most high-profile protégés to date, demonstrating the program's effectiveness at discovering and showcasing young talent on the world's biggest stages.

## A Set to Remember

Performing on the Elixir stage at Tomorrowland's sprawling festival grounds in Boom, Belgium, Knox drew a crowd of curious festival-goers and dedicated fans alike. The stage setup included a practical addition: a step stool placed by organizers so the young DJ could comfortably reach the mixer controls and perform his set without any limitations.

"It was a bit stressful at first, but it went perfectly," Knox told Belgian radio station MNM after his performance, as reported by VRT NWS. He described the experience as a "megavette belevenis" — an extremely cool experience. One surprised festival-goer was overheard saying, "That is just a baby," as they approached the stage.

Photographs from the event show Knox fully immersed in his set, with the step stool a charming but practical detail that endeared him to the crowd. The Elixir stage, known for its intimate atmosphere, proved to be the perfect setting for the young DJ's debut.

## Youngest DJ of This Edition

While VRT NWS correctly identifies Knox as the youngest DJ of the 2026 edition, the all-time record for youngest performer at Tomorrowland belongs to DJ Archie from the United Kingdom, who performed at age nine in 2024. According to [DJ Mag NL](https://djmag.nl/news/de-9-jarige-dj-archie-wordt-de-jongste-dj-die-op-tomorrowland-optreedt), Archie played a drum & bass set on the Atmosphere stage and was officially recognized by Guinness World Records as the world's youngest DJ at just four years and 130 days old. In 2023, 11-year-old DJ Michelle from Dubai also performed at the festival, tying with Knox's age.

This context does not diminish Knox's achievement. Performing at Tomorrowland at any age is a milestone for any DJ, and doing so at 11 places him in an elite group of young artists who have graced the festival's stages.

## A Changing Industry

Knox's rapid ascent exemplifies a broader shift in the electronic music industry. In an era where social media virality can launch careers overnight, young talents are building substantial followings long before they secure traditional festival bookings. His 363,000 Instagram followers and the endorsements from Tiësto and David Guetta created a groundswell that made his Tomorrowland debut a natural next step rather than a mere novelty.

The Tomorrowland Academy's role in his journey also highlights how major festival brands are investing in the next generation. By creating formal pathways for young talent to develop and perform, Tomorrowland is securing its cultural relevance for years to come while giving promising artists like Knox the platform to shine.

## What Lies Ahead

With a triumphant debut at one of the world's most prestigious festivals now behind him, the question is what comes next for Knox Kind. The Netherlands has a strong connection to Tomorrowland, with Dutch DJs like Martin Garrix, Armin van Buuren, and Tiësto being regular headliners. Knox now follows in this national tradition.

If his trajectory so far is any indication — from bedroom DJ sharing videos online to performing on a Tomorrowland stage before his 12th birthday — Knox Kind is a name the electronic music world will be hearing for years to come. For now, he is content to savor what he called a "really cool experience," one that most DJs twice his age can only dream of.
