# Espionage at NATO's Mons HQ Was Only a Matter of Time

A Canadian woman of Chinese descent has been arrested on suspicion of espionage while working as an intern at NATO's Supreme Headquarters Allied Powers Europe (SHAPE) in Mons, Belgium. The arrest, announced on July 25 by the Belgian Federal Prosecutor's Office, came as no surprise to intelligence experts who had long warned that NATO and EU institutions on Belgian soil were prime targets for foreign intelligence services.

The suspect was placed under a detention warrant on charges of espionage on behalf of a third country and participation in a criminal organization. She had recently begun an internship at SHAPE — NATO's principal military headquarters, from which all allied operations worldwide are planned and directed.

## A Predictable Breach

According to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/07/26/spionage-navo-kenneth-lasoen/), the arrest was flagged as predictable by Kenneth Lasoen, an intelligence and counterterrorism expert at the University of Antwerp. "We know that NATO and the European Union are primary targets of our strategic opponents," Lasoen said in an interview on Radio 1. "Because those institutions are located on our territory, it was only a matter of time before something like this would happen with us too."

Lasoen noted that using interns for espionage is a classic infiltration method. Intelligence services often target individuals in seemingly innocuous or low-level roles precisely because they still retain access to sensitive information.

## How the Arrest Unfolded

SHAPE's own security services flagged the intern for suspicious behavior — accessing computer systems and files that were not part of her duties. The matter was immediately reported to Belgium's military intelligence service, the General Intelligence and Security Service (ADIV).

Ann Lukowiak of the Federal Prosecutor's Office explained: "That could range from searches within computer systems or checking things in files — things that are not part of her duties." After searches of the suspect's home and workplace, the investigating judge of the Court of First Instance of Hainaut, Mons division, ordered her detention. The case is being investigated by the Federal Judicial Police of Charleroi.

## Operational Impact Unaffected

Despite the seriousness of the breach, SHAPE spokesperson Col. Martin L. O'Donnell stated that there is no indication NATO or SHAPE operational readiness, command and control arrangements, or ongoing tasks have been adversely affected. "SHAPE continues to fulfill its responsibilities without interruption," he said.

Belgian Minister of Defense Theo Francken expressed satisfaction with the response. "Counterespionage remains a priority task for services such as the VSSE and ADIV," Francken said. "Our country is, after Washington D.C., the most important diplomatic hub in the world. Espionage activities are legion. Staying vigilant is the message."

## The China Question

While the federal prosecutor has not officially confirmed which country the suspect allegedly spied for, multiple signals point toward China. The official communication explicitly mentioned the suspect's Chinese origin — a detail Lasoen described as "perhaps not such a subtle hint."

NATO has designated China a "security challenge" since 2021, and the arrest occurs amid heightened geopolitical tensions. If Chinese involvement is confirmed, Lasoen noted that Chinese spy operatives typically receive rudimentary training in espionage techniques. "She may have been tasked with gathering certain information and simply looking around," he said, potentially as a first step to approach individuals at higher levels within the organization.

## Implications for NATO Security

The incident exposes potential vulnerabilities in NATO's personnel vetting and access control systems. While SHAPE hosts its own Counter Intelligence Unit, which handles information security and personnel safety, the case demonstrates that even temporary personnel — including interns — can access sensitive information.

Rob Van herck, a defense expert with VTM NIEUWS and Het Laatste Nieuws, noted the strategic importance of the site. "It is the beating heart of NATO on a military level. From Mons, all operations worldwide are directed, so there is a huge amount of sensitive information to be obtained."

The detention warrant is scheduled to be confirmed by the Council Chamber in the coming week. The federal prosecutor's office has declined further comments to avoid compromising the ongoing investigation. As the case unfolds, it is likely to prompt renewed scrutiny of security protocols for temporary personnel at NATO facilities and may accelerate the alliance's efforts to counter foreign intelligence threats.

## What to Watch

The coming days will bring several key developments: the Council Chamber's ruling on the detention warrant, potential diplomatic responses from Canada and any implicated foreign power, and possible announcements from NATO regarding security protocol reviews. For intelligence experts like Lasoen, the incident serves as a stark reminder that in the current geopolitical climate, vigilance is not optional — it is essential.
