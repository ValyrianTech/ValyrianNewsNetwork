# Belgium Scorches at 35°C as Wildfire Rages in Tielt-Winge

Belgium is sweltering through its hottest day of the week on Wednesday, with temperatures soaring to 35°C as a prolonged heatwave and severe drought create hazardous conditions across the country. In Tielt-Winge, a wildfire ignited by young people playing with fire in the Kaaskorf nature area rapidly spread through dry vegetation, requiring emergency services to intervene.

## Context: A Nation Under Heat

The July heatwave follows an exceptionally severe heatwave in June, which [VRT NWS reports](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/liveblog/vandaag-wordt-heetste-dag-van-de-week-met-maxima-tot-35-graden-~1784180635133/) was the most extensive ever recorded in Western Europe. The June heatwave lasted 12 days from June 18 to 29, featuring seven tropical days above 30°C and a peak of 35.5°C in Ukkel. It caused 1,747 excess deaths in Belgium, with 641 deaths recorded on June 27 alone — the second deadliest day of the 21st century in the country. In France, 5,764 excess deaths were recorded during the same period.

Flanders has been under code orange drought warning since July 16, officially classified as a “phase of effective water scarcity.” Pumping bans have been expanded across Antwerp province, and water levels in the Maas and Rhine rivers have dropped critically low, affecting shipping and drinking water supplies. Water experts have warned that without better cooperation between European countries, the drought could lead to tensions over shared river water.

## The Tielt-Winge Wildfire

On Tuesday evening in the Kaaskorf neighborhood of Houwaart, Tielt-Winge, a group of young people playing with fire inadvertently sparked a wildfire that spread rapidly through the parched landscape. The fire department of Hulpverleningszone Oost Vlaams-Brabant responded quickly and extinguished the blaze before it could reach any buildings, as [VRT NWS reported](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/07/29/natuurbrand-kaaskorf-tielt-winge/).

“No injuries were reported and no buildings were damaged,” said Acting Mayor Jan Loddewykx (CD&V) in an interview with [HLN](https://www.hln.be/tielt-winge/kijk-jongeren-spelen-met-vuur-tijdens-extreme-droogte-en-veroorzaken-brand-in-natuurgebied-in-tielt-winge-je-ziet-hoe-snel-dit-kan-gaan~a59272dd/). “The damage has fortunately been limited to vegetation. My thanks go out to the fire department, police, and all emergency services who intervened quickly and professionally.”

Mayor Katrien Houtmeyers (N-VA) confirmed there were no indications of malicious intent, telling VRT NWS: “Young people were playing with fire, and it got out of hand. Aanwijzingen voor kwaad opzet zijn er inderdaad niet, maar dat neemt niet weg dat de gevolgen ernstig zijn.” She added: “We must keep repeating this. What may start as an innocent game can have serious consequences in no time.”

The fire department classified the incident as accidental, stating that it was “a combination of playing with fire and extreme drought” and emphasizing that “that does not diminish the seriousness of the consequences.”

## An Escalating Risk

The Tielt-Winge incident serves as a stark warning about fire danger during the ongoing drought. Forest rangers at Kalmthoutse Heide have been maintaining extra vigilance from watchtowers, warning that the dry heathland is highly combustible. “These high temperatures are dangerous because the heathland is very dry,” said forest ranger Lucas Bergmans. “The wind can quickly fan a spark.”

The fire department of Oost Vlaams-Brabant has renewed its call for caution, urging the public not to smoke, barbecue, or light campfires in natural and forested areas. The extreme conditions have also prompted Renewi, a waste management company, to warn about increased fire risk from batteries disposed of in household waste.

## Broader Implications

The combination of extreme heat and drought carries serious consequences beyond wildfire risk. The June heatwave demonstrated the deadly potential of such conditions, with elderly and vulnerable populations particularly at risk. Unusually warm nights prevented buildings from cooling, compounding the health impacts. Record electricity prices exceeded €1 per kWh during evening air conditioning peaks, while train services on non-electrified lines were suspended due to diesel trains’ heat sensitivity.

Climate scientists have attributed the severity of these heatwaves to climate change. Jean-Pascal van Ypersele, a climate scientist at UCLouvain, has publicly expressed his anger at what he describes as two decades of inadequate political action on climate. Political scientist Dave Sinardet (VUB) noted a striking “political silence” surrounding the thousands of heat-related deaths and wildfires devastating European summers, observing that the climate theme has been pushed to the background, particularly among center-right parties.

The water scarcity situation adds another layer of concern. Low river levels are disrupting barge shipping on the Maas and Rhine, driving up transport costs for essential goods. Drinking water companies in several regions of Flanders are operating under heightened vigilance, urging consumers to avoid waste.

## What to Watch For

Temperatures are forecast to remain high, with no significant rainfall expected in the coming days. Meteorologists at the KMI predict that the heat and drought conditions could persist into early August. The drought code orange remains in effect for Flanders, and further water restrictions — including potential bans on non-essential water use such as car washing and pool filling — could be imposed if conditions worsen.

The Tielt-Winge fire, while quickly contained, underscores the heightened risk facing Belgium as heatwaves become more frequent and intense — a trend scientists at World Weather Attribution have directly linked to climate change. With vulnerable populations, strained emergency services, and critical water supplies under pressure, the coming weeks will test the country’s preparedness for a hotter, drier future.
