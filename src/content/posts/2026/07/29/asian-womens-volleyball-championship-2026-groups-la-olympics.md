# Asian Volleyball Groups Set, Champion Earns LA Olympic Berth

The draw for the 2026 Asian Women's Volleyball Championship was held on July 28 in Tianjin, China, with 12 teams divided into three groups for the tournament that will run from August 21 to 30. In a historic first, the champion will earn a direct qualification spot for the 2028 Los Angeles Olympics, while the top three finishers will secure berths at the 2027 FIVB World Championship.

The ceremony, held at Tianjin's Olympic center, was officiated by Olympic gold medalists Zhang Na and Wei Qiuyue, who served as draw masters. According to [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/sports/20260729/a2339121f2d348c49d1a02fb3fefe9fd/c.html), the host nation China was drawn into Group A alongside Chinese Taipei, Iran, and Iraq. Group B features Thailand, Kazakhstan, Indonesia, and Australia, while Group C brings together Japan, Vietnam, South Korea, and Hong Kong China.

## Tournament Format and Stakes

Under the championship format, each group will play a single round-robin. The top two teams from each group, along with the two best third-placed finishers, will advance to the knockout quarterfinals. The tournament's unique dual-qualification structure—offering both Olympic and World Championship berths—has elevated the stakes dramatically.

Asian Volleyball Confederation (AVC) President Ramón Suzara described the championship as a watershed moment for the sport in the region. "This edition of the Asian Championship is a landmark event in the development of Asian volleyball," Suzara said during the ceremony. "The addition of both Olympic and World Championship qualification will make every set, every point fiercely contested. I look forward to seeing the teams showcase the highest level of women's volleyball in Tianjin, a city with a rich volleyball tradition."

## China's Path and Recent Form

For China, the tournament represents a critical juncture. The Chinese women's team finished fourth at the 2026 FIVB Volleyball Nations League finals on July 26, falling to Turkey in the semifinals and Italy in the bronze medal match. According to [Qilu Evening News](https://news.e23.cn/tiyu/2026-07-27/2026072700066.html), head coach Zhao Yong faces a crucial test as the team aims to secure Olympic qualification on home soil.

The report highlights that China's performance in the VNL revealed both strengths and vulnerabilities. The absence of star attackers Li Yingying and Wu Mengjie due to injury was keenly felt, and questions remain about the team's setters and attacking depth. With the Asian Championship now less than a month away, the coaching staff must finalize the 14-player roster and address tactical shortcomings exposed during the VNL.

Japan is widely seen as China's primary rival for the title, with Thailand and South Korea also capable of causing upsets.

## Preparations Underway in Tianjin

Tianjin, a city with a strong volleyball culture, has been preparing to host the championship since April. Local organizers have been coordinating with the FIVB and AVC, hosting venue inspection teams and studying best practices from cities that have hosted VNL events.

According to the [Workers' Daily](https://www.workercn.cn/c/2026-07-29/8857891.shtml), Tianjin Municipal Volleyball Management Center Director Zhang Fan confirmed that ticketing plans are being finalized and will be announced shortly following the draw ceremony. "All aspects of preparation—security, competition organization, transportation, catering, information technology, and fan services—are progressing steadily," Zhang said. "We aim to provide the best possible conditions for all participating teams and create a vibrant atmosphere for fans."

## Looking Ahead

The championship marks a new chapter in Asian volleyball, where Olympic qualification is now directly tied to continental performance. For teams like China, Japan, and Thailand, the path to Los Angeles 2028 begins in Tianjin this August.

As the August 21 opening date approaches, attention will turn to squad announcements and final preparations. The tournament not only determines who represents Asia on the world's biggest stage but also signals the shifting balance of power in women's volleyball across the continent.
