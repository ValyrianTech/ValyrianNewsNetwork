# China Honors Software Pioneer Wang Ji as 'Era Model'

On July 29, 2026, China's Central Propaganda Department officially awarded Wang Ji, a researcher at the National University of Defense Technology (NUDT), the prestigious "Era Model" (时代楷模) title in a formal ceremony in Beijing. The honor recognizes his pioneering contributions to high-trust software technology and his extraordinary personal journey of resilience after surviving a catastrophic illness that led to bilateral above-knee amputation, as reported by [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/politics/20260729/9256ffdf24e348e282c7ec50de85e5a3/c.html).

## What Is the Era Model Title?

The "Era Model" (时代楷模) is one of China's highest civilian honors, established by the Central Propaganda Department to recognize exemplary citizens whose deeds embody the core socialist values of the new era. Past recipients have included Zhang Guimei, who founded a free girls' high school in Yunnan; Huang Wenxiu, a young Party official who died in a flash flood while working on poverty alleviation; and Shan Xinghua, the lead developer of China's 12306 railway ticketing system. The award typically triggers a nationwide publicity campaign, with the recipient's story promoted across state media as a model for emulation.

## The Man Behind the Award

Wang Ji, 56, was born in Shanghai in November 1969 and moved to Changde, Hunan as a toddler when his parents relocated for the Third Front construction initiative. A mathematics prodigy, he was admitted to NUDT's Computer Science department at just 13 or 14 years of age in 1983 — the same year China's first billion-times supercomputer, the Galaxy-I, was successfully developed at the same institution.

He went on to study under Academician Chen Huowang, the chief software architect of the Galaxy-I, earning his bachelor's, master's, and doctoral degrees. By 33, Wang had been promoted to full professor; by 38, he had received the National Outstanding Youth Science Fund. According to his [in-depth profile by Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/politics/20260728/e16f5c61ac1240a29d9faf290d5006d4/c.html), Wang Ji is described as "the main pioneer in China's high-trust software field."

## Pioneering High-Trust Software

High-trust software (高可信软件) uses mathematical methods — known as formal methods — to verify software correctness from its underlying logic. As Wang Ji's colleague Dong Wei, a professor at NUDT, explained: "High-trust software is the 'doctor of software.' By performing 'full-body CT scans' and precise diagnosis on the software systems of major national equipment, it finds deeply hidden vulnerabilities and risks."

In 2003, Wang Ji and his mentor Chen Huowang published "High-Trust Software Engineering Technology," widely considered the first systematic paper in this field in China. It remains highly cited today. Wang Ji also spearheaded the establishment of the China Computer Federation's Formal Methods Technical Committee, growing the national research community from just a few dozen in 2015 to over 1,000 today.

His work has direct applications in aerospace, military equipment, autonomous systems, and AI reliability. As [China News Service](https://www.chinanews.com.cn/sh/2026/07-29/10668406.shtml) reported, Wang Ji's research has been applied to "major national equipment" including defense systems, though specific military applications are not disclosed due to security concerns.

## A Life Interrupted

On January 23, 2013, Wang Ji suffered a catastrophic aortic dissection — a life-threatening cardiovascular emergency with a mortality rate of up to 20 percent. He underwent 13 hours of emergency surgery, followed by multiple complications that required bilateral above-knee amputation. Over the next six months, he endured more than 10 surgeries — including seven deep debridement procedures — and spent 147 days in intensive care.

In 2021, he was diagnosed with kidney failure, and he has since undergone over 700 dialysis sessions. As he put it in a remarkably understated analogy: "My body crashed, but I just needed to reboot."

## Triumph Over Adversity

Despite losing both legs, Wang Ji returned to work at NUDT in 2015 using a wheelchair. He went on to serve as the Chief Technical Expert for a National Defense 973 Project — a major national research initiative. After six years of relentless effort, his team developed a comprehensive "toolbox" for software reliability, and the project passed evaluation in 2021. In 2025, this work earned him the First Prize in Military Science and Technology Progress.

His wife, Huang Chun, is the lead designer of the compiler team for the "Tianhe" (天河) supercomputer. Their family was rated as a National Civilized Family. Wang Ji continues to travel for work — he has developed his own method for transferring in and out of vehicles, which he calls the "vaulting horse" technique — and he continues to supervise graduate students using the "whiteboard teaching method" he inherited from his own mentor.

## Broader Significance

The timing of the award is notable. With the 100th anniversary of the People's Liberation Army's founding approaching in 2027, Wang Ji's story reinforces key Party messaging around military modernization, defense technology self-reliance, and the integration of civilian and military innovation. The Central Propaganda Department's official decision stated that Wang Ji "fully demonstrates the political character and glorious tradition of the people's army in firmly upholding the core and resolutely following the Party's command."

Beyond the political framing, Wang Ji's story carries a universal human dimension. As he told Xinhua: "I'm very lucky, my brain and hands are fine, my basic working conditions haven't been lost. I think everything can be managed." His team has now turned its attention to the trustworthiness of artificial intelligence — the next frontier for high-trust software.

## What to Watch

Wang Ji continues to lead research into AI reliability, a field where China is racing to establish technical leadership. The formal methods community he helped build now numbers over 1,000 researchers nationwide. As China pushes forward with its "15th Five-Year Plan" period — described as a critical window for building a technology powerhouse — Wang Ji and his team are positioned at the intersection of software security, military modernization, and AI safety.

The "Era Model" award ensures that Wang Ji's story — of unbreakable spirit, technical brilliance, and unwavering dedication — will be told to millions across China, serving as both inspiration and a symbol of the country's ambitions in science and technology.
