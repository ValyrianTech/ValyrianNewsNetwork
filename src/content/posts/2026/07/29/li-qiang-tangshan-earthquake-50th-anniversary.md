# Li Qiang Commemorates 50 Years Since Tangshan Earthquake

On the 50th anniversary of the Great Tangshan Earthquake, Chinese Premier Li Qiang traveled to Tangshan, Hebei Province, on July 28, 2026, to pay tribute to the victims and inspect disaster preparedness efforts. Entrusted by President Xi Jinping, Li Qiang laid wreaths at the Tangshan Earthquake Ruins Memorial Park, visited paraplegic survivors at a rehabilitation center, and inspected a rescue base, underscoring the government's commitment to disaster resilience.

## The 1976 Catastrophe

The Great Tangshan Earthquake struck in the early morning of July 28, 1976, with a magnitude of 7.8 and a shallow depth of approximately 12 kilometers. The industrial city of roughly one million people was nearly razed, with over 85% of buildings destroyed. The official death toll stood at 242,769, with more than 164,000 severely injured, making it the deadliest earthquake of the 20th century and one of the most devastating natural disasters in recorded history.

Tangshan's reconstruction over the following decades became a symbol of resilience under the leadership of the Chinese Communist Party. Previous milestone anniversaries — the 20th, 30th, and 40th — were all marked by visits from senior Chinese leaders.

## Commemoration and Condolences

At the Tangshan Earthquake Ruins Memorial Park, [Li Qiang](http://politics.people.com.cn/n1/2026/0729/c1024-40769758.html) laid a wreath on behalf of the CPC Central Committee and the State Council and bowed three times before the memorial wall. “The disaster 50 years ago is unforgettable; the heroic and moving scenes of earthquake relief at that time are deeply touching,” Li said, as reported by People’s Daily. “Over the past half century, the people of Tangshan have rebuilt a modern, emerging city from the ruins — this is a vivid example of our Party leading the people in overcoming various difficulties and challenges.”

The premier then visited the Tangshan Paraplegic Rehabilitation Center, the same facility President Xi Jinping visited during the 40th anniversary in 2016. Speaking with survivors, Li conveyed Xi’s personal concern: “General Secretary Xi Jinping is very concerned about all of you, always thinking about your health and living conditions. This time, he specially entrusted me to come and see you.”

According to [Xinhua News Agency](https://www.news.cn/politics/leaders/20260728/26e14dc2f2c44eaabca4f4f26a763482/c.html), Li Qiang also inspected a rescue base operated by the China Aneng Group First Engineering Bureau’s Tangshan branch, where he reviewed emergency response equipment and spoke with rescue personnel about disaster preparedness.

## Disaster Preparedness and Policy Integration

Li Qiang emphasized that “disaster prevention, mitigation, and relief work concerns the safety of the people and national security.” He called for regularizing practical training and joint exercises, and comprehensively improving professional capabilities for rapid response, scientific rescue, and efficient handling.

Noting that China is in the critical flood season — the period from late July to early August when rainfall peaks — Li stressed the need for comprehensive preparedness, including monitoring, early warning systems, and adequate material reserves.

The visit also carried broader policy significance. Li Qiang linked disaster resilience to the “15th Five-Year Plan” (2026–2030) and the Beijing-Tianjin-Hebei coordinated development strategy, framing Tangshan’s recovery within national development objectives.

## Leadership Dynamics

The fact that Li Qiang made the 50th anniversary visit rather than Xi Jinping — who attended the 40th anniversary in person — drew attention from regional observers. [Lianhe Zaobao of Singapore](https://www.zaobao.com.sg/news/china/story20260729-9435892) noted this departure from the pattern of senior-most leadership attendance, while also reporting a three-day drone flight ban over Tangshan during the commemorations.

Senior State Council official Wu Zhenglong accompanied the premier on the visit.

## Looking Ahead

As Tangshan enters its sixth decade of post-earthquake development, the city’s transformation from rubble to a modern industrial center continues to serve as a powerful narrative within China’s broader story of national resilience. With the “15th Five-Year Plan” now underway and the Beijing-Tianjin-Hebei integration strategy advancing, Tangshan’s future development — and its disaster preparedness infrastructure — will remain areas of focus for policymakers and observers alike.
