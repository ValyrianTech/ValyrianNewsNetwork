# Belgium Daily Roundup: Economy Stalls, NATO Spy Held

Belgium faced a turbulent Wednesday, July 29, 2026, as the economy recorded its first zero-growth quarter since the COVID crisis, a Canadian intern at NATO's SHAPE headquarters was ordered held on espionage charges, and a brutal heatwave pushed temperatures to a record 34.4°C in Brussels. From government waste and healthcare funding delays to a historic WWII deportation lawsuit, the day's events painted a picture of a nation grappling with multiple simultaneous challenges.

## Economy: First Zero Growth Since COVID

The Belgian economy recorded 0% growth in the second quarter of 2026 compared to the first quarter, according to a flash estimate from the [National Bank of Belgium](https://www.hln.be/economie/belgische-economie-valt-stil-in-tweede-kwartaal-eerste-nulgroei-sinds-covidcrisis~ab8938f3/). This marks the first stagnation since Q2 2020 during the COVID crisis. Year-over-year growth stood at just 0.5%. Industry contracted by 0.8% and construction by 0.5%, while services eked out a modest 0.2% gain. The National Bank had previously warned of stagnation, and economists now watch closely for signs of a potential recession.

## Security: Canadian Intern Held at SHAPE for Espionage

A Canadian national of Chinese origin, serving as an intern at NATO's Strategic Headquarters Allied Powers Europe (SHAPE) in Mons, remains in pretrial detention after the Chamber of Council ruled on July 28 to maintain her arrest. According to [RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/la-stagiaire-canadienne-du-shape-maintenue-en-detention-preventive-pour-espionnage-11763770), she is suspected of espionage for a third country and participation in a criminal organization. SHAPE's own security services detected the alleged activities and alerted Belgian intelligence, triggering an operation on July 23 by federal police and the Computer Crime Unit. The case has drawn international attention from outlets including the New York Times, Euronews, and Stars and Stripes, raising questions about NATO's vetting procedures.

## Weather: Record Heat, Wildfires, and Worsening Drought

Belgium sweltered through its hottest day of the week as Ukkel recorded a new daily temperature record of 34.4°C, breaking the previous record of 33.5°C set in 1911, according to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/liveblog/vandaag-wordt-heetste-dag-van-de-week-met-maxima-tot-35-graden-~1784180635133/). The extreme heat fueled multiple fire incidents: a youth wildfire in Tielt-Winge, a cigarette-butt fire in Brakel, and a 4,000-square-meter forest fire in Putte-Kapellen's Mastenbos.

The broader drought crisis intensified across Europe. The Rhine River at Kaub reached historic lows threatening shipping, Hungary shut down a nuclear reactor at Paks due to low Danube water levels, and Flanders remained under code orange for effective water scarcity. Agricultural losses mounted, with 50% fewer plums in Haspengouw. Belgium recorded 651 deaths on June 27 alone — the second deadliest day of the 21st century — and more than 2,000 excess deaths overall from the summer heatwaves. Climate scientist Jean-Pascal van Ypersele expressed fury at political inaction, while analyst Dave Sinardet noted that climate remains a low priority for center-right parties.

## Government Waste: 52 Empty Buildings Costing Millions

An investigation by [HLN](https://www.hln.be/binnenland/52-gebouwen-van-de-overheid-staan-al-langer-dan-jaar-leeg-en-dat-kost-belastingbetaler-miljoenen~a136943f/) revealed that 52 government buildings have been fully vacant for more than a year, representing 245,000 square meters — the equivalent of 34 football fields. The total government property portfolio spans 2,317 buildings on 867 sites. Utility and security costs for the empty buildings have doubled from €408,000 in 2023 to €800,000 in 2025, while property taxes add another €1.25 million annually. MP Britt Huybrechts estimates the annual rental cost of unused space at €27 million. Minister Vanessa Matz has initiated a consolidation plan expected to save €7.9 million per year.

## WWII History: Foundation Sues NMBS for Deportations

The Stichting Nico Gunzburg foundation has filed a lawsuit against Belgian railway company NMBS and the Belgian State for their role in World War II deportations, as reported by [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/07/29/schadevergoeding-nmbs-deportaties-wereldoorlog-ii/). Between 1942 and 1944, 28 convoys deported 25,490 Jews and 353 Roma from Mechelen to Auschwitz; only 1,195 survived. NMBS received 51 million Belgian francs — over €15 million today — from the Nazi regime for the transports. The foundation is seeking €1 in provisional damages, with the potential for millions more. Attorney Arthur Flieger accused the railway of delay tactics, stating: "They want to wait until the victims and survivors are no longer living." NMBS promised an apology in May 2025 but has yet to deliver. The Dutch Railways have already provided compensation for their wartime role.

## What's Next

As July draws to a close, all eyes will be on Q3 economic data to determine whether Belgium enters a technical recession. The SHAPE espionage case will proceed through the courts with significant geopolitical implications for NATO. Meanwhile, the heatwave and drought show no signs of abating, with the KMI predicting continued high temperatures into early August. The NMBS deportation lawsuit could set a precedent for other European railways confronting their wartime histories.
