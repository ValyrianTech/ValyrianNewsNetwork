# China Warns Taiwan Separatism Has 'No Survival Space' After Thailand Backs One-China Policy

China's Taiwan Affairs Office delivered a forceful warning on Wednesday, declaring that "Taiwan independence separatism has no survival space and is increasingly desperate," as Beijing rebuffed a protest from Taipei over Thailand's reaffirmation of the One-China principle.

At a regular press conference on July 29, spokesperson Chen Binhua responded to a reporter's question regarding Thailand's official position on the Taiwan issue following Prime Minister Anutin Chanwirakun's visit to China. According to [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/tw/20260729/e864692175474429beb2120528dcbf59/c.html), Chen expressed "high appreciation" for Thailand's stance and framed the One-China principle as a "universal consensus of the international community and a basic norm of international relations."

## Thailand's Strategic Reaffirmation

The press conference was prompted by developments during Prime Minister Anutin's official visit to China from July 16 to 20 — his first state visit since being re-elected in March 2026. During the visit, China and Thailand issued a joint statement in which Bangkok reaffirmed its support for China's peaceful reunification, explicitly opposed any form of "Taiwan independence," and expressed support for the "One Country, Two Systems" framework.

Anutin, who is of Chinese descent from Guangdong and has publicly identified as a "100% Chinese descendant," met with President Xi Jinping in Shanghai on July 17 and Premier Li Qiang in Beijing on July 20. His re-election in March made him Thailand's first prime minister in two decades to serve a full term and win a second term, providing continuity in Thailand's foreign policy orientation.

## Taipei's Protest

Taiwan's foreign affairs department responded to the joint statement by alleging that "China uses every opportunity in international engagement to diminish Taiwan's status." Taipei also expressed "regret" that "certain countries follow China" on the Taiwan issue, according to the Xinhua report.

The protest underscores the persistent diplomatic challenges Taiwan faces as it seeks to maintain international recognition and bilateral relationships, particularly as a growing number of nations align their Taiwan policies with Beijing's One-China principle.

## Beijing's Rebuttal

Addressing Taipei's protest directly, Chen Binhua stated that "facts have repeatedly proven that the One-China principle is a universal consensus of the international community and a basic norm of international relations." He characterized the principle as representing "the trend of the times, the cause of righteousness, and the will of the people."

The spokesperson concluded with a stark warning: "Taiwan independence separatism has no survival space and is increasingly desperate" — language that signals Beijing's uncompromising posture on cross-strait issues.

## Broader Implications

This exchange is part of a recurring pattern in which China leverages bilateral engagements with other nations to secure public reaffirmations of the One-China policy, gradually narrowing Taiwan's diplomatic space on the international stage. For Thailand, a Southeast Asian nation with deep economic ties to China, the reaffirmation reflects a pragmatic diplomatic calculus balancing relationships with both Beijing and Washington.

Analysts note that the Taiwan Affairs Office's use of the term "desperate" (穷途末路) represents an escalation in rhetoric, suggesting Beijing perceives Taiwan independence movements as weakening but still requiring forceful condemnation. The strong wording also serves a dual purpose: thanking Thailand for its position while issuing a warning to Taiwan independence advocates.

## What to Watch

Observers will be watching for any further diplomatic fallout between Thailand and Taiwan, as well as Taipei's response to what it views as China's ongoing efforts to curtail its international engagement. The incident also raises questions about how other Southeast Asian nations will navigate the growing pressure to take public positions on the Taiwan issue as China continues to press for diplomatic alignment across the region.
