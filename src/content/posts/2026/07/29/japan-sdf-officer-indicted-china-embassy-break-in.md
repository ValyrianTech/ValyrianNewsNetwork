# Japan SDF Officer Indicted Over China Embassy Break-In

Japanese prosecutors on Monday indicted Kodai Murata, an active-duty officer in the Japan Ground Self-Defense Force (JGSDF), on charges including trespassing, weapons violations, and criminal intimidation for an armed break-in at the Chinese Embassy in Tokyo in March. The indictment, handed down by the Tokyo District Public Prosecutors Office, adds an intimidation charge that acknowledges the threat posed to diplomatic personnel, according to [Caixin Global](https://www.caixinglobal.com/2026-07-28/japanese-military-officer-indicted-over-armed-break-in-at-chinese-embassy-102468677.html).

## The Incident

On March 24, 2026, Murata, then 23, scaled a barbed-wire wall from the fourth floor of an adjacent building to enter the embassy compound in Tokyo's Minato ward, sustaining cuts on his hands. He was carrying an 18-centimeter kitchen knife and his Self-Defense Force identification in his backpack. Once inside, he called out to staff and demanded to see the ambassador, according to [Mainichi Shimbun](https://mainichi.jp/articles/20260727/k00/00m/040/210000c). Embassy staff subdued him before police arrived.

Murata told investigators that he "heard God say in a dream" that China's tough rhetoric against Japan needed to be stopped. He told embassy staff: "Patriots, on behalf of Japan's gods, will execute you," according to prosecutors. He also threatened to commit suicide if the ambassador refused to hear his protest.

## The Suspect

Murata is a third lieutenant assigned to the Ebino Garrison in Miyazaki Prefecture. He graduated from Sophia University with a degree in Philosophy in March 2025 before entering the JGSDF Officer Candidate School and graduating in January 2026. He was promoted to third lieutenant just nine days before the incident, on March 15.

A three-month psychiatric evaluation confirmed that Murata is mentally fit to stand trial, clearing the way for the indictment on charges of trespassing, violating the Firearm and Sword Control Law, and criminal intimidation. He was initially arrested on trespassing charges on the day of the incident and re-arrested in April on weapons charges.

## Diplomatic Backdrop

The embassy intrusion occurred against the backdrop of a severe diplomatic crisis between China and Japan that began in November 2025. The crisis erupted after Japanese Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi stated in parliament that a Chinese attack on Taiwan could constitute an "existential crisis" for Japan under the 2015 Legislation for Peace and Security, potentially allowing Japan to take military action in collective self-defense.

China reacted forcefully. The Chinese Consul-General in Osaka posted a violent threat against Takaichi on social media, which was later deleted. Beijing subsequently issued travel advisories, restricted cultural exchanges, cut off Japanese seafood imports, and later restricted exports of dual-use items and rare earth materials to Japan.

As reported by [Xinhua News Agency](https://english.news.cn/20260727/aa5685beb5434028a222a38b40a1c2f2/c.html), the indictment comes after months of diplomatic tensions that have seen both countries exchange letters at the United Nations and conduct military posturing, including Chinese live-fire exercises in the Yellow Sea.

## China's Response

Chinese Foreign Ministry spokesperson Lin Jian welcomed the indictment but urged Japan to go further. On July 28, Lin called on Japan to "have a thorough probe into, do soul-searching on, rectify its wrongdoing" regarding the incident, according to [People's Daily](https://en.people.cn/n3/2026/0728/c90000-20482723.html). He demanded that Japan provide "a responsible explanation to China and the international community."

The Chinese Embassy in Japan has reported receiving multiple threats during the same period, including a handwritten letter on March 5 claiming to be from a "military elite force" that used a derogatory term for Chinese people and threatened to "eliminate all Chinese people in Japan without leaving any survivors." Embassy officials say they have negotiated with Japanese police nearly 30 times over the series of threats but have seen little progress in investigations.

## What's Next

Murata now awaits trial, with no date yet set. Japanese officials, including Chief Cabinet Secretary Minoru Kihara and Defense Minister Shinjiro Koizumi, have expressed regret over the incident and promised strengthened security at the Chinese Embassy. The JGSDF Chief of Staff has also promised disciplinary action.

The case continues to strain bilateral relations already at their lowest point in decades. Observers will be watching to see whether the legal proceedings — and Japan's handling of broader security concerns at the embassy — can help de-escalate tensions or whether the incident becomes another flashpoint in the deteriorating relationship between Asia's two largest economies.
