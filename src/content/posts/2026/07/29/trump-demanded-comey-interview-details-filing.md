# Trump Demanded Comey Interview Details, New Filing Alleges

A new court filing by former FBI Director James Comey's legal team alleges that President Donald Trump personally demanded detailed, regular updates from the Secret Service investigation into Comey over a May 2025 Instagram post that prosecutors have characterized as a threat. The filing, submitted late Tuesday in federal court in North Carolina, accuses the Trump administration of conducting warrantless surveillance and pursuing a politically motivated prosecution against a longtime presidential critic.

According to [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/jul/29/james-comey-secret-service-seashells-investigation-trump), Comey's lawyers argue the case should be dismissed on grounds of vindictive and selective prosecution, alleging that the government engaged in a "yearlong campaign by the President to use the criminal process to punish Mr. Comey for his protected speech."

## Background: A Decade-Long Feud

The conflict between Trump and Comey stretches back nearly a decade. Trump fired Comey as FBI director in May 2017 amid the investigation into Russian interference in the 2016 election. Since then, Comey has emerged as one of Trump's most prominent critics, while Trump has repeatedly branded Comey a "dirty cop" and mused about prosecuting him.

The immediate controversy began in May 2025, when Comey posted a photo on Instagram of seashells arranged on a North Carolina beach to spell "86 47." The number "86" is restaurant slang for getting rid of something, and Trump is the 47th president. Comey later deleted the post, saying he had found the shells while on vacation and opposed violence of any kind. [CNBC reported](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/29/james-comey-trump-8647-seashells-threat-secret-service.html) that the Secret Service launched an investigation, and Comey voluntarily agreed to an interview with investigators.

## Key Allegations in the New Filing

The latest filing—accompanied by a sworn affidavit from Comey's attorney, former U.S. Attorney Patrick Fitzgerald—contains several explosive allegations about the government's conduct:

**Presidential involvement**: The filing claims that Secret Service agents sent frequent updates to Air Force One in response to direct requests from Trump, who wanted to be briefed before speaking to the press about the Comey investigation.

**Warrantless surveillance**: According to [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/james-comey-claims-doj-conducted-illegal-surveillance-new-motion-to-dismissal-trump-threat-charges/), Comey's attorneys allege that the Secret Service "knowingly conducted illegal electronic surveillance" of Comey's phone at the request of a high-level Department of Justice official, tracking him and his wife as they drove from North Carolina to Virginia—including a stop at their deceased son's grave.

**Internal resistance**: An unnamed Secret Service supervisor described the surveillance as "legally questionable" and "a bad idea," writing that "we did not believe that anyone's life was in immediate danger." Despite these concerns, the agency proceeded with the tracking.

**Extraordinary investigative measures**: The filing details how law enforcement struggled to establish that "86" was a term implying violence. The FBI interviewed Salvatore "Sammy the Bull" Gravano, a former mafia hitman, about the meaning of "86." Agents searched records for the Gambino case—which Comey handled as a federal prosecutor—for references to "86" and found none. Investigators even turned to ChatGPT and Google AI to search for meanings, with inconclusive results.

**A stalled case revived**: According to The Guardian, Comey's lawyers wrote that the investigation "appeared to be going nowhere" until Acting Attorney General Todd Blanche and U.S. Attorney Ellis Boyle revived it. A previous indictment against Comey for lying to Congress was dismissed in 2025 after a judge ruled that the prosecutor had been unlawfully appointed.

## DOJ Response

The Department of Justice has denied any political motivation. A DOJ spokesperson told multiple news outlets: "A North Carolina grand jury indicted Comey, and he'll have his day in court like any other defendant."

Acting Attorney General Todd Blanche defended the prosecution, telling CBS News: "If anybody in this country thinks that it is okay for anybody to threaten the president of the United States and then have the media or others say, well that's not serious, then we have a bigger problem than I even imagined in this country."

## Analysis and Implications

The filing represents one of the most detailed allegations yet of a sitting president personally directing law enforcement resources against a political adversary. Legal experts note that selective and vindictive prosecution claims face a high bar in court—defendants must show they were singled out for discriminatory reasons while others who engaged in similar conduct were not prosecuted.

However, the extensive evidentiary record attached to the filing, including internal Secret Service notes and communications, may strengthen Comey's case. The allegations also raise broader questions about the independence of the Justice Department and the Secret Service from political influence.

## What's Next

Comey is scheduled to be arraigned on September 30 in New Bern, North Carolina, before U.S. District Judge Louise W. Flanagan. His legal team has also requested a Franks hearing, which would determine whether the government misled the magistrate who approved search warrants in the case. The judge's rulings on these motions could have significant implications for the boundaries of presidential power and the independence of federal law enforcement.
