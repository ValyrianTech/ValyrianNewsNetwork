# Kumamoto Earthquake: 13 Dead, 10,000 Evacuated in Japan

A powerful earthquake with a magnitude of 7.1 struck Japan's Kumamoto Prefecture on July 28, 2026, killing at least 13 people and forcing nearly 10,000 residents to evacuate as rescue operations continue through the night. The quake, which registered the maximum 7 on Japan's shindo seismic intensity scale, triggered building collapses, an explosion at a major shopping complex, and widespread infrastructure damage across the Kyushu region.

According to [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/world/20260729/d4403ebd973047e0a0e77da1dddb3a25/c.html), the shallow strike-slip earthquake struck at approximately 4:27 p.m. local time near the Hinagu Fault Zone, a system that previously caused the devastating 2016 Kumamoto earthquake sequence. Seismologists have warned that further large aftershocks or a second major event remain possible.

## Casualties and Displacement

As of the morning of July 29, Japanese authorities confirmed 13 deaths, with several more cases under investigation regarding their connection to the disaster. At least seven people remain missing, primarily at the Nippon Paper Group Yatsushiro Factory where a chimney collapsed during the quake. Over 9,800 people have sought shelter in evacuation centers across the prefecture.

[The Yomiuri Shimbun](https://www.yomiuri.co.jp/national/20260729-GYT1T00131/) reported that Prime Minister Takaichi Sanae, speaking at the second meeting of the Emergency Disaster Response Headquarters, stated: "There are still people waiting for help. It's a race against time. We will devote all our efforts to saving as many lives as possible."

## Key Incidents

The most dramatic incident occurred at Aeon Mall Kumamoto in Kashima Town, where an explosion at approximately 6:00 p.m. on July 28 caused a partial building collapse. Rescue operations through the night saved eight people, but two women were confirmed dead and one person remains in cardiopulmonary arrest. The facility had been designated as a disaster prevention base under an agreement with local authorities, raising concerns about the vulnerability of such designated shelters.

At the Nippon Paper Group factory in Yatsushiro City, a chimney collapse left two workers in cardiopulmonary arrest and two others seriously injured, with seven still unaccounted for as search operations continue.

Kumamoto Castle, a historic landmark that also sustained damage in the 2016 earthquakes, saw stone walls collapse at 28 locations, with four structures damaged including the Kanmon Yagura watchtower.

## Infrastructure and Economic Disruption

Power outages affected approximately 37,000 households, while water supplies were disrupted across multiple areas. Transportation networks suffered severe damage, with the Kyushu Shinkansen suspended from the first train on July 29 and the Kyushu Expressway closed between Mashiki-Kumamoto Airport Interchange and Ebino Interchange. Mobile phone services remained difficult to use in affected areas.

According to [Nikkei](https://www.nikkei.com/article/DGXZQOSG286TM0Y6A720C2000000/), the economic impact is significant. Toyota suspended operations at three factories in Fukuoka until July 31, while semiconductor production faced disruptions in Kumamoto, a region increasingly vital to global chip manufacturing. Nippon Express and Yamato Transport suspended collection and delivery services in the area, and Seven-Eleven closed approximately 100 stores.

## Government Response

Prime Minister Takaichi announced a "push-type support" strategy, proactively sending food, water, air conditioners, and power sources to affected areas without waiting for formal requests. She emphasized the priority on infrastructure restoration and warned residents about heatstroke risks amid extreme summer temperatures, urging evacuation centers to prepare for the heat.

## Seismological Warnings and Historical Context

Experts have drawn troubling parallels to the 2016 Kumamoto earthquake sequence, which saw two magnitude 7+ earthquakes strike within 28 hours. The 2026 earthquake appears to have occurred on a previously "unruptured segment" of the Hinagu Fault Zone — what Japanese experts call "warenokori" — that did not rupture during the 2016 events.

Dr. Obara Kazushige, Fellow at the National Research Institute for Earth Science and Disaster Resilience, warned: "Similarly to 2016, there is a possibility of many aftershocks, or tremors of the same scale or larger occurring." Professor Kato Aitaro of the University of Tokyo's Earthquake Research Institute added: "This time it occurred further south. Caution is needed as fault rupture could chain further south in the future."

The Japan Meteorological Agency confirmed that dozens of aftershocks of magnitude 3 or higher have been recorded, with Seismologist Shimoto Shinji stating: "There is a possibility of successive earthquakes, so caution is needed."

## International Response

The United Nations expressed readiness to provide support, with the UN Deputy Spokesperson offering condolences during a press conference.

## Looking Ahead

Rescue operations continue as emergency workers search for the missing at the Nippon Paper factory and assess the full extent of damage across the region. The possibility of further major earthquakes on adjacent fault segments remains a concern among seismologists, while authorities race to restore power, water, and transportation links to affected communities. The disaster also raises critical questions about the vulnerability of Kumamoto's growing semiconductor industry, a sector of increasing global strategic importance.
