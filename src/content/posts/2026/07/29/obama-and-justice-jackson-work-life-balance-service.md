# Obama and Justice Jackson on Work-Life Balance and Service

Former First Lady Michelle Obama and Supreme Court Justice Ketanji Brown Jackson sat down for their first extended public conversation, appearing together on the podcast "IMO with Michelle Obama and Craig Robinson" in an episode that deliberately steered clear of politics to focus on work-life balance, motherhood, and the weight of historic public service. Released Wednesday, the episode titled "Move Through the Hard Times with Justice Ketanji Brown Jackson" pairs two of the most visible Black women in American public life in a conversation that is at once deeply personal and broadly resonant, as [The Daily Beast reported](https://www.thedailybeast.com/obsessed/ketanji-brown-jackson-reveals-her-new-startling-reality/).

## Two Historic Figures, One Conversation

Both Obama, 62, and Jackson, 55, are Harvard-trained lawyers who shattered barriers as the first Black women to hold their respective roles — Obama as First Lady from 2009 to 2017, and Jackson as an Associate Justice of the Supreme Court following her appointment by President Joe Biden in 2022. The conversation, recorded in May 2026 in Los Angeles after the Supreme Court's oral arguments concluded, drew on their shared experiences navigating high-profile careers while raising families. As [The Sheffield Press noted](https://thesheffieldpress.com/justice-ketanji-brown-jackson-joins-michelle-obama-on-imo), the pairing "underscored how often Black women at the highest levels of American life are expected to embody resilience while helping institutions look more relatable, more credible and more human."

## Security, Sacrifice, and Service

The discussion ranged from co-parenting while holding demanding jobs to the jarring transition into a life under constant security. Jackson described the adjustment to Supreme Court security as the "most startling" change in her life, telling Obama that she "can't just go out in any meaningful way as I used to."

"And that can be a downside — just not being able to live a normal life," Jackson said, adding, "I know you know what it's like to feel confined."

Obama, who spent eight years in the White House under round-the-clock protection, responded with characteristic warmth: "Girl! I remember this the way you describe, the hardship and the culture."

But Jackson also spoke about the privilege of serving. "It's just extraordinary," she said. "I'm so privileged to do this work to serve the American people in this way." Obama echoed the sentiment, noting the challenge of expressing gratitude: "You never want to come off as not being grateful for the opportunity to serve, especially people like us who were raised to be public servants."

## The Importance of Judicial Independence

While the conversation largely avoided contentious legal issues, Jackson offered a reflection on the role of the judiciary that carried weight given the current political climate.

"We all have to believe that there is a judicial system that is going to be fair, that is going to be neutral — that when you bring your dispute to the court, that judge doesn't have any preexisting stake in the particular issue," Jackson said. "Somebody is going to win, somebody is going to lose, but we have to have that forum so that we can live together peacefully in our society." The full conversation is available on [Spotify](https://open.spotify.com/episode/4GDmCB05ZamtpA58p6J0td).

These remarks come at a time when the Supreme Court faces declining public trust and Jackson has been increasingly outspoken about threats to judicial independence. During the 2025-2026 term, Jackson was one of three liberal justices on the Court's 6-3 conservative majority and authored pointed dissents in several high-profile rulings.

## A Deliberately Personal Format

The podcast, produced by Higher Ground (the Obamas' production company), launched in 2025 and focuses on listener dilemmas and personal stories rather than political commentary. As [Variety reported at the time of its launch](https://variety.com/2025/digital/news/michelle-obama-imo-podcast-craig-robinson-1236331493/), guests have included Issa Rae, Tyler Perry, Serena Williams, Steven Spielberg, Spike Lee, and former President Barack Obama. The show's format — built around candid, advice-oriented conversation — provided a natural setting for Jackson to speak about the young adult version of her 2024 memoir "Lovely One" and her experiences as a working mother.

## Why This Conversation Matters

By choosing to focus on personal experiences rather than Supreme Court decisions, both Obama and Jackson offered a model of public conversation that bridges divides rather than deepening them. Their dialogue humanizes the highest levels of American governance — reminding audiences that even justices and first ladies grapple with the same questions of balance, sacrifice, and purpose that define ordinary life. In an era of intense polarization, that might be the most radical choice of all.
