# Why Foreign Tourists Are Flocking to China This Summer

A record number of foreign tourists—particularly from Europe—are choosing China as a summer destination to escape the most severe heatwaves ever recorded across the Northern Hemisphere. Official data from China’s Ministry of Public Security shows 22.914 million foreign entries in the first half of 2026, a 20.4% year-over-year increase, with 17.815 million visa-free entries—up 30.6%, as [CCTV News reported](https://news.sina.com.cn/c/2026-07-24/doc-iniixfyf1684102.shtml).

## Europe Under Extreme Heat

The surge comes as Europe experiences what the World Weather Attribution group has called the most severe heatwave ever recorded over the region studied, according to [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_European_heatwaves). Starting in late May 2026, multiple heatwaves swept across the continent, breaking temperature records in 17 or more countries.

Temperatures reached 48.4°C in Casteltermini, Italy on July 18, while France recorded 44.3°C—its hottest day since 1947. Germany hit 41.7°C, and Denmark set an all-time record of 37°C. The Copernicus Climate Change Service confirmed June 2026 as the hottest June on record for western Europe and the second warmest globally. Estimated excess deaths across Europe surpassed 25,000.

## China’s Tourism Boom

The numbers tell a clear story. Finnish travelers purchased 12.4 times more tickets to China year-over-year, while Greek bookings nearly doubled, according to [China Youth Net](https://m.youth.cn/qwtx/xxl/202607/t20260717_16769410.htm). Shenzhen Airport recorded 1.034 million foreign entries and exits by July 15—a milestone reached 71 days earlier than in 2025.

Among Shenzhen’s arrivals, 45.2% listed sightseeing and leisure as their primary purpose, up 25.7% year-over-year. European arrivals specifically increased 31.6% since June, according to Deputy Captain Mai Xiaoyu of the Shenzhen Border Inspection.

## Cool Destinations, Warm Welcome

Several Chinese destinations stand out. Zhangjiajie in Hunan province attracts 15,000 daily visitors, with international tourists making up 15% of the total—up 51.72% from 2025. The area’s 240-hour transit visa policy and 13 air routes connecting to 8 countries have made it a key gateway.

Changbai Mountain in Jilin province offers average summer temperatures below 22°C and draws over 30,000 visitors daily. It benefits from visa-free policies for Russian and South Korean nationals. In Shenzhen, theme parks report foreign visitor numbers up more than 50% year-over-year, with weekend water festivals drawing thousands.

Ctrip data shows summer inbound orders from European countries grew 275% year-over-year, with ticket orders up more than 20 times. Russia accounted for 53.5% of orders (up 553%), while the UK, France, and Germany combined for nearly 42%.

## China’s Competitive Advantage

China’s appeal rests on multiple pillars. Its diverse geography offers natural climate relief through cool microclimates in Yunnan-Guizhou (~25°C), the Tibetan highlands, and northeastern mountains. Infrastructure is a major draw—China boasts the world’s largest power system with ubiquitous air conditioning, well-maintained public facilities, and an extensive high-speed rail network connecting cool destinations.

Analysts point to China’s infrastructure advantages as a key differentiator. According to analysis from [12371.gov.cn](https://www.12371.gov.cn/web/article/1529048679047979008/web/content_1529048679047979008.html), China operates the world’s largest power system with over 40 billion kilowatts of installed capacity, ensuring stable electricity even during peak summer demand. In contrast, parts of Europe’s aging power grids and building stock—many lacking modern air conditioning—struggled under the extreme heat.

Visa policies have also played a crucial role. As [People’s Daily reported](https://en.people.cn/n3/2026/0128/c90000-20420105.html), China now offers visa-free entry to citizens of 76 countries, while travelers from 55 countries can use a 240-hour visa-free transit policy at 65 entry points. A French tourist interviewed by CCTV noted: “Yunnan is only 25°C, very comfortable... plenty of AC, shade, drinking water—much better than Europe’s stuffy old subways and buildings without AC.”

Beyond climate and scenery, China has become a shopping destination—a phenomenon some call “reverse cross-border shopping.” Foreign tourists flock to Shenzhen’s Huaqiangbei electronics market for cooling products like spray fans and cooling towels. In Shanghai, Chinese fashion brands attract international crowds, and some items sell out. A Colombian tourist told CCTV she saw a brand on social media and came specifically to buy it, only to find her preferred style was already sold out.

## A Shift in Global Tourism Patterns

The trend signals a broader transformation. Hotels in Shenzhen are shifting from business to leisure clientele, and the benefits are spreading beyond tier-one cities. According to China Youth Net, arrivals at Yining and Linzhi—secondary cities in western China—surged 6.3 times year-over-year, while tourist numbers to Datong, Yiwu, Jiamusi, and Dunhuang all doubled.

Shangri-La Group Assistant Vice President Lu Jingyue noted that foreign visitors to China are increasingly choosing leisure travel over business trips, with demand shifting from basic accommodation to immersive cultural and culinary experiences.

Industry observers suggest that if European heatwaves become an annual norm, this could permanently reshape global tourism flows. China’s visa liberalization strategy, combined with its climate diversity and infrastructure strengths, is positioning the country as both a climate refuge and a premier travel destination—offering summer heat relief that an increasing number of global travelers are eager to embrace.
