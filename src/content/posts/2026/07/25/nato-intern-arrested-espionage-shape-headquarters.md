# NATO Intern Arrested for Espionage at SHAPE Headquarters

A Canadian national of Chinese descent working as an intern at NATO's Supreme Headquarters Allied Powers Europe (SHAPE) in Mons, Belgium, has been arrested on suspicion of espionage for a third country and participation in a criminal organization. The arrest, carried out on Friday, was announced Saturday by the Belgian Federal Prosecutor's Office, underscoring heightened security vigilance at NATO facilities amid a tense geopolitical climate.

## The Arrest and Investigation

The suspect, whose identity has not been released, was placed under an arrest warrant by an investigating judge at the Court of First Instance of Hainaut, Mons division, on Friday, July 24. According to the Federal Prosecutor's Office, the case began when SHAPE's own security services detected the intern engaging in suspicious activities, including accessing computer systems and files outside her duties. SHAPE reported the matter to the General Intelligence and Security Service (SGRS/ADIV), Belgium's military intelligence service, which triggered a coordinated investigation.

As [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/07/25/canadese-van-chinese-afkomst-verdacht-van-spionage-in-navo-hoofd/) reported, the case was immediately assigned to the Federal Prosecutor's Office, which has exclusive competence over espionage matters. The Federal Judicial Police (PJF) of Charleroi took charge of the investigation, supported by the Computer Crime Unit (CCU) and technical and scientific police units from Charleroi and Mons. Searches were conducted at the suspect's home and workplace at SHAPE on Thursday, July 23, one day before the arrest warrant was issued.

"That could include searches within computer systems or checking files. Things that are not part of her duties," said Ann Lukowiak, spokesperson for the Federal Prosecutor's Office, describing the suspicious activities that alerted SHAPE security.

The suspect is facing charges of espionage "on behalf of a third country" and participation in a criminal organization. The arrest warrant must be confirmed by the council chamber (raadkamer) in the coming week.

## Heightened Alert in a Geopolitically Charged Environment

Belgian authorities emphasized that the swift response reflects an elevated state of alert across security services. "In the current geopolitical context, all security and police services, as well as the public prosecutor's office, are particularly alert for facts of espionage and interference," Lukowiak said, as [reported by RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/une-stagiaire-du-shape-placee-sous-mandat-d-arret-en-belgique-soupconnee-d-espionnage-11762489). "As soon as the suspected facts were noticed, action was taken immediately."

Belgian Defense Minister Theo Francken (N-VA) welcomed the outcome, expressing satisfaction with "the alertness of the security services of SHAPE and the swift action of the Belgian security services and justice." Francken emphasized that counter-espionage remains a top priority, noting that Belgium, as host to both NATO and EU institutions, is the second most important diplomatic hub in the world after Washington, D.C.

"Espionage activities are legion. Staying vigilant is the message," he said, according to [The Brussels Times](https://www.brusselstimes.com/2245139/nato-intern-from-canada-arrested-on-espionage-charges-in-belgium).

## Broader Implications

The case raises significant questions about security vetting procedures at NATO facilities. SHAPE, located in Casteau near Mons, is NATO's strategic military command responsible for planning and executing all alliance military operations. That an intern — typically a short-term, low-position role — could allegedly access sensitive information has drawn attention to potential gaps in security clearance processes for non-permanent staff.

The involvement of a Canadian citizen of Chinese descent, suspected of spying for an unspecified "third country" widely understood to be China, creates a complex diplomatic dynamic involving Belgium, Canada, and China. As [POLITICO Europe](https://www.politico.eu/article/nato-intern-arrested-over-espionage-suspicions/) noted, the case adds to a pattern of alleged foreign intelligence operations targeting Western institutions through diaspora members.

Belgium's counter-intelligence framework — involving both the civilian State Security Service (VSSE) and the military intelligence service (SGRS/ADIV) — has been under strain as the country remains a persistent target for espionage activities. The Federal Prosecutor's Office stated that no further details would be released at this stage to protect the ongoing investigation.

## What to Watch For

The arrest warrant must be confirmed by the council chamber in the coming week. Beyond the immediate legal proceedings, the case is expected to prompt a review of NATO's vetting procedures for interns and temporary personnel. Canada's official response to the arrest of its citizen remains pending, and the specific nature of the evidence supporting the "criminal organization" charge — a charge distinct from espionage — will be closely scrutinized as the investigation unfolds.

This case serves as a stark reminder of the persistent espionage threats facing NATO and allied nations, and the critical importance of vigilance at even the most basic levels of access within sensitive military installations.
