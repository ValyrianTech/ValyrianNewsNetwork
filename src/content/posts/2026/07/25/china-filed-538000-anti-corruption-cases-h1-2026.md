# China Filed 538,000 Anti-Corruption Cases in First Half of 2026

China's disciplinary inspection and supervision agencies filed over 538,000 cases nationwide in the first half of 2026, according to data released on July 25 by the Central Commission for Discipline Inspection (CCDI). The figures, reported by [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/legal/20260725/255768e4b6014d859ff5545fa935701e/c.html), reveal a sustained intensification of the country's anti-corruption campaign, with investigations targeting senior officials surging dramatically compared to the same period last year.

## Context: A Campaign Under Xi Jinping

Since President Xi Jinping took power in 2012, China has waged a sweeping anti-corruption campaign that has investigated millions of officials at every level of government and the Communist Party. The campaign employs a "four forms" system of disciplinary oversight, ranging from criticism and education to criminal prosecution. A hallmark slogan — "catch tigers and flies together" — encapsulates the strategy of targeting both high-ranking "tiger" officials and grassroots "fly" level cadres.

2026 marks the first year of China's 15th Five-Year Plan period, a context that has lent added significance to the anti-corruption push. At the 4th Plenary Session of the 20th CCDI in January, Xi called for a "relentless" posture, declaring the campaign would "never pause, never retreat."

## Key Developments: The Numbers in Detail

The CCDI report offers a granular breakdown of the anti-corruption effort. During H1 2026, agencies received 2.188 million信访 (letters and visits) reports, including 562,000 accusation-type reports, and processed 1.274 million problem leads.

By rank, the cases filed included:
- **Provincial/ministerial level and above**: 50 cases
- **Department/bureau level**: 2,773 cases
- **County/department level**: 23,000 cases
- **Township/section level**: 75,000 cases
- **Current/former village party secretaries and committee directors**: 54,000 cases

A total of 412,000 people received disciplinary punishment, including 307,000 party disciplinary measures and 134,000 administrative disciplinary measures. Among those disciplined, 74 were at the provincial/ministerial level or above, 2,274 at the department/bureau level, 18,000 at the county/section level, 56,000 at the township/section level, 56,000 were general cadres, and 280,000 were classified as others including rural and enterprise personnel.

The "four forms" system was applied to 843,000 person-times: Form 1 (criticism and education) accounted for 424,000 cases (50.4%); Form 2 (light disciplinary action) for 341,000 (40.4%); Form 3 (heavy disciplinary action) for 41,000 (4.9%); and Form 4 (prosecution) for 36,000 (4.3%).

In a sign of the campaign's expanding scope, 19,000 bribe-givers were investigated, with 1,938 transferred to prosecutorial authorities, as reported by [CCTV](https://news.cctv.com/2026/07/25/ARTIpWTgkDfZBurgnznIaAcX260725.shtml).

## Analysis: The Surge in Senior-Level Targeting

While the overall scale of the anti-corruption effort remains consistent with previous periods, the targeting of higher-ranking officials has intensified markedly. Analysis by [Zaochenbao](https://www.zaochenbao.com/news/china/202607/2577239.html) highlights several striking year-over-year comparisons:

| Metric | H1 2025 | H1 2026 | Change |
|--------|---------|---------|--------|
| Provincial/ministerial+ cases filed | 43 | 50 | +16.3% |
| Department/bureau level cases filed | 2,335 | 2,773 | +18.8% |
| Provincial/ministerial+ disciplined | 30 | 74 | +146.7% |
| Total bribe-givers investigated | 16,000 | 19,000 | +18.75% |

The number of provincial/ministerial-level cadres disciplined more than doubled from 30 to 74 — a 146.7% increase — while department/bureau-level cadres disciplined rose from 1,876 to 2,274. This suggests a strategic emphasis on pursuing "tigers" alongside the continued crackdown on lower-level corruption.

This intensification comes amid a broader policy push against emerging forms of corruption. In January 2026, the CCDI released a documentary series titled "One Step Never Stops, Half Step Never Retreats," which highlighted new challenges including "shadow companies," "options corruption," and cryptocurrency bribery.

## Implications: What the Data Signals

The sustained increase in high-level investigations serves multiple purposes within China's political system. It reinforces the credibility of the anti-corruption campaign as a disciplinary tool, supports political consolidation, and helps maintain the Communist Party's legitimacy. The simultaneous focus on bribe-givers — with investigations rising 18.75% year-over-year — reflects a policy of "bribe-taking and bribe-giving jointly investigated" that could have ripple effects on business-government relations.

The significant number of village-level officials investigated — 54,000 current or former village party secretaries and committee directors — demonstrates continued attention to grassroots governance, where corruption directly affects citizens' daily lives.

## What to Watch For

Historical patterns suggest that full-year totals for CCDI cases typically exceed double the H1 figure, potentially placing 2026 on track for well over 1 million cases filed. How the campaign evolves in the second half of the year, particularly with continued focus on senior officials and bribe-givers, will offer further insight into the trajectory of China's internal disciplinary apparatus and its broader political implications.
