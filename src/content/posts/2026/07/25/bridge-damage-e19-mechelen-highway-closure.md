# Bridge Damage on E19 Near Mechelen Forces Highway Closure

The Flemish Agency for Roads and Traffic (AWV) has partially closed the E19 highway near Mechelen after discovering structural damage to a bridge during a routine inspection, authorities [confirmed](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/07/25/schade-aan-brug-over-e19-bij-mechelen/) on Saturday. Traffic heading toward Brussels has been reduced to a single lane, with officials warning of significant delays — particularly during peak hours as summer holiday travel continues.

## A Critical Artery

The E19 is one of Belgium’s most vital motorways, connecting the port city of Antwerp with the capital, Brussels. The bridge at the Mechelen-Noord interchange spans the Dijle river and is a key junction where the E19 intersects with the R6 ring road around Mechelen. On a normal workday outside the summer holiday period, approximately 53,400 vehicles cross the bridge toward Brussels. Even during the quieter summer months, that figure remains around 51,600 vehicles, according to [AWV data](https://wegenenverkeer.be/nieuws/schade-aan-brug-van-e19-ter-hoogte-van-mechelen-noord-beperkte-doorgang-richting-brussel). Any disruption on this corridor has immediate cascading effects on regional mobility.

## The Discovery

The damage was discovered on Friday afternoon, July 24, during a routine inspection by AWV bridge inspectors. They identified structural damage in one of the transverse support beams — a critical load-bearing element — of the bridge construction at the Mechelen-Noord junction. By Friday night, emergency traffic measures were already being implemented to ensure safety while keeping the route operational.

Experts from Expertise Beton en Staal, a specialized unit within the Department of Mobility and Public Works (MOW), are now investigating how best to address the damage and whether a full repair is feasible. Their assessment will determine whether the bridge can be fixed with targeted repairs or requires more extensive — and potentially longer — structural intervention.

## Traffic Measures

AWV has implemented several risk-mitigating measures for traffic traveling toward Brussels:

- **Lane reduction**: Traffic is currently limited to one lane, with a target of restoring two narrowed lanes by Sunday morning using adjusted road markings
- **Speed limit**: Reduced to 70 km/h on the affected section to minimize stress on the damaged structure
- **Overtaking ban**: Prohibited for trucks on this section
- **R6 on-ramp closure**: The entrance from the Mechelen ring road (R6) to the E19 toward Brussels is closed; drivers are being diverted via the Rumst interchange, which will likely experience increased congestion as a result

“Due to the limited passage, road users should best expect traffic jams and possibly significant travel time loss, especially during peak moments,” AWV warned in a statement. The agency also advised drivers to monitor real-time traffic information closely.

Traffic heading toward Antwerp is not affected by the measures, AWV confirmed.

## Broader Disruptions

The bridge damage comes at a difficult time for the E19 corridor, which is already under significant strain. Since June 25, AWV has been conducting a three-phase surface renovation on the E19 toward Brussels near Mechelen-Zuid and Rumst, scheduled to run through August. This ongoing project means the motorway has been experiencing multiple concurrent disruptions even before the bridge issue emerged.

Furthermore, the primary alternative route — the A12 motorway between Antwerp and Brussels — is also affected by road works in Meise that are expected to continue until the end of September 2026. This creates a compounding effect: travelers on the Antwerp-Brussels axis face disruptions on both major routes simultaneously, leaving few efficient alternatives.

The Flemish Traffic Center has [warned](https://www.dhnet.be/regions/bruxelles/bruxelles-mobilite/2026/07/25/des-embarras-de-circulation-attendus-sur-le19-partiellement-fermee-en-raison-dun-pont-endommage-IWXUOUKZCFCGPC5IM777ZFDHLM/) of severe congestion and significant travel time loss. The closure coincides with the summer holiday period, when many Belgians are traveling to and from vacation destinations — weekend traffic that would normally flow freely on the E19 is now likely to face substantial delays. French-language media [reported](https://www.lalibre.be/dernieres-depeches/2026/07/25/le19-entre-bruxelles-et-anvers-partiellement-fermee-en-raison-dun-pont-endommage-PHH3ZUB64ZAHZI244QNOECM3KQ/) that traffic would be reduced to one lane through the weekend, with two narrowed lanes expected no earlier than Sunday morning.

## What’s Next?

The timeline for full repairs remains unclear pending expert assessment. If the damage is limited and repairable, normal traffic flow could be restored relatively quickly. However, if the transverse support beam requires replacement or if further structural issues are uncovered during the investigation, disruptions could persist for weeks or even months.

Several open questions remain: What is the exact age and maintenance history of the Dijlebrug? Has this bridge experienced prior structural issues? And are there broader concerns about similar bridges in the Belgian motorway network?

For now, travelers are advised to check real-time traffic information and, where possible, avoid the affected section of the E19 toward Brussels during peak hours. The situation serves as a reminder of the ongoing infrastructure maintenance challenges facing one of Belgium’s busiest and most economically important motorway corridors.
