# Evenepoel Protects Podium Hopes on Alpe d'Huez Queen Stage

Belgian cycling star Remco Evenepoel faces the most decisive stage of the 2026 Tour de France on Saturday as the race tackles the legendary Alpe d'Huez climb in a grueling queen stage featuring 5,600 meters of elevation gain. Sitting second in the general classification, 58 seconds ahead of third-placed Isaac Del Toro, Evenepoel has adopted a cautious strategy aimed at protecting his podium position rather than launching risky attacks.

## Context & Stakes

The 20th stage — the final mountain test before Sunday's procession into Paris — takes riders from Bourg-d'Oisans over five major Alpine climbs including the Col de la Croix-de-Fer, Col du Télégraphe, Col du Galibier, and the Col de Sarenne before the iconic ascent to Alpe d'Huez. For Evenepoel, a successful defense of second place would mark the first time a Belgian has finished runner-up in the Tour since Lucien Van Impe in 1981, as [WielerFlits](https://www.wielerflits.nl/nieuws/dag-van-de-waarheid-voor-remco-evenepoel-hangt-af-van-hoe-je-hersteld-bent/) noted.

The 26-year-old Red Bull-BORA-hansgrohe leader has already won the stage 16 individual time trial earlier in the race and has shown consistent climbing form throughout the three-week event. However, the cumulative fatigue of the Tour — combined with the brutal profile of Stage 20 — presents his sternest test yet.

## Evenepoel's Cautious Strategy

Speaking ahead of the stage, Evenepoel made clear his priority is securing the podium. "Ik ga geen gekke risico's nemen waardoor mijn tweede plaats in gevaar komt," the Belgian told Sporza. "I won't take crazy risks that would endanger my second place."

He acknowledged the challenge of the queen stage in comments reported by [Eurosport](https://www.eurosport.nl/wielrennen/tour-de-france/2026/remco-evenepoel-wil-vooral-tweede-plaats-behouden-ik-ga-geen-onnodige-risicos-nemen_sto23322292/story.shtml). "If the legs aren't there in the 20th stage, then nothing is working for anyone. Everything depends on how you feel and how well you've recovered from yesterday. Today you can't hide: if the legs aren't there, you have a problem."

Evenepoel also expressed concern about safety on the narrow, crowded climb of Alpe d'Huez. "The road is narrow and the crowds often stand very close," he said. "Hopefully barriers are placed at the bottom and fences in the final kilometers."

## The Threat from Paul Seixas and Decathlon AG2R

While Evenepoel plans a conservative approach, he expects others to take more risks — particularly 19-year-old French prodigy Paul Seixas (Decathlon AG2R), who sits fourth in the general classification and is eyeing a podium spot. Seixas finished ninth in Stage 19 in the same group as Evenepoel and has the backing of an aggressive team.

"I expect Decathlon to go all-out to knock Del Toro off the podium," Evenepoel said. "They want to find out if he's also affected by that illness."

This dynamic could shape the entire stage. With UAE Team Emirates-XRG weakened by illness — Brandon McNulty forced to abandon, while Adam Yates and Tim Wellens have been visibly struggling — the team's control over the peloton is less dominant than earlier in the race, creating opportunities for attacks.

## Stage 19 Recap: Pogacar Dominates

Friday's stage from Gap to Alpe d'Huez set the stage for the finale. Tadej Pogacar (UAE Team Emirates-XRG) produced a stunning solo performance to win Stage 19, even breaking Marco Pantani's long-standing climbing record on Alpe d'Huez. The Slovenian yellow jersey holder finished ahead of Lenny Martinez and Richard Carapaz.

As [WielerFlits reported](https://www.wielerflits.nl/nieuws/tour-2026-imponerende-tadej-pogacar-blaast-alles-en-iedereen-weg-op-alpe-dhuez/), Pogacar's dominance was absolute: he bridged a three-minute gap to the breakaway on the final climb and dispatched the race leaders with multiple accelerations. Evenepoel finished sixth at 2 minutes and 29 seconds behind, a result that preserved his GC position but confirmed Pogacar's superiority.

## Safety Concerns on Alpe d'Huez

The chaos surrounding Friday's stage has amplified concerns about rider safety. Massive crowds on Alpe d'Huez forced the partial closure of the road from Thursday afternoon due to extreme traffic congestion. A Shimano neutral service vehicle nearly struck Richard Carapaz during the ascent, while Einer Rubio (Movistar) crashed into a UAE team car, requiring 20 stitches and forcing his abandonment.

Evenepoel himself was forced to slow down when fans blocked the road for motorcycles. The incidents have prompted renewed discussion about crowd management on cycling's most famous climb.

## UAE's Health Crisis

Perhaps the most significant factor shaping the final weekend is the health situation within UAE Team Emirates-XRG. Multiple riders have fallen ill in the final week, with McNulty abandoning and both Yates and Wellens showing clear signs of physical distress. While Pogacar and third-placed Isaac Del Toro have not shown symptoms, the weakened support structure has opened tactical possibilities for rival teams.

Evenepoel, however, struck a sporting tone when asked about UAE's struggles. As reported by Eurosport, he said: "I hope the UAE riders recover quickly. They've dominated this Tour. It would be a shame if they had to abandon due to health issues."

## What to Watch For

As Stage 20 unfolds, several key questions remain:

1. Can Evenepoel hold his 58-second buffer over Del Toro across 5,600 meters of climbing?
2. Will Paul Seixas and Decathlon AG2R succeed in putting Del Toro under pressure?
3. How will the weakened UAE team manage the race dynamic?
4. Can Evenepoel become the first Belgian since 1981 to finish second in the Tour de France?

Saturday's queen stage promises to be a brutal test of endurance, strategy, and nerve. With his teammate Jai Hindley in the breakaway providing tactical options, Evenepoel has cards to play. But as he himself admitted, the outcome depends on the most basic element of cycling: "We just need good legs, and then we'll see what happens."

The 170.9-kilometer stage from Bourg-d'Oisans to Alpe d'Huez is expected to reach its dramatic conclusion in the late afternoon, setting the final general classification before Sunday's ceremonial sprint into Paris.
