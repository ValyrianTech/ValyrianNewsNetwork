# Hawaii Lieutenant Governor Indicted in Corruption Probe

Hawaii Lieutenant Governor Sylvia Luke, a Democrat and the first South Korean-born official elected to statewide office in the United States, was indicted by an O'ahu grand jury on July 24 on charges of criminal conspiracy to commit bribery, bribery by a public servant, and falsifying candidate committee reports. Governor Josh Green immediately called for her resignation, marking a stunning fall from grace for one of the state's highest-ranking officials, who has been on unpaid leave since April.

## The Alleged Scheme

According to the indictment, the charges stem from an alleged scheme orchestrated in early 2022 when Luke was still a state representative. Prosecutors say lobbyist Tobi Solidum, who represented the National Kidney Foundation of Hawaii, promised Luke $70,000 in campaign contributions in exchange for her support of COVID-19 testing contracts that the nonprofit had with the state.

On January 20, 2022, at a dinner at Morton's Steakhouse in Honolulu's Ala Moana Shopping Center, Solidum gave Luke two $5,000 campaign checks. "By next week we'll have 35, so it will be halfway to our 70," Solidum told Luke, according to the indictment, to which Luke allegedly replied, "Oh wow" and "That's terrific."

The next day, Luke contacted Representative Linda Ichiyama to schedule a meeting on emergency appropriations. House Bill 2392, an emergency appropriation benefiting the COVID-19 testing program, was introduced by then-Representative Ryan Yamane and advanced by Luke's finance committee, as [reported by Fox News](https://www.foxnews.com/politics/hawaiis-democratic-lieutenant-governor-indicted-state-corruption-investigation).

## The Defendants

Five individuals were indicted in the case. Besides Luke and Solidum, the charges extend to Ryan Yamane, a former state representative and former director of the Department of Human Services; Ford Fuchigami, a Department of Transportation Airports administrator; and Leo Asuncion, a former Public Utilities Commission chair who served as Luke's campaign treasurer.

Deputy Attorney General David Van Acker, the lead prosecutor, told the court that Yamane is considered the "most culpable" individual in the case, according to [Honolulu Civil Beat](https://www.civilbeat.org/2026/07/bribery-charges-sylvia-luke-ex-state-rep-and-others-indicted-in-corruption-case/). Yamane is alleged to have taken thousands of dollars from Solidum while helping secure funds for the COVID-19 testing contracts. In a February 2022 text message cited in the indictment, Yamane wrote to Solidum: "Might not be good to be seen with me."

The charges against Fuchigami include obstruction of justice for allegedly falsely stating he never received money from Solidum, who prosecutors say wrote him a $7,000 check on January 30, 2022. Asuncion faces charges related to falsifying campaign committee reports.

## Legal Status and Political Fallout

Luke surrendered to authorities on July 24 and was released after posting $80,000 bail. She had already withdrawn her re-election campaign in April and taken unpaid leave, with State Comptroller Keith Regan serving as acting lieutenant governor. Solidum is believed to have fled to the Philippines, and prosecutors obtained a warrant for his arrest without bail.

Governor Green, who had urged Attorney General Anne Lopez to expedite the investigation, called for Luke's formal resignation on Friday. "The Lieutenant Governor needs to consider formally resigning to address this matter and so that the state of Hawai'i can move forward," Green said in a statement.

Luke has denied wrongdoing. "I never granted special favors to a contributor," she said in a social media post, adding that she "prized integrity and honesty above all throughout her career."

## Hawaii's Deep-Rooted Corruption Problem

The investigation is an offshoot of a broader federal bribery probe that has already ensnared two of Luke's former House colleagues — Ty Cullen and J. Kalani English — both of whom were convicted and served federal prison time. A 2022 legislative commission described corruption in Hawaii as "glaring, embarrassing, deep-rooted and systemic."

The state created the Special Investigation and Prosecution Division (SIPD) in 2022 specifically to investigate government corruption. The Luke case represents the highest-profile test of that mandate yet.

## What's Next

Luke's refusal to resign so far raises the possibility of impeachment proceedings. Solidum's potential extradition from the Philippines remains an open question, and it is unclear whether federal charges will follow the state indictment. The case also threatens to cast a shadow over the 2026 gubernatorial election in a state where Democrats have held uninterrupted power since statehood.
