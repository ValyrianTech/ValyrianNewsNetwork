# Brussels Firefighters Strike Cripples Emergency Response

Brussels' emergency response capabilities have been severely compromised as an ongoing indefinite strike by firefighters left only 17 percent of standby personnel reporting for duty on July 25, according to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/07/25/staking-brandweer-brussel-hulpverlening-in-het-gedrang/). Fire department spokesperson Walter Derieuw warned that "due to this severely reduced staffing, emergency assistance to Brussels residents is at risk."

Firefighting operations are currently running at approximately 20 percent of normal capacity, while urgent ambulance interventions operate at roughly 60 percent. The 112 emergency call center and fire dispatch are functioning at only about half their usual capacity, meaning significant delays in call handling and emergency response are expected. The Brussels fire department has urged the public to contact the emergency center only for genuine life-threatening situations.

## Background: A Long-Simmering Crisis

The strike, now in its fifth day, began as a 24-hour walkout on Belgium's National Day (July 21) but escalated into an indefinite strike after talks with Brussels Minister-President Boris Dillies (MR) failed to produce a breakthrough. The dispute centers on chronic understaffing, working conditions, and a controversial reorganization plan presented by management in May 2026 that unions say would further reduce operational resources, according to [The Bulletin](https://www.thebulletin.be/brussels-firefighters-strike-over-lack-resources-and-staff).

Unions have long warned that the Brussels fire service (DBDMH-SIAMU) has been systematically underfunded for years. Employees reaching retirement age are not being replaced, while the number of emergency interventions continues to rise annually, as reported by [BRUZZ](https://www.bruzz.be/actua/veiligheid/brusselse-brandweerbonden-keren-ontevreden-terug-van-gesprek-met-kabinet-persoons). The unions are demanding the recruitment of approximately 200 additional firefighters to bring the service to adequate staffing levels.

## Escalation and Failed Negotiations

On the first day of the strike, the Brussels fire service reported an attendance rate of just 42 percent, with only 65 firefighters present across all stations — far short of the minimum daily requirement of 153, according to The Bulletin. Some staff were called in to ensure a minimum service, and with support from the Red Cross and other emergency zones, operational capacity was maintained at approximately 70 percent for National Day celebrations.

The strike was initially planned as a one-day action, but unions voted to continue indefinitely after negotiations with Minister-President Dillies on July 22 left them deeply dissatisfied. As [VRT NWS reported](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/07/22/brandweer-staking-brussel-dillies-vakbond/), union representative Steven Gilissen (ACV) stated: "Dillies offers us even less than what was initially decided by the government."

A central point of contention is the use of a 10 million euro transfer from Federal Interior Minister Bernard Quintin, which was intended for the fire service. Union representative Yves De Vogelaere (ACV) claimed that "that money was used to fill the financial hole. The fire department saw none of it." The unions demand that the full amount be dedicated to firefighting resources.

"We don't want to strike, but it's our last resort," De Vogelaere said, adding that "the willingness to strike only increases." Dillies expressed regret that talks failed, stating: "I hear the anger and concerns expressed today. Some of the problems mentioned are real and must be addressed... My door remains open."

## No Emergency Plan Declared

Despite the severity of the situation, the fire department directorate and the Brussels government have refused to declare the emergency plan, which would allow reinforcements from other Belgian regions, according to [BRUZZ](https://www.bruzz.be/actua/samenleving/slechts-17-procent-brusselse-brandweer-zaterdag-aan-het-werk-hulpverlening-gedrang-2026-07-25). One striking firefighter noted that doing so would signal "a major political problem." The refusal is widely seen as an attempt by the Brussels regional government to avoid acknowledging the full scale of the crisis.

## Analysis: A Crisis of Confidence

The strike represents more than a labor dispute — it reflects a fundamental breakdown in public service funding and political accountability in the Brussels-Capital Region. The structural underfunding of the fire service has been evident for years, with previous strike actions on New Year's Eve 2025 and in early 2026 failing to produce lasting solutions.

Even if a short-term agreement is reached, the underlying issues will persist. New recruits promised by the end of 2026 will not be operationally ready until 2029, leaving a critical gap in emergency response capacity for years to come. The political dimension is significant: the refusal to declare the emergency plan suggests the Brussels government is prioritizing political optics over urgent public safety needs.

## What's Next

Unions are waiting for revised proposals from Minister-President Dillies, with further talks expected in the coming days. However, union leaders have made clear that the strike will continue indefinitely until concrete commitments are made. In the meantime, Brussels residents face the reality of severely degraded emergency services, with only the most life-threatening situations guaranteed a response.

The crisis raises urgent questions about the sustainability of public services in Belgium's capital and whether the political will exists to address a problem that has been years in the making.
