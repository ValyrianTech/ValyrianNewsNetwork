# OpenAI's $20B Georgia Data Center Sparks Promise and Backlash

OpenAI has announced plans to build a $20 billion AI data center campus in Effingham County, Georgia — one of the largest single data center investments in U.S. history — but the project has ignited a fierce community debate over transparency, water usage, and the rapid expansion of AI infrastructure in the Southeast.

Dubbed "Project Camellia," the 3.2-gigawatt facility will occupy approximately 1,400 acres at the Savannah Gateway Industrial Hub in Rincon, about 40 minutes from downtown Savannah. The campus will comprise up to four buildings developed in phases between 2028 and 2032, according to [USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/tech/news/2026/07/24/openai-georgia-ai-data-center/91043217007/).

## A New Era for AI Infrastructure

The project marks a strategic shift for OpenAI. Rather than leasing data center capacity from third-party providers, the company is building its own ground-up campus — a signal that the demand for AI computing power is outstripping existing infrastructure. OpenAI has said the facility will be privately funded, with no use of public money or local tax dollars.

According to the [Project Camellia website](https://projectcamellia.com/faq), the site was chosen because it sits within a pre-existing industrial zone already designated for large-scale development. The company has emphasized that families in Georgia "will not subsidize this project" and that OpenAI will pay the full cost of infrastructure and electric service.

## Power and Grid Innovation

Georgia Power will supply the facility's massive energy needs under a 25-year agreement. In a [press release](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/georgia-power-to-serve-openai-project-in-effingham-county-302832224.html), the utility announced that OpenAI has committed to providing up to 1,000 megawatts of flexible demand response — meaning the company will voluntarily reduce power consumption during peak demand periods to support grid stability. Georgia Power described this as "among the largest single-facility demand response commitments in the country."

Aaron Mitchell, senior vice president of Strategic Growth at Georgia Power, said the agreement demonstrates how large-energy users can contribute to reliability. "Companies like OpenAI are making real commitments when it comes to responsible operation and development," Mitchell said, "and demonstrating the benefits that these facilities can bring to Georgia communities."

## Water and Environmental Concerns

The project arrives as Georgia grapples with a historic drought. Earlier this year, 100% of the state experienced severe to exceptional drought conditions for the first time on record. Sen. Raphael Warnock (D-GA) wrote to the Environmental Protection Agency seeking guidance on data center water impacts, warning that "new demands on Georgia's limited water resources" pose significant risks.

OpenAI has committed to using a closed-loop recirculating water system, similar to a car radiator, that reduces water consumption by up to 70% compared to traditional cooling methods. The company promises that ongoing water needs will match those of a typical office building and will not divert water from local communities, according to a [The Current GA](https://thecurrentga.org/2026/07/22/20-billion-openai-data-center-to-open-in-effingham-county/) report. However, the state's regulatory review — Georgia's Developments of Regional Impact (DRI) process — must still evaluate the project's environmental impacts, and a DRI form submitted on July 23 left key sections on water usage and energy supply blank pending further analysis.

## Community Backlash and Transparency Questions

The most contentious aspect of the project has been the secrecy surrounding its negotiation. Many Effingham County residents say they learned about the data center only when it was publicly announced on July 22, despite months of behind-the-scenes discussions between OpenAI, county officials, and state leaders.

An open house held on July 23 drew approximately 1,000 residents, many of whom expressed anger about the lack of community input. A Change.org petition opposing the project garnered more than 10,000 signatures within days. In follow-up reporting, [The Current GA](https://thecurrentga.org/2026/07/23/openai-data-center-project-sparks-outrage-among-effingham-county-residents/) documented residents being repeatedly redirected between county officials, the development authority, and OpenAI representatives when seeking information.

Effingham County Commissioner Roger Burdette defended the closed-door approach, telling reporters: "We didn't want to scare them away." County Manager Tim Callanan argued that the project's planning distinguished it from problematic data center developments elsewhere. "We tried to work on everything that we could think of in advance to mitigate some of the trouble points," Callanan said.

## Economic Promises and Tax Questions

OpenAI has pledged $80 million in community benefits for local education, public safety, healthcare, and small business resources, separate from tax revenue. An additional $71 million in Codex credits will be provided to students at Georgia colleges, community colleges, and technical schools.

The project is expected to create 400 permanent on-site jobs and thousands of construction positions, with a priority on local contractors. OpenAI will receive a 50% property tax abatement for 15 years but is still projected to become Effingham County's largest property taxpayer. County officials have suggested that tax revenue from the data center could fund "Project Zero," an initiative to eliminate homestead property taxes, potentially reducing the average homeowner's property rate by 40%.

A publicly available audit conducted annually by an independent third party will track OpenAI's compliance with its commitments.

## Regulatory and Political Landscape

The project must clear Georgia's DRI evaluation process within 30 days, with the Coastal Regional Commission assessing its regional impacts. The outcome remains uncertain, particularly given the blank fields in the initial filing.

Political reactions have split along familiar lines. Republican Gov. Brian Kemp, a strong supporter of data center development, recently joined a Trump-backed pledge by GOP governors to promote such facilities while protecting ratepayers. Meanwhile, some neighboring communities are heading in the opposite direction: Camden County has enacted a data center moratorium, and Palm Beach County, Florida, voted against a similar project.

## What to Watch For

As the DRI process unfolds over the coming weeks, several key questions remain: Will the regulatory review result in modifications to the project? How will the $80 million community fund be governed? And what will the actual water consumption be once the facility is operational?

Project Camellia represents both the promise and the peril of the AI era — a massive infusion of investment and innovation that also tests the capacity of local communities, regulatory frameworks, and natural resources to keep pace with technological ambition. The outcome in Effingham County could set a precedent for how similar projects are negotiated and evaluated across the country.
