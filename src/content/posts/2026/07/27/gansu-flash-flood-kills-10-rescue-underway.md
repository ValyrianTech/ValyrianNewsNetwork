# Gansu Flash Flood Kills 10 as Rescue Teams Save 240

A sudden flash flood at the Shuangshimen Scenic Area in Weiyuan County, Gansu Province, has claimed 10 lives and injured 23 others, according to [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/local/20260726/b0c3222b79a748238b0acf371cbf2480/c.html). Rescue operations, involving over 1,000 personnel, have successfully freed 240 people from the stricken area as search efforts continue.

## Context

The flood struck on the afternoon of July 26, triggered by short-duration extreme rainfall accompanied by hailstorms that caused mountain streams to swell rapidly. The Shuangshimen Scenic Area, located in Huichuan Town about a two-hour drive from Lanzhou, is known as "Gansu's Little Altay" and draws up to 4,500 summer visitors daily with its canyons, streams, and grasslands. Despite clear regulations prohibiting camping and overnight stays, the area has become a popular social media "hidden gem" for day-trippers.

## Rescue Operations

Within hours of the disaster, Gansu's emergency response mobilized on a large scale. The provincial fire rescue corps deployed 305 firefighters, 68 fire trucks, 4 search dogs, and 15 search teams using a combined "human plus machinery plus dogs" approach, on-scene reporters from [China News Service](https://www.chinanews.com.cn/sh/2026/07-27/10666984.shtml) detailed. More than 1,000 professional rescuers, 300 public security personnel, and 164 pieces of heavy machinery were deployed across the mountainous terrain, which hampered efforts due to weak mobile signals and difficult access.

Gansu Provincial Party Secretary Hu Changsheng and Governor Ren Zhenhe personally rushed to the scene. China's National Flood Control Headquarters and the Ministry of Emergency Management dispatched work groups and activated a Level 4 flood control emergency response.

## Eyewitness Accounts

Eyewitness Chen, who arrived at the scenic area gate just before the disaster, described the weather change as instantaneous. "Within just over ten seconds, heavy rain poured down, accompanied by dense hail," he told [Chaoxinwen/Tencent News](https://news.qq.com/rain/a/20260726A09LBY00). He noted that despite receiving multiple weather warnings that morning, he proceeded with what he considered a routine local trip. "There has never been such severe flooding here in previous years," he said.

Visitor Wang, who had visited the area the day before, observed that most visitors were local day-trippers who gathered by the creek without awareness of flash flood dangers. "The scenic area has clear regulations prohibiting camping and overnight stays. But most visitors were local day-trippers who gathered by the creek, lacking awareness of flash flood dangers," he said.

## Weather Warnings and Systemic Gaps

The disaster unfolded despite advance warnings. The Dingxi Meteorological Station issued a yellow rainstorm warning on the morning of July 26, and provincial authorities jointly issued a geological disaster risk warning at the same time. Yet the scenic area remained open to visitors, raising questions about the gap between meteorological warnings and on-the-ground action.

A rescue worker noted a critical operational window: "Fortunately, the rain paused at night, giving rescue operations a valuable window." The pause allowed the fourth round of overnight searches to proceed, though teams worked through thick mud and debris-strewn terrain.

## Broader Implications

The Gansu flood is part of a broader pattern of extreme weather affecting China. Typhoon Hongxia had recently struck Guangdong, and July-August marks north China's critical flood prevention period. The incident highlights growing climate risks in traditionally arid northwest China, where mountainous terrain creates high geological disaster potential during sudden heavy rains.

For the Shuangshimen area specifically, the disaster underscores a dangerous pattern: popular but unregulated natural attractions drawing large crowds who underestimate the risks of mountain weather, even when official warnings are in place.

## What's Next

Search operations at the Shuangshimen Scenic Area continue, with authorities working to account for all visitors. The incident has prompted renewed scrutiny of safety protocols at China's natural scenic areas during the peak summer travel season. With extreme weather events becoming more frequent, the gap between issuing warnings and enforcing closures remains a critical challenge for disaster prevention.
