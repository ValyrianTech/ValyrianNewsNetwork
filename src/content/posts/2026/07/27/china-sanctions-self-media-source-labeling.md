# China Sanctions 3,704 Self-Media Over Source Labeling

China's cyberspace regulator has penalized 3,704 self-media accounts and corrected over 48,000 short videos for failing to properly label their information sources, marking the latest targeted enforcement action under the country's expanding internet governance framework.

The Cyberspace Administration of China (CAC) announced the enforcement on Monday, warning that improper source labeling on content involving domestic and international current affairs, public policy, and social events is undermining public trust in online information. The notice, carried by [Xinhua News Agency](https://www.news.cn/politics/20260727/44e13df8bd8d4ae99506a0c32d492ed9/c.html), cited three distinct categories of violations with named examples.

The CAC emphasized that source information labeling is "the basic prerequisite for ensuring the truthfulness, accuracy, credibility, and traceability of online information content," underscoring the regulator's view that proper attribution is fundamental to a healthy information ecosystem.

## Scope of the Crackdown

The CAC identified accounts across major Chinese platforms including Douyin (TikTok China), Kuaishou, Weibo, and Bilibili as having violated source labeling requirements. According to the [regulator's official notice](https://www.cac.gov.cn/2026-07/27/c_1786897886145841.htm), the enforcement falls under Article 3 of the foundational 2023 "Notice on Strengthening Self-Media Management," which specifically requires accurate source labeling for content covering current affairs, public policy, and social events. The 2023 framework also mandates that self-shot images include time and place metadata, that AI-generated content be clearly labeled, and that recycled news reference its original publication date.

Three violation types were detailed:

**Direct copying without attribution**: Accounts such as Douyin's "致静" (Zhijing) and Kuaishou's "大秦说书" (Daqin Shuoshu) were found to have directly copied or minimally repackaged international news content without any source labels, preventing users from tracing information to its original出处.

**Vague or evasive labeling**: Accounts including Weibo's "骑着电驴去荡圩" and Kuaishou's "路路讲局势" used deliberately ambiguous phrases such as "网传" (net says), "网友爆料" (netizen tips), and "外媒消息" (foreign media reports) to circumvent review systems, making it difficult for viewers to assess information authenticity.

**Sensationalism with unlabeled material**: Accounts on Douyin and Bilibili employed exaggerated headlines like "雷霆出击" (Thunder Strike) and "又打起来了" (Fighting Again), pairing them with stock footage or spliced images and videos to distort events and mislead the public.

## Escalating Enforcement

This action follows a broader [enforcement operation in May 2026](https://www.cac.gov.cn/2026-05/03/c_1779492291101867.htm), during which the CAC dealt with over 98,000 accounts across four violation categories including unlabeled sources, missing AI-generated content labels, and policy information without proper sourcing. While the May sweep was broader in scope, the July action is more narrowly focused on source labeling failures, suggesting a strategic shift toward sustained, targeted compliance checks.

The regulatory framework has been steadily tightened since 2023, when the CAC issued its comprehensive 13-point notice establishing requirements for everything from impersonation prevention to monetization restrictions. A subsequent [2025 Qinglang campaign](https://www.cac.gov.cn/2025-07/29/c_1755503642582366.htm) specifically targeted self-media accounts spreading misinformation and expanded source labeling requirements.

## Implications for Creators and Platforms

For self-media creators, the consequences of non-compliance are escalating. The CAC warned that "self-media creators should always adhere to the bottom line of content authenticity, abide by compliance operation standards, consciously and proactively label information sources in a standardized and accurate manner, and take responsibility for the information content they publish." Violations increasingly result in account suspension or permanent termination.

Platform companies — including ByteDance (Douyin), Kuaishou, Alibaba (Weibo), and Bilibili — bear the operational burden of implementing source labeling systems and policing compliance. The CAC directed platforms to "continuously optimize source labeling functions, establish long-term mechanisms for standardized labeling, and achieve full coverage of source labeling requirements." This places significant technical and administrative demands on platforms that collectively host millions of self-media accounts.

The crackdown also has implications for how international news circulates within China's domestic platforms. By targeting vague references to "foreign media reports" (外媒消息) as insufficient sourcing, the regulations effectively require self-media accounts to cite specific, identifiable sources rather than relying on opaque attributions. This could reshape how Chinese netizens access and discuss global current affairs, potentially limiting the reach of unverified international content while ensuring traceability for authorized sources.

The broader context is China's ongoing "Qinglang" (Clear and Bright) series of internet governance campaigns, which have progressively tightened controls over online content since 2021. The inclusion of AI-generated content labeling requirements in the May 2026 enforcement reflects growing official attention to the challenges posed by generative AI in content creation — a concern shared by regulators worldwide.

## What to Watch

As the CAC signals a shift from broad enforcement sweeps to sustained compliance monitoring, self-media operators face an increasingly stringent regulatory environment. Key questions remain about whether source labeling requirements will expand to cover all content types, how platforms will implement mandated "long-term mechanisms" for labeling, and how the rules will interact with the growing prevalence of AI-generated content on Chinese social media platforms.
