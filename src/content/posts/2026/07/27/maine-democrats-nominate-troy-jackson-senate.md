# Maine Democrats Nominate Troy Jackson for Senate

BANGOR, Maine — Maine Democrats formally nominated former state Senate President Troy Jackson as their candidate for U.S. Senate on Saturday, setting up a high-stakes November showdown against six-term Republican incumbent Susan Collins in what is expected to be one of the most competitive races of the 2026 midterm elections.

Jackson, a fifth-generation logger from Allagash, secured the nomination with 566 out of 571 delegate votes at a special nominating convention held at the Bangor Cross Insurance Center, according to [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/07/25/nx-s1-5902982/democrats-maine-senate-race). The near-unanimous vote capped an abbreviated and tumultuous replacement process triggered by the withdrawal of the original Democratic nominee, Graham Platner, earlier this month.

## A Nomination Forged in Crisis

Platner won the Democratic primary on June 9 with over 150,000 votes — more than any candidate in Maine Democratic primary history — but suspended his campaign on July 8 after a woman he once dated accused him of rape. Platner has denied the allegation. The [Bangor Daily News](https://www.bangordailynews.com/2026/07/21/politics/elections/maine-election-senate-troy-jackson-anger-temper/) reported that Jackson's rapid consolidation of party support, including pledges from more than 450 of 500 county delegates during weekend meetings, effectively cleared the field of potential challengers.

Under Maine law, the party replaced Platner through a convention of 571 delegates rather than a new statewide primary — a process the Collins campaign has criticized as undemocratic. Collins campaign spokesperson Shawn Roderick said in a [statement](https://www.themainewire.com/2026/07/collins-campaign-opens-general-election-with-sharp-criticism-of-democratic-nominee-troy-jackson/) that Jackson was "appointed to run for the Senate by fewer than three-tenths of one percent of its primary voters."

## Jackson: From Logger to Progressive Standard-Bearer

Jackson, who served as president of the Maine Senate from 2018 to 2024 and spent over two decades in the Maine Legislature, is running on an unapologetically progressive platform that includes Medicare for All, expanded union organizing rights, federalizing abortion rights, and raising taxes on the wealthiest Americans and corporations. As [The Maine Wire](https://www.themainewire.com/2026/07/troy-jackson-wins-democratic-senate-nomination-setting-up-november-showdown-with-susan-collins/) reported, Jackson drew repeatedly on his background as a logger and his leadership during protests by Maine loggers against Canadian timber companies, telling delegates: "We're only made to feel powerless when we stand alone."

Jackson's political evolution is also a notable feature of his candidacy. Once known as one of the Legislature's few openly pro-life Democrats, he has shifted significantly leftward in recent years on issues including abortion rights and LGBTQ protections. State Sen. Stacy Brenner defended this evolution, telling NPR: "What I find compelling about legislators and policymakers is when they are able to evolve with the thinking of where their heart is, where their mind is."

However, Jackson's confrontational leadership style has drawn scrutiny. Reports in the Washington Post and CNN detailed incidents including a 2021 episode in which Jackson threw a water bottle during a heated argument over prescription drug pricing, and a 2024 meeting with then-House Speaker Rachel Talbot Ross that grew so heated she kicked him out of her office, as the Bangor Daily News reported. The Collins campaign has seized on these reports, with Roderick framing the race as a choice between Collins' "record of accomplishment" and what he called Jackson's "angry sound bites and far-left ideology."

## The Collins Factor

Susan Collins, first elected in 1996, is seeking a sixth term and chairs the powerful Senate Appropriations Committee. She has consistently defied political gravity in Maine, winning re-election by nine points in 2020 even as Democrat Joe Biden carried the state by a similar margin, according to [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_United_States_Senate_election_in_Maine). She reported $11 million in cash on hand, giving her a significant financial advantage over Jackson, who has raised more than $1 million since announcing his candidacy.

Yet Collins is widely viewed as the most vulnerable Republican incumbent in the country. She is the only GOP senator representing a state that Donald Trump did not win in any of his three presidential campaigns. Maine voted for Kamala Harris by approximately seven percentage points in 2024, and Democrats control the governorship, legislature, and both U.S. House seats. As [The New York Post](https://nypost.com/2026/07/25/us-news/who-is-troy-jackson-maines-swap-in-for-graham-platner-in-senate-race/) noted, Democrats are betting that Jackson's rural roots and populist economic message can connect with voters beyond the party's traditional base.

## National Stakes

Democrats need to net four seats to win back control of the Senate, and Maine stands as one of their top pickup opportunities. The race is expected to draw massive national spending from both parties. In his acceptance speech, Jackson sought to rally the progressive coalition that powered Platner's historic primary win, telling delegates: "If you believe our freedoms are worth defending, stand with us. And if you believe that Maine deserves a senator who will fight like hell for working class people, well stand with us."

Delegate Kaia De Vries, 22, captured the sentiment of many party activists, telling NPR: "This isn't about Graham Platner. This isn't even about Troy Jackson. This is about a movement of working-class Mainers."

With just over three months until Election Day on November 3, the race is set to become a national battleground that could determine the balance of power in the U.S. Senate. The question is whether Jackson can unite the progressive movement that Platner ignited, overcome a massive fundraising gap, and convince enough Maine voters to send a Democrat to the Senate for the first time since 1988.
