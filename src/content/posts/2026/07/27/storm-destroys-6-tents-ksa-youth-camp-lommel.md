# Storm Destroys 6 Tents at KSA Youth Camp; Community Rallies

A powerful gust of wind destroyed six of eight tents at a KSA Buggenhout youth camp in Lommel, Belgium, on Sunday evening, just hours before 70 children were scheduled to arrive. Remarkably, no injuries were reported despite 10 people being inside one of the tents when it was blown away.

Camp leaders worked through the night with help from local residents, neighboring youth organizations, and parents to rebuild the campsite. By Monday morning, the camp was ready to receive its young participants as originally planned.

## The Incident

The storm struck the campsite at Oude Maai, a recreational area in Lommel, Limburg province, in the early evening on Sunday, July 26. According to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/07/27/windvlaag-richt-zware-schade-aan-op-ksa-kamp-in-lommel/), the gust arrived suddenly, accompanied by heavy rain, flattening the camp within seconds.

Camp leader Rune Willems described the moment the storm hit. "We really didn't see the gust coming last night," Willems told VRT NWS. "Suddenly it started pouring heavily and after one gust everything was flattened."

Willems recounted the frightening moment when a tent containing 10 people was swept away. "We were in shock afterwards. In one tent, 10 people were sitting when it flew away. And after five seconds everything was over. Fortunately, no one was injured."

## Background: KSA and the Camp

KSA (Katholieke Studenten Actie, or Catholic Student Action) is a Flemish Catholic youth movement for boys and young men, one of the major traditional youth organizations in Flanders alongside Chiro and the Scouts. KSA Buggenhout, based in the East Flanders municipality of Buggenhout, had chosen the Oude Maai area in Lommel — a popular camping location near the Dutch border — for its annual summer camp.

Camp leaders had arrived on Saturday, July 25, to set up the site. By Sunday evening, the camp was nearly ready. The destruction of six of their eight large tents threatened to cancel the entire week-long activity.

## Community Rallies

What followed was an outpouring of community solidarity that turned the situation around. As word spread of the damage, help arrived from multiple directions. A local farmer donated a tent from another youth organization. Other youth groups in the area contributed additional tents. Fathers of several camp attendees arrived with welding equipment to repair bent tent poles.

The [De Lommelse Gazet](https://www.delommelsegazet.be/stevige-windvlaag-vernielt-tenten-kamp-ksa/) reported that leaders from nearby camps also offered assistance. The coordinated effort transformed what could have been a disaster into a testament to rural Belgian community spirit.

"We've already set up an extra tent. We were able to recover a few tents," Willems explained. "The farmer here happened to have a tent from another youth organization, and the rest we received from other youth organizations."

He added, "Fathers of several children also came by yesterday with welding equipment to repair the tent poles. That was super handy."

## Determination to Continue

Throughout Sunday night and into Monday morning, the camp leaders worked tirelessly to restore the campsite. Despite the shock of the incident and the scale of the damage, their determination never wavered.

"Some of the tents are truly destroyed. But in the end, we thought the damage would be greater," Willems said, acknowledging the material loss while emphasizing the positive outcome.

"Today we got up extra early to rebuild the camp so we can properly receive the 70 kids later. So it will definitely work out."

## Broader Context

The incident occurred amid unstable summer weather across Belgium. The Royal Meteorological Institute (KMI) had activated a heat plan warning for the coming days due to temperatures expected to exceed 30°C, but localized severe thunderstorms with strong wind gusts occurred intermittently. The storm at the KSA camp was described as short but fierce, with the sun emerging again shortly after it passed.

July and August are peak summer camp season for Flemish youth movements, and weather-related incidents during this period are not uncommon. However, the speed and effectiveness of the community response highlighted the strong social networks that support youth organizations across Flanders.

## Looking Ahead

With the camp now operational as planned, KSA Buggenhout's 70 young campers will enjoy their week in Lommel as scheduled. The organizers, having faced one of the worst-case scenarios for an outdoor camp and overcome it through sheer determination and community backing, have ensured that this year's camp will be remembered not for the storm, but for the resilience that followed.

While the material damage to tents and equipment represents a significant loss for a volunteer-run organization, the incident ultimately became a powerful demonstration of community solidarity in rural Belgium.
