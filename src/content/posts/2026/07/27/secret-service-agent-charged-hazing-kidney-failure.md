# Secret Service Agent Charged in Near-Fatal Fraternity Hazing

A U.S. Secret Service agent and two other men have been arrested in connection with a brutal four-day fraternity hazing ritual that left a University of Miami student with kidney failure and requiring emergency medical treatment, according to the [Miami-Dade State Attorney’s Office](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/us-news/secret-service-agent-charged-fatal-hazing-canes-paddles-led-kidney-fai-rcna589409).

Marquez Christopher Pinder, 29, a Secret Service agent assigned to the agency’s Miami Field Office, served as "dean of pledges" for the Kappa Alpha Psi fraternity at the University of Miami during the hazing. He has been charged alongside Jared Lamar James, 26, and Elijah Delano Dyous, 29. All three face counts of attempted manslaughter with a deadly weapon, aggravated battery with a deadly weapon, and hazing with a deadly weapon.

## The Hazing Ritual

According to arrest warrants, the hazing took place over four days in early April 2026 at two separate locations — an apartment in Sweetwater and a residence in unincorporated Southwest Miami-Dade County. Prosecutors said pledges were subjected to "prolonged and repeated beatings with canes and paddles" after failing to answer fraternity-related questions correctly.

One victim, identified only as Victim 1, suffered catastrophic injuries. Arrest documents describe his backside as "blackened by the presence of necrotic skin/tissue," requiring extensive skin grafts. He was diagnosed with kidney failure that doctors determined would have been fatal without immediate medical care. The victim continues to experience "extreme pain and discomfort," with his mobility and quality of life "significantly impacted," according to the warrants, which were detailed by NBC News.

A second victim initially denied being part of the hazing but admitted to seeking medical care for similar injuries to his backside. Investigators said the external and internal injuries matched those of Victim 1 and were consistent with severe beatings.

## Official Response

The Secret Service placed Pinder on administrative leave immediately upon learning of the charges. Special Agent in Charge Michael Townsend confirmed the agency is "fully cooperating with the investigation," as reported by [NBC Miami](https://www.nbcmiami.com/news/local/secret-service-agent-2-others-arrested-in-hazing-incident-in-sweetwater-sw-miami-dade/3838780/).

Miami-Dade State Attorney Katherine Fernandez Rundle condemned the violence, drawing attention to Florida’s long history of deadly hazing. "Among some, fraternity hazing might be seen as a tradition creating a sense of belonging and brotherhood, but since 2001, hazing in Florida has killed Chad Meredith, Robert Champion and Andrew Coffey while seriously injuring others," she said in a statement. "This case, with the victims requiring hospitalization, provides clear-cut examples of hazing’s danger."

The national president of Kappa Alpha Psi, Jimmy McMikle, issued a strong statement disavowing the suspects, saying they "do not have an official affiliation with any chapter nor any official role with the organization." He emphasized that "Kappa Alpha Psi is a non-hazing fraternity" and said the organization is cooperating fully with law enforcement.

## Florida’s Troubled History With Hazing

The case has reignited attention on Florida’s struggle to combat hazing despite multiple legal reforms. The Chad Meredith Act of 2005, signed by then-Governor Jeb Bush, made clear that a victim’s consent is not a defense and that hazing does not need to occur at an official fraternity event to be illegal. Andrew’s Law of 2019, passed after Florida State student Andrew Coffey died from forced alcohol consumption, provides immunity to the first person who calls 911 and makes hazing that causes permanent damage a crime, as explained in the [NBC Miami background report](https://www.nbcmiami.com/news/local/what-is-hazing-a-look-at-when-fraternity-initiation-rituals-violate-florida-law/3838926/).

Under current Florida law, hazing that results in permanent injury, serious bodily injury, or death is a third-degree felony. The charges in this case — attempted manslaughter with a deadly weapon — indicate prosecutors believe the severity of the beatings could have been fatal.

## Implications and Questions

The involvement of an active-duty Secret Service agent in such a violent crime raises questions about off-duty conduct monitoring within the agency, which has faced previous scrutiny over workplace culture. How Pinder maintained a role as "dean of pledges" at a university fraternity while serving as a federal agent remains unclear.

The suspects remained in custody as of Monday, though Pinder was placed on house arrest after posting bond. All three are expected to face court proceedings in the coming weeks. It was not immediately clear if they had retained attorneys.

The case also raises broader questions about fraternity oversight at the University of Miami and whether additional individuals may face charges. The FBI has been notified of the investigation.

As the legal process unfolds, this incident stands as the latest grim reminder that despite legislative reforms, hazing remains a persistent and dangerous problem on American college campuses.
