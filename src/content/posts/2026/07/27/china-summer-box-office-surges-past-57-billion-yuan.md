# China Summer Box Office Surges Past 57 Billion Yuan

As of midnight on July 27, 2026, China's summer box office has surpassed **57 billion yuan (¥5.7 billion)** including presales, according to data from network platforms reported by [CCTV News](https://news.cctv.com/2026/07/27/ARTIdZxJHLkuxsuOSRTzpzV2260727.shtml). The annual total box office has also exceeded **214 billion yuan (¥21.4 billion)**, signaling robust momentum for the country's film industry recovery.

## Context: A Rebounding Industry

China's film sector has been on a steady recovery trajectory following pandemic-era disruptions. The summer 2026 box office milestone builds on earlier gains: data from the first half of the year showed total box office exceeding 262 billion yuan, a 52.8% year-on-year increase. The summer season, running from June through August, is traditionally the most lucrative period for Chinese cinemas, and this year has proven no exception with over **120 Chinese and foreign films** scheduled for release.

## Comedy Films Dominate the Summer Lineup

The 2026 summer season is notable for the overwhelming dominance of comedy films across multiple sub-genres. Leading the charge is **Stephen Chow's *Kung Fu Women's Football*** (功夫女足), the director's first film in seven years. Released on July 11, the sports comedy earned 260 million yuan on its opening day and followed with 240 million yuan on its second day, propelling the daily box office above 300 million yuan on consecutive days. As of July 27, the film's total box office has exceeded **1.9 billion yuan**, with analysts upgrading their forecasts to over 2.5 billion yuan, as reported by the [Eastmoney Research Center](https://finance.eastmoney.com/a/202607163808801378.html).

Another major contributor is the animated comedy ***The Eight Immortals!*** (八仙！), which features a distinctive green-blue landscape painting aesthetic inspired by classical Eastern art. The film, which follows the adventures of eight ordinary mortals, earned over 600 million yuan in just eight days following its advanced release on July 18. Its success has prompted the producers to announce international releases in Oceania, North America, and Europe.

The sentimental comedy ***Love Letter to Grandma*** (给阿嬷的情书) continues to perform strongly, with its global total surpassing 2.1 billion yuan.

## Policy Support Bolsters the Market

On July 3, the National Film Bureau and the State Administration for Market Regulation jointly issued a landmark 10-measure policy titled the "Notice on Promoting the Diversified Operations of Cinemas and Thriving Cinema Culture." The measures encourage cinemas to integrate diverse business formats, strengthen brand operations, and transform into comprehensive cultural experience spaces combining viewing, socializing, entertainment, and consumption.

## Market Momentum and Economic Implications

The summer box office surged dramatically from **34.65 billion yuan on July 16** to **57 billion yuan on July 27** — an increase of over 22 billion yuan in just 11 days. This acceleration was primarily driven by the release of *The Eight Immortals!* and sustained strong performance of *Kung Fu Women's Football*.

The strong box office performance has boosted China's film industry stocks, with the film concept stock index on the A-share market seeing a significant recovery in July. Securities firms including Wanlian Securities and Zheshang Securities have noted ample elasticity for summer box office growth, pointing to a dense lineup of upcoming releases.

## What's Next

Looking ahead, the summer schedule remains packed with highly anticipated titles. The comedy sequel *年会不能停2!* is set for release on August 7, followed by major imports including ***Spider-Man: Fresh New Day*** and Christopher Nolan's ***The Odyssey***. With this lineup, the summer box office is expected to continue its upward trajectory.

The international expansion of *The Eight Immortals!* also represents a notable milestone for Chinese animation, following the global success of earlier titles like *Ne Zha* and *Nezha 2*, signaling growing ambitions for Chinese cultural exports.
