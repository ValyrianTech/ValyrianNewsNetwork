# Nanjing's Upstairs-Downstairs AI Commercialization Hub

In Nanjing's Yuhuatai District, a novel approach to artificial intelligence development is taking shape—one where companies develop products on upper floors and showcase them on the ground floor of the same building. The “AI·镜界” (AI Mirror World) Artificial Intelligence Ecological Block, as reported by [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/tech/20260727/d617ca10b5c0482d8eeec8b10dcf644c/c.html), is accelerating the cycle from R&D to commercialization by collapsing the distance between innovation and the marketplace.

## Context: A District Built for AI

The AI Mirror World block, spanning 1.96 square kilometers across Yuhuatai District, is part of China’s broader push to transform its software industry into an AI powerhouse. The district sits within the China (Nanjing) Software Valley, one of the country’s largest software clusters, which recorded an estimated 350 billion yuan in software revenue in 2025. According to [Baidu Baike](https://baike.baidu.com/item/AI%E9%95%9C%E7%95%8C/66941382), the block has already attracted 402 AI-related enterprises employing over 50,000 practitioners, achieving an AI core output value of 18 billion yuan and an associated output value of 29 billion yuan by the end of 2025.

## Key Developments: Where Technology Meets the Market

At the heart of the block lies the “AI·模坊” Intelligent Agent Hub Center, a marketplace that connects developers with buyers. Since launching services in November 2025, the center has onboarded over 200 enterprises and nearly 300 intelligent agent products, successfully facilitating more than 300 transactions totaling nearly 70 million yuan (approximately $9.6 million USD).

“Through this model, we hope to let people with technology find application scenarios here, and people with demand scenarios find technology here,” said Teng Liang, Deputy General Manager of Nanjing Software Valley Scene Innovation Development Co., Ltd., as reported by Xinhua News.

The hub’s “Life Hall” demonstrates how AI is permeating everyday life. Visitors can try exoskeleton devices that recognize wearers’ movement intentions, play Gomoku against an AI-powered robot, or dine at an AI-themed restaurant. Local resident Mr. Chen, visiting with his nine-year-old son, told Xinhua: “I had heard of this place before, but seeing so many AI applications in one go is really fascinating.”

## Institutional Innovation: A New Model of AI Governance

What distinguishes the Yuhuatai model is not just the physical co-location of R&D and exhibition space, but the institutional scaffolding surrounding it. On January 27, 2026, the district established the Yuhuatai District AI Development Bureau—the first district-level AI government agency in Jiangsu Province with dedicated administrative staff. The bureau immediately released a “2026 Action Plan to Build an AI Industry Highland” and a package of ten incentive policies covering computing power subsidies, talent recruitment, and scene development.

This institutional innovation reflects a growing trend among Chinese local governments to create specialized agencies capable of coordinating AI industry policy across departments. The bureau’s mandate includes issuing “computing power vouchers,” establishing dedicated funds, and opening public-sector application scenarios to AI companies—a comprehensive approach designed to accelerate adoption.

The block also hosts an AMD ROCm Lab, one of the chipmaker’s three global innovation hubs. Its presence signals continued international semiconductor engagement with China’s AI ecosystem despite ongoing US-China technology tensions.

## Ambitious Growth Targets

The AI Mirror World block has set aggressive targets for the 2027–2030 period: attracting 1,000 AI enterprises, employing 100,000 AI professionals, launching 500 vertical AI products—including industry-specific models and agents—and achieving a core output value of 100 billion yuan with an associated output value of 300 billion yuan. For 2026 alone, the district targets AI core revenue exceeding 35 billion yuan, according to the action plan released by the new AI Development Bureau.

## Forward Look: A Template for China’s AI Future

The “development upstairs, exhibition downstairs” model represents more than a real estate innovation—it is a microcosm of China’s strategy to accelerate AI commercialization by bridging the gap between research and real-world application. By concentrating talent, infrastructure, institutional support, and market access in a single geographic area, Yuhuatai District is creating a template that could be replicated across other Chinese cities pursuing AI-driven economic transformation.

As the block continues to grow, the key metrics to watch will be whether its transaction volumes and enterprise growth rates can sustain their current trajectory, and whether the model proves as effective in practice as it appears on paper. The establishment of the AI Development Bureau suggests that local government is prepared to provide the sustained institutional support needed to turn ambitious targets into reality.
