# China Unveils 15th Five-Year Plan to Build Sports Powerhouse

China has released two landmark policy documents that signal a historic elevation of sports in national strategy: the 15th Five-Year Plan for Building a Sports Powerhouse and the National Fitness Plan 2026–2030. Approved by the State Council and published on July 22, the plans mark the first time a Chinese sports development framework has been designated as a "national-level special plan," reflecting what officials describe as an unprecedented prioritization of sports in the country's development agenda.

According to [People's Daily](http://health.people.com.cn/n1/2026/0727/c14739-40768370.html), the 15th Five-Year Plan consists of 11 parts, 38 key tasks, and 8 supporting special projects covering venue construction, sports event layout, outdoor sports industry upgrades, and sports technology innovation.

## A Historic First for Chinese Sports

The elevation of sports planning to a national-level special plan represents a fundamental shift in how Beijing views the role of athletics and fitness in society. No longer treated primarily as recreation or competition, sports are now positioned as a strategic tool for public health, economic growth, cultural soft power, and international diplomacy.

As the [State Council](https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/202607/content_7076376.htm) noted in its official policy interpretation, this is the first time sports planning has been included in national-level special planning. "It reflects the Party and state elevating sports to an unprecedented important position," a National Sports Administration official stated.

The previous 14th Five-Year Plan period (2021–2025) saw China "enter the ranks of world sports powers and Olympic powerhouses," according to the National Sports Administration. The 15th Five-Year Plan period (2026–2030) is now defined as a critical phase for "comprehensively accelerating and improving the quality of sports power building."

## Five Key Targets for 2030

The plan sets five ambitious development indicators to be achieved by 2030:

- **Per capita sports venue area:** approximately 4 square meters
- **Regular exercise participation:** approximately 40% of the population
- **National physique excellence rate:** 31.1%
- **World championships:** among the top ranks globally
- **Total sports industry scale:** exceed 7 trillion yuan (approximately USD 960 billion)

The 7 trillion yuan target represents nearly a doubling from 2023's 3.7 trillion yuan, reflecting an aggressive growth trajectory for the sports economy. [Interface News](https://m.jiemian.com/article/14812454.html) reported that multiple investment banks, including Zhongyin Securities and CITIC, are broadly bullish on the sports industry as a new consumption pillar.

## Infrastructure: Smaller, Closer, More Accessible

A notable shift in the new plans is the emphasis on community-level fitness infrastructure. Rather than continuing to build large centralized sports venues, the policy focuses on creating "small but beautiful" fitness spaces in neighborhoods.

Over the next five years, China plans to build or renovate more than 5,000 small and micro sports parks, fitness centers, multi-sport fields, and public ice rinks. The initiative will also develop 100 high-quality outdoor sports destinations and continue encouraging schools, government agencies, and enterprises to open their sports facilities to the public.

Bao Mingxiao, chief expert at the China Sports Development Research Institute at Beijing Sport University, explained that after municipal and district-level large fitness centers have established a foundation, "public resources and policy focus should tilt toward the community level. Let residents conveniently engage in scientific fitness near their homes after handling work and family affairs, integrating fitness into daily life."

## Scientific Fitness and New Social Realities

The plans mark a transition from simply ensuring citizens have a place to exercise to ensuring they exercise scientifically. Key provisions include AI-powered fitness development, scientific fitness guidance services, and expanded availability of physical fitness testing and exercise capacity assessment.

Significantly, the National Fitness Plan for the first time addresses the fitness needs of China's growing gig economy workforce — delivery drivers, ride-hailing drivers, and live-streamers — by requiring accessible fitness facilities at workplaces and rest stops. The plans also emphasize facility retrofitting for elderly and disabled populations, and targeted health interventions for youth dealing with rising rates of myopia, obesity, and mental health challenges.

## Economic Implications and Industry Outlook

The 7 trillion yuan target positions the sports industry as a potential leading service sector in China's economy. Key growth drivers identified in the plan include the event economy, outdoor sports (mountain, water sports, ice/snow, and low-altitude sports), technology integration (smart venues, AI-powered training), and the silver economy (targeted programs for China's aging population).

The plan also calls for the expansion of local "Super League" models that have proven successful at the city level, and the development of brand-name outdoor sports events and tourism routes.

## Implementation and Oversight

The National Sports Administration will serve as the lead coordinating body, working with the National Development and Reform Commission, the Ministry of Finance, and the Ministry of Natural Resources. The plan includes mechanisms for dynamic monitoring, mid-term evaluation, and final assessment to ensure accountability at local government levels.

"The sports bureau will make overall coordination, strengthen inter-ministerial collaboration, and clarify the responsibilities of local sports administrative departments and relevant institutions," the National Sports Administration Policy and Regulation Department head stated.

## What to Watch

As China pursues its vision of becoming a global sports powerhouse by 2035, several key questions remain: How will the 5,000+ new facilities be distributed between urban and rural areas? What specific budget allocations accompany the plan? And how effectively will compliance and monitoring be enforced at local government levels?

For now, the release of these two documents signals that China is betting big on sports — not just as a matter of national pride and international prestige, but as a cornerstone of public health, economic growth, and social well-being in the decade ahead.
