# China Rescues 47 Vietnamese Nationals After Shipwreck

Chinese authorities have rescued 47 Vietnamese nationals whose cargo vessel sank in the South China Sea near Yongshu Reef (Fiery Cross Reef) in the Spratly Islands, according to [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/world/20260727/80da310ccb1847bdb891d92b9e55f823/c.html). The multinational operation, which began after the vessel went down on the night of July 24, involved multiple Chinese maritime assets working in coordination with Vietnamese authorities and represents one of the largest humanitarian rescue efforts in the disputed waters in recent years.

## Vessel and Crew

The Vietnamese-registered cargo ship KHOI NGUYEN 18 was carrying 62 crew members — all Vietnamese nationals — when it encountered distress in poor weather conditions and sank near Yongshu Reef. The vessel measured 69.9 meters in length with a beam of 10.8 meters and a capacity of up to 999 tonnes, as reported by [CCTV News](https://news.cctv.com/2026/07/26/ARTIsmI1Kl2aPRlcf3CKKefF260726.shtml). Crew members evacuated onto life rafts and drifted with ocean currents toward the reef, where they were later discovered by Chinese rescue assets.

## Rescue Operation Timeline

The Chinese rescue vessel Nanhaijiu 115, operated by the Ministry of Transport's Nanhai Rescue Bureau, was the first to respond to the distress. On the evening of July 25 — approximately 24 hours after the sinking — the vessel spotted distress signals and discovered multiple Vietnamese nationals on life rafts near Yongshu Reef. In two separate operations conducted that evening, the Nanhaijiu 115 rescued 36 crew members.

China Coast Guard vessels Xuande and Nansha were also dispatched to the area after the Hainan Maritime Search and Rescue Center issued an alert. The coast guard ships conducted continuous searches throughout the following days, locating and rescuing additional survivors in open waters.

By the morning of July 26, Chinese state media reported that 39 individuals had been rescued, with 23 still unaccounted for. The number rose steadily as search operations continued through the day, reaching 44 rescued by the afternoon according to some reports. The final two survivors were located and rescued late on July 26 by the coast guard vessel Xuande, bringing the Chinese total to 47.

According to [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/7/26/eighteen-people-missing-in-south-china-sea-after-vietnamese-ship-sank), China deployed six vessels and a rescue helicopter to the search effort. Vietnam also dispatched its own assets to assist in the operation, and Vietnamese authorities independently rescued one additional crew member, though reporting on this figure varied across sources.

## Survivor Care and Handover

The two latest survivors were transferred to the Nanhaijiu 115 for proper medical accommodation and monitoring. Chinese authorities confirmed that all rescued individuals were in stable condition with normal vital signs. In a coordinated handover process, the rescued Vietnamese nationals were gradually transferred from Chinese vessels to Vietnamese vessels operating in the area. The Chinese crew of the Nanhaijiu 115 provided food, water, and medical care to the survivors during their time aboard.

## Search for the Missing

Despite the extensive multinational effort, at least 15 to 18 crew members remain unaccounted for, with figures varying across different reporting agencies during the dynamic operation. Search efforts continued as of July 27, with Chinese and Vietnamese authorities maintaining coordination in the ongoing operation. The final casualty count remains uncertain, and the search may continue for several more days.

## Humanitarian Cooperation Amid Geopolitical Tensions

The sinking occurred in the Spratly Islands (Nansha Qundao), a disputed archipelago in the South China Sea claimed by China, Vietnam, the Philippines, Taiwan, Malaysia, and Brunei. China has constructed artificial islands and military facilities on several features in the area, including Fiery Cross Reef, which has been a point of contention among claimant states.

This humanitarian operation stands in notable contrast to the broader geopolitical tensions in the region. In the same week, China and the Philippines experienced coast guard clashes in the South China Sea, and China conducted a long-range missile test into the Pacific Ocean that drew criticism from Australia, New Zealand, and Japan.

Despite these frictions, the rescue demonstrates that Chinese and Vietnamese authorities can cooperate effectively on maritime safety and humanitarian emergencies when circumstances require. Both sides provided regular public updates throughout the operation, with Vietnamese media including VOV and BBC Vietnamese reporting on the situation alongside Chinese state media — a measured approach that focused on the human impact rather than territorial dimensions.

## What to Watch

The focus now shifts to the final accounting of the missing crew members and the investigation into what caused the KHOI NGUYEN 18 to sink. Poor weather conditions are believed to have been a significant contributing factor, though the full circumstances surrounding the vessel's distress remain under review. The conclusion of search operations and successful transfer of all survivors to Vietnamese authorities will mark the final phase of this multinational rescue effort. Questions also remain about whether this demonstration of bilateral humanitarian cooperation could have any positive effect on the broader South China Sea dialogue between Hanoi and Beijing.
