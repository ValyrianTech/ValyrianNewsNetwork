# China's Cold Chain Innovation: Fresher, Cheaper Durians

For Chinese consumers, the once-extravagant "king of fruits" is finally becoming an everyday indulgence. Advanced cold chain logistics innovations are reshaping China's fruit supply chain, making imported durians significantly fresher and roughly 30% more affordable than in previous years, according to [CCTV News](https://news.cctv.com/2026/07/27/ARTINdEvY51xJINUzzvCI3zw260727.shtml).

"Fresher and more affordable, about 30% cheaper than before," said Yu Mengyuan, a shopper in a Chinese supermarket, reflecting a sentiment now common across the country. Store operations supervisor Feng Xuefeng added that fruit loss rates have dropped by nearly 20%, logistics costs have fallen by approximately 15%, and retail sales volumes have surged by 30%.

## The Infrastructure Behind the Price Drop

China's cold chain logistics sector has undergone a dramatic transformation. As of the end of June 2026, the nation's total cold storage capacity reached 270 million cubic meters, up 4.6% year-on-year. The country now operates 105 national backbone cold chain logistics bases spanning eastern, central, and western regions. Meanwhile, the national fleet of refrigerated vehicles has grown to 604,000 units, a 12.8% increase from the previous year.

Perhaps the most impactful development has been the expansion of international cold chain railway services. Yunnan province opened a new international cold chain train route this year, with peak-season frequency reaching approximately six trains per day. Customs clearance times have been compressed from three to five days to as fast as 20 hours.

"The customs clearance time for imported fruit has been reduced from the original three to five days to the fastest 20 hours or so," Zhou Jie, Deputy Director of the Foreign Cooperation Department at China Railway Kunming Bureau, told CCTV News.

## The China-Laos Railway: A Game Changer for Durian Trade

The China-Laos Railway has emerged as a critical artery for durian imports. Durians now arrive in Kunming in just 26 hours and reach more than 30 cities across China within 48 hours, as reported by [Guangming Online](https://m.gmw.cn/2026-05/10/content_1304451450.htm). Train frequency has increased from two to six daily services during peak season.

Ouyang Bing, General Manager of China Logistics Group's Railway Supply Chain Technology Company, confirmed that his company has transported 557 containers of durians via the China-Laos Railway, a 50% increase year-on-year, with approximately 7,000 TEUs projected annually. Ren Zhengwei, Deputy General Manager of China Railway Container Kunming Branch, noted that the full cold chain rail service now delivers from Thai orchards to the Chinese market in under three days — down from seven to ten days by road — with loss rates falling by 10% and transport costs dropping by 15%.

The impact on trade volumes has been staggering. In the first two months of 2026, Yunnan ports alone imported durians worth 2.05 billion yuan (approximately $283 million), a 351.6% increase year-on-year, according to Kunming Customs data.

## Multiple Suppliers, Falling Prices

Supply-side dynamics are also working in consumers' favor. Thailand, the traditional dominant supplier, produced 1.6825 million tons of durians in 2025, with approximately 55% exported to China. Production is projected to reach 1.781 million tons in 2026, a 16% increase.

Meanwhile, Vietnam is expanding rapidly. According to [Jiangnan Metropolis Daily](https://jndsb.jxnews.com.cn/system/2026/05/18/031134044.shtml), Can Tho province's durian acreage surged from 2,500 hectares in 2022 to approximately 15,000 hectares in 2026 — a fivefold increase. Cambodia, the Philippines, and Malaysia have also gained fresh durian export access to China, creating a competitive market that benefits consumers.

## Greening the Cold Chain

China's cold chain transformation extends beyond speed and scale — it is also becoming more sustainable. New-energy refrigerated trucks are entering batch production, offering significant cost savings. Zhao Yansong, an engineer at a new-energy refrigerated truck manufacturer, told CCTV News that electric refrigerated vehicles save over one yuan (about $0.14) per kilometer compared to diesel alternatives. Government purchase subsidies and favorable urban access policies for new-energy vehicles are accelerating the transition.

Kang Min, Deputy Director of the Economic and Trade Department at China's National Development and Reform Commission (NDRC), outlined the government's vision. "We will further improve the cold chain logistics facility system, focusing on national backbone cold chain logistics bases to drive the development of production and sales cold chain distribution centers and end-point cold chain logistics facilities," Kang told CCTV News. The NDRC is aligning these efforts with China's upcoming 15th Five-Year Plan for modern logistics (2026-2030).

## Domestic Durian Cultivation: A Long-Term Ambition

While imported durians dominate the market, China continues to pursue domestic cultivation. Efforts date back to 1958, with a major breakthrough in Hainan province in 2019. In April 2026, a naturally ripened Jinzhen durian weighing 3.7 kilograms was harvested in Yunnan's Honghe Prefecture after eight years of cultivation, Guangming Online reported.

Ao Pingxing, Director of the Durian Industry Research Center at Yunnan Agricultural University, noted that more than 70 durian varieties are under research. "With technological progress, we can ultimately grow high-quality domestic durians," he said. However, experts caution that domestic production remains small-scale and higher-cost than imports, with full commercial production likely five to eight years away.

## What's Next

China's cold chain logistics revolution is far from complete. With the 15th Five-Year Plan for modern logistics on the horizon, further infrastructure investment and green technology adoption are expected. For the average consumer, the trend points in one direction: fresher fruit, lower prices, and a broader selection of imported produce year-round.

As Feng Xuefeng, the store operations supervisor, summarized: "Quality has improved, and our sales have increased by 30%." For China's durian-loving consumers, that equation appears to be working in everyone's favor.
