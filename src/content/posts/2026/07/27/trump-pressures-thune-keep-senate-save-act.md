# Trump Pressures Thune: Keep Senate In Session For SAVE Act

President Donald Trump escalated his confrontation with Senate Majority Leader John Thune on July 27, publicly demanding that the Senate cancel its upcoming August recess until the SAVE America Act is passed—or that Republicans terminate the legislative filibuster entirely. The demand, posted on Truth Social, marks the most forceful public pressure yet in a months-long intra-party struggle over voting rights legislation, according to [USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/politics/2026/07/27/trump-thune-tension-save-america-act/91061245007/).

## What Is the SAVE America Act?

The Safeguard American Voter Eligibility Act (SAVE Act) would impose strict proof-of-citizenship and photo-ID requirements for federal elections. The legislation would require voters to present a passport or birth certificate when registering and valid photo identification before casting ballots in person, while also scaling back mail-in voting options. The House passed the bill on a mostly party-line vote of 218-213 in February 2026, but it has since stalled in the Senate.

Voting rights experts estimate the bill could disenfranchise over 20 million eligible American voters who lack ready access to citizenship documents. The Associated Press reports that about one in ten Americans does not have readily available proof of citizenship.

## Trump’s Ultimatum

In his Truth Social post, Trump wrote: “John Thune should not allow the United States Senate to ‘leave town’ until it passes The Save America Act or, far better still, TERMINATES THE FILIBUSTER, where Republicans can then quickly pass everything they ever dreamed of, including a full and deep throated SAVE AMERICA ACT, the Budget, and the ever looming Debt Ceiling disaster, 1929!”

The President added a sharp warning: “The Dumocrats will do it on day one, and can’t believe how lucky they got with this Senate leadership. Remember, stupidity always brings LOSING & DEATH!”

Speaking to reporters on Air Force One the same day, Trump softened his tone slightly, saying he has a “good relationship” with Thune but that the Majority Leader “has to get his job done.” When asked about replacing Thune, Trump said, “I haven’t even thought of it.”

## The Senate Math Problem

Republicans hold a 53-47 Senate majority, but most legislation requires 60 votes to overcome the filibuster. All 47 Democrats oppose the SAVE Act, and four Senate Republicans—Susan Collins (ME), Mitch McConnell (KY), Lisa Murkowski (AK), and Thom Tillis (NC)—voted against adding the bill to a budget package in June. This leaves supporters short of the necessary 60 votes.

Thune has defended his approach, firing back at the White House after Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt said Trump’s “patience is running out.” “Maybe she or somebody else ought to get on the phone and get the votes,” Thune told reporters. “Instead of pointing the finger at Republicans, they might think about going after the people who are stopping it on the floor, which is the Democrats.”

## Filibuster Divide

The confrontation has reopened a deep philosophical divide within the Republican conference over the Senate filibuster. Trump has called for its elimination, arguing that a simple majority would allow Republicans to pass their entire agenda. But Thune and most Senate Republicans have resisted, with Thune calling the filibuster a “bad idea” to end and saying it “has protected this country.”

Sen. Thom Tillis (R-NC), who supports the SAVE Act itself, made his position clear: “I’m not going to nuke the filibuster.”

Some Republicans have proposed a “talking filibuster” as a middle ground—requiring Democrats to speak continuously to block the bill—but this approach faces procedural hurdles and would still require a 60-vote threshold to pass.

## Budget Reconciliation as a Workaround

House Speaker Mike Johnson advanced a $95 billion budget framework through the House on July 22 that includes $10 billion for voting-related changes aligned with the SAVE Act. Using budget reconciliation would allow Republicans to bypass the filibuster with a simple majority. However, Thune has said Republicans don’t currently have the votes for that approach either, and the Senate parliamentarian could strike down provisions deemed non-budgetary.

## What’s at Stake

The standoff comes as the Senate races toward its scheduled August recess beginning August 7, with plans to address government funding, cryptocurrency legislation, and Russia sanctions. Trump’s demand threatens to derail that agenda entirely.

Sen. Mike Lee (R-UT), who has led the push for the SAVE Act in the Senate, warned that how Republicans handle the bill “could impact not only the November midterm elections, but also the Senate GOP leadership elections”—a pointed threat to Thune’s position.

The fight is unfolding against the backdrop of the 2026 midterm elections, where Republicans are defending slim majorities in both chambers. Several GOP senators face competitive races, including Collins in Maine, Jon Husted in Ohio, and Dan Sullivan in Alaska, while broader headwinds—including an unpopular war in Iran and surging gas prices—complicate the party’s prospects.

## Looking Ahead

With the August 7 recess deadline fast approaching, Thune faces an increasingly difficult choice: defy Trump and risk a leadership challenge, or find a path to pass legislation that currently lacks the votes. Senate Republicans are scheduled to meet in weekly policy luncheons that could determine the next steps.

If the SAVE Act fails to advance before recess, the issue will almost certainly become a defining campaign theme heading into November, energizing both parties’ bases. The broader question of whether the filibuster survives this fight may have implications that extend far beyond the current legislative session.
