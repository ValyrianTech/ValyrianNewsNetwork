# Trump in Michigan: Defending Economic Policies Amid Tariffs

President Donald Trump visited a General Motors facility in Milford, Michigan, on Monday to tout his handling of the economy, arriving in a crucial swing state on the same day a new border bridge opened with Canada and as new 50% tariffs on Canadian goods threaten to disrupt the state's manufacturing backbone. The visit, his third to Michigan in his second term, comes with early voting already underway for the state's Aug. 4 primary and amid growing voter dissatisfaction with the president's economic stewardship.

## Tariffs and Trade Tensions

On July 20, Trump signed three proclamations imposing additional 50% tariffs on a broad range of Canadian goods, including motor vehicles, alcohol, and dairy products, under the rarely used Section 338 of the Tariff Act of 1930. According to [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/20/trump-tariffs-canada-trade.html), the tariffs, which have gone unused since 1949, are set to take effect on August 19 — just weeks before the November midterm elections. A senior Trump administration official said Canada "has to be held accountable for this continued discrimination" against American exports.

The Supreme Court previously struck down many of Trump's sweeping tariffs, but his administration has sought alternative legal authorities to press forward with trade actions against Canada, arguing the country discriminates against U.S. products including automobiles.

## Michigan's Economic Exposure

No state is more closely tied to Canada through integrated supply chains than Michigan, according to supply chain experts. The [Detroit Free Press](https://www.freep.com/story/news/politics/2026/07/24/trump-touts-new-canadian-tariffs-even-as-canada-opens-new-bridge-to-detroit/91039282007/) reported that roughly 180,000 to 190,000 commercial trucks cross between Michigan and Canada each month, carrying auto parts, raw materials, and consumer goods.

Jason Miller, a supply chain management professor at Michigan State University, told [Spectrum Local News](https://spectrumlocalnews.com/mi/michigan/news/2026/07/22/michigan-tariff-canada-trade-) that Michigan exports more to Canada per worker than almost anywhere else in the country. Miller noted that tariffs create a chain reaction for businesses: "You're forcing small businesses to grapple with hiring trade attorneys. That's money that instead could have gone into launching new products or expanding their business."

Contrary to the administration's claims that tariffs bring manufacturing jobs back, Miller said Michigan has fewer manufacturing jobs today than it did a year ago. "We have less manufacturing employment today than we did last year at this time," he said, pointing to federal employment data.

## The Gordie Howe Bridge Opening

Trump's visit coincided with the official opening of the $4.7 billion Gordie Howe International Bridge connecting Detroit and Windsor, Ontario. A joint U.S.-Canada opening ceremony was scrapped after Trump announced the new tariffs. Canada instead held its own celebration on July 24.

Trump responded on Truth Social, writing: "Canada disinvited the United States of America to the opening of the Gordie Howe Bridge, which is fine, considering they are paying substantial TARIFFS to the United States." He claimed to have renegotiated the deal so the U.S. now receives 50% of the profit, though Canada paid upfront for the bridge's construction costs and is to be repaid through toll revenue.

## Political Stakes in a Battleground State

Michigan, which backed Biden in 2020 before flipping to Trump in 2024, remains one of the most closely watched swing states. The Aug. 4 primary features contentious races in both parties, including a GOP gubernatorial contest between Trump-backed Rep. John James and businessman Perry Johnson. Republican Mike Rogers is unopposed in the Senate primary, while the Democratic Senate primary pits Rep. Haley Stevens against progressive Abdul El-Sayed.

According to [AP News](https://apnews.com/article/trump-michigan-tariffs-economy-midterms-gm-7afc50b1ec74988e63880947efe0b818), the White House says Trump will highlight manufacturing job gains and foreign investment. "The President pledged to put Michigan workers back to work, and he's doing it," White House spokesperson Liz Huston said in a statement.

However, former Michigan Republican Party executive director Jason Roe warned that tying Trump too closely to top Republicans before the general election could backfire, calling it "kryptonite to November voters." Michigan Democratic Party chair Curtis Hertel welcomed the visit, saying: "It's good for us every time the president is here because he's incredibly unpopular."

## Broader Economic Headwinds

Trump's approval ratings have sagged as inflation remains high, gas prices rise again, and the war in Iran escalates. Voters across the country are increasingly dissatisfied with Trump's handling of the economy, according to AP polling. These kitchen table issues could prove decisive in the November midterms, where Republicans are fighting to hold control of Congress.

## What's Next

All eyes are now on the Aug. 4 primary results, which will serve as an early test of Trump's electoral influence in Michigan. Whether the 50% tariffs take effect as scheduled on August 19 or whether last-minute negotiations produce a deal remains uncertain. Ontario Premier Doug Ford has already signaled Canada will respond "tariff for tariff, dollar for dollar" if the duties proceed, setting the stage for further escalation in North American trade relations.

As MSU's Miller put it: "As the domestic auto sector goes, that is how goes Michigan's overall economy." With tariffs threatening to disrupt that sector, the stakes for Michigan — and for Trump — could hardly be higher.
