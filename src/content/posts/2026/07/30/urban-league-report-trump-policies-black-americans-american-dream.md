# Urban League: American Dream Slips for Black Americans

WASHINGTON — The American Dream is further away for Black Americans than at any point since the Civil Rights Movement, according to the 50th annual "State of Black America" report released Thursday by the [National Urban League](https://nul.org/state-of-black-america). The report explicitly blames policy changes under President Donald Trump's administration for worsening economic and political prospects.

The report, titled "Is the American Dream Dead?," marks the 50th anniversary edition of a landmark publication first issued in 1976. According to a copy obtained by [The Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/black-america-report-national-urban-league-2026-40ab741f272a0e88cd00c895b0d66868), it documents rollbacks of long-sought civil rights policies through the overhaul of the Justice Department's Civil Rights Division, changes to the Equal Employment Opportunity Commission, and the Safeguard American Voter Eligibility Act.

"It may be the focus is us, but the impact is not just us," said Marc Morial, president and CEO of the National Urban League. "While they have targeted and focused on Black Americans, these attacks are going to impact broadly working Americans, poor Americans, aspirational middle-class Americans, and this is what this report points to."

## Context and Background

The "State of Black America" report was created in 1976 as a response to President Gerald R. Ford's State of the Union address and the Democratic response, neither of which mentioned the economic outlooks for Black Americans. The New York Times described the inaugural edition as "a profoundly depressing document." Several of its recommendations were later adopted by President Jimmy Carter.

Fifty years later, the new report echoes those early frustrations. Tracing a 250-year arc of American history, it describes Black Americans' struggle for emancipation and equal rights as a gruesome but optimistic part of the American story — now threatened by the current administration's policies.

## Key Developments

The report specifically criticizes the Trump administration's overhaul of the Justice Department's Civil Rights Division and the Equal Employment Opportunity Commission, as well as the push for the [SAVE Act](https://apnews.com/article/save-act-documents-requirements-citizenship-voting-congress-dfb43bcdd0255d3665da588a60286b4e), which would require documentary proof of citizenship for voter registration. Critics argue the law would disproportionately disenfranchise minority voters.

The report also condemns the administration's efforts to roll back diversity, equity, and inclusion initiatives in the private sector, describing them as an "economic assault" on Black Americans. Morial noted that while some companies have made only "cosmetic changes" to their DEI programs, others have been in "full and complete retreat" under administration pressure.

"This has been a campaign of coercion and oppression directed at these institutions who have been out here working hard to, if you will, change America," Morial said.

In contrast, the report praises former President Joe Biden's administration for what it calls "the most consequential federal investments in Black America since the Great Society," including the COVID-19 stimulus package, laws supporting minority small businesses, and the bipartisan infrastructure bill.

"Not every promise was kept, and the current administration is pushing to roll our wins back, but these wins serve as a blueprint for what is possible through sustained advocacy and a clear vision," the report's authors write.

## Report Contributors and Political Implications

This year's report draws contributions from several prominent Democratic figures, including U.S. Senators Angela Alsobrooks, Lisa Blunt Rochester, and Raphael Warnock, as well as Maryland Governor Wes Moore and Baltimore Mayor Brandon Scott. Moore, widely viewed as a potential 2028 presidential contender, submitted an essay focused on closing the racial wealth gap.

"With wisdom and grit, my mom was able to lift me and my family to a higher rung on the economic ladder," Moore wrote. "But too often wisdom and grit aren't enough."

The report's release comes ahead of the 2026 midterm elections, adding political weight to its findings. Morial urged Americans to respond at the ballot box and with their pocketbooks, saying, "We cannot be dejected. We cannot be cynical. We must act."

## Analysis and Broader Significance

The report contends that the challenges facing Black communities are "warning signs for the prosperity of all Americans." The Urban League's findings suggest the current political environment represents a stark reversal from the post-George Floyd era, with the report noting that "the calls for racial healing and righting of this nation's wrongs in the aftermath of the murder of George Floyd feel like a fever dream."

However, the report also acknowledges that Black Americans have overcome more dire moments in history. It calls for the civil rights community to "consolidate its legal resistance and turn courtroom wins into durable policy" and to "build economic infrastructure that doesn't depend on the goodwill of any one administration."

Morial acknowledged that the Urban League's assessments may lead to pessimism but urged Americans to remain engaged. "We have to fight to make sure that those that are really trying to kill the essence of the American dream don't win," he said.

## What's Next

The Associated Press reached out to the White House for comment on the report but received no response. The [full report](http://stateofblackamerica.org/reports/2026) is available on the State of Black America website, with an executive summary and downloadable version for public review.

As the midterm elections approach, the Urban League's 50th anniversary assessment serves as both a historical benchmark and a call to action — a reminder that the American Dream remains an unfinished promise for millions of Americans.
