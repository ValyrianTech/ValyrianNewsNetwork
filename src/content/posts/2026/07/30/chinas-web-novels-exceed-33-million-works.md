# China's Web Novels Exceed 33 Million Works, 500M Readers

China's online literature industry has reached a historic milestone, with total works surpassing 33 million and a domestic reader base exceeding 500 million, according to the 2025 China Online Literature Blue Book released by the China Writers Association on July 30. The annual report, published by the association's Online Literature Center, paints a picture of an industry that continues to expand both domestically and internationally at a remarkable pace.

"As of the end of 2025, our country's total online literature works exceeded 33 million, with a reader scale of over 500 million," the Blue Book states, according to [Xinhua News Agency](https://www.news.cn/politics/20260730/c0b57a673f834ed9989c20c9e767b474/c.html). The report notes that creative standards have generally improved across major categories including realism, science fiction, history, and fantasy.

## A Digital Giant

Originating in the late 1990s, China's online literature ecosystem has grown into the world's largest digital literature market. The industry spans web novel platforms, intellectual property (IP) adaptation for film, television, animation and gaming, and increasingly, micro-short drama production. Key platforms such as Yuewen Group and China Literature Limited have built a multi-billion-dollar ecosystem around serialized fiction.

The latest Blue Book data shows that between the end of 2023 and the end of 2025, total works grew from over 30 million to more than 33 million—a net increase of approximately 3 million titles. Meanwhile, a broader user metric tracked 575 million registered users as of the end of 2025, according to the 2025 Blue Book, as [reported by Guangming Daily](https://www.chinawriter.com.cn/n1/2026/0427/c404027-40709177.html). This represents more than 51.9 percent of all Chinese netizens, a share maintained from the prior year, when the [2024 China Online Literature Blue Book](https://www.chinawriter.com.cn/n1/2025/0618/c404023-40503324.html) had reported 575 million users as of the end of 2024.

Industry analysis from Guangming Daily notes that approximately 15 million people now identify as online literature authors, hailing from diverse professional backgrounds including programmers, police officers, delivery drivers, security guards, and maintenance workers. This diversity is mirrored in the readership: while younger readers remain the key growth demographic, readers aged 45 and above now approach 30 percent of total readership, and the over-60 "silver-haired" demographic has broken 10 percent.

## Global Reach

Perhaps the most striking figures in the 2025 Blue Book concern overseas expansion. China's online literature industry has cumulatively exported over 130,000 works, with existing overseas registered users numbering approximately 250 million. In 2025 alone, 40 million new overseas users were added, covering more than 200 countries and regions.

"The total daily active users of overseas online literature and related micro-short drama platforms reached 60 million," the Blue Book reports, "and overseas revenue exceeded 80 billion RMB [approximately $11 billion]." This revenue figure includes the fast-growing micro-short drama business, where short-form video adaptations of popular web novels have proven particularly popular.

Overseas readers show distinct regional preferences. Latin American audiences gravitate toward fantasy romance, European and North American readers favor Western fantasy, and Southeast Asian markets show strong demand for xianxia (cultivation) and xuanhuan (fantasy) romance genres. More than 60 percent of overseas readers belong to Generation Z, making them a core demographic for future growth.

## Industry in Transition

The 2025 Blue Book reflects an industry undergoing significant transformation. Reality-based fiction focused on rural revitalization, poverty alleviation, innovation and entrepreneurship, and national industrial development has seen notable growth, signaling a shift toward content that aligns with broader cultural policy objectives.

At the same time, the convergence of online literature with micro-short drama platforms represents a major commercial trend. The integration of short-form video adaptations has opened new revenue streams and attracted audiences who may not traditionally read long-form web fiction.

Artificial intelligence is also reshaping the landscape, with AI-powered translation enabling rapid localization of Chinese web novels for overseas markets, and AI-assisted tools supporting both content creation and IP development.

## Looking Ahead

The milestone of 33 million works and half a billion domestic readers cements China's position as the world's undisputed leader in online literature. As the industry continues to mature, key questions remain: How will the balance between commercial imperatives and content quality evolve? Can overseas growth maintain its current trajectory? And what role will emerging technologies play in the next phase of expansion?

What is clear is that China's online literature industry has moved far beyond its niche origins. With a reader base larger than the population of most countries and a growing global footprint, it has become a significant force in both digital culture and cultural export.
