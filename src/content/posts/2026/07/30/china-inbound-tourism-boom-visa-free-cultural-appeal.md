# China's Inbound Tourism Booms on Visa-Free, Cultural Appeal

China's inbound tourism is experiencing a significant surge in 2026, with the country receiving 22.914 million foreign visitors in the first half of the year — a 20.4% year-on-year increase, according to data from the National Immigration Administration cited by [China Tourism News](https://www.ctnews.com.cn/jujiao/content/2026-07/29/content_190196.html). Of those arrivals, 17.815 million — or 77.7% — entered using visa-free arrangements, marking a 30.6% rise compared to the same period last year.

The top source countries for inbound travel are Korea, Russia, Malaysia, Vietnam, Thailand, Singapore, the United States, Japan, Mongolia, and Australia, which together account for 62% of all foreign visitors to China.

## Policy Drivers Behind the Boom

The surge is largely attributed to China's expanding visa-free policies. The country currently offers unilateral visa-free access to citizens of 50 nations and 240-hour transit visa-free access to travelers from 55 countries. The recently unveiled “15th Five-Year Plan” for expanding consumption further calls for steadily widening the visa-free country range and optimizing the inbound consumption environment.

As [CCTV News](https://news.cctv.com/2026/07/30/ARTIr2M5yOeAJyiiCifea7Ac260730.shtml) reports, these measures have transformed the travel landscape. Reuters has described China’s approach as making it possible for tourists to “come easily, play conveniently, and buy to their heart’s content.”

## Chongqing Emerges as a Standout Destination

The southwestern metropolis of Chongqing has seen particularly dramatic growth. The city received over 210,000 visa-free and 240-hour transit passengers in the first half of 2026, an 83.6% year-on-year increase. The economic impact has been substantial: tax refund transactions in Chongqing surged 21.5 times, with sales amounts and refund values both up 67%, according to CCTV News.

Foreign visitors are flocking to experience Chongqing’s unique cultural offerings. A Norwegian tourist watching a Sichuan Opera performance at the Chongqing Sichuan Opera Theater described the experience as “novel, unique, and the scene is particularly stunning,” noting he had only seen pictures before and never video. A local actor reflected on the broader significance, stating: “Chinese traditional culture has spread to the whole world, which is a very happy thing. Art is universal. People will always resonate with beautiful things.”

## The ‘Chinamaxxing’ Phenomenon

Beyond policy changes, a grassroots social media movement is helping drive interest in China as a travel destination. The term “Chinamaxxing” — roughly translating to “extreme Chinesification” — began circulating on overseas social media in early 2026, with related hashtags accumulating over 4 billion views in just months, according to [Phoenix News/IFENG](https://news.ifeng.com/c/8v9iQY1KXJl).

Western Gen Z users have been increasingly emulating Chinese lifestyle habits — drinking hot water, practicing Baduanjin exercise routines, foot soaking, and watching Chinese television dramas. The broader “China travel” tag has surpassed 28 billion views on overseas platforms. Foreign Ministry spokesperson Lin Jian noted that “more and more foreign friends choose to measure China with their footsteps and experience Chinese life in the Chinese way.”

## Beyond the Major Cities

Foreign tourists are now visiting nearly 500 cities across China, according to the Ministry of Culture and Tourism Data Center. The trend has shifted from traditional hubs like Beijing, Shanghai, and Guangzhou to include “Country Walk” experiences in rural areas. German travel blogger Ken Abroad posted a video titled “Someone Warned Me Not to Go to the Chinese Countryside,” which showed clean streets, modern facilities, and warmly welcoming locals — challenging many viewers’ preconceptions. American cyclists Ludwig and Michael, who accidentally stumbled upon a village banquet in Shaoyang, Hunan, saw their video accumulate over 100 million views internationally.

## ‘Reverse Cross-Border Shopping’ Takes Off

A notable emerging trend is “reverse cross-border shopping,” where foreign visitors purchase Chinese products during their travels. From new-energy vehicle showrooms to trend-focused pop-up stores, foreign tourists are increasingly drawn to the quality and creativity of “Chinese design” and “Chinese manufacturing,” Lin Jian said. This trend is allowing the world to experience firsthand the texture and innovation of Chinese consumer goods.

## Analysis: Soft Power and Economic Impact

The inbound tourism boom represents a significant soft power victory for China. As The Guardian noted, China is sending a signal: “Come here and judge China for yourselves.” The data strongly supports the effectiveness of China’s visa liberalization strategy, while the economic spillover — from tax refund growth to the reverse shopping phenomenon — contributes meaningfully to China’s service trade balance.

Looking ahead, key questions remain: whether the trend will sustain momentum beyond the peak summer season, how infrastructure capacity will keep pace with surging demand, and whether the “Chinamaxxing” social media trend represents a lasting cultural shift or a temporary phenomenon. For now, the numbers tell a story of a country successfully opening its doors — and a growing number of visitors eager to walk through them.
