# Shanghai-Gifted Red Pandas Charm Crowds at Taipei Zoo

Two red pandas gifted by Shanghai Zoo made their public debut at Taipei Zoo this week, drawing enthusiastic crowds as a symbol of cross-strait cooperation — even as political controversy swirled over the exclusion of a Shanghai delegation from the welcoming ceremony.

The three-year-old male, Le Le (乐乐), was the first to meet the public on Wednesday, July 29, while the two-year-old female, Tian Tian (甜甜), made her debut on Thursday, July 30. The pair arrived in Taipei on June 6 following a handover ceremony at Shanghai Zoo and completed more than a month of quarantine and acclimatization before being cleared for their public appearances.

According to [Xinhua](https://english.news.cn/20260730/d02da754aec745018ef7f4a52b9b05e7/c.html), Le Le quickly charmed onlookers with his reddish-brown coat and bushy, ringed tail, eagerly exploring a bamboo enrichment toy in his outdoor enclosure. Zoo officials described Le Le as lively and outgoing with a hearty appetite, while Tian Tian was more cautious but remained active and in good health.

## Exchange Agreement and Enclosure Upgrades

The pandas were gifted under a Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) signed during the 2024 Shanghai-Taipei City Forum, an annual exchange mechanism between the two cities established in 2010. As [Focus Taiwan](https://focustaiwan.tw/society/202606060004) reported, in return for the pandas, Taipei Zoo is expected to send white-handed gibbons to Shanghai Zoo. Originally, African penguins were planned, but Shanghai’s penguin breeding program was already successful, so white-handed gibbons were selected instead.

To welcome the newcomers, Taipei Zoo upgraded the red panda enclosure with interconnected climbing structures, rope ladders, shaded shelters, and additional hiding spaces to better replicate the animals’ natural habitat. Viewing areas were redesigned with floor-to-ceiling glass panels and lower sightlines to improve visibility for children.

“We upgraded the enclosure to provide them with a richer environment for climbing and hiding, while also improving the viewing experience for visitors,” said Chen Hui-lun, head of the zoo’s temperate animal area, as quoted by Xinhua.

## Political Controversy Erupts

The debut was not without political friction. The Mainland Affairs Council (MAC) barred Shanghai Deputy Secretary-General Zhang Yuxin, Shanghai Taiwan Affairs Office Director Jin Mei, and an accompanying media delegation from entering Taiwan to attend the welcoming ceremony, according to [Taiwan News](https://www.taiwannews.com.tw/news/6410468).

MAC Minister Chiu Chui-cheng criticized the Taipei City Government for organizing the ceremony, arguing the event was part of China’s “united front tactics.” Taipei Deputy Mayor Lin Yi-hua disputed this account, saying the city government had formally submitted its application on June 17 and urged the MAC to review its records.

Taipei Mayor Chiang Wan-an defended the exchange, saying the red pandas “symbolize Taipei Zoo’s commitment to conserving endangered species and represent an achievement of the Taipei-Shanghai City Forum,” as reported by the [Taipei Times](https://www.taipeitimes.com/News/taiwan/archives/2026/07/29/2003861589). He rejected the characterization of the event as a united front tactic.

China’s Taiwan Affairs Office spokesperson Chen Binhua condemned the MAC’s decision, saying it reflected the Democratic Progressive Party’s “tendency toward confrontation and Taiwan independence.”

## Conservation Significance

Red pandas are listed as endangered on the International Union for Conservation of Nature’s Red List of Threatened Species, with wild populations declining due to illegal hunting and habitat loss. Taipei Zoo already had red pandas from three family lines; the new arrivals bring fresh bloodlines essential for long-term genetic diversity.

Zoo officials said the newly arrived red pandas are expected to be paired with the zoo’s resident red pandas during the next breeding season. “We hope they will find their ‘perfect match’ in Taiwan,” Chen Hui-lun said.

## A History of Cooperation

Shanghai Zoo and Taipei Zoo have conducted more than 10 exchange visits since 2010, cooperating in animal husbandry, veterinary medicine, and scientific research. The current exchange also includes professional training and conservation education components, extending beyond the animals themselves.

Among Wednesday’s visitors was Mr. He, who brought his granddaughter to see the pandas. “I saw giant pandas Tuan Tuan and Yuan Yuan when they first arrived in Taipei, and I’ve also seen red pandas up close in Sichuan,” he told Xinhua. “Their adorable appearance has always left a deep impression on me.”

## What’s Next

The political fallout over the Shanghai delegation’s exclusion may complicate future cross-strait exchanges, including the upcoming Shanghai-Taipei City Forum. However, for now, the red pandas have captured the public’s imagination, drawing visitors to Taipei Zoo during the summer vacation and serving as an unlikely bridge between two cities with a complex relationship.
