# Seattle Mayor Fires Police Chief After Mass Shooting Fallout

Seattle Mayor Katie Wilson has relieved Police Chief Shon Barnes of his duties, according to multiple sources, following widespread criticism of his handling of a deadly mass shooting at the Bite of Seattle food festival. The decision, reported Wednesday night, marks a dramatic leadership shakeup in a city already grappling with intense debates over public safety and policing.

## The Shooting and the Response

On Sunday, July 26, gunfire erupted outside the Seattle Center Armory during the annual Bite of Seattle festival. Seven people were struck by gunfire: three died — a 19-year-old man, a 44-year-old man, and a 56-year-old woman — and four others were hospitalized, including a 2-year-old boy. According to [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/seattle-shooting-bite-of-seattle/), a 15-year-old has been arrested and charged in connection with the shooting, which police believe was gang-related. Authorities continue to search for additional suspects.

At the time of the shooting, Barnes was in Dallas, Texas, attending the National Organization of Black Law Enforcement Executives (NOBLE) annual conference. Assistant Chief Tyrone Davis led the department's first public briefing several hours after the incident — a delay that drew sharp criticism from residents and local officials who were demanding answers.

## A Tense Week Unfolds

The situation escalated rapidly over the following days. At a Monday press conference with the mayor, Barnes defended his absence, saying, "I wish that I had a crystal ball, and we could schedule gun violence, but we can't." 

Video also surfaced of a confrontational exchange between Barnes and a reporter who questioned how often he was in Seattle, given that his family lives in Chicago. "Those reports are wrong, do you understand?" Barnes responded before a staffer stepped between them.

According to [FOX 13 Seattle](https://www.fox13seattle.com/news/spd-chief-shon-barnes-fired), Barnes had been scheduled to conduct one-on-one interviews with local media on Wednesday afternoon, but those interviews were abruptly canceled. That evening, he attended a 6:30 p.m. silent vigil for the shooting victims at Seattle Center, left abruptly, and then met with Mayor Wilson, who asked him to resign. Barnes began texting individuals about his dismissal shortly afterward, sources told the outlet.

## A Sharp Reversal

Barnes' ouster represents a significant reversal for Wilson. Just days earlier, the mayor had announced she would retain Barnes and work with him to emphasize professionalism, accountability, and community partnership within the Seattle Police Department. The [Fox News](https://www.foxnews.com/politics/embattled-seattle-police-chief-relieved-duties-democratic-socialist-mayor-black-protest-planned) report noted that as of publication, neither the mayor's office nor the Seattle Police Department had publicly confirmed the change or announced an interim chief.

Barnes was appointed in 2024 by former Mayor Bruce Harrell and sworn in in 2025 — the first outsider to lead the department in over a decade, according to The Seattle Times. He previously led the Madison, Wisconsin, Police Department and held leadership roles in Chicago and North Carolina. Upon arriving in Seattle, Barnes introduced "Seattle-Centric Policing," a data-driven initiative that identified precinct-level crime priorities and emphasized community partnerships.

## Community Response and Political Implications

The decision has already sparked backlash from Black community leaders. An open letter published by The Seattle Medium urged Wilson to retain Barnes, arguing that he was being made a "scapegoat for the complex challenges of public safety." A rally in support of Barnes was planned for Thursday morning at Seattle City Hall, organized by groups including the Seattle NAACP, the Urban League of Metropolitan Seattle, and the MLK Coalition.

The shooting brought the total number of mass shootings in the United States in 2026 to 271, according to the Gun Violence Archive. The incident and subsequent firing have become part of a broader national political debate about crime and progressive governance in major U.S. cities.

## What's Next for Seattle

Barnes' departure leaves the Seattle Police Department facing another leadership transition at a critical moment. Detectives continue to investigate the Bite of Seattle shooting — one of the city's deadliest recent acts of gun violence — while searching for additional suspects. City officials have not yet named an interim chief, and it remains unclear whether Barnes formally resigned or was terminated. The outcome of Thursday's planned protest and broader community reaction will likely shape the political fallout for Mayor Wilson, who has been in office since January 2026 after winning election as a democratic socialist candidate with no prior elected experience.
