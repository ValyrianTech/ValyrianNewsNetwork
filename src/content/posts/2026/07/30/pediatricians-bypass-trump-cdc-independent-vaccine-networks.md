# Pediatricians Bypass Trump's CDC to Form Vaccine Networks

Pediatricians and public health officials across the United States are increasingly bypassing the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention for vaccine guidance, creating an alternative network of evidence-based recommendations amid widespread concerns that the country's premier public health agency has become irreversibly politicized under the Trump administration.

According to [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/07/30/nx-s1-5866740/pediatricians-health-officials-sidestepping-trump-cdc-vaccine-advice), the rupture in trust follows the appointment of longtime anti-vaccine activist Robert F. Kennedy Jr. as Secretary of Health and Human Services, who swiftly dismantled key advisory structures and removed multiple vaccines from the universal childhood schedule — prompting a legal battle, a federal court intervention, and the emergence of independent vaccine guidance networks.

## A Crisis of Trust at the CDC

For decades, the CDC served as the world's leading authority on vaccine recommendations through its Advisory Committee on Immunization Practices (ACIP). But within months of his confirmation, Kennedy [dismissed every member](https://www.npr.org/sections/shots-health-news/2025/06/09/nx-s1-5428533/rfk-jr-vaccine-advisory-committee-acip) of ACIP and replaced them with new appointees, some of whom are prominent vaccine skeptics.

Dr. John Snyder, a pediatrician at Amherst Pediatrics in Massachusetts who has been practicing for 30 years, described the shift in stark terms. "The CDC used to be, worldwide, the most trusted source of information pertaining to public health and infectious disease," Snyder told NPR. "It turned into a completely unreputable source of information."

Snyder's practice changed its website to state directly: "The CDC is no longer considered to be a reliable or legitimate source of information."

"It's shocking that we have to say that," Snyder said. Instead, the site directs families to get information from their own doctor, the state of Massachusetts, or nongovernmental medical organizations including the American Academy of Pediatrics.

## Vaccine Schedule Reduced by One-Third

On January 5, 2026, the CDC [removed six vaccines](https://www.npr.org/sections/shots-health-news/2026/01/05/nx-s1-5667199/cdc-vaccine-schedule-children) from the universal childhood immunization schedule — for rotavirus, COVID-19, influenza, hepatitis A, hepatitis B, and meningococcal disease — slashing the number of recommended vaccines from 17 to 11.

The American Academy of Pediatrics [sued the administration](https://publications.aap.org/aapnews/news/34722/AAP-s-historic-victory-in-vaccine-lawsuit-a), arguing the changes violated the Administrative Procedure Act. In March 2026, a federal judge ordered the Trump administration to revert to the previous guidelines. The administration has appealed, leaving the schedule in legal limbo.

"The back-and-forth creates a whole bunch of confusion in the public," Snyder said. "And it's very confusing even for healthcare providers."

In his exam room, Snyder has experienced this confusion firsthand. During one spring afternoon, he met with Tenzin Dekey, a mother who brought in her 1-year-old son for a routine visit. Snyder recommended the MMR vaccine and chickenpox shot — four injections in total. He handed her CDC information sheets about the vaccines, as required by law, even though his practice has explicitly directed patients away from the agency.

"This has been a source of heated discussion," Snyder said. "How can we be saying, 'Do not trust the CDC when it comes to vaccines,' yet here is the information we're giving our own patients when they're in the office about vaccines?"

Another mother, Melissa, told Snyder she was a "hard no" on the COVID-19 vaccine and declined to discuss it further. "I worry that it's been politicized a lot, and I don't like politics," she explained afterward. "Now I prefer to hear things from people, healthcare professionals." She did agree, however, to the HPV vaccine for her 14-year-old son — a shift from a previous visit when she had refused.

## Alternative Networks Fill the Void

In response to the erosion of federal guidance, pediatricians and local health departments are banding together to create independent sources of vaccine information.

In western Massachusetts, Snyder joined Valley Vax, a coalition of pediatric practices and health departments in the Connecticut River Valley that launched a website providing evidence-based vaccine guidance without relying on the CDC. According to the [Greenfield Recorder](https://www.recorder.com/2026/01/24/valleyvax-website-vaccine-safety/), the campaign bought ads on the sides of buses featuring local doctors to promote the site.

"We wanted something that felt familiar and appealing and that they might be more likely to trust," said Kiko Malin, director of Amherst's public health department, who coordinates the Valley Vax effort.

At the state level, California, Hawaii, Oregon, and Washington formed the West Coast Health Alliance to ensure the public has access to credible information. And governors of 14 states plus Guam established the Governors Public Health Alliance in response to declining trust in federal health agencies.

## Political Pressure From the Top

The push to reshape vaccine policy is being driven directly by the White House. The [Guardian reported](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/jul/28/trump-rfk-jr-cutting-vaccines) that Trump told Kennedy at a northern Virginia golf course in May that the health secretary had the "yips" — referring not to golf but to Kennedy's failure to cut vaccines aggressively enough. Trump reportedly hopes to make "alleviating autism" a component of his legacy, despite decades of research showing no link between vaccines and autism.

Dr. Paul Offit, director of the Vaccine Education Center at Children's Hospital of Philadelphia, described Trump as "the most powerful anti-vaxxer in the world." "I think Donald Trump has a fixed, immutable belief that vaccines cause autism," Offit told the Guardian.

Trump's anti-vaccine stance dates back years. In 2014, he posted on social media about a debunked link between vaccines and autism. During the 2015 Republican primary debate, he claimed autism had gotten "totally out of control" and advocated for giving vaccines in "smaller doses over a longer period of time." His Truth Social posts have recently called for breaking up shots into individual components and delaying some childhood vaccines for more than a decade.

Kennedy also sought to divert $5 billion from the National Institutes of Health — more than 10% of its budget — toward research on vaccines and autism, according to [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/americas/inside-rfk-jrs-push-dismantle-decades-us-vaccine-policy-2026-07-14/).

## Measles Surge and Public Health Fallout

As federal vaccine policy has been disrupted, preventable diseases have rebounded dramatically. Measles surged to its highest rate in 30 years in 2026, with over 2,300 cases recorded — surpassing the previous year's record. Whooping cough, flu, and tetanus deaths have also risen as vaccine confidence has eroded.

"As top leaders have downplayed the safety and importance of vaccines," Offit said, "our commander in chief, our vaccine guy in chief, is our president. He's fine with this. Just as RFK Jr. is fine with this." He added: "Trump has become the president of Measles Land. That should bother him."

Notably, even Trump's own pollster found the anti-vaccine posture to be politically risky. Tony Fabrizio, in a December 2025 memo, warned that "skepticism toward vaccine requirements is politically risky for both parties." The White House reportedly put a temporary hold on vaccine changes after polling indicated the issue could hurt Republicans in the midterm elections.

## A New Landscape for Vaccine Information

For pediatricians on the front lines, the confusion has made an already challenging job harder. Parents like Melissa, who asked to be identified by only her first name, told NPR she no longer trusts the CDC. "I worry that it's been politicized a lot, and I don't like politics," she said. "Now I prefer to hear things from people, healthcare professionals."

Local health departments are now directing families to professional medical organizations like the AAP instead of the CDC. Adriane Casalotti, a spokesperson for the National Association of County and City Health Officials, said advice from medical groups is unlikely to change "with each lawsuit against the government."

## What to Watch For

The legal battle over the vaccine schedule continues, with the administration's appeal pending. Meanwhile, the alternative networks — from Valley Vax to the Governors Public Health Alliance — are solidifying as permanent fixtures in the public health landscape.

Kiko Malin acknowledged the discomfort of contradicting the CDC. "That's not a comfortable place to be in at all," she said. But for Snyder and many other pediatricians, the choice is clear: fill the information gap themselves or watch preventable diseases return.

"This will 100% happen," Snyder warned of future outbreaks. "We don't want to see children fall ill to completely preventable diseases and then dying from them."
