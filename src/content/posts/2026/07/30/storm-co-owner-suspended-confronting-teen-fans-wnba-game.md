# WNBA Suspends Storm Co-Owner Over Teen Fan Confrontation

The WNBA has suspended Seattle Storm minority co-owner Celeste Keaton for five home games and issued a fine following an incident in which she allegedly confronted and used profane language toward two teenage fans during a game at Climate Pledge Arena in Seattle, according to [Fox News Digital](https://www.foxnews.com/outkick-sports/storm-co-owner-suspended-incident-sophie-cunningham-fans-star-breaks-silence-controversy).

The teenagers, seated courtside for the July 28 matchup between the Seattle Storm and the Indiana Fever, were holding signs reading "We love you Sophie" and "Thank you Sophie for speaking up for girls" in support of Fever guard Sophie Cunningham, who had recently publicly stated her opposition to transgender women competing in women's sports.

## Background: Cunningham's Comments and the Rally

Cunningham's stance first drew national attention when an [ESPN profile](https://www.espn.com/wnba/story/_/id/49306657/sophie-cunningham-finger-point-indiana-fever-wnba) published approximately one week before the game quoted her saying she wanted to "protect young girls in the locker room" and that "they shouldn't have to go against biological men." She later doubled down in comments to Indianapolis media, calling her position "common sense."

The White House publicly defended Cunningham's remarks, with Press Secretary Karoline Leavitt calling criticism of the player "astonishing" and framing the issue as one of basic fairness in women's sports, as [Fox News reported](https://www.foxnews.com/outkick-sports/white-house-defends-wnba-star-sophie-cunningham-speaks-support-protecting-womens-sports).

Before the game, a rally organized by groups including Women are Real, journalist Brandi Kruse, and Known Heretic drew a few dozen demonstrators outside Climate Pledge Arena to support Cunningham and promote Washington state ballot initiatives restricting transgender athlete participation in schools. A [small group of counterprotesters](https://www.foxnews.com/outkick-sports/sophie-cunningham-rally-rocks-wnba-game-save-womens-sports-activists-face-counterprotest) supporting transgender inclusion also gathered.

## The Confrontation

Kruse, who accompanied the girls during portions of the game, told Fox News Digital that Keaton approached them and called them "f---ing insane." According to Kruse, the girls had been excited for their courtside seats — a "once-in-a-lifetime opportunity" — and had stayed up the night before making their signs. One of them broke down in tears at halftime after the alleged exchange.

When Kruse alerted arena security, she said the guard identified Keaton as "one of the owners of the team." Kruse argued that Keaton's alleged conduct violated the arena's code of conduct, which prohibits abusive or obscene language, but no ejection occurred. Kruse also claimed that Storm players made mocking thumbs-down gestures toward the girls during the game.

"Had this been any other fan under any other circumstances who swore at and called some teenage girls 'f---ing insane' until they cried, they would've been ejected," Kruse told Fox News Digital.

The girls were wearing clothing from XX-XY Athletics, a brand advocating against transgender inclusion in women's sports.

## Official Response and Fallout

Storm majority owner Ginny Gilder issued an apology statement on July 29, saying the organization was "aware one of our co-owners made comments to two fans in attendance at last night's game" and reaffirming the team's commitment to creating "an inclusive environment where every fan is treated with respect."

The WNBA confirmed to [Yahoo Sports](https://sports.yahoo.com/wnba/article/storm-minority-owner-celeste-keaton-suspended-from-5-home-games-fined-after-incident-with-sophie-cunningham-fans-051043012.html) that Keaton was fined and suspended from the Storm's next five home games. The league's swift action demonstrated its willingness to enforce its code of conduct even against ownership, though the suspension applies only to home games.

Meanwhile, Storm center Stefanie Dolson entered the arena wearing a shirt reading "Trans rights are human rights," a visible counter-message to Cunningham's position, as [the New York Post reported](https://nypost.com/2026/07/29/sports/wnba-rival-trolls-sophie-cunningham-with-shirt-as-fans-rally-after-trans-athlete-controversy/).

The Indiana Fever distanced itself from the rally, issuing a statement that "neither our organization nor Sophie had any knowledge of this demonstration and no contact with its organizers." Neither Cunningham nor teammate Caitlin Clark was made available to reporters after the game, according to the [Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/fever-cunningham-rally-6b5edacdea71d1ed5f6e73ad77ca7de1).

## Cunningham Breaks Her Silence

Late Wednesday night, Cunningham responded on X, calling Keaton's actions "truly embarrassing" and vowing to send the girls gifts. "I truly believe there is a safe space and love for EVERYBODY. Biological girls, biological boys, trans women, and trans men. Everyone is welcome at my table!" she wrote. "You can disagree and still be kind. Bravo to those girls for standing up for what they believe."

The incident drew attention from prominent figures, including author J.K. Rowling, who called the girls "heroines" for protesting what she described as "grotesque unfairness."

## Analysis and Implications

The confrontation has become a flashpoint in the broader national debate over transgender participation in women's sports — an issue that has divided state legislatures, federal courts, and now professional basketball. The WNBA, a league historically aligned with progressive causes and LGBTQ+ inclusion, now finds itself navigating internal tensions between ownership, players, and fans on opposite sides of the issue.

The [Indianapolis Star](https://www.indystar.com/story/sports/basketball/wnba/fever/2026/07/30/seattle-storm-co-owner-celeste-keaton-suspended-confrontation-sophie-cunningham-fans-indiana-fever/91105560007/) noted that Cunningham's stance has put her at odds with the league's predominant culture, even as she maintains widespread fan support. Reports indicate another rally is being planned for an upcoming Fever game against Chicago, suggesting the controversy is far from resolved.

## What's Next

As the WNBA season continues, the league faces unresolved questions: Will it adopt a formal policy on owner conduct at games? How will the internal dynamics within the Storm organization — between Keaton, Gilder, and players like Dolson — play out? And will Cunningham's stance affect her relationships with teammates, sponsors, or the league's broader cultural environment?

For now, the suspension of Celeste Keaton establishes a clear baseline: regardless of the underlying political debate, the league will not tolerate owners confronting fans with abusive language. The deeper ideological conflict, however, remains very much alive.
