# Former Minmetals GM Guo Wenqing Surrenders to Authorities

Guo Wenqing, former deputy Party secretary and general manager of China Minmetals Group, one of the country's largest state-owned metals traders and miners, has voluntarily surrendered to anti-corruption authorities, according to [The Paper](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_33688943).

The Central Commission for Discipline Inspection (CCDI) said on July 31 that Guo is suspected of serious violations of discipline and law and is undergoing disciplinary review and supervisory investigation. The announcement came more than two years after Guo's name disappeared from the executive leadership list on China Minmetals' official website, an exit the company never publicly explained.

## A Career Across Two Metals Giants

Guo, born in December 1964 in Xinglong, Hebei province, spent more than two decades in provincial government and infrastructure roles before joining the metallurgical sector in 2008. He rose to lead China Metallurgical Group Corporation (MCC), a state-owned construction and equipment giant, before its strategic restructuring into China Minmetals.

The merger of the two central state-owned enterprises was announced by the State-owned Assets Supervision and Administration Commission in December 2015 and consolidated at a Beijing conference in June 2016. At the time, the combined entity's scale surpassed global mining majors BHP Billiton, Rio Tinto and Vale. [China Daily](https://www.chinadaily.com.cn/kindle/2016-05/23/content_25426834.htm) reported that Guo, previously MCC's chairman, was appointed board director, general manager and vice Party secretary of China Minmetals in May 2016.

He held the general manager post for about eight years, until May 2024.

## A Quiet Exit

Guo's last publicly recorded appearance on the company website was on April 8, 2024, when Minmetals reported his attendance at the group's annual rule-of-law work conference five days earlier. His name then vanished from the leadership page.

On May 22, 2024, China Minmetals announced that Chen Dexin, a former vice governor of Gansu province, had been appointed general manager and deputy Party secretary, without any explanation of Guo's departure. By December 2024, Chen had been promoted to chairman and Party secretary, with Zhu Kebing named general manager, according to [Beijing News](https://m.bjnews.com.cn/detail/1742024578129895.html).

## The MCC Anti-Corruption Storm

Guo's surrender comes against the backdrop of a sustained anti-corruption campaign inside the MCC system, which was fully absorbed into China Minmetals in 2016. Since 2024, at least six former MCC-system executives have been placed under investigation, The Paper reported.

[Shanghai Observer](https://m.jfdaily.com/wx/detail.do?id=793359) noted in September 2024 that three MCC-system officials had already fallen within six months, including Zhang Mengxing, former Party secretary and president of Metallurgical Corporation of China, probed in April 2024 just five months after retirement, and Qu Haiyan, then union chair of MCC Group, investigated about five weeks after her last reported public appearance.

The list has since grown to include Song Zhanjiang, former Party secretary and chairman of China First Metallurgical Group (June 2024); Leng Tiesong, former Party secretary and chairman of MCC Communications Construction Group (October 2024); Shen Jian, a former assistant general manager at Shanghai Baoye Construction (January 2026); and Zhao Guangli, former Party secretary and chairman of China No. 3 Metallurgical Group, investigated on July 2.

## What Voluntary Surrender Means

Guo's decision to voluntarily surrender is legally significant. Under China's Supervision Law, voluntary surrender is a mitigating circumstance, and the final outcome will be determined in light of the facts of the case, restitution of illicit gains, confession and other factors, according to [Manager Magazine via NetEase](https://www.163.com/dy/article/L35J9M2L0530IU8N.html).

The case also underscores a broader pattern in China's anti-corruption drive: the CCDI has continued to target central state-owned enterprises and does not spare executives who have already left their posts. Guo's case stands out both for the two-year gap between his quiet removal from the leadership page and his surrender, and for the absence of any public explanation at the time of his exit.

## What to Watch

The disciplinary review and supervisory investigation will determine the specific allegations against Guo and any eventual punishment. Beyond the individual case, observers will be watching whether the Minmetals-MCC anti-corruption campaign widens further and whether other former executives of the merged metals giant come under similar scrutiny.
