# Belgian Coast Breaks Records in July

The Belgian coast recorded its best July since the Covid-19 pandemic, according to figures released on Friday by Westtoer, the provincial tourism office of West Flanders. As [La Libre Belgique reports](https://www.lalibre.be/belgique/societe/2026/07/31/la-cote-belge-a-battu-des-records-en-juillet-UDWJ5OCE2JBH7BP7NDZLJZNWQM/), 5.6 million overnight stays were booked in tourist accommodation and 2.9 million day visitors were counted last month, the latter based on mobile phone data. Both figures marked increases of 14% and 20% respectively compared with July 2025. Westtoer described the month as the coast's strongest since the coronavirus crisis.

## Context

The North Sea coast is a 67-kilometre strip in West Flanders, Belgium's only coastline and a centrepiece of domestic summer tourism, home to popular resorts such as Ostend, Knokke-Heist and Blankenberge. Westtoer tracks the season using accommodation bookings for overnight stays and mobile phone data for day visitors. Tourism along the coast collapsed during the strict travel restrictions of 2020–2021 and has since recovered strongly, with some segments now surpassing pre-pandemic levels. This July's performance builds on a solid [July 2025](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2025/07/30/westtoer-tevreden-over-kusttoerisme-in-juli-2025/), when the coast drew 4.9 million overnight stays, up 6% year on year, alongside 2.4 million day tourists.

## Key Developments

Flemish visitors continue to dominate the seaside resorts, accounting for 61% of overnight stays. French-speaking Belgians represent 23%, while foreign visitors make up 19% — a share that is growing, particularly among German holidaymakers. As Belga, quoted by La Libre, put it, the coast "recorded its best month of July since the Covid-19 pandemic, on a tourism level."

A striking feature of this July's balance sheet is the absence of a single-day peak. Westtoer attributes the even spread to sustained fine weather across the whole month, rather than a short-lived surge during a heatwave. The tone had already been set during the 21 July national holiday weekend, when Westtoer counted around 950,000 overnight stays, up from 700,000 a year earlier, as [VRT NWS reported](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/07/21/kust-beleeft-gezellig-druk-verlengd-weekend-met-bijna-1-miljoen/). Hotel occupancy averaged about 83%, with the busiest night — Saturday to Sunday — reaching just under 90%, while day visitors numbered roughly 425,000. "But that remains a particularly good figure," said Michelle Martens of Westtoer.

## Analysis

The record month reinforces the Belgian coast's status as a magnet in hot summers. Westtoer has long described holidaymakers' attachment to the shoreline as "sea mode" — a preference for the cooler home coast over sweltering southern European destinations. The sustained warmth of July 2026 appears to have amplified that effect, spreading visitors over the entire month instead of concentrating them on a few scorching days.

The economic stakes reach well beyond the beach. Tourism is a pillar of the West Flanders economy, and an exceptional July gives seaside businesses a powerful start to the peak season. The lack of extreme single-day congestion may also provide relief for resorts that have faced crowding-related strain during earlier heatwaves.

## What's Next

The late-summer outlook is more measured. Based on a hotel sample, Westtoer says August bookings have not yet reached the level seen at the same point last summer. One factor is that the 15 August public holiday falls on a Saturday in 2026, ruling out the traditional long weekend that usually drives demand. The sector is nonetheless counting on last-minute reservations if the fine weather holds, a pattern that has often benefited coastal businesses in recent years.
