# Belgium Sends Water Tankers to France as Wildfires Rage

Belgium has broadened its aid offer to France, making water tanker trucks, support vehicles and around two dozen crew members available to help fight wildfires that have ravaged thousands of hectares in the country's southwest. Announced on Friday by the cabinet of Interior Minister Bernard Quintin, the offer runs through the EU Civil Protection Mechanism, and the equipment will remain on stand-by until French authorities make a formal request, [RTBF reported](https://www.rtbf.be/article/feux-de-forets-la-belgique-met-des-camions-citernes-et-d-autres-vehicules-a-disposition-de-la-france-11764898).

## Context

The fires, concentrated in the Gironde region of southwestern France, are part of a fierce European wildfire season. Nearly 270,000 hectares have burned in France and Spain since the start of 2026, according to [Euronews](https://fr.euronews.com/my-europe/2026/07/29/incendies-en-france-et-en-espagne-comment-lue-coordonne-laide-internationale-depuis-bruxel). Heatwaves and dried-out vegetation — conditions scientists link to human-caused climate change — have made the fires exceptionally hard to control. France activated the EU Civil Protection Mechanism, which centralises requests for support from member states and offers of aid from the countries taking part in the system.

## Key Developments

Belgium is ready to deploy four water tanker trucks holding 10,000 to 12,000 litres each, a 28,000-litre semi-trailer tanker, a command vehicle, two pick-ups, a minibus and a truck, along with some 24 personnel from the Belgian Civil Protection service and fire brigades. Quintin, who has been in regular contact with his French counterpart Laurent Nuñez since the fires began, said France can count on these additional Belgian capabilities if it calls on them.

The civilian offer complements the military deployment announced on 29 July, when Belgian Defence sent 29 soldiers, two Panther firefighting vehicles, nine water tankers and a liaison officer to France. As [RTBF noted](https://www.rtbf.be/article/des-militaires-belges-et-du-materiel-envoyes-en-france-pour-combattre-les-incendies-11763888), the first Panther left Melsbroek air base aboard an A400M transport plane, while the tankers — too large for the aircraft — travelled by road from Marche-en-Famenne. The Panther can carry up to 10,500 litres of water and 600 litres of foam, reach flames more than 90 metres away and travel at up to 135 km/h.

Defence Minister Théo Francken, who cut short his holiday to attend the departure ceremony, stressed that the Belgian crews would operate under French command and that the equipment was brand new. "We are operational and ready in less than 24 hours," he said. French Ambassador Xavier Lapeyre de Cabanes said the aid was practical, not symbolic: "We really need this help; it is not merely symbolic. It has practical use."

By Friday, Francken announced that four additional firefighters from Florennes would join the operation, bringing the Panther crews to 12 firefighters in two teams able to keep the vehicles running around the clock, [RTL.be reported](https://www.rtl.be/actu/belgique/politique/operation-bouclier-de-feu-theo-francken-annonce-lenvoi-de-nouveaux-pompiers-en/2026-07-31/article/796093). The Belgian teams had arrived well, were well received and were fully integrated with military firefighters at the Cazaux air base, he said.

## Analysis

Belgium's two-track response — one military, one civilian — illustrates how European countries are pooling firefighting resources through Brussels. The EU's Emergency Response Coordination Centre (ERCC) monitors crises around the clock and coordinates aircraft, helicopters, trucks and teams offered by 27 member states and 10 partner countries. Water-bombing aircraft from Slovakia, Slovenia, Portugal and Turkey have already been deployed to southwestern France, and Serbia has offered helicopters.

"We are still missing certain capabilities, so we keep looking," said Maria Zuber, head of the ERCC unit, describing the hunt for additional aircraft. European officials acknowledge the system is being strained: "We have put in place mechanisms that have been improved over time, because we have seen that wildfires are becoming a more and more important challenge," said Borja Miguelez, deputy director of the emergency centre, adding that the EU had so far answered all requests for help.

## What's Next

The Belgian tankers and vehicles remain on stand-by, ready to move if Paris submits a request matching its needs. With renewed fire outbreaks reported in the Var on Friday afternoon and another heatwave expected in the days ahead, the strain on European firefighting resources is unlikely to ease soon. For now, the message from Brussels is one of continued solidarity — and continued vigilance.
