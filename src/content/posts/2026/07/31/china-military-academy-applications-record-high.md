# China Military Academy Applications Surge to Record High

More than 192,000 students across China applied to military academies this year—a record—with 22 academies admitting over 17,500 candidates, according to the [Ministry of National Defense](http://www.mod.gov.cn/gfbw/xwfyr/lxjzh_246940/16476759.html). The ministry said the average admitted candidate ranked in the top 6 percent of their home province, placing both application enthusiasm and student quality at historic highs.

"Students across the country have continued to enthusiastically apply to military academies," ministry spokesperson Colonel Jiang Bin told [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/politics/20260730/14606dfd2dce4c6aab611b0bf2713ec8/c.html) reporters at a routine press conference July 30. "Approximately 192,000 people from 31 provinces nationwide applied, and 22 military academies admitted more than 17,500 candidates. The admitted candidates' average score ranking was in the top 6% of their respective provinces. Both application enthusiasm and student quality have reached historic highs."

Jiang, who serves as deputy director of the ministry's information office, described military academies as "the cradle for cultivating high-caliber, specialized new-type military talent" and urged incoming cadets to "contribute youthful strength to the high-quality advancement of national defense and military modernization."

The figures were released during the annual gaokao (college entrance examination) admission season, when military academies remain among the most sought-after choices for students and parents. The academies are the primary channel for training officers for the People's Liberation Army (PLA) and the People's Armed Police Force. [People's Daily](http://edu.people.com.cn/n1/2026/0731/c1006-40771608.html) carried the announcement on page 11 of its July 31 edition, and [China News Service](https://www.chinanews.com.cn/gn/2026/07-30/10669277.shtml) flagged the enrollment results as a standalone item from the briefing.

The 2026 intake extends a clear upward trend. In 2025, the MND reported nearly 135,000 applicants, with 16,900 admitted and a 100 percent plan completion rate. In 2024, the ministry said more than 17,000 candidates were admitted, with average scores 90.2 points above the first-tier or special-type enrollment control line. This year's applicant pool is roughly 42 percent larger than last year's, although ministry reporting methodology has shifted over time—earlier counts referred to physical examination participants, while recent figures count applicants.

The surge follows a restructuring of military academy recruitment announced in June 2025. As [China Education Online](https://news.eol.cn/yaowen/202506/t20250615_2674713.shtml) reported, the number of academies recruiting high school graduates directly was cut from 27 to 22, with the Army Special Operations Academy, Army Border Defense Academy, Armed Police Special Police Academy, and Armed Police Maritime Police Academy moving to segmented, multi-academy training programs. The reform also ended the "command vs. non-command" classification and, starting in 2025, allowed all military academy undergraduates to apply directly for master's degree programs.

The enrollment boom comes against a backdrop of accelerated PLA modernization. The MND frames the record interest as evidence of patriotic sentiment and youth enthusiasm for the "strong military" cause, with officials linking the trend to the PLA's 99th founding anniversary on August 1, 2026, and the centenary in 2027 that Beijing has tied to its goal of building a world-class military. Repeated official campaigns promoting the National Defense Education Law and patriotic education have also been cited in state media as contributing factors. The announcement arrived alongside a wide-ranging press conference covering naval deployments, joint exercises with Thailand, and security concerns about Japan's new intelligence agency—an indication of an increasingly active regional security posture that may be shaping young people's career calculations.

At the same time, the "historic high" characterization remains an official claim. No independent third-party analysis of the 2026 figures had been published at the time of writing, and the MND has not released a provincial or gender breakdown of applicants. Cross-year comparisons, too, come with a methodological caveat: the ministry's 2024 figure counted candidates who took military physical examinations, while the 2025 and 2026 counts refer to applicants.

With the 2026 admission cycle complete, attention now shifts to how the incoming cohort will be trained under the restructured academy system. The MND has said all military academy undergraduates can now pursue master's degrees directly—a change that may further bolster the academies' appeal in coming years. The enrollment figures offer a telling measure of how military careers are resonating with China's youth, and how the country's defense establishment is attracting the talent it says it needs for the next phase of modernization.
