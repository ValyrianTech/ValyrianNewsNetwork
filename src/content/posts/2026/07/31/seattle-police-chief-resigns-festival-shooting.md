# Seattle Police Chief Resigns After Deadly Festival Shooting

Seattle Police Chief Shon Barnes resigned Thursday after Mayor Katie Wilson pushed for a change in leadership in the aftermath of a fatal gang-related shootout at the Bite of Seattle food festival near the Space Needle, [AP News reported](https://apnews.com/article/seattle-festival-shooting-police-chief-space-needle-9cda1dbb5e4d825363fcdf8695e9be8d). Three people were killed and four others wounded when gunfire erupted outside the Armory building at Seattle Center on Sunday evening.

## A Deadly Afternoon at Seattle Center

Gunfire broke out around 6 p.m. on July 26 during the Bite of Seattle, an annual three-day festival that draws hundreds of food vendors and performers to Seattle Center. Seven people were struck by bullets. The dead included Carlos Israel Sanchez Villalba, 44, and Ashley Whitehead, 56, both innocent bystanders, and Junior Cee Niko Semo, 19, who police believe was one of the shooters. The wounded included a 2-year-old boy, two men ages 23 and 27, and a 39-year-old woman; all were hospitalized and later discharged.

A 15-year-old was arrested at the scene after an officer saw him firing into the crowd and persuaded him to surrender. According to the [Seattle Police Department](https://spdblotter.seattle.gov/2026/07/27/gun-violence-at-seattle-center-takes-three-lives/), the teenager was armed with a Polymer80 "ghost gun" equipped with an extended magazine. Investigators recovered two other weapons and 14 shell casings and believe at least three suspects were involved. Charging documents indicate the gun the 15-year-old was seen firing was not involved in the deaths of the two bystanders.

## Communication Failures Drew Sharp Criticism

Although dozens of officers were on the festival grounds and responded within seconds, the city's handling of public information came under scrutiny. After an initial social media post, police took nearly five hours to release additional details, including whether any threat remained. The city did not send notifications through its emergency alert system, and Wilson initially said two people were in custody before retracting that statement.

"What I did not fully understand in that moment was how little information had reached the public or the press since the incident began at 6 p.m.," Wilson said in a video statement, [according to AP](https://apnews.com/article/seattle-mayor-katie-wilson-festival-shooting-police-be27c0d53f4fff77ac435bacbaf9a070).

## Resignation and Immediate Fallout

Barnes, who was out of town at a law enforcement conference in Dallas when the shooting occurred, submitted his resignation Thursday. Wilson accepted it and named Deputy Chief Andre Sayles — a law enforcement veteran with more than 20 years of experience and a former Beloit, Wisconsin, police chief — as interim chief, per the [SPD Blotter](https://spdblotter.seattle.gov/2026/07/30/seattle-mayor-katie-wilson-names-andre-sayles-interim-chief-of-the-seattle-police-department/).

Wilson defended the decision Friday as the "right decision at the right time," citing "multiple factors" and saying "the events of Sunday played into it."

The move drew immediate opposition. Four City Council members, including Council President Joy Hollingsworth and Public Safety Chair Bob Kettle, [issued a joint statement](https://council.seattle.gov/2026/07/30/city-councilmembers-react-to-police-chief-barnes-departure-voice-major-concerns/) warning that "having cycled through four police chiefs in just two and a half years, our priority must be stability, not further disruption." Black community organizations, including the Seattle NAACP and the Urban League of Metropolitan Seattle, wrote in an open letter that "Chief Barnes should not be made a scapegoat for the complex challenges of public safety."

## A City at a Crossroads

The leadership shake-up arrives at a delicate moment for Seattle. Barnes, the city's second Black police chief, was credited with boosting officer recruitment and reducing downtown crime. Wilson — a democratic socialist who once supported defunding the police — kept Barnes on after winning last year's mayoral race, a signal to the business community that she would not radically overhaul public safety.

Jon Scholes, president of the Downtown Seattle Association, said Seattle "had been on a positive course on public safety with police hiring trending up and crime trending down" and urged that the next chief maintain that progress.

The resignation also comes as Seattle heads into another major public event: Seafair Weekend Festival begins this weekend. Sayles said the department would boost police staffing at public festivals for the rest of the summer and that investigators are close to arresting additional suspects in the shootout.

## What's Next

With a search for a permanent chief underway, Seattle faces immediate tests: restoring public confidence in emergency communications, addressing youth gun violence, and safely managing a summer of large gatherings. Interim Chief Sayles' handling of the Seafair weekend and the ongoing investigation will be closely watched as the city works to move past a tragedy that exposed deep fault lines in its public safety leadership.
