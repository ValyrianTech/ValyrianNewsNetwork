# Long March 6A Launches Two Communication Test Satellites

China successfully launched Communication Technology Test Satellites 27A and 27B aboard a Long March 6A carrier rocket on July 30, 2026, in a dual-satellite mission from the Taiyuan Satellite Launch Center in Shanxi Province. The rocket ignited at 09:00 Beijing time (01:00 UTC) and delivered both spacecraft into their preset orbits. Declared a complete success, the flight marked the 660th launch of China's Long March rocket family, according to [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/tech/20260731/99a2594164434261b7fb85a67fbcd8c4/c.html).

## Mission Details

Satellite 27A was developed by the Fifth Academy of China Aerospace Science and Technology Corporation (CASC), while Satellite 27B was built by the Eighth Academy, also known as the Shanghai Academy of Spaceflight Technology. As [China News Service](https://news.qq.com/rain/a/20260730A03QYA00) reported, the two spacecraft are intended mainly for satellite communications, radio and television broadcasting, data transmission, and related technology testing and validation.

The launch also set more specific program milestones. [The Paper](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_33684439) reported that the mission was the 26th flight of the Long March 6A and the 272nd Long March launch developed by the Eighth Academy — the team behind the rocket itself.

## A Workhorse Designed for Density

The Long March 6A is a new-generation solid-liquid bundled medium-lift launch vehicle developed by CASC's Eighth Academy. According to The Paper, the rocket can accommodate single or multiple satellites in tandem, parallel, stacked, wall-mounted, or piggyback configurations, and offers a sun-synchronous orbit capacity of no less than 4.5 tons at 700 kilometers altitude. Since its first flight in March 2022, it has completed 26 consecutive successful missions, with its modular, industrialized design helping to streamline production and shorten turnaround times.

That flexibility points to the rocket's broader purpose: supporting high-density launch schedules as China builds out space-based infrastructure. The 27A/27B pair continues the country's communication technology test satellite program, an experimental series that lets engineers validate new technologies for satellite communications, broadcasting, and data relay in orbit before they are deployed on operational spacecraft — work widely seen as groundwork for larger low-Earth-orbit internet constellations.

## A Quickening Pace

The 660th-flight milestone underscores how dramatically China's launch operations have accelerated. Analysis of the mission on [MSN China](https://www.msn.cn/zh-cn/news/other/长征六号改一箭双星圆满完成第660次飞行/gm-GM8352E9F9) noted that the Long March family took nearly five decades to complete its first 500 launches, yet finished its most recent 160 flights in under six years — a sign of China's shift toward industrialized, high-density space operations. The program, which began in 1970, has become the backbone of China's space effort, launching crewed missions, lunar probes, and navigation satellites. The accelerating cadence also reflects a global trend in which falling launch costs and reusable rocket technology have made rapid, repeated access to orbit routine.

## Commercial Space Momentum

The launch also comes amid intensifying competition for orbital resources. In its market analysis, [National Business Daily](https://www.163.com/dy/article/L35HQFKD0512B07B.html) cited research institutions describing satellite orbital slots and frequency bands as non-renewable strategic resources whose scarcity has pushed international competition to a "white-hot" stage. The report added that maturing reusable rocket technology and declining launch costs have driven rapid growth in global launch numbers, with commercial spaceflight now the dominant force in the space economy. Demand drivers include low-Earth-orbit resource competition, space computing capacity, and 6G technology development.

## What's Next

China's launch calendar shows no sign of easing. National Business Daily also reported that a Long March 8A launch was expected on the afternoon of August 4, 2026, just days after the 660th flight, testing a newer addition to the Long March family. With reliable access to orbit demonstrated once again and launch infrastructure scaling up, industry watchers expect the Long March family's next milestones to arrive sooner than any before.
