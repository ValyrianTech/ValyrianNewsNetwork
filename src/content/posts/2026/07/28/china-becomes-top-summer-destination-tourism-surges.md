# China Becomes Top Summer Destination as Tourism Surges

China is experiencing an unprecedented surge in inbound tourism, recording 22.91 million visits in the first half of 2026 — a 20.4% increase year-on-year — as visa liberalization measures and diversified cultural offerings transform the country into a premier summer destination for global travelers, [CGTN reported](https://news.cgtn.com/news/2026-07-26/What-makes-China-such-an-appealing-summer-gateway-for-global-tourists--1P5OL710a4M/p.html).

The boom builds on a record 150 million inbound visits in 2025, a 17% annual increase, according to [People’s Daily](https://en.people.cn/n3/2026/0724/c90000-20481265.html). [CCTV News](https://news.cctv.com/2026/07/28/ARTIB52vlU7LibF8oEcnQkQR260727.shtml) noted this summer has witnessed a remarkable influx of international visitors drawn by China’s natural landscapes, cooler climates, and increasingly accessible travel policies.

## Visa Facilitation as a Catalyst

The surge is underpinned by China’s systematic expansion of visa-free access. The country currently offers unilateral visa-free entry to nationals of 50 countries and has signed mutual visa exemption agreements with 29 nations. A 240-hour transit visa-free program now covers 55 countries via 65 designated ports.

The impact is striking: 17.8 million visa-free entries were recorded in H1 2026 alone, up 30.6% year-on-year, with over 75% of inbound travelers now entering without a visa.

“With the continued effects of visa-free policies and other facilitation measures, more overseas tourists and students are choosing China as a preferred summer destination,” said Yang Lina, a tourism operator in Jilin’s Toushan Village, as reported by People’s Daily.

## European Travelers Lead the Charge

European markets are driving much of the growth. Countries from Europe account for nearly 30% of China’s top 20 inbound source markets this summer, with travel bookings surging 275% year-on-year and attraction ticket orders increasing by 20 times. Visitors from Britain, France, and Germany alone make up 42% of all European arrivals, CGTN reports.

American tourist Max Slonim, visiting the “cool city” of Liupanshui in Guizhou Province, told CGTN: “Whenever you go to scenic and beautiful places, you can really experience nature and the peaceful atmosphere without huge crowds and long queues.”

## Destinations in the Spotlight

Summer hotspots across China are reporting remarkable figures. In Zhangjiajie, Hunan Province — famous for its towering, UNESCO-listed sandstone mountains — daily visits to the Grand Canyon Scenic Area approach 10,000, with inbound tourists accounting for over 50% of the total. Direct flights now connect the city with eight countries and regions.

Qinhuangdao, a coastal city in Hebei Province, received 43,000 inbound visits in the first half of 2026, up 50% year-on-year, while the average stay of overseas tourists doubled from seven to fifteen days. Liupanshui is hosting over 20 themed summer events including music festivals and wilderness camping.

Further west, Yunnan received 7.86 million inbound arrivals from South and Southeast Asia in 2025, accounting for 52.2% of its total inbound traffic. Sascha Scholz, a German tourist visiting Yunnan, told [China Daily](https://www.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202602/28/WS69a22827a310d6866eb3aa73.html): “Yunnan is one of the most beautiful places in China. We enjoyed the province’s beautiful landscape, such as the tropical forests, green mountains and clear lakes.”

## Infrastructure and Digital Transformation

Behind the boom lies a world-class transport network. By the end of 2025, China’s railway network reached 165,000 km in operating length, highways stretched to 5.58 million km, and certified civil airports increased to 270. The country’s railways are expected to handle 1.01 billion passenger trips during the 62-day summer rush ending August 31.

Professor Tong Yun of Hainan University noted: “The super-sized modern transportation network, paired with supply-side innovations, including summer tourism special trains and customized transport services, has converted potential travel demand into real mobility of hundreds of millions of people.”

Digital tools are also smoothing the experience. AI-powered translation devices, multilingual ticketing machines, and customer support in English, French, Spanish and other languages are now widely available at major attractions, while big data scheduling ensures the smooth flow of large passenger volumes.

## The Shuyun Phenomenon

A new term — “Shuyun” — has been coined to describe the summer travel boom, echoing “Chunyun” (the Spring Festival travel rush). Sun Zhe, an associate professor at Jilin University, observes that summer travel is shifting from “enjoying the scenery” to “experiencing life,” as destinations create diverse consumption scenarios such as study tours, health tourism, and cultural events.

Tong Yun added that the boom reflects China’s economic and social progress: “The trend mirrors the country’s consumption upgrading and evolving public lifestyles. Underpinning the boom is the steady growth of residents’ disposable income and continuous improvement of the paid leave system.”

## What to Watch For

China’s new five-year tourism plan targets 190 million inbound visits by 2030. However, questions remain about the renewal of visa-free policies currently set to expire on December 31, 2026. NPC Deputy Yang Yaqin has proposed extending the 240-hour transit visa to 30 days with nationwide travel privileges, a move that could further accelerate growth.

Managing the scale of travel also presents challenges. Balancing the environmental sustainability of sensitive natural areas with growing visitor numbers, and addressing the concentration of arrivals from European markets, will be key priorities.

For now, the trend is unmistakable: foreign travelers are venturing beyond Beijing and Shanghai to explore ancient towns, mountain villages, and lesser-known destinations, discovering a China that is increasingly accessible, welcoming, and vibrant.
