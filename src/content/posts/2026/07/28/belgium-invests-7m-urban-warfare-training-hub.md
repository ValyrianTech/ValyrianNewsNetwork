# Belgium Invests Nearly €7M in Urban Warfare Training Hub

The Belgian Ministry of Defense is investing €6,942,000 in a state-of-the-art indoor urban warfare training complex in Leopoldsburg, Limburg, signaling a major upgrade to the country’s military training capabilities. The facility, known as a “skillhouse,” is designed to prepare soldiers for the increasingly central role of urban combat in modern warfare and is expected to be operational by 2028.

The investment was disclosed by N-VA MP Wouter Raskin and reported by [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/07/28/leger-investeert-7-miljoen-euro-in-nieuw-trainingscomplex-in-leo/) and local media. It comes as part of a broader push by Belgian Defense to modernize its infrastructure under the recently approved Kwartierplan (Quartering Plan), which aims to prepare the armed forces for growth through 2040.

## A Dedicated Urban Combat Facility

The training complex will be built on a 1,450 m² indoor skillhouse and an additional 1,750 m² polyvalent (multi-purpose) training zone. The skillhouse is designed to be a flexible space that can be reconfigured to simulate various urban environments, from small villages to densely built-up city blocks. Soldiers will train in close-quarters combat, room clearing, hand-to-hand fighting in confined spaces, and vehicle operations with running engines indoors—made possible by a specialized ventilation system that also enables live-fire shooting exercises.

According to [Internetgazet](https://www.internetgazet.be/leopoldsburg/nieuwe-investeringen-in-leopoldsburg.aspx), the facility will feature an elevated instructor footbridge allowing trainers to observe exercises in real time and provide immediate feedback. “The people receiving training can be monitored from that bridge,” Raskin explained. “This way they can also be corrected afterwards.” Classrooms for theoretical instruction will complement the practical training areas.

## Consolidating Fragmented Training

MP Raskin noted that current military training in Belgium is “too fragmented,” conducted across multiple buildings that are sometimes used for other purposes, such as vehicle storage. The new Leopoldsburg complex aims to consolidate all urban warfare training in one dedicated, safe location. “It is necessary to have quality training,” Raskin said. “It is also intended to roll this out to other Defense sites in Belgium.”

Leopoldsburg is already one of the country’s most important military garrisons, combining combat functions, logistical support, and extensive training grounds. Defense expert and former Colonel Roger Housen said the investment strengthens that position: “With this, Leopoldsburg strengthens itself as one of the most important training centers in our country.”

## Lessons from Ukraine

The investment is explicitly linked to lessons drawn from the war in Ukraine, where urban combat has become a defining feature of the conflict. Housen highlighted how modern surveillance technology—particularly drones—has dramatically increased visibility on the battlefield. “Through modern technologies, the enemy can see every movement,” he told VRT NWS. “We must learn to camouflage better and move differently. That’s why these new training facilities are needed.”

The facility is designed to provide realistic, safe training environments that simulate the complexities of urban operations—a need that has become urgent as European defense forces adapt to the realities of modern, technology-driven warfare.

## Part of a Broader Modernization Push

The Leopoldsburg skillhouse is one element of the Belgian government’s ambitious Kwartierplan, approved by the Ministerial Council in March 2026. The [Belgian Ministry of Defense](https://www.mil.be/nl/news/defensie-infrastructuur-klaar-voor-de-toekomst/) describes the plan as addressing “decades of underinvestment” in military infrastructure, with new and upgraded facilities across the country. The plan includes reopening former military sites, expanding the western presence from 14% to 22.5%, and investing in modern energy and safety standards.

For the Limburg region, the economic impact is significant. Johann Leten, CEO of Voka - KvK Limburg, said: “The strengthening of military presence in Limburg not only provides certainty in terms of security and international cooperation, but also creates concrete economic opportunities.” His organization noted in a [March analysis](https://www.voka.be/limburg/nieuws/kwartierplan-biedt-hefboom-voor-defensie-industrie-limburg) that the Kwartierplan could act as a catalyst for the region’s defense industry, creating opportunities in logistics, technology, and innovation.

## Looking Ahead

With completion targeted for 2028, the Leopoldsburg training complex represents a concrete step in Belgium’s commitment to modernizing its armed forces. The facility will not only improve the quality of training for Belgian soldiers but also enhance interoperability with NATO allies who increasingly emphasize urban warfare preparedness. Whether the skillhouse model will be replicated at other Belgian Defense sites, as Raskin suggested, remains to be seen, but the investment signals a clear recognition that Europe’s changing security landscape demands better-prepared troops—starting with the environments where future conflicts are most likely to be fought.
