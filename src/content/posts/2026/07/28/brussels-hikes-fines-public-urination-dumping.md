# Brussels Hikes Fines for Public Urination and Illegal Dumping

The City of Brussels will impose sharply increased fines for public urination and illegal dumping starting August 1, 2026, with penalties rising by up to 167 percent as part of a sweeping crackdown on urban incivilities. Public urination will cost 400 euros per incident — up from 250 euros — while illegal dumping of hazardous waste will be fined at 2,000 euros per cubic meter, double the current rate.

## Why the Crackdown?

The city’s Alderman for Public Cleanliness, Anas Ben Abdelmoumen (PS), said the increases are designed to make polluters pay rather than to generate revenue. “In 2025, processing all waste cost our city no less than 2 million euros, of which 600,000 euros for the 1,891 illegal dumps we had to clean up,” he told [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/07/28/stad-brussel-verhoogt-taksen-op-sluikstorten-en-wildplassen-fors/). He added that it is logical for those who “harm the quality of life of Brussels residents” to bear the costs.

Speaking to [RTBF Info](https://www.rtbf.be/article/ce-qui-change-ce-1er-aout-nouvelles-regles-pour-les-preavis-consultations-video-pour-les-logopedes-et-depots-clandestins-plus-lourdement-taxes-a-bruxelles-11763459), Ben Abdelmoumen put it bluntly: “We’re going to attack the wallets of those who don’t respect the laws.”

## The New Penalty Schedule

The revised tax regime introduces the following penalties:

- **Standard illegal dumping**: 1,000 euros per cubic meter (up from 500 euros)
- **Hazardous and construction waste**: 2,000 euros per cubic meter (up from 1,000 euros)
- **Unauthorized garbage bags on public streets**: 400 euros per bag (up from 150 euros)
- **Regulated bags or cardboard left outside permitted hours**: 200 euros per bag or cubic meter (new category)
- **Illegal graffiti**: 1,000 euros per square meter (up from 500 euros)
- **Public urination**: 400 euros per incident (up from 250 euros)

The city classifies these penalties as taxes rather than administrative fines, allowing enforcement officers from the municipality and the Brussels public cleanliness agency Net Brussel to issue them directly without judicial procedures.

## Carrot Alongside the Stick

The city is simultaneously expanding free alternatives to discourage incivilities proactively. From autumn 2026, households will receive **two free bulky waste collections per year** (up from one), with each collection covering up to three cubic meters (up from two). Combined with Net Brussel’s annual service, residents can now access up to 15 cubic meters of free bulky waste collection per year — a 67 percent increase.

On July 23, the city also expanded its ‘gastvrij toilet’ (hospitality toilet) network, with roughly 50 cafes and restaurants joining and receiving a 1,000-euro municipal subsidy to cover maintenance costs. A [new interactive digital map](https://www.bruzz.be/actua/samenleving/brussel-breidt-netwerk-gastvrije-toiletten-uit-en-verhoogt-boetes-voor-wildplassen-2026-07-23) now shows 90 public toilets across the municipality, including accessibility information — a response to Brussels’ long-standing shortage of public lavatories.

## Analysis: A Dual Strategy

This carrot-and-stick approach targets a persistent urban challenge. In 2025, the city issued 7,731 fines for illegal dumping — a 61 percent increase over 2024 — and one in five fines went to “waste tourists” from neighboring municipalities. The scale of the problem has driven the city to take increasingly aggressive measures.

While the steep fines may raise equity concerns — particularly the 400-euro penalty for public urination, which could disproportionately affect lower-income individuals and homeless populations — the city argues that the simultaneous expansion of free bulky waste collection and public toilets provides a meaningful alternative.

The effectiveness of the new regime will hinge on enforcement capacity. With Net Brussel agents empowered to issue taxes directly, the city hopes to increase detection and deterrence without overburdening the judicial system.

## Broader Reforms Taking Effect August 1

The cleanliness crackdown is one of several changes coming into force on August 1. At the federal level, the package includes:

- **Employment**: New rules allowing employers and workers to terminate contracts within the first six months with only one week’s notice, a reform championed by Federal Employment Minister David Clarinval (MR) but criticized by unions as effectively reintroducing probation periods.
- **Healthcare**: Speech therapists (logopèdes) can now offer video consultations under certain conditions, following physiotherapists and midwives who received the same right on July 1. Federal Health Minister Frank Vandenbroucke said the change “will lead to fewer no-shows” and “better follow-up” for patients.
- **Energy**: Excise duties on gas and electricity begin a phased shift through 2029, with higher taxes on gas and heating oil and lower taxes on electricity — a policy adopted by the Chamber in May to incentivize greener energy use.

## What’s Next

As the August 1 deadline approaches, residents and visitors to Brussels face a significantly stricter urban cleanliness regime. The city’s message is clear: polluters will pay. Whether the combination of heavier penalties and improved municipal services can sustainably reduce illegal dumping and public urination will be closely watched in the months ahead.
