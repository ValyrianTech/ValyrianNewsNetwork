# Wisconsin Tornado Destroys Homes, Cuts Power to Thousands

A powerful EF3 tornado tore through northeastern Wisconsin on Monday, carving a 12-mile path of destruction through the Fox Cities region that left homes splintered, businesses reduced to rubble, and more than 30,000 residents initially without power. Remarkably, officials reported no fatalities as of Tuesday evening, attributing the outcome to the storm's midday arrival and effective early warnings.

The tornado touched down just north of Wisconsin Avenue in Appleton at approximately 12:00 p.m. CDT on July 27 and remained on the ground for 26 minutes, according to the [National Weather Service](https://apnews.com/article/tornado-wisconsin-power-outages-appleton-menasha-854e3fa75280bb316f6081eaf0443798). It carved a path 12.1 miles long and up to 1,500 yards wide, with peak winds of 140 mph. The NWS upgraded the preliminary rating to EF3 on Tuesday, confirming winds capable of tearing roofs from well-constructed houses and tossing heavy vehicles.

## A Trail of Destruction Through the Fox Cities

The tornado swept through Appleton before cutting into Menasha, Neenah, Fox Crossing, and Grand Chute, affecting both Winnebago and Outagamie Counties. In Menasha, entire blocks were devastated. Heather Schroeder, a witness who works near the corner where a laundromat, bakery, and tavern once stood, told the Associated Press: "There's nothing left." Rescue workers pulled at least one person from a damaged building in the area.

Greg Van Driest, working in downtown Appleton when the storm hit, described the scene as deeply unsettling. "The word we all were using was 'ominous,'" he said. "You looked outside and you thought it was three in the morning, it was that dark."

The storm flipped cars onto lawns, stripped roofs from homes—some reduced to their outer walls—and downed countless trees and power lines. A transit bus in Appleton was trapped under a fallen tree. Fox Crossing Fire Station 41 was destroyed, with a fire truck buried under the rubble, and the community center suffered significant damage. A gas leak at a laundromat in Menasha added to the hazards. Major roadway US 10/WIS 441 was closed in both directions due to storm damage and gasoline spills.

## Power Outages and Emergency Response

The entire city of Menasha, home to roughly 18,000 residents, was initially believed to be without power. More than 30,000 customers lost electricity across the region. As of Tuesday, over 15,000 customers in Winnebago and Outagamie Counties remained in the dark, with power restoration expected to take several days.

Winnebago County Executive Gordon Hintz described the scene as "incredible, devastating," telling reporters at a Tuesday news conference that officials were "really still at the assessment stage." He noted that while some people were injured, "we're lucky" the tornado struck at midday when many residents were at work rather than at home.

Local states of emergency were declared Monday evening in Neenah, Menasha, and Grand Chute. On Tuesday, Wisconsin Governor Tony Evers declared a statewide state of [emergency for eastern Wisconsin](https://www.wbay.com/2026/07/28/governor-declares-state-emergency-after-mondays-fox-valley-tornado/), writing: "This emergency declaration will ensure every available resource is deployed so we can respond quickly to the needs on the ground. Together, we will recover and recover stronger."

Curfews from 10 p.m. to 5 a.m. were imposed in Fox Crossing and Menasha. A boil-water advisory was issued for residents on the east side of Fox Crossing and Menasha residents served by the Fox Crossing water utility after the system lost pressure. Shelters opened at Christ the Rock Community Church, the Elks Lodge, and Calvary Bible Church. Emergency crews conducted door-to-door searches to ensure no one was trapped or missing.

## A Rare and Unusual Storm

According to NWS Meteorologist Roy Eckberg, an EF3 tornado is relatively rare in northeastern Wisconsin. "To get that kind of tornado is pretty rare in northeast Wisconsin, but they do happen from time to time," he said, also noting the tornado's unusual southward movement—most tornadoes in the region track east or northeast. The twister was part of a larger storm system moving through the Great Lakes, fueled by a buildup of heat and humidity.

A follow-up assessment from the [National Weather Service](https://apnews.com/article/wisconsin-tornado-weather-c528fec31f227f13721f92fb7d93194e) confirmed the EF3 rating and detailed the tornado's 12.1-mile path. Outagamie County Executive Tom Nelson said, "All indications are that this was a devastating system that has caused extensive damage."

## Recovery and What’s Ahead

With no fatalities reported and door-to-door searches nearing completion, attention is turning to recovery. Wisconsin Emergency Management is working with counties to assess damage and coordinate resources. The widespread destruction—particularly the loss of a fire station, the closure of a major highway, and prolonged power outages—will have significant economic consequences for the Fox Cities region, which has a population of approximately 250,000.

The local and state emergency declarations are prerequisites for seeking federal disaster assistance, should it become necessary. For now, residents are beginning the long process of cleanup and rebuilding, supported by volunteers coordinated through Volunteer Fox Cities and donations pouring into area relief efforts.
