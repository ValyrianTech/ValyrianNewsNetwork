# Gansu Campsite Flood Kills 10, Injures 23 as Warnings Ignored

A sudden flash flood swept through the Shuangshimen scenic area in Gansu province on the afternoon of July 26, killing at least 10 people and injuring 23 others, after the scenic area received advance weather warnings but failed to evacuate visitors in time, according to [Caixin Global](https://www.caixinglobal.com/2026-07-27/flash-flood-at-campsite-in-northwestern-china-kills-10-injures-23-102468528.html).

The disaster struck at approximately 1:40 p.m. local time in Weiyuan County, Dingxi City, when torrents of water surged through the narrow gorge, sweeping away tents, vehicles, and panicked tourists. A total of 174 people were rescued from the scene, with 247 ultimately evacuated or rescued. State media [Xinhua News Agency](https://www.news.cn/gongyi/20260727/d5d0b7fa4a0a4e77b490ec8d7cf0365d/c.html) confirmed the official casualty figures and reported that search and rescue operations have been completed.

## Warnings Issued But Unheeded

Investigations have revealed a troubling sequence of missed opportunities to prevent the tragedy. The Weiyuan County Meteorological Station issued a yellow thunderstorm warning at 9:06 a.m. on July 26, valid for six hours, specifically noting risks to outdoor areas and tourist attractions. Three hours later, at 10:27 a.m., the Dingxi Emergency Management Bureau published a strong rainfall warning via its WeChat public account, explicitly cautioning about risks to valley areas and tourist sites.

According to [The Paper](https://m.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_33663621), the warning from the Dingxi bureau advised that "this strong precipitation may trigger moderate-to-high risks of secondary disasters including flash floods and geological hazards in rivers, valleys, and mountainous areas" and urged that "tourist attractions and outdoor recreational sites should closely monitor meteorological information." Despite these alerts, the scenic area did not implement an evacuation.

## Eyewitness Accounts of Sudden Destruction

The flood was triggered by sudden, intense rainfall upstream. Witnesses described the storm's ferocity to [NetEase](https://www.163.com/dy/article/L2Q4NCEI05565VL4.html): "Within ten-plus seconds, torrential rain poured down, along with dense hail." A tourist who arrived at the scenic area around noon witnessed the rapid escalation. He told reporters that hailstones were already pelting his car when he reached the entrance, prompting him to turn back. An hour later, floodwaters had reached the gate and swept away a vehicle.

One survivor's story captured both the tragedy and the narrow margin between life and death. A man and his fiancée were swept away by the floodwaters but were saved when a truck blocked their path. At 1:41 p.m., as emergency services scrambled to the scene, the man texted his family four words: "活着真好" ("It's good to be alive").

The normally narrow river, less than two meters wide, expanded to over 50 meters during the flood. A rescuer described the scene: "More than 30 vehicles were washed into a pile." The riverbed became choked with rocks, debris, and overturned cars, while up to 30 centimeters of silt accumulated at the scenic area's entrance.

## Emergency Response and Investigation

Local authorities launched a large-scale rescue operation immediately after the disaster. A total of 305 firefighters, 68 vehicles, and four search dogs from the national comprehensive fire rescue team were deployed. The Gansu Provincial Party Secretary and Governor traveled to the scene, and the provincial flood defense response was raised from Level IV to Level II. The National Flood Control Headquarters activated a Level IV emergency response for Gansu and dispatched a working group to the disaster site.

As rescue operations concluded, attention turned to accountability. The Shuangshimen scenic area is operated by Weiyuan County Cultural Industry Development Company, a county-level state-owned enterprise. The Paper reported that local government documents from 2024 described a surge in tourist numbers at the site during summer vacations, and in 2025, the Dingxi Municipal Bureau of Culture, Sports, Radio, Television and Tourism specifically promoted the area as an example of "ecological adventure camping."

The disaster has raised serious questions about the gap between China's meteorological early warning systems and on-the-ground implementation of safety protocols. Despite two separate official warnings issued hours before the flood, no evacuation order was given — a failure that cost 10 lives.

## Broader Context and What’s Next

This tragedy comes amid a period of severe weather across China. Earlier in July 2026, floods left 39 dead and sparked warnings over escaped animals. In 2025, natural disasters claimed 742 lives in the first nine months alone, with floods and geological events as the primary causes. China's Meteorological Administration activated a Level II emergency response for major meteorological disasters on July 26, underscoring the widespread nature of the severe weather.

Questions remain about whether officials or scenic area managers will face disciplinary action, what specific evacuation protocols existed at Shuangshimen, and whether this incident will lead to policy changes regarding campsite safety regulations across China. For the families of the 10 victims, those answers cannot come soon enough.
