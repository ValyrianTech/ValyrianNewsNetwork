# Zelenskyy and Trump Meet at White House Before Graham Funeral

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy met President Donald Trump at the White House on Tuesday, seeking continued military and diplomatic support from the United States as the war with Russia enters its fifth year. The meeting took place ahead of Senator Lindsey Graham's funeral at Washington National Cathedral, with Zelenskyy traveling to Washington specifically to honor the late South Carolina Republican.

The Oval Office sit-down, closed to the media and lasting approximately one hour, was their second face-to-face meeting in July 2026. Trump and Zelenskyy previously met at the NATO summit in Ankara, Turkey, where the president announced the U.S. would grant Ukraine a license to produce Patriot defense systems — a long-standing request from Kyiv to counter Russian missile attacks.

## Key Discussions on Patriot Production and Diplomacy

According to [AP News](https://apnews.com/article/trump-zelenskyy-ukraine-laura-loomer-graham-funeral-fc0dc04777dc7a1d2a3fd88502e8365b), the two leaders discussed licenses for Patriot interceptor production, several other ideas to aid Ukraine's defense, and the diplomatic process to end the conflict. Zelenskyy, in a post on X following the meeting, described it as “a good meeting” and thanked Trump for the administration's efforts.

“The President and I discussed licenses for Patriot interceptor production and several other ideas that could help,” Zelenskyy wrote. “We also spoke about diplomacy – it’s important that the diplomatic process be reinvigorated.”

The Ukrainian leader also offered condolences to Trump for Graham's death, calling the late senator “a true friend of Ukraine.”

White House press secretary Karoline Leavitt described both the Zelenskyy meeting and a separate sit-down with Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu as “positive and productive,” [NBC News reported](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/trump-administration/live-blog/trump-lindsey-graham-funeral-live-updates-rcna589564). U.S. officials present included Secretary of State Marco Rubio, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, and Secretary of War Pete Hegseth. Vice President JD Vance was absent, attending the Capitol ceremony for Graham.

## Graham’s Legacy and the Sanctions Bill

Graham, who died July 9 from a tear in his aorta at age 71, was one of Congress’s most consistent advocates for Ukraine. His final official act was visiting Kyiv, where he secured agreement on a sanctions package targeting Russian oil and gas purchasers. In a Fox News Channel interview on Tuesday, Trump recalled Graham’s hawkishness on Ukraine.

“Ukraine, he’s very militant about,” Trump said on “Fox & Friends.” “I mean, Lindsey liked war, to be honest with you.”

Republican leadership is now advancing Graham’s sanctions legislation, which would impose tariffs on goods from the world’s top five purchasers of Russian oil and natural gas — including China and India — and includes sanctions against President Vladimir Putin, senior Russian political and military leaders, and Russian financial institutions. The bill has dozens of bipartisan backers, though Senator Rand Paul has raised objections over tariff provisions.

## Shifting Political Dynamics

The meeting comes amid notable shifts in U.S. political dynamics regarding Ukraine. Far-right activist and Trump ally Laura Loomer, who has the president’s ear, recently traveled through Ukraine and met with Zelenskyy, admitting publicly that she was “bamboozled by Russian propaganda.” Loomer told The Associated Press ahead of the White House meeting: “It’s looking good for Ukraine, right? Going into this meeting next week at the Oval Office is looking pretty good.”

Her about-face represents a significant evolution in Trump-aligned circles’ perception of the Russia-Ukraine conflict and could influence Republican base opinions on continued aid.

## Russian-Iran Connection

Ahead of the meeting, Zelenskyy warned that Russia has been aiding Iran by capturing satellite imagery of U.S. bases in the Gulf region that later appear in Iran, asserting a “clear correlation” between Russian images and subsequent Iranian strikes. Trump downplayed the impact on Monday, telling reporters on Air Force One: “I don’t think they’ve been doing it, certainly not at a high level. And if they have, it has been very un-impactful.” Still, he said he would raise the issue with Putin.

## Broader Implications and What’s Next

The Trump administration’s embrace of Zelenskyy marks a significant evolution from their contentious Oval Office meeting in February 2025, when Trump, Zelenskyy, and Vance engaged in a heated exchange before reporters. Relations have improved markedly since, culminating in the NATO summit deal on Patriot licenses.

Following the White House meeting, Zelenskyy was scheduled to attend Graham’s funeral that afternoon alongside Trump and Netanyahu, and meet with a bipartisan group of senators at the Capitol that evening. The Senate is expected to vote on Graham’s Russia sanctions bill later Tuesday evening and to confirm Jay Clayton as director of national intelligence.

The day’s events underscore the critical importance of U.S. backing for Ukraine’s defense against Russian aggression. With U.S. midterm elections approaching, the administration faces pressure to balance foreign commitments with domestic economic concerns. Zelenskyy’s emphasis on “reinvigorating the diplomatic process” hints at potential openness to negotiations, though significant hurdles remain as the conflict continues to evolve.
