# 2026 Senate Map Shifts as Primary Season Nears Finish

With just a handful of primary elections remaining, the competitive landscape for the 2026 Senate midterms is coming into focus — and control of the chamber hangs in the balance. Democrats need to flip four Republican-held seats to win the majority, given that a Republican vice president holds the tie-breaking vote. According to [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/07/27/nx-s1-5907379/2026-midterm-election-senate-races), party strategists are bracing for potential Democratic gains of between one and three seats, enough to make every battleground state intensely competitive.

## Background

The president's party historically loses Senate seats in midterm elections — an average of roughly four since World War II. This cycle, President Trump's low approval ratings, the ongoing war with Iran, and economic frustration driven in part by tariffs on agricultural products are creating significant headwinds for Republican candidates. With several vulnerable incumbents and open seats on the map, the battle for the Senate is shaping up to be one of the most competitive in recent memory.

## North Carolina: Democrats' Best Opportunity

The retirement of Republican Sen. Thom Tillis has created an open-seat contest between former Democratic Gov. Roy Cooper and Michael Whatley, the former Republican National Committee chairman and a close Trump ally. Cooper, who won statewide twice even as Trump carried North Carolina, leads in both polling and fundraising. The Cook Political Report rates the race as Lean Democratic, making it the party's clearest pickup opportunity.

## The Toss-Up States

**Alaska** — The race has taken an unusual turn. Democrat Mary Peltola, a former U.S. representative and the first Alaska Native to serve in Congress, is challenging Republican Sen. Dan Sullivan. A retired teacher named Dan J. Sullivan — sharing the incumbent's name — has also entered the race. Under Alaska's ranked-choice voting system, both Sullivans could appear on the November ballot, potentially splitting the Republican vote. Peltola has led in eight consecutive polls and vastly outraised the incumbent, as reported by [Alaska Public Media](https://www.ktoo.org/2026/07/16/peltola-vastly-outraises-sullivan-in-alaskas-u-s-senate-race/). The Cook Political Report has shifted the race from Lean Republican to Toss-Up.

**Maine** — Five-term Republican Sen. Susan Collins — the only GOP senator running in a state Trump lost in 2024 — faces Democrat Troy Jackson, the former state Senate president running on an economic populist platform. Collins has historically drawn crossover support from Democratic voters, but the national environment may test her resilience.

**Michigan** — The open Democratic seat has produced an intense primary battle between centrist Rep. Haley Stevens and progressive Abdul El-Sayed, a Detroit health official. The winner will face Republican Mike Rogers, who narrowly lost the state's 2024 Senate race. The Aug. 4 primary will determine which direction the party takes.

**Ohio** — Former Democratic Sen. Sherrod Brown is seeking a rematch against incumbent Republican Sen. Jon Husted, who was appointed to the seat vacated by Vice President JD Vance. According to [Emerson College Polling](https://emersoncollegepolling.com/ohio-2026/), Husted leads Brown 50% to 44%. Brown's narrow 2024 loss in a state Trump has carried three times makes Ohio a reach, but Democrats believe his focus on kitchen-table economic issues could put the state in play.

## The Reaches

**Georgia** — Democratic incumbent Jon Ossoff is the only Democrat defending a seat in a state Trump won in 2024. He faces Republican Rep. Mike Collins, a Trump ally who won his primary with a last-minute endorsement from the president. Midterm electorate dynamics may favor Republicans, but Ossoff has been campaigning for the general election while Collins was battling his primary.

**New Hampshire** — Sen. Jeanne Shaheen's retirement has opened a seat Democrats have held for nearly two decades. Democratic Rep. Chris Pappas faces former Republican Sen. John Sununu in a state where 60% of residents disapprove of Trump, according to a University of New Hampshire survey.

**Iowa** — Republican Sen. Joni Ernst's retirement, combined with tariffs battering the state's soybean farmers and the economic fallout of the Iran war, has put the state in play. Democratic state Rep. Josh Turek, a Paralympic gold medalist, is challenging Republican Rep. Ashley Hinson.

## Texas: The Long Shot

The Texas Senate race has already made history. The primary between Attorney General Ken Paxton and then-incumbent Sen. John Cornyn was the most expensive Senate primary in U.S. history, with combined spending exceeding $100 million. Paxton now faces Democratic state Rep. James Talarico, who has built a campaign around affordability and anti-corruption. A [New York Times/Siena College poll](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/06/30/us/politics/texas-senate-poll-talarico-paxton.html) from late June found the race tied — a remarkable finding in a state that has not elected a Democrat to statewide office since the 1990s.

## The Path to the Majority

For Democrats to flip the Senate, the most plausible path runs through North Carolina, Maine, and Alaska — three races where they have strong candidates and favorable dynamics — plus at least one additional pickup from among Michigan, Ohio, Georgia, New Hampshire, Iowa, or Texas. Republicans privately acknowledge the possibility of Democratic gains of one to three seats but remain confident they can hold their narrow majority.

## What to Watch

Remaining primaries in Michigan (Aug. 4), Minnesota (Aug. 11), Alaska (Aug. 18), and New Hampshire (Sept. 8) will finalize the general election matchups. The outcome of the Michigan Democratic primary — establishment vs. progressive — will signal which wing of the party has momentum heading into November. With control of the Senate on the line, every battleground will be fiercely contested through Election Day on Nov. 3.
