# Oregon Wildfires Surge Past 1.3M Acres, Thousands Evacuated

Wildfires across Oregon have exploded to more than **1.3 million acres** across 34 major blazes, forcing thousands of residents to flee their homes as firefighters struggle to contain the flames amid dry conditions, high winds, and relentless lightning storms. As of July 27, over 6,200 people were under Level 3 "Go Now!" evacuation orders, with another 26,000 under lower evacuation levels, according to [OPB](https://www.opb.org/article/2026/07/27/wildfire-oregon-weather-public-safety/).

## A Season Primed for Disaster

Oregon entered the 2026 fire season under precarious conditions. Governor Tina Kotek declared a state of emergency on June 16, warning that "increasing heat, dry vegetation, and shifting winds continue to align and create dangerous conditions that demand immediate action." At the time, 414 fires had already burned more than 8,000 acres, and the governor cited record-low snowpack and persistent drought across nearly half the state's counties, as reported by [OPB](https://www.opb.org/article/2026/06/16/oregon-governor-kotek-declares-emergency-wildfire-threat/).

Those warnings proved prescient. By late July, a series of lightning storms — including one that produced over 2,000 strikes in just a few hours — ignited dozens of fires across central and eastern Oregon, rapidly overwhelming firefighting resources.

## Key Blazes and Evacuations

The largest fire currently burning is the **Crosswhite Fire** in Wheeler County, part of the Rowe Creek Complex, which has consumed 277,296 acres with only 27% containment. It has merged with multiple other fires including the Box Springs and Green Mountain fires. The **Big Grass Fire** near Jordan Valley has reached 196,963 acres at 5% containment, prompting Level 3 evacuations in the area, according to the [Statesman Journal](https://www.statesmanjournal.com/story/news/wildfires/2026/07/27/oregon-wildfire-updates-bench-beachcomb/91064283007/).

Other major fires include the **Hay Creek Complex** (181,821 acres, 60% contained), the **Coleman Creek Fire** (131,377 acres, uncontained), and the **Bench Fire** on the Warm Springs Reservation (40,200 acres), which forced the evacuation of the entire Warm Springs Agency area after merging from two separate blazes.

Warm Springs resident Alysia Klick'ump described the chaos of evacuation, telling OPB: "We got packed up in camper to evacuate, and the highway closed on each end of our reservation. We couldn't evacuate and were up until 1 or 2 a.m."

In Wasco County, the **Grasshopper Fire** east of Mount Hood reached 15,000 acres, placing more than 2,000 people under Level 3 evacuation orders. An evacuation shelter has been set up at Maupin High School.

## Second-Worst Season on Record

Oregon is now closing in on its worst wildfire season on record. With 1.47 million acres burned so far in 2026, the state is rapidly approaching the 1.97 million-acre record set in 2024. Notably, these figures have been reached before the end of July, with peak fire season typically extending through August and September.

At least 20 homes have been lost to wildfires this season, with that number expected to rise. One fatality has been reported, according to [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Oregon_wildfires).

"Any small ember right now could potentially start the next large wildfire," warned Jessica Neujahr, Public Affairs Officer for the Oregon Department of Forestry, as quoted by OPB.

## Transportation and Air Quality Impact

The fires have forced the closure of multiple major highways, including an 80-mile stretch of Interstate 84 between Baker City and Ontario, sections of U.S. Highway 20 and U.S. Highway 26, and Oregon Highway 245. The Oregon Department of Transportation has warned drivers not to rely on GPS for alternate routes, as smaller highways cannot handle diverted interstate traffic.

An air quality advisory has been issued for 14 Oregon counties — Baker, Crook, Gilliam, Grant, Harney, Jefferson, Malheur, Morrow, Umatilla, Union, Wallowa, Wasco, and Wheeler — until further notice, with intermittent smoke expected in neighboring counties.

## Emergency Response

More than 12,500 fire personnel are deployed across the state, including multiple West Coast task forces and National Guard units. Jordan Valley resident Tara Echave, whose community has been directly impacted by the Big Grass Fire, told the Statesman Journal: "We cannot do it on our own. We need air support, and we need ground support from people who are skilled firefighters and have the right equipment."

Kyle Williams, Deputy Director of Fire Operations at the Oregon Department of Forestry, noted that firefighters on the front lines are well-supported logistically, but encouraged donations to the Wildland Firefighter Foundation for those looking to help.

## What Lies Ahead

Forecasters expect additional lightning storms in northeastern Oregon, raising concerns that new fires could ignite even as crews work to contain existing blazes. With the state approaching its all-time acreage record and peak wildfire season still ahead, Oregon faces the prospect of a historically devastating fire year.

As Governor Kotek noted in her emergency declaration: "Prevention starts with every Oregonian — at home, at work, and out and about enjoying our great state." With conditions expected to remain hot and dry, the coming weeks will be critical in determining whether 2026 becomes the worst wildfire season Oregon has ever recorded.
