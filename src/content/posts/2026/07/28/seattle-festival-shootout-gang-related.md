# Seattle Festival Shootout Kills 3, Suspected Gang-Related

A shootout between rival groups at the Bite of Seattle food festival near the Space Needle on Sunday evening left three people dead and four others wounded, including a two-year-old boy, in what authorities believe was a gang-related attack. The violence erupted around 6 p.m. PDT on the final day of the annual three-day festival at Seattle Center, sending hundreds of panicked attendees scrambling for safety.

## The Incident

According to the [Seattle Police Department](https://spdblotter.seattle.gov/2026/07/27/gun-violence-at-seattle-center-takes-three-lives/), officers already stationed at the festival for security heard gunfire and responded within seconds. Preliminary evidence indicates that two individuals exchanged gunfire outside the Seattle Center Armory, with bullets striking seven bystanders in the crowded area. One officer witnessed a suspect firing.

Two victims were pronounced dead at the scene, and a third died later at Harborview Medical Center. The four surviving victims — a toddler, two men aged 23 and 27, and a 39-year-old woman — were hospitalized and later discharged in stable condition, according to hospital officials.

## The Victims

The King County Medical Examiner's Office identified the deceased as Carlos Israel Sanchez Villalba, 44, a husband and father of three described by family as a kind man who was always willing to help others; Ashley Whitehead, 56, who had recently moved to Washington from Alabama and was attending the festival with one of her adult daughters; and Junior Cee Niko Semo, 19, who authorities said was one of the perpetrators, [according to the Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/seattle-center-shooting-food-festival-space-needle-90548c65364e742a97fb7cb0fea31209).

Michelle Whitehead, cousin of victim Ashley Whitehead, remembered her as "a free spirit" with "the best sense of humor, the best gorgeous smile."

## Arrest and Investigation

A 15-year-old male suspect surrendered to police at the scene and was taken to SPD Headquarters for questioning. He waived his initial appearance in juvenile court on Monday, July 27, and a judge ordered him held in juvenile detention for investigation of firearms violations and first-degree assault. The King County prosecutor's office is expected to receive the case for a charging decision.

Seattle Police Assistant Chief Nicole Powell stated at a Monday press conference that investigators believe the shooting was gang-related, with at least three shooters involved. At least one suspect remains at large, and police have not released a description. The SPD Homicide Unit and Gun Violence Reduction Unit are leading the investigation, with support from the FBI and the Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives. Washington Governor Bob Ferguson deployed the Washington State Patrol SWAT team to assist local authorities.

## Official Response and Communication Challenges

The response was complicated by communication issues between city officials and police. Seattle Mayor Katie Wilson initially stated that two suspects were in custody, then later corrected herself, saying only one person had been detained. Police waited approximately five hours on Sunday before publicly announcing the arrest, drawing criticism.

Acting Chief Tyrone Davis, who managed the initial response while Police Chief Shon Barnes was out of town attending a law enforcement conference in Dallas, commended officers' rapid actions. "Our officers were there within seconds. They performed emergency aid to the wounded until the Seattle Fire Department was on scene," Davis said. "Simultaneously, officers launched a search for the people responsible for this senseless violence."

## Community Response

In the aftermath, acts of heroism emerged alongside grief. Moises Perez, a 30-year-old construction worker who lives next to Seattle Center, ushered more than a dozen people — mostly women and children — into his apartment during the shooting. "I was like, 'OK, we need to help these people,'" Perez told [the AP](https://apnews.com/article/seattle-shooting-space-needle-92628a1f1597e32ba44d6a2de3272646). His roommate, William Large, described survivors as being "in complete fear."

On Monday evening, dozens gathered at Seattle Center for a vigil, holding a moment of silence and raising flashlights in remembrance of those killed.

## Background

The Bite of Seattle, established in 1982, is an annual three-day festival that draws hundreds of food and retail vendors and approximately 350,000 attendees to Seattle Center, the site of the 1962 World's Fair. Sunday was scheduled to be the final day of the 2026 edition when the shooting occurred.

## What's Next

As of Tuesday, police continue to search for at least one remaining suspect while the 15-year-old in custody awaits a charging decision from the King County prosecutor's office. The investigation remains active, with authorities urging anyone with information to contact the SPD tip line. The tragedy adds to a broader pattern of mass shootings in the United States and raises renewed questions about public safety at large-scale community events.
