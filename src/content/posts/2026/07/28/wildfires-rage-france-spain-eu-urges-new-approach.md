# Wildfires Rage in France and Spain as EU Urges New Approach

Devastating wildfires sweeping across southern France and Spain have forced the evacuation of more than 300,000 people, destroyed tens of thousands of hectares of land, and claimed the lives of at least four firefighters. As a fourth heatwave descends on the region, Belgian European Commissioner for Crisis Management Hadja Lahbib has called for a fundamental shift in how the European Union prepares for and responds to such disasters, urging member states to adopt a "holistic approach" that prioritizes anticipation over reaction.

## The Worst Fire Season on Record

The 2026 wildfire season is already the most destructive in French history, with 116,085 hectares burned nationwide—shattering the previous record of 72,000 hectares set in 2022. According to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/liveblog/campings-en-vakantieparken-in-franse-badplaats-lacanau-preventie~1783176592077/), French President Emmanuel Macron described the situation as "the worst since World War II," noting that more than 160,000 hectares have been destroyed across the country and that "the fire season is far from over."

Spain is facing a parallel catastrophe. The fire raging across the provinces of Ávila, Madrid, and Toledo—now the largest in recent Spanish history—has consumed over 77,000 hectares and forced more than 91,000 people from their homes. A first fatality was reported near the eastern city of Castellón in the Valencia region, where 15,000 residents were evacuated from a fire with a perimeter of 21 kilometers.

## France: The Gironde Inferno

The epicenter of the French crisis remains the Gironde department in the Nouvelle-Aquitaine region, where a single "mega-fire" has advanced to within 15 kilometers of Bordeaux. More than 42,000 hectares of forest and vegetation have been incinerated, 240 homes destroyed, and 75 firefighters injured. On July 28, all campsites and holiday parks in the coastal town of Lacanau were preventively evacuated, displacing approximately 4,000 tourists.

The fire's behavior has been described as unprecedented by French authorities. Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez warned that the blaze "generated its own wind and advanced erratically toward the urban area of Bordeaux," while a pyrocumulonimbus cloud—a thunderstorm created by the fire's own heat—contributed to its ferocious spread.

More than 220,000 people have been evacuated in the Gironde alone. The highway A63 remains partially closed, rail traffic has been disrupted, and 13,000 businesses have been forced to halt operations. French Economy Minister Roland Lescure called the situation "an economic bomb for a region that didn't ask for this," while insurers have agreed to cover temporary housing for evacuees for three weeks.

In the Var department in southern France, a separate fire on the Gros Bessillon mountain has damaged 60 homes, with some completely destroyed. A 23-year-old man has been arrested on suspicion of arson for a series of fires set between July 19 and 24. In total, 162 people have been arrested in France since July 6 for suspected arson.

## Spain: A Historic Blaze

Spain's largest recorded wildfire has devastated the region west of Madrid, with 50,000 hectares burned in Ávila province alone. Spanish Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez visited affected areas and warned of "difficult, complex hours ahead," while King Felipe and Queen Letizia visited evacuees sheltering in a sports hall in Villamanta. The government has announced it will declare heavily affected areas as disaster zones.

Nine evacuated towns in the Toledo province have since been allowed to return home, but authorities caution that a new heatwave approaching the Iberian Peninsula could reignite the crisis. More than 131,000 hectares have burned in Spain so far in 2026.

## Europe's Response

Thirteen firefighting aircraft have been mobilized through the EU Civil Protection Mechanism—seven sent to France and six to Spain—with support from the Czech Republic, Croatia, Germany, Portugal, Slovakia, Sweden, Turkey, Greece, and Italy. Portugal alone dispatched 134 firefighters and 41 vehicles to Spain. The Copernicus satellite program is providing emergency mapping.

The EU maintains a strategic reserve of 22 firefighting aircraft and five helicopters—capacity that Commissioner Lahbib says is set to nearly double, with approximately ten additional Canadair aircraft on order.

## Lahbib's Call for a New Approach

In an interview with [RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/megafeux-de-forets-en-europe-pour-hadja-lahbib-les-etats-membres-ont-un-peu-tendance-a-appeler-quand-ils-sont-depasses-il-faut-une-approche-holistique-11763422), Lahbib argued that the current model of waiting for crises to overwhelm national capacities before activating EU solidarity is no longer sustainable. "Member states have a tendency to call when they are overwhelmed," she said. "We invite them to activate the solidarity mechanism as quickly as possible before being submerged."

Lahbib stressed that anticipation requires political and financial commitment: "Anticipating means also freeing up the right budgets, making the right decisions at the right time—that is, outside of crises." She pointed to the EU Commission's proposed €10.7 billion budget for preparedness, currently under negotiation as part of the next Multiannual Financial Framework, adding: "I sincerely hope member states remember what is happening today when it comes time to vote."

The Commissioner acknowledged that preparedness levels vary widely across the EU, with Nordic countries better prepared for armed conflict, Italy for earthquakes, and Belgium for floods. She urged member states to use restructuring funds, cohesion funds, and EU solidarity funds for risk anticipation.

## Political Pressure Mounts

The Belgian Green party (Groen) has called for an emergency summit of European leaders on wildfires. Chairman Aimen Horsch argued that current EU capacity is insufficient, stating that "firefighters on the ground say themselves that it is inadequate" and that Europe is "not prepared for forest fires and climate disasters."

Macron, meanwhile, has defended France's preparedness, noting that the budget for civil security has nearly doubled over the past decade and that a record 67 aircraft have been mobilized, 38 of which are French-owned. "We have deployed maximum resources," he said, while promising a thorough evaluation once the fires are extinguished.

## What's Ahead

The immediate concern is a fourth heatwave expected to push temperatures to 40°C in southwestern France and across the Iberian Peninsula starting this week. The combination of extreme heat, dry vegetation, and shifting winds threatens to reverse any progress made by firefighting crews. The fire in Gironde has been "stabilized" but is not under control, and authorities fear re-ignition as conditions deteriorate.

For Lahbib, the lesson is clear: "Every summer, every extreme episode is an opportunity to learn lessons." Whether Europe seizes that opportunity will depend on the political will of 27 member states to invest in anticipation before the next fire season arrives.
