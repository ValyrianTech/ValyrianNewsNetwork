# US Envoys Walk Out on France at UN Over Rights Criticism

UNITED NATIONS — American diplomats walked out of a United Nations Security Council meeting on Monday as France's delegation began speaking, escalating a bitter diplomatic rift between the two NATO allies over human rights. The extraordinary public rebuke followed France's sharp criticism of the Trump administration's opposition to renewing the UN human rights chief for a second term.

Deputy US Ambassador Dan Negrea delivered a blistering statement after the walkout, accusing France of "disingenuous grandstanding" and "politicized drivel," and declaring that the United States "will not be affording them the benefit of listening" until France renounces its "condescending and disrespectful rhetoric," according to [AP News](https://apnews.com/article/un-us-france-human-rights-walkout-bbca1f1381a40aa2870889fd8fa99442).

## The Trigger

The confrontation began on July 24, when the UN General Assembly voted 144-10 with 13 abstentions to grant Volker Türk, the UN High Commissioner for Human Rights, a second four-year term. The United States was among only 10 countries voting "no," alongside Russia, Israel, North Korea, Nicaragua, and Mali, as [Al Jazeera reported](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/7/27/us-diplomats-walk-out-as-france-addresses-un-security-council).

The following day, France's UN Mission in Geneva posted on social media: "The US used to be a beacon of human rights. Not anymore. Today, it stands alongside North Korea, Nicaragua, Mali & Russia, isolated. And the world no longer listens to it."

The post ignited a firestorm. US UN Ambassador Mike Waltz responded on X, accusing France of "coddling some of the worst human rights abusers" and voting "for someone who has been lecturing, free, sovereign democracies like the United States, the UK, and Israel, while cozying up to the world's worst oppressors," according to [France 24](https://www.france24.com/en/france/20260727-us-walks-out-of-un-security-council-meeting-as-france-condemns-rights-vote).

## The Walkout

At Monday's Security Council meeting on Russia's war in Ukraine, the US delegation physically left the chamber when France's turn to speak arrived. In his follow-up remarks, Negrea did not hold back.

"Today, I remind them that it is the United States that remains the beacon of liberty for the world," Negrea said. "The United States has stood by France in every conflict where their freedom has been imperiled, and tolerated their grandstanding out of a sense of camaraderie and mutual respect, but no more."

Negrea said the US would continue to walk out on France until it renounces its "condescending and disrespectful rhetoric."

France's UN Ambassador Jerome Bonnafont did not directly acknowledge the walkout when he took the floor. Instead, he noted France's historic role in helping the United States gain independence and said France works to preserve the UN's independence and "its ability to act in service of the charter and act to serve human rights and peace."

## Broader Strains

The incident is the latest flashpoint in steadily deteriorating US-France relations under President Donald Trump. The two allies have clashed over NATO defense spending commitments, US troop deployments in Europe, trade policy, and Trump's interest in acquiring Greenland, a territory of NATO member Denmark. Macron has also taken a leading role in building European military autonomy, including discussions of a European nuclear deterrent.

The dispute also reflects the Trump administration's increasingly adversarial posture toward multilateral institutions. The US has cut funding for multiple UN agencies and withdrawn from dozens of UN entities since 2025.

## A Polarizing UN Rights Chief

Volker Türk, an Austrian human rights lawyer, has in his role sharply criticized Russia's invasion of Ukraine, Israel's conduct in Gaza, and US immigration policy — urging a "massive rethink" before the 2026 World Cup. Rights groups argue his work is "more critical than ever as autocracies gain influence globally," while the US maintains he unfairly targets democratic nations.

Monday's walkout — an unusually aggressive diplomatic maneuver between treaty allies — illustrates how fundamentally the US and its European partners now disagree on the foundational question of who holds legitimate authority to critique human rights practices on the global stage.

## What's Next

The incident raises significant questions about the future of US-France cooperation within the UN Security Council and NATO. With the US increasingly isolated on human rights votes alongside Russia and North Korea, the traditional Western alliance on human rights norms is fracturing. France under Macron is likely to accelerate its push for European strategic autonomy, while the Trump administration has signaled it may further reassess US engagement with UN human rights bodies.

Whether this public rift heals or widens will depend largely on whether the two sides can de-escalate — something neither appears inclined to do at present.
