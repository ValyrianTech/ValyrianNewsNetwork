# Trump DOJ Settles with Pro-Life Father for 7 Figures

A Christian father of 11 who was arrested at gunpoint by the Biden administration's Department of Justice for peacefully protesting outside a Tennessee abortion clinic has reached a seven-figure settlement with the Trump administration, resolving a case that became a flashpoint in the national debate over the federal government's treatment of pro-life activists.

Paul Vaughn, a Tennessee father of 11, was among a wave of individuals prosecuted under the Freedom of Access to Clinic Entrances (FACE) Act by the Biden DOJ following the Supreme Court's 2022 decision to overturn Roe v. Wade. On July 28, 2026, the Trump administration finalized a settlement acknowledging that the original prosecution constituted an abuse of power, according to a press release from the [Thomas More Society](https://www.thomasmoresociety.org/news/trump-doj-reaches-settlement-with-paul-vaughn-acknowledging-biden-administrations-abuse-of-federal-power-against-pro-life-father), the legal organization representing Vaughn.

## Background and Arrest

In March 2021, Vaughn participated in a peaceful protest at a Tennessee abortion clinic where attendees prayed and sang hymns. He served as a messenger between police and protesters. In October 2022, he was charged with violating the FACE Act—a 1994 federal law prohibiting threats of force, obstruction, or property damage intended to interfere with reproductive health services—as well as a felony Conspiracy Against Rights charge carrying up to 10.5 years in prison.

Two days after the charges were filed, federal agents raided Vaughn's home at gunpoint in the early morning hours, according to [Fox News](https://www.foxnews.com/media/first-fox-trump-doj-reaches-settlement-pro-life-protester-targeted-biden-admin), which first reported the settlement. Seven of Vaughn's 11 children—ranging from 18 months to 18 years old—were home at the time. Three of his children were "basically held at gunpoint" by an agent in the yard during the encounter. Vaughn said his 5-year-old continues to express anxiety due to the event.

## Conviction and Pardon

Vaughn was convicted of a misdemeanor FACE Act violation and a felony Conspiracy Against Rights violation, despite his involvement being limited to serving as a messenger between police and peaceful protesters. On January 23, 2025, President Donald Trump pardoned Vaughn and 23 other pro-life activists convicted under the FACE Act during the Biden administration.

Shortly after taking office, the Trump DOJ also issued new FACE Act charging guidance, limiting future prosecutions to "extraordinary circumstances" or cases involving death, serious bodily harm, or major property damage. As [NPR](https://www.northernpublicradio.org/2025-03-09/trump-dojs-limits-on-face-act-enforcement-fuel-concern-from-abortion-providers) reported, the department characterized the prior enforcement as "the prototypical example" of what it called the weaponization of law enforcement against conservatives.

The policy shift drew concern from abortion providers. Planned Parenthood nurse practitioner Phebe Brandt told NPR that the new guidelines "basically tells them that there will be no consequences for them to come into our centers, disrupt things, and potentially even be violent." Melissa Fowler of the National Abortion Federation argued that "it really shouldn't take an abortion provider being murdered for the federal government to enforce a law that has been effective."

## Settlement and Reaction

Vaughn's settlement follows a similar seven-figure payout in April 2026 to Mark Houck, another pro-life activist whose home was raided by the FBI in front of his children. [Fox News reported](https://www.foxnews.com/media/pro-life-dad-whose-home-raided-fbi-wins-1-mil-settlement-from-doj) that Houck received more than $1 million after filing a lawsuit over his 2022 arrest.

Speaking about the settlement, Vaughn said: "Obviously, money doesn't equate justice, and there's not a true sense of justice in the settlement, but it is an acknowledgment of wrong, and it is something to help make up for the time lost."

Steve Crampton, Senior Counsel at the Thomas More Society, said: "What was done to Paul and his family was a profound injustice, and we are grateful that this settlement acknowledges that. We also commend the Trump Department of Justice for having the courage to look at what its predecessor did, call it what it was—an abuse of power—and change course."

## Broader Implications

The settlement creates a financial precedent that may encourage other pro-life activists prosecuted under the Biden DOJ to pursue similar claims. It also reinforces the conservative narrative of federal law enforcement weaponization that has been central to political criticism of the Biden administration.

Meanwhile, the FACE Act's future as an enforceable statute remains uncertain under the Trump administration's limited enforcement policy. Abortion-rights advocates argue that the reduced federal oversight will embolden clinic disruptions, while conservative groups maintain that the original prosecutions targeted constitutionally protected religious expression.

## What's Next

With the settlement now finalized, Vaughn and his family are seeking to move forward. "My family has been through a traumatic ordeal that never should have happened. But today brings an end to this portion of the story," Vaughn said in a statement. The question remains whether additional lawsuits from other pro-life activists will follow, and whether congressional scrutiny of the original prosecutions may lead to further consequences for officials involved.
