# China-Africa Trade Surges After Zero-Tariff Expansion

China's policy of granting zero-tariff treatment to all 53 African countries with which it has diplomatic ties has been widely welcomed across the continent, with trade data showing a 23.5% year-on-year surge in Chinese imports from Africa during the first two months of implementation, [Global Times reported](https://www.globaltimes.cn/page/202607/1366894.shtml) on July 27, citing Foreign Ministry Spokesperson Lin Jian.

The landmark policy, which took effect on May 1, 2026, expands on China's previous tariff elimination for 33 African Least Developed Countries (LDCs) that began in December 2024. The new measure extends zero-tariff treatment to 20 non-LDC African nations — including Kenya, Egypt, and Nigeria — for an initial two-year period, during which China plans to formalize the arrangement through bilateral economic partnership agreements.

## Context and Background

The policy was first announced by Chinese President Xi Jinping in a message to the African Union Summit in Addis Ababa, Ethiopia, in February 2026. It represents China's latest effort to deepen economic ties with a continent that has been its largest trading partner for 17 consecutive years.

As [English.gov.cn](https://english.www.gov.cn/policies/policywatch/202605/01/content_WS69f45e35c6d00ca5f9a0ac01.html) reported, goods previously facing tariffs of 8% to 30% — including cocoa from Côte d'Ivoire and Ghana, coffee and avocados from Kenya, citrus and wine from South Africa — are now entering China duty-free. The first shipment to benefit from the expanded policy was 24 metric tons of fresh apples from South Africa, which cleared customs in Shenzhen on the first day of implementation.

## Trade Data Highlights

In the first half of 2026, total China-Africa trade reached 1.41 trillion RMB (approximately $208 billion), the highest figure ever recorded for a six-month period, Lin Jian revealed at a July 27 press briefing. Full-year trade is expected to hit a new record.

Lin noted that China's imports from Africa in May and June — immediately after the zero-tariff policy took effect — totaled 193.8 billion RMB ($28.61 billion), marking a 23.5% year-on-year increase. He also highlighted that four categories of African products — dried chili peppers, coffee beans, cashews, and wild aquatic products — have achieved regional integrated access to the Chinese market.

According to Lin, 75% of China's exports to Africa consist of capital goods and intermediate products — machinery and equipment intended to support the continent's industrialization and agricultural modernization, rather than consumer goods.

## African Leaders and Experts React

African Union Commission Chairperson Mahmoud Ali Youssouf praised the policy as a "very brotherly gesture that all Africans appreciate," describing it as "very timely" for a continent grappling with global crises and rising protectionism elsewhere, as reported by English.gov.cn.

Tang Xiaoyang, Dean of International Relations at Tsinghua University, noted that unlike preferential policies offered by some Western countries, China's initiative "does not require reciprocal openness as a prerequisite and attaches no strings to Africa's internal affairs." He said the policy shows "China's respect for African countries and its sense of responsibility in driving common development with them."

David Monyae, Director of the Africa-China Research Centre at the University of Johannesburg, wrote that "the coherence of China's trade policy, especially its policy toward Africa, reflects China's commitment to promoting the construction of a fair global trading system."

## Geopolitical Implications

Analysts view the zero-tariff expansion as part of a broader strategic calculus. As [Asia Times](https://asiatimes.com/2026/07/chinas-zero-tariff-play-aims-to-lock-in-african-loyalty/) noted, the policy is "a deliberate geostrategic lever in Beijing's long-term competition with the United States and Europe for influence over the Global South."

By offering unfettered market access without the conditionalities often attached to Western aid or trade agreements, China is positioning itself as a more pragmatic partner for African nations. The policy also aligns with goals outlined in China's 15th Five-Year Plan (2026-2030), which calls for expanding high-standard opening-up and deepening South-South cooperation.

Lin Jian dismissed concerns about trade imbalances, calling them "discourse traps fabricated by those with ulterior motives, attempting to interfere with China-Africa friendly cooperation." He emphasized that "international trade is a two-way choice; there is no such thing as forced buying or forced selling."

## Forward Look

The zero-tariff program runs through April 30, 2028, after which it is expected to be formalized through China-Africa Economic Partnership agreements. Commercial operators are already responding: Long Sulan, a regional director of Chinese tea processing firm Hunan Rift Valley Purple, said the policy "will not only increase the income of Kenyan tea farmers, but also bring the unique flavors of high-quality African tea to more Chinese consumers."

As Africa's population — projected to reach nearly 2.5 billion by 2050 — continues its rapid expansion, the continent's economic relationship with China is likely to deepen further. The zero-tariff policy represents a significant milestone in that trajectory, offering African exporters unprecedented access to the world's second-largest consumer market while strengthening Beijing's ties across the Global South.
