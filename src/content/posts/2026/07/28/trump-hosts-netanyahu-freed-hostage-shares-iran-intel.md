# Trump Hosts Netanyahu; Freed Hostage Shares Iran Intel

President Donald Trump hosted Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu at the White House on Tuesday for their first face-to-face meeting since the two leaders launched a joint military campaign against Iran approximately five months ago. The roughly 90-minute closed-door session, which [AP News](https://apnews.com/live/trump-netanyahu-iran-news-updates-07-28-2026) reported included discussions on scaling down the Iran war, expanding the Abraham Accords, and Iran's nuclear activities at Pickaxe Mountain, came as the White House weighs diplomatic and military options.

## A Pivotal Meeting Amidst Escalating Conflict

The meeting marked a critical juncture in the U.S.-Israel relationship as both leaders face mounting domestic pressures. Netanyahu, who arrived at the White House just before 11 a.m. and departed at 12:25 p.m., is up for reelection amid a strained relationship with Trump. The president, meanwhile, confronts an unpopular conflict that has [Fox News](https://www.foxnews.com/live-news/trump-netanyahu-meeting-iran-war-white-house-july-28-2026) described as driving economic hardship ahead of the November midterm elections.

Israel's UN Ambassador Danny Danon told reporters that scaling down the war was "number one on the agenda." Israeli Defense Minister Israel Katz added that Israel "very much wants to strike Iran's energy targets" but that the United States has not approved such strikes over fears of triggering a global oil crisis.

White House press secretary Karoline Leavitt described both the Trump-Netanyahu meeting and an earlier session with Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy as "positive and productive."

## Freed American Provides Harrowing Prison Intel

In a dramatic development, Morad Tahbaz, an American-Iranian conservationist freed from Iranian captivity in September 2023, provided top-secret intelligence about conditions inside Iran's Evin Prison. According to [Fox News](https://www.foxnews.com/world/freed-american-reveals-brutal-iran-prison-ordeal-warns-hostages-face-greater-danger), Tahbaz detailed life inside Ward 2A — a high-security detention facility controlled by the intelligence branch of Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC).

Tahbaz spent nearly six years in Evin Prison, including four months in solitary confinement under a constant bright light, allowed out only twice daily for about 10 minutes and blindfolded each time. "This is probably the harshest and toughest imprisonment that any prisoner will go through," Tahbaz told Fox News Digital.

He described how Iranian authorities once accused him of training lizards to spy on military installations for Israel. "You can't make this stuff up," he said. "It just gives you an idea of the kind of narrative that this regime comes up with."

Tahbaz warned that Americans still held in Iran face heightened danger amid the ongoing conflict. "The hostages are, you know, just a commodity from Iran's perspective, to be used at the right time," he said.

## Diplomatic Crossroads

The meetings unfolded against the backdrop of renewed diplomatic efforts to end the conflict. Mediators from Pakistan, Egypt and Qatar believe the U.S. and Iran are close to reviving a failed memorandum of understanding (MOU) that briefly paused hostilities in June. Iran and Oman have reportedly signed off on a new proposal, though the White House waited to decide until after Trump's consultation with Netanyahu.

Trump has publicly threatened devastating consequences if no deal is reached, claiming he could destroy Iran's bridges "in less than two hours" and its power plants "within one day." However, he also expressed openness to diplomacy, telling Fox News that Iran knows he'll "finish the job" if negotiations fail but that he would prefer to avoid further destruction.

## Economic Fallout

The conflict's economic toll continues to mount. The Conference Board reported Tuesday that U.S. consumer confidence fell to 90.8 in July from 92.2 in June, the third straight monthly decline. Gas prices have climbed to $4.10 per gallon, according to AAA, as the Iran war disrupts global energy markets. Brent crude oil briefly fell to around $86 per barrel during the pause in strikes.

## A Day of Mourning and Diplomacy

Tuesday's events were intertwined with memorial services for the late Sen. Lindsey Graham, the South Carolina Republican and close Trump ally who died earlier in July. Trump, Netanyahu, and Zelenskyy all participated in tributes to Graham, whose flag-draped casket arrived at the U.S. Capitol for a congressional tribute. Trump recalled Graham as "a guy that liked war" but noted the senator had recently advocated for making a deal with Iran.

## What to Watch For

With the Senate expected to confirm Jay Clayton as permanent intelligence director and mediators reporting progress on reviving the U.S.-Iran MOU, the coming days will test whether diplomatic channels or military escalation prevails. The next round of Israel-Lebanon peace talks is scheduled for Aug. 4-6 in Rome, while the White House weighs its next move on Iran — a decision shaped significantly by Tuesday's pivotal discussions.
