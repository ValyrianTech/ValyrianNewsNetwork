# Teen Pleads Guilty to Apalachee School Shooting, 4 Killed

A 16-year-old pleaded guilty on Thursday to all 55 counts stemming from the 2024 mass shooting at Apalachee High School in Winder, Georgia, a case that has become one of the deadliest school shootings in state history and a landmark legal test for parental accountability.

According to [AP News](https://apnews.com/article/apalachee-georgia-school-shooting-colt-gray-8be7ba5efae9cce53e0f8c61253a5f1b), Colt Gray entered the plea in Barrow County Superior Court before Judge Nicholas Primm. The plea was non-negotiated, meaning no deal was struck with prosecutors.

## The Shooting and Its Victims

The attack on September 4, 2024, claimed the lives of two 14-year-old students — Mason Schermerhorn and Christian Angulo — and two teachers: Cristina Irimie, 53, a math teacher, and Richard "Ricky" Aspinwall, 39, a math teacher and assistant football coach. Nine others were wounded, seven by gunfire. Gray was 14 at the time and was charged as an adult.

The weapon used was a SIG Sauer M400 semi-automatic rifle, an AR-15 style firearm, given to Gray by his father as a Christmas present in December 2023, according to [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Apalachee_High_School_shooting).

## Victim Impact: A Community Grieves

The [Atlanta Journal-Constitution](https://www.ajc.com/news/2026/07/apalachee-high-school-shooting-suspect-pleads-guilty/) reported that the hearing included devastating victim impact statements from families who described lives shattered by the violence.

Breanna Schermerhorn, mother of Mason, told the court she could "acknowledge that he was failed without excusing what he did." She added: "I have no doubt in my mind that if Colt had actually attended school as he should have, Mason would have befriended him."

Shayna Aspinwall, wife of teacher Richard Aspinwall who was shot and killed when he exited his classroom to investigate, described the lasting toll. "Every holiday, birthday, anniversary and ordinary day is a reminder of what has been stolen from us," she said. "It is a life sentence of grief and trauma for those left behind."

Principal Jessica Rehberg called Gray a "thief" and a "murderer," saying his actions "ripped a community of its pride." She noted that even the sound of a balloon popping remains triggering for students and staff.

## A History of Missed Warnings

The case has been marked by a series of systemic failures that allowed the shooting to unfold despite multiple red flags. Wikipedia reports that in May 2023, the FBI investigated Colt Gray for making school shooting threats on Discord — his username was a Russian transliteration of "Lanza," referencing the Sandy Hook shooter — but the case was closed when the responding deputy could not open an email attachment containing the evidence on his phone.

Gray did not attend a single day of school during the 2023-2024 academic year despite being registered, and he was promoted to ninth grade. Eight days before the shooting, his maternal grandmother sought to have him admitted to a crisis center but was told parental permission was required; his father, Colin Gray, did not follow through.

Investigators found a detailed step-by-step plan in Gray's black notebook, including notations like "Don't shoot anyone in the bathroom. You will alert people." They also discovered a shrine in his bedroom to Nikolas Cruz, the perpetrator of the 2018 Parkland school shooting. Gray told investigators he had "became obsessed with him on an emotional level."

## A Landmark Case for Parental Accountability

The guilty plea arrives just months after a separate, precedent-setting conviction. According to [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/7/24/us-school-shooter-pleads-guilty-in-georgia-following-fathers-conviction), Colin Gray was convicted in March 2026 on 29 counts including second-degree murder and involuntary manslaughter for giving his son the rifle — the first time a Georgia parent has been criminally charged in connection with a mass school shooting.

Colin Gray is scheduled to be sentenced the week of July 27, 2026. His conviction used Georgia's second-degree murder statute to link a parent's criminal negligence in child-rearing directly to third-party deaths, a legal approach that legal experts view as a significant expansion of parental accountability.

## What Comes Next

Judge Nicholas Primm will now determine whether Colt Gray should be eligible for parole after hearing remaining evidence, case summaries, and victim statements. Gray faces a mandatory minimum sentence of life in prison. The sentencing proceedings are expected to continue into next week.

The Apalachee shooting remains the deadliest school shooting in Georgia's history, and the parallel prosecutions of both the teenage shooter and his father mark a pivotal moment in the evolving legal landscape surrounding school shootings in the United States.
