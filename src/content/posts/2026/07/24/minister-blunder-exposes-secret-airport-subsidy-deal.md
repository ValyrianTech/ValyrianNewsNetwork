# Belgian Minister's Blunder Exposes Secret Airport Subsidy Deal

Flemish Mobility Minister Annick De Ridder (N-VA) faced a major political embarrassment this week after her cabinet accidentally shared a strictly confidential settlement agreement with all members of the Flemish Parliament via the parliamentary intranet. The document — a settlement between the Flemish government and Egis, the French operator of Antwerp-Deurne and Ostend-Bruges airports — contained a single sentence that has ignited a political firestorm: a commitment to subsidize the regional airports until 2039.

## The Accidental Disclosure

The blunder occurred on Monday, July 20, when De Ridder's cabinet sent routine documents to parliament for an upcoming committee meeting. Among them was the confidential settlement, which was meant to be kept secret under penalty of a substantial damages claim. The error only came to light on Wednesday during a parliamentary committee meeting, when Green party MP Bogdan Vanden Berghe calmly informed the minister that all MPs had already received the document. According to [Het Laatste Nieuws](https://www.hln.be/binnenland/een-specifiek-zinnetje-veroorzaakt-beroering-dit-staat-in-geheime-deal-die-minister-de-ridder-per-ongeluk-deelde~afadcda3/), the minister's visible shock spoke volumes.

## What the Settlement Contains

The settlement resolves a long-running legal dispute between the Flemish government and Egis. The airport operator was claiming €14.83 million from Flanders for unpaid subsidies, PFAS soil investigations, and other costs, while Flanders was seeking €12.1 million from Egis for the "loan" of civil servants. Both parties have agreed to drop their mutual claims.

However, the most controversial element is a single sentence: "Both parties conclude a new subsidy agreement, running from January 1, 2026, to October 26, 2039." This effectively commits the Flemish government — and all future governments — to subsidizing the regional airports for another 13 years. The end date coincides with the duration of Egis's concession to operate the airports.

As [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/07/23/regionale-luchthavens-vlaams-parlement-annick-de-ridder-dading/) reported, the settlement also includes commitments from Flanders to renovate the aircraft parking platform at Deurne and cover future soil remediation costs, while Egis has pledged to pay two overdue invoices by January 2028 and keep current with future payments.

## Political Fallout

The disclosure has united opposition parties across the political spectrum in their criticism. Green MP Bogdan Vanden Berghe questioned whether the Finance Inspectorate would approve the deal, telling VRT NWS: "The airports are getting a very good deal. I cannot imagine the Finance Inspectorate agrees with this."

Jos D'Haese of the radical left PVDA party struck a populist tone, saying: "I don't understand why we have to contribute tax money when millionaires want to go on holiday with their private jets." Liberal opposition MP Egbert Lachaert (Anders) argued that the controversy obscures a more fundamental question: "Why are we still giving subsidies to airports without a future vision, where only the jet set still departs?"

Even coalition partner CD&V expressed frustration. MP Mien Van Olmen stated: "I really don't understand that we as parliament members cannot inspect the comprehensive, overarching note about this dossier," warning that the lack of transparency undermines parliament's oversight function.

## Minister's Defense

De Ridder has pushed back against the criticism, arguing that the settlement was a necessary legal instrument to end years of costly litigation. In a written statement reported by [Het Laatste Nieuws](https://www.hln.be/binnenland/minister-de-ridder-weerlegt-dat-dading-met-regionale-luchthavens-afkopen-van-rechtszaak-is~a9259ce3/), she emphasized that Flanders had already lost a court case in 2024 on part of the dispute, making a settlement the prudent course.

"We are not giving a blank check, and that is a big difference with the past," De Ridder said. She noted that the subsidies remain subject to five-year evaluations, with the first adjustment possible in 2031. Coalition partner Melissa Depraetere (Vooruit) supported this position, stating that the settlement "mainly serves to close a discussion from the past" and does not negate the current subsidy reductions.

## The Broader Subsidy Debate

The controversy comes just days after the Flemish government announced a 25% reduction in regional airport subsidies — from €131 million for 2020-2024 to €96 million for 2025-2029. Critics argue that the settlement contradicts the spirit of these cuts, effectively locking in subsidies for more than a decade.

The regional airports have been loss-making for years and rely heavily on government funding to cover safety, personnel, and firefighting costs. Antwerp-Deurne, in particular, has seen its commercial traffic decline sharply after TUI withdrew its flights, leaving the airport primarily serving private jets and training flights — 6,400 business flights in 2024, with top destinations being Cannes and Nice.

## What's Next

The incident raises serious questions about information security within the minister's cabinet and government transparency more broadly. The opposition has called for the Finance Inspectorate to review the settlement, and parliament may exercise its right to inspect the document under confidentiality rules. With coalition partners already expressing unease, the affair could test the stability of the Flemish government and may lead to broader reforms in how confidential government documents are handled.

As right-leaning publication [V-Nieuws](https://www.v-nieuws.be/minister-de-ridder-n-va-belooft-om-verlieslatende-luchthavens-te-subsidieren-tot-2039/) noted, Vlaams Belang MP Tom Lamont has proposed a fundamentally different approach: phasing out subsidies in favor of a strategic plan focused on public core tasks and defense interests. The coming weeks will reveal whether this political storm leads to substantive policy change or simply becomes another chapter in Belgium's long-running debate over regional airport subsidies.
