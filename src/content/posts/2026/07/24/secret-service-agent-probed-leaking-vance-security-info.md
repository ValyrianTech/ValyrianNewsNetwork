# Secret Service Agent Probed for Leaking Vance Security Info

A U.S. Secret Service agent assigned to Vice President JD Vance's protective detail is under investigation for allegedly leaking operational security information, the agency confirmed Thursday. The unidentified member of the Vice Presidential Protective Division has been placed on administrative leave pending both an internal probe and a potential criminal inquiry, according to [Fox News](https://www.foxnews.com/politics/secret-service-agent-jd-vance-detail-investigated-allegedly-leaking-operational-security-info).

## Background of the Investigation

The investigation stems from a report published by MS Now around July 17, 2026, which detailed frustrations within Vance's security detail over last-minute personal and family travel requests. The article revealed that agents had grown weary of hastily arranged trips, including a planned Marine Two helicopter flight from the vice president's residence at the U.S. Naval Observatory to Joint Base Andrews so that Vance's son could attend a golf lesson. The flight was eventually canceled due to severe thunderstorms.

The [Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/vance-secret-service-investigation-travel-d4364b9715854764030a77ee1f39621c) reported that the unnamed agent is believed to have been a source for that story, prompting the Secret Service to launch its investigation.

## Official Statements

Secret Service Chief of Communications Anthony Guglielmi said in a statement that the agent is "the subject of an administrative investigation, and potential criminal inquiry, involving allegations of compromising operational and information security." Guglielmi added that while the agency would not comment on specifics, "any conduct that potentially threatens the safety of a protectee will not be tolerated."

USSS Deputy Director Matthew Quinn emphasized the demanding nature of protective assignments. "Our agents work tirelessly to ensure protectees' safety and security, while also preserving normalcy to the extent possible," Quinn said. "There is no room for compromise."

A Vance spokesperson told Fox News that while protecting a vice president with "a large policy portfolio and a young and growing family presents a unique challenge, agents of the Secret Service do so with excellence every day."

## Details of the Alleged Leak

The MS Now report alleged that some agents had created custom coins and stickers mocking the frequency of Vance's last-minute travel, referencing the vice president's Secret Service code name "Bobcat" — with the coins reading "Bobcat OTR Survivors Club." Agents reportedly told the outlet that "off-the-record" (OTR) trips sometimes force them to cancel personal plans and require hastily constructed security arrangements.

According to the [New York Post](https://nypost.com/2026/07/23/us-news/secret-service-suspends-agent-accused-of-leaking-jd-vances-travel-details/), the MS Now piece also reported that Vance and his wife Usha had taken "several" last-minute helicopter trips while house hunting in northern Virginia. The administration defended the travel, noting that helicopter transport minimizes disruptions to the public compared to motorcades — a point Usha Vance herself made during a CBS "Sunday Morning" interview.

A Trump administration official explained that "most past presidents and vice presidents have not had young children, and therefore the detail has not been presented with this nature of protective mandate." The Vances have four children, including newborn Alec Neel, who was born on July 19 — the first baby born to a sitting vice president in more than 150 years.

## Broader Context: Leak Crackdown and Threat Environment

The investigation comes amid an aggressive Trump administration crackdown on leaks to journalists. Earlier in July 2026, four New York Times reporters were subpoenaed to testify about a story on security flaws in Trump's new Air Force One. The government later withdrew those subpoenas after admitting legal errors.

Separately, Secret Service Director Sean Curran warned on July 23 that the agency has opened probes into 10,000 threats so far in 2026, describing the threat picture as "as large as I've ever seen it." The agency has recently dealt with a shooting at the White House Correspondents' Dinner and an armed man near the White House.

## What's Next

The investigation raises several unresolved questions — chief among them whether the agent will face criminal charges in addition to administrative discipline, and how the probe will affect morale within the Vice Presidential Protective Division. The agent's identity has not been publicly disclosed, and it remains unclear whether the suspected leak was a single instance or part of a broader pattern of disclosures.

As the investigation proceeds, the case highlights the unique security challenges posed by protecting a vice president with young children — a scenario unprecedented in modern American history.
