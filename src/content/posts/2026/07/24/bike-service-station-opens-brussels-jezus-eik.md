# Bike Service Station Opens on the Edge of Brussels

A new kind of service station has opened on the southeastern edge of Brussels — one designed not for cars, but for bicycles. Bike Square, a Belgian bicycle retail and service company, has launched what it describes as the first "decarbonated service station" for cyclists in Jezus-Eik (Overijse), directly adjacent to the F204 cycle highway leading into the Belgian capital.

## A Strategic Location for Commuters

The new hub, located at Chaussée de Bruxelles 582 in Jezus-Eik, sits at a critical junction for cyclists commuting from the southeastern suburbs into Brussels. Positioned alongside the F204 cycle highway — a major cycling artery being built parallel to the E411 motorway — the facility is designed to serve the growing number of daily cyclists who need reliable maintenance and repair services along their route.

According to [La Libre Belgique](https://www.lalibre.be/belgique/mobilite/2026/07/23/une-station-service-pour-les-velos-ouvre-ses-portes-a-la-lisiere-de-bruxelles-ZV3Q2J5RCBDPXAZHTECJR2QG3A/), the opening is part of an ambitious expansion plan. Bike Square, which already operates two stores in Ixelles and Woluwé-Saint-Pierre, opened two new locations simultaneously — the Jezus-Eik hub and another at Rue JB Timmermans 52 in Woluwé-Saint-Lambert. The company aims to grow from four to seven stores by 2027, covering the South-East of Brussels and its periphery.

## More Than a Bike Shop

Bike Square's Jezus-Eik location goes beyond traditional bike retail. The facility features an "Open Source" workshop capable of servicing and repairing all bicycle brands, with a particular focus on electric bikes and speed pedelecs — a rapidly growing segment that requires more specialized technical expertise.

As the company's CEO Michel Lagasse, former head of bicycle leasing company Ubike, explained through the company's communications, the strategic fundraising that enabled this expansion marks the launch of an ambitious growth plan. The exact amount raised and investor details have not been publicly disclosed.

## Infrastructure Needs Services

The concept behind the Jezus-Eik hub is rooted in a broader philosophy about what makes cycling infrastructure truly effective. In a [blog post](https://www.bikesquare.be/en_GB/blog/news-that-keeps-you-rolling-1/bike-highway-services-mobility-hub-jezus-eik-7) published in June, Bike Square argued that bike paths alone are insufficient for achieving meaningful modal shift.

"For too long, cycling infrastructure has been reduced to a simple matter of civil engineering: drawing lines, pouring asphalt, and separating traffic flows," the company wrote. "A bike path without services is dead infrastructure. It's just a route, not a solution."

The post drew a compelling analogy: "Infrastructure is the skeleton. Service is the muscle. The skeleton provides direction and structure. But without the muscle — technical assistance, local maintenance, and logistical reassurance — the body doesn't move."

## The F204 Cycle Highway

The timing of the opening is closely tied to the development of the F204 cycle highway, a project managed by De Werkvennootschap (Werken aan de Ring), the Flemish agency responsible for works around the Brussels Ring Road. Construction on the cycle highway began in February 2023, with expected completion by mid-2028.

According to the [official project page](https://www.werkenaandering.be/en/working-on/cycling-infrastructure/f204-bicycle-highway), the first section of the cycle highway is already in use between Kersenbomenlaan and Vlierbeekberg in Overijse, with the second phase set to begin in August 2026. The route will eventually continue across the Walloon border to Rosières and Wavre, and a new cycle and pedestrian bridge over the E411 in Jezus-Eik will connect the cycle highway to the redeveloped town centre.

The F204 addresses a critical gap: despite Overijse being within cycling distance of Brussels, there was previously no convenient route. The existing path was narrow, had significant elevation changes, and ran alongside the busy motorway.

## A Model for Public-Private Synergy

The Bike Square Jezus-Eik location represents an emerging model of public-private collaboration in mobility infrastructure. The F204 cycle highway is a public investment by the Flemish government, while Bike Square's service hub is a private sector response to that investment — a dynamic that could be replicated along other cycle highways across Belgium and Europe.

Bike Square's Jezus-Eik location had its grand opening on March 28, 2026, with a Family Day event featuring guided tours, test rides of electric bikes, and activities for young cyclists. The La Libre Belgique article covering the strategic significance of the opening was published on July 23, 2026, highlighting the broader implications for sustainable mobility in the Brussels region.

## What's Next

With plans to reach seven stores by 2027, Bike Square is betting that the demand for professional bicycle services will continue to grow as more commuters make the switch from cars to bikes. The company's "decarbonated service station" concept — offering sales, repairs, and maintenance at strategic points along major cycling routes — could become a template for cycling infrastructure development across Europe.

As the F204 cycle highway nears completion and Brussels continues to invest in cycling infrastructure, the question is no longer just about building more bike lanes. It is about building the service networks that make cycling a reliable, everyday choice for commuters.
