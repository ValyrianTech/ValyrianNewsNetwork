# Gentse Feesten: Violence Rises as Overcrowding Sparks Safety Debate

Police in Ghent have reported a significant increase in violence during the 2026 edition of the Gentse Feesten, one of Europe's largest free city festivals, with 47 registered fights in the first five days — a 31% rise compared to 36 during the same period in 2025. Visitors and residents are pointing to severe overcrowding as a key driver of tensions, with one attendee describing the conditions as "packed like sardines."

## Context: A Festival Under Pressure

The Gentse Feesten, first held in 1843, is a 10-day cultural extravaganza drawing 1.5 to 2 million visitors annually to Ghent's historic city center. This year, over 770,000 visitors arrived in the first five days alone — approximately 5,000 more than the same period in 2025, according to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/07/22/gentse-feesten-halfweg-meer-meldingen-grensoverschrijdend-gedr/). The festival reached the one million visitor milestone on July 24.

## Rising Incidents and New Concerns

Beyond the 47 registered fights, police recorded 36 reports of transgressive behavior in the first five days — more than triple the 11 reports from the same period in 2025. Of those, 15 were related to sexual transgressive behavior. A stabbing incident near the Sint-Michielsbrug left one person hospitalized, though the victim was released the following day. Four suspects have been identified, with three already interrogated.

Police spokesperson Matto Langeraert cautioned that the statistics may overstate the problem. "Not everything registered as a fight is actually a fight," he told [Het Laatste Nieuws](https://www.hln.be/gent/meer-geweld-op-gentse-feesten-volgens-politie-dit-zeggen-bezoekers-zelf-als-je-als-sardines-op-elkaar-staat-is-een-kleine-duw-soms-al-genoeg~a65fd0d3/). "Thanks to our camera shield, we see very quickly where tensions are rising. Police often intervene before a single punch is thrown, but those interventions are also registered as 'fights.'"

Authorities have responded by installing an extra camera on the Sint-Michielsbrug — a known hotspot near McDonald's where many incidents occur — and introducing a mobile reporting point for transgressive behavior, operating nightly from 23:30 to 08:30.

## Voices from the Festival: Overcrowding as a Root Cause

Visitors themselves offer a more nuanced picture. Robin Tacq, a festival attendee, noted a qualitative shift: "There have always been people who had one too many and caused trouble. But what stands out now is that the aggression in groups has increased, and the nature of the fights is much more aggressive."

Alain Rose, another visitor, pointed directly to overcrowding. "At certain performances, you're standing packed together like sardines. Most people follow the rules, but a small push is sometimes enough for a few angry looks."

However, not all share this view. Simon Schellaert, a bar worker with a decade of experience at the festival, said: "I've been walking around here for five days, from around 6 PM to past 8 AM, and I haven't seen a single fight." He suggested that incidents are concentrated at specific hotspots like the Korenmarkt, while most of the festival zone remains peaceful.

## Political Fallout

The rising violence has become a political flashpoint. Opposition parties N-VA and Vlaams Belang have accused the city government of downplaying the problem. Mathieu Cockhuyt of N-VA told [Het Laatste Nieuws](https://www.hln.be/gent/n-va-en-vlaams-belang-halen-uit-naar-stadsbestuur-na-toenemend-geweld-op-gentse-feesten-iedereen-ziet-wie-komt-vieren-en-wie-komt-vechten~a9d28726/) that "from city hall, we mainly hear a good-news show. Many Ghent residents have felt for some time that the atmosphere in certain places was turning grim. Now the figures confirm that violent incidents are increasing."

Festival Mayor Joris Vandenbroucke (Vooruit) pushed back, arguing that "on social media and by the far right, a very negative image of the Gentse Feesten is being created that does not match reality at all. For 99 percent of visitors, everything proceeds without problems." Mayor Mathias De Clercq (Voor Gent) emphasized that "hundreds of thousands of people are enjoying themselves here, every day and every evening. It is a handful of individuals who come to the Feesten with bad intentions, and we deal with them strictly."

## Analysis: Perception vs. Reality

The debate highlights a central tension: is violence genuinely increasing, or are better surveillance and ubiquitous smartphones simply making it more visible? The police themselves initially reported fewer fights before correcting the figures upward. What is clear is that the festival's enormous scale — with record visitor numbers concentrated in a historic city center with limited space — creates conditions where tensions can easily escalate.

The appearance of knife-related incidents this year marks a particularly concerning new development. As visitor Krono Van Laethem observed: "In the media, I read a lot about fights and people pulling knives. That last one is a new and disturbing phenomenon this year."

## What's Next

The Gentse Feesten continues through the weekend with enhanced security measures in place. The political debate over crowd management and public safety is likely to intensify as more data becomes available. Longer-term questions about the festival's scale and sustainability in its current form — including potential calls for downsizing, ticketing, or relocation of certain activities — may gain traction once the music fades and the city takes stock.
