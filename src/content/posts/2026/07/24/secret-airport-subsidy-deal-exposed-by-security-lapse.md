# Secret Airport Subsidy Deal Exposed After Security Breach in Flemish Parliament

A routine document submission has spiraled into one of the most damaging political controversies of the year for the Flemish government, after a confidential settlement agreement guaranteeing regional airport subsidies until 2039 was accidentally distributed to every member of the Flemish Parliament.

## The Disclosure That Shook the Chamber

The drama unfolded on Wednesday, July 22, during a parliamentary committee meeting on finance. Flemish Mobility Minister Annick De Ridder (N-VA) had just informed MPs that they could inspect a confidential settlement with Egis, the French operator of Antwerp-Deurne and Ostend-Bruges airports, under strict conditions. Green party MP Bogdan Vanden Berghe then delivered a bombshell: the document was already in every MP's inbox, having been accidentally included among routine committee materials sent on Monday.

According to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/07/23/regionale-luchthavens-vlaams-parlement-annick-de-ridder-dading/), the minister's visible shock spoke volumes. The document carried a strict confidentiality clause with penalties for unauthorized disclosure — a clause her own cabinet had just violated.

## What the Settlement Reveals

The settlement resolves a long-running legal dispute that had been draining both sides. Egis was claiming €14.83 million from Flanders for unpaid subsidies, PFAS soil investigations, and other costs, while Flanders was seeking €12.1 million from Egis for the "loan" of civil servants. Both parties have agreed to drop their mutual claims.

But the explosive element is a single sentence committing both parties to "a new subsidy agreement, running from January 1, 2026, to October 26, 2039." This effectively locks in government funding for the loss-making regional airports for another 13 years, coinciding with the duration of Egis's concession. As [Het Laatste Nieuws](https://www.hln.be/binnenland/een-specifiek-zinnetje-veroorzaakt-beroering-dit-staat-in-geheime-deal-die-minister-de-ridder-per-ongeluk-deelde~afadcda3/) reported, Flanders also committed to renovating the aircraft parking platform at Deurne and covering future soil remediation costs, while Egis pledged to pay two overdue invoices by January 2028.

## A United Opposition

The disclosure has united opposition parties across the political spectrum in their criticism, though from sharply different angles.

Green MP Bogdan Vanden Berghe questioned whether the Finance Inspectorate would approve the deal, telling VRT NWS: "The airports are getting a very good deal. I cannot imagine the Finance Inspectorate agrees with this."

Jos D'Haese of the radical left PVDA struck a populist tone: "I don't understand why we have to contribute tax money when millionaires want to go on holiday with their private jets."

Liberal opposition MP Egbert Lachaert (Anders) argued the controversy obscures a more fundamental question: "Why are we still giving subsidies to airports without a future vision, where only the jet set still departs?"

Even coalition partner CD&V expressed frustration. MP Mien Van Olmen stated: "I really don't understand that we as parliament members cannot inspect the comprehensive, overarching note about this dossier," warning that the lack of transparency undermines parliament's oversight function.

## Minister's Defense

De Ridder has pushed back, arguing the settlement was a necessary legal instrument to end years of costly litigation after Flanders lost a court case in 2024 on part of the dispute. In a written statement reported by [Het Laatste Nieuws](https://www.hln.be/binnenland/minister-de-ridder-weerlegt-dat-dading-met-regionale-luchthavens-afkopen-van-rechtszaak-is~a9259ce3/), she emphasized: "We are not giving a blank check, and that is a big difference with the past."

She noted that subsidies remain subject to five-year evaluations, with the first adjustment possible in 2031. Coalition partner Melissa Depraetere (Vooruit) supported this position, stating the settlement "mainly serves to close a discussion from the past."

## The Subsidy Paradox

The controversy comes just days after the Flemish government announced a 25% reduction in regional airport subsidies — from €131 million for 2020-2024 to €96 million for 2025-2029. Critics argue the settlement contradicts the spirit of these cuts, effectively guaranteeing subsidies for more than a decade.

The regional airports have been loss-making for years and rely heavily on government funding for safety, personnel, and firefighting costs. Antwerp-Deurne, in particular, has seen its commercial traffic collapse after TUI withdrew its flights, leaving the airport primarily serving private jets and training flights — 6,400 business flights in 2024, with top destinations being Cannes and Nice.

As right-leaning publication [V-Nieuws](https://www.v-nieuws.be/minister-de-ridder-n-va-belooft-om-verlieslatende-luchthavens-te-subsidieren-tot-2039/) noted, Vlaams Belang MP Tom Lamont has proposed a fundamentally different approach: phasing out subsidies in favor of a strategic plan focused on public core tasks and defense interests.

## What Comes Next

The incident raises serious questions about information security within the minister's cabinet and government transparency more broadly. The opposition has called for the Finance Inspectorate to review the settlement, and parliament may exercise its right to inspect the document under confidentiality rules. With coalition partners already expressing unease, the affair could test the stability of the Flemish government and may lead to broader reforms in how confidential government documents are handled.

The coming weeks will reveal whether this political storm leads to substantive policy change or simply becomes another chapter in Belgium's long-running debate over regional airport subsidies.
