# Iran Rejects U.S. Cease-Fire Proposal as Strikes Intensify

Iran has reportedly rejected a 15-point U.S. cease-fire proposal delivered by Iraqi Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi, even as American forces completed their 13th consecutive night of airstrikes on Iranian targets and President Donald Trump threatened a "massive attack" to force Tehran back to negotiations, according to [Time](https://time.com/article/2026/07/24/us-iran-ceasefire-attack-energy-prices-oil-hormuz-strait/). The rejection marks the latest failure in diplomatic efforts to end a conflict that began on February 28, 2026, and has since destabilized energy markets and spread across the Middle East.

## Conflicting Narratives Around the Cease-Fire Proposal

The [New York Times reported](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/07/23/world/middleeast/iran-iraq-cease-fire-proposal.html) that Iraqi Prime Minister al-Zaidi — who had visited the White House on July 14 — delivered the U.S. proposal to Iranian officials in Tehran on July 23, only to have it rejected outright. Iranian officials reportedly described the offer as a "temporary deal" that failed to resolve the core dispute over control of the Strait of Hormuz.

However, both Tehran and Baghdad have pushed back forcefully against the characterization of the events. Iran's state media IRNA called the NYT report "completely diversionary and misleading," claiming the proposal was for a "temporary 10-day ceasefire," as reported by [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/world/live/2026/jul/24/us-iran-donald-trump-iraq-bahrain-kuwait-jordan-latest-news-updates). Baghdad also denied the report, with al-Zaidi's media office calling the claims "completely baseless and had no connection to reality."

Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi addressed the matter on state television, stating: "The problem is not passing messages. The problem is America's outlook, which is illogical, greedy and controlling."

## Escalation on the Battlefield

While diplomatic channels remain mired in confusion, military operations have continued to intensify. The [New York Post reported](https://nypost.com/2026/07/24/world-news/iran-rejects-latest-cease-fire-proposal-as-trump-considers-massive-attack-report/) that U.S. Central Command strikes on July 23 targeted Iranian military command centers, drone storage facilities, communication networks, coastal surveillance sites, and maritime capabilities "to further diminish the threat Iran poses to civilian mariners and commercial vessels transiting the Strait of Hormuz."

Iran retaliated by launching drone strikes targeting U.S. bases in Bahrain, Jordan, Kuwait, and Iraq, according to The Guardian. The head of Iran's Khatam al-Anbiya central military command, Ali Abdollahi, threatened: "For every proud citizen of the Islamic Republic of Iran who is martyred, one American service member will be sent to hell." Iran's Revolutionary Guard Corps warned civilians in countries hosting American forces to stay at least 500 meters away from U.S. positions.

## Trump Threatens Escalation

President Trump has signaled he is prepared to dramatically expand the scope of military operations. In interviews with Axios and Israel's Channel 12 on July 23, Trump said he is considering a "massive attack" — larger than Operation Epic Fury, the opening salvo of the war.

"I am considering a massive attack. Bigger than ever before. I am close to making a decision. We are all set for it," Trump told Axios, as reported by Time. He added that Iran "hasn't received enough pain yet," suggesting that the administration's strategy is to inflict sufficient damage to force a diplomatic breakthrough on Washington's terms. Trump said Israel "would join in two minutes" if asked but that "we don't need anybody."

The [Associated Press reported](https://apnews.com/article/iran-us-hormuz-strait-war-24-july-2026-78c2dbf538f6e61ab816479a4d9bdd85) a series of explosions near a military base in northern Iraq on July 24 following the latest round of U.S. strikes.

## The Strait of Hormuz: The Core Dispute

The unresolved status of the Strait of Hormuz — through which approximately 20% of the world's oil passes — remains the central obstacle to any cease-fire. Iran militarized the waterway at the start of the war, halting commercial shipping and sending energy prices soaring. The June 17 U.S.-Iran memorandum of understanding left the question unresolved, deferring discussions to Oman and Iran. Iran has insisted it wants to manage passage through the Strait, potentially charging fees.

On July 24, an Omani delegation arrived in Iran to discuss a framework for managing ship traffic, according to IRNA, suggesting diplomatic channels remain open despite the battlefield escalation. However, Iranian officials have reportedly indicated they are not interested in any deal that leaves the Strait question unresolved.

## Regional Spillover and Economic Impact

The conflict continues to widen beyond the U.S.-Iran theater. Iran-backed Houthi rebels in Yemen have begun attacking Saudi ships in the Red Sea, threatening the Bab-el-Mandeb Strait — a second major maritime chokepoint. Trump warned that "major military punishment will be inflicted upon Iran and, of course, the Houthis, themselves" if attacks continue. Brent crude topped $100 per barrel on July 24 for the first time since May 2026, reflecting growing market anxiety.

Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu is scheduled to meet Trump at the White House on July 28, a meeting that could shape the next phase of the conflict. The war has already cost U.S. taxpayers an estimated $113.3 billion as of June 2026, with thousands of casualties on both sides.

## What's Next

With both sides locked in a cycle of escalation — U.S. strikes triggering Iranian retaliation and vice versa — the path to de-escalation remains unclear. Trump's "massive attack" threat may be a negotiating tactic designed to force Iran back to the table, or it could signal a genuine expansion of hostilities. The Omani mediation effort on the Strait of Hormuz offers a potential diplomatic off-ramp, but the fundamental disagreement over control of the waterway must be resolved for any lasting cease-fire to take hold. The July 28 Trump-Netanyahu meeting will be closely watched for signs of a coordinated shift in strategy.
