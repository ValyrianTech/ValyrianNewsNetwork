# Pentagon's Iran War Death Toll Drops from 18 to 14

WASHINGTON — The Pentagon's official count of U.S. troop deaths in the Iran conflict fell from 18 to 14 between Wednesday and Thursday, removing four service members killed during renewed fighting this month from the tally. The sudden discrepancy has triggered bipartisan scrutiny and two mutually conflicting explanations from military officials and Pentagon leadership.

The shift on the Defense Department's Defense Casualty Analysis System (DCAS) website came one day after President Donald Trump traveled to Dover Air Force Base for the dignified transfer of four fallen troops, whom he publicly counted among 18 American war dead. A separate "Names of Fallen" list on the same site currently includes just 13 service members, according to [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/jul/23/pentagon-service-members-killed-iran).

## The Conflicting Explanations

Three anonymous military officials told the New York Times that the four troops were removed because their deaths occurred after Trump declared a ceasefire in April, suggesting an administrative decision to limit the officially recognized scope of the conflict. The service members — three killed in Jordan and one in northern Iraq — died during the weekend of July 18-19 as fighting reignited.

However, acting Pentagon press secretary Joel Valdez attributed the change to "temporary data disruptions" that he said would be corrected quickly, according to [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/24/pentagon-removed-four-official-iran-war-death-toll.html). As of July 24, the website still listed the death toll at 14, with no timeline for correction provided.

The contradictory explanations have drawn sharp criticism from current and former military leaders. Retired Lt. Gen. Ben Hodges, former commander of U.S. forces in Europe, told reporters: "The administration seems to have a real hard time being honest about what is going on — not just about casualties, but about the whole war in general," as reported by [19FortyFive](https://www.19fortyfive.com/2026/07/the-pentagons-count-of-americas-iran-war-dead-just-dropped-overnight-and-the-explanations-dont-match).

## The Four Fallen Service Members

The service members omitted from the count have been identified by the Defense Department. In Jordan, the fallen include 1st Lt. Tyler James Feehan, 25, of Ewa Beach, Hawaii; Isabella Gonzales, 19, of Carrollton, Texas; and Angel S. Rampersad, 28, of Ozone Park, New York. In northern Iraq, Staff Sgt. Michael Emmanuel Swinton, 30, of Spring Lake, North Carolina, was killed in a controlled detonation of an Iranian drone at Erbil airbase, according to [Military.com](https://www.military.com/pentagons-official-iran-war-death-toll-suddenly-drops-from-18-to-14).

## Trump's Contradictory Stance

Trump himself has used the higher figure. At a July 23 event, he said: "I had a very sad day yesterday. I went to Dover, four great American patriots killed. That's 18 … and one is too many, but it's 18." Earlier, he posted a chart on Truth Social comparing the 18 deaths in the Iran conflict to casualties in previous U.S. wars.

## Broader Transparency Concerns

The casualty count discrepancy is part of a larger pattern of opacity in the Trump administration's handling of the Iran conflict. The Pentagon has withheld information about dozens of service members injured in recent attacks, with more than 100 troops injured in the last two weeks alone. Pentagon spokesman Sean Parnell described the vast majority as "minor concussions," with 96 percent of injured troops returning to duty.

Congressional leaders from both parties have raised alarms. Rep. Adam Smith (D-Wash.), ranking member on the House Armed Services Committee, said Trump has not shared casualty statistics with Congress, stating there has been "no transparency from the President or the Pentagon about the full extent of equipment losses and casualty statistics."

Rep. Thomas Massie (R-Ky.) went further, suggesting the Pentagon is "pretending there have been two Iran wars separated by a brief cease-fire" to justify excluding the recent deaths. Massie accused Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth of breaking the law by operating more than 90 days without congressional authorization under the War Powers Resolution.

## The War So Far

Operation Epic Fury, the Pentagon's designation for the U.S.-Israeli war on Iran, began February 28, 2026 — approximately five months of active conflict. Trump signed a temporary ceasefire with Iran in April, and a subsequent interim deal was agreed in June but has since collapsed. Renewed fighting escalated in mid-July, with US military strikes on Iran entering their 13th consecutive night as of July 24.

All 14 deaths currently listed on the DCAS website occurred in March 2026, including seven service members killed in a tanker refueling accident in Iraq. Only seven of the 14 are classified as "hostile" deaths.

## What to Watch For

The central question remains unresolved: will the Pentagon correct the count back to 18, or will it remain at 14? The Pentagon has not held a major briefing on the war since early May, and lawmakers are calling for oversight hearings into the casualty reporting discrepancies. Meanwhile, the conflict shows no signs of de-escalation, with Iran attacking commercial shipping in the Strait of Hormuz and Trump threatening "major military punishment" against regional adversaries.
