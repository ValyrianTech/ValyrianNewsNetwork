# Xi Departs for SCO Summit, State Visits to Kyrgyzstan and Egypt

Chinese President Xi Jinping departed Beijing on the morning of August 30 aboard a special aircraft bound for Bishkek, where he will attend the 2026 Shanghai Cooperation Organization (SCO) Summit and conduct state visits to Kyrgyzstan and Egypt at the invitation of Presidents Sadyr Japarov and Abdel Fattah El-Sisi, according to [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/politics/leaders/20260830/c38cfb09f97249eb943a12b81eb4b2f8/c.html). The five-day trip, running from August 30 to September 3, marks Xi's second overseas visit of 2026 and his first state visit to Egypt in a decade.

## A Landmark SCO Summit

The 2026 SCO Summit, scheduled for August 31 to September 1 in Bishkek, coincides with the organization's 25th anniversary. Under the theme "25 Years of the SCO: Together Towards Sustainable Peace, Development and Prosperity," the summit will bring together leaders from member states including Russia, India, Pakistan, Iran, and Belarus, as [CGTN](https://www.cgtn.com/specials/2026/SCO-Summit-2026.html) reports.

Since its founding in Shanghai in June 2001 with six members, the SCO has expanded to 27 countries across Asia, Europe, and Africa, with a combined economic output of nearly $30 trillion. The organization's guiding philosophy—the "Shanghai Spirit" of mutual trust, mutual benefit, equality, consultation, respect for diverse civilizations, and pursuit of common development—has become a defining feature of its approach to regional cooperation.

According to the [Chinese Foreign Ministry](https://www.news.cn/world/20260826/aa30f4fc3f124a3aa39c63b43ec77c19/c.html), Xi will discuss deepening cooperation and major international and regional issues with participating leaders, building on the strategic blueprint established at last year's Tianjin Summit, which proposed the Global Governance Initiative and mapped a 10-year development path for the organization.

## Deepening China-Kyrgyzstan Ties

Ahead of his arrival, Xi published a signed article titled "Jointly Opening up New Prospects for the High-Quality Development of China-Kyrgyzstan Relations" in the Slovo Kyrgyzstana newspaper and Kabar National News Agency, as [reported by Xinhua](https://news.sina.com.cn/gov/xlxw/2026-08-28/doc-inipwhrr4517425.shtml). In the article, Xi described China and Kyrgyzstan as "close neighbors that depend on each other like lips and teeth, good partners sharing a common destiny, and good friends who achieve mutual success."

The visit comes amid deepening economic integration between the two countries. China has remained Kyrgyzstan's largest trading partner and largest source of foreign investment for years, with bilateral trade reaching $11.7 billion in the first seven months of 2026. Key infrastructure projects, including the China-Kyrgyzstan-Uzbekistan Railway and the New North-South Road, are advancing under the Belt and Road Initiative.

Kyrgyz officials have expressed strong expectations for the visit. Erlist Akunbekov, Kyrgyz Deputy Chairman of the Cabinet of Ministers, said the Kyrgyz side "always keeps the door open to Chinese enterprises and looks forward to more long-term investment, advanced technologies and high-quality projects in Kyrgyzstan," as [CGTN reported](https://news.cgtn.com/news/2026-08-29/Kyrgyzstan-anticipates-new-opportunities-from-Xi-s-upcoming-visit-1Q0sbVg3o1q/p.html). Former Kyrgyz Foreign Minister Alikbek Jekshenkulov described Xi's visit as "another important milestone in bilateral relations."

## A Historic Return to Egypt

Xi's state visit to Egypt, scheduled for September 1-3, carries particular significance as it coincides with the 70th anniversary of China-Egypt diplomatic relations. Egypt was the first Arab and African country to establish diplomatic relations with the People's Republic of China, and the two nations elevated their ties to a Comprehensive Strategic Partnership in 2014.

The visit is Xi's first state visit to Egypt in 10 years, following his January 2016 trip when he and President Sisi jointly inaugurated Phase II of the China-Egypt Suez Economic and Trade Cooperation Zone. That zone has since attracted over 200 companies, brought in more than $4.7 billion in investment, and created over 10,000 direct jobs, according to [Global Times](https://www.globaltimes.cn/page/202608/1369362.shtml).

Chinese Foreign Ministry spokesperson Lin Jian noted that the visit "holds special significance of carrying forward the past and opening up the future for the development of bilateral relations," as [Al Jazeera reported](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/26/chinas-xi-to-visit-kyrgyzstan-egypt). The trip comes amid ongoing US-Iran tensions that have disrupted shipping through the Strait of Hormuz, with China maintaining significant economic ties to the Middle East as its top source of crude oil imports before the conflict.

## Geopolitical Implications

Beyond bilateral diplomacy, the summit provides a platform for major power engagement. Russian President Vladimir Putin is expected to meet Xi on the sidelines, with the Power of Siberia 2 gas pipeline project on the agenda, according to [South China Morning Post](https://www.scmp.com/news/china/diplomacy/article/3365710/xi-putin-meet-bishkek-power-siberia-2-pipeline-talks-kremlin). The meeting would mark their second face-to-face talks this year.

Analysts see the SCO's expanding role as significant for global governance. Kazakhstani political analyst Alibek Tazhibayev noted that "the Shanghai Spirit offers, to some extent, an alternative to the rigid logic of bloc politics," while Zhu Yongbiao, a Middle East affairs expert at Lanzhou University, said the summit "will also bring more opportunities for global peace and development."

## What to Watch

As the summit unfolds, key questions will center on how the SCO navigates its expansion, the outcomes of the Xi-Putin meeting on energy cooperation, and the concrete initiatives emerging from the 25th anniversary gathering. Xi's subsequent visit to Egypt will be closely watched for signals on deepening China's engagement with the Middle East amid regional volatility.

The trip underscores China's continued commitment to multilateral diplomacy through the SCO framework and its strategic outreach across Central Asia and the Middle East—a diplomatic push that carries implications for regional stability and the evolving global order.
