# Tupac Murder Trial Heads to Closing Arguments After Defense Rests

The murder trial of Duane "Keffe D" Davis in the 1996 killing of rap legend Tupac Shakur is headed to closing arguments after the defense rested Thursday following testimony from three former Las Vegas police officers. Jurors are scheduled to return Monday for closing arguments before beginning deliberations in the only prosecution ever brought in the killing of the hip-hop icon, according to [AP News](https://apnews.com/article/tupac-shakur-duane-keffe-d-davis-01cf59fb7a4d9c4bb974e584bb4676f0).

The presentation of evidence ended after nine days of testimony. Davis, 63, did not take the stand in his own defense.

## The 1996 Shooting

Shakur was riding in a black BMW driven by Death Row Records co-founder Marion "Suge" Knight on Sept. 7, 1996, when a white Cadillac pulled up next to it at a red light near the Las Vegas Strip and gunfire erupted. Shakur was shot multiple times and died six days later at age 25. Knight survived with minor injuries, as [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/tupac-murder-trial-duane-keffe-d-davis-closing-arguments-monday/) reported.

The shooting occurred hours after Shakur's entourage beat up Davis's nephew, Orlando "Baby Lane" Anderson, in a brawl at the MGM Grand casino following a Mike Tyson fight. Prosecutors allege Davis orchestrated the shooting in retaliation for that beating.

Years later, Davis would say he procured the gun and his nephew, who has since died, fired the shots. He faces life in prison if convicted of murder with a deadly weapon with the intent to promote, further or assist a criminal gang. He has pleaded not guilty.

## The Case Against Davis

Prosecutors rested Wednesday after calling 24 witnesses over eight days of testimony. Their case relies heavily on Davis's own accounts over the years, including police interviews, his 2019 memoir "Compton Street Legend," and recorded jail calls, as [AP News reported](https://apnews.com/article/tupac-shakur-shooting-murder-trial-1239e5e027cd6879913db1de012976f8).

In a 2008 interview with a federal task force investigating the killing of the Notorious B.I.G., Davis described handing a gun to men in the backseat of a white Cadillac that fired on Shakur's BMW. In recorded jail calls after his 2023 arrest, Davis complained, "They're trying to use my words against me."

A former Compton gang detective, Robert Ladd, testified that Davis was a high-ranking "shot caller" whose authority meant younger gang members would not have opened fire on Shakur with Davis present unless he allowed it. Ladd said Shakur's shooting triggered a 10-day war in Compton between the South Side Crips and Mob Piru, resulting in three killings and 10 attempted murders.

Under Nevada law, a person can be convicted of murder for helping another person commit the crime.

## The Defense's Strategy

Defense attorney Michael Sanft has argued that Davis fabricated or embellished his accounts of the killing for money and notoriety and that investigators failed to independently corroborate his claims. In his opening statement, Sanft told jurors, "What they are telling you, they are representing as fact, when it's really fiction. There's no facts," as [AP News documented](https://apnews.com/article/tupac-shakur-keffe-d-las-vegas-trial-00b666bad0c1cadb4b647e5976fd1f9e).

Retired Las Vegas homicide detective Dan Long, the defense's final witness, testified that he tried unsuccessfully to verify several details Davis provided during a 2009 police interview, including hospital records for Anderson and rental records for the Cadillac. Long acknowledged he had no independent facts outside of what Davis told him confirming Davis was in the Cadillac that night.

The defense also called two former Las Vegas police officers who were assigned to keep the peace at Knight's home on the night of the shooting. They testified about vehicles entering and leaving the gated residence, including a cream- or white-colored Cadillac, according to [Fox5 Vegas](https://www.fox5vegas.com/2026/08/27/defense-set-begin-tupac-shakur-murder-trial-after-prosecution-rests/).

## A Decades-Old Case Finally in Court

The case remained unsolved for decades until Davis's public interviews and 2019 memoir helped revive scrutiny of the killing. Davis was indicted in 2023, nearly 27 years after Shakur's death, and has been jailed since late 2023.

A jury of 6 men and 10 women was selected for the trial, which began Aug. 10 at the Clark County District Court in Las Vegas, as [AP News reported](https://apnews.com/article/tupac-shakur-trial-jury-las-vegas-a828bde9016cf616364273fc3ba6138f).

Davis is the only surviving person from the Cadillac. The other three men in the car — Anderson, Deandrae "Freaky" Smith, and Terry "Bubble Up" Brown — have all died in the years since.

## Family's Long Quest for Justice

For Shakur's family, the trial represents a decades-long wait for answers. His cousin Zayd Akinyela said the details have helped the grief process. "I think we've been waiting 30 years to grieve properly," he told [AP News](https://apnews.com/article/tupac-killing-trial-keffe-duane-davis-autopsy-2a87380f91ad6759cb500311b73396ed).

Shakur's stepbrother Maurice "Mopreme" Shakur said he hopes the trial is fair and the truth comes out. "That would be justice for me," he said. "But we all know that is unattainable."

## What's Next

Closing arguments are scheduled to begin Monday morning, after which the jury will begin deliberations. The trial is expected to last about a month total, with the verdict potentially marking the final chapter in one of hip-hop's most enduring mysteries.

The central question for the jury: whether Davis's own words — his memoir, interviews, and jail calls — constitute sufficient evidence to convict him of orchestrating Shakur's murder, or whether they represent fabricated bravado from someone seeking money and notoriety.

Shakur, considered one of the most influential rappers of all time, sold over 75 million records worldwide. His legacy continues to resonate three decades after his death, as [AP News noted](https://apnews.com/article/tupac-shakur-killing-las-vegas-trial-4529257714634e87ed258fde044f948b). The verdict in this case will determine whether anyone is finally held accountable for the killing that has captivated the public for 30 years.
