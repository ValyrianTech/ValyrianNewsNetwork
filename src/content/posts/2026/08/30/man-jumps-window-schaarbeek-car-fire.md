# Man Jumps from Window to Escape Smoke in Schaarbeek Car Fire

A man was injured in the early hours of Sunday morning after jumping from a first-floor window in Schaarbeek, Brussels, to escape thick smoke from two burning vehicles. The incident occurred in the Van Hamméestraat, according to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/08/30/schaarbeek-brand-man-springt-uit-raam-appartementsgebouw-persone/).

## The Incident

The Brussels fire brigade was called to the scene shortly before 2 AM for two vehicles—a Renault and a Dacia—that were fully engulfed in flames. The intense heat caused damage to the facades of two adjacent homes, including cracked windows, though firefighters managed to prevent the fire from spreading to the buildings.

"Due to smoke spread, a resident of an adjacent home jumped out of the first-floor window," said Walter Derieuw, spokesperson for the Brussels fire brigade. "He injured one of his legs and was taken to the hospital." The victim was described as lightly injured.

The fire also produced heavy smoke that penetrated the sewer system, forcing the evacuation of several neighborhood residents in the middle of the night—including people living several streets away, as [BRUZZ](https://www.bruzz.be/actua/samenleving/twee-autos-uitgebrand-schaarbeek-persoon-springt-uit-raam-2026-08-30) reported.

"We were able to avoid full fire spread," Derieuw confirmed.

The burnt-out vehicles have since been removed, and the cause of the fire remains under police investigation. None of the reporting sources have ruled out arson.

## A Recurring Problem in Brussels

Vehicle fires have become a persistent concern across the Brussels-Capital Region. During New Year's night 2025, the Brussels fire brigade responded to approximately 30 interventions for burning cars, resulting in 60 to 70 vehicles burned out across the region, as documented in [VRT NWS's liveblog](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/liveblog/live-jaarwisseling-auto-in-brand-aan-brusselse-zuidlaan-ongeva~1739888611389/) from that night. Firefighters were also targeted with stones, fireworks, and Molotov cocktails during the disturbances.

More recently, the neighboring municipality of Sint-Gillis has experienced a notable series of vehicle fires. On August 13, four car fires occurred on the same day, damaging eight vehicles. On August 19, two cars were deliberately set on fire in the Vanderschrickstraat, with the fire brigade confirming arson, as [BRUZZ reported](https://www.bruzz.be/actua/samenleving/twee-autos-uitgebrand-sint-gillis-2026-08-19). Just days later, on August 23, two more car fires broke out in Sint-Gillis, prompting Mayor Jean Spinette to suggest the fires were deliberately set as "intimidation between rival gangs," contributing to "a climate of fear," according to [BRUZZ](https://www.bruzz.be/actua/veiligheid/alweer-een-autobrand-sint-gillis-2026-08-23).

Schaarbeek itself has not been immune. In March 2026, a BMW was completely burned out in the Voltairelaan, near the Josaphatpark. Fire brigade spokesperson Walter Derieuw stated at the time that "criminal intent cannot be ruled out," as [VRT NWS reported](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/03/20/auto-brandt-volledig-uit-in-schaarbeek/). A Mercedes also burned out completely in the Huart Hamoirlaan in July 2026.

The Van Hamméestraat itself has seen previous fire incidents. In September 2025, a person was taken to hospital in "concerning condition" after a house fire in the same street, as [BRUZZ reported](https://www.bruzz.be/actua/veiligheid/persoon-afgevoerd-zorgwekkende-toestand-na-woningbrand-schaarbeek-2025-09-28).

## Fire Risks in Residential Areas

Vehicle fires in densely populated residential areas pose significant dangers beyond the immediate flames. Smoke can spread rapidly through buildings and sewer systems, as demonstrated in this incident, forcing residents to make dangerous escape decisions. Heat can damage building facades and crack windows, and evacuations may be necessary in the middle of the night.

The dramatic escape in Schaarbeek highlights how quickly a vehicle fire can escalate into a threat to human life in an urban setting. The man's decision to jump from a first-floor window—while resulting in a leg injury—underscores the urgency residents can face when smoke fills their homes.

## What's Next

Police continue to investigate the cause of the fire in the Van Hamméestraat. The burnt-out vehicles have been removed, and the affected facades will require repairs. As vehicle fires continue to occur across Brussels, authorities and residents alike will be watching closely for any patterns or developments in the investigation.

The incident serves as a reminder of the importance of fire safety awareness in residential neighborhoods and the need for swift emergency response when vehicle fires occur near homes.
