# China's Early Rice Output Stable Above 56 Billion Jin for 6 Consecutive Years

China's national early rice production reached 565.8 billion jin (28.29 million metric tons) in 2026, marking the sixth consecutive year that output has remained stable above the 560 billion jin threshold, according to data released by the [National Bureau of Statistics](https://www.stats.gov.cn/sj/sjjd/202608/t20260821_1965087.html) on August 21. The figure represents a decrease of 4.4 billion jin (0.8%) from 2025, driven primarily by adverse weather conditions during the growing season.

## Stable Production Despite Weather Challenges

According to [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/20260821/0b7947e6f0e34802a145399eff143aa0/c.html), the 2026 early rice sowing area expanded slightly to 71.164 million mu (4.744 million hectares), an increase of 22,000 mu from the previous year. Per-unit yield, however, declined by 3.2 kg per mu to 397.6 kg per mu, a decrease of 0.8% year-on-year.

Wei Fenghua, Director of the Rural Department of the National Bureau of Statistics, explained that the production data was derived from sample surveys conducted across 10 provinces and regions using field cutting and measurement methods. The survey methodology involves pre-harvest field assessment, plot selection, sample collection, threshing, drying, moisture measurement, and weighing.

## Weather Impacts on Key Production Regions

The four main early rice production areas — Guangxi, Hunan, Guangdong, and Jiangxi — all experienced production declines due to typhoon impacts. Guangxi saw the largest decrease at 2.1 billion jin (-2.2%), followed by Hunan at 1.1 billion jin (-0.8%), Guangdong at 1.0 billion jin (-0.9%), and Jiangxi at 0.9 billion jin (-0.7%).

Wei noted that during the heading-flowering period, persistent heavy rainfall in the Jiangnan and South China main production areas caused what agricultural experts call "rain-washed rice flowers," affecting pollination and grain setting. During the maturity-harvest period, multiple typhoons hit South China, causing waterlogging in low-lying fields and lodging in some areas.

"Although this year's early rice production was slightly affected by adverse weather, it has still remained stable above 560 billion jin for six consecutive years," Wei said, as reported by [China News Service](https://www.chinanews.com.cn/cj/2026/08-21/10681538.shtml). "In 2026, China's summer grain achieved increased production and early rice remained stable, laying a good foundation for the year's overall grain harvest."

## Policy Support Bolsters Farmer Confidence

The government has maintained the minimum purchase price for early indica rice, protected farmland fertility subsidies, and increased crop rotation subsidies to stabilize farmers' planting expectations. [China News](https://www.chinanews.com.cn/gn/2026/08-21/10681448.shtml) reported that local governments have also implemented grain production responsibilities layer by layer, coordinating funds from major grain-producing county rewards and green, high-quality, high-efficiency action programs to support early rice production.

"This year, the state has continuously increased support for grain production, implemented multiple policies to strengthen agriculture, benefit farmers, and enrich rural areas," Wei said. "All regions have effectively mobilized farmers' enthusiasm for planting."

## Broader Food Security Context

Early rice is the first grain harvest of the year in China, typically harvested in July, making it an early indicator of the year's overall grain production. The stability of early rice production comes amid broader efforts to ensure national food security.

China's "15th Five-Year Plan" (2026-2030) sets a target of grain production capacity of approximately 1.45 trillion jin by 2030, up from the current level of about 1.39 trillion jin. The 2026 Central Document No. 1, the first policy document of the new plan period, calls for keeping grain production stable at around 1.4 trillion jin.

As [Yicai](https://www.yicai.com/news/103327734.html) noted, the NBS announcement provides official confirmation of the production figures, with the total output calculated by multiplying surveyed sowing area by per-unit yield.

## Outlook for the Year Ahead

The 2026 summer grain harvest achieved increased production, and early rice remained stable, providing solid support for a good start to the "15th Five-Year Plan" period. Wei emphasized that the combination of policy support and technological measures — including mechanized transplanting, reasonable densification, and side-deep fertilization — has helped maintain production stability despite challenging weather conditions.

Looking ahead, analysts will be watching whether the government's continued agricultural support policies can sustain the momentum of stable grain production as China works toward its 2030 grain production capacity target of 1.45 trillion jin. The resilience demonstrated by the early rice sector this year suggests that China's food security framework remains robust even in the face of climate-related challenges.
