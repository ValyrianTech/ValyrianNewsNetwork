# China's Inbound Tourism Surges on Visa-Free Policies and Cultural Appeal

China's inbound tourism has achieved what industry analysts describe as "leapfrog growth" this summer, with official data showing 22.914 million foreign visitors entered the country in the first half of 2026, a year-on-year increase of 20.4 percent, according to [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/fortune/20260821/cd5f20eb1bc7419dbd75e90ee1db4e28/c.html). The surge, driven by expanded visa-free policies and growing international fascination with Chinese culture, marks a new phase in the country's tourism recovery.

## Visa-Free Expansion Reshapes Travel Decisions

The numbers tell a striking story. Of the 22.914 million foreign arrivals in the first half of 2026, 17.815 million entered China visa-free — accounting for 77.7 percent of all foreign arrivals and representing a 30.6 percent year-on-year increase. China currently grants unilateral visa-free entry to citizens of 50 countries, maintains mutual visa-free arrangements with 29 countries, and offers 240-hour transit visa-free privileges to travelers from 55 countries.

"The core factor in the continued warming of inbound tourism is policy promotion," said Li Yaoqi, professor and director of the Cultural Tourism Consumption Research Center at Sun Yat-sen University's Tourism College. "Visa-free expansion is the most core driving factor — unilateral visa-free and 240-hour transit visa-free have changed travel decision-making habits."

The policy momentum continues to build. On August 20, China expanded its 240-hour transit visa-free policy to include citizens of Kyrgyzstan and Vietnam, bringing the total to 57 countries, as [Dotdotnews](https://english.dotdotnews.com/a/202608/20/AP6a869bede4b04b6c5d3741b8.html) reported. The expansion also extended the 30-day visa-free entry policy for Hainan Province to cover 61 countries.

## 'Chinamaxxing' Fuels Cultural Curiosity

Beyond policy, a cultural phenomenon is reshaping how the world views China. The "Chinamaxxing" trend — an internet meme popularized by Generation Z that involves embracing Chinese lifestyle habits, wellness practices, and daily culture — has accumulated over 4 billion views on overseas social media platforms. The "China Travel" tag has surpassed 28 billion views.

The trend gained mainstream attention earlier this year when [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/03/13/nx-s1-5743795/chinamaxxing-gen-z-word-of-week) featured it as its "Word of the Week," examining how young Americans are increasingly adopting what they consider Chinese cultural habits — from drinking hot water to practicing traditional wellness routines. Popular livestreamers like Hasan Piker broadcast visits to Chinese megacities to millions of viewers, while diaspora influencers shared herbal skincare recipes and lifestyle content.

"Superficial trends can sometimes create the easiest entry point because cultural rivalries and geopolitical competition put people into defensive postures," Shaoyu Yuan, a New York-based scholar of Chinese soft power, told NPR. "But lifestyle content, memes, can lower the temperature. That matters because dialogue usually starts with familiarity, not with agreement."

This cultural curiosity is translating into real travel decisions. Online travel platform Qunar data shows July inbound tourism bookings grew over 20 percent year-on-year, while Feizhu reported inbound tourism bookings surged 237 percent year-on-year in the two weeks before summer, as [Beijing News](https://m.bjnews.com.cn/detail/1780645735129211.html) reported.

## From Landmarks to Immersive Experiences

Foreign tourists' relationship with China is evolving beyond the traditional "Great Wall and pandas" itinerary. The China Tourism Association's "2026 First Half Tourism Market Insight Report," released in August, highlights a shift from "check-in sightseeing" toward deeper, more immersive experiences, as reported by [People's Daily Overseas Edition](https://www.163.com/dy/article/L4HA1RT70514R9OJ.html).

City-level data reflects this transformation. Beijing received 3.2727 million inbound tourists in the first half of 2026, up 32.6 percent year-on-year. Shanghai welcomed 5.3149 million, up 27.8 percent, while Chongqing saw 1.448 million visitors, a remarkable 56.8 percent increase.

Smaller cities and rural destinations are also emerging as major draws. In July, Lijiang's inbound tourism bookings grew 1.2 times year-on-year, with Vietnamese tourists to Lijiang and Kunming surging 4.5 times and 2.4 times respectively. Guizhou's Fanjingshan received over 40,000 overseas visitors by the end of July, up 26.24 percent, while summer hotel bookings in the province grew 84 percent year-on-year.

"In recent years, driven by policy, the inbound tourism market has shown rapid growth since last year, and China's inbound tourism has entered a new normalized growth cycle," said Wu Liyun, professor at Beijing International Studies University's China Culture and Tourism Industry Research Institute.

## Infrastructure and Services Win Over Visitors

The appeal extends beyond scenic beauty. Wu Liyun noted that improved sanitation infrastructure, convenient high-speed rail and public transportation, and diverse experiences like food delivery and mobile payments are making China an increasingly attractive destination. "Traveling to China offers not just value for money, but multi-dimensional safety guarantees that let tourists feel at ease," she said.

A French tourist echoed this sentiment: "China's heat-relief facilities surprised me — adequate air conditioning, plenty of shade structures, and convenient drinking water. This is so thoughtful."

The "Departure Tax Refund 2.0" policy, implemented on July 1, lowered the tax refund threshold to 200 yuan and enabled "instant buy, instant refund" services, further encouraging foreign visitors to shop. Since June, inbound tourist consumption has grown 72.9 percent year-on-year, with visitors covering nearly 500 Chinese cities.

As of June 30, inbound flights for foreign tourists covered 160 Chinese cities, according to [CCTV News](https://news.cctv.com/2026/07/21/ARTIHFibOjaIKDKIHNcX2D6x260721.shtml). The expansion of flight connectivity has made it easier for travelers to explore beyond the major gateways.

## A New Era of Chinese Tourism

The momentum shows no signs of slowing. In 2025, China received more than 150 million inbound tourist visits, up over 17 percent year-on-year. The "Tourism Powerhouse Construction 15th Five-Year Plan" targets 190 million inbound tourist visits by 2030, with total spending exceeding $150 billion.

"Now many foreign tourists go deep into city streets, villages and fields, experiencing real Chinese life," said Wu Guoping, president of the Wuxi Tourism Association. "From urban night economy to rural revitalization, a three-dimensional, vivid, and vibrant national image is being transmitted to the world through the eyes of tourists."

As [China Daily](https://www.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202605/12/WS6a028139a310d6866eb4810a.html) noted, Beijing has been leading the way in improving entry policies, with immigration authorities rolling out multilingual services, fast-track inspection channels, and streamlined clearance processes to accommodate the growing influx of travelers.

Industry observers see this summer as a turning point. "The emergence of the summer travel boom reflects China's economic and social progress," said Tong Yun, a professor at Hainan University's College of International Tourism and Public Administration. The trend mirrors the country's consumption upgrading and evolving public lifestyles.

For international travelers, the message is increasingly clear: China is not just a destination to visit — it's a place to experience, to understand, and increasingly, to return to. As the "Chinamaxxing" trend continues to spread and visa-free policies expand further, the world's fascination with China shows no signs of cooling down.
