# Foreign Tourists Flock to Shenzhen for Tech Shopping

Shenzhen has become a magnet for international tourists seeking cutting-edge technology products, with visitors arriving "empty-handed" and leaving with "full boxes" of AI glasses, drones and smart devices from the city's famed Huaqiangbei electronics market. The trend underscores China's growing appeal as a destination for technology-driven tourism amid a broader surge in inbound travel.

According to [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/fortune/20260815/be1831b6a4ff4e5cb6de2643e71bb213/c.html), merchants at Huaqiangbei report that foreign customers typically arrive with their whole families to purchase products, drawn by the exceptional value for money the market offers. "Many people arrive empty-handed and leave with full boxes," said Xiao Wenlong, a merchant at Huaqiang Electronic World.

## A Global Shopping Destination

Huaqiangbei, long known as one of the world's largest electronics marketplaces, is at the epicenter of this shopping boom. The district packs more than 115,000 business entities and 35 specialized electronics markets into just 1.45 square kilometers, offering over one million types of electronic components, according to [CGTN](https://news.cgtn.com/news/2026-08-15/Why-global-visitors-are-shopping-in-Shenzhen-s-Huaqiangbei-1PCyrXwvENq/p.html).

The area now records more than 8,000 foreign merchants and visitors from 183 countries and regions on an average day, making international sourcing part of the everyday rhythm of the market.

US tourist Brian Lamotte-Aris was busy selecting wireless earbuds during a recent visit. "I'm very interested in the e-bikes and various flying devices here," he said. "When I go back, I'll tell my friends - come here! It's full of tech products everywhere!"

South African visitor Janwick Harting echoed that sentiment, noting that "this place is quite advanced, with every tech product you could imagine." During his trip, he purchased a pair of AI glasses and had his computer and daughter's watch repaired.

The shopping surge is reflected in sales data. In the first seven months of 2026, AI product sales across all categories at Huaqiangbei grew more than 55 percent year-on-year, with AI glasses sales up 100 percent and drones and robots up 60 to 70 percent, according to [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/fortune/20260810/2ea60fb60f2946f4a3e3c218823d6518/c.html).

## Technology Tourism Takes Off

The phenomenon extends well beyond Huaqiangbei. Across Shenzhen, foreign tourists are flocking to technology experiences - from the world's first robot 6S store in Longgang district to the DJI flagship store and the INNO100 global innovation retail space.

At the robot 6S store, visitors from Jamaica were seen interacting with humanoid robots, while a Nepali visitor named Prabesh marveled at the technology on display. "The robot made me a drink. I was so impressed. The technology here is next level," he told [China Daily](https://kunming.cn/en/c/2026-08-07/14064692.shtml).

Store manager Zhou Fangzheng reports about 500 visitors daily, with foreign visitors accounting for more than 30 percent of foot traffic - a figure that doubles during summer. At the DJI flagship store, staff noted that roughly 40 percent of orders now come from foreign customers.

Spanish winery founder Marta Cerrolaza was among those exploring agricultural drones at the DJI store, hoping to "bring this technology back and create Spain's first tech winery."

Malaysian tourist Yan Yawen captured the sentiment of many visitors: "What you see on screen isn't real. I want to experience how amazing life here is firsthand. I came here to see the future."

## Policy Support Fuels Growth

The surge in tech tourism is underpinned by China's expanding visa-free policies. China has granted unilateral visa-free access to 50 countries and signed mutual visa-exemption agreements with 29 countries, while travelers from 55 countries are eligible for 240-hour visa-free transit at 65 ports, according to [People's Daily Online](https://en.people.cn/n3/2026/0810/c90000-20487075.html).

The results are striking. China recorded 22.91 million foreign entries in the first half of 2026, up 20.4 percent year-on-year, with visa-free entries accounting for 77.7 percent of the total. In 2025, China welcomed more than 35 million foreign visitors, up 30 percent year-on-year, with international tourism revenue rising nearly 40 percent, according to a [Trip.com Group report](https://www.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202606/02/WS6a1ea068a310d6866eb4c0ba.html) cited by China Daily.

Shenzhen has been a major beneficiary. Over 5 million foreigners entered or exited through Shenzhen ports in 2026 as of early August, and summer inbound tourism ticket orders increased over 70 percent year-on-year, with one-day tour orders surging over 1,000 percent.

The departure tax-refund system has also been streamlined to encourage spending. China reduced the minimum same-day purchase eligible for a departure tax refund from 500 yuan to 200 yuan last year and expanded its "refund-upon-purchase" service nationwide. The number of overseas travelers using the system increased 366 percent year-on-year in the first half of 2026, with sales of eligible goods and amount refunded rising 69 percent, according to the State Taxation Administration, as reported by [China Daily](https://govt.chinadaily.com.cn/s/202608/14/WS6a7e7e9c498e36855033f207/tourists-add-tech-trips-to-itineraries.html).

Smartphones, drones, smart glasses and AI wearables account for nearly 80 percent of purchases for which overseas customers sought tax refunds at Huaqiangbei.

## A New Dimension of Chinese Tourism

Experts say technology is fast becoming a defining feature of China's tourism offering. Wu Liyun, a professor at the China Academy of Culture and Tourism at Beijing International Studies University, told China Daily that "inbound visitors are no longer coming only to experience traditional culture. They also want to experience ordinary life, the convenience brought by economic development and China's technology at close range."

Travel operators are responding to the shift. Pan Shunyou, general manager of Shenzhen Wanyue Travel Service Co, noted that "visitors are no longer satisfied with just conventional sightseeing. They want to experience China's technology as much as its culture."

Huaqiangbei's ecosystem is uniquely positioned to capitalize on this demand. The district's famous "Huaqiangbei Speed" - design in the morning, prototype in the afternoon, mass production the next day and global shipment within a week - has made it a hub for rapid product innovation. Zhang Guoping, founder and CEO of Shenzhen CHUPAD Electronic Technology Co, told CGTN that "it's really quick to develop a product here. If you need a prototype or need to match hardware with software, companies might even work overnight to get it done for you."

## Looking Ahead

Shenzhen is preparing to host APEC Economic Leaders' Week in November 2026, which is expected to draw renewed international attention to the city. The district is upgrading multilingual services, international payment support and visitor facilities to make the market easier for overseas visitors to navigate.

As China's inbound tourism enters what industry analysts describe as a "strategic window of opportunity," the fusion of technology and tourism is likely to deepen. For many international visitors, Shenzhen has become more than a shopping destination - it is a window into China's technological future, and they are arriving in record numbers to see it for themselves.
