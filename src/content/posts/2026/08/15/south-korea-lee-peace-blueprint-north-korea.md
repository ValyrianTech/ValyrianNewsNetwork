# South Korea's Lee Proposes Peace Blueprint for North Korea

South Korean President Lee Jae-myung on Saturday proposed a comprehensive "peaceful coexistence blueprint" for North Korea, calling for direct talks between the two Koreas to formally end the Korean War and discussing ways to halt Pyongyang's nuclear program. The proposal was delivered during his speech at the 81st Liberation Day ceremony in Seoul, marking Korea's independence from Japanese colonial rule.

"Let us put down our intentions to threaten each other and begin discussions to end the long-running war as the directly involved parties," Lee said, according to [Yonhap News Agency](https://en.yna.co.kr/view/AEN20260815001451315). "In the process, (we) may also discuss effective ways to halt the advancement of the North's nuclear capabilities."

## A Three-Pillar Vision

The blueprint, which builds on Lee's previous policy commitments, rests on three foundational pillars: inclusive peaceful coexistence with mutual respect, stable peaceful coexistence without the need to fight, and responsible peaceful coexistence contributing to world peace.

On the first pillar, Lee emphasized that the two Koreas "must respect each other and stop unnecessary confrontations," calling for a gradual reduction of hostility "starting from perceptions, norms, and systems." The second pillar focuses on establishing institutions to control tensions and multilayered safety mechanisms to prevent crises and escalation. The third pillar frames peace on the peninsula as a stepping stone toward a nuclear-free world.

"I will turn the energy of hostility and confrontation into momentum for peaceful coexistence and mutual prosperity," Lee declared, as reported by [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/world/20260815/4438941b4feb452f842c11e18d726038/c.html). "As a pacemaker and a party to peace on the Korean Peninsula, I will fulfill my role and responsibility."

## A Historic Shift in Policy

The Korean Peninsula has technically remained at war since the 1950-53 Korean War ended with an armistice rather than a peace treaty. Lee's proposal represents a significant departure from the previous Yoon Suk-yeol administration's hardline stance, which prioritized denuclearization as a precondition for dialogue.

Since taking office in June 2025, Lee has consistently pursued a policy of peaceful coexistence. His administration's 5th Inter-Korean Relations Development Basic Plan (2026-2030), deliberated in March and reported to the National Assembly in April, placed "peaceful coexistence" at the center of inter-Korean policy, replacing the previous emphasis on denuclearization, according to [Yonhap News](https://www.yna.co.kr/view/AKR20260319060851504). The first Unification White Paper of his administration, published in May, similarly emphasized inter-Korean exchange and cooperation while reducing emphasis on North Korean human rights issues, as [Kyunghyang Shinmun](https://www.khan.co.kr/article/202605181633011) reported.

In his Liberation Day speech last year, Lee pledged to respect North Korea's current system, not pursue unification by absorption, and refrain from hostile acts toward the North. Saturday's address moves beyond those principles to present a concrete roadmap for implementation.

## Unresponsive North, Domestic Opposition

The proposal comes despite North Korea's continued unresponsiveness to Seoul's repeated reconciliatory gestures. Pyongyang has rebuffed engagement offers, criticized Seoul's plan for nuclear-powered submarines, and denounced joint U.S.-South Korean military drills as provocations. Just days before the speech, the North reportedly conducted a ballistic missile provocation in the East Sea.

The opposition People Power Party swiftly condemned the proposal. Senior Spokesperson Park Sung-hoon called it "truly an out-of-body experience and deception toward the people," according to [News1](https://www.news1.kr/politics/assembly/6260207). "Peace slogans without the guarantee of a firm ROK-US alliance and credible strength are nothing less than 'security self-harm' that leaves the safety of the nation and people at the mercy of the other side's goodwill," Park said.

## Broader Vision and Japan Relations

Beyond inter-Korean affairs, Lee used the Liberation Day address to outline a broader vision for an "Irreplaceable Republic of Korea," calling for a "new liberation" for the country's next stage of development. He also addressed South Korea-Japan relations, highlighting active "shuttle diplomacy" over the past year and expressing hope to open "a new 60 years of peace and prosperity" with Japan as "close and even closer neighbors."

Lee additionally announced plans to confiscate assets of pro-Japanese collaborators under the recently enacted Pro-Japanese Assets Confiscation Act, which was promulgated on June 2, 2026. The ceremony, attended by approximately 3,000 people including patriots, independence fighters' families, and foreign ambassadors, also commemorated the 150th birth anniversary of independence activist Kim Gu.

## What to Watch

The full text of Lee's speech, published by [Newspim](https://www.newspim.com/news/view/20260815000033), underscores the administration's determination to pursue dialogue despite setbacks. However, the path forward remains uncertain. North Korea has shown no indication of responding positively, and domestic political opposition continues to challenge the policy's premises. Whether Lee's "peaceful coexistence blueprint" can break through the entrenched stalemate on the peninsula will likely depend on both Pyongyang's willingness to engage and the durability of Seoul's policy consensus at home.

International observers, including [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/aug/15/south-koreas-lee-urges-talks-with-north-korea-about-finally-ending-korean-war) and [Reuters](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/south-koreas-lee-says-mechanisms-015649349.html), noted that Lee's call largely builds on his previous commitments while adding new specificity about mechanisms and safeguards. The coming months will reveal whether this blueprint can translate into tangible diplomatic progress on one of the world's most enduring conflicts.
