# US Sends NATO Allies Loyalty Questionnaire on Trump Policies

The United States has circulated a detailed questionnaire among NATO allies asking about their loyalty to President Donald Trump's foreign policy, according to a document seen by Bloomberg and reported by multiple news outlets. The move has raised concerns among European allies about the future of the transatlantic alliance and whether US military protection will now be conditional on political alignment with Washington.

The document, first circulated to countries in Washington before making rounds at NATO headquarters in Brussels, asks member states whether they have "been publicly supportive" of US foreign policy initiatives, including those in the Middle East and Western Hemisphere, according to [Türkiye Today](https://www.turkiyetoday.com/world/us-questionnaire-asks-nato-allies-to-prove-fealty-to-trump-policies-3226094). It also probes what "restrictions" allies have placed on US military use of their bases and whether they are willing to reduce or eliminate such restrictions.

## Key Questions and Context

The questionnaire arrives weeks into the Trump administration's six-month review of its military presence in Europe, expected to conclude by December 2026. The review was announced by Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth in June, who said at the time that "some countries will fail, and where others will pass with flying colors," as [VRT NWS reported](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/06/18/navo-defensie-vs-minister-hegseth-en-secretaris-generaal-van-de/).

Among the questions asked, the document inquires whether countries buy weapons and equipment from American defense contractors, whether they have "publicly opposed regulations" that "inhibit" contracts with American firms, and whether the country has "shown alignment with the US approach to 'strong, clear, and quiet'" — a reference to Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth's directive, as [Meduza reported](https://meduza.io/en/news/2026/08/14/bloomberg-u-s-questionnaire-tests-nato-allies-loyalty-to-president-trump-s-policies).

The questionnaire also asks for evidence of compliance with the 5 percent of GDP defense spending target agreed at the 2025 Hague summit, and probes whether countries have spoken out against new "made-in-Europe" defense policies that favor European suppliers over American firms.

## Allied Reaction

NATO allies have reacted negatively to the questionnaire, viewing it as an attempt to force ideological alignment in exchange for US protection, according to people familiar with the matter cited by Bloomberg. The [upday report](https://www.upday.com/uk/wait-what-us-now-testing-nato-allies-on-ideological-alignment-with-trump/5ljx273) notes that linking America's long-standing security commitments to such loyalty tests would upend the post-World War II transatlantic relationship, which was built on US military guarantees that superseded political disagreements.

NATO Secretary-General Mark Rutte has publicly defended the review, telling reporters in July: "It is, I think, bringing NATO closer together. And having a review regularly, I think, is only wise," as Türkiye Today reported.

## Iran War and Base Restrictions

The questionnaire indirectly touches on the US-Israel war with Iran, which was launched without consulting NATO allies. Several countries, including Spain, barred Washington from using local bases to strike Iran. Hegseth branded these restrictions as "shameful," arguing they had put American troops "at risk," according to [RT World News](https://rtrunews.com/news/644266-washington-quizzes-nato-allies-on-loyalty/).

The document also references US operations in the Western Hemisphere, including the January 2026 arrest of Venezuelan leader Nicolas Maduro, which many European countries considered a violation of international law, as [Ukraine Today reported](https://ukrainetoday.org/us-tests-nato-allies-for-loyalty-to-trump-policies-bloomberg/).

## Troop Movements Across Europe

The US has already announced plans to withdraw approximately 5,000 troops from Germany without full replacement, following tensions between Trump and German Chancellor Friedrich Merz over the Iran war. The withdrawal, announced on May 1, is expected to be completed within 6-12 months, as [VRT NWS reported](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/05/01/vs-haalt-5-000-militairen-weg-uit-duitsland-na-ruzie-tussen-trum/).

At the same time, Trump has pledged to send an additional 5,000 troops to Poland, citing the election of nationalist Karol Nawrocki as president. The announcement was made in May via Trump's Truth Social platform, as [VRT NWS reported](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/05/21/vs-sturen-5-000-militairen-naar-polen/).

The review is also examining the presence of strategic bombers, drones, naval vessels, and other military capabilities that could be deployed for the defense of European NATO allies. The US is cutting military assets earmarked for responding to an attack on NATO allies, potentially affecting capabilities that Europe does not possess.

## Analysis: A Qualitative Shift in Alliance Relations

The questionnaire represents a fundamental change in how Washington approaches its NATO commitments. Historically, the alliance has operated on the principle that members can disagree politically while maintaining collective defense commitments. By explicitly linking military protection to ideological alignment with the US president's policies, the Trump administration is transforming the nature of the transatlantic relationship.

From Washington's perspective, the questionnaire is a practical assessment of reliability and burden-sharing. Trump has long argued that the US bears an unfair share of NATO's defense burden, contrasting nearly $1 trillion in annual US defense spending with significantly lower European outlays. When allies restricted base access during the Iran campaign, the administration concluded that formal treaty commitments were insufficient.

European allies, however, see several problems with the approach. Restrictions on base use were made on legal, constitutional, or political grounds. Framing these sovereign decisions as "disloyalty" transforms a defensive alliance into a vehicle for enforcing alignment with one member's foreign policy. Questions about "made-in-Europe" defense policies are read as pressure to keep European procurement open to American firms, potentially at the expense of strategic autonomy goals.

Defense expert Jens Franssen of VRT NWS has noted that "there is a 'Europeanization' of NATO happening. Europe has come to the realization that it must take more responsibility for its own security," as reported by VRT NWS.

## What to Watch For

The US is scheduled to meet with NATO allies in Brussels later this month to discuss the ongoing review. The outcome of the December 2026 review will be critical in determining whether the questionnaire was a one-off pressure tactic or the beginning of a new conditional security framework.

If US protection becomes increasingly conditional on political conformity, the logic for building independent European capabilities grows stronger. The coming months will reveal whether the transatlantic alliance can adapt to this new dynamic or whether the questionnaire marks a point of no return in the post-World War II security architecture that has underpinned European stability for nearly eight decades.
