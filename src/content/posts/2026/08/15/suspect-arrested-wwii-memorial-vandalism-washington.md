# Suspect Arrested in WWII Memorial Vandalism in Washington

Federal prosecutors have arrested and charged a Kentucky woman with two felonies in connection with the vandalism of the World War II Memorial on the National Mall in Washington, D.C. U.S. Attorney for the District of Columbia Jeanine Pirro announced the charges on Friday, just one day after the iconic monument was found defaced with red paint and soap-filled bubbles in its fountain.

Melissa L. Farris, 41, of Elizabethtown, Kentucky, faces charges of "Depredation Against Property of the United States" and "Destruction of Veterans' Memorials," offenses that carry penalties of up to 10 years in prison, according to [AP News](https://apnews.com/article/wwii-memorial-vandalized-paint-bubbles-ac1f767c2bfb81f0902b1914c2dfe252).

## The Vandalism

On Thursday afternoon, U.S. Park Police discovered that the words "Clean hands dirty $" had been painted on the memorial's Atlantic Pavilion, with red paint dumped onto the walls and floor. A substance poured into the memorial's fountain caused it to bubble, as [WJLA 7News](https://wjla.com/news/local/disgusting-act-world-war-ii-memorial-vandalized-with-graffiti-and-soap-in-fountain-us-park-police-august-2026) reported. Cleanup and restoration costs have already exceeded $1,000, according to federal prosecutors.

Farris was identified through witness photographs circulated among U.S. Park Police officers, according to the federal complaint. One officer recognized her from an arrest on August 10 for unlawfully camping on federal property just blocks away from the memorial—an arrest she livestreamed. Investigators also found social media accounts under the name "Melissa Lovewell" where Farris posted videos admitting to defacing the monument.

"I will wait here to be arrested for spraying our veterans' fountain," Farris says in one video. "It was me, I'm doing it, it's on camera." In a Facebook live video posted Friday, she said she was turning herself in.

## Outrage and Condemnation

Pirro condemned the vandalism in stark terms. "Vandalizing our World War II Memorial is a despicable attack on a sacred monument honoring the Americans who fought and died for our freedom," she wrote on X. "Those Americans include my father and grandfather."

President Donald Trump also reacted forcefully, posting on Truth Social: "Our beautiful World War II Memorial was just hit by Spray Painting Vandals. THERE CAN BE NO GREATER INSULT TO THOSE AMERICAN HEROES WHO DIED IN WORLD WAR II. First the Reflecting Pool, now this."

The U.S. Department of the Interior called the vandalism "an utter disgrace," stating that "the public should know we will find the person responsible for this disgusting act." The Friends of the National World War II Memorial said the monument "should never be used as a canvas for vandalism," while the Veterans of Foreign Wars called the act "a slap in the face to the veterans who served and sacrificed."

Visitors at the memorial expressed similar outrage. "It makes me mad," said Scott Wimpfheimer, who traveled from Minnesota. "It's taxpayer money to clean up a mess of somebody trying to make a point, which he's not doing." Joshua Szklarz, whose great grandfather served in World War II, called the vandalism "very sad" and "so disrespectful to the veterans."

## The Reflecting Pool Context

The arrest comes amid an ongoing political controversy over the nearby Lincoln Memorial Reflecting Pool. Trump had claimed the pool's damage was the work of vandals after his administration oversaw a $14 million renovation, but U.S. Attorney Pirro moved to dismiss charges against former Olympian David Hearn and three others on August 1, citing evidence that the damage resulted from a "botched installation" by the contractor rather than vandalism, as [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/08/03/nx-s1-5918001/reflecting-pool-vandalism-charges-dropped) reported.

Trump sharply criticized Pirro for dropping the charges, saying she "folded like an umbrella." The case dismissal was described as a rare moment during Trump's second term of a political appointee openly defying him. Trump connected the two incidents in his Truth Social post, writing "First the Reflecting Pool, now this."

## The Memorial's Significance

The National World War II Memorial honors the 16 million Americans who served in the U.S. Armed Forces during World War II and commemorates the more than 400,000 who died in the conflict. Authorized by legislation signed by President Bill Clinton in 1993, the memorial was designed by architect Friedrich St. Florian and opened to the public on April 29, 2004, as detailed by [Grokipedia](https://grokipedia.com/page/World_War_II_Memorial). It features 56 granite pillars representing U.S. states and territories, two 43-foot-tall arches denoting the European-African and Pacific theaters, and the Freedom Wall bearing 4,048 gold stars.

The memorial had recently reopened in mid-May after a $4 million renovation project that included updates to its fountains and lighting.

## What's Next

Farris appeared at a hearing at the federal courthouse in Alexandria on Friday afternoon, where a judge granted her lawyer's request to move the case to federal court in Washington, D.C. A detention and preliminary hearing is scheduled for Monday. Farris's attorney said she would be represented by the public defender's office.

As the case moves forward, the incident has underscored the deep reverence Americans hold for the memorial and the broader political tensions surrounding the treatment of national landmarks in the nation's capital. The federal government has pledged to hold those responsible accountable, and the public will be watching closely as the legal process unfolds.
