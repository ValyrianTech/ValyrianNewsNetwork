# Mangione Pleads Guilty in UnitedHealthcare CEO Killing

Luigi Mangione pleaded guilty on Friday to federal stalking charges in the killing of UnitedHealthcare CEO Brian Thompson, admitting matter-of-factly that he trailed the executive to an investor conference in 2024 and gunned him down on a Manhattan street. The plea, entered at the Thurgood Marshall United States Courthouse, marks a dramatic turning point in one of the most closely watched criminal cases in recent American history.

"On the morning of Dec. 4, 2024, I shot Mr. Thompson in Manhattan, and he died," Mangione, 28, told U.S. District Judge Margaret M. Garnett, according to [AP News](https://apnews.com/article/luigi-mangione-plea-unitedhealthcare-ceo-3b8a5bb41589c9f5f4775dba2beea66f). Listening in the audience, Thompson's wife, Paulette, took several deep breaths and repeatedly wiped tears from her eyes.

## A Surprise Admission Without a Deal

The guilty plea came without any plea agreement or concessions from federal prosecutors. U.S. Attorney Jamie McDonald for the Southern District of New York stated there were "no accommodations or concessions" given to Mangione, and that his office would seek the maximum penalty. Mangione faces up to life in prison on the two stalking charges, with sentencing scheduled for December 18.

In his allocution, Mangione described how he planned the attack. He said he used a 3D printer to make a gun equipped with a silencer, traveled to New York, and posed as an investor to obtain details about UnitedHealth Group's annual investor conference. "I emailed UnitedHealthcare with the intent to shoot Brian Thompson," he told the court. He explained his motive, saying he pursued Thompson "after years of enduring severe pain from a broken back, navigating the obstacles of the health insurance system and witnessing similar experiences of countless others."

Mangione's defense lawyer, Karen Friedman Agnifilo, said outside court that her client "has accepted full responsibility for the death of Brian Thompson," while noting that he "believed that the system had failed him and destroyed his life." [BBC News](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cwy0nlq1l2wo) reported that Mangione appeared solemn and serious throughout the hearing, wearing a beige prison outfit.

## The Road to the Plea

The case began on December 4, 2024, when Thompson, 50, was shot from behind outside the New York Hilton Midtown as he walked to UnitedHealth Group's annual investor conference. Surveillance video captured the masked gunman, and the words "delay," "deny," and "depose" were found written on ammunition at the scene, mimicking a phrase used to describe how insurers avoid paying claims.

Mangione was arrested five days later at a McDonald's in Altoona, Pennsylvania, about 230 miles west of Manhattan, after a nationwide manhunt. Police found a 3D-printed pistol and a notebook in his backpack, along with writings that authorities said described his intent to kill an insurance executive. As [AP News](https://apnews.com/article/luigi-mangione-timeline-plea-unitedhealthcare-ceo-af6fe88f1868e4027c91c7141db99f5f) detailed in its timeline, Mangione was initially charged in Pennsylvania with forgery and false identification before being extradited to New York and facing both state and federal charges.

The legal saga was marked by dramatic twists. In April 2025, then-U.S. Attorney General Pam Bondi directed federal prosecutors to seek the death penalty against Mangione, calling Thompson's killing "a premeditated, cold-blooded assassination that shocked America." But in January 2026, Judge Garnett barred federal prosecutors from seeking capital punishment, dismissing the federal murder charge that enabled it.

In the state case, Judge Gregory Carro threw out terrorism charges against Mangione in September 2025, rejecting the prosecution's theory that the killing was "intended to evoke terror." The ruling eliminated the top two charges in the state case, sparing Mangione a mandatory life sentence without parole, while keeping other charges including second-degree murder.

## The Double Jeopardy Question

Within minutes of the federal plea, Mangione's lawyers filed a motion seeking to dismiss the state murder case on double jeopardy grounds. New York has unusually strong double jeopardy protections compared to federal law and other states, and the defense argues that the federal stalking conviction and the state murder charges stem from the same criminal act.

Legal experts say the defense has a strong case. Neama Rahmani, a former federal prosecutor and founder of West Coast Trial Lawyers, told [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/aug/15/luigi-mangione-double-jeopardy-state-case) that "dual prosecutions are prohibited if they're part of the same criminal act or occurrence," adding that while the dismissal push "could go either way," he believes "they'll be successful."

Anna Cominsky, a professor of law at New York Law School, said Mangione's statement in court "was really focused on the same elements that the state would have to prove," making it difficult for the state to argue the factual allegations can be distinguished. Ron Kuby, a longtime criminal defense attorney, was more blunt: "The state case is dead. It hasn't been taken off life support yet, but it will be."

However, the Manhattan District Attorney's office said it remains committed to its case. "We are encouraged that Mr. Thompson's family received a measure of accountability today," the office said in a statement. "While federal sentencing is pending, we are prepared to litigate the defense motions."

## A Case That Divided the Nation

The killing of Thompson, a husband and father of two sons, sparked a national conversation about the health insurance industry. Mangione, an Ivy League graduate from a wealthy Maryland family, became a cause célèbre for some critics of the industry, with an online fundraiser for his legal defense raising more than $1.5 million. Supporters have shown up to court appearances wearing green clothing and "FREE LUIGI" T-shirts.

But elected officials and law enforcement have condemned the killing in the strongest terms. "To those who idolize Luigi Mangione, I say this. Violence is not a cause. Murder is not a message. And a killer is certainly not a hero," NYPD Commissioner Jessica Tisch said outside court on Friday, as [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/08/14/nx-s1-5930700/mangione-federal-charges-guilty-plea) reported.

U.S. Attorney McDonald echoed that sentiment: "There can be no celebrity in assassination. No grievance, political belief, or ideological cause can justify murder."

Thompson's family, in a statement, said the plea "marks an important step toward justice for Brian and for our family," adding: "While nothing will ease the pain of losing him, we are grateful that the federal justice system has held the person responsible for this heinous act accountable."

## What Comes Next

Mangione's sentencing is set for December 18. Federal sentencing guidelines call for a range of 24 to 30 years, but prosecutors have said they will seek life in prison. Judge Garnett noted that she alone will determine the sentence, telling Mangione that "no one, not your lawyers, not the government's lawyers, no one can give you any assurance of what your sentence will be."

Meanwhile, the fate of the state murder case rests with Judge Carro, who will need to rule on the defense's double jeopardy motion. As [USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/nation/2026/08/14/luigi-mangione-guilty-federal-charges/91290392007/) reported, the plea "allows him to avoid a federal trial and attempt to dismiss a separate case in the fatal shooting of a health care executive."

Whatever the outcome, the case has already left an indelible mark on American legal history. From the unprecedented public fascination with the defendant to the complex legal questions it raised about dual prosecutions, the Mangione case will be studied for years to come. The immediate question now is whether the state trial scheduled for September 8 will proceed, or whether the double jeopardy motion will bring it to an end.
