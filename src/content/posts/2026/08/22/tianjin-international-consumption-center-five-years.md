# Tianjin's Five-Year Push as Consumption Hub

Five years after being designated as one of China's first international consumption center cities, Tianjin is showcasing the fruits of a strategy that has transformed its commercial landscape — from flagship brand openings and immersive retail experiences to international festivals and cruise tourism. The northern port city has introduced more than 2,200 first stores across catering, retail, culture, and automotive sectors since 2021, according to [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/local/20260822/0d1bfd551ba34b2bab4c4d518dc6d714/c.html).

## A National Mandate Takes Shape

In July 2021, the State Council approved Shanghai, Beijing, Guangzhou, Tianjin, and Chongqing as China's first five international consumption center cities. The designation came with a clear mandate: build globally attractive consumption environments that drive domestic demand and expand high-level opening-up.

The five cities have since become the backbone of China's inbound consumption. In 2025, they accounted for one-third of inbound foreign visitors to China, half of consumer goods imports, and two-thirds of tax-refund sales, as reported by the [People's Daily Overseas Edition via Xinhua](http://www.xhnet.com/fortune/20260609/94ff1bbb11984deb856f3a17f8400285/c.html).

For Tianjin, the path to this status was not without challenges. The city had long been known as a manufacturing and industrial powerhouse rather than a consumer destination. But the international consumption center designation provided a catalyst for transformation, leveraging Tianjin's unique advantages — its dual sea and air port capabilities, historical European-style architecture, and deep commercial heritage.

## First-Store Economy Takes Off

At the heart of Tianjin's strategy is the first-store economy — attracting brands to open their first Tianjin or first China location in the city. The approach has yielded impressive results.

In 2025 alone, Tianjin introduced 576 first stores, averaging 48 per month, including more than 300 quality first stores — a 96 percent year-on-year increase, according to [Xinhua Tianjin](http://tj.news.cn/20250807/b094165b2e6541f499450cba95e893c2/c.html). Central urban districts including Heping, Hexi, and Nankai account for over half of all first-store openings.

The influx spans global and domestic brands alike. Hourglass, a US beauty brand, opened its first Tianjin store at Tianjin Joy City, where monthly sales have exceeded 500,000 yuan since launch. "Since opening, store foot traffic has continued to grow, with monthly sales exceeding 500,000 yuan, performing at a good level among the brand's national stores," said Liu Qingxi, the store's manager.

MINISO FRIENDS, the Chinese lifestyle retailer's IP-themed concept store, opened its first Tianjin location with over 100 globally recognized IP products across distinct themed spaces. Store manager Sun Huaiyu noted the store draws approximately 7,000 paying customers daily, with strong recognition for its spatial design and immersive shopping experience.

Even China's time-honored brands are joining the first-store wave. Ostrich Ink (鸵鸟墨水), a century-old Tianjin brand that traces its origins to 1875 and produced China's first drop of ink in 1935, opened its first national writing experience space at Tianjin Joy City in August 2026, featuring nearly 400 new ink colors.

## Consumption Scenes Reimagined

Beyond brand attraction, Tianjin has focused on transforming its urban spaces into vibrant consumption destinations. The Tianjin Commerce Bureau recently released the "Tianjin Consumption New Scenes 100+" handbook, cataloguing over 100 projects across six dimensions — river, sea, buildings, port, streets, and mountains, as [reported by Xinhua Tianjin](http://www.tj.xinhuanet.com/20260811/39ce27c71a6d4dd0831dc44e6f48cc94/c.html).

Historical districts, industrial heritage sites, foreign-style buildings, and port waterfronts are being converted into experiential consumption spaces. At Jinwan Square, a cultural exhibition on Shanxi's five-thousand-year civilization brings traditional Chinese culture to the city's core commercial district. At the former residence of Li Jifu, an art collaboration exhibition pairs historical architecture with domestic trend brands, turning old buildings into new cultural consumption venues.

"Tianjin continues to innovate distinctive consumption scenarios, deepen the integration of culture, commerce, tourism, and sports, create consumer brands and distinctive IPs with Tianjin identity, and unleash consumption momentum," said He Xiaoyang, director of the Consumption Promotion Division at the Tianjin Commerce Bureau.

## International Appeal Grows

Tianjin's international credentials are also expanding. In August 2026, the "Russia Made" festival market made its Tianjin debut at the historic Heping Road Golden Street, with nearly 60 Russian companies showcasing agricultural products, cosmetics, daily consumer goods, and jewelry, as [reported by China News Service](https://www.tj.chinanews.com.cn/wl/2026-08-17/detail-ihfimfee7174498.shtml).

The five-day event, themed around the Russian "dacha" summer countryside lifestyle, featured performances by the Moscow Classical Ballet Theatre and Russian folk music. Veronika Nikishina, general director of the Russian Export Center, said the goal was to let Chinese consumers "experience the real Russian summer life."

The cruise economy is another pillar of Tianjin's international consumption strategy. The Tianjin International Cruise Home Port has welcomed multiple international cruise ships in 2026, including Adora Mediterranea, residential luxury cruise World, Oceania Cruises' Regatta, and Azamara's Azamara Quest. "With the coordinated improvement of port channels, cruise tourism, and inbound services, Tianjin's international trade distribution and passenger flow reception capacity continue to strengthen, and the consumption ecosystem continues to optimize," said Yang Dejin, an expert at Nankai University's China Modernization Research Institute, in an [interview with Xinhua](http://tj.news.cn/20260731/7918726168e643aab19f91ecd06836ca/c.html).

## Policy Support and Forum Momentum

The 2026 Haihe International Consumption Forum, held on July 30 at the National Convention Center (Tianjin) Marriott Hotel, marked the fifth anniversary of Tianjin's designation and brought together more than 300 guests including multinational enterprise leaders and international experts, as [reported by Xinhua Tianjin](http://www.tj.xinhuanet.com/20260731/1b0f92561a8140c7a10b0f78cea394ec/c.html). At the forum, Nankai University and the Tianjin Commerce Bureau jointly released the "Five-Year Practice Report on Tianjin's International Consumption Center City Construction."

Supportive policy has been crucial. In 2025, the State Council approved the Ministry of Commerce's "Several Measures to Support the Cultivation and Construction of International Consumption Center Cities," providing a national framework for Tianjin and other designated cities. Locally, Tianjin issued its "Action Plan for Building a 'Big Consumption' Pattern" and introduced 16 measures to promote high-quality development of the first-launch economy, as [detailed by CNR](https://www.cnr.cn/tj/sgjs/20260330/t20260330_527566943.shtml).

## Looking Ahead

Tianjin Commerce Bureau Director Sun Jiannan outlined the city's forward-looking approach: "Tianjin will continue to deepen the construction of an international consumption center city, strengthen the improvement of the consumption promotion policy system, create consumer brands and consumption scenarios that lead consumption trends, and further create a consumption environment with global appeal."

Yang Dejin of Nankai University identified four development opportunities for Tianjin's next phase: seizing innovation and creativity opportunities, capitalizing on digital technology development, leveraging Tianjin-style culture for cultural and creative growth, and expanding opening-up through Beijing-Tianjin-Hebei coordinated development and the SCO Tianjin Summit.

As China's broader strategy to boost domestic consumption continues to unfold, Tianjin's five-year experiment offers a compelling case study in urban economic transformation — one that blends global brand attraction with local cultural heritage, and pairs policy innovation with grassroots commercial energy. The city's ability to sustain momentum will depend on how effectively it converts its growing consumer ecosystem into lasting economic vitality.
