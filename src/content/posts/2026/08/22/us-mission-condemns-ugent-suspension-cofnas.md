# US Mission Condemns UGent Suspension of Professor Nathan Cofnas

The United States Mission to Belgium has condemned in the strongest terms Ghent University's (UGent) decision to suspend American researcher Nathan Cofnas, calling it an act of retaliation against a scholar who exposed academic fraud. The diplomatic rebuke has escalated an already volatile controversy surrounding the death of Cambridge professor Jason Arday into a transatlantic dispute over academic freedom and institutional accountability.

US Ambassador to Belgium Bill White announced on Friday that Washington is [reviewing its relationships with Ghent University](https://humanevents.com/2026/08/21/ambassador-to-belgium-to-review-us-relationship-with-ghent-university-over-scapegoating-of-nathan-cofnas-for-jason-ardays-death), stating that "dishonest, corrupt institutions that engage in, or reward, scapegoating mob behavior are not desirable partners for us." The ambassador's remarks came after UGent [suspended Cofnas](https://www.rtbf.be/article/l-universite-de-gand-suspend-le-chercheur-nathan-cofnas-apres-la-mort-de-jason-arday-11773176) on August 20, pending a preliminary disciplinary investigation.

## The Arday Affair

The controversy traces back to mid-July, when Cofnas published an article on Substack titled "DEI Fraud and Cover-Up at Cambridge," alleging that Jason Arday's doctoral thesis contained numerous passages lifted directly from another research work with minimal editing. Arday, who had become Cambridge's youngest Black professor in 2023, [resigned on August 5](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/08/06/jason-arday-professor-ontslagen-camebridge/) after Cambridge announced it would investigate his academic qualifications and career. He was [found dead at his home in Battersea, south London](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/08/14/voormalig-cambridge-professor-jason-arday-dood-teruggevonden-na/) on August 14, a week after his resignation.

More than 30,000 people attended a vigil for Arday in Trafalgar Square on August 17, with many blaming the intense media scrutiny and what they described as a "racist feeding frenzy" for his death. The affair has sparked a broader debate in the UK about the role of media coverage and DEI policies in higher education.

## US Condemnation and Diplomatic Review

Ambassador White's statement on August 21 marked a significant escalation of the affair into international diplomacy. "The United States Mission to Belgium condemns, in the strongest terms, Ghent University's retaliation against an American scholar following his accurate whistleblower reporting on academic fraud," White said, according to [RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/la-mission-des-etats-unis-en-belgique-condamne-dans-les-termes-les-plus-forts-l-acte-de-represailles-de-l-ugent-declare-bill-white-11773628).

The ambassador emphasized that the US government regularly funds research and academic exchanges with overseas universities, and that institutions engaging in what he called "scapegoating mob behavior" would not be desirable partners. "We are therefore reviewing any relationships we have with Ghent University," he stated.

White also pushed back against critics who frame the controversy as a referendum on Cofnas's controversial views on race and intelligence. "Free speech means that controversial views are protected," he said. "And Cofnas's views were known to Ghent University when he was hired."

## Cofnas's Controversial Background

Nathan Cofnas is a self-described "race realist" who has argued that Black people are genetically predisposed to have lower IQs than white people and that in a meritocracy, Black people would disappear from almost all high-profile positions outside of sports and entertainment. He was [dismissed from Emmanuel College, Cambridge in 2024](https://www.theguardian.com/uk-news/2026/aug/20/academic-who-accused-jason-arday-of-plagiarism-suspended-by-his-university) over his views on race and has since launched legal action against the college.

His appointment at Ghent University in March 2026 [sparked significant protests](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/03/27/ugent-nathan-cofnas-aanstelling-omstreden-onderzoeker-protest-ra/), with more than 2,000 signatures collected against it. Rector Petra De Sutter defended the appointment at the time, citing academic freedom. Cofnas's views were well documented before his hiring, a point Ambassador White emphasized in his statement.

Cofnas announced his suspension on X, stating: "I was just suspended by Ghent University. They will almost certainly fire me." He also noted the decision was made by Rector De Sutter, whom he described as "a former leader of the Green Party."

## UGent's Position and Internal Criticism

UGent stated it had launched a "preliminary disciplinary investigation" and suspended Cofnas as a precautionary measure. Rector De Sutter and Vice-Rector Herwig Reynaert said in a joint statement: "We attach great importance to academic freedom and to open academic debate, even when views are controversial. However, that freedom is not unlimited. It goes hand in hand with responsibility and may be restricted in order to protect the rights of others."

The university's decision has drawn criticism from within its own ranks. Professor Ignaas Devisch, a professor of medical philosophy and ethics at UGent, has publicly questioned the timing of the investigation. "My first reaction was: why this timing?" Devisch told [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/08/22/ignaas-devisch-over-nathan-cofnas-petra-de-sutter-communicatie/). "Unless I missed something, Cofnas had already said things before he was appointed to a temporary research project at my university that are no different from what he has said now."

Devisch called on Rector De Sutter to communicate more clearly about the decision, warning that "the entire start of the academic year will be dominated by this" otherwise. "You link the preliminary investigation to the fact that Cofnas brings plagiarism to light," he said. "This gives the impression that you want to get back at him, that you want to restrict freedom of expression and that you have a problem with the fact that plagiarism was exposed. That is very unfortunate."

## Cofnas's Response

In an extensive interview with [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/08/21/nathan-cofnas-reactie-jason-arday-ugent-cambridge/), Cofnas acknowledged that his actions had set in motion a chain of events with tragic consequences. "I have set in motion a series of events that led to a tragic outcome," he said. "I had zero interest in Jason Arday. I had no ill will towards him. I wanted him to remain a professor and live his best life. This was about DEI and Cambridge. Arday played only a supporting role."

Regarding his controversial comment about heading a "eugenics and racial science department at Harvard," Cofnas described it as a joke. "It was a joke. I didn't even realize the camera was running at the time," he said. "The joke is based on a kind of truth. Namely, that I believe there should be a racial science. I believe academia should study race. It should study hereditarianism."

## Analysis: A Test for Academic Freedom

The Cofnas affair has become a lightning rod for competing visions of academic freedom. For supporters of Cofnas, the suspension represents an attack on whistleblowing and free expression by an institution that hired him knowing his views. For critics, the case highlights the limits of academic freedom when speech crosses into what they describe as racist pseudoscience.

Devisch articulated the tension at the heart of the controversy. "Plagiarism must be brought to light. That is terrible. And whether it is Cofnas who does it, or someone else, that doesn't matter," he said. But he also criticized what he called Cofnas's "classic" pattern of "shouting about everything that's going wrong in the world, then making racist statements and then saying I am not allowed to say anything anymore."

The diplomatic dimension adds another layer of complexity. The US government's review of its relationship with UGent could have significant financial implications for the Belgian university, which receives US funding for research and academic exchanges. It also raises questions about whether foreign governments should intervene in the internal disciplinary proceedings of academic institutions.

## What's Next

The immediate future of the controversy hinges on the outcome of UGent's preliminary disciplinary investigation into Cofnas. The university has said it is assessing whether there are sufficient grounds to refer the case to its disciplinary body, but has declined to provide further details due to confidentiality requirements.

Devisch has urged the university leadership to be more transparent. "I understand the confidentiality of this file, but you must make clear to the public opinion what is happening," he said. "Otherwise the entire start of the academic year will be dominated by this. That would be a very bad thing for everyone who works or studies at the university."

Meanwhile, the US Mission's condemnation has raised the stakes considerably. Whether Washington follows through on its threat to review relationships with UGent, and how the university responds to the diplomatic pressure, will determine whether this affair remains a Belgian academic controversy or becomes a lasting transatlantic dispute.
