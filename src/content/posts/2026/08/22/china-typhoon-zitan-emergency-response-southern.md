# China Activates Emergency Response as Typhoon Zitan Threatens South

Chinese authorities have activated coordinated emergency response mechanisms as Typhoon Zitan, the 19th named storm of the 2026 Pacific typhoon season, lingers over the Beibu Gulf and threatens southern China with extreme rainfall. The China Meteorological Administration (CMA) upgraded its Level IV emergency response to cover both typhoon and rainstorm warnings on Saturday morning, while the Ministry of Water Resources simultaneously activated a Level IV flood defense response for Guangdong, Guangxi, and Hainan provinces, according to [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/20260822/9206c321c99b44af8236ab888e46eb68/c.html).

## A Lingering Threat in the Beibu Gulf

Typhoon Zitan, named by the Japan Meteorological Agency on Saturday morning, was located approximately 30 kilometers south of Dongxing City in Guangxi. The storm is currently a tropical storm with maximum wind speeds of 18 meters per second (Force 8), according to the CMA's National Meteorological Center, which has issued blue typhoon and rainstorm warnings.

The storm's most dangerous characteristic is its expected prolonged stay in the Beibu Gulf. Forecasters predict Zitan will circle and linger in the gulf through the night of August 23, gradually strengthening to a severe tropical storm before slowly moving northeast toward the Leizhou Peninsula or the southern coast of Guangxi starting August 24.

Meteorological experts warned that the extended lingering period would bring persistent heavy rainfall to Hainan, Guangxi, Guangdong, and Fujian, with cumulative rainfall in some areas reaching extreme levels. Hainan has already recorded 300-400 millimeters of rainfall, and the continued accumulation poses significant risks of secondary disasters.

## Coordinated Multi-Agency Response

The response involves an unprecedented level of coordination across multiple government agencies. The National Flood Control and Drought Relief Headquarters and the Ministry of Emergency Management activated a Level IV flood and typhoon emergency response for Hainan, Guangxi, and Guangdong on Friday evening. The Guangdong Provincial Flood Control Headquarters followed with its own Level IV wind prevention response, while the Guangdong Maritime Safety Administration activated a tropical cyclone response requiring all ships west of Zhuhai to implement typhoon prevention measures, as reported by [China News Service](https://www.chinanews.com.cn/sh/2026/08-19/10680426.shtml).

The Ministry of Water Resources' [flood defense directive](https://www.news.cn/20260822/f65cbecd69924d57a7c407bee0e8f325/c.html) emphasizes protecting against mountain torrent disasters and flooding of small and medium rivers, implementing safety controls for mobile populations including summer tourists and construction workers, and establishing clear evacuation protocols.

## Rivers Under Pressure

From August 22-24, heavy to torrential rain is expected across western and southern Guangxi, Guangdong, and Hainan. The Yujiang River and its tributaries in Guangxi, along with the Qinjiang, Fangcheng River, and Nanliu River on the southern Guangxi coast, are expected to see rising water levels. Parts of Guangxi's Yujiang tributaries may experience above-warning-level flooding, and some small and medium rivers in the rainstorm area could exceed warning levels.

## A Season of Heightened Vulnerability

The approaching storm comes just weeks after Typhoon Meisak devastated Guangxi in early July, causing catastrophic flooding that resulted in 159 deaths and 10 missing persons across 16 counties, affecting over 1.65 million people. As [Observer Network](https://m.guancha.cn/ChengShi/2026_08_21_828098.shtml) reported, this was described as the most severe natural disaster in Guangxi in recent years, triggering the province's first-ever Level I emergency response.

The 2026 typhoon season has been exceptionally active, with 18 typhoons formed in the Western Pacific and South China Sea by late August—significantly above the long-term average. Four have made landfall in China. The current situation is further complicated by multiple simultaneously active tropical systems, including Typhoon Shadel, which is rapidly strengthening and could reach super typhoon strength, potentially affecting East China or Japan/Korea around August 26-29, as detailed in [CCTV News analysis](http://m.cnhubei.com/content/2026-08/21/content_20151609.html).

## Lessons Learned and Forward Preparedness

The coordinated response to Typhoon Zitan reflects lessons learned from the Meisak disaster. The Ministry of Water Resources has emphasized the "five key responsibility links" framework—who organizes, who to transfer, when to transfer, where to transfer, and no unauthorized return—representing an institutionalized approach to evacuation management refined after July's tragedy.

As Typhoon Zitan continues its slow churn in the Beibu Gulf, authorities are urging vigilance across the affected regions. The storm's extended presence means the threat is not just from wind but from sustained, potentially extreme rainfall accumulation over multiple days. With soil already saturated from previous weather systems this season, the risk of landslides, mudslides, and secondary disasters remains elevated.

Residents in affected areas are advised to monitor official warnings closely and follow evacuation instructions from local authorities as the situation develops over the coming days.
