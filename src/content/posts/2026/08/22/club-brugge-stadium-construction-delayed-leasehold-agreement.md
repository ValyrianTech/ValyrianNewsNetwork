# Club Brugge Stadium Construction Delayed by Leasehold Agreement with City

The construction of Club Brugge's new stadium must wait as the club awaits a new leasehold (erfpacht) agreement with the Bruges city government, according to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/08/22/bouw-stadion-club-brugge-moet-wachten-op-erfpachtakkoord-met-sta/). The delay adds another chapter to the long-running saga of the football club's stadium project, which has faced numerous obstacles over more than two decades.

The club won a significant legal victory on July 9 when the Raad voor Vergunningsbetwistingen (Council for Permit Disputes) rejected neighborhood residents' objections to the environmental permit, as [VRT NWS reported](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/07/09/19-jaar-juridische-strijden-club-en-stad-voor-een-nieuw-stadion/). That ruling cleared the way for construction to begin, but a new administrative hurdle has emerged: the leasehold agreement for the Olympiasite in Sint-Andries must be renegotiated before the shovel can hit the ground.

## The Leasehold Obstacle

The Olympiasite, where the current Jan Breydelstadion stands, is owned by the City of Bruges. Club Brugge and Cercle Brugge currently hold a joint leasehold agreement with the city that governs usage rights, maintenance obligations, duration, and compensation. For the new stadium project, a new agreement must be drafted.

"There have been discussions about this in the past," said Pieter Marechal (CD&V), Bruges City Alderman for Properties. "The framework with general principles has been established for a while. We were waiting for an executable permit to proceed with making concrete agreements. This indeed concerns, among other things, the compensation, duration, and rules around the use of the site."

## Municipal Council Timeline

Club Brugge wants to begin construction this autumn and hopes to start the 2028-2029 season in the new stadium. However, the leasehold dossier is not on the agenda for the municipal council meeting of August 31, and the September session is also too early.

"We will be able to present the dossier to the municipal council for approval at the earliest in October," Marechal stated, as reported by [Voetbalprimeur](https://www.voetbalprimeur.be/nieuws/2126027/club-brugge-wacht-nog-op-akkoord-voor-bouw-nieuw-stadion.html).

## A 20-Year Legal Battle

The stadium project has been one of the most contentious infrastructure initiatives in Belgian football. Plans were first officially announced in January 2007 at a press conference at Bruges City Hall, with an initial location proposed in Loppem. Since then, the project has weathered three different proposed locations, multiple annulled regional spatial implementation plans (GRUPs), and numerous legal challenges from neighborhood residents, action groups, and even business competitors.

The breakthrough came on July 9, 2026, when the Council for Permit Disputes rejected the appeal of 20 neighborhood residents against the environmental permit. As [Sporza reported](https://sporza.be/nl/2026/07/09/club-brugge-mag-na-20-jaar-eindelijk-zijn-nieuwe-stadion-beginnen-te-bouwen-bezwaren-buurtbewoners-verworpen~1783585378389/), the permit was declared definitive and executable, allowing Club Brugge to move forward with its plans for a 40,116-seat stadium southwest of the current Jan Breydelstadion.

Six neighborhood residents filed a cassation appeal with the Council of State on August 19, but this does not have suspensive effect and does not block construction, as [VRT NWS detailed](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/08/19/stadion-club-brugge-dossier-beroep-cassatieberoep-raad-van-state/). Alderman for Spatial Planning Mathijs Goderis (Vooruit) expressed confidence that the decision would stand, noting that "in a cassation procedure, the Council of State will not re-examine the content of the dossier, but the court will only review whether all rules and procedures were correctly applied."

## UEFA Pressure and the Need for a New Stadium

The urgency behind the project is underscored by UEFA's persistent pressure on the club. Mayor Dirk De fauw revealed that UEFA had repeatedly emphasized that it could no longer accept European matches being played at Jan Breydel indefinitely, as [Sporza reported](https://sporza.be/nl/2026/07/09/brugse-burgemeester-dirk-de-fauw-reageert-opgelucht-op-stadionnieuws-veel-nachten-van-wakker-gelegen~1783588766688/).

The current Jan Breydelstadion, built in 1975, has a capacity of only 29,062 seats and suffers from aging infrastructure, limited accessibility, and safety concerns. The new stadium, by contrast, will feature 40,116 seats, closed and covered stands, and a low cornice designed to minimize visual impact. It will be embedded in a park environment called the Olympiapark, serving as a neighborhood park on non-match days.

## What's Next

The immediate path forward depends on the leasehold negotiations between Club Brugge and the city government. Once an agreement is reached, it must be approved by the municipal council, with October cited as the earliest possible date. Club Brugge remains optimistic that construction can begin this autumn, with the goal of opening the new stadium for the 2028-2029 season.

For a project that has survived more than two decades of legal battles, permit annulments, and political obstacles, the leasehold agreement represents one final administrative step before the long-awaited construction can finally begin. As [Voetbalkrant noted](https://www.voetbalkrant.com/nieuws/2026-08-22/bouw-jan-breydel-stuit-op-ng-een-probleem), the saga continues, but the finish line is now in sight.
