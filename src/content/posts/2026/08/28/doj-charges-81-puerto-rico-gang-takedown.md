# DOJ Charges 81 in Puerto Rico Gang Takedown

The U.S. Justice Department has charged 81 individuals in one of the largest gang prosecutions in Puerto Rico's recent history, targeting the violent drug trafficking organization known as "Los Baja Deo" (LBD) operating in the western city of Mayagüez. Attorney General Todd Blanche announced the sweeping indictment during a visit to San Juan on Thursday, as federal agents and Puerto Rico police arrested 70 defendants a day earlier in a coordinated takedown operation.

## The Indictment

A federal grand jury in the District of Puerto Rico returned the indictment on August 19, accusing the defendants of conspiracy to possess controlled substances with intent to distribute, drug possession and distribution, and firearms-related offenses. According to the [Justice Department](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/81-members-violent-gang-puerto-rico-charged-drug-trafficking-and-firearms-violations-part), the gang allegedly trafficked fentanyl, cocaine, heroin, crack cocaine, and marijuana since 2021.

"These 81 defendants are alleged drug traffickers of 'Los Baja Deo' also known as 'LBD,' who maintained a steady supply of highly addictive and potentially lethal substances that endangered the community, fueled addiction and exposed entire public housing projects in Mayagüez and nearby neighborhoods to violence and chaos," Blanche said.

The organization allegedly distributed at least 340 kilograms of fentanyl or fentanyl-laced narcotics and 2,200 kilograms of cocaine, generating over $49 million in proceeds. Prosecutors are seeking forfeiture of $49.7 million. Law enforcement also seized 21 firearms during the investigation, and 21 defendants face an additional charge of possessing firearms in furtherance of a drug trafficking crime.

## The Operation

The gang allegedly operated 23 drug sale points in public housing projects and surrounding neighborhoods in Mayagüez, a city of approximately 72,000 residents on Puerto Rico's western coast. The organization maintained a structured hierarchy including leaders, drug point owners, suppliers, enforcers, runners, sellers, lookouts, and facilitators, according to the indictment.

Members communicated using two-way radios, cell phones, instant messaging, and social media. When gang members perceived threats to the organization, they committed violence including shootings, kidnappings, and murders to protect their operations.

The investigation was led by the FBI and Puerto Rico Police Department as part of the Homeland Security Task Force (HSTF) initiative, established by Executive Order 14159. FBI Director Kash Patel praised the operation, calling it "yet another huge success for the FBI, the Homeland Security Task Force, and all of our law enforcement partners in San Juan."

"These arrests disrupted one of western Puerto Rico's most violent drug-trafficking organizations, dismantled its leadership and distribution networks, and cut off yet another key pipeline of dealt drugs flowing into our country," Patel said.

## Puerto Rico's Role in Drug Trafficking

The case highlights Puerto Rico's critical role as a corridor for narcotics entering the U.S. mainland. According to Blanche, approximately 80% of drugs that arrive in Puerto Rico end up being shipped to the United States and Canada. The gang allegedly used the U.S. Postal Service to send drugs from the island to the continental United States, taking advantage of the fact that packages moving within U.S. territories do not cross a customs border.

Acting U.S. Attorney Héctor Ramírez-Carbó for the District of Puerto Rico said the arrests send a clear message to criminal organizations. "These 81 violent gang members will now face justice and so will any other that try to take their place," he said. "Today, the families of Mayagüez can peacefully enjoy what their communities have to offer without fear of harm."

## Broader Context

The takedown is part of the Trump administration's expanded federal efforts to combat drug trafficking in U.S. territories and across the Western Hemisphere. The administration has designated Latin American criminal groups as "foreign terrorist organizations," allowing authorities to pursue them under counterterrorism laws. As [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/27/us-charges-81-in-puerto-rico-gang-case-as-trump-cartel-crackdown-continues) reported, the administration has also conducted aerial strikes on suspected drug-smuggling boats, resulting in approximately 227 deaths since September 2025.

Blanche's visit to Puerto Rico was part of the administration's broader push to highlight its anti-crime efforts, with the attorney general warning that "our Homeland Security Task Forces around the country are unified to end the presence of criminal cartels, foreign gangs, and transnational criminal organizations throughout the United States."

## Legal Consequences

If convicted on drug charges, defendants face a minimum of 10 years in prison and up to life. Those convicted of both drug and firearms charges face a minimum of 15 years and up to life. All defendants are presumed innocent until proven guilty beyond a reasonable doubt in a court of law.

As [AP News](https://apnews.com/article/los-baja-deo-gang-drug-charges-indictment-02775045f52f2454495dee3b16ace6ca) reported, the operation represents one of the largest gang prosecutions in the territory's recent history, signaling continued federal pressure on drug trafficking organizations operating in U.S. territories.

## What's Next

The case will proceed through the federal court system in the District of Puerto Rico, with a team of assistant U.S. attorneys prosecuting the matter. Questions remain about the 11 defendants not arrested during the initial operation, and whether additional indictments will follow as the investigation continues. The takedown is expected to have significant impacts on drug availability and violence in Mayagüez, though experts will be watching to see whether other organizations move to fill the vacuum left by LBD's dismantled operations.
