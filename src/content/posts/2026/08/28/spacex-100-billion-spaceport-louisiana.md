# SpaceX to Build $100 Billion Spaceport in Louisiana

SpaceX has announced plans to invest $100 billion to build its largest launch facility in Vermilion Parish, Louisiana, a sprawling spaceport designed to support thousands of launches annually and serve as the primary hub for its next-generation Starship rocket. The project, unveiled Tuesday at a ceremony in Abbeville, represents one of the largest economic development commitments in state history and positions Louisiana at the center of the rapidly expanding commercial space industry.

## A Transformational Investment

Louisiana Gov. Jeff Landry hailed the announcement as a watershed moment for the state, drawing a parallel to the 1803 Louisiana Purchase. "Today is nothing short of a transformational moment," he told lawmakers and officials gathered for the announcement, as [reported by AP News](https://apnews.com/article/musk-spacex-louisiana-spaceport-rockets-201a0f8a7b479c8f2fff955495471162). "This is America First. This is Louisiana First."

The spaceport, dubbed "Starbase Louisiana," will be built on approximately 125,000 acres of land formerly owned by Exxon along the state's Gulf Coast, roughly 200 miles west of New Orleans near the unincorporated community of Pecan Island. The site offers key strategic advantages, including access to abundant natural gas for fuel production and a coastal location enabling launches across a wide range of trajectories.

According to the [Louisiana Economic Development news release](https://www.opportunitylouisiana.gov/news/spacex-launches-new-era-of-commercial-spaceflight-with-100-billion-louisiana-campus), the facility will become the company's fourth and largest launch site. At full buildout, it will feature five launch complexes with two launch pads each—10 total pads—along with propellant production facilities, power generation, vehicle processing, and residential housing for employees and their families.

## Economic Impact and Job Creation

The project is expected to create 3,000 direct new jobs over the next decade, paying an average annual salary of $92,600—192% above the Vermilion Parish average wage. Louisiana Economic Development estimates the project will generate more than 8,100 indirect jobs, totaling over 11,100 new employment opportunities across the Acadiana region.

Elon Musk, SpaceX's founder and CEO, projected even more ambitious numbers in a promotional video, saying the company would bring "probably 10,000 really exciting jobs" to Louisiana, as [CNBC reported](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/25/spacex-louisiana-spaceport.html). Musk described the venture in terms befitting his long-term vision for space exploration: "We're preparing to build a spaceport that, until now, has only existed in science fiction."

The state offered a competitive incentives package that includes tax incentives, a $25 million charitable donation to the Community Foundation of Acadiana, and participation in the new Aerospace Facilities and Activities Rebate created under Act 190 of the 2026 legislative session. SpaceX has also entered into a Payment in Lieu of Taxes agreement with local taxing bodies, expected to generate more than $820 million in direct local payments over 25 years.

SpaceX stock rose about 2% to close at $137.95 on the day of the announcement, reflecting investor confidence in the company's expansion plans following its historic IPO in June—the largest initial public offering ever.

## The Starship Connection

Starbase Louisiana is central to SpaceX's strategy for scaling up Starship, its fully reusable launch system that is the largest rocket ever built. The company is preparing for Starship's 14th test flight in September and faces a tight timeline to qualify the rocket for operational missions, including NASA's Artemis program lunar landings.

SpaceX President and COO Gwynne Shotwell explained the need for the new facility, noting that the company's existing infrastructure cannot support the launch cadence it anticipates. "Put all of this together, and these tasks will require thousands of launches," she said, as [Space.com detailed](https://www.space.com/space-exploration/private-spaceflight/spacex-announces-enormous-usd100-billion-starbase-louisiana-starship-launch-site). "Starbase Louisiana is being designed from day one to support this dramatic increase in launch cadence."

Sheila McCorkle, SpaceX's vice president of Starship legal and regulatory affairs, revealed that the company conducted a seven-year search for the ideal site. "There are only a few places on Earth where we can do what we need to do to launch Starship fully reusable at rate," she said, as [American Press reported](https://americanpress.com/2026/08/25/spacex-announces-massive-100-billion-spaceport-in-vermilion-parish/). "Louisiana happens to be the perfect candidate."

Construction is expected to begin in 2027, with the first launch targeted for as early as 2029.

## Environmental Concerns and Coastal Restoration

The project has raised significant environmental concerns. The site sits in the Chenier Plain, an important wintering area for migratory wildfowl, adjacent to the Rockefeller Wildlife Refuge. Environmental advocates have warned about potential impacts on fragile coastal ecosystems, particularly as the FAA has proposed waiving requirements under 13 wildlife, water, and public health laws for commercial space operations.

Anne Rolfes, director of the Louisiana Bucket Brigade, expressed concern about the speed of the project. "It should be thoughtfully done—with public hearings, and a public process that is transparent," she said, according to AP News. "The speed of the project and the fanfare and bombast make it all highly questionable."

Rebecca Triche, executive director of the Louisiana Wildlife Federation, echoed these worries, telling [Fox8/WVUE](https://www.fox8live.com/2026/08/25/environmental-advocates-worry-about-faa-changes-louisiana-prepares-spacex/) that "there's a lot of habitat and wildlife concerns about the site." The wildlife groups have filed formal comments opposing the FAA's proposed regulatory changes, which would waive requirements under the Endangered Species Act, the Clean Water Act, and the Clean Air Act.

SpaceX, for its part, has pledged a significant coastal restoration effort. McCorkle said the company will "literally shore up the shoreline" as part of a "historic coastal restoration in partnership with Pecan Island, Vermilion Parish, and the state to bring the marsh back and ensure proper storm protection." Shotwell added that "very large portions" of the 125,000-acre project will be left untouched to preserve high-value wetlands.

## Transparency Questions

The project's secretive development has also raised transparency concerns. Louisiana Economic Development signed a nondisclosure agreement with SpaceX in February, and some residents say they were kept in the dark about the project.

"Not knowing anything, the people of Pecan Island should have been told something first," resident Crystal Mhire told [Fox8/WVUE](https://www.fox8live.com/2026/08/28/landry-defends-use-ndas-law-professor-says-they-cant-override-publics-right-know/).

Gov. Landry defended the use of NDAs, saying they are essential to competing for major economic development projects. But Loyola constitutional law professor Dane Ciolino argued that NDAs cannot override the public's right to know under the Louisiana Constitution. "There are no exceptions to the public records laws that would shield from disclosure these NDAs," he said.

## A New Era for Louisiana's Space Coast

The announcement builds on Louisiana's six-decade legacy in the American space program, from NASA's Michoud Assembly Facility manufacturing the Saturn V rockets that took humans to the moon to today's Artemis missions. Michael Hecht, president and CEO of Greater New Orleans, Inc., framed the investment as confirmation of the state's aerospace capabilities. "Louisiana has been powering the American space program for six decades," he said, as [Fox8/WVUE reported](https://www.fox8live.com/2026/08/25/spacex-announces-plan-build-100-billion-starbase-louisiana-launch-facility/). "SpaceX's decision to build here confirms what we've always known: This state has the workforce, the industrial capacity, the energy supply and the location to lead the next era of space exploration."

As SpaceX prepares to begin construction in 2027, the project promises to reshape Louisiana's economic landscape while testing the balance between industrial ambition and environmental stewardship. The coming months will bring town hall meetings and community engagement events, as well as a public comment period on the FAA's proposed regulatory changes that expires August 31. For Louisiana, the stakes could not be higher—or the potential rewards greater.
