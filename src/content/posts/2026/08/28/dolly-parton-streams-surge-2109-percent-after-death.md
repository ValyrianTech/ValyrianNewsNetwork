# Dolly Parton Streams Surge 2,109% Following Her Death

Dolly Parton's music catalog has experienced an unprecedented surge in streaming activity following her death on Tuesday, with audio and video streams skyrocketing 2,109% in the United States and 1,280% globally, according to [AP News](https://apnews.com/article/dolly-parton-streams-songs-jolene-169ceb8052c6b0b8c3b41950d6a48569). The country music icon died at age 80 at the Vanderbilt-Ingram Cancer Center in Nashville after a brief battle with cancer.

## A Songwriter's Legacy Remembered

Parton was many things — a country star, actor, philanthropist, entrepreneur, and theme park enthusiast. But for many, her death has served as a reminder that at the heart of her legacy was her role as a songwriter. Fans around the world have responded by streaming her songs in record numbers.

Luminate, the industry data and analytics company, found that streams of Parton's discography climbed from 1.2 million to 26.3 million in the U.S. on the day of her death. Worldwide, her streams jumped from 2.5 million on Monday to 35 million on Tuesday, according to the data.

[Spotify reported](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/music/music-news/dolly-parton-spotify-streams-jump-after-death-1236682484/) Parton's overall streams skyrocketed over 1,260% globally on Aug. 25 compared to the seven-day average from Aug. 18-24, and an even higher 2,320% in the U.S. alone. On August 25 alone, her catalog pulled 19.8 million streams on Spotify, representing a 1,300% increase over the day prior.

## 'Jolene' Leads the Chart Revival

Her timeless hit "Jolene" rose to No. 1 on Spotify's Daily Global chart for August 26, pulling in 4.727 million streams. The track also topped Apple Music's U.S. songs chart, surpassing Ella Langley's "Choosin Texas" for the No. 1 spot — a notable feat given Langley's nearly 20-week run atop the Billboard Hot 100.

On the U.S. Spotify chart, "Jolene" debuted at No. 2 with 1.29 million streams, followed by "9 to 5" at No. 3 with 1.13 million streams. Parton occupied 92 of the top 200 positions on the U.S. iTunes chart, capturing 65 spots within the top 100 and breaking the record for simultaneous tracks in the U.S. iTunes Top 30 with 26 songs.

According to [Luminate data](https://www.ctvnews.ca/entertainment/article/dolly-parton-streams-skyrocket-after-her-death-up-1280-globally/), the top 10 streamed songs in the U.S. included "Jolene" (growing from 196.2K to 2.7 million streams), "9 to 5" (from 208.6K to 2.3 million), "Islands in the Stream" with Kenny Rogers (from 165.9K to 1.8 million), "I Will Always Love You" (from 64.9K to 1.7 million), and "Here You Come Again" (from 48.7K to 1.3 million).

## Fans Mourn at Parton's Brentwood Home

The streaming surge coincided with an outpouring of tributes from fans and fellow artists alike. Flowers, balloons, photographs, and handwritten messages now cover the entrance to Parton's Brentwood, Tennessee home, as [WSMV Nashville reported](https://www.wsmv.com/2026/08/26/fans-leave-flowers-heartfelt-messages-outside-dolly-partons-brentwood-home/). By Wednesday morning, the memorial had grown considerably, with bouquets stretching across the gate and signs carrying messages including "We will always love you."

Matt Kushma, a fan from Georgia who happened to be staying at a hotel just minutes from Parton's home while traveling for work, said he felt he was meant to be there to lay flowers on behalf of his mother.

"My mom was a huge Dolly Parton fan," Kushma said. "Sang her songs, sings her songs at karaoke … We grew up watching 'Steel Magnolias.' We did all that."

Kushma reflected on Parton's far-reaching influence: "She touched so many people throughout the world. Through her music, through her acting, through her theme park … she just brought love to everybody."

## Tributes from Fellow Artists

Parton's death inspired tributes from Jane Fonda, Taylor Swift, Beyoncé, Paul McCartney, Reba McEntire, Miley Cyrus, Kacey Musgraves, Blake Shelton, Sabrina Carpenter, Mariah Carey, and many others. Cyrus, Parton's goddaughter, penned an emotional tribute, writing, "I have an angel by my side. I always have."

Parton's longtime manager, Danny Nozell, noted that she "worked up until the day she died. She loved seeing her dreams come to life and loved knowing her work brought people joy."

## What's Next

The Grand Ole Opry is scheduled to dedicate its Saturday show to Parton, with Carly Pearce among the performers, according to [The Tennessean](https://www.tennessean.com/story/entertainment/music/2026/08/28/dolly-parton-grand-ole-opry-tribute-livestream/91487487007/). Parton's estate has also confirmed biopic, docuseries, and scripted series projects in development.

As [The Guardian noted](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2026/aug/25/dolly-parton-country-singer-dead), Parton was one of the greatest singer-songwriters in country music history, writing two of the 20th century's most enduring songs — "Jolene" and "I Will Always Love You" — in a single day. Her catalog of hundreds of songs, 25 U.S. country No. 1 singles, and 47 albums in the country Top 10 stands as a testament to her extraordinary career.

The streaming surge reflects a global moment of collective mourning and celebration of an artist whose appeal transcended generations, geography, and politics. Whether the spike will be sustained remains an open question, but the cultural impact of Parton's music is undeniable.
