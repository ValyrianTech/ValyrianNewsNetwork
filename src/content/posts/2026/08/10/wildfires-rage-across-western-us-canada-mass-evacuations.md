# Wildfires Rage Across Western US and Canada

Wildfires burning across the western United States and Canada have forced tens of thousands of people from their homes, destroyed hundreds of structures, and claimed multiple lives as firefighters battle blazes under extreme conditions. British Columbia declared a state of emergency over the weekend, while crews in Washington state, Utah, and Oregon continue to fight uncontained fires amid one of the most severe fire seasons in decades.

## British Columbia Declares State of Emergency

British Columbia [declared a state of emergency](https://apnews.com/article/wildfire-canada-evacuation-british-columbia-okanagan-61002c95f641a4060b78195b016fbea3) Saturday after a fast-moving wildfire forced more than 20,000 people from communities along Okanagan Lake. The Bald Range fire, first reported Friday evening, grew to more than 100 square kilometers within hours, racing roughly 15 kilometers toward the lake and destroying homes as it advanced.

The entire community of Summerland, home to about 12,000 people, was ordered evacuated overnight, along with approximately 8,000 people in and around nearby Peachland. An 80-year-old woman died while trying to escape the fire, the Royal Canadian Mounted Police confirmed Sunday.

Premier David Eby called the fire's sudden growth "a remarkable escalation in fire activity" and said the blaze became so intense that it generated its own weather system and lightning. More than 100 wildfires were burning across the province, nearly half of them out of control amid hot, windy weather and prolonged drought.

Eighteen firefighting aircraft rescued more than 50 people trapped by the advancing flames. The fire, which grew to approximately 13,618 hectares by Sunday, also forced the closure of Highway 97, a major route through the region, as roads filled with evacuees. The small community of Faulder was largely destroyed, with dozens of structures reduced to foundations.

Federal Emergency Management Minister Eleanor Olszewski approved a request for federal assistance from British Columbia to provide shelter and accommodations for those forced to flee. Officials said re-entry into evacuated areas could take "days to weeks."

## Okanagan Indian Band Devastated by Bradley Creek Fire

Earlier this month, a separate fast-moving wildfire destroyed about 230 homes in the Okanagan Indian Band community near Vernon, making it [one of the country's most destructive fires in recent years](https://apnews.com/article/wildfire-canada-homes-destroyed-british-columbia-fbbb7f027adb04a9c9b47a109de74082). Chief Dan Wilson said 30 homes of band members and 200 non-band homes had burned.

The Bradley Creek fire erupted late Friday, Aug. 1, driven by wind gusts of up to 115 kilometers per hour. Some band members had only five minutes to evacuate. Aerial firefighting crews helped save critical infrastructure, including a daycare center and a newly built school.

"It was hard to recognize my community from the air with all the structures and the forest gone," Wilson said after surveying the area by helicopter. "The fire spread so fast that some of our members only had five minutes to evacuate."

Prime Minister Mark Carney expressed solidarity with those affected, saying, "My thoughts are with everyone whose life has been upended by the wildfires in British Columbia."

## Spokane Fires Force 67,000 Evacuations

In Washington state, three wildfires burning in the Spokane area have [damaged or destroyed about 920 structures](https://apnews.com/article/wildfire-arson-spokane-evacuation-arrest-3738a2f795ca9cc0cca93eae76fb7ff0) and forced the evacuation of around 67,000 people since they erupted in early August. By Sunday, crews continued mop-up operations near containment lines, and none of the fires were growing.

Aaron F. Farinacci, 37, of Spokane, was arrested and [charged with first-degree arson](https://apnews.com/article/spokane-wildfires-arson-washington-old-trails-f23e7a45456dc166344f08fc09db4843) for intentionally setting the Old Trails Fire, the biggest of the three blazes. The other two fires were also started by people, though not necessarily intentionally. No deaths have been reported from the Spokane fires.

Farinacci served about a decade in prison after killing his father in Arizona in 2010 and had been questioned in two previous fire investigations in 2025. A witness who reported seeing him near where the fire started told authorities, "He was bent over in the brush, acting nervous. You get the feeling this guy's up to something."

Spokane County Sheriff John Nowels credited vigilant citizens for the quick apprehension: "As it happens in so many critical cases, it was citizens who were paying attention to their surroundings, being aware and willing to step up and say something that led to the relatively quick apprehension of Mr. Farinacci."

Washington is experiencing one of its worst wildfire seasons in more than three decades, with about 450,000 acres burned so far this summer. More than 7,000 fire personnel were fighting 16 uncontained wildfires across the state, burning more than 672 square miles.

## Fatalities in Utah and Oregon Firefighting Efforts

Two helicopter pilots were killed when their Sikorsky Skycrane [crashed while fighting a wildfire](https://apnews.com/article/wildfire-utah-helicopter-crash-a42a74bfa41c20a12313de6d5972ed23) in Utah's Fishlake National Forest on Friday. The pilots, employed by Helicopter Transport Services and contracted by the U.S. Forest Service, were [confirmed dead Saturday](https://apnews.com/article/wildfires-helicopter-crash-mt-rainier-utah-a8a570c9caf8357ee3c809e040a009b7) after crews were able to reach the crash site.

The crash ignited a new fire that spread to connect with the existing Widemouth 2 Fire, which has scorched nearly 174 square miles. The National Transportation Safety Board is investigating the cause of the crash.

In Oregon, Jason Ensign, 47, was killed while operating a bulldozer for a timber company fighting the Wrights Spring fire. The blaze grew rapidly and cut off Ensign's escape route.

## Climate Change Amplifying Fire Risk

A [World Weather Attribution study](https://www.worldweatherattribution.org/climate-change-canada-wildfires-2026/) released Aug. 6 found that human-caused climate change doubled the likelihood of the tinderbox weather conditions that sparked Canada's massive summer wildfires. In the Northwest Territories, the 7-day fire weather event observed in July had a return period of 2 years in today's climate, but would have been 50 years in a 1.4C cooler climate.

More than 3 million hectares have burned in Canada to date in 2026, prompting a Level 4 National Preparedness response. Approximately 94% of the total area burned occurred during the five weeks between the end of June and end of July. Over 120 million people in the Midwest and Northeast U.S. were affected by dangerous air quality from Canadian wildfire smoke.

Mike Flannigan, a professor of wildland fire at Thompson Rivers University, warned that conditions remain dangerous: "You walk through the forest, it crunches; if you look sideways, you start a fire." He added, "It looks grim... there's no rain. I'm scared for the coming weeks."

## What's Next

Southern BC and the Pacific Northwest have had little to no rain in the past six weeks, leaving the regions bone dry. Current conditions indicate the fire season could extend into the autumn. Fire management agencies in BC and Alberta are now fighting fires after dark using night-vision helicopters, a practice that would have been unthinkable just a few years ago.

With more than 100 new wildfires reported across 15 U.S. states over a single weekend, extraordinary pressure remains on national firefighting resources. As officials continue to assess damage and plan for re-entry into evacuated areas, the immediate focus remains on containing active fires and protecting communities still at risk.
