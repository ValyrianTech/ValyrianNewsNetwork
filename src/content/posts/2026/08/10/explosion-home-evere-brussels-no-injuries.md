# Explosion at Home in Evere, Brussels: No Injuries Reported

An explosion occurred at a home on Pater Damiaanstraat, near Evere station in Brussels, during the night from Sunday to Monday. Emergency services were called to the scene in the early hours of the morning, and while no injuries were reported, the blast caused damage to the front door and facade of the building, according to [BRUZZ](https://www.bruzz.be/actua/veiligheid/ontploffing-aan-woning-pater-damiaanstraat-evere-2026-08-10).

## The Incident

The Brussels fire brigade confirmed that they were dispatched to the residential street in the early hours of Monday morning. Upon arrival, crews found no fire at the scene, but significant damage to the property's entrance.

"We were called in the middle of the night. There appeared to be no fire. The cause of the explosion is not yet clear," the Brussels fire brigade stated, as reported by [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/08/10/ontploffing-aan-woning-in-pater-damiaanstraat-in-evere/).

While no injuries were reported, one person at the scene was treated for shock. "There were no injuries, but one person on site was in shock and was treated on site," the fire brigade added.

The cause of the explosion remains under investigation, and it is not yet known whether the Brussels prosecutor's office will open a formal inquiry.

## A Concerning Pattern

Monday's incident in Evere is the latest in a series of explosions at homes across Brussels that have occurred with alarming frequency in recent months. The circumstances — a nighttime blast, damage to a front door, and no fire at the scene — closely mirror numerous other incidents documented across the capital.

In July, an [explosion at a home on Piersstraat in Molenbeek](https://www.bruzz.be/actua/veiligheid/explosie-aan-woning-de-piersstraat-molenbeek-2026-07-09) shattered windows and damaged a front door around midnight. The prosecutor's office opened an investigation into that incident.

Earlier in June, two explosions occurred at front doors of buildings in Anderlecht within minutes of each other. As [BRUZZ reported](https://www.bruzz.be/actua/veiligheid/woonblok-peterboswijk-opgeschrikt-door-ontploffing-2026-06-09), "Brussels has been increasingly shaken by explosions, usually at night," with security services often suspecting a link to the drug milieu.

A similar explosion in [Ganshoren in early June](https://www.bruzz.be/actua/veiligheid/explosie-aan-woning-ganshoren-2026-06-03) damaged the door of a building, prompting the prosecutor's office to order a forensic investigation. And in May, an [explosion at a home in Etterbeek](https://www.bruzz.be/actua/veiligheid/ontploffing-aan-woning-etterbeek-geen-gewonden-2026-05-27) led the Brussels prosecutor's office to open an investigation, with the police lab and DOVO bomb disposal unit dispatched to the scene.

This pattern extends well beyond the past few months. Since December 2025, Brussels has recorded at least ten explosions or explosive incidents targeting homes, building entrances, and even a nightclub. The incidents span multiple municipalities — Molenbeek, Anderlecht, Ganshoren, Etterbeek, Sint-Gillis, Schaarbeek, and now Evere — suggesting a citywide phenomenon rather than isolated events.

## Criminal Connections

In several of these cases, authorities have explicitly linked the explosions to criminal activity. In May, the Brussels prosecutor's office described an explosion in Molenbeek as being "of criminal nature," opening an investigation for intentional destruction by explosion. According to [BRUZZ](https://www.bruzz.be/actua/samenleving/parket-noemt-ontploffing-molenbeek-van-criminele-aard-2026-05-19), a local merchant speculated that the blast was a settling of scores in the drug milieu, though police could not confirm this at the time.

The pattern is well-documented: most explosions occur at night, typically between midnight and 4 AM; they target front doors of homes or building entrances; and while injuries are rare, property damage is often significant. In many cases, the fire brigade finds no fire at the scene, and the prosecutor's office opens investigations with the support of bomb disposal units and forensic labs.

Security services have repeatedly noted the potential connection between these explosions and drug-related crime in Brussels. The targeting of residential front doors — often in the middle of the night — has become a recurring tactic associated with intimidation and score-settling in the drug milieu, though authorities have been cautious about drawing definitive conclusions in individual cases without sufficient evidence.

## What's Next

As authorities work to determine the cause of Monday's explosion in Evere, the incident raises renewed questions about safety in Brussels residential neighborhoods. Whether the prosecutor's office will open a formal investigation — as it has in numerous similar cases — remains to be seen.

Residents in the area will be watching closely for answers, particularly given the frequency with which such incidents have occurred across the capital in recent months. The explosion in Evere serves as a stark reminder of a troubling trend that shows no signs of abating.
