# Micro-Film Exposes Telecom Fraud Targeting Job Seekers

A new micro-film released by [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/legal/20260810/38143a0bc13b427b93b85f4e697c48bd/c.html) on Monday exposes the deceptive tactics used in telecom fraud schemes that lure Chinese citizens with promises of high overseas salaries. Titled "He Wants to Marry Me" (《他要娶我》), the film is designed to raise public awareness and protect potential victims from falling for these increasingly sophisticated scams.

## A Story of Deception at the Border

The film is set in Menglian County, Yunnan Province, near the China-Myanmar border. It follows an immigration management police officer who encounters his ex-girlfriend during a nighttime border patrol. Six years earlier, she had ended their relationship because he was perpetually stationed at the border. Now, she has been lured by a "high overseas salary plus romance" compound fraud scheme and is on her way to meet a "boyfriend" who promised to marry her.

The officer shows her an old notebook she left behind when they broke up, containing 73 names—each representing a person deceived by telecom fraud tactics. Faced with the same greeting patterns and script templates she is currently experiencing, she hesitates. Using real lessons from fraud victims, the officer successfully persuades her to turn back.

"This story, adapted from real cases, please share with people around you who want to go overseas to 'pan for gold'—you can save one person at a time," the article accompanying the film urges.

## The Growing Threat of "Overseas High Salary" Scams

The micro-film addresses a serious and ongoing issue in China: telecom fraud schemes that promise lucrative overseas employment, often targeting those seeking to "pan for gold" abroad. These scams typically operate through social media platforms, advertising high-paying jobs in Southeast Asian countries such as Myanmar, Cambodia, and Laos.

Victims are often lured with promises of high salaries, free travel, and accommodation, only to be trafficked into telecom fraud compounds. The [Chinese Embassy in Myanmar](https://m.cyol.com/gb/articles/2025-01/11/content_YO2BJ2Tm96.html) has repeatedly warned citizens about such "overseas high salary recruitment" scams, urging them not to believe such information and to exercise caution when encountering suspicious job offers.

The film highlights a specific type of compound fraud that combines the lure of high overseas salaries with romance or friendship scams. This pattern was documented in a separate case [reported by Xinhua](http://fj.news.cn/20250617/48ead10bfe1742a4afbe0269164bb540/c.html) in June 2025, in which an overseas romance and investment fraud ring was uncovered with total fraud amounts reaching 180 million yuan, with individual victims losing up to 5 million yuan.

## A National Crackdown on Fraud

The release of the micro-film comes amid a broader national effort to combat telecom fraud. In June 2026, the Central Propaganda Department and the Ministry of Public Security [jointly launched](https://www.xinhuanet.com/20260610/801ffc250a72411b922c6b492805b8c9/c.html) the "National Anti-Fraud Action" concentrated publicity month campaign under the theme "Don't listen, don't believe, don't be greedy—build an anti-fraud 'mental defense line'" (不听不信不贪恋，构筑反诈'心'防线).

The campaign aims to increase public awareness of fraud schemes and improve citizens' ability to identify scams, according to [China News Service](https://www.chinanews.com.cn/sh/2026/06-10/10637765.shtml).

The scale of the problem is significant. In 2025, Chinese public security authorities solved 258,000 telecom fraud cases. Through international law enforcement cooperation, 58,000 fraud suspects were transferred from overseas to China. The "Four Families" criminal groups in northern Myanmar were completely eliminated, and major cross-border gambling and fraud group leaders were brought back to China. Large-scale gambling and fraud parks in Myanmar's Myawaddy and key areas of Cambodia were cleared.

Authorities also intercepted 3.6 billion scam phone calls and 3.3 billion scam text messages, and froze 217.07 billion yuan in fraud-related funds. More than 6.7 million people were personally warned and prevented from being defrauded, avoiding 35 billion yuan in losses.

## Menglian: A Frontline in the Fight Against Fraud

Menglian County, where the micro-film is set, has been a key location for cross-border law enforcement operations. The Menglian port has been used for the transfer of hundreds of telecom fraud suspects from Myanmar to Chinese authorities. In August 2024, 307 telecom fraud suspects from the Wa State region of northern Myanmar were transferred through the port, and in June 2026, 78 individuals were transferred through the Meng'a port in the county.

## A Human Approach to Prevention

The micro-film's emotional narrative—centered on a personal relationship strained by border duty and a woman nearly deceived by false promises—reflects the human cost of these fraud schemes. By telling the story through the lens of a border police officer who has witnessed the devastating consequences of fraud firsthand, the film aims to resonate with viewers on a personal level.

The message is clear: the fight against telecom fraud is not just about law enforcement crackdowns, but also about protecting individuals from falling victim to increasingly sophisticated deception. As the article accompanying the film states, sharing this story could "save one person at a time."

## What to Watch For

As China continues its national anti-fraud campaign, authorities are expected to intensify both enforcement and public awareness efforts. The micro-film represents a creative approach to fraud prevention, using storytelling to reach audiences that traditional warnings may not. Observers will be watching to see whether such public awareness initiatives translate into reduced fraud victimization rates in the coming months.
