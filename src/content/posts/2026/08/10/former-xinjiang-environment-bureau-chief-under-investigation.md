# Former Xinjiang Environment Bureau Chief Under Investigation

Wu Bin, former Party Secretary and Director of the Ecological Environment Bureau of the Xinjiang Production and Construction Corps, is under investigation for suspected serious violations of discipline and law, according to an announcement published by [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/legal/20260810/13295e8c6ce34762ad91a13b8c29c6fe/c.html) on August 10, 2026. The case is being handled by the Xinjiang Production and Construction Corps Commission for Discipline Inspection.

## Background of the Investigation

The announcement, released on the Central Commission for Discipline Inspection (CCDI) website, states that Wu Bin is currently undergoing disciplinary review and supervisory investigation. The standard formulation of "suspected serious violations of discipline and law" is typically used in Chinese anti-corruption announcements before specific charges are detailed.

Wu Bin served as Party Secretary and Director of the Ecological Environment Bureau of the Xinjiang Production and Construction Corps. He was still serving in this capacity as of December 2025, when he commented on the Corps' Party Committee Economic Work Conference, discussing environmental protection priorities including the construction of beautiful blue skies, rivers and lakes, and villages, as well as battles for blue skies, clear water, and clean soil, as [reported](https://www.7rts.com/c228/content_2025456.html) by the Corps Daily.

## Connection to Party Inspection

The investigation follows a Party inspection of the Ecological Environment Bureau conducted from November 11, 2024 to January 15, 2025. The inspection feedback, delivered on March 3, 2025, identified several problems including "inadequate implementation of the main responsibility for comprehensive Party governance" and "insufficient promotion of Party conduct, integrity, and anti-corruption in the ecological environment system," according to the [inspection feedback report](https://sthjj.xjbt.gov.cn/c/2025-03-04/8387061.shtml) published on the Bureau's website.

The inspection team also noted it had "received clues about problems involving some leading cadres" and transferred them to relevant disciplinary inspection and supervision organs for handling. Wu Bin chaired the feedback meeting and pledged to "seriously study and understand, fully accept the problems, sincerely accept the opinions, and effectively implement rectification."

## Broader Anti-Corruption Campaign in Xinjiang

Wu Bin's investigation is part of a broader anti-corruption campaign targeting officials in the Xinjiang Production and Construction Corps and the wider Xinjiang region. In 2026 alone, several Corps officials have been investigated:

- **January 8**: Li Xu, Member of the Corps Party Committee Standing Committee and Deputy Commander, was investigated by the CCDI, as [documented](https://m.dzplus.dzng.com/share/general/0/NEWS3053719QQOZXQCLUAFRY) in a Dazhong News anti-corruption report.
- **January 22**: Ruan Boping, former first-level inspector of the Second Division, was investigated by the Corps Commission for Discipline Inspection, according to [CE.cn](http://district.ce.cn/newarea/sddy/202601/t20260122_2720609.shtml).
- **April 24**: Xia Jianguo, former Party Secretary and Chairman of the Xinjiang Corps Construction Engineering (Group), was investigated.

These cases follow the April 3 investigation of Ma Xingrui, a member of the CCP Central Committee Politburo and former Xinjiang Party Secretary. In the period around his fall, at least eight Xinjiang officials were investigated, as [reported](https://www.chineseheadlinenews.com/20260428657681.html) by Chinese Headline News.

## Ongoing Inspection Efforts

The Corps has continued to strengthen its inspection and anti-corruption efforts. On April 9, 2026, the Corps held a meeting to deploy its ninth round of inspections, which includes "look-back" reviews of five government departments, one division-city, 13 regimental farms, and three development zones, as [reported](https://bt.news.cn/20260410/1900b9eaf1f34889b72f589a42d3e2c0/c.html) by Xinhua News' Bingtuan channel. The meeting emphasized the need to "continuously enhance the deterrent, penetrating, and driving force of inspection supervision."

## Analysis and Implications

The investigation of Wu Bin appears directly connected to findings from the 2024-2025 Party inspection of the Ecological Environment Bureau. The inspection's explicit identification of inadequate anti-corruption work within the ecological environment system, combined with the transfer of leads about leading cadres to disciplinary authorities, suggests the investigation may have been in progress for some time.

The case also signals continued anti-corruption enforcement within the Xinjiang Production and Construction Corps, a quasi-military organization that has been a focus of anti-corruption efforts throughout 2026. The Corps has also faced international scrutiny, with the US government sanctioning the organization and several of its officials in July 2020.

## What to Watch For

The official announcement provides minimal details about the specific allegations against Wu Bin. As the investigation progresses, further details may emerge about the nature of the alleged violations. The case could also lead to additional probes within the ecological and environmental sector in Xinjiang, given the inspection's findings about systemic issues in anti-corruption work within the Bureau.

Observers will be watching whether Wu Bin's case leads to further investigations of other officials in the Corps' environmental protection system, and whether it signals a broader crackdown on the ecological sector in China's western regions.
