# Ocasio-Cortez Freezes Eggs, Eyes 2028 Presidential Run

Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez (D-NY) announced this weekend that she is freezing her eggs, a deeply personal decision she says is partly driven by the political environment under the Trump administration. The 36-year-old progressive leader made the announcement through a series of Instagram Stories videos on August 8, documenting the beginning of the fertility preservation process, and elaborated on her decision during an appearance on ABC News' "This Week" the following day.

"This is a choice that I am making to feel more in control of my life," Ocasio-Cortez said in her Instagram announcement, according to [AP News](https://apnews.com/article/ocasiocortez-freezes-eggs-aoc-2028-7c6eb0517ac7ec2151f6822fd444bb0a). "And if people want to make assumptions about my decision, let them make those assumptions. I feel empowered to make this choice."

## Political Context and 2028 Speculation

The announcement comes at a pivotal moment for the New York congresswoman, who has emerged as a leading figure in the Democratic Party's progressive wing. A [University of New Hampshire poll](https://www.fosters.com/story/news/politics/2026/07/22/aoc-vance-lead-2028-primary-new-hampshire-poll/91010048007/) released July 22 showed Ocasio-Cortez leading the potential 2028 Democratic presidential field in New Hampshire at 22% support, narrowly edging out former Transportation Secretary Pete Buttigieg at 21%. The poll marked the first time she had led the field since the UNH Survey Center began tracking the race in October 2025.

During her [ABC News interview](https://abcnews.com/Politics/rep-alexandria-ocasio-cortez-opens-decision-freeze-eggs/story?id=135496654) on "This Week," Ocasio-Cortez said she has not ruled out running for president in 2028 or challenging Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer for his New York Senate seat.

"I haven't ruled out the possibility, and I'm very humbled to the tremendous amount of support out there," she told host Jonathan Karl. When asked about a potential Senate challenge to Schumer, she said, "Anything is possible at this juncture."

The congresswoman, who was first elected to the House at age 29, has been the subject of intense speculation about her political future. Vice President JD Vance said in June that he believed Ocasio-Cortez would be the leading Democratic candidate for 2028, a comment that drew significant attention.

## Reproductive Rights and the Trump Administration

Ocasio-Cortez framed her decision to go public with her fertility journey as both a personal choice and a political statement. She said the Trump administration's policies on reproductive care played a role in her decision to speak openly about the process.

"In this political environment where this administration is denying reproductive care to women across the country, from abortion rights to the ability to carry out a healthy pregnancy, I think it's important for us as leaders to have these conversations and share these processes, especially for working women across the country and normalize them," she said on "This Week."

She went further in her Instagram posts, arguing that the administration has made pregnancy more dangerous for American women. "Frankly, this administration has made pregnancy more dangerous than probably at any point in the last 30-plus years," she said.

Ocasio-Cortez, who sponsored a Democratic bill in 2022 to codify abortion rights into federal law after the Supreme Court struck down Roe v. Wade, has been a vocal critic of the administration's reproductive health policies. She also noted the gender double standards women face in politics, saying, "Men run for office, or frankly they interview for jobs, and people don't think in the back of their minds, 'OK, this guy's running for office, how old is he? Oh, well, is he going to want to start a family?'"

## Personal Journey and Public Reactions

The congresswoman, who has been engaged to Riley Roberts since May 2022, documented the process of taking hormone injections, including in the green room before her ABC interview. She said she had been saving for the procedure for "a very long time" and had been weighing the decision for years.

"I got sworn into the House of Representatives when I was 29 years old," she reflected in her Instagram posts. "There's this flip side where you have to then grow up in the public eye in a time that most people usually get to grow more privately."

The announcement drew praise from progressive figures and organizations. Amanda Litman, co-founder and president of Run for Something, wrote on X: "I'm just so grateful for her honesty & vulnerability." Democratic strategist Xochitl Hinojosa echoed that sentiment, saying, "We don't talk about women freezing their eggs or what women have to go through to start a family. Thank you @AOC for your bravery."

Conservative commentators were more critical. Andrew Kolvet, a Turning Point USA spokesman, argued on X that the announcement reflected what he described as a broader progressive message prioritizing careers over family formation, while MAGA influencer Laura Loomer dismissed the decision, according to [The Daily Beast](https://www.thedailybeast.com/maga-melts-down-over-congresswomans-family-planning/).

## Democratic Party Dynamics

Ocasio-Cortez's announcement comes amid a broader shift within the Democratic Party, as progressive candidates have made significant inroads in recent primaries. The August 4-5 Michigan Senate primary victory of progressive Abdul El-Sayed over a Schumer-backed candidate was seen as a significant moment for the party's left flank.

"I think that we are in a time of renewal for the Democratic Party," Ocasio-Cortez said on "This Week." "We have really an ascendance of a young, inspiring, exciting electorate, energizing wave of candidates that I think really inject a new energy to the Democratic Party."

She also pushed back against what she described as a reductive media understanding of political ambition, saying, "They assume that my ambition is a title or a seat. And my ambition is way bigger than that. My ambition is to change this country."

## What's Next

As Ocasio-Cortez continues her fertility treatment and weighs her political options, her announcement has added a deeply personal dimension to the ongoing speculation about her future. With the 2028 presidential election more than two years away and the midterm elections approaching this November, the congresswoman said she remains focused on the immediate task at hand.

"I think that we have a responsibility to remain focused on the midterms and not on our individual aspirations or plans," she said, according to [USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/politics/2026/08/09/aoc-2028-presidential-run-egg-freezing/91236093007/).

Whether she ultimately pursues the presidency, challenges Schumer, or charts a different course, Ocasio-Cortez's decision to share her fertility journey has already sparked a national conversation about women's health, reproductive rights, and the personal sacrifices that come with public service.
