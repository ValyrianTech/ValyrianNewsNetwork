# Dolly Parton, Iconic Singer and Philanthropist, Dies at 80

Dolly Parton, the country music icon whose soaring vocals, poignant songwriting and sparkling costumes defined her rise from a log cabin in the Tennessee mountains to the height of global stardom, has died at age 80. Her publicist, Marcel Pariseau, confirmed that Parton died Tuesday at the Vanderbilt-Ingram Cancer Center in Nashville after what he called "a brief battle with cancer."

"After bravely facing a brief battle with cancer, Dolly departed her Earthly life today at the Vanderbilt-Ingram Cancer Center surrounded by loved ones," Pariseau said in a statement, as [AP News](https://apnews.com/article/dolly-parton-death-country-icon-87156f3e6a1547b88bf414529b644ad3) reported. She was admitted to the cancer center on Friday, August 21, and died four days later.

## A Life From the Smoky Mountains to the World Stage

Born on January 19, 1946, in Locust Ridge, Tennessee, Parton was the fourth of 12 children in a family that lived in a one-room cabin in the Smoky Mountains. Her father, a tobacco farmer, paid the missionary who delivered her with a sack of cornmeal. Music was woven into her earliest memories — she began singing in church at age six, where her grandfather was the pastor, and by age 13 she was performing at the Grand Ole Opry in Nashville, introduced by Johnny Cash.

Following her uncle Bill Owens to Nashville after graduating high school in 1964, Parton's partnership with country star Porter Wagoner proved pivotal to her career. Their first duet, "The Last Thing on My Mind," was released in 1967, and Wagoner advocated for her to secure a record deal at RCA. Her first No. 1 solo single, "Joshua," arrived in 1971.

Parton wrote more than 3,000 songs, including timeless classics like "Jolene," "Coat of Many Colors" and "I Will Always Love You." She sold more than 100 million records worldwide and amassed more than 1 billion online streams. Her 10 Grammy Awards from 55 nominations made her the third-most nominated woman in Grammy history, behind only Beyoncé and Taylor Swift, as [BBC News](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cz9xdl4g25zo) noted.

## From "9 to 5" to Dollywood

Parton's crossover appeal extended far beyond country music. Her starring role in the 1980 film "9 to 5" alongside Jane Fonda and Lily Tomlin showcased her natural screen presence, and the title song became a No. 1 pop hit. She also earned acclaim for roles in "The Best Little Whorehouse in Texas" and "Steel Magnolias."

As a businesswoman, Parton built an empire that included the Dollywood theme park in East Tennessee, cosmetics, pet apparel and a truck stop. Her estimated net worth was $450 million, and she donated approximately $600 million to various charities throughout her lifetime. She was listed by Forbes as one of America's richest self-made women.

Her philanthropy was as legendary as her music. In 1999, she launched the Imagination Library to send free books to children, inspired by her father who struggled to learn to read. The program now distributes millions of books each year across the US, Canada, Australia, the UK and Ireland. In 2020, she donated $1 million to Vanderbilt University Medical Centre, which helped fund the Moderna COVID-19 vaccine.

## Tributes From Around the World

The White House lowered its flag to half-mast in Parton's honor, and US flags were flown at half-staff around the Washington Monument. Tributes poured in from across the entertainment world and beyond.

Miley Cyrus, Parton's goddaughter who appeared alongside her on "Hannah Montana," paid an emotional tribute, writing on Instagram: "The feeling of losing my Aunt Dolly is beyond what feels right to share. Our most sacred moments were private and they'll stay there behind the stage, beneath the glamour and between our hearts." Cyrus added, "I have an angel by my side. I always have," as [Variety](https://variety.com/2026/music/news/miley-cyrus-dolly-parton-tribute-1236842817/) reported.

Taylor Swift, who began her career in country music in Nashville, said in a statement: "A world without Dolly doesn't feel possible, real, or right. But because of the eternally generous way she spent her time on this earth and the countless lives she changed for the better, her legacy will be an everlasting one." Swift described Parton as "true grace and true grit, all in one dazzling person," according to [Variety](https://variety.com/2026/music/news/taylor-swift-dolly-parton-tribute-dead-1236842794/).

Jane Fonda, Parton's "9 to 5" co-star, wrote on Instagram: "More than anyone I have ever known, Dolly will always be with us because she was so present, so powerful, so generous and playful that she is woven into all our lives and hearts forever." Celine Dion called Parton "pure light, with a kind heart and a generous spirit," while Reba McEntire thanked her for "all the wonderful things you did for us here on earth."

Billy Ray Cyrus, who toured with Parton in the 1990s, recalled: "Like every little boy, I grew up loving Dolly Parton. She was a part of our lives, on our TV's, right in our living rooms." He shared a memory of apologizing to Parton for tabloid rumors about an affair, to which she replied with a wink: "Honey, this trash sells records!"

## Family's Final Words

Parton's death was announced in an emotional video posted to her Instagram page by her nephew Bryan Seaver, who served as her head of security for more than two decades. In the family statement, Seaver said: "Dolly opened doors, not just for me, but for her entire family to reach the potential we didn't even know was possible. Dolly also opened doors for generations of singers, songwriters, musicians, and dreamers from every walk of life and across every continent," as [Yahoo News UK](https://uk.news.yahoo.com/dolly-parton-family-statement-full-182508310.html) reported.

"Dolly was a farmer, just like her Daddy. He farmed the soil; she farmed songs and happiness," Seaver said. He revealed Parton's final wish: "Dolly called me and said she wants to rest in a beautiful bed of plush cotton because after a life wearing heavy rhinestones, she finally deserves to be comfortable and have some rest."

Her funeral will be small and private, per her wishes.

## A Legacy That Endures

Parton's appeal transcended generations, geography and politics. She was a feminist role model who held the reins of her own career, writing her own songs, owning her content and managing her finances in an entertainment world dominated by men. She was also a beloved figure in the LGBTQ community and spanned social and political divides with her famous refrain: "I don't do politics. I have too many fans on both sides of the fence."

Her final interview with People magazine, published August 21, found her characteristically resilient: "I am dealing with some health issues that I just didn't pay attention to when I was watching over Carl," she said, referring to her husband Carl Dean, who died in March 2025 after nearly 60 years of marriage. "Seriously though, even though I'm still healing, I'm still working."

The Broadway musical "DOLLY: A True Original Musical" is still set to open on January 19, 2027, which would have been her 81st birthday. As the world mourns the loss of one of its most beloved entertainers, Parton's songs, her generosity and her indomitable spirit ensure she will always be remembered.

As her nephew said in the family's announcement: "She never saw a door she couldn't open, and the doors she opened made and changed history."
