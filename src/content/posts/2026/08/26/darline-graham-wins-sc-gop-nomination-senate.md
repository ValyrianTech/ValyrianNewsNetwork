# Darline Graham Wins SC GOP Nomination for Senate Seat

U.S. Sen. Darline Graham won the South Carolina Republican nomination for a full Senate term on Tuesday, defeating U.S. Rep. Ralph Norman in a special primary runoff and delivering another victory for President Donald Trump, who threw his support behind the political newcomer. With 99% of expected votes counted, Graham received 193,396 votes (52.5%) to Norman's 175,327 votes (47.5%), according to [NBC News election results](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/2026-primary-special-elections/south-carolina-senate-runoff-results).

The victory sets up a November general election matchup against Democratic nominee Annie Andrews, a pediatrician from Charleston, in a state that has not elected a Democrat to the Senate since 1998. The winner will make history as the first woman elected to the U.S. Senate from South Carolina.

## A Seat Born of Tragedy

The special election was triggered by the sudden death of Graham's brother, Sen. Lindsey Graham, on July 11 at age 71. The D.C. Medical Examiner's preliminary findings attributed his death to an aortic dissection caused by arteriosclerotic cardiovascular disease, as [TIME reported](https://time.com/article/2026/07/13/lindsey-graham-death-aortic-dissection/). Lindsey Graham had served in the Senate for 23 years and was known as a foreign policy hawk and close ally of Trump.

Gov. Henry McMaster appointed Darline Graham to fill her brother's seat on July 13, and she was sworn in the following day, becoming the first woman to represent South Carolina in the Senate, according to [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darline_Graham). Trump endorsed her before she formally entered the race and campaigned for her at a Myrtle Beach rally on August 21.

## Trump's High-Stakes Bet

The race was one of Trump's biggest political bets of the midterm primary cycle. He staked his political capital on a political newcomer over more experienced party establishment figures, and his super PAC MAGA Inc. spent nearly $830,000 on voter calls and text messages supporting Graham in the final days of the runoff, as [The Jerusalem Post reported](https://www.jpost.com/american-politics/article-906598).

"I'm doing this to honor Lindsey. That's why I'm here," Trump said at the Myrtle Beach rally. "So, Darline, we got to win that race."

Graham, a career public administrator who led the South Carolina Commission for the Blind from 2019 to 2026, had never held elected office before her appointment. She embraced her outsider status throughout the campaign.

"The critics say I shouldn't run for the Senate. They say I'm not a politician. You know what I say? Guilty as charged," she said at the rally, according to [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/08/25/nx-s1-5943041/south-carolina-senate-runoff-graham-norman-trump-endorsement). "By the way, President Trump has never served in elected office before and I think he's done a damn good job."

## Norman's Experience Argument

Norman, a five-term congressman and member of the conservative House Freedom Caucus, campaigned on his Washington experience. He received endorsements from Sens. Rick Scott of Florida and Mike Lee of Utah, as well as former South Carolina Gov. Nikki Haley and Turning Point Action.

During the only runoff debate, Graham stumbled when asked whether the U.S. has a national security interest in Taiwan and the South China Sea — a signature issue of her late brother. She admitted, "National security is not my thing. I'm not that informed on national security."

Norman seized on the moment, arguing she was unqualified for the office. "This isn't a 'get training on the job.' You can't do that," he said during the debate, as [Newsmax reported](https://www.newsmax.com/politics/darline-graham-donald-trump-south-carolina/2026/08/25/id/1267323/).

After the results came in, Norman expressed frustration with Trump's involvement. "I never understood why he got in this race," Norman said. "It is what it is and we accept it."

## What's Next

Graham will face Annie Andrews in November in a state where Republicans hold a commanding advantage. When Lindsey Graham last ran in 2020, he defeated his Democratic opponent by a 10-percentage-point margin in the most expensive Senate race in state history.

Graham's victory speech struck an emotional tone, acknowledging the personal toll of the past few weeks. "The past few weeks have been an unbelievably challenging time for our family, but you know what? We did it," she said, according to [AP News](https://apnews.com/article/south-carolina-election-lindsey-graham-03899983c3702842039e99d62e16ddad).

The race also served as a test of Trump's influence ahead of the November midterms. While Graham's victory preserved Trump's perfect record in Senate Republican primaries this year, the narrower-than-expected margin — 52.5% to 47.5% — suggests the president's endorsement has limits even in a deeply Republican state.

As [The Guardian noted](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/aug/25/south-carolina-senate-runoff-election), Trump made explicit at the rally that he views the race as a test of whether his supporters will turn out when he's not on the ballot. "The problem that we have is, they say that Trump does great when he's on the ballot, but when he's not on the ballot, his people don't come," he said. "So what I really want you to do is pretend, please, that I'm on the ballot. Just come and vote."

All eyes now turn to November, where Graham's inexperience on national security issues and her close alignment with Trump will be tested against a Democratic challenger in a state that has been reliably red for nearly three decades.
