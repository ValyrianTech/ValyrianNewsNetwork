# Typhoon Shadel Barrels Toward China's Zhejiang, Fujian Coasts

Typhoon Shadel, the 18th named storm of the 2026 Pacific typhoon season, is bearing down on China's eastern coast, with authorities issuing an orange typhoon warning and activating emergency response protocols across Zhejiang and Fujian provinces. The storm, classified as a strong typhoon with maximum sustained winds of 42 meters per second near its center, is expected to make landfall between Xiangshan in Zhejiang and Fuqing in Fujian on the night of August 27 through the morning of August 28, according to the [Central Meteorological Observatory](https://www.news.cn/politics/20260826/7a63bde6cec44699bf6707fa9f4bfd69/c.html).

## An Unusually Active Typhoon Season

Shadel's approach comes amid one of the most active typhoon seasons in recent memory, with multiple storms simultaneously churning across the Western Pacific. The storm formed on August 19 in the northwest Pacific and rapidly intensified, reaching strong typhoon status as it moved toward China's coast. Its path bears striking similarities to Typhoons Bavi and Baiduhai from earlier this season—all were far-ocean-generated storms that reached super typhoon strength with long lifecycles, as noted by [Hangzhou News](https://hznews.hangzhou.com.cn/chengshi/content/2026-08/25/content_9288675.htm).

The 2026 season has already produced a remarkable "four typhoons dancing together" event on August 24, when Shadel, Jianlavei, Zitan, and Aishani were all active simultaneously. Typhoon Aishani was subsequently absorbed by Shadel, providing additional moisture that could intensify rainfall during landfall.

## Storm Path and Forecast

As of the early morning of August 26, Shadel's center was located approximately 720 kilometers east of the Zhejiang-Fujian border in the East China Sea, moving west-northwest at about 15 kilometers per hour. The storm is expected to turn west-southwest on the morning of August 27 and slow down as it approaches the coast, with landfall projected at typhoon or strong typhoon intensity (35-42 m/s, 12-14 levels), according to the [China Daily report](https://cn.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202608/26/WS6a8e2318e4b09a165c785fce.html).

"At present, the landfall location is mainly considered to be in the Zhejiang-Fujian coastal area," said Wang Haiping, chief forecaster at the Central Meteorological Observatory. "After landfall, it will continue moving westward. Shadel will mainly affect East China to South China, and is not expected to move directly northward or affect northern China."

## Emergency Response and Preparedness

Both provinces have activated significant emergency response measures. Zhejiang upgraded its typhoon emergency response to Level III on August 25, while Fujian activated Level III flood response and Level IV typhoon response. The national government had earlier activated a Level IV response for Fujian on August 22, as reported by [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/politics/20260822/4d107375781849d59b85a624e820f93c/c.html).

The Zhejiang Provincial Flood Control and Drought Relief Headquarters has called for all localities to "closely monitor the typhoon's development, efficiently operate the '1833' joint command system, and further improve monitoring, forecasting, risk assessment, personnel evacuation, and public awareness campaigns."

Maritime authorities have taken extensive precautions. The Zhejiang Maritime Safety Administration activated a Level III typhoon response on August 24, with more than 90 passenger ferry routes suspended along the province's coast. In Ningbo, 39 offshore construction projects have been halted, and workers on key infrastructure projects including the Tongsu-Jiayong High-Speed Railway's Hangzhou Bay sea-crossing bridge have been evacuated to shore, according to [Lianhe Zaobao](https://www.zaobao.com.sg/news/china/story20260824-9567386).

## Transportation Disruptions

The Yangtze River Delta Railway announced temporary suspensions of trains on the Hangzhou-Wenzhou High-Speed Railway, Hangzhou-Taizhou High-Speed Railway, Jinhua-Wenzhou Railway, Jinhua-Taizhou Railway, and Ningbo-Jinhua Railway for August 27-28, and the Hangzhou-Shenzhen Railway for August 27-29, as reported by [Huanqiu](https://3w.huanqiu.com/a/24d596/4Swxl7xoJ2L?agt=8). Passengers with tickets for affected trains can receive full refunds within 30 days of the scheduled departure date.

## Heavy Rainfall and Flood Risk

The Zhejiang Provincial Meteorological Observatory has warned of heavy to torrential rain across coastal and southern Zhejiang on August 27-28, with some areas in Wenzhou, Taizhou, Lishui, and central-southern Ningbo expected to experience extreme rainstorms. Forecasts indicate the East China Sea will see winds of 13-14 levels near the typhoon center, with gusts reaching 15-16 levels, according to the [Jimu News report](https://m.163.com/dy/article/L58N2U6S053469LG.html).

## Path Uncertainty Remains

Despite improved forecasting, meteorologists note that Shadel's exact landfall location remains uncertain. The storm's trajectory depends significantly on the behavior of the subtropical high pressure system. If cold air from the north pushes the subtropical high eastward, Shadel could be steered away from China; however, if the high maintains its current belt-like form, it will block northward movement and steer the storm directly toward China's eastern coast, as explained by [Guangzhou Bendibao](https://m.gz.bendibao.com/news/371553.html).

## What to Watch For

With landfall expected within 48 hours, the focus now shifts to the storm's final track and intensity. The key concerns are extreme rainfall triggering flooding and landslides in mountainous coastal areas, and the cumulative impact of multiple typhoons hitting the region in rapid succession. Residents in the projected landfall zone should monitor official warnings closely and follow evacuation orders as authorities work to protect lives and property across this densely populated and economically vital stretch of China's coastline.
