# Car Thefts Persist at Franco-Belgian Border Hotspot

The Franco-Belgian border region remains a persistent hotspot for car theft, with approximately 700 vehicles stolen between August 1, 2025 and May 12, 2026 in the Mons and Tournai judicial districts, according to Vincent Macq, King's Prosecutor. The Wallonie Picarde area — particularly the communes of Tournai, Estaimpuis, and Mouscron — concentrates the highest number of thefts, as [RTBF reported](https://www.rtbf.be/article/vols-de-voitures-la-frontiere-franco-belge-continue-d-attirer-les-voleurs-11775643).

## Record Rates in Border Communities

The statistics are stark. Estaimpuis recorded a rate of 48.6 thefts per 10,000 inhabitants in 2025 — nearly ten times the Belgian national average. Mouscron holds the record for the sheer number of incidents, with 135 vehicles stolen near the border, as [L'Avenir detailed](https://www.lavenir.net/regions/wallonie-picarde/tournai/2026/08/23/vols-de-voitures-frontalieres-un-record-en-un-an-a-mouscron-estaimpuis-et-tournai-WHXW5YJXANCTTGIM7CJXUOMCJY/).

Prosecutor Macq described the situation as "of an intensity never seen before," a characterization echoed in [Le Vif's investigation](https://www.levif.be/societe/mobilite/auto/cest-dune-intensite-jamais-vue-pourquoi-les-vols-de-voitures-atteignent-des-sommets-dans-cette-region/). Yet Commissioner Magali Delannoy of the Mouscron police offers a more measured perspective, noting "a certain stability over the last ten years" in her zone. She attributes the heightened perception of the problem partly to increased visibility of thefts through social media.

## Organized Crime and Opportunity
Theft

Commissioner Delannoy estimates that approximately 50% of thefts are committed by organized criminal networks, with the remainder classified as "opportunity thefts." Vehicles stolen by organized groups are either resold or dismantled for parts to feed a parallel market of spare parts. "We discover abandoned parts, vehicles that have been stripped," Delannoy confirmed.

The sophistication of modern car theft is striking. According to [France Info](https://www.ici.fr/hauts-de-france/nord-59/le-nord-parmi-les-departements-les-plus-touches-par-les-vols-de-voitures-4205031), 70% of vehicles are stolen without visible break-in, using sophisticated electronic devices — key signal copying, remote hacking, and jammers. Some thieves need only two minutes to start a car. Orders are increasingly placed via social media, with thieves selecting targets based on buyer criteria.

## A Border That Complicates Police Work

The international border itself remains a significant obstacle for law enforcement. "The fact that there's a border does complicate police work a bit," Delannoy acknowledged. Administrative burdens still slow exchanges with French counterparts despite numerous initiatives in recent years. The proximity of Lille, one of France's most crime-prone cities, partly explains the phenomenon.

The scale of the problem extends across the border. The Nord department is the second most affected by car thefts in France, representing 6% of national thefts with over 3,300 vehicles stolen in 2025. Nationally, 44,104 cars were stolen in France in 2025, down 6% from the previous year. The most targeted models include the Toyota RAV4, Hyundai Tucson, Toyota CH-R, Renault Mégane, and Peugeot 508/5008.

## Cross-Border Enforcement Efforts

Authorities have not been idle. On January 27, 2026, gendarmes from Valenciennes dismantled a major stolen vehicle trafficking network, arresting nine people — including one minor — suspected of nearly 70 car thefts across the Nord, Pas-de-Calais, and Belgium. The damage was estimated at €1.45 million, with vehicles, mainly SUVs, sent to Germany via Belgium, as [France Info reported](https://www.ici.fr/hauts-de-france/nord-59/un-trafic-de-voitures-volees-entre-la-france-et-la-belgique-demantele-par-les-gendarmes-de-valenciennes-4143645).

On June 11, 2026, a major cross-border police operation called "FUTURE" was conducted from De Panne to Tournai, deploying over 100 Belgian and French police officers. The operation checked 707 vehicles and 447 people, resulted in two judicial arrests, and saw five drivers have their licenses immediately withdrawn, according to [DH Les Sports+](https://www.dhnet.be/regions/tournai-ath-mouscron/2026/06/12/de-la-panne-a-tournai-plus-de-700-vehicules-controles-lors-dune-vaste-operation-franco-belge-contre-la-criminalite-transfrontaliere-HG7UKJ3N4BFKZM5AQ3QOW5ONKI/).

These operations build on a long-standing cooperation framework. The Accord de Tournai, signed in 2001, formalized cross-border police and customs cooperation, leading to the establishment of the Centre de coopération policière et douanière (CCPD) in Tournai, where French and Belgian teams work together 24/7, as [Médiaconnect explains](https://www.media-connect.eu/d/la-cooperation-policiere-transfrontaliere/).

## What's Next

While cross-border cooperation has improved significantly since the 1990s, when the infamous "Kappa" gangs exploited weak coordination between Belgian and French police, the persistence of car theft suggests more needs to be done. The technological arms race between car manufacturers and thieves continues to evolve, with criminals developing ever more sophisticated methods.

For residents of small border communities like Estaimpuis, where theft rates are nearly ten times the national average, the problem is deeply felt. The economic cost is substantial — the €1.45 million damage from just one dismantled network illustrates the scale. As authorities continue to refine their cross-border strategies, the question remains whether the border can ever be made truly impermeable to determined criminal networks.
