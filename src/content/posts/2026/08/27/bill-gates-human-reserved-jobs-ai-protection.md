# Bill Gates Proposes 'Human Reserved' Jobs to Protect Workers

Bill Gates has proposed reserving certain jobs exclusively for humans as a way to protect society from the disruptive impacts of artificial intelligence, calling the concept "Human Reserved" in a nearly 6,000-word essay published on his personal website. The Microsoft co-founder's intervention marks a significant escalation in the global debate over AI's role in the workforce, as he now describes AI as "the most dangerous tool ever invented."

## A Shift in Tone

Gates' essay, titled "The turbulent AI era is here. The choices we make are critical," represents his first lengthy piece on AI in three years and a notable departure from his earlier optimism. In an hourlong interview with [MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/08/26/1142946/bill-gates-ai-danger-threshold/), Gates said society has already crossed dangerous thresholds.

"We've crossed the threshold in terms of [AI's] bio-capabilities, cyber-capabilities, psychosocial capabilities, job-market-destruction capabilities, and even the lack of control," he told the publication. "I'm just stunned at the lack of concern and discussion outside of the industry."

According to [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/aug/26/bill-gates-human-reserved-jobs-ai-takeover), Gates likened the need to spare certain roles from digital encroachment to how nature reserves operate to protect the environment. "Many jobs will disappear forever," he wrote. "I believe that as AI and robots improve, we'll set aside certain things for only people to do."

## The 'Human Reserved' Concept

Gates compares the concept to nature reserves—places where society could build but chooses not to because the loss would be too great. He cites childcare and jury service as roles humans are clearly better suited to, while education and healthcare would become mixed fields where some functions remain human.

Gates' thinking was shaped in part by the care his father received before dying of Alzheimer's disease in 2020. "Something in the care they gave my dad was irreplaceably human, no robot could or should have done it," he said, as reported by [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/26/why-we-need-human-reserved-jobs-bill-gates-ai-memo.html). "Imagine a robot giving you the awful news that you have an incurable disease. There's no technical reason why it couldn't. Yet it shouldn't."

Gates puts the ceiling at roughly 40% of jobs that could realistically be reserved for humans under the most aggressive version of the concept, according to [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/bill-gates-wants-40-jobs-103651782.html).

## Taxing AI and Robots

Alongside the "Human Reserved" proposal, Gates is reviving and expanding his idea of a robot tax, first floated in 2017. He now also proposes a "token tax" on AI usage to slow the rush away from human labor and raise money for retraining and a stronger safety net.

"Right now, if you're an employer and you hire someone, you pay payroll taxes on their earnings. But if you buy a robot, you can usually write it off right away as a business expense," Gates wrote, as quoted by [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/26/bill-gates-wants-to-see-a-robot-tax-and-human-reserved-jobs-to-mitigate-harms-from-ai/). "The tax system nudges you toward replacing people with machines. A tax would slow the rush away from human labor a little and raise money for retraining and a stronger safety net."

## Warnings of Mass Unemployment

Gates warned that "many jobs will disappear forever" and that AI could trigger mass unemployment, enable bioterrorism, and even slip beyond human control. He told the [New York Post](https://nypost.com/2026/08/26/business/bill-gates-sounds-alarm-on-ai-fueled-mass-unemployment-bioterrorism-most-dangerous-tool-ever-invented/) that he is "staking everything I know" on his belief that AI will break with centuries of technological progress by destroying jobs without creating enough new ones to replace them.

Gates cited a Stanford Digital Economy Lab study showing that early-career workers (ages 22-25) in AI-exposed occupations experienced 16% relative employment declines, as detailed by [explainx.ai](https://www.explainx.ai/blog/bill-gates-turbulent-ai-era-essay-jobs-education-august-2026). He also expressed concern about AI's impact on education, noting that "heavier AI use was associated with less critical thinking."

## Government Preparedness

Gates expressed deep concern that governments are not prepared for the scale of AI's impact. "The highest priority is a monumental task: creating a domestic and international framework for dealing with AI," he wrote, as reported by [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/bill-gates-human-reserved-jobs-ai). "After the attacks of 9/11 the US government went through its biggest reorganisation since World War II for the purpose of improving just one function, national security. AI will require much, much more."

His staff are working to set up a meeting with China's president, Xi Jinping, in November to discuss global efforts to mitigate AI risks, according to The Guardian.

## What's Next

Gates' essay enters an ongoing global debate about AI regulation, with voices ranging from Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei calling for more robust tax policies to Pope Leo XIV's encyclical on AI issued in May 2026. The Belgian public broadcaster [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/08/27/bill-gates-microsoft-ai-voorbehouden-banen/) noted that Gates' proposal for a "Gereserveerd voor mensen" label on the labor market emphasizes the importance of human interaction as "a key source of dignity and social connection."

As Gates acknowledged in his essay, the "Human Reserved" concept raises many unanswered questions: Who decides which jobs are reserved? What criteria should be used? How would it be enforced internationally? What is clear is that the debate over AI's impact on the workforce has moved from theoretical speculation to concrete policy proposals from one of the technology industry's most influential figures.

"Waiting until people are already displaced or underemployed will be too late," Gates wrote, as reported by [Business Insider](https://www.businessinsider.com/bill-gates-ai-jobs-warning-robots-blue-collar-human-roles-2026-8). "AI is a structural challenge to the way our economy is organized, and it requires thinking and action now."
