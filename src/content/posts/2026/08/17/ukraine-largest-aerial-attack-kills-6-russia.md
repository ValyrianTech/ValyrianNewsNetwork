# Ukraine Launches Largest Aerial Attack of War, Killing 6 in Russia

Ukraine launched hundreds of drones across Russia in one of its largest aerial attacks of the war, killing at least six people and marking a significant escalation in the ongoing conflict, according to [NPR/AP reports](https://www.npr.org/2026/08/16/nx-s1-5933006/ukraine-aerial-attack-6-dead-russia). Russia's Ministry of Defense said it destroyed 822 Ukrainian drones overnight, with Moscow Mayor Sergei Sobyanin reporting that approximately 600 drones were detected heading toward the Russian capital, a third of which were destroyed over the Moscow region itself.

The massive assault represents a dramatic intensification of Ukraine's long-range strike campaign, now in its fifth year of the full-scale war that began in February 2022. Russian authorities described the attack as the "largest-scale" Ukrainian aerial assault of 2026.

## Casualties and Destruction Across Russia

At least six people were killed in Russia as a result of the attack. Five people died in Russia's southwestern Rostov region, where more than 150 drones targeted three towns, damaging several homes and a railway station and sparking a forest fire, according to [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/aug/16/ukrainian-drone-attack-on-russia-wildberries-warehouse-fire). An 83-year-old man was killed after a drone hit a private home in the Ramensky district of the Moscow region, local Governor Andrey Vorobyov confirmed. A woman was also killed in the Belgorod region after a bus was struck.

A massive fire engulfed a 250,000-square-meter Wildberries distribution warehouse at the Koledino Industrial Park near Podolsk, approximately 25 miles south of Moscow. Ukraine's defense ministry said it had disabled the company's largest warehouse and that seven of Wildberries' 10 largest logistics centers had been "disabled" in recent weeks. The company, often described as "Russia's Amazon," has become a focal point of Ukraine's campaign to damage Russia's economic infrastructure.

Ukraine said it targeted a factory producing rocket fuel in Kamensk-Shakhtinsky in the Rostov region, according to [France 24](https://www.france24.com/en/europe/20260816-live-moscow-targeted-by-600-drones-overnight-explosions-heard-in-kyiv). Moscow region Governor Andrey Vorobyov described the assault as "one of the most massive drone attacks in recent memory."

## Russian Strikes on Ukraine

In retaliation, Russia launched missile and drone attacks across Ukraine, killing at least seven people and injuring more than 39, according to [BBC News](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cn7n4lm11vro). Two people were killed at an ArcelorMittal steel mill in Kryvyi Rih, two in the Zaporizhzhia region, and one in the Sumy region. A ballistic missile attack on Kyiv sparked fires in several districts and injured six people.

A Russian strike also destroyed a book market in Kyiv's Pochayna district, reducing hundreds of stalls to rubble. "There is nothing. Nobody has anything left," said Natalya, a bookstore owner interviewed by Ukraine's Hromadske website. "Everything burned down. I was selling foreign language [textbooks] for schoolchildren. We were waiting for September so that everyone would have textbooks... But what happened happened."

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said Russia had launched more than 1,550 attack drones, nearly 1,560 guided aerial bombs, and 62 missiles at Ukrainian cities over the past week. "Wherever the Russians can reach with their ballistic missiles, they strike civilian infrastructure," he said, calling for European interceptors to be deployed rather than "sit in storage."

## NATO Involvement and UK Drone Support

A Spanish F-18 fighter jet on a NATO air-policing mission shot down a drone that breached Romanian airspace in the early morning hours, according to [Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/my-europe/2026/08/16/12-killed-in-russia-and-ukraine-after-an-overnight-exchange-of-drone-and-missile-strikes). NATO spokesperson Martin O'Donnell said the drone "appears to be Russian." This was the fourth unmanned aircraft shot down over Romania this year.

Meanwhile, [LBC reported](https://www.lbc.co.uk/article/uk-drones-deep-strikes-russia-ukraine-huge-aerial-attack-5Hjdg6p_2/) that UK-manufactured drones from two British companies have been used for deep strikes within Russia for the first time, causing an estimated £35 billion in economic damage. A BBC military source confirmed the story. British Storm Shadow missiles had previously been used, but drone-led deep strikes have proven more effective.

## Escalating Air War and Civilian Toll

The exchange represents a significant escalation in the ongoing conflict, now in its fourth-and-a-half year. With front-line fighting at a near standstill and US-backed talks aimed at ending the conflict frozen, both sides have significantly stepped up long-range missile and drone attacks deep behind each other's lines, pushing civilian death tolls to their highest levels since the war's first months in 2022.

In July 2026 alone, 437 civilians were killed in Ukraine, the highest monthly toll since May 2022, according to the UN. Russian authorities reported 79 civilians killed in Ukrainian attacks in July, as reported by [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/17/at-least-six-people-killed-in-ukrainian-missile-attack-on-russias-belgorod).

Ukraine has rapidly developed largely homegrown technologies, including the Flamingo cruise missile and the FP-1 long-range fixed-wing drone from Fire Point, allowing it to bomb warehouses, oil refineries, ports, and military facilities up to 1,500 miles from its border. Ukraine can now almost match the deep-strike capability that Russia has had since the beginning of the war, though it still does not possess high-speed ballistic missiles.

## Expert Analysis and Implications

Serhii Kuzan, chair of the Ukrainian Security and Cooperation Center, told The Guardian that Russian authorities "deliberately inflate their figures" to make exaggerated claims about the effectiveness of their air defense. But he acknowledged that "with every successful strike, Russia's economic and military capabilities are diminished, and the Kremlin faces a new dilemma: how to counter this in a war of attrition."

Dr. Hanna Shelest, a security expert, noted the psychological impact on Russian civilians who are now experiencing the war firsthand for the first time. "Civilians in Russia, for four years, didn't feel the war existed," she told the BBC. "For them, it was [like] a nice soap opera on TV and propaganda statements by the leadership. But now they really see the fire, they see what the war means... they see the impact on the economy and they see the visual pictures, which is really important for the perception of the war."

Both sides' air defenses are stretched to the point of near exhaustion. Ukraine is effective at stopping slower-moving drones but has little to no defense against high-speed ballistic missiles, with its stock of US-made Patriot interceptor missiles having been run down. Russia's claimed interception numbers are viewed with caution by experts.

## What to Watch For

Following the August 16 attack, [BBC Live Coverage](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/live/c61k110gny00t) reported that nine people were killed and 23 injured in subsequent Ukrainian strikes on Russia on August 17, including eight in the Belgorod region. Russian attacks on Ukraine killed at least four more people the same day.

The escalation raises critical questions about the trajectory of the conflict: Will the cycle of long-range strikes continue to intensify? How will Russia respond to the destruction of Wildberries warehouses and the economic damage from Ukrainian drone strikes? And will the use of UK-manufactured drones in deep strikes within Russia escalate tensions between NATO and Moscow?

What remains clear is that the war has entered a new and more dangerous phase, with civilian populations on both sides bearing the brunt of an increasingly sophisticated and devastating aerial campaign. As both nations' air defenses deplete and long-range strike capabilities expand, the human cost of the conflict appears poised to rise further in the months ahead.
