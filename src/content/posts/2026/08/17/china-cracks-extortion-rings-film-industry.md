# China Cracks Three Extortion Rings Targeting Film Industry

Chinese public security cyber departments have dismantled three criminal organizations that used online platforms to extort money from the film and television industry through threats of negative publicity, arresting 24 suspects in coordinated operations across multiple provinces. The cases, announced by the Ministry of Public Security's Cybersecurity Bureau on August 17, expose a sophisticated "public opinion extortion" scheme that targeted popular dramas, variety shows, and celebrities during key promotional periods.

## The "Drama Protection" Scheme

According to [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/legal/20260817/e35cab9fd70048cb8a01c2504b42b8e7/c.html), the three cases involved criminal gangs that registered cultural media companies and controlled networks of social media accounts with millions of followers. The gangs fabricated and posted negative information about TV dramas, variety shows, and celebrities, creating public opinion pressure to force victims to purchase so-called "drama protection" services.

In the first case, cracked in Hangzhou, Zhejiang Province, a gang led by individuals surnamed Li and Liao operated multiple self-media accounts with million-level follower counts. The [China News Service](https://www.chinanews.com.cn/gn/2026/08-17/10678815.shtml) reported that the gang targeted popular TV dramas, variety shows, and "traffic stars," fabricating and posting large volumes of negative content to create pressure and force victims to buy "drama protection" services. Ten suspects were arrested in a synchronized operation spanning eight provinces and ten cities. As [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/legal/20260813/4283c1e01746415694103b912ab64aaa/c.html) detailed, the Hangzhou gang controlled Weibo accounts with a combined following exceeding 20.2 million, specifically targeting new drama premieres and variety show promotional periods.

A second case, cracked in Fuzhou, Fujian Province, involved two suspects surnamed Zheng and Huang who collected "negative information" about new dramas and celebrities, manipulated multiple popular platform accounts to fabricate false content, and charged fees to delete negative posts during key drama airing and celebrity event periods. Both suspects have been placed under criminal coercive measures as the investigation continues.

## AI-Assisted Extortion in Guangdong

The third case, uncovered in Heyuan, Guangdong Province, involved the most sophisticated operation. According to [Sina News](https://k.sina.com.cn/article_1279746217_m4c4760a903301gd1i.html), the gang led by Li Jun established an MCN institution and controlled multiple WeChat public accounts and Weibo accounts with over 10 million total followers. The gang employed network writers to gather celebrity gossip and used AI tools to control multiple "big-V" accounts, deliberately creating negative public opinion during drama and variety show airings.

The gang's "drama protection" services included posting positive promotional content, deleting previously posted negative posts about celebrities, and promising not to post negative content during airing periods. As [Hunan Daily New Media](https://m.voc.com.cn/xhn/news/202606/33025905.html) detailed, the operation involved over 400,000 yuan in illicit gains, with deletion fees ranging from 2,000 to 3,000 yuan per post. The victims included several famous actors and multiple hit TV drama distributors, some of whom had previously filed reputation infringement lawsuits against the gang's accounts.

## Broader Crackdown on Cyber-Enabled Crime

The crackdown is part of the broader "Deepening Special Campaign Against Gang Crime" targeting new types of gang crimes that have emerged through online platforms. As [Xiamen Net](https://news.xmnn.cn/gnxw/202608/t20260811_444443.html) reported, the Ministry of Public Security's first round of concentrated operations in August 2026 dismantled over 1,000 criminal groups, arrested more than 8,200 suspects, and solved over 5,400 criminal cases nationwide.

The campaign specifically targets cyber-enabled crimes including online loan sharking, soft violence debt collection, public opinion extortion, malicious claims, labor fraud, forced consumption, and online violence. Provincial operations have yielded significant results, with Zhejiang arresting 109 suspects for online loan sharking, Shandong detaining 27 for staged traffic accidents, and Guangdong dismantling eight labor fraud rings targeting construction sites.

## Legal and Industry Implications

The Ministry of Public Security's Cybersecurity Bureau emphasized that "cyberspace is not a lawless place," stating that using online platforms to organize public opinion extortion seriously disrupts network order and constitutes a crime. The bureau pledged to maintain a high-pressure crackdown posture and dig deep into the entire black-gray industry chain.

Workers' Daily [commentary](https://www.workercn.cn/c/2026-07-01/8836767.shtml) described the scheme as "first setting the fire, then putting it out," noting that the practice of fabricating scandals and then selling "protection" services represents a direct challenge to network order. The commentary called for legal measures, technological firewalls, and platform responsibility to work together to combat the phenomenon.

The cases also highlight the growing sophistication of cyber-enabled crime in China's digital economy. The use of AI tools in the Heyuan case to control "big-V" accounts represents an emerging challenge for regulators, as does the professionalization of MCN-based criminal operations. As [First Financial](https://www.yicai.com/news/103320115.html) noted, the announcement underscores the government's recognition that these new crime types require targeted enforcement responses.

## What's Next

All three cases remain under further investigation, with questions remaining about potential penalties for the arrested suspects and whether related networks will face additional enforcement action. The cases highlight the growing sophistication of cyber-enabled extortion in China's digital economy, particularly the use of AI tools to amplify criminal operations. As the broader campaign continues, authorities are expected to maintain pressure on these emerging forms of organized crime while calling on netizens to engage in rational and civil online discourse. The entertainment industry, meanwhile, may need to reassess how it manages online reputation in an environment where fabricated scandals can be weaponized for financial gain.
