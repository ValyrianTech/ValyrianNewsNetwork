# Hayden Panettiere, 'Heroes' and 'Nashville' Star, Dies at 36

Hayden Panettiere, the actress best known for her starring roles in the hit television series *Heroes* and *Nashville*, has died at the age of 36. She was found unresponsive in her apartment in Greenville, South Carolina, on Sunday, August 16, and pronounced dead at the scene. Her father, Skip Panettiere, confirmed her death in a statement shared by her representative, according to [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/08/17/nx-s1-5935226/hayden-panettiere-dies).

"It is with profound sadness that we share the tragic passing of our beloved Hayden," the statement read. "She was an incredible light and a force of nature who brought immeasurable love and joy to all who knew her—and to the millions who watched her onscreen. We ask for privacy as our family takes time to process this unimaginable loss."

## The Circumstances of Her Death

A friend called 911 at 1:51 p.m. local time Sunday to report an unresponsive female in cardiac arrest at Panettiere's luxury apartment complex, law enforcement sources told [TMZ](https://www.tmz.com/2026/08/17/hayden-panettiere-found-dead-by-a-friend-inside-greenville-home/). Paramedics administered advanced cardiac life support, including chest compressions and epinephrine, but Panettiere was pronounced dead at 2:32 p.m.

911 dispatch audio obtained by [Page Six](https://pagesix.com/2026/08/17/celebrity-news/hayden-panettiere-found-dead-by-friend-in-south-carolina-home-report/) referenced a possible overdose, and [TMZ reported](https://www.tmz.com/2026/08/17/hayden-panettiere-death-narcan-found-in-condo/) that Narcan—a medication used to reverse opioid overdoses—was found on a mattress in the apartment. Two ambulances responded to the scene, leaving unclear who received the medication.

The Greenville County Coroner performed an autopsy on Monday, August 17. Initial findings showed no signs of trauma, [BBC News reported](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cq5665zgg1po), though the official cause and manner of death remain pending further investigation. Police stated the preliminary investigation has not indicated any signs of foul play or suspicious circumstances.

Panettiere's on-again, off-again boyfriend Brian Hickerson reportedly flew with her from Los Angeles to South Carolina on Saturday, the day before her death. Hickerson was previously convicted of domestic abuse against Panettiere, receiving a 45-day jail sentence in 2021.

## A Career Built From Childhood

Born August 21, 1989, in Palisades, New York, Panettiere began her career as an infant, appearing in her first commercial at 11 months old. Her mother, Lesley Vogel, was a soap opera actress who set her daughter on the same path. By age 4, Panettiere was on *One Life to Live*, and she voiced Princess Dot in Pixar's *A Bug's Life* in 1998, becoming one of the youngest Grammy nominees ever.

She achieved mainstream fame at age 17 when *Heroes* became a cultural phenomenon in 2006. Her character, Claire Bennet—a cheerleader who could regenerate—was central to the show's iconic "save the cheerleader, save the world" storyline. The role earned her a Teen Choice Award and catapulted her into stardom.

Panettiere later starred as country singer Juliette Barnes in *Nashville* (2012-2018), a role that earned her two Golden Globe nominations. As [she told NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/05/15/nx-s1-5686493/im-ok-with-my-scars-hayden-panettiere-on-the-struggles-that-shape-her-life) in May, the character's storylines increasingly mirrored her own life struggles. "I stopped being able to tell where the character started and where, you know, I ended," she said. "Like we were just mushed together."

## Public Struggles and a Memoir

Earlier this year, Panettiere published her memoir *This Is Me: A Reckoning*, detailing her struggles with child stardom, addiction, postpartum depression, and personal trauma. In it, she described being given "happy pills" by a publicist at age 15-16, which she called "the gateway drug that ushered me toward the good of pharmaceuticals and the downfall of addiction."

She was open about battling opioid and alcohol addiction for years, revealing she drank a bottle of wine a night just four months after giving birth to her daughter Kaya in 2014. A doctor told her she'd be dead within five years if she didn't stop drinking. She checked into rehab in 2020 and spent eight months in treatment.

In 2018, Panettiere signed legal papers giving her former fiancé, Ukrainian boxing champion Wladimir Klitschko, full custody of their daughter. She described this as "the most gut-wrenching experience of my life," saying "no one should ever have to raise a child on FaceTime." Her younger brother Jansen died in February 2023 at age 28 from an undiagnosed heart condition.

## Tributes From Colleagues and Friends

Tributes poured in from across Hollywood following the news of her death. [Deadline reported](https://deadline.com/2026/08/hayden-panettiere-tributes-scream-actors-1237042809/) that *Scream* co-stars David Arquette and Melissa Barrera paid tribute, along with Emma Roberts, who starred with Panettiere in *Scream 4*. Selma Blair wrote simply, "I love you. Don't be gone. Please."

Bryan Fuller, who wrote Claire Bennet's storyline in the first season of *Heroes*, remembered her as "more than an actress, she was a muse. Wise beyond her years and already saddled with so much of a life, she left an indelible mark on me as a writer and a storyteller."

Viola Davis, who co-starred with Panettiere in the 2016 film *Custody*, called her "an amazing talent" and "an old, wise soul with such a deep heart," adding, "I wish the world had more time with you because I had a burning curiosity about who you were becoming."

Longtime friend Bethany Joy-Lenz of *One Tree Hill* wrote an emotional tribute, recalling Panettiere as a child actress: "She knew her lines and everyone else's. Her intelligence was astonishing. She understood scenes and nuance and human struggle like no child I'd ever seen on camera."

## What Comes Next

The Greenville County Coroner's Office continues its investigation into the cause and manner of Panettiere's death. The actress is survived by her 11-year-old daughter Kaya, her father Skip, and her mother Lesley. Her death marks the loss of a beloved television star whose on-screen charisma was matched by a deeply personal and often painful journey through the darker sides of fame.

As the entertainment world mourns, the conversation around her life—one marked by extraordinary talent, public battles with addiction, and a relentless pursuit of healing—will undoubtedly continue. Her memoir, published just months before her death, stands as her final testament to a life lived in the spotlight, and her hope that sharing her story might help others feel less alone.
