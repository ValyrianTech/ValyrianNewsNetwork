# McDonald's App Cuts Costly Missed Shifts for Workers

A McDonald's franchise in suburban Washington, D.C., has found an unlikely solution to a costly problem: an app that gives workers last-minute Uber rides, grocery gift cards, and childcare support when life gets in the way of showing up for a shift.

The pilot, run by [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/08/17/nx-s1-5893721/workers-mcdonalds-work-app-jobs-ai)-reported startup Escalate, has saved the seven-restaurant Hopkins-Navies Ford Management franchise roughly $35,000 per month in callout costs, according to the company. Missed shifts in the fast-food industry cost at least $300 per shift, and the app is proving that addressing workers' practical barriers can be good for business.

## The Problem Behind Missed Shifts

For years, the franchise's longtime human resources manager Chantel Johnson would personally pick up employees to prevent callouts. "A number of times, I've just picked employees up," she said. "It's challenging because that keeps me from doing what I need to get done. But I realize how important it is to get these restaurants staffed."

The cost of absenteeism extends far beyond the missed shift itself. Customers abandon drive-through lines when service slows, and remaining staff face heavier workloads. "If it's taking too long, they'll pull out of line," said Mary Hopkins-Navies, who has been in the McDonald's business for 35 years. "If they come in here and it takes too long to get service, they're going to walk out the door."

## A Startup Born From Nonprofit Experience

Escalate was founded by Sienna Daniel and Sean Segal, two veterans of the nonprofit world who spent years running bootcamp-style job training programs for unemployed and underemployed people. They kept seeing the same pattern: workers wanted jobs but couldn't keep them because of practical barriers like broken-down cars, childcare gaps, and food insecurity.

"Opportunities might be there, but if they aren't stabilized and they don't have what they need to be able to get to where they need to go, there's a problem," Daniel told [WAMC](https://www.wamc.org/2026-08-17/missed-shifts-were-costly-to-this-mcdonalds-an-app-has-fixed-the-problem).

In 2025, Escalate received a grant from the state of Maryland to run a six-month pilot with Hopkins-Navies Ford Management. Through the app, employees can request Uber vouchers for transportation, grocery gift cards when food is short, and Visa gift cards to pay family or neighbors for childcare.

## A Winter Storm Changes Everything

Initially, employees were skeptical. "Nobody really believed it, so no one was using it," Johnson said. Escalate translated the app into Spanish after realizing a majority of the franchise's employees were Spanish speakers.

The real turning point came in late January when a winter storm hit. Snow turned to ice, making commutes treacherous. Veronica Allmon, who relies on an electric scooter for transportation, would normally have called off. Instead, she used the app to get an Uber to work.

"No other job that I've worked at ever went this far for their employees," said Allmon, who was recently promoted to crew trainer.

More than 80% of requests from McDonald's workers are for transportation help, and most come in 30 to 90 minutes before a shift starts. Employees get three requests per month, after which Escalate connects them with a coach to develop a longer-term plan.

## A Business Case That Works

Since the grant-funded pilot ended, the franchise has been paying for the service. Escalate charges $250 per month per store, plus the cost of vouchers and gift cards, which averaged about $250 per month per store during the pilot.

"Having that app, and having our employees make it to work, has been crazy beneficial for us," said Imani Ford, who operates the franchise with her mother. "We really aren't seeing a lot of callouts. It's absolutely helping with retention."

The startup keeps costs low by employing overseas coaches and incorporating AI to handle routine requests. Escalate is already working with other companies in retail and manufacturing and is talking with other McDonald's franchise owners.

## A Broader Shift in Frontline Workforce Management

The Escalate model reflects a growing trend of using technology to address frontline workforce challenges. [GE Appliances](https://www.npr.org/2026/07/08/nx-s1-5876084/manufacturing-flexible-part-time-work) has embraced flexible scheduling through the MyWorkChoice app at its Georgia plant, and manufacturers including Stanley Black & Decker and Georgia-Pacific are following suit.

Next month, Hopkins-Navies plans to bring Daniel to a meeting of the National Black McDonald's Operators Association in Atlanta to introduce the program to other franchise owners.

"I'm taking her to everybody that I know [to] introduce this program to them," Hopkins-Navies said. "We could all benefit from it. It really does show a culture of care when you go that far, you go that extra step."

As more employers recognize that supporting workers' real-life needs can improve the bottom line, Escalate may become a model for how the fast-food industry tackles one of its most persistent operational challenges.
