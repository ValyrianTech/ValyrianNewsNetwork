# Francken Pushes for US Chinook Helicopters Amid Fire Debate

Belgian Defense Minister Theo Francken has announced his intention to acquire American Boeing CH-47 Chinook helicopters for the Belgian armed forces, using the devastating wildfire in the Hautes Fagnes nature reserve as a backdrop to push back against critics of US military equipment. The announcement, made Monday via a post on X, comes as the Netherlands deployed Chinook helicopters to assist Belgium in fighting what has become the largest wildfire in Belgian history.

## The Fire That Exposed a Capability Gap

The wildfire, which broke out on Friday, August 14, in the Hautes Fagnes nature reserve in eastern Belgium, has burned approximately 3,000 hectares and forced the evacuation of around 600 residents from Sourbrodt and Bütgenbach. The fire is a peat fire, making it particularly difficult to extinguish as it burns underground through peat layers and can reignite for months.

Belgium activated the EU Civil Protection Mechanism to request international assistance, and Dutch Chinook helicopters equipped with Bambi Buckets capable of carrying approximately 7,500 liters of water have been instrumental in the aerial firefighting effort. As [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/liveblog/brand-hoge-venen-breidt-uit-1600-hectare-verwoest-turfbodem-e~1785314118283/) reported, the Dutch military transport helicopters have been operating alongside Swedish Air Tractor water bombers and German NH-90 helicopters.

The fire has exposed significant gaps in Belgium's aerial firefighting capabilities. According to [BruxellesToday](https://www.bruxellestoday.be/actualite/hautes-fagnes-belgique-pas-envoyer-bombardiers-eau.html), Belgium does not own any water-bomber aircraft, and the private firefighting planes from Aquarius Aerial Firefighting (a subsidiary of Cargolux) that are sometimes stationed at Zwartberg airport in Genk are currently contracted to the Turkish government and deployed in Turkey and Greece.

## Francken's Push for Chinooks

Francken, a member of the N-VA party, emphasized that the purchase of heavy transport helicopters like the Chinook is already included in the Strategic Vision of Defense, though it was planned for 2033. He suggested this timeline could be accelerated.

"But they are American. And for some parties, including all the left-wing opposition parties that are shouting so loudly today, everything American is false and bad," Francken wrote on X, as reported by [La Libre Belgique](https://www.lalibre.be/belgique/politique-belge/2026/08/17/francken-veut-des-helicopteres-chinooks-mais-pour-certains-tout-ce-qui-est-americain-est-mauvais-YV3WAW3LUNCVRKTCXBLNMINTOE/).

The minister's comments highlight a growing political divide in Belgium over dependence on American military equipment. Vooruit, the Flemish socialist party that is part of the governing coalition, has expressed concerns that Belgium cannot be too dependent on deliveries from the United States. Left-wing opposition parties including PS, PTB, and Ecolo have been critical of American weapons procurement.

Francken has been a consistent advocate for maintaining strong ties with the US, previously defending the purchase of American F-35 fighter jets and other US military equipment. [Aviation Week](https://aviationweek.com/defense/aircraft-propulsion/boeing-fielding-ch-47-interest-belgium-czech-republic) noted as early as 2022 that Belgium had expressed interest in the CH-47 Chinook.

## Broader Political Reactions

The wildfire has also triggered a wider political debate about climate change and emergency preparedness. According to [RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/incendie-dans-les-hautes-fagnes-les-presidents-de-partis-francophones-entre-emotion-appels-a-l-action-et-demandes-de-sanctions-11771140), PS President Paul Magnette expressed "immense respect" for emergency services and noted the disaster reminds us "with particular brutality" of "the reality of the climate emergency." PTB President Raoul Hedebouw denounced "political inaction" and pointed to a "cruel lack of means" for firefighters facing such "megafires."

Meanwhile, MR President Georges-Louis Bouchez made no link between the disaster and climate change, emphasizing that the fire was the consequence of individuals who "either voluntarily or through negligence" started it.

Interior Minister Bernard Quintin defended Belgium's decision to have its private water bombers deployed abroad, stating: "That is the principle of European solidarity. We cannot expect everyone to come and help us while we don't help anyone." His comments were reported by [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/08/16/zwartberg-genk-blusvliegtuig-brand-hoge-venen/).

## What's Next

As firefighters continue to battle the blaze, with rain providing some relief on Monday, the debate over Belgium's defense procurement and firefighting capabilities is likely to intensify. Francken has indicated that the Chinook acquisition timeline could be moved forward, though political opposition within the coalition government may complicate the process.

Marc Gilbert, President of the Royal Belgian Fire Brigade Federation, has called for the creation of a European fleet of water-bomber aircraft, noting that "the 2 helicopters of the (Belgian) police are already very nice, but they are not enough. We must act quickly."

With the fire expected to be contained "today, tomorrow, or Wednesday," according to emergency services, the focus will increasingly shift to the political and strategic lessons drawn from Belgium's largest-ever wildfire.
