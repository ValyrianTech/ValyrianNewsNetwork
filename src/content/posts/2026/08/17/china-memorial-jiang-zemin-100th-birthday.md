# China Holds Grand Memorial for Jiang Zemin's 100th Birthday

A grand memorial ceremony was held Monday at the Great Hall of the People in Beijing to commemorate the 100th birthday of Jiang Zemin, the former Chinese president and general secretary of the Communist Party of China (CCP) from 1989 to 2002. President Xi Jinping attended the ceremony and delivered a keynote speech honoring Jiang's contributions to the nation, according to [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/politics/20260817/564676d6ea454c0281e8727ace633f78/c.html).

The ceremony, broadcast live by China Media Group and xinhuanet.com, was relayed simultaneously across major Chinese media platforms, including people.cn, cctv.com, and mobile apps of the People's Daily, Xinhua News Agency, and CCTV, as [China.org.cn](http://www.china.org.cn/2026-08/16/content_118649727.shtml) reported.

## A Leader's Legacy

In his address, Xi described Jiang as "a recognized outstanding leader with high prestige among the whole Party, the entire military, and people of all ethnic groups across the country," and "the core of the Party's third generation of central leadership." Xi praised Jiang as "a great Marxist, a great proletarian revolutionary, statesman, military strategist, and diplomat," and the main founder of the "Three Represents" important thought, as [Lianhe Zaobao](https://www.zaobao.com.sg/news/china/story20260817-9529416) reported.

Xi highlighted Jiang's role in leading China through pivotal moments, noting that Jiang "had the courage to make decisive decisions at major historical junctures and critical moments." He credited Jiang with successfully resisting the impact of the Asian financial crisis and winning the comprehensive victory in the 1998 flood relief efforts, according to [Lianhe Zaobao's coverage of Xi's speech](https://www.zaobao.com.sg/news/china/story20260817-9529678).

Jiang served as CCP general secretary from 1989 to 2002 and as president from 1993 to 2003. During his tenure, he expanded Deng Xiaoping's market reforms, pushed state-owned enterprise and financial reforms, secured China's WTO accession in 2001, and opened party membership to private entrepreneurs.

## Commemorative Events Across China

The centennial commemoration was accompanied by a series of official events. China Post issued a set of two commemorative stamps and one souvenir sheet for Jiang's 100th birthday, with specifications identical to those issued for Mao Zedong, Deng Xiaoping, and other former top CCP leaders. The government also rescheduled monthly economic data release from the morning to the afternoon of August 17 to accommodate the ceremony, as [Lianhe Zaobao](https://www.zaobao.com.sg/news/china/story20260816-9527636) reported.

CCTV aired a 12-part documentary series titled "Jiang Zemin" twice daily during prime time from August 11-16. On July 6, the Central Propaganda Department designated Jiang's ancestral home in Yangzhou, Jiangsu Province as a national patriotic education base.

The commemoration came just days after the death of former Premier Zhu Rongji, who served alongside Jiang and was a key figure in China's market reforms. Zhu died in Beijing at age 98 on August 12, just five days before the centennial ceremony. The two leaders who steered China toward a market economy and global trade system were invoked together in the commemorative events, as [The Chosun Daily](https://www.chosun.com/english/world-en/2026/08/14/TT43CM3UGFEV5FYLD3XCQC2AXU/) reported.

## Notable Attendees and Absences

According to [Lianhe Zaobao](https://www.zaobao.com.sg/news/china/story20260817-9529502), a wide array of retired leaders attended the ceremony, including former Premier Wen Jiabao, former CPPCC Chairman Jia Qinglin, former NPC Standing Committee Chairman Zhang Dejiang, and former Vice President Wang Qishan, among others. Former Politburo members including Chen Xi, Yang Jiechi, and Liu He also attended.

However, several prominent former leaders were notably absent, including former CCP General Secretary Hu Jintao, former CPPCC Chairman Li Ruihuan, former Discipline Inspection Secretary Wu Guanzheng, and former Political and Legal Affairs Commission Secretary Luo Gan. Hu Jintao, who turned 84 later this year, has not made a public appearance since attending Jiang's funeral in December 2022.

## Political Significance

Analysts suggest the commemorative efforts go beyond mere remembrance, aiming to reposition Jiang's era within the CCP's historical narrative. The leadership is highlighting two key images of Jiang: as the "successor of reform and opening-up" and as a "crisis manager" who made decisive moves when "the party and state faced peril," according to The Chosun Daily's analysis.

The emphasis on Jiang's reform legacy comes amid significant economic challenges, including real estate slumps, weak domestic demand, local government debt, and U.S.-Europe trade and tech conflicts. Highlighting Jiang's legacy signals continued prioritization of the private economy and Western economic cooperation.

With attention growing on Xi's succession ahead of next year's party congress, analysts interpret the focus on Jiang as emphasizing a unified CCP lineage from Mao to Deng, Jiang, Hu, and Xi, rather than promoting specific factions. Professor Chien-Wen Kou of National Chengchi University in Taiwan noted the centennial events could "affirm Jiang's historical contributions while strengthening the current leadership's authority and stabilizing party morale."

## Looking Forward

In his speech, Xi quoted Jiang's own words from the past: "In the new century, the Chinese people will unswervingly continue to advance along the road of building socialism with Chinese characteristics." Xi then declared, "The best commemoration of Jiang Zemin today is to unswervingly follow the path of socialism with Chinese characteristics... and promote the comprehensive construction of a strong country and the great cause of national rejuvenation with Chinese-style modernization."

The ceremony underscored the CCP's effort to maintain historical continuity while reinforcing the authority of the current leadership. As one diplomatic source in Beijing remarked, "The key is which Jiang Zemin today's China chooses to remember—emphasizing both the reformist leader who opened markets and the crisis manager who defended the system."
