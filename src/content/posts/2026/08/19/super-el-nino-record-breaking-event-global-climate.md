# Super El Niño: Record-Breaking Event Looms Globally

China's National Climate Center has issued a stark assessment: the equatorial central-eastern Pacific is experiencing a rapidly intensifying El Niño event that is projected to become the strongest on record, with potentially far-reaching consequences for global weather patterns, agriculture, and economies through 2027.

According to [People's Daily](http://env.people.com.cn/n1/2026/0819/c1010-40782028.html), sea surface temperature (SST) anomalies in the key monitoring region reached 2.09°C in July and climbed to 2.64°C in the first half of August—a rate of warming described as "extremely rapid." The National Climate Center projects the event will peak around November-December 2026, with the three-month running mean likely exceeding the 2.5°C threshold that defines a "super" El Niño.

## What Makes This Event Unprecedented

El Niño—Spanish for "the Christ Child"—refers to the abnormal warming of sea surface temperatures in the central and eastern equatorial Pacific. Under normal conditions, trade winds push warm water westward, but during El Niño events these winds weaken or reverse, allowing warm water to accumulate in the east. The Niño 3.4 monitoring region alone covers approximately 6 million square kilometers.

The National Marine Environmental Forecasting Center's latest model projects a peak intensity near 3.5°C, which would shatter the records set by the three previous super El Niño events of 1982/1983, 1997/1998, and 2015/2016. As [Science and Technology Daily](https://www.stdaily.com/web/gdxw/2026-08/18/content_565693.html) reported, the current SST anomaly intensity already exceeds the same-period levels of those historic events, and the pace of development is more rapid than anything previously observed.

The World Meteorological Organization (WMO) has been tracking this development closely. In its June update, the [UN News](https://news.un.org/zh/story/2026/06/1142245) reported an 80% probability of El Niño forming between June and August, with conditions expected to persist through at least November. By July, the WMO confirmed that strong El Niño conditions were developing rapidly, with forecast models showing high consistency.

European Centre for Medium-Range Weather Forecasts (ECMWF) expert Tim Stockdale described the situation as unprecedented, noting he had "never seen such strong and consistent predictions" across forecast models, and that the event would "break records."

## China's Summer Already Reflecting El Niño's Signature

China's unusual summer of 2026 is largely a product of the rapidly developing El Niño, according to the National Climate Center. The country has experienced two distinct rain belts across the east, with intense rainstorms, overlapping precipitation zones, and compounding disaster impacts. Typhoon activity has been unusually active and intense, with prolonged wind and rain effects.

The [Beijing Daily](https://m.takefoto.cn/news/2026/07/09/11853244.shtml) reported that National Climate Center experts identified torrential rain, meteorological drought, heatwaves, and typhoon flooding as the key weather risks for mid-summer. Looking ahead, the autumn of 2026 is expected to bring above-normal rainfall to central-southern East China, Central China, northern South China, and eastern Southwest regions, while northern Central China and eastern Northwest areas face drier conditions. Most of China is projected to experience above-normal temperatures.

## Global Heatwaves and Extreme Weather

The most widespread impact of El Niño is extreme heat. This summer has already seen record-breaking temperatures across Europe, with Germany, the Czech Republic, and Poland all setting all-time national highs. Germany recorded 41.7°C, while parts of India exceeded 45°C, with some areas surpassing 50°C.

The WMO's [July update](https://news.un.org/zh/story/2026/07/1142425) warned that strong El Niño conditions would develop rapidly from July through September, increasing the likelihood of heatwaves, drought, heavy rainfall, and other extreme weather events across many parts of the world. UN Secretary-General António Guterres stated there is a 90% probability of El Niño, which will compound the effects of global warming.

WMO Secretary-General Celeste Saulo urged countries to prepare for a high-intensity El Niño, warning of increased drought, heavy rainfall, and heatwave risks. "The intensity of El Niño is critical because it determines the extent to which it increases the probability of extreme weather and climate events in different parts of the world," she said.

## Economic Ripple Effects

The [Securities Times](https://www.stcn.com/article/detail/4036276.html) has analyzed the economic implications, noting that the FAO projects global grain production will decline 1.9% to 2.98 billion tons, with wheat production down 4.3%. The Asia-Pacific region faces elevated food supply risks, particularly for rice, sugar, and palm oil.

Metal markets are also vulnerable. The Asia-Pacific region is the global processing hub, contributing over 70% of aluminum, copper, and nickel smelting capacity. Copper production is highly water-dependent, while aluminum relies heavily on hydropower electricity. Extreme weather events could disrupt mining, smelting, and transportation, potentially tightening supply and pushing up prices.

## The Lag Effect: 2027 Could Bring the Real Test

One of the most critical aspects of this El Niño is its delayed impact. As National Climate Center Chief Forecaster Chen Lijuan explained, the influence of a strong El Niño on global climate typically peaks in the following year due to the ocean-atmosphere lag effect—usually six months to a year after the event's peak intensity.

Chinese Academy of Sciences Academician and Nanjing University President Tan Zhemin warned that precipitation in the middle-lower Yangtze River and South China regions increases significantly in the year following an El Niño. He highlighted risks of flooding, rice damage from waterlogged roots, and increased crop disease in humid conditions.

The [Guangming Online](https://m.gmw.cn/2026-03/16/content_1304378630.htm) report quoted climate scientist Zhou Bing, who noted that while the probability of El Niño this year is high, the historical spacing between super events (15-18 years) suggests a super El Niño before 2030 is less likely—though this event may prove the exception.

## Preparing for What's Ahead

Tan Jing, Senior Engineer at the National Marine Environmental Forecasting Center, offered a sobering perspective: "We are witnessing new records that mean something." The full impacts of this event are expected to manifest in spring-summer 2027, with elevated flood risk in the Yangtze River basin, mountain flash floods, and coral bleaching threats.

Experts emphasize the need for comprehensive preparation across multiple sectors. Agriculture must prepare for flooding and crop disease. Infrastructure needs strengthened resilience against urban flooding, landslides, and transport disruptions. Public health systems should prepare for increased dengue fever risk and forest fire hazards.

As the [Cover News](https://www.thecover.cn/news/jkViuogIrAOH90qSdq8Jkw==) reported, climate scientist Zhou Bing warned that "global warming is again on a fast track, and new temperature records are extremely likely to follow." The convergence of a potentially record-breaking El Niño with ongoing human-induced climate change represents a critical test for global preparedness.

Tan Zhemin's advice is straightforward: "Based on rolling real-time monitoring and dynamic forecasts, make solid preparations." With the event still developing and its full impacts yet to unfold, the coming months will determine whether this El Niño lives up to its billing as the strongest on record—and how well the world is prepared for what follows.
