# China Opens Community Jobs to College Graduates

Three Chinese government departments have jointly issued a notice to optimize urban and rural community positions to absorb college graduates into employment, as the country faces a record graduating class of 12.7 million students in 2026. The Ministry of Human Resources and Social Security, the Central Social Work Department, and the Ministry of Civil Affairs signed the document on July 31, with the [full text published](https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202608/content_7078505.htm) on the State Council website on August 18.

## Context: Record Graduate Numbers Meet Grassroots Needs

The policy arrives as China's 2026 graduating class is expected to reach 12.7 million students, an increase of 480,000 from the previous year, according to [Xinhua reporting](https://news.haiwainet.cn/n/2026/0611/c3544276-32959918.html). The government has rolled out multiple initiatives throughout 2026 to address the employment challenge, including 16 new policy measures issued in March and the Ministry of Education's "100-Day Sprint" employment action launched in June.

The new notice builds on two foundational policy documents: the April 2024 "Opinions on Strengthening the Construction of Community Worker Teams," which established the standard of 18 community workers per 10,000 urban permanent residents, and the March 2026 "Opinions on Promoting the Construction of Social Work Professional Personnel Teams," as [reported by the MOHRSS](https://www.mohrss.gov.cn/SYrlzyhshbzb/dongtaixinwen/buneiyaowen/rsxw/202608/t20260818_582361.html).

## Four Channels to Community Employment

The notice establishes four distinct pathways for graduates to enter community employment, according to the [People's Daily report](http://paper.people.com.cn/rmrb/pc/content/202608/19/content_30175774.html):

**Optimized community full-time worker recruitment**: City and county-level social work departments must develop annual recruitment plans based on the 18-workers-per-10,000-residents standard. In principle, all newly recruited positions will be open to college graduates, with priority given to those willing to commit to community governance.

**Development of urban-rural community service positions**: Localities with suitable conditions are encouraged to create positions for graduates in urban management and grassroots governance, including dedicated graduate employment projects where feasible.

**Mobilization of community service enterprises and social organizations**: Companies and professional social organizations in the community services sector will be supported through government service procurement and project cooperation to recruit more graduates, with incentives including expansion subsidies and social insurance subsidies.

**Community internship positions**: The "Million Employment Internship Development Plan" will be expanded to village and community settings, offering 3-12 month internships with relevant subsidies. Graduates with community internship experience will receive priority consideration in future community worker recruitment.

## Support and Career Development

Beyond recruitment channels, the notice outlines comprehensive support measures. The policy requires unified job posting through public employment service websites and market-based HR platforms, along with offline recruitment activities featuring dedicated community position zones, as detailed in the [China News Service report](https://www.chinanews.com.cn/gn/2026/08-18/10679954.shtml).

Training will be delivered through talent training bases, with a "mentor-mentee system" pairing newly recruited graduates with experienced community workers. Qualified personnel can participate in social worker professional qualification evaluation and receive relevant professional allowances.

Notably, the policy creates clear advancement pathways: outstanding graduates serving as community workers can be nominated as candidates for village and community "two committees" membership, party congress representatives, people's congress representatives, and CPPCC members. This represents a significant incentive for graduates considering long-term careers in grassroots governance.

## Analysis: A Two-Way Solution

The initiative reflects a deliberate strategy to address two challenges simultaneously: absorbing the record graduate cohort into the workforce while strengthening community-level governance capacity. This is not China's first such effort - a similar 2022 policy resulted in 82,000 urban community full-time worker positions being opened to graduates, with 25,000 positions specifically reserved for them, as [documented by Shenzhen News](http://www.sznews.com/news/content/2022-08/22/content_25323640.htm).

The policy's emphasis on professional development - including preferred majors in sociology, social work, psychology, law, and community management - signals an intent to professionalize community governance rather than simply warehouse unemployed graduates. The "two-way benefit" approach positions graduates as valuable human capital for grassroots modernization.

## What to Watch

Implementation will depend heavily on local government execution, with city and county-level social work departments responsible for translating the policy into concrete recruitment plans. Key questions include how quickly localities can develop the infrastructure for unified job posting and whether funding commitments - drawn from employment, talent development, and community governance budgets - will be sufficient to support the expanded positions.

The policy's success will also hinge on whether graduates see community work as a viable long-term career path. The incentive mechanisms, including political representation pathways and professional qualification support, suggest the government is making a concerted effort to make grassroots positions attractive. As the 2026 graduating class enters the job market, the effectiveness of these measures will become increasingly clear.
