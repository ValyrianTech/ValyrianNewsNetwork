# Wang Yi Begins Diplomatic Tour of South Korea, Indonesia

Chinese Foreign Minister Wang Yi arrived in Seoul on Wednesday, launching a four-day diplomatic tour of South Korea and Indonesia that marks his first official visit to the South Korean capital in five years. The trip, announced by the [Chinese Foreign Ministry](https://www.mfa.gov.cn/web/wjdt_674879/wsrc_674883/202608/t20260819_12006397.shtml), comes at a pivotal moment for regional diplomacy, with the APEC summit scheduled for November in Shenzhen and new peace initiatives on the Korean Peninsula taking shape.

## A Return to Seoul After Five Years

Wang's visit to South Korea, scheduled for August 19-20, comes at the invitation of South Korean Foreign Minister Cho Hyun. According to [Yonhap News Agency](https://en.yna.co.kr/view/AEN20260818004500315), the top Chinese diplomat will hold talks with Cho on Wednesday covering bilateral relations, North Korea-China ties, and other issues concerning the Korean Peninsula.

On Thursday, Wang is set to pay a courtesy call on South Korean President Lee Jae Myung at the Blue House, where the two leaders will discuss the development of Seoul-Beijing relations and regional security. National Security Adviser Wi Sung-lac will also hold a separate meeting and luncheon with Wang to discuss the security situation on the peninsula.

The visit is Wang's first official trip to South Korea since September 2021, and comes just months after President Lee made a state visit to China in January 2026, meeting with President Xi Jinping. As [Yonhap reported](https://en.yna.co.kr/view/AEN20260819000800315), South Korean Foreign Ministry spokesperson Park Doo-soon said the talks are expected to serve as "a chance to review the achievements made in fully restoring South Korea-China relations, and to begin preparations in earnest for the next steps and high-level exchanges ahead of the APEC summit slated for November in Shenzhen."

## Korean War Peace Initiative in Focus

Wang's arrival comes days after President Lee proposed multiparty dialogue to formally end the 1950-53 Korean War in his Liberation Day speech on August 15. The war ended with an armistice, leaving the two Koreas technically at war. Lee's proposal was widely seen as reinforcing his vision for improving inter-Korean relations and Seoul's efforts to serve as a "pacemaker" in helping resume dialogue between the North and the United States.

"As the president has laid out his vision for peaceful coexistence on the Korean Peninsula, discussions are expected to take place on this matter," a South Korean foreign ministry official said of the upcoming Cho-Wang meeting, suggesting that the proposed multiparty dialogue may be on the agenda.

Wang's visit also follows his trip to North Korea in April 2026 — his first in six years — which scholars viewed as Beijing attempting to restore influence over Pyongyang, as [Lianhe Zaobao reported](https://www.zaobao.com.sg/news/china/story20260729-9437000). The North has remained largely unresponsive to Seoul's rapprochement efforts since Lee took office in June 2025.

## Geopolitical Context: Shifting Alliances

The visit coincides with significant shifts in the US-South Korea alliance. According to [Deutsche Welle](https://www.dw.com/en/south-korea-us-shorten-military-drills/a-78421117), President Trump has ordered a substantial reduction in US participation in joint military drills with South Korea, with the Ulchi Freedom Shield exercises cut short after Trump's directive. Trump has also been pursuing dialogue with North Korea's Kim Jong-un, with the APEC summit in Shenzhen discussed as a possible occasion for a meeting, as [DW reported](https://www.dw.com/en/trump-north-korea-united-states/a-78413321).

These developments create a complex backdrop for Wang's visit, as Beijing seeks to strengthen its position in Northeast Asia while Washington's security commitments to the region appear to be evolving.

## Indonesia: New Strategic Dialogue Mechanisms

Following his Seoul stop, Wang will travel to Indonesia on August 21-22 at the invitation of Indonesian Foreign Minister Sugiono. During the visit, Wang will chair the first meeting of the China-Indonesia Comprehensive Strategic Dialogue mechanism and, together with Defense Minister Dong Jun, attend the second ministerial meeting of the China-Indonesia Foreign and Defense Ministers "2+2" dialogue mechanism.

The "2+2" mechanism was first established in April 2025, when the inaugural meeting was held in Beijing — the first such dialogue between China and a foreign country. As [Global Times](https://www.globaltimes.cn/page/202504/1332550.shtml) reported at the time, Wang noted that the mechanism reflects the high level of strategic mutual trust between the two countries and enriches the connotation of a China-Indonesia community with a shared future.

The deepening security cooperation comes amid questions about Indonesia's nonaligned posture. In August, an Indonesian frigate briefly joined a naval exercise with China's PLA Navy in waters off Taiwan's east coast, drawing scrutiny from Taipei. As [Deutsche Welle reported](https://www.dw.com/en/indonesia-china-naval-drill-military-neutrality-taiwan-geopolitics/a-78400288), the exercise raised concerns about Indonesia's "free and active" foreign policy, though Jakarta stressed the vessel's presence was simply part of its transit route back from Russia.

## Ahead of the APEC Summit

Wang's broader tour is widely seen as preparation for the APEC Economic Leaders' Meeting scheduled for November 18-19 in Shenzhen, China — the third time China will host the event. As [China Daily](https://www.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202608/10/WS6a79f4c2a310986e2b469fd5.html) reported, Shenzhen has been strengthening economic links with APEC economies across maritime logistics, business expansion, and international consumption ahead of the summit.

## What to Watch

The visit will be closely watched for signals on several fronts: whether China signals support for Lee's Korean War peace initiative, how Beijing navigates the evolving US-South Korea alliance dynamics, and the trajectory of China-Indonesia security cooperation. With the 35th anniversary of Seoul-Beijing diplomatic relations approaching next year, and the APEC summit serving as a potential venue for high-level meetings, Wang's four-day tour could set the tone for regional diplomacy in the months ahead.
