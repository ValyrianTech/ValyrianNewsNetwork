# China Mourns Passing of 'People's Artist' Guo Lanying at 97

China is bidding farewell to one of its most beloved cultural icons. Guo Lanying, the legendary opera performer and vocalist best known for her iconic rendition of "My Motherland," died of illness on August 11, 2026, in Guangzhou, Guangdong Province, at the age of 97. The [China National Opera and Dance Drama Theatre](https://www.news.cn/politics/20260812/72aa5d5a285b4e51bbf77d5979e8d0ad/c.html) issued an official obituary early Wednesday morning, confirming that per Guo's final wishes, no mourning hall, funeral ceremony, or farewell ceremony will be held, and all funeral arrangements will be conducted in a simple manner.

## A Voice That Defined a Nation

Guo Lanying was far more than a singer—she was a cultural touchstone whose voice accompanied generations of Chinese people through decades of national transformation. Her performance of "My Motherland" (《我的祖国》), the theme song of the 1956 film "Shangganling," became one of the most recognizable musical phrases in modern Chinese culture. The song's opening line, "A great river with wide waves" (一条大河波浪宽), has been sung at national celebrations, memorial events, and cultural gatherings for over seven decades.

The song's enduring resonance was captured in a [Zhihu community poll](https://www.news.cn/politics/20260812/780b6aa7954a4f6a83fcf49515a7953c/c.html) asking which song since the founding of New China makes people tear up when they hear it—"My Motherland" ranked first among nearly 15,000 responses. As [Global Times](https://www.globaltimes.cn/page/202608/1368027.shtml) noted, Guo earned national acclaim not only for this anthem but also for immortalizing songs such as "Nanniwan" and "People Say Shanxi Has Beautiful Scenery," which have been sung across China for generations.

## From Poverty to National Icon

Guo's remarkable journey from abject poverty to national icon is a story of extraordinary resilience. Born on December 31, 1930, in Pingyao, Shanxi Province, into a poor tenant farming family with twelve children, she was sold at age four to a local Jin opera troupe in exchange for grain to feed her family. Despite brutal conditions—including a severe illness at age 11 that nearly killed her—she became a renowned young Jin opera star by age 13, earning the nickname "Little Mei Lanfang of Jin Opera."

Her life took a pivotal turn in 1945 when she witnessed a performance of the revolutionary opera "The White-Haired Girl" and was deeply moved, deciding to join the revolution. According to [CCTV](https://people.cctv.com/2026/08/12/ARTINKpHlMST2ThRVpGlLQBL260812.shtml), when she reported to the literary troupe, she told the leader: "Comrade, I joined the revolution because I want to play Xi'er, because Xi'er was written about me." She first performed the role of Xi'er in 1947 in Shijiazhuang, achieving great success and establishing herself as a foundational figure in Chinese national opera.

In 1956, after multiple other famous singers had failed to achieve the desired effect, Guo recorded "My Motherland" for the film "Shangganling." Her simple, heartfelt delivery instantly captivated audiences—the song became a national sensation even before the film's release. She went on to create iconic roles in "The Marriage of Little Er Hei" and performed "Nanniwan" in the 1964 music and dance epic "The East Is Red." In 1963, she held her first solo vocal concert, pioneering the genre of solo national vocal music performances in China.

## A Life Devoted to Education

After retiring from the stage in 1982, Guo dedicated herself to nurturing the next generation of Chinese vocal talent. In 1986, at age 56, she and her husband Wan Zhaoyuan used their life savings to establish the Guo Lanying Art School in Panyu, Guangdong, on a barren hillside. The school provided free education to underprivileged children from mountain areas, and Guo insisted that graduates spend three months performing in rural areas to stay connected with the people.

Her teaching philosophy emphasized "learning art before learning to be a person." As [Sohu](https://www.sohu.com/a/1061766913_122543251) reported, Guo once said: "Heaven didn't give me biological children, so I'll nurture thousands of children who love to sing. Artistic inheritance is another form of bloodline continuation." Many of China's most celebrated contemporary singers—including Wu Bixia, Wang Hongwei, Abao, and Wang Erni—received guidance from her. In October 1994, she donated the copyright of her performances to the state.

Even at age 90, Guo took the stage of CCTV's Spring Festival Gala in 2020, delivering a heartfelt performance of "My Motherland," as Global Times reported. In 2019, she received the "People's Artist" national honorary title—one of China's highest honors for cultural figures—along with the "Most Beautiful Striver" award and a lifetime achievement recognition in theatre from the China Federation of Literary and Art Circles. She also served as a deputy to the 1st, 2nd, 3rd, 5th, and 6th National People's Congresses.

## Tributes Pour In

News of Guo's passing has prompted an outpouring of grief across China. Li Yugang, a singer at the China National Opera and Dance Drama Theatre, posted a tribute on Weibo: "One song of the great river echoes through the ages, a lifetime of steadfast art for the people. She pioneered and laid the foundation for Chinese national opera and vocal music. Her virtue and art endure forever."

Yang Xianrang, an artist and lifelong friend who worked with Guo since 1948, wrote: "She gave up her flourishing Jin opera career to join the revolution and never complained about hardship. Later she sold her assets to establish a school, spending decades supporting underprivileged children without seeking any return. She had no children of her own, but gave all her warmth to art, to students, and to ordinary people. She truly deserves the title 'virtuous and artistically accomplished.' She left quietly, but her voice and spirit will never disappear."

Guo's younger sister, Guo Zhuangying, the only surviving sibling, told reporters: "My sister's life was too hard. She left home at age four for the opera troupe, spent her life performing, never got the child she wanted, and lived alone in Guangzhou in her later years. At least her students were often with her. Now she's gone, no longer suffering from illness. It's a release."

## An Enduring Legacy

Guo Lanying's contributions to Chinese cultural heritage are immeasurable. As [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guo_Lanying) notes, she was a noted Chinese operatic soprano who pioneered the integration of traditional Chinese opera techniques with modern vocal forms, helping establish the Chinese national opera performance system and aesthetic paradigm. Her work bridged the gap between traditional folk arts and contemporary musical expression, creating a uniquely Chinese vocal tradition that continues to influence performers today.

Her song "My Motherland" remains a symbol of Chinese patriotism and national identity—a musical thread connecting generations. As she herself once said: "This song completely represents my heart. Without the motherland, there would be no Guo Lanying." And in her own words about her artistic philosophy: "Melt folk elements and the thoughts and feelings of the people into our singing. This is art-life, life-art. It comes from the people, and we return art to the people."

Guo Lanying may have left quietly, without grand ceremonies, but her voice—that unmistakable, warm, and deeply human sound—will continue to echo through the ages. As the lyrics of her most famous song proclaim: "On this ancient land, everywhere there is the power of youth. On this warm land, everywhere there is the light of peace." Her legacy, like the great river she sang about, flows on.
