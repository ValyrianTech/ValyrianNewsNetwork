# US Senators Demand Answers From Antwerp Diamond Dealers Over Trump Ring Gift

Two Democratic US senators have demanded answers from Belgium's Antwerp diamond industry over an 18-karat gold ring encrusted with 321 diamonds and 75 gemstones that was gifted to President Donald Trump — warning that the gift, which preceded a favorable tariff decision by nearly four weeks, "looks like bribery." Senators Elizabeth Warren (D-Mass.) and Richard Blumenthal (D-Conn.) sent a letter on August 10 to the Antwerp World Diamond Centre (AWDC) and jeweler David Gotlib, who designed the ring, demanding details about who conceived the gift, who approved it, and what it cost.

## The Ring and Its Controversial Timing

The ring, described as "watch-sized," features a gem-encrusted presidential seal, diamond-encrusted letter 'T', the numbers 45 and 47 (referencing Trump's two terms), and the Superman logo. Its interior engraving reads "Crafted in Antwerp for Donald John Trump." Designed by Antwerp jeweler David Gotlib and certified by HRD Antwerp diamond laboratory, the ring was inspired by American Super Bowl rings, according to [HLN](https://www.hln.be/binnenland/antwerpse-diamantsector-schenkt-ring-met-321-diamanten-aan-trump~a8b50d6df/).

Two independent jewelers estimated the ring's value at $25,000 to $35,000 (€22,000 to €30,000), according to the Associated Press.

The ring was presented on June 28 by Isidore Mörsel, president of the AWDC, to US Ambassador to Belgium Bill White during celebrations of the 250th anniversary of American independence at Jubelpark in Brussels. Trump thanked his "friends from Antwerp" for the "magnificent Freedom 250 ring" in a video message during the event.

Nearly four weeks later, on July 24, the Trump administration officially exempted natural European diamonds from new Section 301 tariffs. The timing has raised alarm bells among Democratic lawmakers.

## Senators Raise Bribery Concerns

In their letter, Warren and Blumenthal warned that those responsible could face criminal penalties under US federal anti-bribery law. They also noted that the AWDC's public-private partnership with the Belgian government could trigger the Emoluments Clause of the US Constitution, which forbids federal officers from receiving gifts from foreign states without Congress' consent, as reported by [Common Dreams](https://www.commondreams.org/news/trump-belgium-ring-bribe).

"At best, your gift to President Trump amidst high-stakes tariff decisions creates the glaring appearance of impropriety," the senators wrote. "At worst, it reinforces that foreign corporate special interests can buy lucrative tariff exemptions simply by showering President Trump with shiny gifts."

Warren amplified the concerns on social media, tweeting: "June 28: Belgium's diamond industry gifted Trump an 18-karat gold ring encrusted with 321 diamonds and 75 gemstones. July 24: European diamonds got their exemption from Trump's tariffs. I'm investigating what looks like cartoonish corruption."

The senators have given Mörsel and Gotlib until August 21 to respond to a series of questions, including who came up with the idea for the gift, who approved it, how much it cost, whether it was paid for by the AWDC, member companies, or external donors, and whether any discussions about tariffs were held with the Trump administration prior to the exemption announcement.

## White House Denies Connection

White House spokesperson Kush Desai denied that the gift influenced Trump's decision, saying the administration "agreed to provide preferential tariff treatment for diamonds as part of our historic trade deal with the European Union that was signed last summer." He added that "the only special interest guiding the Trump administration's decision-making is the best interest of the American people."

However, [HLN](https://www.hln.be/binnenland/amerikaanse-senatoren-eisen-uitleg-van-antwerpse-diamantairs-die-18-karaats-gouden-ring-aan-trump-schonken-dit-lijkt-op-omkoping~a9dee32e/) reports that the trade deal statement of August 21, 2025 does not mention diamonds. The diamond sector received a tariff exemption in September 2025, but that exemption disappeared in February 2026 when the US Supreme Court struck down the legal basis for Trump's tariffs. A 10 percent surcharge was imposed, and diamonds were only added to the exception list on July 23, 2026 — nearly four weeks after Trump received the ring.

## A Broader Pattern of Gifts and Tariff Concessions

The senators argue the ring fits into a broader pattern. According to [HLN's reporting on Swiss gifts](https://www.hln.be/buitenland/zwitserland-stuurt-rolex-klok-en-gepersonaliseerde-goudstaaf-van-1-kilo-naar-witte-huis-trump-verlaagt-heffingen-van-39-naar-15-procent~a0065c95/), Swiss executives, including Rolex CEO Jean-Frédéric Dufour, gifted Trump a personalized gold bar worth over $130,000 and a luxury Rolex desk clock in November 2025. Days later, tariffs on Swiss products were slashed from 39 percent to 15 percent.

Apple CEO Tim Cook donated $1 million to Trump's inauguration committee and gave a 24-karat gold gift to secure exemptions for Apple products. Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang purchased a Mar-a-Lago table for $1 million, after which the administration reversed course on rules that would have prevented Nvidia from selling advanced chips to China.

The Antwerp ring would be the fourth case this year where a foreign gift preceded a favorable decision on import tariffs.

## Economic Stakes for Antwerp

Belgium exported $2.1 billion (€1.8 billion) in polished diamonds to the United States in 2024. At a 10 percent tariff, importers would avoid paying about $200 million annually under the exemption. The US is the most important export market for Antwerp's natural diamonds, a sector already under pressure from competition with cheaper synthetic diamonds, the boycott of Russian diamonds, and tariff uncertainty.

The AWDC celebrated the tariff exemption as a "significant boost" for Antwerp's diamond industry and "its international competitive position." The sector argues that the US has no domestic diamond mining or cutting industry needing protection.

## Additional Controversy: The Friedman Complaint

The ring has also become entangled in a separate controversy. Moshe Friedman, a self-proclaimed Antwerp rabbi whose status is disputed by recognized Jewish institutions, filed a complaint against Trump in July, claiming the ring was used to pressure the Belgian investigation into ritual circumcisions. Friedman called the gift "a clear case of bribery" in an interview with HUMO magazine, as reported by [HLN](https://www.hln.be/binnenland/antwerpse-rabbijn-dient-klacht-in-tegen-trump-nadat-diamantsector-hem-ring-met-321-diamanten-schonk-regelrechte-omkoping~a1881ab5/).

US Ambassador Bill White has called Friedman "a fraudster without a mandate" and urged media to describe him as "an individual activist who speaks only for himself."

## Legal and Constitutional Questions

An administration official noted that the diamond ring remains federal property, adding that if the president intends to retain it, he must pay the government the full market price. The senators' letter also raises the Emoluments Clause issue, given the AWDC's close ties to the Belgian government.

## What to Watch For

Mörsel and Gotlib face an August 21 deadline to respond to the senators' questions. Their answers — or lack thereof — could determine whether this escalates into a formal congressional investigation. The broader pattern of gifts preceding tariff decisions is likely to draw continued scrutiny from Democratic lawmakers, who have long conducted oversight on what they describe as Trump family profiteering.

For the Antwerp diamond industry, the stakes are significant. A prolonged controversy could threaten the very tariff exemption the sector worked so hard to secure, potentially costing Belgian exporters hundreds of millions of dollars annually.
