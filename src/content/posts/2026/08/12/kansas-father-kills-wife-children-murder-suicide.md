# Kansas Father Kills Wife and 4 Children in Murder-Suicide

A 53-year-old Kansas man fatally shot his wife and four young children before calling 911 to confess and then turning the gun on himself in an apparent murder-suicide on Tuesday, according to [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/us-news/kansas-father-kills-4-children-wife-turning-gun-authorities-say-rcna592049). The incident occurred at a home in the 400 block of West 9th Avenue in Winfield, Kansas, approximately 43 miles southeast of Wichita.

## The Incident

At approximately 8:50 a.m. Tuesday, Winfield Police received a 911 call from a man who identified himself as the shooter and stated he had killed his entire family and was preparing to kill himself, Police Chief Robbie DeLong said at a news conference.

"At approximately 8:50 a.m., our officers received a 911 call from a male individual who stated that he had killed his entire family and he was getting ready to kill himself," DeLong said.

Officers who were already at an unrelated car crash two blocks away were dispatched to the scene. The first officer to arrive heard a single gunshot and retreated. A perimeter was established, and a regional SWAT team and Cowley County sheriff's deputies joined the standoff. Shortly before 11 a.m., authorities breached the home and found all six family members dead from gunshot wounds.

The deceased were identified as Ronald H. Williams Sr., 53; his wife Kelly L. George, 44; and their four children: Carol A. Williams, 9; Ronald H. Williams Jr., 7; Sarah K. Williams, 5; and Kelly M. McGee Williams, 3. Authorities believe Williams killed the five family members before placing the 911 call and died from a self-inflicted gunshot wound shortly after law enforcement arrived.

"I want to make something very clear to the community," DeLong said. "This was an isolated incident. There is no ongoing threat."

## Prior Police Contact and Criminal History

DeLong said police had contact with Williams as recently as the last few weeks and that officers had responded to the area based on reports of gunfire but were unsuccessful in finding evidence. Police had received calls about "erratic behavior" and "aggressive behavior," including confronting and yelling at people in public.

"We have calls of just aggressive behavior, but not enough really to act on," DeLong said. "I can't say that anything would have indicated that we would be where we are today."

Williams was a registered sex offender, according to the state sex offender registry. In September 1998, a judge accepted guilty pleas to two felony counts of attempted indecent liberties with a child and one count of battery involving the same victim, a 15-year-old girl, according to Kansas criminal court records reported by [KCTV](https://www.kctv5.com/2026/08/12/winfield-man-was-registered-sex-offender-charged-with-multiple-felonies-eastern-kansas-before-killing-family/). His registration was due to expire in July 2028.

Court records also revealed Williams had aliases including Freddie Williams, Laron Williams, and Jerome Williams, and faced felony charges for drugs, battery, assault on a law enforcement officer, burglary, attempted rape, and kidnapping spanning incidents from 1995 to 2024 across Wyandotte, Johnson, and Franklin counties in Kansas.

## Investigation Underway

The Kansas Bureau of Investigation (KBI) has joined the probe, with Special Agent in Charge Jason Diaz leading the investigation. A key goal is identifying a motive, with agents interviewing relatives and neighbors.

"I believe when he called 911 that he was accurate in what he said, and when officers arrived on scene, that final gunshot wound was a self-inflicted one," Diaz said. "This is a very difficult case for the agents and the crime scene personnel that are working it."

## Community Shock and Grief

The tragedy has deeply shaken Winfield, a small community where residents expressed disbelief and heartbreak. Neighbors interviewed by [KWCH](https://www.kwch.com/2026/08/12/somebody-failed-winfield-residents-grapple-with-grief-questions-after-family-murdered/) described the shock of learning that an entire family had been lost.

"I heard what happened, and once I heard it was a whole family and children, it just made my stomach churn, actually," said Ann Stofferan, a neighbor. "And I just felt so sad and heartbroken for that family. Because I just can't imagine what that man was thinking to do that. I just can't."

Tonya Asbury, who lives a block away, expressed frustration over whether the tragedy could have been prevented. "I hate to say it, but somebody failed. Somebody in this community failed that family. Failed him and that family."

In response to the violence, the Youth Peace & Justice Foundation (also known as the Uvalde Foundation For Kids) said it is extending its Community Crisis Response Initiative to Winfield, offering 24-hour crisis support, professional counseling connections, trauma-informed support for children and families, and support for educators and first responders, as [reported by KWCH](https://www.kwch.com/2026/08/12/organization-offering-support-winfield-community-following-familys-murder/).

"The first hours and days after a tragedy can be extremely difficult," said Daniel Chapin, Founder and National Director of the organization. "Children may be frightened or confused. Families may be struggling to process what happened. Teachers, first responders, and others helping the community may also need support. Our message to Winfield is simple: people should not have to navigate the aftermath of a tragedy alone."

## Second Familicide in Two Weeks

According to [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/aug/12/kansas-familicide-winfield), this incident marks the second familicide in the Midwest in two weeks. On July 28, Kristopher Karolkiewicz, 47, fatally shot his wife Amanda, 39, and their six children (ages 5 to 15) in Grand Haven Township, Michigan, before setting their home on fire and killing himself.

The Michigan mass murder was the 13th in the US this year, according to the nonpartisan Gun Violence Archive. Tuesday's killings in Kansas raised the total to 15, with at least nine of those fitting the definition of "family annihilation" — a type of crime most commonly committed by an armed male on multiple close family members.

## What's Next

As the KBI continues its investigation, questions remain about what motivated the killings, whether there were prior domestic violence reports involving the family, and how Williams legally obtained firearms given his criminal history. Chief DeLong noted that in his more than 20 years of service, he could not recall anything remotely close to this tragedy.

"In my 20-plus years here, no, I can't remember anything remotely close to this," he said.

The community of Winfield now faces the difficult task of healing from a loss that has left more questions than answers. As support organizations move in and investigators continue their work, residents are left to grapple with the aftermath of a tragedy that has shaken the very foundation of their small town.
