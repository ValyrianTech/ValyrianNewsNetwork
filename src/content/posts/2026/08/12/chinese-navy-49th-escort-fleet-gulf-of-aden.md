# Chinese Navy's 49th Escort Fleet Sets Sail for Gulf of Aden

The Chinese Navy's 49th escort fleet departed from a military port in Sanya, Hainan on the morning of August 12, heading to the Gulf of Aden and Somali waters to relieve the 48th escort fleet, according to [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/politics/20260812/5ca952f5bcec44caae1db2a9f26cce5b/c.html). The deployment continues China's uninterrupted naval escort presence in the region since 2008.

At approximately 10:30 AM local time, accompanied by military music, the fleet's ships untied one by one and slowly departed the dock. On the decks, escort personnel stood in orderly formation, waving farewell to their homeland and loved ones.

## Fleet Composition

The 49th escort fleet is primarily composed of forces from the Southern Theater Command Navy, including:

- **Kunming (hull 172)**: A Type 052D guided-missile destroyer and lead ship of its class, undertaking its first escort mission
- **Yueyang (hull 575)**: A Type 054A guided-missile frigate
- **Luomahu (hull 907)**: A Type 903A large ocean-going comprehensive supply ship

The fleet carries two ship-borne helicopters and dozens of special operations personnel. All three warships were independently designed and built by China, as [China News Service](https://www.chinanews.com.cn/gn/2026/08-12/10676266.shtml) reported.

## Mission Preparation

During the mission preparation phase, the fleet command closely monitored potential risks and actual threats that the escort operation might face. In accordance with full-element combat training requirements, the fleet focused on targeted exercises including live-fire drills, accompanying escort procedures, and underway replenishment operations.

## Continuing a Two-Decade Mission

The deployment marks the 49th Chinese naval escort fleet to operate in the region since the program began in December 2008, when China's first escort fleet departed from Sanya for the Gulf of Aden and Somali waters. The mission was China's first use of military force for overseas operations since the founding of the People's Republic.

In line with relevant United Nations Security Council resolutions, China has maintained a regular naval escort presence in the region, escorting over 7,000 Chinese and foreign vessels and assisting ships in distress, as [China Diplomacy](https://en.chinadiplomacy.org.cn/2026-08/11/content_118642883.shtml) noted. Chinese naval ships have also participated in evacuations of Chinese citizens and foreign nationals from war-torn countries.

The previous rotation, the 48th escort fleet, departed from Qingdao, Shandong on October 11, 2025, and was composed of the missile destroyer Tangshan, missile frigate Daqing, and supply ship Taihu. That fleet was primarily drawn from the Northern Theater Command Navy, while the current deployment marks a rotation to the Southern Theater Command Navy, as [Lianhe Zaobao](https://www.zaobao.com.sg/news/china/story20260811-9502135) reported.

## Strategic Significance

The deployment underscores China's ongoing commitment to international maritime security and its role in protecting vital shipping lanes in the region. The announcement of the deployment was made by China's Ministry of National Defense on August 11, which stated that the fleet would depart in mid-August.

In its statement, the ministry emphasized that the Chinese navy is "a firm builder and defender of the concept of a maritime community with a shared future," always upholding the principles of peace, development, cooperation, and win-win, and contributing China's strength to safeguarding the security of international maritime passages and maintaining regional peace and stability, as [Xinhua reported](https://www.news.cn/politics/20260811/02dc71372522432fa7c61f36cbe32fbc/c.html).

The Kunming destroyer's participation marks a significant milestone as the lead ship of the Type 052D class undertakes its first escort mission, reflecting the PLA Navy's continued modernization and expanding operational experience with its newest vessel classes.

## What's Next

The 49th escort fleet will now transit to the Gulf of Aden and Somali waters to relieve the 48th escort fleet, which is expected to return to China after the handover. The new fleet will conduct escort missions for approximately 4-6 months before being relieved by the 50th fleet.

Observers will be watching how the fleet's presence contributes to maritime security in a region that remains critical to global trade, as well as how China continues to expand its naval capabilities and international maritime engagement through these ongoing deployments.
