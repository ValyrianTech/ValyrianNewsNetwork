# C919 Launches First International Route to Ulaanbaatar

China's domestically-produced C919 aircraft is set to make history on August 12, 2026, as Air China flight CA723 departs Beijing Capital International Airport for Ulaanbaatar, Mongolia — marking the first international commercial flight for the country's homegrown narrow-body jet. The daily service, which covers approximately 724 miles (1,166 km) with a flight time of about two hours and fifteen minutes, represents a significant milestone in China's efforts to compete in the global commercial aviation market.

According to [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/politics/20260812/2d74e89fee6448f6881c7aaad09a4e22/c.html), the CA723 flight will depart Beijing in the mid-afternoon and arrive in the Mongolian capital just over two hours later, with the return flight CA724 departing Ulaanbaatar in the early evening. The aircraft on this route features a two-class configuration with eight business class and 150 economy seats, replacing the Boeing 737 MAX 8 previously used on this route, as [Simple Flying](https://simpleflying.com/china-comac-c919-begin-1st-ever-international-route/) reported.

## A Milestone for China's Aviation Ambitions

The C919, developed by the state-owned Commercial Aircraft Corporation of China (COMAC), is China's first domestically-developed jet airliner built according to international airworthiness standards with independent intellectual property rights. The program was launched in 2008 as a direct competitor to the Airbus A320 and Boeing 737 families, representing Beijing's ambition to break into a market long dominated by the Western duopoly.

The aircraft completed its first commercial flight on May 28, 2023, with China Eastern Airlines, and has since expanded rapidly across domestic routes. As of August 10, 2026, C919 aircraft operated by Air China, China Eastern Airlines, and China Southern Airlines have opened 57 routes, served 25 cities, and safely transported over 7.5 million passengers, according to data cited by [IT Home](https://www.ithome.com/0/988/229.htm).

Air China currently operates 12 C919 aircraft and has opened 11 domestic routes with the type, making this its 12th C919 route. While the airline already operates the C919 daily on the Beijing-Hong Kong route, that service is classified as regional since Hong Kong is a Special Administrative Region of China. The Ulaanbaatar route marks the first truly international commercial service for the aircraft, as [VnExpress International](https://e.vnexpress.net/news/news/traffic/china-made-c919-aircraft-set-for-first-international-commercial-flight-next-month-5099401.html) noted.

## Strategic Significance and Expert Perspectives

The choice of Ulaanbaatar as the first international destination is widely seen as a calculated, manageable first step. The relatively short route allows the C919 to prove itself in cross-border operations without the complexity of a long-haul international service. However, analysts caution against overinterpreting the milestone.

"This is essentially a public relations move," said Scott Kennedy, senior adviser at the Centre for Strategic and International Studies, as reported by [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/aug/12/comac-c919-chinese-passenger-jet-china-plane-first-international-flight). "Ulaanbaatar was likely chosen because of how close it is to Beijing. It doesn't change the fact that the plane was years overdue, is composed of western components, and is less efficient than its Boeing and Airbus cousins."

Kyle Chan, a fellow at Brookings focused on China's industrial policy, emphasized the immense difficulty of the undertaking. "Large passenger aircraft and jet engines are extremely complex and challenging to manufacture," Chan said. "There's a reason there are basically only two major commercial aircraft makers in the world. Building large passenger jets that are safe and reliable is an order of magnitude more difficult than making EVs."

## Certification Challenges and Trade War Context

The C919 has not yet been approved by European (EASA) or American (FAA) regulators, which limits its ability to operate internationally outside China. The aircraft received its type certificate from the Civil Aviation Administration of China on September 29, 2022, as documented on [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comac_C919). The European Union Aviation Safety Agency has begun test flights of the C919 in Shanghai as a key step toward securing Western safety certification.

The program has also been caught up in US-China trade tensions. In 2025, the US reportedly suspended the export of some products to COMAC for use in the C919 amid tit-for-tat restrictions during the trade war. The two countries later agreed to a truce set to expire in November 2026. When US President Donald Trump visited Beijing in May 2026, one of the few takeaways was an agreement for China to buy 200 jets from Boeing, in return for US supply guarantees for aircraft engine parts and components.

## Production and Order Book

Despite the challenges, COMAC's order book remains robust. More than 1,100 C919 aircraft are on order from various airlines including Air China, China Eastern, China Southern, Hainan Airlines, Hebei Airlines, Lao Airlines, Sichuan Airlines, and Tibet Airlines, alongside various Asian-based aircraft lessors, according to Simple Flying. Approximately 40-44 C919 aircraft have been delivered to airlines, with more than 42,000 commercial flights recorded since the aircraft's introduction.

COMAC is also expanding the C919 family. The company is developing the C919-600, a shorter-fuselage high-altitude variant that recently completed its maiden test flight, and the C919-800, a stretched version seating 240 passengers, as [Topwar](https://en.topwar.ru/287394-kitaj-rasshirjaet-linejku-samoletov-c919-dlja-konkurencii-boeing-i-airbus.html) reported. These variants would bring the entire C919 family to a capacity range of 130 to 240 seats, directly competing with the Boeing 737 MAX and Airbus A320neo subfamilies.

## What's Next

China views building its own commercial aircraft as a national security priority. Zhang Yanzhong, an academic of the Chinese Academy of Engineering and a leading aviation expert who worked on the C919, said reliance on other countries for large aircraft technology would "threaten national strategic security," though he acknowledged there was still "a long way to go before achieving commercial success."

The launch of the Ulaanbaatar route represents a carefully calibrated step forward for China's aviation ambitions. As more C919 aircraft enter service and the fleet accumulates operational experience, observers will be watching closely to see whether this first international foray leads to broader expansion across Asia — or whether the aircraft's dependence on Western components and lack of international certification will continue to constrain its global aspirations.

For now, the sight of a Chinese-made narrow-body jet crossing an international border for the first time in commercial service marks a symbolic achievement for a program that has been decades in the making. As former civil aviation authority head Li Jiaxiang declared at the C919's unveiling in 2015: "A great nation must have its own large commercial aircraft."
