# Chinese Climber Confirmed Dead in Pakistan Avalanche

The Chinese Embassy in Pakistan has confirmed that a Chinese mountaineer was among the 10 climbers killed in a devastating avalanche on Broad Peak in Pakistan's Karakoram range, according to [Xinhua News Agency](https://www.news.cn/20260802/8f2c50995bb240649a1db59f5912d16f/c.html). The embassy activated its consular protection emergency mechanism and urged Pakistani authorities to make every effort in search and rescue operations.

## The Incident

On July 30, 2026, an avalanche struck a group of 10 summit-bound climbers on Broad Peak (8,051 meters), the world's 12th-highest mountain, between Camp 2 and Camp 3 at approximately 6,600 meters elevation. The multinational expedition included climbers from Nepal, Pakistan, Oman, the United States, China, and one other country, as [The Kathmandu Post](https://kathmandupost.com/national/2026/07/31/broad-peak-avalanche-what-we-know-so-far-about-nirmal-nimsdai-purja-and-the-missing-climbers-in-pakistan) reported.

The team, led by renowned Nepali-British climber Nirmal Purja, had last contacted their tour operator at approximately 9:00 AM that morning. GPS trackers recorded rapid falls of several hundred meters when the avalanche struck at around 9:30 AM local time.

## The Chinese Victim: Wang Zhong

The Chinese climber has been identified as Wang Zhong (王钟), a member of the Imagine Nepal trekking team. Wang, originally from Sichuan Province and residing in San Francisco, was pursuing the "Grand Slam" of climbing all 14 eight-thousanders — the world's peaks exceeding 8,000 meters. He had successfully summited 11 of the 14 peaks and was only two away (Broad Peak and Shishapangma) from completing the challenge.

Wang had recently completed Nanga Parbat on July 3, 2026, and Gasherbrum II in late July, according to the [Global Times](https://www.globaltimes.cn/page/202608/1367330.shtml). He had previously summited Mount Everest in 2021.

## Confirmation of All 10 Deaths

On August 1, Elite Exped, Purja's expedition company, confirmed that all 10 climbers had died. The deaths were also confirmed by the Alpine Club of Pakistan, as [AP News](https://apnews.com/article/pakistan-missing-climbers-search-resumed-broad-peak-2dae7b8a316072a82b84d27189795ec5) reported.

"The world has lost one of mountaineering's greatest climbers," Elite Exped said in a statement. "No words can ease the pain of this unimaginable loss."

The expedition comprised climbers from Nepal (5), Pakistan (1), Oman (1), United States (1), China (1), and one other foreign climber. Among the deceased were several accomplished mountaineers, including Pur Bahadur Gurung of Nepal, an IFMGA-certified guide with 12 eight-thousander summits; Nadhira Al Harthy of Oman, the first Omani woman to summit Mount Everest; and Mallory Geis of the United States, on her first 8,000-meter peak expedition.

## Search and Rescue Operations

Pakistan Army Aviation helicopters, drones, and ground teams were deployed for search and rescue operations. Three bodies were initially recovered on Friday — those of Al Harthy, Gurung, and Geis — before worsening weather conditions forced a suspension of operations. The search resumed on Saturday with additional Sherpas from other expeditions joining the rescue effort, as [BBC News](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cddjz1r01l8o) reported.

Teams on neighboring K2 aborted their summit pushes to assist in the search, demonstrating the solidarity of the mountaineering community in the face of tragedy.

## Chinese Government Response

The Chinese Embassy in Pakistan confirmed the incident and activated its consular protection emergency mechanism, urging Pakistani authorities to make every possible effort in the search and rescue operations while providing timely updates on progress, according to [People's Daily Online](http://world.people.com.cn/n1/2026/0802/c1002-40772201.html).

Chinese Foreign Ministry spokesperson Mao Ning stated on July 31 that China was "urgently verifying and understanding the situation" and would provide assistance to Chinese citizens in need, adding that China was willing to provide help based on Pakistan's needs.

## A Devastating Loss to Global Mountaineering

The disaster has had profound international impact. Most significantly, it claimed the life of Nirmal Purja, one of the most celebrated mountaineers in the world. Purja, a former British Army soldier and Royal Marines member, set a world record in 2019 by climbing all 14 eight-thousanders in just 189 days — an achievement featured in the Netflix documentary "14 Peaks: Nothing is Impossible." He had 57 eight-thousander summits, more than anyone else in the world, and was attempting to become the first person to climb all fourteen peaks three times.

Irfan Arshad Khan, president of the Alpine Club of Pakistan, paid tribute to the victims: "The loss of these courageous climbers, who came from different nations united by their passion for the mountains, is an immeasurable loss to the global mountaineering fraternity."

He added: "The mountains inspire us with their beauty, but they also remind us of the immense risks faced by those who venture into the high Himalaya and Karakoram."

## The Risks of High-Altitude Mountaineering

Accidents are common on climbing expeditions in northern Pakistan due to avalanches, falling ice and rock, high altitude, and rapidly changing weather conditions. Broad Peak, first successfully climbed in 1957, is considered one of the most challenging peaks for high-altitude expeditions. Its deadliest year was 2013, when at least six fatalities were recorded.

Conditions before this year's avalanche were particularly unstable. A climber who left the mountain earlier in the season reported a major wind storm on July 18, with high winds and elevated humidity throughout the region, indicating likely fresh snow through July 29 — conditions that created a high risk of avalanches.

The tragedy also underscores the disproportionate risks faced by Sherpa guides and local climbers who support international expeditions. Five of the ten victims were Nepali Sherpas, and one was a Pakistani guide — representing a significant loss to the mountaineering community, as [Outside Online](https://www.outsideonline.com/outdoor-adventure/exploration-survival/broad-peak-avalanche-missing-climbers/) noted.

## What's Next

Search and recovery operations continue on Broad Peak as authorities work to recover the remaining bodies and conduct identification procedures. The incident has drawn renewed attention to the dangers of high-altitude mountaineering and the growing participation of Chinese climbers in international expeditions.

For China, the loss of Wang Zhong represents a significant blow to its mountaineering community. His pursuit of the "Grand Slam" — completing all 14 eight-thousanders — was cut tragically short just two peaks from its conclusion.

The broader mountaineering world mourns the loss of some of its most accomplished and inspiring figures. As the [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Broad_Peak_avalanche) entry on the disaster notes, this was one of the deadliest mountaineering accidents in recent memory, and its impact will be felt across the global climbing community for years to come.
