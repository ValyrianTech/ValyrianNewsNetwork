# Hengyang Official Faces Discipline Over Parking Lock Damage

Authorities in Hengyang, Hunan Province, have announced that a public official who was captured on surveillance video damaging a parking space lock in a residential community will be "seriously handled according to regulations and discipline," according to [The Paper](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_33696810). The incident, which went viral on Chinese social media, has sparked public debate about official conduct and property rights.

## The Incident

The confrontation occurred on July 31 at approximately 4 PM in the underground parking garage of the Dacheng Commercial Residential Community in Qidong County, Hengyang. Surveillance footage shows a white SUV attempting to reverse into a parking space blocked by a lock. The driver, identified as Zhu (surname only), exited the vehicle and kicked the lock multiple times before attempting to pry it apart with his hands. He was about to retrieve an iron bar when a person in a white shirt arrived to stop him, according to the [original report by Shangyou News](https://www.wenxuecity.com/news/2026/08/01/socialnews-268104.html).

Zhu is a staff member of the Municipal Social Credit Information Center, a second-level institution under the Hengyang Development and Reform Commission. He also serves as deputy director of the community's owners' committee.

## Property Rights Dispute

The parking space in question belongs to Xiao, a shareholder of the original property developer. Xiao had rented the space to a foot massage shop, which installed the lock seven to eight months prior to protect customer parking rights. The owners' committee had previously engaged in multiple disputes with Xiao over parking space property rights.

Zhu claimed he was enforcing the owners' committee rule that "no one may install locks before parking space property rights are determined." However, Xiao disputed this justification, telling Shangyou News: "The property rights of this parking space are clear and have nothing to do with Zhu. Zhu previously parked elsewhere in the garage. On the day of the incident, the spots without locks were all full."

When contacted by reporters, Zhu said: "I didn't expect this to blow up so big. I'm now trying to find Mr. Xiao to talk. I'm not convenient to answer your questions."

## Police Intervention and Official Response

On August 1, after failed negotiations between Zhu and Xiao, the matter was reported to the Hongqiao Police Station. Police mediated the dispute, and Zhu and Ouyang—the individual who installed the lock—reached a settlement. However, Xiao rejected the mediation, stating: "I don't want him to compensate for the lock. I don't accept mediation. I hope the police handle this as property damage."

On August 2, the "Hengyang Release" WeChat public account issued an official notice confirming the investigation. The notice, reported by [China News](https://www.chinanews.com/sh/2026/08-02/10670760.shtml) and [Chongqing News](https://www.cqnews.net/web/content_1533451425759186944.html), stated that the Hengyang Development and Reform Commission and Qidong County had launched a joint investigation and that Zhu's "improper behavior and related issues will be seriously handled according to regulations and discipline."

## Public Reaction and Commentary

The incident drew significant attention on Weibo and WeChat after surveillance footage circulated widely online. Commentators questioned whether Zhu's position as a public official influenced his behavior. In a commentary published by [Jimu News on The Paper](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_33697613), columnist Wu Shuangjian wrote: "As a public official, one should take the lead in abiding by laws and regulations, establish a good image, and set an example for the masses. Respecting others' property rights is the most basic bottom line. How could one illegally damage others' property to occupy a parking space?"

The commentary further noted that Zhu's remorse appeared to stem from the incident's viral spread rather than from the act itself, observing that "he violated the law and damaged others' property, but his regret is not rooted in doing something wrong—it's that he didn't expect this to blow up so big."

## Broader Context

The case highlights ongoing challenges in Chinese residential communities where parking space ownership is contested between developers and homeowners' committees. It also reflects the role of social media as an accountability mechanism, with ordinary citizens using widely shared video footage to bring official misconduct to public attention.

Qidong County has seen previous incidents involving public officials' misconduct, including gambling cases in 2023 and a public official fighting in a barbershop while intoxicated in March 2026, suggesting a pattern of official conduct issues in the area.

## What's Next

Zhu faces likely disciplinary action from his employer and potentially from the Communist Party disciplinary system. If the property owner pursues the matter, Zhu could also face legal consequences for property damage under Chinese law. The case will be closely watched as a test of whether authorities can effectively address public concerns about official misconduct while maintaining public trust in governance.
