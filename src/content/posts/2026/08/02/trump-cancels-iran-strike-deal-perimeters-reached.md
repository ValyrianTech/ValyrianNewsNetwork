# Trump Cancels Iran Strike, Says Deal Perimeters Reached

President Donald Trump announced late Saturday that he agreed to cancel a planned large-scale military strike on Iran, claiming that Tehran and other Middle Eastern countries requested a halt because "the perimeters of a deal has been agreed to." The announcement came amid sharp escalation in the five-month US-Iran war that began on February 28, according to [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/world/20260802/03f910e129f64f469de8a65f7266ad77/c.html).

## A Sudden Reversal

In a Truth Social post on August 1, Trump said the US was "locked and loaded and ready to go against the Islamic Republic of Iran, at levels of Military Terror, Strength, and Power not seen since World War II." However, he stated that "Iran, and other Middle Eastern Countries" asked Washington to hold off because "the perimeters of a deal has been agreed to."

"Based on this request, I have agreed, for the future benefit of the WORLD and, likewise, the survival of a successful and prosperous Iran, to cancel the attack, subject to being able to rapidly make a DEAL," Trump wrote, as [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/08/02/nx-s1-5917113/trump-says-hes-cancelling-iran-strikes-deal-pending) reported. He added that the agreement would include the "Immediate, Complete, and Total OPENING OF THE HORMUZ STRAIT, and an end to Iran's nuclear threat," and stated that "The Country of Israel joins me in this commitment."

The reversal came just a day after Trump convened his Cabinet at Camp David, where he said the US would hit Iran "very hard," according to [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/02/trump-planned-attack-on-iran-canceled-after-reaching-outline-of-deal.html). US media had reported that Washington and Israel were preparing to strike Iran's energy infrastructure this weekend.

## Regional Pressure and Diplomatic Push

The decision followed intense diplomatic pressure from regional powers. Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman spoke with Trump on Saturday, emphasizing "the necessity of prioritizing dialogue to de-escalate tensions and the importance of exerting all possible efforts to achieve a truce...for preserving the security and stability of the region," according to a statement carried by Saudi Arabia's state news agency, as [The Jerusalem Post](https://www.jpost.com/middle-east/iran-news/article-904283) reported. Qatar, the UAE, Turkey, and Pakistan also called for de-escalation.

Qatari mediators held talks with Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi, US President's special envoy Steve Witkoff, and Omani officials, seeking to reach an agreement to restore normal shipping through the Strait of Hormuz. According to [TASS](https://tass.com/world/2168115), Israeli TV Channel 12 reported that Araghchi accepted a Qatari compromise proposal on resuming the strait's operation, which would see vessels entering the Persian Gulf pass through Iran's territorial waters while vessels exiting would use Omani waters.

## Iran's Response: Denial and Defiance

Iran has firmly denied Trump's claims. Mehr, a semi-official news agency sponsored by the Iranian government, dismissed Trump's assertions as "simply a new lie," citing unnamed Iranian military officials. Iran's Fars International called Trump's demands a "wish list," according to [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/2/trump-cancels-iran-strike-says-deal-perimeters-reached).

Iran's Acting Defense Minister Seyyed Majid Ibn Al-Reza said: "Although the enemy's recent statements are part of a psychological and cognitive warfare campaign, we consider every threat to be real and take it seriously. We will neither be caught off-guard nor remain passive."

Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi warned against any "adventurous action" by the US in calls with Turkish and Pakistani counterparts, stressing Iran's readiness to respond decisively to any "aggression." He told his Saudi counterpart that attacks would be met with a "proportionate response."

## The Broader Conflict

The war began on February 28, 2026, when the US and Israel launched airstrikes on Iran, killing several officials including Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei. Iran retaliated with missile and drone attacks and blocked the Strait of Hormuz in late March, disrupting global oil supplies through a waterway that carried about a fifth of the world's oil before the war, as [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Iran_war) documents.

A temporary ceasefire following a June 17 memorandum of understanding has effectively broken down. The US reimposed a naval blockade on Iranian ships on July 13, and both sides have continued trading strikes. The US completed a "heavy wave" of strikes against IRGC targets on July 29, and Iran attacked US bases in Kuwait and Bahrain. A drone hit two ships at Egypt's Damietta port on July 30, marking the first attack on Egyptian soil.

## Economic and Military Strain

The conflict has taken a heavy toll. The war has cost the US an estimated $113.3 billion as of June 2026, and the Trump administration has asked for $1.5 trillion for the Pentagon. US Patriot interceptor inventories have fallen from about 2,300 before the war to fewer than 827, according to the Center for Strategic and International Studies, as [Al Jazeera's explainer](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/2/why-has-trump-halted-iran-attacks-and-what-is-the-deal-he-is-hinting-at) noted. An AP poll found that almost two-thirds of Americans say the war is not worth the fight.

Oil markets remain volatile, with WTI futures closing at $84.67 per barrel on Friday and Brent at $90.12, according to [USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/world/2026/08/01/trump-cancels-new-iran-attack/91138887007/). US embassies in Iraq, Jordan, and the UAE have warned Americans to consider leaving the region.

## What's Next?

Analysts remain skeptical that the latest announcement will lead to a lasting resolution. Ross Harrison of the Middle East Institute told Al Jazeera that "from the Iranian perspective, they probably will believe that Donald Trump blinked because of the potential risk of escalation." He cautioned: "Unless there's some kind of clear agreement on opening the strait and removing the blockade, this isn't going to hold. The announcement we saw is only going to be another pause."

The key questions remain unresolved: Will a deal actually materialize? Can the Strait of Hormuz be reopened? Iran insists it will never return to the pre-war status quo. The previous June ceasefire collapsed over ambiguity in the memorandum, and without clear terms, this pause may prove equally fragile.

As [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/08/01/trump-iran-strikes-mideast-allies-emerging-strait-of-hormuz/) noted, the stakes for continued US military action are high for Trump and his party as the conflict, unpopular among many Americans, has jolted the world economy just months before critical midterm elections in November. The coming days will reveal whether this is a genuine diplomatic breakthrough or yet another temporary pause in a war that has already lasted five months.
