# Spain, France, UK: Climate Change Is Security Emergency

In an unprecedented coordinated diplomatic move, Spain, France and the United Kingdom issued joint statements on July 29 declaring that climate change has become a national security emergency facing Europe, directly threatening "our way of life." The declarations came as devastating wildfires continue to ravage the continent, forcing more than 300,000 people to evacuate in one of the largest fire-related displacements in European history.

![British Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband met Spain's Minister for Foreign Affairs, José Manuel Albares, on July 29, shortly after Miliband's appointment](https://j6n3r3q2.delivery.rocketcdn.me/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Miliband-meets-with-Spanish-Minister-of-Foreign-Affairs-Jose-Manuel-Albares-Bueno1-e1785422997236.jpg)

## A Continent on Fire

The joint statements follow one of the most severe wildfire seasons Europe has ever witnessed. Blazes near Madrid and Bordeaux have burned tens of thousands of hectares, destroyed hundreds of homes, and forced more than 300,000 people to flee, according to [Politico EU](https://www.politico.eu/article/spain-france-uk-warn-megafires-show-climate-change-is-threatening-our-way-of-life/). At least 13 people died in the Spanish village of Bédar in Almería, including British couple Pete and Fran Gillam, while three firefighters lost their lives in Greece.

The crisis unfolded against the backdrop of Europe's hottest June on record, followed by widespread drought conditions. In the UK, firefighters responded to 393 wildfires across England and Wales in July — the busiest month on record — including a major incident near the Sizewell B nuclear power station in Suffolk that burned over 80 hectares and forced hundreds to evacuate, as [The Guardian reported](https://www.theguardian.com/uk-news/2026/jul/30/major-incident-fire-suffolk-coast-dunwich-heath-leiston). Half of England was officially declared in drought, and Wales was on course for its driest July since records began nearly 200 years ago.

## The Joint Statements

UK Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband, recently appointed under new Prime Minister Andy Burnham, met with Spanish Foreign Minister José Manuel Albares in Madrid on July 29. The two ministers [issued a joint statement](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/joint-statement-by-the-ministers-of-foreign-affairs-of-the-united-kingdom-of-great-britain-and-northern-ireland-and-the-kingdom-of-spain-on-wildfires-) declaring that "this summer's wildfires demonstrated that climate change was now a national security emergency facing Europe and threatening our way of life."

In a [separate joint statement](https://www.diplomatie.gouv.fr/en/presse-et-ressources/decouvrir-et-informer/actualites/joint-statement-by-the-ministers-of-foreign-affairs-of-france-and-the-united-kingdom-of-great) with French Foreign Minister Jean-Noël Barrot, Miliband affirmed that "this summer's wildfires demonstrated the risks posed to Europe by climate change," calling on all countries to intensify efforts to tackle its causes while boosting resilience to its effects.

Both statements reaffirm support for the 2015 Paris Agreement and the goal of limiting global temperature rise to 1.5°C above pre-industrial levels. They call for drastically reducing dependence on fossil fuels, strengthening cooperation on clean energy, and supporting countries of the Global South in their energy transition.

"Decarbonisation, energy security, environmental protection and resilience are inseparable from our security and the prosperity and competitiveness of our economies," the UK-Spain statement read, as reported by [The Leader](https://theleader.info/2026/07/30/uk-and-spain-pledge-joint-action-on-wildfires-and-climate-change/).

## The Science Behind the Warning

A rapid attribution study by the [World Weather Attribution (WWA) consortium](https://www.worldweatherattribution.org/climate-change-increases-likelihood-of-compounding-drivers-of-severe-wildfire-conditions-in-france-and-spain/) found that man-made climate change made the fire weather conditions in Spain at least 20 times more likely and in France at least twice as likely. The analysis examined hot, dry and windy conditions during the worst fire week near Madrid and Bordeaux, comparing them to pre-industrial climate simulations.

"What we're seeing in France and Spain isn't just bad luck, it is a clear sign of the escalating impacts of anthropogenic warming," said Dr Clair Barnes, a climate scientist at Imperial College London and lead researcher on the analysis, as [Politico reported](https://www.politico.eu/article/climate-change-spain-france-fire-likely/). "Every fraction of a degree of warming pushes us into unknown territory, and these fires show exactly what that risk looks like."

A key driver was "weather whiplash" — a wet winter and spring boosted vegetation growth, which then dried out during summer heat, providing abundant fuel for fires. Global heating has raised temperatures by approximately 1.4°C above pre-industrial levels.

## UN Amplifies the Alarm

UN Secretary-General António Guterres warned that the "climate crisis is in overdrive" and that global heat seen so far is just a "warm up act" as El Niño intensifies "adding fuel to a planet already on fire," according to [Climate Change News](https://www.climatechangenews.com/2026/07/31/un-chief-warns-climate-crisis-in-overdrive-as-el-nino-threatens-to-fuel-the-fire/).

UN Climate Change Executive Secretary Simon Stiell said: "These European megafires show how fast climate-driven extreme heat and dry landscapes can turn wildfires into devastating national disasters." He added that "humanity continuing to burn colossal amounts of coal, oil and gas is baking our planet, making these conditions more dangerous, and these megafires more deadly and destructive."

## Human Cost and Questions of Accountability

The tragedy in Bédar has raised difficult questions about emergency preparedness. Danielle Kirton, daughter of British couple Pete and Fran Gillam who died in the wildfire, has called on the UK Foreign Office to press Spanish authorities for a full investigation. The evacuation order came more than three hours after the initial fire alert, and Spain's official emergency alert system was not used, [The Guardian reported](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/jul/31/spain-wildfire-bedar-british-couple-pete-fran-gillam-killed).

"My parents did not ignore instructions," Kirton said. "They took the correct route, and their only route, to the evacuation point. It is a weak and cowardly act to blame victims in the face of tragedy."

## Analysis: A New Chapter in European Climate Diplomacy

The joint statements represent a significant escalation in how climate change is being characterized at the highest levels of European government. By explicitly framing climate change as a "national security emergency," the three countries move the debate beyond environmental policy into the realm of collective security.

The political alignment is notable: the UK under a new Labour government, Spain under a socialist-led administration, and France under a centrist government have come together despite different political orientations. This signals broad consensus on climate as a security issue across the European political spectrum.

Miliband's appointment as Foreign Secretary and his immediate prioritization of climate diplomacy signals that the new UK government intends to make climate change a central pillar of its foreign policy. The UK-France statement also calls on all parties to publish nationally determined contributions (NDCs) aligned with the 1.5°C temperature goal — a pointed reminder that only three countries (UK, Nigeria, Norway) have submitted NDCs with 2035 emissions targets compatible with 1.5°C, while 52 countries have yet to submit an NDC at all.

The statements also intervene in a dispute over the timing of the IPCC's next flagship assessment report, calling it a "critical input" to the Second Global Stocktake, and commit to accelerating methane emissions reductions at COP31.

## What to Watch For

The coming months will test whether these joint statements translate into concrete policy action. Key questions include: Will the UK's new government balance climate finance commitments with military spending increases? Will the IPCC assessment report timing dispute be resolved? And how will the El Niño event expected between now and October affect the ongoing fire crisis?

As Julie Arrighi, Director of Programmes at Red Cross Red Crescent Climate Centre, warned: "What we are seeing now in Spain and France goes well beyond what we would expect from past events." The joint warnings from Europe's three major powers suggest that governments are beginning to treat climate change not as a distant threat, but as an immediate security challenge demanding urgent response.
