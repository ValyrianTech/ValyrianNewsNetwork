# Xiaomi Unveils Extended-Range EVs Amid Cooling Market

China's Xiaomi Corp. unveiled two new extended-range hybrid SUVs on Thursday, pushing into a shrinking segment of the world's largest auto market. The SUV series, dubbed SkyNomad, is scheduled for official release in September with pre-sale prices starting at roughly 259,900 yuan ($38,389), according to [Caixin](https://www.caixinglobal.com/2026-07-31/xiaomi-unveils-new-extended-range-evs-amid-cooling-market-102470286.html).

The launch marks Xiaomi's first foray into extended-range electric vehicles (EREVs), featuring large batteries designed to allow a week of pure-electric commuting with an internal combustion engine as a backup. CEO Lei Jun emphasized that the vehicles are "optimized for electric-only daily driving," highlighting the large battery capacity that allows a week of pure-electric commuting with the internal combustion engine as backup.

## The SkyNomad Lineup

The SkyNomad series, also known as Xiaomi Pengcheng in Chinese, comprises two models built on the company's newly unveiled "Kunlun Architecture" — a comprehensive platform covering vehicle space layout, powertrain systems, and safety capabilities. As Lei Jun explained, "This architecture comprehensively reconstructs vehicle space layout, powertrain systems, and safety capabilities, with the goal of redefining what a smart extended-range SUV should be," as reported by [Mdroid](https://mdroid.my/cn/2026/07/31/xiaomi-skynomad-n70-n90-max-china-launch/).

The flagship N90 Max is a 7-seat (2+2+3) full-size SUV measuring 5,285mm in length with a 3,080mm wheelbase. It features dual-motor all-wheel drive (210kW rear + 100kW front), a 76kWh ternary lithium battery, and a 1.5T range extender, delivering a CLTC combined range of up to 1,705km with a pure EV range of 505km. The N70 Max, positioned as a large 5-seat SUV, offers dual-motor AWD with 310kW total power and a claimed 505km pure EV range — the longest in the EREV segment — with a combined range of 1,461km.

Both models feature innovative interior designs including rotating front seats, flat-floor layouts, and modular cargo space, with a camping edition offering an electric pop-up tent.

## A Cooling Market

The launch comes at a challenging time for China's extended-range vehicle segment. Domestic retail sales of extended-range vehicles fell 19.4% year-on-year in the first half of 2026, according to [OFweek/帮宁工作室](https://nev.ofweek.com/2026-07/ART-71008-8420-30692864.html). In May 2026 specifically, pure EV retail sales reached 637,000 units (up 3.9% YoY — the only positive growth), while extended-range retail sales were only 85,000 units, down 28% YoY. Pure EV sales were 7.5 times that of extended-range vehicles.

Industry observers attribute the shift to improved charging infrastructure (22.5 million charging points as of May 2026, up 44.9% YoY), lower operating costs for pure EVs, better space utilization without engine components, and the end of purchase tax exemptions for new-energy vehicles at the end of 2025.

NIO President Qin Lihong has been notably vocal about the trend: "In Q1 2025, extended-range sales in the large 5-seat segment were 23x that of pure electric; by Q1 2026, the gap had narrowed to 2.2x. By Q3, the situation will reverse." Tsinghua University Professor Ouyang Minggao similarly argued that "pure electric is the most efficient way to utilize green electricity, and will push PHEV and EREV into a premature technology decline."

## Financial Pressures and Strategic Bet

Xiaomi's EV division posted a 3.1 billion yuan operating loss in Q1 2026, a sharp reversal from the 1.1 billion yuan profit in Q4 2025, as reported by [Caixin](https://www.caixinglobal.com/2026-03-25/xiaomi-ev-unit-posts-profit-but-smartphone-business-falters-102427311.html). The loss was attributed to a product transition period, a 44% drop in Q1 deliveries to 80,800 units, rising raw material costs, and heavy R&D investment.

The company delivered approximately 180,000 vehicles in H1 2026, with monthly sales stabilizing above 30,000 units in Q2. Xiaomi set a 2026 EV delivery target of 550,000 units — up 34% from 2025's 410,000 — as Lei Jun announced during a January livestream, according to [Caixin](https://www.caixinglobal.com/2026-01-05/xiaomi-targets-550000-ev-sales-in-2026-102400370.html). However, the company has only achieved about one-third of this target by mid-year.

The SkyNomad launch represents a direct challenge to Li Auto and AITO (Wenjie), the dominant players in China's extended-range SUV segment. At 299,900 yuan, the N90 Max undercuts both the Li Auto L9 and AITO M9 while offering comparable or better specifications, as [OFweek/互联网乱侃秀](https://nev.ofweek.com/2026-07/ART-71008-8420-30696565.html) noted.

## Safety Concerns Loom

The launch also comes amid heightened safety scrutiny following a highly publicized fatal crash and fire involving a Xiaomi vehicle earlier in 2026. According to [The Paper](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_32664749), a Chengdu crash in October 2025 involving a Xiaomi SU7 Ultra revealed that the vehicle's low-voltage system lost power 9 seconds after impact, disabling the electronic door handles and hindering rescue efforts. Xiaomi responded with safety upgrades in the revamped SU7 launched in March 2026, including mechanical emergency door release mechanisms.

## What's Next

The SkyNomad series is scheduled for official release in September 2026, with deliveries expected to follow. Industry analysts will be watching whether Xiaomi's brand recognition, fan base, and aggressive pricing can disrupt the extended-range SUV segment — even as the broader market shifts toward pure electric vehicles.

The key question remains whether Xiaomi can meet its ambitious 550,000-unit delivery target for 2026. With the SkyNomad launch potentially adding significant volume in Q4, and with the company's expanding product portfolio, the second half of the year will be critical for the tech giant's automotive ambitions.
