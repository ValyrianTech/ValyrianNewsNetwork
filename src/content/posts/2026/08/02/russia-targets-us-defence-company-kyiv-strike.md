# Russia Targets US Defence Company in Massive Kyiv Strike

Russia launched a massive coordinated attack on Kyiv in the early hours of August 1, firing 35 missiles and 185 drones at the Ukrainian capital in what officials described as one of the most devastating strikes of the conflict. The assault killed at least nine people and injured dozens more, while a Russian ballistic missile destroyed a drone factory owned by Terminal Autonomy, a US corporation registered in Delaware — marking the first time Moscow has targeted a US defence company in the war, according to [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/jul/30/us-firm-drone-factory-terminal-autonomy-ukraine-russia).

The attack came as Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky revealed that Ukraine had run out of interceptor missiles for its US Patriot air defense systems, and as US President Donald Trump walked back an earlier promise to grant Ukraine a license to produce the missiles domestically.

## A Night of Devastation in Kyiv

Residents of the Ukrainian capital had only minutes of warning before the first explosions rocked the city at approximately 1:33 a.m. local time, according to [The Kyiv Independent](https://kyivindependent.com/kyiv-hammered-by-ballistic-missiles-as-russia-launches-2nd-major-attack-in-48-hours/). Multiple waves of strikes followed over the next two hours, with ballistic missiles — which travel at hypersonic speeds and are nearly impossible to intercept without advanced systems — striking residential and industrial areas across at least five districts.

"We heard explosions almost immediately after the air raid alert went off. Then another one. And another," 38-year-old Lidiia Synovska told the Kyiv Independent outside her damaged apartment in the capital's Solomianskyi district. "How were we supposed to get to a shelter? We didn't even have time to take cover."

At least nine people were killed and more than 30 injured, including four children, according to Ukrainian authorities. In the Darnytskyi district alone, seven people lost their lives. Eighteen residential buildings, a school, the Lithuanian Embassy, and infrastructure facilities were damaged across the city.

Lithuanian President Gitanas Nauseda condemned the attack, saying that a missile had exploded just steps from the embassy. "This is yet another reminder that Russia's terror knows no limits," he said. "And that Ukraine is defending not only its own people, but the security of all Europe."

## First Strike on a US Defence Company

Among the targets destroyed was a drone production facility owned by Terminal Autonomy, a US corporation registered in Delaware in 2023. The company produces precision "deep-strike" drones — the AQ-400 Scythe and AQ-100 Bayonet — with guidance systems resistant to Russian jamming. The factory was producing up to 1,000 unmanned aerial vehicles per month with help from Ukrainian-American specialists.

Anthony Vinci, a former senior US intelligence officer, told The Guardian that the strike signals a new dimension in the conflict. "The Russians don't care who owns the manufacturing facility. It's still a target. They don't care if it's American or someone else's," he said. "I do think if Patriot missiles or any American system was manufactured in Ukraine, I think the Russians would target that manufacturing facility."

Russian military expert Alexander Stepanov of RANEPA echoed this assessment, telling [TASS](https://tass.com/defense/2167721) that the destruction of the Terminal Autonomy plant sends "a message to everyone who planned to localize defense production in Ukraine." He predicted that Western military-industrial complexes would likely relocate production to adjacent NATO countries rather than risk assets on Ukrainian soil.

## Ukraine's Air Defense Crisis

Zelensky's revelation that Ukraine had exhausted its Patriot interceptor missiles represents a critical turning point in the conflict. Of the 27 ballistic missiles Russia launched on August 1, only one was intercepted.

"Only one ballistic missile was shot down tonight simply because there are no missiles for the Patriots," Zelensky said, as reported by [The Moscow Times](https://www.themoscowtimes.com/2026/08/01/russia-pounds-kyiv-with-missiles-killing-at-least-10-a93392). "And it is precisely this shortage of interceptors against ballistics that only encourages Russia to launch such strikes against life."

The shortage stems in part from the ongoing Iran war, which has seen interceptor missiles previously earmarked for Ukraine diverted to US military forces and Gulf allies. Russia has exploited this vulnerability, firing a record 126 ballistic missiles at Ukraine in July 2026 — surpassing the previous record of 118 set in February, according to AFP analysis.

Foreign Minister Andrii Sybiha issued a fresh plea for urgent air defense supplies. "It is the battle for the skies that will define the trajectory of this war," he said. "The stronger the air shield over Ukraine, the closer peace becomes."

## Trump Walks Back Patriot Promise

Compounding Ukraine's crisis, Trump told a Cabinet meeting at Camp David on July 31 that the US had not agreed to grant Ukraine a license to produce Patriot missiles — despite announcing at the NATO summit in Ankara on July 8 that he would do so, and despite Zelensky's claim after their White House meeting on July 28 that Trump had "accepted that he will give us licenses."

"We have not agreed to that," Trump said, according to [The Kyiv Independent](https://kyivindependent.com/we-have-not-agreed-to-that-trump-walks-back-promise-of-patriot-licenses-for-ukraine/). "We're talking about it, but it's a hard thing to give away that kind of technology."

The president expressed concern about sharing American defense technology, saying: "These weapons are incredible. We have to be very careful about letting somebody build them. ... I don't think this would ever happen, but you know those people that you give that technology, they can someday turn on you. You know, that is possible."

Currently, only Japan and Germany hold licenses to produce Patriot missiles outside the US. Ukraine had announced on July 23 that it would co-produce Patriot interceptors with US defense contractor Raytheon, but no clear timeline has been established.

## Ukrainian Counterstrikes

Despite the devastating attack on Kyiv, Ukraine demonstrated its own long-range strike capabilities. Zelensky said Ukrainian forces struck three oil refineries in the Bashkortostan region, logistics centers in Sarapul, Kazan, and Volgograd, and a maritime terminal in Krasnodar region. Ukraine also sank a sanctioned Russian container ship, the Yanina, in the Black Sea, according to [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/1/russian-missile-attack-kills-three-in-ukraines-kyiv).

"Our targets are consistently defined facilities that sustain the war effort," Zelensky said.

## A Conflict at a Crossroads

The events of the past 48 hours underscore how the Russia-Ukraine war has entered a new and more dangerous phase. Russia's willingness to target US-owned defence infrastructure marks a significant escalation, while Ukraine's depleted air defenses leave its cities increasingly vulnerable to ballistic missile barrages.

The conflict, now in its fifth year, has lasted 1,590 days — exceeding the 1,418 days of the Soviet-German war in World War II. Both sides appear committed to continued escalation rather than negotiation. As Zelensky and Vice President JD Vance spoke by phone about Kyiv's air defense needs, the question of whether the US will provide the Patriot interceptors Ukraine urgently requires — or the license to produce them domestically — remains unresolved.

For the residents of Kyiv, the immediate concern is simpler: whether the next night will bring more explosions, and whether the world will respond before it's too late. As one Kyiv resident, Zhanna Maidanska, told the Kyiv Independent: "I want to ask the world, which is so afraid of Putin, not to be afraid of him. He has to be stopped. He is a monstrous person, and the society that supports him is deeply sick."

## What to Watch For

The coming weeks will be critical. Key questions include whether the US will accelerate Patriot interceptor deliveries to Ukraine, whether Trump will reverse his position on production licenses, and how Russia will respond to Ukraine's increasingly sophisticated long-range strikes deep inside Russian territory. The battle for the skies over Ukraine may well determine the trajectory of the entire war.
