# Fire in Bozhou, Anhui Kills Two People

A fire broke out in a residential house in Qiaocheng District, Bozhou City, Anhui Province on the evening of August 1, killing two people, according to [Xinhua News Agency](https://www.news.cn/20260801/4a3c3d0c20964e5a8712f52b01e1a59f/c.html). The incident occurred in Chengfu Town, a township in the southeastern part of Qiaocheng District, approximately 33-35 kilometers from the city center.

## Incident Timeline

The Bozhou Fire Department received the alarm in the early evening of August 1, according to relevant departments of Bozhou City. Firefighters responded to the scene in Chengfu Town, where a fire had broken out in a residential dwelling, initially causing two deaths.

Open flames were extinguished approximately 21 minutes after the initial alarm was received. The cause of the fire remains under further investigation, as confirmed by local authorities.

The report was filed by Xinhua reporter Guo Chen from Hefei, the provincial capital, and was also carried by [Sina News](https://news.sina.com.cn/c/2026-08-01/doc-inikvtiv0229107.shtml) and [The Paper](https://m.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_33695606).

## Recent Fire Incidents in Anhui

The Bozhou fire is the latest in a series of residential fire incidents across Anhui Province in recent months. In February 2026, a fire in a residential house in Linquan County, Fuyang City, killed four people. An investigation report released on June 12 found that improper electrical wiring by the homeowner was the cause, with direct economic losses amounting to 253,540 yuan.

More recently, two fires were reported in Qiaocheng District itself. On June 10, a fire occurred at a building's sunshade canopy, though no casualties were reported. On June 26, a fire at a food stall in Gujing Town also resulted in no casualties.

The pattern of residential fires in rural areas has drawn increasing attention from safety officials. The Linquan County investigation highlighted issues with non-standard electrical wiring in rural homes, a problem that safety experts say is common in older housing stock across China's countryside.

## Rural Fire Safety Concerns

Rural residential fires remain a recurring concern in China, particularly in areas with older housing infrastructure. Common causes include electrical faults, improper wiring, and unsafe heating or cooking practices. Local governments have been increasingly focused on fire safety inspections and public education campaigns aimed at reducing these risks.

The Bozhou incident underscores the ongoing challenges facing rural communities in fire prevention and response. While the specific cause of this fire has not yet been determined, the incident adds to a growing body of evidence pointing to the need for improved fire safety standards in rural residential buildings.

Nationally, the issue of fire safety has gained prominence following the major factory fire in Jinjiang, Fujian Province on July 9, which killed 28 people. That incident prompted renewed calls for stricter enforcement of fire safety regulations across various sectors.

## Investigation and Next Steps

Local authorities in Bozhou have confirmed that the cause of the fire is still under further investigation. The relevant departments of Bozhou City have not yet released additional details about the victims or the circumstances surrounding the fire.

As the investigation continues, attention will likely focus on identifying the fire's origin and determining whether any safety violations contributed to the tragedy. The outcome of the investigation could have implications for fire safety enforcement in the region, particularly in rural residential areas.

For now, the community in Chengfu Town is left to mourn the loss of two lives in what has become a stark reminder of the persistent dangers posed by residential fires in rural China. The findings of the investigation, when released, will be crucial in determining what measures may be needed to prevent similar tragedies in the future.
