# Cake Shop Owner Refunds 16,000 Yuan in Membership Fees Before Closing, Earns Widespread Praise

A cake shop owner in Fukang City, Xinjiang, has become an internet sensation after refunding more than 16,000 yuan (approximately $2,200) in membership fees to over 100 customers before closing her business. The act of integrity, reported by [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/fortune/20260802/e85a24c71b764291b0b05bd274f52a27/c.html), has resonated deeply across Chinese social media as a powerful counter-example to the widespread problem of businesses disappearing without honoring prepaid balances.

## A Simple Act of Integrity

Zhang Xiaoli, who operated her cake shop in Fukang City for more than eight years, announced the closure on July 28. Rather than quietly shutting her doors, she proactively contacted every member with an outstanding balance through WeChat and phone calls to arrange refunds. The largest single refund was over 400 yuan, while the smallest was just 5 yuan—less than one US dollar.

"Everyone chose to become a member because they trusted me," Zhang said, as quoted by [Sichuan Daily](https://cbgc.scol.com.cn/news/7814802). "Although the business is temporarily ending, integrity cannot be compromised. I cannot let down the trust of every consumer."

## Customer and Public Reactions

The response from customers has been overwhelmingly warm. Many expressed surprise and gratitude, with some admitting they had completely forgotten they had outstanding balances. One customer remarked, "The refund isn't much, but it's very heartwarming," while others promised to return and support Zhang if she ever reopens.

The story quickly spread across social media platforms, where netizens showered Zhang with praise. One commenter, Chen Xiaogang, wrote: "A boss with this kind of integrity can make money doing anything!"

Reflecting on the outpouring of support, Zhang told [CNR](https://food.cnr.cn/gstj/20260802/t20260802_527742507.shtml): "I've been operating for over 8 years, and now that it's closing, I feel a bit sad. But seeing the netizens' comments makes me feel very warm. Over these 8 years, everyone has been very supportive. I cannot betray this trust."

## The Prepaid Consumption Problem in China

Zhang's actions stand in stark contrast to a persistent problem in China's consumer market. Prepaid membership systems are extremely common among small businesses—from cake shops and gyms to salons and restaurants—where customers pay upfront for future goods or services in exchange for discounts. However, when businesses close, many owners simply disappear without refunding customer balances, leading to widespread consumer frustration and numerous disputes.

Under Article 53 of China's Consumer Rights Protection Law, operators who provide goods or services on a prepaid basis are legally required to either fulfill their obligations or return prepayments if they cannot. Yet in practice, enforcement has been inconsistent, and stories of customers losing their prepaid balances are all too common.

## A Growing Pattern of Business Integrity

Zhang's story is not an isolated case. In May 2026, a barbecue shop owner in Jinan was forced to close his seven-year-old business after his five-year-old son was diagnosed with leukemia. He proactively offered to refund memberships to his 470 members—nearly 30,000 yuan—though most customers refused the refunds and instead sent additional money to support the family. That story received over 35,000 likes, comments, and shares on social media.

These parallel stories highlight a broader cultural trend in China where business integrity and customer trust are deeply valued, and acts of honesty generate significant public resonance.

## Looking Ahead

As for her own future, Zhang remains measured. "I'll temporarily give myself a vacation to rest for a while, think things over, and then start again," she said.

For now, her small act of integrity has become a powerful reminder of the values that underpin trust in business relationships. As one customer's comment captured it: "Integrity is always the golden signboard." The story, republished by outlets including [Phoenix News](https://news.ifeng.com/c/8vF2gcZ5PQY) and [Jinwanbao](https://jinwanbaoepaper.enorth.com.cn/jwb/html/2026-08/02/content_87648_3696241.htm), serves as a heartwarming testament to the simple principle that doing the right thing—even when no one is watching—matters.
