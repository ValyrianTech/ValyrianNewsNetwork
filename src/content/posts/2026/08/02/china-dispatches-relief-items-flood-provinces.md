# China Dispatches 11,000 Relief Items to Flood-Hit Provinces

China's National Disaster Prevention, Reduction and Relief Committee Office and the Ministry of Emergency Management have dispatched 11,000 central disaster relief items—including blankets, quilts, and family emergency kits—to Jilin and Heilongjiang provinces to support communities affected by severe flooding, according to [CCTV News](https://news.cctv.com/2026/08/01/ARTIXdROOu5GzONoU3afbDUX260801.shtml). The relief effort, coordinated with the National Food and Strategic Reserves Administration, aims to assist local authorities in sheltering and caring for displaced residents.

## Background: A Region Under Water

The dispatch comes as northeastern China grapples with one of its most severe flood seasons in recent memory. Working groups have been sent to both provinces to assess damage on the ground and guide relief operations, as [People's Daily Online](http://jl.people.com.cn/n2/2026/0802/c349771-41656971.html) reported.

The flooding crisis escalated dramatically in late July. On July 29, Wangqing County in Jilin's Yanbian Korean Autonomous Prefecture experienced extreme rainfall, with all 197 monitoring stations recording precipitation and maximum levels reaching 205.5mm at Dangshuhe Forest Farm. The county escalated its flood emergency response four times in rapid succession, ultimately declaring a Level I emergency and implementing "five stops"—suspending outdoor activities, classes, work, business, and transportation—according to [People's Daily's Jilin Channel](http://jl.people.com.cn/n2/2026/0729/c349771-41653820.html).

Across the border in Heilongjiang, Ning'an City faced its own crisis. Jingpo Lake overflowed for the second time in two weeks, sending torrents down the Mudan River. The Xiama River Bridge on the G201 National Highway collapsed, with approximately 50 meters of the structure destroyed, and railway tracks were washed out near Dougouzi station, as [Epoch Times](https://www.epochtimes.com/gb/26/7/30/n14820353.htm) reported.

## Widespread Impact and Evacuations

The human toll has been significant. In Wangqing County alone, 8,145 residents were safely evacuated from 107 villages across 9 townships, with zero casualties reported. Fire rescue teams successfully rescued 22 trapped residents using heavy machinery, according to [People's Daily's on-scene reporting](http://jl.people.com.cn/n2/2026/0730/c349771-41654550.html).

Residents describe a region battered by repeated disasters. "It rained heavily again, the reservoir water level reached the limit, so they had to release water," one Wangqing County resident told [NTD Television](https://www.ntdtv.com/gb/2026/07/30/a104119884.html). "Tianqiaoling, Chunyang, Jiguan and other towns were flooded. Many villages were flooded."

In Ning'an, a villager from Bohai Town described the devastation: "Downstream of Jingpo Lake, the Mudan River has had two major floods this year. The water came fiercely and rose very fast—faster than last time. Once rice and corn are flooded, they're done for."

## Recovery Underway

Recovery efforts are now in full swing. In Wangqing County, clean-up operations are proceeding across affected villages. Cui Xuehua, Deputy Party Secretary and Mayor of Wangqing Town, told [People's Daily](http://jl.people.com.cn/n2/2026/0731/c349771-41655553.html) that all 155 houses in the town's Chunhe Village were submerged, with water reaching 1.4 meters and nearly 30 centimeters of mud accumulating inside homes. "We will go all out to advance the village reconstruction and help all villagers return home and resume normal life as soon as possible," she said.

Infrastructure repair is a top priority. Power crews are working through mud and water to restore electricity, while telecom operators are re-establishing communication networks. Road repair teams are clearing debris and restoring access to isolated communities.

## A Region Under Continued Threat

The crisis is far from over. The Ministry of Water Resources activated a Level IV flood defense emergency response for Heilongjiang on August 1, and a red mountain flood disaster meteorological warning was jointly issued by the Ministry of Water Resources and China Meteorological Administration. Nineteen rivers across Heilongjiang, Gansu, Shaanxi, Sichuan, and Chongqing have experienced above-warning-level floods, with the Songhua River and Gan River both recording numbered floods of 2026 in early August.

Heavy rainfall is forecast to continue across the northeast through August 7, according to meteorological agencies. The Ministry of Water Resources has deployed six working groups to assist flood defense efforts in Heilongjiang, Inner Mongolia, Sichuan, Guangdong, and Gansu, while targeted warnings have been issued to 16 provinces.

## What to Watch

As the flood season continues, the effectiveness of China's disaster response coordination will be tested. The dispatch of relief supplies represents a significant logistical operation, but the scale of damage—spanning multiple provinces, affecting thousands of residents, and destroying critical infrastructure—underscores the severity of this year's flood crisis. The coming weeks will reveal how quickly affected communities can rebuild and whether further relief measures will be needed as the region braces for more rain.
