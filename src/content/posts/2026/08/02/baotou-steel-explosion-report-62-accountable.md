# Baotou Steel Explosion Report: 62 Held Accountable

The Inner Mongolia Autonomous Region Emergency Management Department has released the official investigation report into the catastrophic explosion at the Baotou Steel plate plant, which killed 10 workers and injured 84 others on January 18, 2026. The report, published on August 2, classifies the incident as a major production safety liability accident and holds 62 individuals accountable for responsibility, according to [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/local/20260802/332627568f084b0e88ad79ad72476ad4/c.html).

The explosion occurred at approximately 3:01 p.m. at the Inner Mongolia Baotou Steel Plate Plant in Kundulun District, Baotou City, when a 650-cubic-meter saturated water and steam spherical tank ruptured in the steelmaking department. The blast sent the roughly 10-meter-diameter tank flying approximately one kilometer, where it crashed into a scrap metal recycling station in Erjiahai Village, damaging nearby homes.

## The Direct Cause

Investigators determined that the explosion's direct cause was corrosion and fatigue damage to the No. 1 spherical tank. According to the report, the tank's inner wall suffered irreversible composite damage from alkaline stress corrosion cracking and corrosion fatigue, resulting from long-term media corrosion, local stress, and alternating loads. The damage accumulated until it penetrated the wall thickness, causing saturated water to leak from the north side of the lower circumferential weld. After approximately 1.5 hours without effective on-site containment measures, pressure exceeded the tank's ultimate bearing capacity, triggering rapid circumferential tearing of the weld, tank disintegration, and instant flash evaporation of the large volume of saturated water inside, as [CCTV News](https://news.cctv.com/2026/08/02/ARTI0pjMvM3x2mJTlDGodICI260802.shtml) reported.

## A Critical Failure: Production Over Safety

Perhaps the most damning finding emerged months before the full report was released. At a February 27 press conference, Li Haowen, Director of the Investigation and Statistics Department at the Ministry of Emergency Management, disclosed that the steam sphere had been severely leaking for a full hour and a half before the explosion. However, to avoid stopping production, two supervisors stationed at the site by the enterprise ordered workers to continue construction. Both supervisors were among those killed in the blast, as reported by the [21st Century Business Herald](https://www.21jingji.com/article/20260227/herald/a80413221ad45651ad906ed49eb6aa17.html).

This decision to prioritize production continuity over worker safety proved catastrophic. The supervisors' insistence on continuing operations despite a major leak directly contributed to the high casualty count, as the tank ultimately failed while workers remained in the immediate vicinity.

## Rescue Operations

Following the explosion, the Inner Mongolia Autonomous Region Party Committee and Government rapidly organized rescue efforts. The Ministry of Emergency Management and the State Administration for Market Regulation dispatched personnel to guide rescue operations. Over 1,300 people and more than 70 emergency and ambulance vehicles were deployed for search and rescue, working in temperatures that plunged to -30 degrees Celsius. As [Shangguan News](https://www.jfdaily.com/wx/detail.do?id=1054538) reported, rescue workers battled extreme cold while searching for survivors, with senior engineer Hao Haibing of the Baotou Fire Rescue Detachment noting that damaged structural components posed significant risks to rescue personnel.

The death toll climbed gradually as search operations progressed, from an initial count of 2 dead and 8 missing to a final toll of 10 confirmed dead and 84 injured. At a January 19 press conference, Baotou officials confirmed that public security organs had already taken measures against responsible enterprise personnel, and the autonomous region had established an investigation team led by Regional Government Chairman Bao Gang, as documented by the [People's Daily App](https://www.peopleapp.com/column/30051244965-500007313447).

## Accountability and Punishments

The investigation report identified systemic problems across multiple entities. Beyond the accident unit itself—the Baotou Steel Plate Plant—the report cited failures at Inner Mongolia Baotou Steel Union Co., Ltd., Baotou Iron and Steel (Group) Co., Ltd., and related units including Beijing Jingcheng Kelin Environmental Protection Technology Co., Ltd., Anshan Huaxin Heavy Industry Machinery Co., Ltd., and the Baotou Branch of the Inner Mongolia Special Equipment Inspection and Research Institute.

Criminal prosecutions have already begun. The Kundulun District Procuratorate approved the arrest of 7 personnel from the plate plant on suspicion of criminal activity, and authorities recommended that 6 additional related personnel be transferred to judicial authorities for investigation. Administrative penalties were imposed on four entities: Baotou Steel Union, Jingcheng Kelin, Anshan Huaxin, and the Baotou Special Equipment Inspection Institute, according to the [Lianhe Zaobao](https://www.zaobao.com.sg/news/china/story20260802-9457494) report.

The Kundulun District Party Committee and Government were required to submit written self-examinations to the Baotou Municipal Party Committee and Government, while the Baotou Municipal Party Committee, Government, and Baotou Steel Group were required to submit written self-examinations to the Autonomous Region Party Committee and Government. The State Council Safety Committee also decided to supervise the investigation of this accident.

## A Troubling Safety Record

This explosion is not an isolated incident for Baotou Steel. From 2016 to 2022, Baotou Steel Union experienced 20 production safety accidents resulting in 28 deaths, according to an analysis by [The Paper](https://h5.ifeng.com/c/vivoArticle/v002T0GRSEMfYu2rR1K56YMxpLQxcpwc2kd5iKr720h-_usw__). In 2022 alone, the company suffered five accidents with 12 deaths in just nine months, including a March fire during desulfurization system maintenance at the rare earth plate plant that killed 7 workers.

The Ministry of Emergency Management issued a notice in September 2022 criticizing Baotou Steel for failing to systematically analyze the causes of recurring accidents, not evaluating the effectiveness of rectification measures, and having weak implementation of safety rules. Notably, 45 percent of the accidents involved contracting companies. Just two days before the January 2026 explosion, Baotou Steel Group's safety committee held a meeting emphasizing safety, and nine days prior, the Baotou Emergency Management Bureau fined Baotou Steel Union 30,000 yuan for failing to eliminate safety hazards.

## Financial Pressures and Outsourcing Risks

The investigation's findings must be understood within the broader context of Baotou Steel's financial struggles and labor practices. The company experienced severe financial difficulties from 2014 to 2016, with losses reaching 8.3 billion yuan in 2015 alone. After a major restructuring in 2017 that cut headquarters staff by half and laid off nearly 4,000 workers, the company returned to profitability but has continued to face margin pressure. In 2022, Baotou Steel Union posted a net loss of 730 million yuan, though it recovered to post net profits of 515 million yuan in 2023 and 265 million yuan in 2024.

Industry expert Wu Kai, who has worked in metallurgical industry consulting for over 20 years, noted that cost-cutting measures during industry downturns can lead to safety risks, such as reducing equipment maintenance budgets and running equipment with known defects. An in-depth investigation by [Lifeweek](https://at.laborinfocn6.com/articles/96536) revealed that most workers at the scene were outsourced workers, many middle-aged or elderly, who receive lower pay, work six days a week, and have less safety training. The explosion occurred on a weekend when only outsourced workers were on duty.

## Looking Ahead

The release of this investigation report marks a significant step toward accountability, but it also raises broader questions about industrial safety in China's steel sector. The Ministry of Emergency Management has used this case as a national example of safety failures, calling for comprehensive safety reforms across industries. The report's findings on the prioritization of production over worker safety, systemic equipment maintenance failures, and inadequate regulatory oversight point to challenges that extend far beyond Baotou Steel.

As the company moves forward with implementing corrective measures and the affected communities continue to recover, the key question remains whether this tragedy will catalyze meaningful, lasting change in how China's industrial enterprises balance production targets against worker safety. The families of the 10 workers who lost their lives—including a 31-year-old man who had been at the plant for only three days—deserve nothing less.
