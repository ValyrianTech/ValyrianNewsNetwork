# Miss North Carolina USA Dethroned Weeks Before Miss USA Pageant

Miss North Carolina USA Brittany Boltinhouse was stripped of her crown on August 5, just weeks before she was set to represent the state at the 75th annual Miss USA pageant. The decision, announced jointly by state pageant organizer A Blaize Productions and the Miss USA Organization, followed what officials described as a "thorough review of recently surfaced information" related to alleged racist and inflammatory social media posts from 2017 to 2019, according to [Fox News](https://www.foxnews.com/us/miss-north-carolina-usa-loses-crown-organizer-condemns-racism-homophobia-transphobia).

## A Rapid Rise and Sudden Fall

Boltinhouse, a 27-year-old entrepreneur from Beulaville, North Carolina, was crowned Miss North Carolina USA on June 28 in her first-ever pageant competition. She was celebrated as the first titleholder of Honduran descent and the first married woman to win the state title. In a feature published by [WITN](https://www.witn.com/2026/08/03/duplin-county-woman-represent-north-carolina-miss-usa-75th-diamond-celebration/) just days before the controversy erupted, Boltinhouse spoke about her journey from small-town roots to the national stage, saying, "It's not about the pageant, it's not about this crown, it's not about this sash. It's the fact that you're able to fight for your dreams no matter who you are and what you've done in your life."

That story, shared on social media, triggered the controversy when commenters began circulating alleged past posts from Boltinhouse under the handle "Sosa the Stallion." According to [The NC Beat](https://thencbeat.com/miss-north-carolina-usa-old-tweets/), the posts from 2017-2019 included repeated use of the N-word, bragging about being "toxic," and explicit relationship drama. The outlet also reported that Boltinhouse had been open about her political views, including a November 2024 Facebook post endorsing Donald Trump.

## Organization Condemns Racism, Homophobia, Transphobia

Miss USA Chairman and CEO Thom Brodeur issued a strongly worded statement condemning the conduct, saying the organization "does not tolerate racism, homophobia, transphobia nor language that strips any person of their dignity — whoever they are."

"A title here isn't a trinket. It is a position of trust, extended by an organization and accepted by a woman who agrees to carry a standard in public," Brodeur said, as reported by [ABC11](https://abc11.com/story/miss-north-carolina-usa-2026-stripped-title-organization-condemns-racism-homophobia/19636969/). "When that standard is broken, the title cannot continue as though nothing happened."

Brodeur emphasized that the decision was not based on a single incident but reflected "conduct over an extended period of time." He also acknowledged the human dimension of the situation, saying, "There is a young woman at the center of this. She has been named, examined, and judged in public in a way that most of us would not survive, and she is still a human being with a family, a future, and a life ahead of her."

A Blaize Productions, the state pageant organizer, echoed those sentiments, stating that while the organization believes "people are capable of growth" and that "grace and accountability can coexist," leadership carries a unique responsibility to uphold the values of the title.

## Myla Hadley Steps In

Under the organization's succession procedures, first runner-up Myla Hadley, 24, has been named Miss North Carolina USA 2026 and will represent the state at the national competition on August 27 in Miami. Hadley, a business broker and founder of Zenith House of Wellness, acknowledged the abrupt transition in a social media post.

"While this may seem like a two-week turnaround given the circumstances, I've been preparing to represent at Miss USA since I first competed with this organization," Hadley said, according to [The NC Beat](https://thencbeat.com/myla-hadley-miss-north-carolina-usa-dethroning/). "I am ready to represent North Carolina."

## Family Speaks Out

As of August 7, Boltinhouse had not publicly responded to the decision. However, an unnamed family member told the Daily Mail, via [TMZ](https://www.tmz.com/2026/08/07/brittany-boltinhouse-family-reacts-to-her-losing-miss-north-carolina-title/), that they believe the organization is "saving the face [of] an organization" and expressed a desire to "put her truth out." The family member said they are taking time "to be in prayer and unity" before responding further.

Brodeur said Boltinhouse's forthcoming statement shows "ownership, accountability and apology," though he noted that "change is not a statement. It is a record. It is built over time, in the open, through conduct rather than apology alone."

## Broader Implications

The controversy has sparked sharply divergent reactions across the political spectrum. Some in the Hispanic community, including Facebook user Paulina Gallegos, argued that Boltinhouse did not represent them, while conservative commentators framed the decision as an example of cancel culture. Community reactions in North Carolina were similarly divided, with Jacksonville resident Lynn Shippen telling [WITN](https://www.witn.com/2026/08/06/community-reacts-after-duplin-county-woman-loses-miss-nc-usa-title-over-resurfaced-posts/) that "no one has the right to judge how people live their lives."

The incident adds another chapter to the Miss USA Organization's recent history of public scrutiny over leadership decisions and titleholder controversies. The organization has also been noted for its inclusivity, with Kataluna Enriquez becoming the first trans woman to win a state title in 2021, as [Them](https://www.them.us/culture/brittany-boltinhouse-miss-nc) noted.

## What's Next

The 75th annual Miss USA pageant is scheduled for August 27 in Miami, broadcast on the CW Network at 8 p.m. ET. The winner will compete at Miss Universe on November 24. Whether Boltinhouse issues a public statement, how Hadley performs with minimal preparation time, and what lasting impact this controversy has on the pageant organization remain open questions.
