# Nancy Guthrie Ransom Notes Trigger Fresh Tips in Probe

Investigators have received a slight uptick in tips following the release of two alleged ransom notes in the Nancy Guthrie case, according to the [Pima County Sheriff's Office](https://www.foxnews.com/us/nancy-guthrie-investigators-uptick-tips-ransom-notes-release-sheriff). The 84-year-old mother of Today show co-host Savannah Guthrie was kidnapped from her Tucson, Arizona, home on the night of January 31-February 1, 2026, and remains missing more than six months later.

"Nancy Guthrie Task Force investigators have seen a slight increase in tips since the two ransom notes were released last week," Pima County Sheriff's Office Public Information Officer Angelica Carrillo told Fox News Digital. "All tips have been followed up on."

## The Ransom Notes

The sheriff's department released the full content of the two alleged ransom notes on July 31 in a bid to identify the sender based on the notes' "unique linguistic style." The first note, sent February 2, demanded millions of dollars in Bitcoin for Nancy's release, threatening she would be killed if payment was not received by the February 9 deadline. The second note, sent February 6, claimed Nancy perished shortly after she was taken from a heart-related condition and was "buried in nature now."

Authorities have not confirmed the notes are authentic, but Savannah Guthrie has said she believes they may be real, and investigators haven't ruled them out. The FBI determined in late June that two early ransom notes and a third reported by TMZ were fakes, but the second note claiming Nancy's death was believed to be a legitimate communication.

## A Key Detail Raises Questions

A crucial detail in the first ransom note has raised new questions about the sender's knowledge of the crime scene. The note mentioned Nancy's Apple Watch on the floor at the foot of her bed and the destroyed white flood light in the backyard — details that were not public at the time the note was sent. [Fox News Digital](https://www.foxnews.com/us/nancy-guthrie-ransom-demands-released-pima-county-sheriffs-department-bid-identify-sender) first learned of the watch detail on the morning of February 3, a day after the first note was sent, indicating the sender knew about it before the information became public.

"He knew about the watch on the floor — almost nobody did," said Jason Pack, a retired FBI negotiator who has analyzed the notes. "In my view, two things the language does support: He writes like a native English speaker. The idiom and the small connecting words are all in the right places, and at this length that is hard to fake. And he writes like someone who spends his day sending business email."

## Forensic Linguistic Analysis

Forensic linguists have examined the notes for clues about the writer's identity. William Eggington, a retired professor from Brigham Young University, told the [BBC](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/c2dkng21dg2o) that the first note's use of passive voice and if-then sentence construction suggests at least a high-school level education, possibly some university exposure. The inconsistent spelling of "received" indicates the person is not particularly tuned in to proper spelling or didn't do a close edit.

Eggington noted that in the second note, the writer is "dehumanising Nancy by talking about her in the third person" and "even putting the blame for her death on her." He believes the same person wrote both notes based on the style of writing.

## The Investigation's Challenges

The investigation has faced scrutiny over early missteps. According to a [Wall Street Journal report detailed by TMZ](https://www.tmz.com/2026/08/02/nancy-guthrie-investigation-missteps-according-to-report/), deputies initially gave little attention to dried blood on Nancy's front porch, failed to tape off the property for three days, and the department's surveillance plane remained grounded for hours because it couldn't find a deputy qualified to operate the camera system. Sheriff Chris Nanos disputed the findings and insisted the case remains active.

Sheriff Nanos has also faced legal challenges. A man left paralyzed after being shot by deputies during a swatting call filed a $176 million lawsuit against Nanos and Pima County, as reported by the [Daily Caller](https://dailycaller.com/2026/08/04/axeel-melendez-sues-pima-county-sheriff-chris-nanos-nancy-guthrie-swatting-shooting-paralyzed-gunshot-victim-176-million-lawsuit/). Another man detained and released during the Guthrie investigation plans to sue for $3 million.

## A Mystery Tipster

A person claiming to have information on the case repeatedly emailed TMZ, using multiple male aliases but always the same Bitcoin address. Investigators reportedly believe the sender could actually be a woman. TMZ founder Harvey Levin said the individual claimed to know the identity of the kidnappers and the location of Guthrie's body. [Fox News](https://www.foxnews.com/us/nancy-guthrie-case-could-hinge-mystery-tipster-emailed-tmz) reported that the FBI asked TMZ to stand down on a planned documentary about the case.

## What's Next

The combined reward for information in the case is over $1.2 million, including a $1 million family reward and a $50,000 FBI reward. Savannah Guthrie has made multiple emotional public pleas for her mother's return, saying, "We are desperate. We need someone to come forward. Someone knows something. Someone suspects something. Someone recognises the writing in the ransom demand notes."

As the investigation enters its seventh month with no suspects identified, authorities remain hopeful that the release of the ransom notes will prompt someone to recognize the writer's distinctive style. Anyone with information is asked to call 1-800-CALL-FBI or 520-88-CRIME to remain anonymous.
