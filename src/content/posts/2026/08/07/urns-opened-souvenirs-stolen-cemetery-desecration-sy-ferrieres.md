# Urns Opened, Souvenirs Stolen in Cemetery Desecration at Sy

A cemetery in the village of Sy, part of the commune of Ferrières in Belgium's Liège province, has been the target of a deeply disturbing act of vandalism. On Tuesday evening, August 4, 2026, authorities discovered that funeral urns had been opened and emptied, and personal souvenirs—including wedding rings—had been stolen from the columbarium, according to [RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/urnes-ouvertes-et-videes-souvenirs-voles-au-cimetiere-de-sy-ferrieres-11767339).

## The Incident

Around 8 PM on Tuesday, a person at the cemetery raised the alarm after discovering that the columbarium, located slightly away from the main paths, had been vandalized. The damage was extensive: several plaques covering the niches had been unscrewed, four funeral urns had disappeared, and the ashes of two additional urns had been emptied directly onto the ground. Two wedding rings, kept as personal mementos in one of the niches, were also stolen.

Police were called to the scene immediately and returned on Wednesday to complete their investigation. One urn was later recovered—it had been thrown from a bridge into the Ourthe river, approximately 500 meters away. Other urns remain unaccounted for.

## Community Shock

Christian Verdin, the mayor of Ferrières, described the scene as deeply moving. "When we arrived here on site, it really touched us, we were very moved to see ashes," he told RTBF. "The columbariums were open, the urns were emptied on the spot, on the ground."

The theft of the wedding rings struck the mayor as particularly senseless. "There was also a theft, meaning that in this small pouch that was in a columbarium, people had put wedding rings, as a souvenir," Verdin said. "Those who opened it, they saw that, they took the rings and left with them. You have to be in an altered state to do this kind of thing. I think we really hit rock bottom."

Speaking to [L'Avenir](https://www.lavenir.net/regions/huy-waremme/ferrieres/2026/08/06/le-columbarium-du-cimetiere-de-sy-a-ferrieres-a-ete-vandalise-cest-quelque-chose-dimpensable-commente-le-bourgmestre-christian-verdin-ZDZQV54PMRDMTBZREQNPTBWLGA/), Verdin added: "We later found one of the urns, thrown from a bridge... We contacted the families concerned and they are obviously disheartened and so are we: it's something unthinkable, we live in a world of madmen..."

The emotional toll on the affected families has been severe. The mayor recounted how the wife of a person whose remains were disturbed began to cry, telling him it was "like reliving the same thing twice."

## Investigation and Response

The commune has filed a complaint with the police, alongside the affected families. Local authorities are investigating the incident, and the commune has announced plans to install surveillance cameras at the cemetery.

## A Disturbing Pattern

Cemetery vandalism targeting columbariums is not unprecedented in Belgium. In 2016, approximately fifty columbariums were forced open at the cemetery in Mont-sur-Marchienne (Charleroi), and several metal urns were stolen—an incident attributed to metal thieves, as [RTBF reported](https://www.rtbf.be/article/mont-sur-marchienne-columbariums-vandalises-et-urnes-funeraires-volees-9399787). Similar incidents had occurred at the same cemetery in 2013.

However, the Sy incident appears to be of a different nature. While the removal of urns may have been motivated by their metal value, the deliberate act of opening and emptying ashes onto the ground, and the theft of deeply personal items like wedding rings, suggests a more calculated act of desecration.

## About Sy and Ferrières

Sy is a small tourist village in the commune of [Ferrières](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferrières_(Belgique)), located in the province of Liège, Wallonia, on the right bank of the [Ourthe river](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sy_(Belgique)). The village is known for its steep valley slopes and the protected "Rochers de Sy" geological site. Ferrières has a population of approximately 5,214 and is part of the Pays d'Ourthe-Amblève tourism region.

## What's Next

The investigation remains ongoing, with police continuing to examine the scene and gather evidence. The commune's decision to install surveillance cameras reflects a broader concern about the security of public spaces dedicated to remembrance. For the families affected, the recovery of the remaining urns and the return of the stolen mementos would be a small measure of closure—but the emotional scars of this desecration will likely endure far longer.

As Mayor Verdin put it: "This kind of situation, I hope it never happens again. It's scandalous, it's shameful in any case."
