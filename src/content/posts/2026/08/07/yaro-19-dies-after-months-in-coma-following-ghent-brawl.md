# Yaro (19) Dies After Months in Coma Following Ghent Brawl

Yaro D'Hoore, a 19-year-old from Oudenburg in West Flanders, has died after spending more than four months in a coma following a violent brawl in Ghent. His grandmother, Claudine Deschacht, confirmed his death on Thursday evening via social media, writing "RIP Yaro, how could this happen. Forever 19."

According to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/08/06/yaro-gentse-feesten-overleden/), Yaro had been receiving palliative care since Thursday morning after developing pneumonia. He received morphine to pass without pain. His grandmother told [Focus-WTV](https://focus-wtv.be/nieuws/alle-hoop-weg-voor-yaro-slachtoffer-van-vechtpartij-in-gen) from the hospital: "We've been with him every day, all that time we hoped it would go the right way. But it's not meant to be. The doctors found pneumonia and can't help him anymore."

## The Night That Changed Everything

The tragedy began on the night of Friday, April 3, to Saturday, April 4, 2026, when Yaro went out with friends in Ghent. In the early hours of the morning, a brawl broke out in the Volderstraat between Yaro's group of five young people and a British young man. Yaro was reportedly hit on the temple and fell on his head.

What happened next has become central to the case. Instead of calling emergency services, Yaro's friends walked 40 minutes with him through Ghent to their car, drove 30 minutes back to Oudenburg, and left him in the entrance hall of the apartment building where he lived with his mother. They even made videos in the entrance hall. On the way back, a police van stopped the group but let them go.

Yaro was found around 9 AM the next morning by a resident. He was taken to AZ Sint-Jan hospital in Bruges in critical condition, where he remained in a coma for over four months.

## The Grandmother's Voice

Throughout the ordeal, Claudine Deschacht became the family's public voice, posting daily updates on Facebook and speaking to media. In an emotional interview with [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/04/10/oma-yaro-oudenburg-interview/) in April, she expressed her disbelief at the friends' actions: "They walked 40 minutes with him in Ghent to the car, then drove another half hour with him. It's incomprehensible. And how long did he also lie in the hallway?"

"First you mainly feel sadness, but now the anger is rising," she said. "Why did they leave him behind?"

In her final message after Yaro's death, she vowed: "I want to remain the voice of Yaro, a voice he hasn't had for four months. How can we ever get over this?"

## Legal Proceedings

Four of Yaro's friends were arrested and charged with failure to assist a person in danger (schuldig verzuim) and intentional assault (opzettelijke slagen), as [reported by VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/04/08/oudenburg-parket/). Three were released under strict conditions, while a fourth — a 19-year-old from Bruges — was held with an electronic ankle monitor.

His lawyer, Carl Alexander, acknowledged the seriousness of the situation, telling [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/04/10/raadkamer-gent-18-jarige-jongen-vechtpartij/) that it "can hardly be denied" that his client failed to see the seriousness of what had happened. He noted that alcohol played a significant role, saying there was "too much to be good."

The ankle monitor was later removed by the Ghent council chamber on May 19, 2026, though the prosecution could appeal the decision, as [documented by VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/05/19/raadkamer-gent-enkelband-verdachte/).

A fifth suspect, who was not part of the friend group, remains wanted for attempted manslaughter. Police also launched appeals for witnesses, seeking a cyclist with a backpack, a young man with short hair in a red t-shirt and dark jacket with white sleeves, and a woman with half-length hair in a ponytail, as [reported by Het Nieuws van West-Vlaanderen](https://www.hetnieuwsvanwestvlaanderen.be/kijk-politie-zoekt-getuigen-vechtpartij-waarbij-jongen-uit-oudenburg-levensgevaarlijk-gewond-werd/).

## A Glimmer of Hope That Faded

Yaro's medical journey was marked by moments of hope and setbacks. He briefly woke up in early June 2026. By July 8, doctors reported he had been breathing independently for 10 days, leading his grandmother to say, "That gives us a spark of hope," as [reported by Focus-WTV](https://focus-wtv.be/nieuws/dokters-geven-yaro-nog-niet-op-we-hebben-weer-hoop).

But in late July, his condition deteriorated again. He fell back into a coma and developed a high fever and pneumonia. Doctors placed him on a coma room before transitioning him to palliative care.

## Broader Context and Security Concerns

While the brawl occurred in April — not during the Ghent Festivities, which take place in July — the case has become intertwined with broader concerns about violence at major public events. During the 2026 Ghent Festivities, social media circulated videos of fights, though police and city officials said these gave a distorted picture.

Festival mayor Joris Vandenbroucke told [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/07/20/gentse-feesten-minder-vechtpartijen-bezoekers/) that social media exaggerates the perception of violence: "Because of the videos, people think the Ghent Festivities is a place where there's fighting everywhere. But nothing could be further from the truth." Police reported fewer incidents than in previous years, with approximately 435,000 visitors during the opening weekend.

## What's Next

The public prosecutor's office of East Flanders confirms that the investigation into the brawl is still ongoing. The case against the four friends will proceed through the Belgian legal system, with the charges of failure to assist and intentional assault carrying significant legal weight.

For Yaro's family, the grief is compounded by the knowledge that the outcome might have been different. As his grandmother said: "Yaro was a good boy. It's incomprehensible why this happened and why his friends didn't help him. Then we wouldn't be where we are now. And soon everything will come back in a trial. I want to remain Yaro's voice. That boy deserves that, and he can't do it himself anymore."

[KW.be](https://kw.be/nieuws/criminaliteit/geweld/yaro-uit-oudenburg-overleden-vier-maanden-na-zware-vechtpartij-in-gent-het-besef-dat-hij-voor-altijd-19-zal-blijven-is-heel-hard/) captured the family's sentiment: "The realization that he will remain 19 forever is very hard."

The investigation continues, and the search for the British suspect and the fifth individual wanted for attempted manslaughter remains active. The case has become a somber reminder of the consequences of failing to seek help for someone in need.
