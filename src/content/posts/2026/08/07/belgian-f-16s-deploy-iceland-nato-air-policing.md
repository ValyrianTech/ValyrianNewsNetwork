# Belgian F-16s Deploy to Iceland for NATO Air Policing

Belgium has deployed four F-16 fighter jets and approximately one hundred military personnel to Keflavik Air Base in Iceland for NATO's Icelandic Air Policing mission, marking the second such deployment in less than a year. The aircraft arrived on Friday, August 7, following a three-hour flight from Melsbroeck military airport, as [RTBF reported](https://www.rtbf.be/article/quatre-f-16-belges-et-une-centaine-de-militaires-vont-securiser-l-espace-aerien-islandais-11767489).

![Belgian F-16 fighter jet during NATO Air Policing mission in Iceland](https://www.icelandreview.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/Belgium_F-16_7550608072.jpg)

## Why Iceland Matters

Iceland holds a major geostrategic position between Europe and North America, sitting at the heart of transatlantic air and maritime routes. As the only NATO member without its own standing armed forces, the country relies entirely on allied nations to monitor its airspace. Since 2008, NATO allies have regularly rotated fighter aircraft through Keflavik Air Base for periods of three to four weeks, typically three times per year.

The Belgian jets will operate in the GIUK gap—the strategically significant corridor between Greenland, Iceland, and the United Kingdom—a route frequently used by Russian bombers and submarines to test NATO defenses. As [Iceland Review noted](https://www.icelandreview.com/news/belgian-f-16s-arrive-in-iceland-for-nato-air-policing-mission/), the F-16s will conduct routine intercepts, airspace surveillance, and identification flights, working closely with the Icelandic Coast Guard and personnel from the "Loki" Control and Reporting Centre.

## A Growing Belgian Commitment

This deployment represents a significant step up in Belgium's Arctic engagement. In 2022, Belgium sent just one pilot to work alongside Portuguese forces in Iceland. The first full Belgian detachment arrived in August 2025, with four F-16s and approximately 100 personnel stationed at Keflavik for a month-long mission. As [Defence Industry Europe reported](https://defence-industry.eu/belgian-air-force-takes-over-nato-air-policing-in-iceland-with-first-keflavik-deployment/), that deployment marked the first time Belgian forces had been stationed at the Icelandic base.

The 2026 deployment was formalized under Belgium's Operational Plan, approved by the Council of Ministers on December 12, 2025. According to the [Belgian Ministry of Defence](https://www.mil.be/fr/news/le-conseil-des-ministres-a-approuve-le-plan-operationnel-2026/), 2026 marks the first time Belgium will conduct two Air Policing missions in a single year—the first from Iceland, the second from Lithuania or Poland under the Enhanced Air Policing framework.

## The Last F-16 Mission

This month-long deployment will be the final F-16 mission to Iceland. Belgium is transitioning to the F-35A stealth fighter to replace its aging F-16 fleet, with pilots already training on the new aircraft in Arizona. As military personnel on the ground explained to RTBF, "Iceland is an ideal training ground because with all this space, our new fighter jets will be able to deploy all their assets."

Defense Minister Theo Francken (N-VA), who traveled to Iceland to highlight the mission, emphasized the strategic importance of the deployment. "Iceland is a NATO country, a brother country, and it's very important to protect Iceland with air policing thanks to our F-16s," he told RTBF.

## Arctic Security in Focus

The deployment comes amid heightened tensions in the Arctic region. During the same trip, Francken announced that Belgium will send approximately 30 military personnel to Greenland in September as part of NATO's "Arctic Sentry" mission—an operation created in response to US President Donald Trump's threats to annex the island. As [RTL Info reported](https://www.rtl.be/actu/belgique/politique/lotan-souhaite-envoyer-un-message-clair-donald-trump-des-militaires-belges/2026-08-07/article/796654), the mission aims to send a "clear message" that NATO can protect the Arctic itself, with soldiers from the first brigade of Leopoldsburg and special forces members participating alongside forces from Canada, Denmark, France, Finland, the Netherlands, Slovenia, and Sweden.

## What to Watch

Belgium's expanding Arctic footprint reflects a broader NATO shift toward the High North, where Russian military activity continues to grow. The F-35 transition will bring enhanced capabilities for future deployments, while the second Air Policing mission later this year will test Belgium's ability to sustain simultaneous commitments across multiple theaters. With the Arctic increasingly contested, Belgium's dual missions in 2026 signal a lasting commitment to NATO's collective defense in the region.
