# Belgium to Deploy Troops to Greenland for NATO Arctic Mission

Belgium will send a delegation of military personnel to Greenland in September as part of the NATO mission 'Arctic Sentry', the cabinet of Belgian Defense Minister Theo Francken confirmed on Friday. The deployment, which runs from September 4 to 18, marks Belgium's concrete contribution to the alliance's effort to strengthen its presence in the strategically vital Arctic region.

## Context: The Arctic Sentry Mission

The mission was established in response to US President Donald Trump's repeated expressions of interest in acquiring Greenland, an autonomous territory within the Kingdom of Denmark. The presence on the island is designed to counter the American argument that security in the strategically important region is not sufficiently guaranteed, according to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/08/07/belgie-stuurt-militairen-naar-groenland/).

The mission also aims to strengthen security in and around Greenland, Iceland, and the Arctic Ocean—a region that has drawn increasing interest from Russia and China as climate change opens new sea routes and makes natural resources more accessible.

## Belgium's Contribution

The Belgian soldiers will participate in the mission from September 4 to 18. The deployment is described as a 'detachment', meaning the Ministry of Defense does not disclose the exact number of soldiers being deployed for operational security reasons. Both soldiers from the 1st Brigade from Leopoldsburg and members of the Special Forces will take part.

The announcement was first reported by VTM Nieuws and confirmed by Francken's cabinet. The minister announced the deployment during a visit to Iceland.

## Origins of the Mission

'Arctic Sentry' was proposed in January 2026 as tensions escalated dramatically when Trump threatened possible military intervention to take control of Greenland. The proposal reportedly originated from the United Kingdom and received support from northern NATO member states and Turkey, as [VRT NWS reported](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/01/09/kan-een-navo-missie-op-groenland-de-bom-onder-het-bondgenootscha/) in January.

Belgian Defense Minister Theo Francken was one of the early proponents of the mission. In an interview with Reuters in January, he called on NATO to launch a military operation in the High North, stating: "We must cooperate and demonstrate unity. There is a need for a NATO operation in the High North." He proposed using existing operations 'Baltic Sentry' and 'Eastern Sentry' as a model, as [UNN reported](https://unn.ua/en/news/belgian-defense-minister-calls-on-nato-to-launch-arctic-operation-over-trumps-claims-to-greenland).

NATO officially announced the beginning of 'Arctic Sentry' on February 11, 2026. The mission is led by the Joint Force Command Norfolk, a NATO operational headquarters in the United States whose area of responsibility was expanded to cover the entire Arctic and High North, according to [NRC](https://www.nrc.nl/nieuws/2026/02/11/navo-kondigt-het-begin-van-noordpoolmissie-arctic-sentry-aan-a4920128).

## Strategic Importance of the Arctic

The Arctic region has become increasingly strategically important due to climate change, which is opening new sea routes and making natural resources more accessible. NATO Secretary-General Mark Rutte emphasized the urgency of the mission, stating: "Given the intensified military activities of Russia and China's growing interest in the High North, it was crucial that we do more," as [Euronews reported](https://de.euronews.com/my-europe/2026/02/11/nato-bundelt-arktis-aktivitaten-kritik-arctic-sentry).

The mission consolidates existing activities, including Denmark's 'Arctic Endurance' exercises and Norway's upcoming 'Cold Response' exercise, under a single operational strategy. The UK has also announced it will double its troops in Norway from 1,000 to 2,000 within three years as part of Arctic Sentry.

## Broader European Response

Belgium was among several European countries that announced they would send military personnel to Greenland following the US threats in January. The deployment is part of a broader European effort to demonstrate commitment to Arctic security and address US concerns, as [NOS reported](https://nos.nl/collectie/14011/artikel/2598896-groenland-en-denemarken-dringen-aan-op-navo-missie-in-noordpoolgebied).

The mission also comes amid ongoing diplomatic negotiations between the US, Greenland, and Denmark. Greenlandic Prime Minister Jens-Frederik Nielsen stated in July that US pressure "remains strong" but that Greenland "will never be for sale," emphasizing the desire for partnerships with countries that share democratic values.

## What's Next

The Belgian deployment in September will be a concrete test of the alliance's ability to project force in the Arctic. As NATO members continue to navigate the complex geopolitics of the region, the mission's success will depend on maintaining unity among allies while addressing legitimate security concerns in the High North.

Observers will be watching how Russia and China respond to the increased NATO presence, and whether the mission can evolve from a crisis response into a lasting framework for Arctic security cooperation.
