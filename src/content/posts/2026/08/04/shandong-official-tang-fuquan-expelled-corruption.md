# Shandong Official Tang Fuquan Expelled for Corruption

Tang Fuquan, former deputy director of the Legal Affairs Committee of the Shandong Provincial People's Congress, has been expelled from the Communist Party and removed from public office for serious disciplinary violations and suspected bribery, according to an [official announcement](https://www.news.cn/legal/20260804/10fdd606895947bebc14126cfed783d7/c.html) published by Xinhua News on August 4.

The disciplinary action, announced by the Shandong Provincial Commission for Discipline Inspection and Supervision with approval from the Shandong Provincial Party Committee, concluded a months-long investigation into Tang's conduct. The case has been transferred to procuratorial authorities for criminal review and prosecution, with related assets also seized.

## Investigation Findings

The investigation found that Tang, who served in senior roles across Shandong for more than three decades, committed a range of violations spanning political, organizational, and integrity disciplines. According to the [official announcement](https://news.cctv.com/2026/08/04/ARTIiqgJ3Ir28MuVomCC7qUK260804.shtml) carried by CCTV News, Tang was found to have lost his ideals and beliefs, betrayed his original mission, and resisted organizational review. He also violated the Central Eight-Point Regulations by accepting banquets and travel arrangements that could affect the impartial execution of official duties.

The investigation further determined that Tang concealed personal matters, illegally sought personnel benefits for others, accepted gifts and cash cards, and illegally held shares in non-listed companies. He engaged in large-scale power-money transactions, using his position to profit from project contracting and business operations for others while illegally accepting substantial amounts of property.

## Career Trajectory

Tang's career followed a steady upward trajectory typical of senior Chinese officials. Born in September 1963 in Rongcheng, Shandong Province, he earned a master's degree in economics management from Shandong University before joining the provincial personnel department in 1989. Over the following decades, he held increasingly senior positions in Zibo City, including district head, district Party secretary, deputy mayor, and member of the municipal Party committee standing committee.

In 2014, Tang was transferred to Tai'an City as a member of the municipal Party committee standing committee and director of the organization department. He was elected director of the Tai'an Municipal People's Congress Standing Committee in 2017, becoming a zhengting-level (department-level) official. In 2023, he moved to the provincial level as deputy director of the Legal Affairs Committee of the Shandong Provincial People's Congress.

## Investigation Timeline

Tang was first placed under investigation on March 13, 2026, when the Shandong Provincial Commission for Discipline Inspection announced he was suspected of serious disciplinary and legal violations. His investigation came amid a broader anti-corruption push in Shandong, with [Lianhe Zaobao reporting](https://www.zaobao.com.sg/news/china/story20260313-8727376) that five Shandong officials were announced as being investigated over a two-day period in mid-March, including three at the zhengting level.

On March 31, Tang's qualifications as a deputy to both the 14th Shandong Provincial People's Congress and the 18th Tai'an City People's Congress were terminated after resignations were accepted. His position as deputy director of the Legal Affairs Committee was correspondingly terminated.

## Broader Pattern

Tang's case is part of a notable pattern of anti-corruption enforcement targeting the Shandong Provincial People's Congress Legal Affairs Committee. He is the third consecutive director or deputy director of the committee to be investigated and punished for corruption, following Li Shaohua, who was expelled from the Party in December 2021, and Chen Yong, who was sentenced to 11 years and 6 months in prison in August 2023 for accepting bribes totaling over 16.2 million yuan.

Tang's predecessor as head of the Tai'an Municipal People's Congress, Cui Honggang, was sentenced to 14 years and 6 months in prison in November 2024 for accepting bribes totaling over 52.99 million yuan. Cui had also served as Party Secretary of Tai'an City before moving to the provincial People's Congress.

## Significance

The official announcement noted that Tang did not restrain himself or stop after the 18th Party Congress, a standard but significant phrase indicating that his misconduct continued well into the current anti-corruption campaign era. His case demonstrates that the anti-corruption campaign continues to target officials even in positions often viewed as semi-retirement posts, such as provincial people's congress committees.

According to a [career analysis](https://www.toutiao.com/article/7616980484979851814/) published on Toutiao, Tang's trajectory highlights a recurring pattern in which officials who reach senior positions late in their careers may be tempted to abuse their authority. The analysis noted that people's congress and political consultative conference roles are no longer "safe havens" from anti-corruption scrutiny.

## What's Next

Tang's case has been transferred to procuratorial authorities for review and prosecution on suspicion of bribery. His illegal gains have been confiscated, and his qualification as a delegate to the 12th Tai'an City Party Congress has been terminated. The case adds to the ongoing nationwide anti-corruption campaign that has continued since the 18th Party Congress, with observers watching for further developments in Shandong's political and legal systems.
