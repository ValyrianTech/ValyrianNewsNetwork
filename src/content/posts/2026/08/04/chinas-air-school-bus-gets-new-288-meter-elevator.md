# China's 'Air School Bus' Gets New 288-Meter Elevator

A second cliffside elevator has begun operations in the Nizhu River Grand Canyon in Yunnan Province, China, expanding the country's only dual-elevator "air school bus" system that carries mountain students to their school at the canyon rim. The new 288-meter "Fuyao Ladder" elevator, which officially launched on July 31, stands alongside the 268-meter "Qingyun Ladder" commissioned in 2022, together with a high-altitude cable car system reducing students' one-way commute from over three hours of treacherous cliff climbing to approximately 30 minutes, according to [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/20260803/d69e3271df1d4be0ab4502e984f787b0/c.html).

## A Generational Challenge Overcome

Nizhuhe Village sits at the bottom of the canyon at approximately 1,100 meters elevation, while Guanzhai Village Complete Primary School stands at 1,650 meters on the canyon rim—a vertical difference of about 550 meters. For generations, children from the valley floor had to scale cliffside paths, navigating dangerous sections nicknamed "Bat Cave," "Gecko Road," and an 11-meter vertical cliff called "Plank Wall Cliff," as [CCTV News](https://www.cqnews.net/1/detail/1327561398921396224/web/content_1327561398921396224.html) documented.

Zhao Suzhen, a mother who experienced both eras, described the transformation: "Before, sending my eldest to school meant climbing the cliff by hands and feet, taking over 3 hours each way, 6-7 hours round trip. Now my second child takes only half an hour from home to school."

## Infrastructure as a Lifeline

In 2017, Xuanwei City introduced the "Nizhu River Grand Canyon Eco-Cultural Tourism Project" to address both tourism development and the community's transportation needs. Construction of cliff elevators began in 2018, and when the scenic area opened in 2022, the first elevator and cable car system became operational—free for local villagers and students, creating the first "air school bus." The canyon is now a 4A-rated scenic area.

The new "Fuyao Ladder" elevator travels at 5 meters per second, completing a one-way trip in 57.6 seconds. With both elevators now operating, the combined hourly capacity has tripled from 400 to 1,200 passengers, as [CNR](https://www.cnr.cn/yn/gstjyn/20260801/t20260801_527740553.shtml) reported. This expansion addresses growing tourist demand while ensuring students retain priority access.

"The new elevator makes livelihood security more solid and cultural tourism development more orderly," said Cai Xiong, chairman of the scenic area's development company, noting that single-elevator capacity had become insufficient as tourist numbers surged.

## Free Access and Safety Measures

Both cliff elevators and the cable car system remain free for residents of Nizhuhe and Guanzhai villages, as well as enrolled students. The local government and scenic area have established dedicated student corridors, with police officers and staff stationed at fixed points during morning and evening peak hours. As of the end of July, the "air school bus" has safely transported nearly 1,000 student trips, according to [People's Daily](http://www.jingji.com.cn/jy/202608/t20260803_3125020.shtml).

The school currently serves 51 students in grades 1-5, with 9 students commuting from Nizhuhe Village. Student Chen Honghan, who previously fell on the cliff path, now marvels: "The elevator is so fast, we're at the top in the blink of an eye." Classmate Lei Xin added, "The cable car is like our 'school bus.' We no longer have to work so hard climbing rocks."

## Beyond the Classroom: Rural Revitalization

The "air school bus" has catalyzed broader economic transformation. Once isolated, the mountain village has become a popular tourist destination, with residents opening homestays, working as tour guides, and selling local products like canyon ham and wild mountain mushrooms. The tourism industry has enabled over 1,600 residents from more than 300 households to earn income without leaving home, according to the [International Poverty Reduction Center in China](https://www.iprcc.org/article/4LcMVFIuNbo). Some migrant workers have returned to start businesses, and school enrollment has remained stable as children return with their parents.

## A Broader Pattern of 'Infrastructure Warmth'

This story is part of a larger narrative about Chinese infrastructure designed to serve people's needs in remote areas. A [CCTV News commentary](https://www.cqnews.net/web/content_1534094422686502912.html) connected the "air school bus" to other examples, including the Tianshan Shengli Tunnel—the world's longest expressway tunnel at 22.13 kilometers, which cut travel time across the Tianshan Mountains from 3 hours to 20 minutes—and the "Little Slow Train" on the Chengdu-Kunming Railway in Sichuan's Liangshan area, which has operated for over half a century with 2-yuan minimum fares and now includes a mobile library.

As one online commenter reflecting on the "air school bus" story wrote: "Finally, someone has built this path to learning for the children who came after, so they don't have to experience what I went through."

## What's Next

The dual-elevator system represents a milestone in integrating tourism development with community welfare, but questions remain about long-term sustainability. As student numbers evolve and tourism continues to grow, the scenic area will need to balance visitor access with the priority needs of students and residents. The success of this model in the Nizhu River Grand Canyon may also inform similar infrastructure solutions for other remote mountain communities across China facing comparable educational access challenges.
