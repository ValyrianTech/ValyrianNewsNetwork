# China Disciplines 1,535 Anti-Corruption Cadres in H1

China's Central Commission for Discipline Inspection (CCDI) announced on August 4 that 1,535 discipline inspection and supervision cadres were disciplined in the first half of 2026, underscoring the country's continued commitment to internal accountability within its anti-corruption apparatus. According to [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/legal/20260804/b543da4a628e45c084537442e4ef9abb/c.html), the CCDI and National Supervisory Commission reported that 1,685 cadres were placed on file for investigation during the period, with 138 transferred to judicial authorities.

## Key Figures and Breakdown

The data reveals that 59 department/bureau-level cadres and 356 county/division-level cadres were among those disciplined. The CCDI received over 15,500 leads or reports involving discipline inspection cadres and processed more than 14,200 of these leads. Additionally, 4,385 talks and inquiries were conducted with cadres under review.

According to [CCTV News](https://news.cctv.com/2026/08/04/ARTIxC5A4v9q3XcwqiqgWckV260804.shtml), the CCDI applied its graduated "Four Forms" supervision system to 5,999 person-times during the first half of the year. This included 4,397 instances of criticism and education (first form), 1,219 light disciplinary measures (second form), 222 heavy disciplinary measures (third form), and 161 criminal referrals (fourth form).

## Context: The "Three Modernizations" Campaign

The announcement is closely tied to the CCDI's "Three Modernizations" construction year action—an initiative focused on standardization, rule of law, and regularization within the discipline inspection system. Launched in January 2025 and deployed at the Fifth Plenary Session of the 20th Central Commission for Discipline Inspection in January 2026, the campaign aims to improve case-handling quality, strengthen team building, and enhance training.

At a July 7 promotion meeting, CCDI Secretary Li Xi emphasized the "one year key breakthrough, two years systematic improvement, three years consolidation and deepening" arrangement, signaling the campaign will continue through 2027, as reported by the [Zhejiang Provincial Commission for Discipline Inspection](https://www.zjsjw.gov.cn/toutiao/202607/t20260708_24148379.shtml).

## Trend Analysis

Comparing data from previous years reveals notable trends. According to [People's Daily](http://fanfu.people.com.cn/n1/2025/0801/c64371-40534173.html), 1,666 discipline inspection cadres were disciplined in H1 2025, and 2,003 in H1 2024. The current figure of 1,535 represents a continued downward trend in the total number of cadres disciplined.

However, the data also suggests that cases are becoming more serious. The 138 cadres transferred to judicial authorities in H1 2026 is significantly higher than the 101 transferred in H1 2025. Similarly, the 59 department/bureau-level cadres disciplined exceeds the 49 recorded in the same period last year, indicating enforcement is reaching higher ranks.

The proportion of more severe disciplinary measures has also increased. The combined 383 instances of third and fourth form measures in H1 2026 surpasses the 325 recorded in H1 2025, suggesting that a greater share of violations uncovered are of a more serious nature.

## Broader Anti-Corruption Landscape

The CCDI's broader H1 2026 data, released on July 27, shows the scale of China's overall anti-corruption effort. According to data published by the [State Administration for Market Regulation](https://www.samr.gov.cn:8085/jjz/ywdt/art/2026/art_0a59a52e93cc4ecf92a7da8cc6c8e259.html), the CCDI received 2.188 million letters and visits, processed 1.274 million problem clues, filed 538,000 cases for investigation, and disciplined 412,000 people nationwide. This included 74 provincial/ministerial-level or above cadres and 2,274 department/bureau-level cadres.

The internal discipline data is part of a broader pattern of the CCDI demonstrating its willingness to hold its own members accountable. As [Guangming Daily](https://politics.gmw.cn/2026-08/04/content_38925629.htm) reported, the CCDI statement emphasized that discipline inspection organs at all levels are "striving to forge a discipline inspection and supervision iron army that is politically qualified, capable, with excellent conduct, and clean and honest."

## What to Watch

With the "Three Modernizations" campaign set to continue through 2027, observers will be watching whether the downward trend in the number of disciplined cadres continues while the severity of cases increases. The higher proportion of senior-level cadres facing discipline, combined with more criminal referrals, suggests the CCDI is intensifying its focus on rooting out corruption within its own ranks at the highest levels.

As the campaign enters its "systematic improvement" phase in 2026-2027, the emphasis on institutional governance and standardized procedures is likely to shape how internal oversight evolves in the coming years. The CCDI's commitment to "turning the blade inward" remains a central pillar of China's broader anti-corruption strategy.
