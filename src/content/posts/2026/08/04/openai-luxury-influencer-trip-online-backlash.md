# OpenAI's Luxury Influencer Trip Draws Online Backlash

OpenAI hosted its first-ever influencer brand trip at a luxury resort in New York's Hudson Valley over the weekend, but the event quickly sparked a wave of online criticism, with detractors calling it "dystopian" and accusing the AI company of "greenwashing." The two-day retreat, dubbed "Summer Club," was held at Wildflower Farms in Gardiner, New York, where weekend room rates typically exceed $2,000 per night, according to [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/openai-brand-trip-influencer-backlash-rcna590647).

## The Retreat

The 140-acre estate, surrounded by meadows, woodlands, orchards, and views of the Shawangunk Ridge, played host to roughly 30 content creators who were treated to farm-to-table dinners, beekeeping sessions, painting workshops, wellness activities, and classes on OpenAI's latest tools. Attendees received OpenAI-branded canvas bags and jars of honey stamped with the company's logo, as [Business Insider](https://www.businessinsider.com/openai-creators-retreat-upstate-new-york-backlash-2026-8) reported.

"I'm still processing how insanely EPIC the last 72 hours has been," AI creator Justyn Warner wrote on Instagram, describing the experience as being surrounded by "some of the best creators on the planet and some of the brightest minds in AI."

OpenAI said the retreat was education-led, focused on teaching creators how to use the newly released ChatGPT Work tool, which helps users draft documents and presentations. Spokesperson Drew Pusateri said the event was part of a broader marketing effort to help people understand practical ways to use ChatGPT.

"Creators are an important part of our community and how people get information and learn about our products today," Pusateri said. "We welcome healthy debate as AI becomes more prevalent, and we value creators who choose to engage with us, attend our events, ask tough questions, and learn alongside everyone else."

## The Backlash

Despite the idyllic setting, the response online was swift and unforgiving. Critics across TikTok, Reddit, X, and Instagram called the trip "dystopian" and accused OpenAI of "greenwashing" amid growing concerns about AI's environmental impact, as [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/03/influencers-draw-backlash-for-attending-openais-first-luxury-trip/) reported.

"This is the proper context to use the word dystopian," one Reddit user wrote in the r/NYCinfluencersnark thread about the event.

Another X user remarked: "The concept of attending an AI brand trip surrounded by nature while it actively hurts the environment is an incredible bit."

The criticism was amplified by the timing. OpenAI is reportedly close to a $500 billion deal to build a 10-gigawatt data center campus in Pike County, Ohio, with Nvidia potentially providing roughly $250 billion in financial guarantees. Commenters also raised the company's $200 million contract with the Department of Defense, awarded in June 2025.

One commenter who left a negative review on an influencer's video told TechCrunch: "These posh influencers' events are, again, giving people ammunition to have negative opinions. The world is on fire. It's not a great time to brag about your $5,000-a-night suite."

## Environmental Context

The backlash comes against a backdrop of mounting public concern about AI's physical footprint. The International Energy Agency projects global data-center electricity consumption will more than double between 2024 and 2030, with AI servers driving nearly half of that growth. US data centers directly consumed an estimated 17 billion gallons of water in 2023, as [Mashable](https://mashable.com/life/openai-influencer-brand-trip-backlash) noted.

Community opposition to data centers has been growing. At least 75 proposed data centers worth approximately $130 billion were delayed or blocked during the first quarter of 2026, and a June report identified 430 local Facebook groups with more than 525,000 combined members opposing data center projects. Reuters reported 142 AI data center protests across 42 states.

OpenAI has attempted to address these concerns. On July 22, the company announced Project Camellia, a data-center campus planned for rural Georgia, promising "minimal" water use and no increase to residents' electricity bills.

## Creators Defend Themselves

Some attendees pushed back against the criticism. Career and business creator Grace McCarrick called the backlash "shocking AI psychosis" in a LinkedIn post, saying she had no idea so many people felt this strongly about the technology. "It didn't for one second occur to me that it could be controversial," she wrote.

In a TikTok response, McCarrick said: "If I have the opportunity to be in the room and hear from people who are at the helm of change, I can do that. It's actually well within my moral system."

Tech journalist Taylor Lorenz noted the discrepancy in public reaction, writing on X: "Influencers are getting cancelled for going on the OpenAI brand summer camp weekend trip, which is very interesting bc I have not seen similar criticism towards influencers who have been attending Anthropic events."

## OpenAI's Influence Strategy

The retreat is part of a broader push by OpenAI to improve its public image. In February, the company hired Charles Porch, Instagram's longtime VP of global partnerships, as its first VP of global creative partnerships. In April, OpenAI acquired TBPN, a daily technology talk show described as "SportsCenter for Silicon Valley," in a deal reportedly worth hundreds of millions of dollars.

Psychologist and researcher Dr. Anna Kallschmidt suggested the strategy is rooted in a trust deficit. "Without trust, you have nothing," she said in a TikTok video. "That is why these companies...are bringing these people in because they know people don't trust them, and they're hoping by bringing in faces that you trust, that trust will build onto [OpenAI]."

As [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/openai-summer-camp-influencer-retreat-greenwashing-backlash) observed, the trip succeeded in getting people to talk about OpenAI—though perhaps not in the way the company intended. "The image problem was the reason for the trip," the outlet noted. "The trip became the image problem."

## What's Next

OpenAI has not disclosed whether attendees were paid, whether the company covered travel and accommodations, or whether creators were expected to post about the event. The company said it "welcomes healthy debate" as AI becomes more prevalent.

As public skepticism toward AI continues to harden, the episode underscores the challenge facing AI companies seeking to build trust through influencer marketing. The question now is whether OpenAI's broader outreach efforts—from the TBPN acquisition to its creative partnerships program—can shift the narrative, or whether events like Summer Club will continue to fuel the very criticism they aim to counter.
