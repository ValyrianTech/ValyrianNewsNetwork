# Thailand's IP Goes Global: The 'Traffic Password' Explained

Thailand's popular intellectual property (IP) "Butter Bear" (黄油小熊) recently made a splash in Shanghai, debuting new characters across 32 art installations that drew crowds of consumers. The exhibition marks the latest milestone in a remarkable global ascent for Thai cultural exports—a wave that has carried characters like Butter Bear, CRYBABY (哭娃), and the viral pygmy hippo Moo Deng (弹跳猪) from local obscurity to international stardom. As [People's Daily](http://world.people.com.cn/n1/2026/0803/c1002-40772469.html) reports, the phenomenon reveals what analysts call the "traffic password" behind Thailand's IP success—a formula blending emotional resonance, social media virality, government support, and strategic partnerships with China's mature IP operations ecosystem.

## The Emotional Core of Thai IP Appeal

At the heart of Thai IP's global appeal lies a profound shift in consumer behavior, particularly among Generation Z. McKinsey research cited by People's Daily shows that compared to product functionality and durability, many Gen Z consumers' purchasing decisions are more influenced by emotional value and community identity. Thai IPs have tapped into this shift with remarkable precision.

Butter Bear's sweet dance routines, Moo Deng's carefree bathing clips, and CRYBABY's embrace of vulnerability offer something increasingly rare in commercial culture: authentic emotional connection. As [ThaiCyclopedia](https://thaicyclopedia.com/butterbear-the-thai-bakery-chain-winning-hearts-with-heart-healing-marketing/) explains, Butter Bear's "heart-healing marketing" strategy "addresses the emotional need for relief from daily stress, offering a sense of calm and positivity through pure, wholesome content." The character's presentation as a mischievous three-year-old bear attending school—complete with daily routines shared in private fan communities—creates a parasocial bond that transforms a bakery mascot into a beloved "comfort character."

This emotional authenticity extends to the characters' imperfect, relatable qualities. Butter Bear's "ordinary office worker" life and CRYBABY's encouragement to express vulnerability deliberately strip away the polished perfection of traditional mascots, allowing consumers to project themselves onto these characters and complete their own identity formation.

## Social Media: The Engine of Virality

If emotional resonance is the core, social media's viral spread is the engine of hot sales, as People's Daily notes. Butter Bear's dance videos, Moo Deng's bathing clips, and CRYBABY's emotional themes have all achieved massive organic reach across TikTok, Instagram, and Chinese platforms like Xiaohongshu.

Butter Bear's rise demonstrates the power of this approach. With over 630,000 Instagram followers and more than half a million TikTok followers, the character has become a genuine influencer—releasing viral hit songs, hosting fan meetings, and even winning "Phenomenon of the Year" at the MChoice & Mint Awards 2024. The brand's dedicated Chinese marketing team has helped it amass over 1 million fans in China, with hashtag #黄油小熊 generating over 110 million views on Xiaohongshu alone, according to [Global Trekker](https://www.globaltrekker.org/home/the-marketing-mastermind-behind-bangkok-butterbear-bakerys-success).

Moo Deng's impact has been equally dramatic. After the baby pygmy hippo went viral in September 2024, visitor numbers at Khao Kheow Open Zoo in Chonburi Province surged fourfold, with [Pattaya Mail](https://www.pattayamail.com/latestnews/news/khao-kheow-open-zoo-overflows-with-visitors-as-moo-deng-draws-huge-crowds-over-long-holiday-475953) reporting massive traffic jams stretching over three kilometers as fans flocked to see the adorable star during the October long holiday.

## Government Support and the Creative Economy

Thailand's IP boom is not accidental—it is underpinned by systematic government policy. In 2025, the Thai government approved the "2026-2027 Intellectual Property Development Plan," which improves the legal framework, strengthens infringement enforcement, and empowers small and medium enterprises. The Creative Economy Agency (CEA) is simultaneously expanding creative and design centers, opening content laboratories, and increasing creative talent cultivation.

The results are measurable. Thailand's creative economy is now valued at $42.4 billion (1.44 trillion baht), accounting for 8.01% of the nation's GDP, according to [The Nation Thailand](https://www.nationthailand.com/business/economy/40061152). Creative economy exports total 378 billion baht ($11.1 billion), with a workforce of nearly one million people. Dr. Chakrit Pichyangkul, Executive Director of the CEA, noted that the sector "has proven more resilient than the broader economy during recent global crises."

Thailand's character industry alone has reached approximately 7.23 billion baht in size. Butter Bear's bakery full industry chain revenue reached 577 million baht in 2025, while CRYBABY contributed nearly 3 billion RMB in revenue to Pop Mart in the same year.

## The Chinese Market Synergy

A distinctive feature of Thailand's IP success story is the role of China's mature IP operations ecosystem. As People's Daily observes, "The deep combination of China's IP operation system with creative content from various countries is helping more IPs go global."

After Butter Bear went viral, Chinese agents quickly introduced mature IP incubation models, converting content heat into brand assets in the short term. The character has opened themed pop-up stores in China—including the recent Shanghai exhibition at HKRI Taikoo Hui featuring 32 art installations—and maintains deep collaboration with a famous Japanese amusement park, integrating the IP into park experiences. The [Inner Mongolia Hotel News](https://www.innermongoliahotel.cn/newsdetail-819579.html) reports that the Shanghai exhibition featured city-exclusive merchandise including a Butter Bear magnolia pendant and modern polka dot pendant, alongside collaborations with brands like CASETiFY and LADY M.

CRYBABY's trajectory illustrates the power of this synergy. After being signed by Pop Mart, the Thai artist-created character transformed from a standalone figure into full-category products, leveraging the company's global retail network and localized marketing to become a globally popular IP. As [36Kr](https://letschuhai.com/d29b57cf) reports, Pop Mart's Southeast Asia expansion—including the Labubu IP theme store that opened in Bangkok in November 2024 with first-day revenue exceeding 10 million RMB—demonstrates how Chinese operational expertise can amplify regional creative content.

## A Global Phenomenon with Lasting Implications

From a global perspective, IP has become a commercially influential brand asset reshaping the competitive logic of retail, tourism, and creative industries worldwide. Whether it's Disney's cross-generational fairy tales or Pop Mart's blind box surprises, successful IP commercialization increasingly shares common threads: strengthening emotional value, leveraging social media spread, and driving diversified consumption.

But the real test lies ahead. As People's Daily cautions, "How to avoid being a flash in the pan, and to settle from a temporary trend into a classic with long-term vitality, is a common industry proposition." For Thai IPs and the global creative economy alike, the challenge is not just going viral—it's building the sustained content innovation and market adaptability needed to transform fleeting trends into enduring cultural and commercial assets.

With government backing, a growing creative workforce, and deepening international partnerships, Thailand appears well-positioned to meet that challenge. The question now is whether the "traffic password" that unlocked global audiences can also unlock long-term sustainability.
