# Flanders invests €1.1M in Ypres war cemetery restoration

The Flemish government is allocating €1.13 million toward the restoration of four military cemeteries in and around Ypres, Belgium, preserving final resting places of approximately 10,000 soldiers from the First World War. The funding, announced by Flemish Heritage Minister Ben Weyts (N-VA), will address deteriorating boundary walls that have become unstable at several of the sites.

The restoration will cover Railway Dugouts Burial Ground and Bedford House Cemetery in Zillebeke, White House Cemetery in Sint-Jan, and Belgian Battery Corner Cemetery in Ypres itself, according to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/08/03/vlaanderen-trekt-1-1-miljoen-euro-uit-voor-restauratie-van-4-oor/).

## A region scarred by war

The Ypres region, known as the Westhoek, was one of the most heavily contested areas of the Western Front during World War I. The front line remained largely static in the Ypres Salient for years, with tens of thousands of soldiers from countries including Canada, India, Australia, New Zealand, and the United Kingdom losing their lives in the area.

The four cemeteries being restored contain the remains of soldiers from many Commonwealth nations. Railway Dugouts Burial Ground alone holds 2,459 Commonwealth burials and commemorations, including 1,358 from the United Kingdom and 551 from Canada, according to [Remembering My Soldiers](https://www.rememberingmysoldiers.be/belgium/west-vlaanderen-r-z/railway-dugouts-burial-ground-1). Bedford House Cemetery, the largest of the four, contains 5,139 Commonwealth servicemen of WWI buried or commemorated.

## Restoration details and funding

The walls surrounding the cemeteries have deteriorated in several places and are at risk of becoming unstable. The restoration work will include repairing foundations, repointing walls, and replacing damaged bricks and coping stones, with existing materials reused where possible.

The total cost of the works is estimated at €1.7 million. Flanders, through the Agency for Immovable Heritage, will fund 66 percent of the total, while the [Commonwealth War Graves Commission](https://www.cwgc.org/visit-us/find-cemeteries-memorials/cemetery-details/8900/belgian-battery-corner-cemetery/) (CWGC) will carry out the works and cover the remaining costs.

"We want to continue conveying the message 'Never again war,' and we do that partly by restoring those cemeteries in honor as serene witnesses to the suffering of war," Minister Weyts said. "Together with the Commonwealth countries, we ensure that these cemeteries remain in good condition, so that they continue to radiate the respect that the fallen deserve. We will never forget their sacrifice and we will never neglect their final resting place."

## UNESCO World Heritage recognition

The restoration aligns with obligations under the UNESCO World Heritage listing. On 20 September 2023, UNESCO designated the "Funerary and memory sites of the First World War (Western Front)" as a World Heritage Site, incorporating 139 cemeteries and memorials across Belgium and France. Bedford House Cemetery is among the 27 Belgian sites included in the listing, as documented on [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funerary_and_memory_sites_of_the_First_World_War_(Western_Front)).

The [Brussels Times](https://www.brusselstimes.com/2257475/flanders-is-investing-1-13-million-euros-in-the-restoration-of-war-cemeteries-in-ypres-tbtb) reports that the project reflects a shared international responsibility for maintaining these historic sites, with the CWGC managing cemeteries and memorials in over 150 countries worldwide.

## Remembrance and tourism

The Ypres region attracts hundreds of thousands of visitors annually who come to pay their respects and learn about the history of the First World War. The preservation of these cemeteries is both culturally and economically significant, supporting remembrance tourism while honoring the sacrifices of those who fought in the region.

Restoration works at Belgian Battery Corner Cemetery are scheduled between June 2026 and February 2027, according to the CWGC cemetery page, with work at the other cemeteries expected to follow a similar timeline.

## What to watch for

As restoration proceeds, visitors to the cemeteries may encounter temporary access restrictions. The Flemish government and the CWGC will continue their partnership to ensure these sacred sites remain in good condition for future generations, upholding the promise that the fallen of the Ypres Salient will never be forgotten.
