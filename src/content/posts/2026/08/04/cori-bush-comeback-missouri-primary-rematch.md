# Cori Bush Seeks Comeback in Missouri Primary Rematch

Former U.S. Rep. Cori Bush is attempting a political comeback Tuesday in a Missouri Democratic primary that tests evolving views on American-Israeli relations, just two years after the progressive "Squad" member was ousted by voters in favor of [Wesley Bell](https://apnews.com/article/missouri-election-2026-primary-5226443026ad3b9a3e9a5979abb910a9). The rematch in the heavily Democratic St. Louis-based 1st Congressional District is one of the most closely watched primaries of the 2026 midterm cycle.

## A Rematch Rooted in Ferguson

Both candidates trace their political careers to the Ferguson protests that followed the 2014 police shooting of Michael Brown. Bush, a nurse and pastor, provided mental health support to protesters and rose through the Black Lives Matter movement before defeating longtime Rep. William Lacy Clay in 2020, ending a half-century political dynasty. Bell, the son of a police officer, won election to the Ferguson City Council in 2015 and later defeated a seven-term St. Louis County prosecutor in 2018 after promising to reopen the Brown case.

The two former allies now find themselves in a bitter rematch that has become a proxy battle over the Democratic Party's direction. Bush lost to Bell by nearly 6 percentage points in 2024 (51% to 46%), with the American Israel Public Affairs Committee (AIPAC) spending approximately $8.6 million through its super PAC to help oust her. She blamed her loss on AIPAC and told supporters that "all they did was radicalize me."

## AIPAC's Role and the Israel Divide

The contest between Bush and Bell tests how much the Democratic Party has shifted over the past two years. Bush was one of the earliest and most forceful critics in Congress of Israel's response to the October 7, 2023 Hamas attack, calling Israel's actions in Gaza a genocide. Bell has maintained strong support for Israel, voting against an amendment to cut military aid and describing the country as a key ally.

AIPAC has spent about $3.1 million in July alone against Bush and in support of Bell, according to [AP News](https://apnews.com/article/missouri-primary-bell-bush-cleaver-b2357852fa9acf304ddb7e9f276228f4). The group's political action committee has invested more than $1.2 million against Bush this year through United Democracy Project. But public opinion has shifted significantly among Democrats since the 2024 primary. An AP-NORC poll found that about 58% of Democrats now say the U.S. is "too supportive" of Israelis, up from 45% in January 2024.

"People are beginning to understand that — much like the crypto industry, AI, Big Oil, and Big Pharma — AIPAC has become yet another way for MAGA-donors to divide the Democratic base by meddling in primaries," Bush told the [Jewish Telegraphic Agency](https://www.jta.org/2026/08/03/default/cori-bush-promised-to-bury-aipac-her-rematch-with-wesley-bell-in-missouri-tests-that).

Bell has dismissed such attacks, emphasizing his work on the House Armed Services and Foreign Affairs committees. "I'm not a fan of Netanyahu, but I think it's important to stand with all of our allies," he told [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/aug/03/missouri-senate-primary-cori-bush-wesley-bell).

## Democratic Socialist Momentum

Bush, a member of the Democratic Socialists of America, has drawn support from organizers who have traveled from across the country to campaign for her. In recent weeks, Democratic voters in New York City and Denver ousted incumbents in favor of candidates who promised to take a hard line against support for Israel. Progressives hope a Bush victory would underscore their movement's momentum.

"I think her victory this cycle would be monumental," said Usamah Andrabi, a spokesperson for Justice Democrats. "We haven't had a lot of cycles to try this out, but this would be the first time that we would be able to take a seat back from Aipac after they bought one and deliver it back to working-class people."

Former Rep. Jamaal Bowman, who was also defeated in 2024 by an AIPAC-backed challenger, called Bush "the knockout blow to AIPAC." Bush has earned endorsements from Sen. Bernie Sanders and Rep. Rashida Tlaib, while Bell rallied with former House Speaker Nancy Pelosi.

## Campaign Finance and Controversies

The race has been marked by mutual accusations of misconduct. Bell has highlighted a previous Justice Department investigation into Bush's campaign spending, including how she paid her husband as part of her security detail. Bush has criticized Bell over a lawsuit from protesters who allege they were assaulted at his town hall, and a settlement over employment discrimination claims.

Bell has significantly outraised Bush, bringing in $3.5 million compared to Bush's $1.3 million. In 2024, Bell raised $5.4 million and Bush raised $4.2 million. Despite the fundraising gap, a poll commissioned by Bush's campaign in April showed the candidates statistically tied, with Bell at 44% and Bush at 40%.

"She's not a serious candidate," Bell said in an interview. "In this election, the choices are clear. I wake up every day working for St. Louis, and the folks in St. Louis know that I'm going to wake up working for them."

## A Fiery Debate

The two candidates clashed in a combative debate on St. Louis Public Radio on July 24, trading barbs over campaign spending and Israel. Bell accused Bush of not working hard enough to elect Kamala Harris in 2024, referencing a comment she made in an October 2024 interview. Bush defended her record, saying, "Did I call them fascists? No. There was a rise in fascism, starting with the Trump administration."

Both candidates have made recovery from last year's deadly tornado a key campaign issue. Bell blamed President Donald Trump for blocking disaster relief funds, while Bush cited her ability to secure quick relief after floods in 2022.

## What's at Stake

Beyond the 1st District, Missouri voters are also weighing in on several other consequential races. In western Missouri, Republicans are picking a candidate to challenge Democratic Rep. Emanuel Cleaver in a dramatically reshaped 5th District that is part of Trump's attempt to retain a slim House majority. In northern Missouri, Republicans are choosing a successor to retiring Rep. Sam Graves. Two ballot measures are also on the ballot, including one that would require citizen-initiated amendments to pass in each congressional district and another that would phase out the individual income tax.

The Bush-Bell rematch will serve as one of the clearest referendums on Democrats' shifting political landscape — including anger at the party establishment, rising skepticism of the U.S. alliance with Israel, and the growing clout of democratic socialists. As [The Intercept](https://theintercept.com/2026/08/01/missouri-cori-bush-wesley-bell-primary-aipac/) reported, Bush told supporters that "the momentum met me" this cycle. "The community asked me to run. The community came to me and said, 'Cori, where is our fighter?'"

Polls close at 7 p.m. CT (8 p.m. ET). The outcome will signal whether the progressive wing of the Democratic Party can reclaim ground lost to AIPAC-backed challengers — and whether the political winds have shifted enough to send Bush back to Washington.
