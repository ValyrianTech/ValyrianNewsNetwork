# Ethics Panel Recommends Censure for GOP Rep. Chuck Edwards

The House Ethics Committee recommended Monday that the full House censure Rep. Chuck Edwards (R-N.C.) after finding that he engaged in "persistent unprofessional and inappropriate conduct" toward two young female staffers. The bipartisan panel concluded there was "substantial reason to believe" Edwards violated House rules related to sexual harassment and hostile workplaces, according to [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/congress/ethics-panel-recommends-censure-gop-rep-chuck-edwards-inappropriate-co-rcna590649).

The recommendation follows a monthslong investigation into the second-term congressman, who represents North Carolina's 11th Congressional District. The full House will consider whether to adopt the censure when it returns from summer recess on August 31.

## The Committee's Findings

The 25-page report, released alongside a 60-page appendix of messages and exhibits, detailed a sustained pattern of behavior that the committee said violated the House Code of Official Conduct. Edwards gave the two staffers lavish gifts—including jewelry totaling over $1,000, designer purses, guns, shoes, flowers, a laptop and cell phone, a KitchenAid mixer, and a robotic vacuum—invited them to "intimate dinners and vacations," and sent notes expressing his "effusive affection" for them, as [WUNC](https://www.wunc.org/2026-08-03/house-ethics-committee-recommends-censure-for-rep-chuck-edwards-over-inappropriate-conduct-toward-female-staffers) reported.

The committee conducted 16 witness interviews and reviewed nearly 1,500 pages of evidence before voting to issue the report on July 22, according to [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/08/03/nx-s1-5918341/chuck-edwards-ethics-report). While the panel found no evidence that Edwards engaged in sexual activity with or propositioned any of his staffers, it concluded that his behavior violated the spirit of House rules prohibiting sexual harassment and unwanted advances.

"A member of Congress should not single out a staffer and isolate them from other staff, make frequent comments regarding that person's appearance and weight, send messages in the middle of the night, text staff deeply personal and seemingly romantic thoughts, intrude on personal occasions without invitation, pay for personal vacations, and spend thousands of dollars on gifts for staffers," the committee wrote, as [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/aug/03/house-ethics-panel-north-carolina-chuck-edwards-censure) reported.

## Specific Allegations

The report catalogued numerous instances of inappropriate behavior. Edwards skipped four House roll-call votes in December 2024 to decorate one staffer's Christmas tree, according to [the Asheville Citizen Times](https://www.citizen-times.com/story/news/local/2026/08/04/chuck-edwards-ethics-report-missed-votes-canceled-town-hall/91163946007/). "Here's another offer I hope you'll take me up on, unless you've made other arrangements," he texted the staffer. "What if I skip votes and help you decorate your tree once you get home tomorrow?"

In another instance, Edwards canceled a tele-town hall with constituents in October 2025 to have dinner with the same staffer in Charlotte. He also had approximately 30 different types of ice cream delivered to a staffer's home, wrote poems for one staffer—including one he read at her going-away party accompanied by a slideshow, during which he cried—and paid for a staffer and her friend to visit New York and see the musical "Hamilton" after the staffer's breakup.

Edwards took one staffer to a White House holiday event, hid the details from other staffers, and texted her that he was "so excited about tonight" and wanted the evening to be "perfect." He then pressured her to continue the evening with him, according to the report. The committee also said he talked to staffers about going to strip clubs in Washington, citing interviews with four staffers.

Even after one staffer left his employment, Edwards sent her a handwritten letter in which he called her "the most amazing woman" and said she had "written a complex chapter in my heart that I will never stop reading," as WUNC reported. He also arranged a Las Vegas trip with her, paying for her flights, accommodation at the Bellagio hotel, and a show at the Sphere.

## Edwards' Defense

Edwards, 65, who has been married since 1980, disputed the committee's findings. His attorneys filed a 15-page rebuttal arguing that the findings "exonerate Representative Edwards under House Rules governing sexual misconduct or harassment." They noted that the committee found no evidence of sexual activity or explicit propositions and that the allegations did not implicate quid pro quo sexual harassment.

"Representative Edwards emphasizes that he has endeavored to comply with all applicable laws regarding his conduct during his time as a Member of Congress and before," his lawyers wrote. "Once more, he states his sincere regret if his conduct caused any staff member discomfort or was unwelcome."

His campaign spokesperson, Paul Shumaker, said committee staff "has taken it upon themselves to write their own rules based upon an interpretation that is inconsistent with the written rule of law or House rules." The campaign said Edwards "looks forward to having his name cleared."

In his testimony to the committee, Edwards said, "I wrote sexual harassment policy [in the corporate world]. I investigated sexual harassment many, many times. I lived a very cautious life."

The committee, however, was unpersuaded. "[Edwards] seemed to believe that by avoiding inappropriate physical touch, explicitly sexual comments, and occasionally telling staffers that they were free to put up boundaries with him, he had immunized himself from such a charge," the report stated. "That is not the case."

## Political Implications

The censure recommendation arrives at a precarious moment for Edwards, who is facing a competitive reelection race against Democrat Jamie Ager, a Fairview farmer who has significantly outraised the incumbent. Ager has raised $3.05 million compared to Edwards' $939,985 through June 30, according to [the Citizen Times](https://www.citizen-times.com/story/news/local/2026/08/03/chuck-edwards-ethics-investigation/91155136007/).

Sabato's Crystal Ball recently moved the race from "leans Republican" to "toss-up," while the Cook Political Report rates it "Lean Republican." Democrats have targeted Edwards as one of their top pickup opportunities in the midterms.

"My heart goes out to the young women who took these roles to serve our community," Ager said following the report's release. "We deserve leaders with the moral character western North Carolina can be proud of."

Michele Woodhouse, a former NC-11 GOP chair who ran against Edwards in the 2022 primary, was blunt about the political damage. "The fact that he missed votes, canceled a town hall—it comes down to dereliction of duty," she said. "Especially in an area where we are still fighting for every dollar to rebuild from Tropical Storm Helene."

## What's Next

A censure is the strongest punishment the House Ethics Committee can recommend short of expulsion. If adopted, Edwards would have to stand in the well of the House while the censure resolution is read aloud—a public shaming on the House floor.

Rep. Michael Guest (R-Miss.), the Ethics Committee chairman, and Rep. Mark DeSaulnier (D-Calif.), the ranking member, plan to bring a privileged resolution to fast-track the censure vote upon the House's return on August 31, according to [the House Ethics Committee](https://ethics.house.gov/press-releases/statement-of-the-chairman-and-ranking-member-of-the-committee-on-ethics-regarding-representative-chuck-edwards-2/).

The committee's report also serves as a broader warning to members of Congress. "This Report should not only hold Representative Edwards accountable for his poor judgment and unbecoming conduct but also serve as a caution to all Members that sexual harassment and discrimination will not be tolerated in the House," the panel wrote.

Edwards, who ousted scandal-plagued Rep. Madison Cawthorn in the 2022 GOP primary, had previously kept a low profile on Capitol Hill. The ethics probe, which was first [confirmed by the committee in May](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/congress/house-ethics-investigating-sexual-harassment-allegations-chuck-edwards-rcna345191), has now thrust him into the national spotlight for reasons far different from those he might have anticipated when he entered Congress.

As the House prepares to weigh the censure recommendation, the case underscores the challenges of accountability in an institution where power imbalances can leave young staffers vulnerable—and where the consequences of misconduct can reverberate far beyond a single congressional office.
