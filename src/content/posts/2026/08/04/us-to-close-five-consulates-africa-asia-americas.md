# US to Close 5 Consulates in Africa, Asia, Americas

The Trump administration has informed Congress that it intends to close five smaller U.S. embassies, consulates, and other diplomatic missions in Africa, Asia, and the Western Hemisphere, according to [AP News](https://apnews.com/article/trump-state-department-embassies-consulates-closures-20f74e71c13a8cbccb7ae3a36a1f7909). The closures mark a rare peacetime reduction in America's global diplomatic footprint, expected to save the government roughly $4.4 million per year.

## The Five Missions Slated for Closure

In separate notices sent to lawmakers late last week, the State Department said it would close the U.S. Embassy in St. George's, Grenada; the consulates in Nagoya, Japan, and Medan, Indonesia; the American Presence Post in Winnipeg, Canada; and an embassy branch office in Douala, Cameroon. The missions employ very few people compared with other U.S. diplomatic posts — the Grenada embassy and Nagoya consulate each have just one American staffer and five local employees, while the Winnipeg post has only two local staffers.

## A Two-Track Diplomatic Strategy

The closures come as the department simultaneously invests millions of dollars into reopening U.S. embassies in Caracas, Venezuela; Damascus, Syria; and Tripoli, Libya, to restore America's formal diplomatic presence following the ouster of long-ruling autocrats in those countries. The U.S. [reopened its embassy in Caracas](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/3/14/us-embassy-in-venezuela-reopens-as-trump-pushes-for-access-to-resources) in March after a seven-year hiatus, and restored its presence in Damascus in July.

This two-track approach reflects the administration's strategy of concentrating diplomatic resources where it sees strategic priorities while eliminating what it considers non-essential posts. Secretary of State Marco Rubio has portrayed the cost-cutting measures as necessary to right-size an organization many conservatives see as bloated and overly bureaucratic.

"Unless we confront the underlying bureaucratic culture that prevents the State Department from carrying out an effective foreign policy, nothing will change," Rubio said. "That is why I am initiating a broad reorganization."

## Grenada: A Contentious History

The proposed closure of the U.S. Embassy in Grenada marks the third time Washington has proposed shuttering the St. George's mission since it opened in February 1984. In May 1994, the Clinton administration proposed closing the post as a post-Cold War cost-saving measure, but the plan was abandoned following strong opposition in Congress — notably from the Congressional Black Caucus and Republican lawmakers who cited the military sacrifices made on the island in 1983.

The embassy serves as the primary point of contact across Grenada, Carriacou, and Petite Martinique for U.S. citizens requiring consular assistance. Its closure carries particular significance for [St. George's University](https://nowgrenada.com/2026/08/us-proposes-closure-of-embassy-in-grenada/), which hosts thousands of American medical and veterinary students — one of the largest concentrations of U.S. citizens in the Southern Caribbean.

## Broader Restructuring Context

The closures represent the first actual implementation of a mission-closure program that began taking shape early in Trump's second term. The White House Office of Management and Budget had advocated for an even more ambitious program targeting up to 30 foreign missions. While the State Department underwent a broad bureaucratic overhaul in 2025 — cutting dozens of bureaus and hundreds of staff — no foreign missions were actually shuttered until now.

The State Department earlier this year also announced plans to permanently close the U.S. consulate in Peshawar, Pakistan, which was the closest diplomatic mission to Afghanistan since American troops withdrew in 2021. The phased closure was announced in May, with responsibilities transferring to the U.S. Embassy in Islamabad, as [Telangana Today](https://telanganatoday.com/us-to-close-consulate-in-peshawar-citing-safety-of-diplomatic-personnel) reported.

## Geopolitical Concerns

The closures have raised concerns about reduced U.S. diplomatic presence in key regions. Notably, China maintains a diplomatic presence in three of the five affected locations — St. George's, Nagoya, and Medan — even as it does not have functioning missions in Douala or Winnipeg. Critics, including Democrats and former foreign policy officials, argue that cuts to the U.S. diplomatic footprint, coupled with the dismantling of USAID, risk undermining American leadership while leaving a dangerous vacuum for adversaries like China and Russia to fill.

According to [Reuters](https://www.cp24.com/news/world/2026/08/04/us-state-department-to-close-consulates-in-canada-japan-and-indonesia-reuters-sources-say/), some administration officials have privately argued that smaller foreign missions are inessential and not cost-effective. The State Department, for its part, issued a statement saying it is "focused on ensuring that the nation's diplomatic footprint is efficient and effective," while committing to follow all congressional notification procedures.

## What's Next

Under U.S. law, the State Department must complete formal consultations with the House Foreign Affairs Committee and Senate Foreign Relations Committee before taking final action to close any overseas diplomatic mission. As congressional review continues, no immediate changes to operations or consular services have been announced, and the affected posts remain open to serve the public.

The outcome of this process will be closely watched — particularly whether Congress pushes back as it did in 1994 over the Grenada embassy, and whether further closures follow beyond these five missions. For residents of Winnipeg, Nagoya, Medan, St. George's, and Douala, the question of where they will access U.S. consular services — and how far they will have to travel — remains open.
