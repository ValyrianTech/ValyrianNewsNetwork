# Miami Voters to Decide Fate of Historic Marine Stadium

MIAMI — The Miami Marine Stadium, a masterpiece of midcentury Brutalist architecture that hosted speedboat races, Elvis Presley's film crew, and legendary performances by Jimmy Buffett, sits silent and graffiti-covered on Virginia Key. On Tuesday, voters will decide whether to approve a 40-year management agreement that could finally bring the shuttered landmark back to life.

The referendum, appearing as item #172 on the ballot, would allow the City of Miami to enter into a management agreement with Global Spectrum L.P., an Oak View Group company, to restore, activate, and manage the historic venue. The stadium has been closed since Hurricane Andrew in 1992, and advocates say this may be the last realistic opportunity to save it.

## A Landmark of Unmatched Architectural Significance

Built in 1963 and designed by Cuban exile architect Hilario Candela — who was just 28 years old at the time — the stadium features a 326-foot cantilevered concrete roof that was the longest in the world when constructed. Its pleated sunshade roof and stands that appear to float above the water have earned it recognition as one of the most significant examples of midcentury Modernist architecture in the United States.

According to [AP News](https://apnews.com/article/miami-marine-stadium-florida-jimmy-buffett-b90b9b96240731f50eb60ee2a51a5716), the stadium was the nation's only race venue for powerboats when it opened, and it quickly became a cultural touchstone for South Florida. The venue hosted concerts by The Who, Queen, Steppenwolf, Gloria Estefan, Ray Charles, and Whitney Houston. It was the backdrop for Elvis Presley's 1967 film "Clambake" and hosted a 1972 campaign rally where Sammy Davis Jr. embraced President Richard Nixon.

Jimmy Buffett, who performed at the stadium in 1985 on a floating stage, wrote a letter to Miami officials before his 2023 passing, urging them to save what he called a "little cathedral of musical fun." Buffett described the show as "one of the most fun shows, for band and fans, that I have ever done."

## Decades of Neglect and Failed Attempts

The stadium was closed in 1992 after Hurricane Andrew, though engineering studies later confirmed the structure was not critically damaged. Demolition plans in 1993 were stopped by public outcry, but the venue has remained fenced off and deteriorating ever since.

Multiple restoration attempts have failed over the decades. In 2017, the city approved a $40 million plan and voted to issue $45 million in bonds, but the effort collapsed after the city cancelled bids seeking operators. In 2018, a $42 million plan was announced but never materialized. The stadium was added to the U.S. National Register of Historic Places in 2018 and has been listed as endangered by the National Trust for Historic Preservation since 2009.

The World Monuments Fund has been engaged with the stadium since 2008 and funded the engineering study that confirmed the structure's viability. The stadium was included on the 2010 World Monuments Watch list of endangered cultural heritage sites.

## The Deal Before Voters

The management agreement would give Global Spectrum the right to operate the stadium and develop its large parking area into a flex park with public recreational facilities and a special events space. The city would receive 93% of gross event sales after a $33,333 monthly management fee, plus 85% of sponsorship revenue. Global Spectrum would contribute up to $10 million toward restoration costs.

Miami Commissioner Damian Pardo, who pushed the deal to the ballot, said the city simply lacks the capacity to run a major event space. "It encapsulated a time period in Miami that's incredibly unique with an incredible story that touches all Miamians," Pardo told AP News. "So this is really a place that connects with Miami at a very deep and personal level."

Project architect Richard Heisenbottle said the stadium's galvanized steel rebar has protected it from serious deterioration, and completed construction documents have already been permitted by the city. He called the vote "the go or no-go decision on saving and restoring the Miami Marine Stadium."

## The Oak View Group Controversy

A significant hurdle for the deal has been the federal indictment of Tim Leiweke, the former CEO of Oak View Group, Global Spectrum's parent company. Leiweke was indicted in July 2025 for allegedly conspiring to rig the bidding process for the Moody Center arena at the University of Texas. OVG paid a $15 million fine, and Leiweke received a presidential pardon from Donald Trump in December 2025 before going to trial. He stepped down as CEO and was replaced by Chris Granger.

The indictment initially derailed the deal in September 2025 when a special city meeting was cancelled due to lack of quorum. But in February 2026, the City Commission unanimously approved placing the referendum on the August 18 ballot.

## Supporters See a Last Chance

Advocates argue that the stadium's restoration is critical not just for its architectural significance but for its cultural importance to Miami's Cuban-American community. Designed by a Cuban exile, the stadium hosted Our Lady of Charity celebrations and Catholic masses for recently arrived Cuban refugees.

Don Worth, co-founder of Restore Miami Marine Stadium, has been advocating for the stadium's restoration since 2008. "The architecture gives it the wow, the history gives it its soul," Worth said. "It's just a special, magical place, and so many people in Miami have had such memorable experiences there."

Worth has compared the stadium's potential to iconic venues worldwide, saying that once restored, it will be "one of the truly iconic destination venues in the world, on par with the Hollywood Bowl, The Sphere in Las Vegas and Red Rocks in Colorado."

Emi Guerra, co-founder of Breakwater Hospitality Group, the Miami-based partner in the deal, said the stadium's view of the city skyline and its location right off Biscayne Bay could make it an entertainment destination that rivals Denver's Red Rocks Amphitheatre. "When it's something that's historic, you have two options: restore it or let it rot," Guerra said.

## Opposition and Concerns

Not everyone is convinced. Ernesto Cuesta, head of the Brickell Homeowners Association, said the group supports preserving the landmark but worries about noise and traffic from a large-scale event space affecting residents across the bay and nearby wildlife. He said he'd prefer a park with more green spaces.

Filmmaker Billy Corben has been more blunt, describing the deal as "another one of these hope-and-pray schemes where the taxpayer always gets short changed." Critics also note that the operating details of the 40-year agreement will only be worked out after voters approve it.

The Bring Back Marine Stadium campaign, which includes Savannah Buffett — Jimmy Buffett's daughter — argues that the deal brings in revenue for the city with no new taxpayer money, and that OVG's proven track record operating venues like the Miami Beach Convention Center makes it the right partner.

## What's at Stake

Christine Rupp, executive director of Dade Heritage Trust, called the vote "probably the last chance to bring this building back to life for the public." The stadium has been featured in the Miami Herald's Pulitzer Prize-winning series of editorials about "Broken Promises" — a testament to how many times restoration efforts have fallen short.

Early voting runs through August 16, and Election Day is Tuesday, August 18. Voters will need a simple majority to approve the measure. If approved, the first five years would focus on generating revenue to create a special bond of $65 to $85 million for restoration.

Commissioner Pardo remains optimistic. "It's the right time to finally do this," he said. "I think you have to believe. The intent is there. The people are in place."

For a landmark that has waited more than three decades for its second act, Tuesday's vote may finally determine whether the "little cathedral of musical fun" will rise again — or continue to fade into memory.
