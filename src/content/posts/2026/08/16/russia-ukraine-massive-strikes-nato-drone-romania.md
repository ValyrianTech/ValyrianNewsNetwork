# Russia-Ukraine Exchange Massive Strikes as NATO Downs Drone

Russia and Ukraine launched some of their most intense cross-border attacks of the war overnight, with hundreds of drones and missiles exchanged between the two capitals, while a Spanish F-18 fighter jet on NATO air policing duty shot down a drone that breached Romanian airspace from Moldova. The escalation underscores the widening regional security implications of a conflict now in its fifth year.

## Massive Overnight Exchanges

Russian forces launched a missile attack on Kyiv early Sunday, sparking fires in at least two districts of the Ukrainian capital, according to Mayor Vitali Klitschko. Three people were injured in the capital as Russia deployed Onyx supersonic anti-ship missiles, ballistic missiles of the Iskander-M, S-400, or North Korean KN-23 model, X-59/69 guided air-to-ground missiles, and 106 long-range attack drones, according to [the Kyiv Independent](https://kyivindependent.com/russian-attacks-across-ukraine-kill-at-least-7-injure-51/). Ukrainian defenses intercepted three X-59/69 missiles and 85 of the 106 drones.

Across Ukraine, at least seven civilians were killed and 51 injured over the past day, regional authorities reported. In Kryvyi Rih, two people were killed and 14 injured after the ArcelorMittal steel plant was hit, killing two employees and injuring 13 workers and contractors. The Kremenchuk oil refinery in Poltava Oblast was also struck, which Russia claims supplies fuel to the Ukrainian military, [BBC News reported](https://www.bbc.com/news/live/c61k110gny00t). Attacks were also reported in Donetsk, Kharkiv, Kherson, Zaporizhzhia, Sumy, Odesa, and Mykolaiv oblasts.

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky called for more European air defense support, saying that "European interceptors cannot simply be allowed to sit in storage. Lives need to be protected." He noted that North Korean ballistic missiles are being used to "destroy infrastructure and claim lives here in Europe."

## Ukraine's Largest Drone Assault on Russia

In one of the largest Ukrainian aerial attacks of the war, hundreds of drones were launched toward Russia overnight, with Moscow Mayor Sergei Sobyanin reporting that about 600 drones were headed toward the Russian capital, of which 201 were intercepted over the Moscow region, according to [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/08/16/nx-s1-5933006/ukraine-aerial-attack-6-dead-russia). Russia's Defense Ministry said it destroyed 822 Ukrainian drones across several regions.

Moscow Oblast Governor Andrey Vorobyov described the attack as "one of the most massive drone attacks (against Moscow Oblast) in recent memory." A fire broke out at a Wildberries warehouse in the village of Koledino near Moscow after being struck by Ukrainian forces, and the Severnoye Domodedovo logistics complex was also set ablaze, [the Kyiv Independent reported](https://kyivindependent.com/ukrainian-forces-target-moscow-oblast-damaging-wildberries-warehouse-in-major-attack/). An 83-year-old man was killed in the Ramensky district of the Moscow region when a drone hit a private home.

At least five people were killed in attacks on southern Russia's Rostov region, where Governor Yury Slyusar reported 150 drones and missiles downed over three cities and nine districts. Ukraine's General Staff reported striking the Kombinat Kamensky, a state-owned enterprise producing solid rocket fuel for Uragan, Smerch, and Tornado-S rocket launchers, saying "the plant's products are directly used to supply the needs of the Russian Armed Forces."

## Spanish F-18 Intercepts Drone Over Romania

A Spanish Air and Space Force F/A-18 Hornet shot down a drone over southeastern Romania in the early hours of August 16, the fourth unmanned aircraft destroyed over Romanian territory this year, [AeroTime reported](https://www.aerotime.aero/articles/spanish-f18-shoots-down-drone-romania-moldova). Romanian surveillance systems detected the aerial target entering national airspace at 04:44 local time from the direction of Moldova, about 24 kilometers north of Galati. The F-18, already airborne on a NATO air policing mission, established radar contact and received approval to engage, destroying the drone at 05:01. Debris fell in an unpopulated area between the villages of Baleni and Cudalbi in Galati County, with no injuries or damage reported.

The drone came from Moldova rather than directly from Ukraine, and NATO spokesman Martin O'Donnell said "the drone appears to be Russian." Romanian Defense Minister Radu Miruta congratulated the pilots on what he called "a successful mission."

This is the second interception by allied fighters on NATO's eastern flank in three days, after NATO jets downed a drone over Latvia on August 14. In July, Romanian F-16 pilots shot down three drones on three consecutive days, the first such engagements inside national airspace. Romania subsequently expelled a Russian diplomat and recalled its ambassador from Moscow. Moldova, which is not a NATO member, has also seen its airspace penetrated repeatedly, with a drone exploding near Crocmaz on August 9, prompting Chisinau to recall its ambassador from Moscow.

## Wildberries: A Strategic Target

Ukraine's targeting of Wildberries, Russia's largest online retailer often described as "Russia's Amazon," has become a central element of Kyiv's strategy to bring the war home to the Russian public. Ukraine says the warehouses are used as logistics facilities for military equipment, providing the Russian military with drone components, navigation equipment, and other gear - claims Russia denies.

In a parallel operation, Ukraine's military intelligence (HUR) claimed that Ukrainian cyber specialists hacked Wildberries on August 10-11, disrupting customer services and payment processing, [the Kyiv Independent reported](https://kyivindependent.com/ukraines-hur-claims-cyberattack-on-wildberries-russian-sellers-complain-of-more-disruption/). The cyberattack disrupted the company's main remote customer-service channel and partially destabilized its payment infrastructure. Wildberries blamed the delays on unspecified "technical problems" and extended the processing period for future payments from five to seven business days.

Security expert Dr. Hanna Shelest told the BBC that Ukraine's new war strategy is designed to make Russian civilians feel the conflict directly. "Civilians in Russia, for four years, didn't feel the war existed," she said. "But now they really see the fire, they see what the war means... they see the impact on the economy and they see the visual pictures, which is really important for the perception of the war."

## Regional Security Implications

The overnight exchanges highlight the escalating nature of the conflict, with both sides now routinely striking deep into each other's territory. Drone debris has been found at multiple locations across Romania and Bulgaria's Black Sea coasts in recent days, [Al Jazeera reported](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/16/russian-strikes-kill-ukrainian-woman-as-nato-jet-downs-drone-over-romania), raising concerns about the broader regional fallout.

Defense and foreign policy analyst Pavel Felgenhauer told Al Jazeera that Ukraine had managed to bypass air defenses in the Moscow region, which has the "best and most dense antiair defences in the entirety of Russia." The attacks did not hit central Moscow but reached the outskirts of the capital.

July 2026 was the deadliest month for Ukrainian civilians in more than four years of war, with at least 437 killed and 2,610 injured, according to the UN Human Rights Monitoring Mission. Russia launched a record number of ballistic missiles and drones, exploiting Ukraine's shortages of air defense systems.

## What to Watch For

As both sides continue to escalate their long-range strike campaigns, several key questions emerge. Will NATO's enhanced air policing posture on its eastern flank deter further drone incursions, or will incidents like the Spanish F-18 interception become more frequent? Can Ukraine sustain its massive drone production and deep-strike campaign against Russian infrastructure? And how will the targeting of civilian infrastructure - from Wildberries warehouses to Ukrainian steel plants - shape public perception of the war on both sides?

Zelensky has made clear that Ukraine's strategy is to reduce Russia's war potential through "concrete damage to specific facilities." As the conflict enters its fifth year, the exchange of fire between the two capitals has become a defining feature of a war that shows no signs of abating - and one whose consequences increasingly reach beyond the battlefield into the wider European security architecture.
