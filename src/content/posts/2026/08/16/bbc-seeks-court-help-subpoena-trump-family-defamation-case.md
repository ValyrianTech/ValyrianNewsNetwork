# BBC Seeks Court Help to Subpoena Trump Family in Defamation Case

The BBC has petitioned a US federal court in Florida for assistance in obtaining testimony and documents from members of President Donald Trump's family in connection with his $10 billion defamation lawsuit against the British broadcaster, according to a [court filing reported by AP News](https://apnews.com/article/bbc-trump-defamation-lawsuit-subpoenas-6e5936f0a221c04927459efdb1e949ef).

The broadcaster is seeking subpoenas for Ivanka Trump, her husband Jared Kushner, and Donald Trump Jr., arguing they have "personal knowledge" and likely possess records relevant to Trump's claims. The filing, submitted Friday, contends that the three family members were directly involved in events surrounding Trump's January 6, 2021 speech, which lies at the center of the dispute.

## The Documentary and the Editing Controversy

The lawsuit stems from a 2024 BBC "Panorama" documentary titled "Trump: A Second Chance?" that spliced together two separate parts of Trump's January 6 speech, delivered almost an hour apart, making it appear he made a continuous call for violent action. The BBC has [apologized to Trump](https://apnews.com/article/britain-bbc-misleading-edit-trump-lawsuit-73a613283de952f5af08fa8ec4d0ca67) for the misleading edit, acknowledging it gave "the mistaken impression that President Trump had made a direct call for violent action," but has consistently denied that the editing constitutes grounds for defamation.

The controversy led to the resignations of BBC Director-General Tim Davie and Head of News Deborah Turness in November 2025.

Trump filed the lawsuit in December 2025, seeking $5 billion in damages for defamation and $5 billion under Florida's Deceptive and Unfair Trade Practices Act, as [AP News reported](https://apnews.com/article/trump-bbc-lawsuit-defamation-a9fd196c4f242decd8f28e8d0ce74442).

## The Subpoena Battle

The BBC argues that Ivanka Trump and Donald Trump Jr. were present when Trump was revising and delivering his speech, and that Kushner drafted a proposed statement for Trump condemning the violence that was never issued. The broadcaster says these family members could provide firsthand information about Trump's intentions and whether the documentary's portrayal was materially false.

The broadcaster has been unable to serve subpoenas because the three have Secret Service protection and other security personnel around them. According to [BBC News](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cd69ely319eo), attempts to serve Ivanka Trump and Kushner at their Miami home were blocked by police, while an attempt to serve Donald Trump Jr. at Trump Tower in New York was also unsuccessful.

The BBC asked Trump to accept the subpoenas on his family's behalf or direct the Secret Service to allow service, but he refused. The broadcaster has now asked the court to permit service by email and certified mail. The judge has given Trump's legal team until Friday to respond.

Trump's legal team dismissed the request, saying in a statement that "the BBC is simply trying to distract away from their own obvious liability."

## Legal Significance

Legal experts say the subpoenas could be significant in establishing the factual record around Trump's January 6 speech. As [Newsweek reported](https://www.newsweek.com/jared-kushner-ivanka-trump-sued-bbc-media-lawsuit-12325145), Alex Morey, a First Amendment specialist at Freedom Forum, noted that "those who helped the President prepare his speech, or were near him when he gave it, could provide the court firsthand information about what Trump intended to convey."

Jonathan Peters, a First Amendment and media-law scholar at the University of Georgia, told Newsweek that "the subpoenas make sense as part of the BBC's effort to develop the factual record surrounding Trump's January 6 speech and, potentially, the damages he claims resulted from the documentary."

However, Peters cautioned that "a subpoena is not a judicial finding that someone has relevant evidence, nor does it mean the recipient must answer every conceivable question."

## Broader Legal Context

The subpoena request comes amid a broader discovery battle. Last week, a federal judge granted Trump a temporary reprieve from an order requiring him to disclose financial records from his family trust, as [AP News reported](https://apnews.com/article/trump-bbc-lawsuit-media-7ac0c0b9754fa4c0d799856810016bcb). Trump has sought to amend his complaint to narrow his claims to focus on reputational rather than business harm.

Trump also recently dropped BBC Studios, the BBC's commercial arm, from the lawsuit after the broadcaster argued the company had no role in creating the documentary. His lawyers have also admitted they have no evidence the documentary was available in the US on BritBox, BBC.com, BBC Select, or through US broadcasters.

## What's Next

The judge has given Trump's legal team until Friday, August 21, to respond to the BBC's request for alternative service methods. A trial has been provisionally set for February 2027.

As [Forbes reported](https://www.forbes.com/sites/alisondurkee/2026/08/14/bbc-says-secret-service-wont-let-it-subpoena-ivanka-don-jr-and-jared-in-trump-defamation-case/), the immediate question is procedural: whether the BBC will be permitted to use alternative methods to serve the subpoenas. If granted, the family members could still challenge the subpoenas, seek to narrow or quash them, or assert applicable privileges.

The case represents one of the most consequential legal battles between a sitting US president and a major international broadcaster, with significant implications for media accountability and the boundaries of defamation law across jurisdictions.
