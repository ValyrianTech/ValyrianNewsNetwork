# Redistricting War's Fallout: A California Congressman's Party Switch

When Republicans were widely declared the winners of the 2025-2026 redistricting competition, California Congressman Kevin Kiley's experience told a different story. California Democrats sliced Kiley's district six ways as payback for Republican-led redistricting in Texas and other states, forcing the two-term conservative to abandon his party affiliation and run as an independent in a newly drawn, slightly Democratic-leaning district.

## A National Redistricting War

The redistricting battle began when President Donald Trump [told GOP-controlled states to redraw their maps](https://apnews.com/article/redistricting-republicans-california-congress-kiley-pan-house-3c2fb8e0c378f8d2914ab77b86bcf7fe) before the 2026 midterm elections to increase the number of winnable seats for Republicans. Texas was the first to respond, redrawing its maps to carve out up to five new conservative-leaning seats. Missouri, North Carolina, Ohio, Florida, Tennessee, Louisiana, and Alabama followed suit.

The effort was turbocharged by the U.S. Supreme Court's April 2026 ruling in *Louisiana v. Callais*, which weakened Section 2 of the Voting Rights Act and cleared the way for more aggressive Republican redistricting across the South. As [the Texas Tribune reported](https://www.texastribune.org/2026/04/29/us-supreme-court-voting-rights-act-section-2-decision-texas-redistricting-maps/), the 6-3 decision made it harder to bring voter discrimination claims against electoral maps. Justice Elena Kagan wrote in dissent that the ruling made Section 2 "all but a dead letter" and would "eliminate the lion's share" of claims brought under that provision.

## California's Response: Proposition 50

California Democrats, led by Gov. Gavin Newsom, retaliated by passing Proposition 50, which voters approved in November 2025 by a 64.42% to 35.58% margin. The measure temporarily suspended the state's independent redistricting commission and replaced California's congressional map with one designed to favor Democrats, creating five new Democratic-leaning seats.

As [CalMatters reported](https://calmatters.org/politics/2025/08/california-redistricting-vote/), Newsom said it was time for Democrats to "play hardball" in response to the Trump administration's moves. "We tried to hold hands and talk about the way the world should be," he said. "We can't just think differently, we have to act differently." The special election cost California taxpayers more than $200 million, according to [the BBC](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/c1lqr30l246o).

The new map formed five new Democratic-leaning seats, effectively canceling out the gains Republicans made in Texas. But it also produced an unexpected casualty: Kevin Kiley.

## Kiley's Dilemma

Kiley, who had represented California's 3rd District since 2022, saw his district sliced six ways by the new Democratic-drawn map. His home was placed in the newly created 6th District, which leans Democratic. Rather than move to a more conservative district or retire, Kiley chose to register as an independent (No Party Preference) and run in the 6th District while continuing to caucus with House Republicans.

"We've seen partisan divisions really spin out of control in really harmful ways, and I think exhibit number one is the redistricting wars," Kiley told voters at a campaign event in Citrus Heights in August.

Kiley has insisted his party switch was not a political gambit. "I could have gone there and run and played the same game these other politicians are playing," he said, referring to the option of moving to a more conservative district. He noted that among his backers is onetime Democratic presidential hopeful Andrew Yang.

Democrats contend Kiley's switch is purely a political trick to survive in a more liberal district. They note he still caucuses with House Republicans and has voted for many GOP priorities, including Trump's sweeping budget and tax cut bill. Richard Pan, the former Democratic state senator running against Kiley, told a crowd at a senior community in Roseville: "He was a MAGA champion when he first ran for Congress, and now he's trying to say 'I'm not.'"

## The Human Cost of Gerrymandering

The redistricting has had deeply personal consequences for voters on both sides. Anne Smull, a retired feedstore owner in Lincoln, California, was heartbroken to learn she can no longer vote for Kiley after her neighborhood was grafted onto an overwhelmingly Democratic seat. "He believes in the same things I do," Smull said. "It feels sneaky and underhanded."

John Coburn, a 68-year-old retired business owner, is furious at the Democrats' redistricting. "I don't care about Texas, I live in California," Coburn said. "40% of the state is Republican, and we have zero voice. Zero. It's not how our Founding Fathers intended."

For Democrats in Placer County, however, the new map represents a rare opportunity. Kathleen Crawford, chair of the county Democratic Party, said: "It's worth it, because we have an opportunity to get Democratic representation, which has been missing from our county. I've been here 15 years, and this is my first real opportunity to elect a Democrat."

## Kiley's Fight Against Mid-Decade Redistricting

Kiley has channeled his experience into legislative action. He introduced HR 4889, a bill to ban mid-decade redistricting nationwide, and has been trying to force a House vote on it. According to [his office](https://kiley.house.gov/posts/americans-overwhelmingly-support-rep-kileys-mid-decade-redistricting-bill), a poll found 66% of Americans support banning mid-decade redistricting, compared to 23% opposed.

"Americans do not want a redistricting war," Kiley said. "It is time for House Leadership to bring my bill to end mid-decade redistricting up for a vote, either as standalone legislation or as part of a continuing resolution."

As [Democracy Docket reported](https://www.democracydocket.com/news-alerts/amid-gops-national-gerrymander-democrats-push-to-require-fair-maps/), congressional Democrats have also introduced bicameral legislation to ban mid-decade redistricting and require independent commissions, though no Republicans have signed on as cosponsors.

## Broader Implications

According to [Wikipedia's comprehensive overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%932026_United_States_redistricting), the redistricting war resulted in 14 Republican seats established and 14 competitive seats dissolved nationally, with no net change to Democratic seats. The elimination of battleground districts is particularly concerning to experts, as representatives from those districts are often crucial to negotiating deals and finding middle ground in Congress.

Democrats in Maryland, New York, Illinois and other strongholds have pledged to retaliate in time for the 2028 elections, suggesting the redistricting arms race is far from over. As Assemblymember James Gallagher, the Republican minority leader in California, warned: "There's really only one way to stop - someone has to refrain from striking back, and show a better way."

## What's Next

Kiley and Pan advanced to the November 3 general election after the June primary, with Kiley leading at about 24.9% of the vote to Pan's 22.7%. The race in the 6th District - which includes West Sacramento, Natomas, parts of northern Sacramento, along with sections of Roseville, Rocklin, and Citrus Heights - will be closely watched as a test of whether an independent can survive the partisan redistricting wars.

Wesley Johnson, a 43-year-old conservative independent in Lincoln, captured the frustration many voters feel: "I feel Northern California is not represented fairly. San Francisco, Sacramento and Los Angeles control everything." He added: "You're just cutting out the little people."

The outcome of Kiley's race - and the broader redistricting war - will help determine control of the House in the next Congress and set the stage for an even more aggressive round of map-drawing in 2028.
