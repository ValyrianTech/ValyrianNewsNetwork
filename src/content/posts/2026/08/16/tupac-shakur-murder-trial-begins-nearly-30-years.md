# Tupac Shakur Murder Trial Begins After Nearly 30 Years

Nearly three decades after legendary rapper Tupac Shakur was fatally shot on the Las Vegas Strip, the only person ever charged in connection with his killing is finally standing trial. Duane "Keffe D" Davis, 63, faces one count of murder with a deadly weapon with the intent to promote, further or assist a criminal gang, as [AP News](https://apnews.com/article/tupac-shakur-murder-trial-4f0260f89d0b55647844d30559c309d3) reports. Opening statements are scheduled for Monday, August 17, in Clark County District Court.

Prosecutors do not allege Davis pulled the trigger. Instead, they contend he orchestrated the September 7, 1996 shooting and supplied the murder weapon. Davis has pleaded not guilty and could face life in prison without parole if convicted. The trial, which began jury selection on August 10, is expected to last four to six weeks.

## The Night of the Shooting

On September 7, 1996, Shakur and Death Row Records co-founder Marion "Suge" Knight attended the Mike Tyson vs. Bruce Seldon boxing match at the MGM Grand in Las Vegas. After the fight, Shakur and his entourage attacked Orlando "Baby Lane" Anderson, Davis's nephew and a member of the South Side Compton Crips, in the MGM Grand lobby. Hours later, as Shakur and Knight headed to a nightclub, a white Cadillac pulled up beside their BMW at a red light near the Las Vegas Strip and gunfire erupted.

Shakur was shot multiple times and died six days later at age 25. Knight survived with minor injuries. Davis was one of four men in the Cadillac; the other three — Anderson, Deandrae "Freaky" Smith, and Terry "Bubble Up" Brown — have all since died. Neither the car nor the gun used in the shooting was ever recovered, according to [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/16/who-killed-tupac-what-to-know-about-the-trial-of-duane-keffe-d-davis).

## How the Cold Case Was Revived

For decades, the case went cold amid witness reluctance, gang codes of silence, and limited physical evidence. It was revived largely by Davis's own words. In 2019, Davis co-authored the memoir "Compton Street Legend," which detailed his involvement in the shooting and claimed he supplied the gun. The book, along with interviews Davis gave to media outlets, reignited the police investigation and ultimately led to his arrest on September 29, 2023.

"Had he never decided to take money and go on BET and talk about this situation, had he decided to never write the book, he would not, probably, have ever been prosecuted for the crime," Chief Deputy District Attorney Marc DiGiacomo said during a hearing last month.

The memoir will be central to the trial. In it, Davis wrote: "We couldn't let no record company studio gangsters do us like that," explaining the retaliation for Anderson's beating. He also described the moment of the shooting: "No words exchanged, the time for talking had passed, the s*** was about to go down!"

Davis's defense attorneys fought unsuccessfully to have the book excluded from trial, arguing it was embellished and co-authored. "We're putting way too much credence and value and weight on statements that are made for entertainment purposes only," defense attorney Michael Sanft said. But Judge Carli Kierny denied the motion, noting that Davis "promotes this book as his book. He encourages people to buy it to get the real truth."

## The Defense's Case

In an interview with [Fox5 Vegas](https://www.fox5vegas.com/2026/08/08/i-wasnt-las-vegas-keffe-d-speaks-jail-days-before-tupac-murder-trial/) days before the trial began, Davis maintained his innocence, claiming he was not in Las Vegas the night of the shooting. "They can't it prove also," he said. He also distanced himself from the memoir, saying, "I did not write that book. A ghost writer wrote that."

Davis told Fox5 he was paid to promote the book and was "just trying to make my family some money." He also disputed a photo in the memoir captioned as him at the Tyson fight, saying it was taken at a New Year's Eve party.

Regarding a 2008 police interview that a judge ruled can be used at trial, Davis claimed his attorney gave him a script to read. "My lawyer gave me a script to read," he said. "He said that's the only way you're going home. So I read the script."

## Key Witnesses and Evidence

Prosecutors expect to call between 35 and 45 witnesses. The witness list includes Knight, the only surviving eyewitness other than Davis, though the imprisoned music mogul has said he wants no part in the trial. "I don't get paid to solve homicides," Knight told ABC News when asked if he would testify. He is currently serving a 28-year prison sentence in California for a fatal hit-and-run.

Trial attorney Shaun Kent told [BBC News](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cgmkl1j3dwvo) that the case is unique because "the two biggest pieces of evidence against Keffe D came from Keffe D." Kent noted that Davis "has the right to take the stand and say 'I made all this up. I lied to sell copies of the book. I lied to the police. I'm a liar but I did not murder Tupac.'"

Sean "Diddy" Combs, whom Davis has alleged offered $1 million to kill Shakur and Knight, will not testify. Combs has denied any involvement in Shakur's death.

## A Cultural Icon's Enduring Legacy

Tupac Shakur sold over 75 million records worldwide with hits like "California Love," "Changes," and "Dear Mama." His music spanned from party anthems to deep social commentary, drawing on his experiences with gang life, racism, and police violence. Born in New York to Afeni Shakur, a Black Panther, he grew up around the black liberation movement, and his music became his politics.

"I can't think of anyone else in the history of hip-hop who's had a greater impact in such a short period of time," said Jeffrey O.G. Ogbar, a history professor at the University of Connecticut, as reported by [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/us-news/duane-keffe-d-davis-charged-organizing-killing-tupac-shakur-trial-rcna590923).

His murder helped elevate him from the face of gangsta rap to a cultural icon. The trial comes amid renewed interest in the case, including a Netflix docuseries on Combs and a wrongful death civil lawsuit filed by Shakur's family against Davis in April.

## What to Watch For

The trial's outcome hinges on whether the jury believes Davis's own admissions in his memoir and interviews were truthful or fabricated, as the defense will argue. Under Nevada law, Davis can be convicted of murder if prosecutors prove he orchestrated the killing, even if he did not fire the weapon.

For Shakur's family, the trial represents a long-awaited moment. "I hope that the trial is fair, and we find out the truth," said Maurice "Mopreme" Shakur, Tupac's stepbrother. His cousin Kendrick Lesane put it more bluntly: "Whoever had anything to do with it, whoever set it up, whoever had any hand in it should be locked up in prison."

As [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/music/2026/aug/15/tupac-shakur-duane-davis-murder-trial) notes, the trial may not resolve the question of who fired the fatal shots. But it marks a significant milestone in one of the most notorious unsolved cases in music history — a case that has captivated fans and conspiracy theorists alike for three decades.

With jury selection complete — six men and 10 women were seated, with four alternates — the courtroom drama is set to begin. The trial is expected to last four to six weeks, and its outcome could finally bring a measure of closure to one of hip-hop's most enduring mysteries.
