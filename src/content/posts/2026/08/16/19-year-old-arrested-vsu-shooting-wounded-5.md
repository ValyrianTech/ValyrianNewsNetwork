# 19-Year-Old Arrested in VSU Shooting That Wounded 5

Police arrested a 19-year-old found hiding in a dormitory closet at Virginia State University on Saturday in connection with an overnight shooting on campus that left five people wounded, including one with critical injuries. The arrest came more than 14 hours after gunfire erupted outside campus dormitories, triggering a campus-wide lockdown just days before the fall semester was set to begin.

## The Arrest

Camron Harris of Henrico, Virginia, was discovered in Seward Hall on the VSU campus at approximately 4 p.m. Saturday and taken into custody, according to [AP News](https://apnews.com/article/virginia-state-university-shooting-campus-lockdown-783d5190d7fe0818fc27a98123311dc3). Harris is not a student at the university, nor were four of the five people injured in the shooting.

Police obtained eight felony warrants for Harris, including four counts of malicious wounding and four counts of use of a firearm in the commission of a felony, as reported by [WTVR CBS 6 Richmond](https://www.wtvr.com/news/local-news/chesterfield-county/henrico-teen-camron-harris-arrested-vsu-shooting-aug-15-2026). He is being held at Chesterfield County Jail without bond, and authorities said additional charges may be filed. It was not immediately clear whether Harris had an attorney who could speak on his behalf.

## The Shooting

Officers responded to the 3300 block of Boisseau Street at approximately 1:30 a.m. Saturday after receiving reports of a shooting outside VSU dormitories near the Quad Annexes, a student housing complex. When they arrived, they found five people suffering from gunshot wounds.

The victims ranged in age from 17 to 23. A 21-year-old man who is not a VSU student remains in critical condition. A 23-year-old man, a 19-year-old woman, and a 17-year-old boy — all non-students — sustained non-life-threatening injuries. A 20-year-old VSU student also suffered non-life-threatening injuries and has been released from the hospital, according to [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/aug/16/virginia-state-university-shooting-suspect-arrested).

Authorities have not disclosed a possible motive for the shooting.

## Campus Lockdown and Community Impact

The shooting prompted an immediate campus-wide lockdown. University officials initially said authorities were searching for multiple suspects, though police provided no additional information about the search Saturday evening. The lockdown was lifted around 8:20 a.m. after university officials said law enforcement did not believe there was an immediate threat to the campus community.

A campus-wide curfew was subsequently implemented, restricting students from coming on campus after 9 p.m., and a significant law enforcement presence remains on campus as investigators process the scene. Chesterfield County Police are leading the investigation with assistance from VSU Police, the Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives, and the Hanover County Sheriff's Office.

The shooting occurred during VSU's "New Trojans Experience" — an orientation program for incoming students — just days before the start of the fall 2026 semester. Residence halls had opened for students a week prior, and nearly 1,500 new students had just completed a pinning ceremony featuring NFL MVP Cam Newton as guest speaker on Friday.

Miles Blake, a VSU freshman engineering student who was in his dorm when the shooting occurred, described the chaos to [WTVR CBS 6 Richmond](https://www.wtvr.com/news/local-news/vsu-freshman-describes-shooting-outside-storms-aug-15-2026). "It was like 100 shots going off like rapid fire," Blake said. "I looked out the window and I saw everyone running. Everybody was just running, so I knew there were shots."

## Leaders Respond

Virginia leaders expressed their support for the VSU community following the shooting. Gov. Abigail Spanberger said she and her husband were "praying for a quick recovery for those who were shot this morning at Virginia State University," while Attorney General Jay Jones said he had been in touch with VSU President Abdullah and was "praying for healing, recovery, and strength for the entire VSU community," as reported by [WTVR CBS 6 Richmond](https://www.wtvr.com/news/local-news/chesterfield-county/virginia-lawmakers-vsu-shooting-aug-15-2026).

U.S. Sen. Mark Warner called the shooting "devastating," noting, "It's devastating to see another Virginia campus harmed by gun violence." U.S. Rep. Rob Wittman said, "No student should ever have to fear for their safety on a college campus."

## Broader Context

As of Sunday morning, there had been more than 300 mass shootings reported across the U.S. so far for the year, according to the Gun Violence Archive, as noted by The Guardian. The archive defines mass shootings as cases in which at least four victims are wounded or killed. A considerable portion of these incidents occur at school and university campuses or activities, prompting ongoing calls for more substantial federal gun control measures.

## What's Next

Classes are scheduled to begin at VSU on Monday, and the university community is grappling with the aftermath of the shooting as students return to campus. The investigation remains ongoing, with authorities continuing to process the scene and pursue additional charges. Questions about the motive, whether other suspects were involved, and the location of the weapon used in the shooting remain unanswered.

"We recognize the concern this incident has caused among our students, parents, alumni, friends, and the broader VSU community," the university said in a statement. "The safety and well-being of our students remain our highest priority."
