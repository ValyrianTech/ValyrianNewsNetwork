# Roger Craig Reveals Vascular Dementia in Hall of Fame Speech

Former San Francisco 49ers running back Roger Craig revealed during his Pro Football Hall of Fame induction speech on Saturday that he has been diagnosed with vascular dementia, saying his doctors believe the condition may be related, at least in part, to concussions he experienced during his 11-year NFL career.

Craig, 66, made the announcement nearly eight minutes into a nine-minute prerecorded video message while sitting on stage wearing his gold jacket at Tom Benson Stadium in Canton, Ohio. Presented by former teammate Jamie Williams, he received a standing ovation from the crowd and hugs from the many Hall of Famers on stage, according to [AP News](https://apnews.com/article/roger-craig-dementia-49ers-hall-of-fame-3946ac71bd8989389a9a373b44dd6be2).

## A Career of Firsts

Craig's path to Canton was historic. Selected by the 49ers in the second round of the 1983 draft as a fullback out of Nebraska, he transformed under coach Bill Walsh into one of the most versatile players in league history. In 1985, Craig became the first player in NFL history to have 1,000 yards rushing and 1,000 yards receiving in a season—a feat only Marshall Faulk (1999) and Christian McCaffrey (2019) have matched since. He was named AP NFL Offensive Player of the Year in 1988 after rushing for a career-high 1,502 yards.

Craig helped the 49ers win three Super Bowls as part of the franchise's 1980s dynasty, finishing his career with 13,100 yards from scrimmage and 73 touchdowns. His 410 yards from scrimmage in Super Bowls rank third all-time behind Hall of Famers Jerry Rice and Franco Harris, as [AP News](https://apnews.com/article/hall-of-fame-roger-craig-c3756a7fcf3e1b95df1c44ac61322f9d) detailed.

"Roger was a pioneer," former 49ers quarterback Steve Young said. "Our offense allowed for new things to happen, and it demanded running backs who could catch the football, and run routes, and be dynamic and still line up in the eye and go downhill off tackle. It asked more of anyone and Roger answered that."

## The Announcement

Craig had not spoken publicly since his election was announced in February as part of the five-member Class of 2026. In his speech, he described the gradual onset of his condition.

"The changes were gradual," Craig said. "At first, they were easy to dismiss as normal aging. Over time, my family noticed, too."

He spoke candidly about the dual nature of football's impact on his life, as [NBC Sports BayArea](https://sports.yahoo.com/articles/49ers-icon-roger-craig-shares-110351854.html) reported.

"Two things could be true," Craig said. "Football gave me opportunities beyond anything I can imagine. It helped shape the man I became. At the same time, we know more today about brain health than when I played. I hope sharing my story encourages people to pay attention to their health and seek help when something doesn't feel right."

Craig emphasized that his diagnosis does not define him. "I want people to know that this diagnosis doesn't define me," he said. "I am still Roger Craig. I am still a father, still a husband, a teammate, a friend and someone who loves this game and the people who brought it to my life."

## Reactions from the Football Community

The revelation resonated deeply among those present. Emmitt Smith, the NFL's all-time leading rusher, was visibly moved as he processed the news backstage, according to [USA TODAY Sports](https://sports.yahoo.com/articles/nfl-greats-said-backstage-roger-113116970.html). "It's a brutal game," Smith said. "It teaches you. But damn."

Craig's son, Rogdrick, who never left his father's side during the ceremony, explained the family's decision to go public. "There are a lot of players that deal with this, and they don't say anything," Rogdrick told USA TODAY Sports. "By the time they get older, it's already too late. We're big proponents of getting on offense early and making sure you're talking to your doctor. He just wants to help as many people as possible. He's the bravest person I know. My hero. I'm so proud of him."

## A Broader Conversation on Brain Health

Craig's announcement comes amid heightened scrutiny of football's long-term effects on the brain. Vascular dementia is the second-most common form of dementia after Alzheimer's disease, caused by decreased blood flow damaging brain tissue, according to Johns Hopkins Medicine. Symptoms include confusion, difficulty paying attention, slowed thinking, and memory issues.

Notably, fellow Class of 2026 inductee Luke Kuechly retired at age 28 in part to preserve his brain health after suffering several concussions. At 35, Kuechly became the second-youngest player inducted into the Hall of Fame, as [AP News](https://apnews.com/article/hall-of-fame-brees-fitzgerald-craig-kuechly-vinatieri-bc76bae869e338694182968cf17bdbf3) reported.

Craig waited 28 years for this honor, having been eligible since 1998. He was selected from the seniors category and entered the Hall with Drew Brees, Larry Fitzgerald, Luke Kuechly, and Adam Vinatieri as part of the Class of 2026. The ceremony drew a record 120 Hall of Famers back to Canton to usher in the new class, the largest number ever. The [New York Post](https://nypost.com/2026/08/08/sports/49ers-legend-roger-craig-reveals-stunning-diagnosis-at-hall-of-fame-ceremony/) noted that Craig's wait for this honor was long overdue.

"You spend your whole life trying to prove you belong and one day you're told you're one of the all-time greats," Craig said. "Man, it's overwhelming."

## Looking Ahead

Craig's decision to share his diagnosis publicly has already sparked conversations about player health and the importance of early medical intervention. His message to current and former players is clear: pay attention to your health and seek help when something doesn't feel right.

"If sharing my story helps even one family or one former player, then it's worth it," Craig said. "Football gave me everything: opportunity, discipline, lifelong friends and the platform to grow as a man. This honor belongs to my parents who started it all."
