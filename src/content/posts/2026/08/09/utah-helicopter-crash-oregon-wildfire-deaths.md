# Utah Helicopter Crash Kills 2 as Oregon Wildfire Claims Dozer Operator

Two separate wildfire tragedies unfolded across the western United States this week, as a firefighting helicopter crashed in Utah killing both pilots aboard, while a bulldozer operator was killed battling a blaze in Oregon. The incidents underscore the extreme dangers facing firefighters during what has become a deadly 2026 wildfire season.

## Helicopter Crash in Utah

A Sikorsky S-64 "Skycrane" helicopter with two pilots aboard crashed around 10:40 a.m. Friday near Richfield, Utah, while battling the massive Widemouth 2 Fire in the Fishlake National Forest, according to [AP News](https://apnews.com/article/wildfire-utah-helicopter-crash-a42a74bfa41c20a12313de6d5972ed23). Both pilots, employed by Helicopter Transport Services under contract with the U.S. Forest Service, were confirmed dead on Saturday after crews were finally able to reach the crash site.

The crash ignited a new fire that spread to connect with the existing blaze, placing the accident site within the fire's perimeter. Fire conditions initially prevented responders from reaching the scene, with Sevier County Sheriff Nathan Curtis describing the harrowing situation at a news conference Sunday.

"If we had not moved some of our responders and the hand crews out, they would have been trapped by the fire as well," Curtis said, as reported by the [Salt Lake Tribune](https://www.sltrib.com/news/2026/08/08/pilot-crewmate-killed-helicopter/). "It was not an easy choice, but … they were able to determine that if we had not done that, we would have lost more lives."

Utah Gov. Spencer Cox paid tribute to the fallen pilots, saying, "Our prayers are with their families and the entire wildland fire community. Utah will never forget their courage, their service, and their sacrifice."

The National Transportation Safety Board and Federal Aviation Administration are investigating the crash. The FAA issued a temporary flight restriction near the fire effective through Aug. 20. Officials said it will likely take the NTSB a year or more to determine the cause.

## Widemouth 2 Fire Becomes Utah's Largest

The lightning-caused Widemouth 2 Fire, which ignited July 27, has burned more than 114,600 acres and is now Utah's largest wildfire of 2026, surpassing the Babylon Fire. As of Saturday, the blaze was 24% contained with more than 600 firefighting personnel assigned.

The fire is spreading aggressively to the south, with crews working to prevent flames from jumping over Interstate 70. Tony DeMasters of the Great Basin Incident Management Team 2 described the fire's behavior: "It's angry, it's mad, and it's wanting to chew through that country on the south, southwest corner," he told the Salt Lake Tribune.

Despite the crash, crews are continuing firefighting operations. "It's not going to change how we operate," Tyler Hecht, deputy incident commander with the Great Basin Incident Management Team, said according to [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/9/helicopter-crash-kills-pilot-and-crew-member-amid-utah-wildfire-battle). "We're going to continue to fight fire and utilize aircraft to support our firefighters on the ground."

## Dozer Operator Killed in Oregon

Separately, 47-year-old Jason Ensign of Klamath Falls, Oregon, was killed Thursday while operating a Caterpillar D-6 dozer for Green Diamond Timber Resource Company during the Wrights Spring Fire in south-central Oregon.

According to the [Oregon Public Broadcasting](https://www.opb.org/article/2026/08/07/wrights-spring-fire-oregon/), Ensign was building dozer lines in the Yahwee Plateau area when the fire experienced "unexpected and rapid growth associated with a weather event," cutting off his escape route to a safety zone. Crews discovered the dozer missing at about 4:30 p.m. and found Ensign deceased inside the extensively damaged machine several hours later.

"This is a heartbreaking loss," Klamath County Sheriff Shane L. Mitchell said, as reported by [KCBY](https://kcby.com/news/local/wrights-spring-fire-kills-oregon-dozer-operator-during-rapid-growth-event). "Behind every helmet, every engine and every piece of heavy equipment is a person who went to work expecting to come home."

The Wrights Spring Fire, which started Wednesday about 6 miles east of Modoc Point, has grown to more than 19,200 acres with no containment. Oregon Gov. Tina Kotek invoked the Emergency Conflagration Act — the 12th time this year — to send additional firefighting resources to the area. Approximately 475 people are under evacuation orders.

## A Deadly Season Across the West

The deaths mark the latest tragedies in an already deadly 2026 wildfire season. Four firefighters were killed in June while battling the Knowles and Gore fires near the Colorado-Utah border, as reported by the [Colorado Sun](https://coloradosun.com/2026/07/25/firefighter-dies-from-injuries-sustained-fighting-fire-in-western-colorado/). This was Utah's second fatal wildfire incident of the summer.

Jamie Barnes, director of Utah's Division of Forestry, described the fires as unlike anything in recent memory, according to [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/aug/07/utah-wildfire-helicopter-crash). "We're seeing fires spread farther and faster under conditions that defy historical expectations," Barnes said. "Some of the fires we've responded to this year are behaving in ways veteran firefighters simply haven't seen before."

Months of dry weather, record-low snowpack in some areas, and erratic winds have fueled blazes across the West. The Wrights Spring Fire was Oregon's first wildfire-related fatality since 2022, and the Big Grass Fire in Malheur County has crossed 504,000 acres — one of the largest active fires in modern U.S. records.

## What to Watch

As the Widemouth 2 Fire continues to threaten communities near Kanosh, Fillmore, and Meadow, crews are racing to prevent the flames from reaching I-70. The NTSB investigation into the helicopter crash is expected to take a year or more, and the Wrights Spring Fire remains completely uncontained with hot, dry conditions forecast to continue.

For the families of the fallen and the firefighters still on the lines, the message from officials is clear: this wildfire season is far from over, and the risks remain extraordinary.
