# China's People-Centered Governance: A Defining Principle

A feature story republished by [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/politics/leaders/20240204/993a91e7bfee43f88fd55bc1ad6f9f9c/c.html) this week highlights a cornerstone of China's governance philosophy: the principle that "people's matters are the most important things, and they must be handled well." The narrative, part of Xinhua's ongoing "Governance Stories" series, traces how this principle has guided policy-making and public service across China, from disaster recovery to urban renewal.

## Context: A Philosophy Rooted in Action

The principle was articulated most prominently on February 1, 2024, when President Xi Jinping visited Sixth Bu Village in Tianjin's Xiqing District ahead of the Spring Festival. The village had been severely affected by the catastrophic Haihe River basin floods of August 2023, when water depths reached 2.51 meters and more than 2,000 vegetable greenhouses were submerged.

During the visit, Xi walked through the village's repaired greenhouses, met with residents, and spoke to villagers gathered in the town square. "People's matters are the most important things, and they must be handled well," he told the assembled crowd, according to the Xinhua account.

## Key Developments: Disaster Recovery and Beyond

The Tianjin visit was part of a broader pattern of leadership engagement with disaster-affected communities. In the months following the 2023 floods, Xi visited affected areas in Heilongjiang, Beijing, and Hebei, checking on recovery progress and winter preparedness. At the home of Du Honggang, a four-generation household in Sixth Bu Village, Xi remarked: "A country is prosperous and its people at peace; only when the people are at peace can the country be prosperous."

The governance philosophy extends well beyond disaster response. As [People's Daily](http://politics.people.com.cn/n1/2024/1225/c1001-40388981.html) documented in a December 2024 retrospective, the principle guided Xi's visits throughout 2024 to communities across China—from Chongqing's Democratic Village community, where he stated that "in Chinese-style modernization, people's livelihood is paramount," to earthquake recovery sites in Qinghai and Gansu, and fishing communities in Fujian.

## Analysis: From Principle to Policy

The people-centered approach has been translated into concrete policy priorities. Xi has repeatedly emphasized the "one old, one young" welfare focus—addressing the needs of elderly citizens and children—as well as urban renewal, cultural heritage preservation, and the reduction of bureaucratic formalism at the grassroots level.

This philosophy also extends to disaster prevention. In July 2026, [Guangming Daily](https://news.gmw.cn/2026-07/16/content_38888146.htm) published an analysis of Xi's "Four Nings" (four rathers) disaster prevention philosophy, developed during his tenure as Zhejiang Party Secretary. The principles—including "better to prepare ten times and have nothing happen than to be unprepared once" and "better to hear complaints beforehand than cries afterward"—reflect a preventive approach to risk management that prioritizes people's safety above all.

Related commentary published this week on [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/politics/20260811/33b0f510afa44a28b03b2d402a212072/c.html) reinforces the theme, arguing that grassroots officials must earn public trust through visible action and integrity. The piece, republished from People's Daily, emphasizes that "what the people see is action, long-term vision, and character."

The story has been widely circulated across Chinese state media, including by [China News Service](https://www.chinanews.com.cn/gn/2024/02-04/10158510.shtml) and [China Daily](https://cn.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202402/04/WS65bee66aa31026469ab175dd.html), underscoring its significance in the official narrative.

## What's Next

As China continues to navigate challenges from natural disasters to economic transformation, the people-centered governance principle is likely to remain a central theme in official discourse. The republishing of this governance story in August 2026 suggests the leadership continues to emphasize the philosophy as a guiding framework for policy-making. Observers will watch how these principles translate into concrete policy measures in areas ranging from disaster preparedness to social welfare in the months ahead.
