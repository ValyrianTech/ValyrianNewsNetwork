# China's Tax Refund 2.0 Sparks Shopping Boom Among Foreign Visitors

China's upgraded departure tax refund policy, known as Tax Refund 2.0, is fueling a new wave of shopping enthusiasm among international visitors, with refund sales in the first half of 2026 surging 69% year-on-year. The enhanced policy, which took effect nationwide on July 1, aims to boost tourism spending and stimulate the domestic consumer market as part of Beijing's broader push to position China as a premier global shopping destination.

## Policy Evolution and Early Results

The Tax Refund 2.0 framework, announced in May by the Ministry of Commerce and five other departments, builds on the 1.0 version launched in April 2025. The initial rollout proved remarkably successful: in its first year, tax refund sales nearly doubled, with annual sales volume roughly equivalent to the total of the previous decade from 2015 to 2024. According to [People's Daily](http://finance.people.com.cn/n1/2026/0811/c1004-40777198.html), tax refund processing numbers grew 367% year-on-year, while refund merchandise sales and refund amounts grew 90%.

The momentum has continued into 2026. State Taxation Administration spokesperson Rong Hailou reported that in the first half of the year, the number of international visitors processing departure tax refunds grew 366% year-on-year, with refund merchandise sales and refund amounts growing 69%. Shanghai's departure tax refund sales grew 63.2% in H1 2026, with "Buy Now, Refund Now" sales growing 2.9 times, as [Xinhua News Agency](https://309.xqgbuv.com/manyvoices/read/xinhuanet_com_fortune_20260806_71a33846c2f44ee2a77c72ec03180691_c_html_e0b34881) reported.

## Eight Key Measures Under Tax Refund 2.0

The 2.0 policy introduces eight core initiatives designed to make tax refunds faster, easier, and more accessible. As [Workers' Daily](https://www.workercn.cn/c/2026-05-19/8804945.shtml) detailed, these include expanding tax refund store coverage, implementing a small-amount spot-check system for refund applications under 10,000 yuan, and introducing paperless tax refund processing.

The policy also optimizes "Buy Now, Refund Now" services with cross-region mutual recognition, standardizes and extends the departure deadline to 28 days, establishes tax refund service zones at major exhibitions, creates international consumption clusters, and improves payment convenience.

Vice Minister of Commerce Sheng Qiuping described the 2.0 policy as an "upgraded version" focused on achieving "more stores, less waiting, digitalization, and standardization" to further facilitate tax refund processing for travelers and optimize the inbound consumption environment. As [the State Council of China](https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/202605/content_7069399.htm) reported, Sheng emphasized that the policy aims to build the "Buy in China" brand.

## Expanding Store Coverage and Going Digital

China's tax refund store network has expanded dramatically. As of end-2025, the country had 12,930 departure tax refund stores nationwide, with over 7,000 offering "Buy Now, Refund Now" services. By 2026, the number had grown to approximately 14,000 stores, about four times the end-2024 figure, according to [MOFCOM's tax-free platform](https://taxfree.mofcom.gov.cn/taxfreeCnPc/xwdt/2026/5/2ff5c59a28c74244bc5d73abbf640834303.html).

The policy has also moved online. In June 2026, Beijing launched China's first online departure tax refund store, developed jointly by Beijing tax and commerce authorities with JD Group, accessible through the JD app. This allows international visitors to shop for tax-refund-eligible goods remotely and have them delivered to their hotels.

Paperless processing represents another significant leap forward. Under the new system, customs and agency personnel input visitor identification information, and the system automatically retrieves refund application forms and invoice data, eliminating the need for physical paperwork. Guangzhou processed its first paperless tax refund in July 2026.

## "Buy Now, Refund Now" Goes Cross-Regional

A centerpiece of the 2.0 policy is the expansion of "Buy Now, Refund Now" services with cross-region mutual recognition. This convenience measure allows international visitors to receive cash refunds immediately at the point of purchase rather than waiting until departure, with the departure deadline now standardized at 28 days nationwide.

Xie Zhenfa, vice dean of Xiamen University School of Economics, explained that the cross-region mutual recognition allows international visitors to plan their travel routes in China more freely and conveniently. "After purchasing goods in City A and receiving cash refunds on the spot, they can go to City B to spend again, further optimizing the consumption experience in China and releasing new consumption demand," Xie said, as [Guangdong Provincial Tax Service](https://guangdong.chinatax.gov.cn/gdsw/swxw/2026-07/20/content_2112a6037fb4425cbe564d7f14c94392.shtml) reported.

Regional mutual recognition zones have been established across the Beijing-Tianjin-Hebei area, five southern provinces including Guangdong and Hainan, and western provinces including Sichuan, Chongqing, and Yunnan.

## The "Reverse Overseas Shopping" Trend

The shopping boom reflects a broader shift in global consumer behavior. As [Xinhua News Agency via ifeng](https://309.xqgbuv.com/manyvoices/read/news_ifeng_com_c_8vmxa3bhs5o_b209e53a) reported, the "reverse overseas shopping" trend has taken hold, with foreign visitors arriving with empty suitcases and leaving with Chinese tech products, national trend brands, cultural creative items, and traditional goods.

Wang Jian, deputy director of the Institute of World China Studies at the Shanghai Academy of Social Sciences, noted that "the 'shopping carts' of foreign visitors carry not just goods, but recognition of China's innovation, culture, and way of life."

Foreign Ministry spokesperson Lin Jian added that the "reverse overseas shopping" boom allows the world to share the quality and creativity of "Chinese design" and "Chinese manufacturing."

## Economic Implications and Outlook

Industry experts project that with full implementation of Tax Refund 2.0, 2026 departure tax refund merchandise sales will exceed 100 billion yuan, up more than 80% year-on-year, potentially reaching 200 billion yuan by 2028. Fu Yifu, special researcher at Suning Bank, emphasized that "vigorously boosting inbound consumption is both a realistic need and a long-term strategy," noting that it helps cultivate new consumption growth points and stabilize the economic base.

Zheng Rong, researcher at the National Academy for Opening Up at the University of International Business and Economics, observed that "China is accelerating its transformation from a 'world factory' to a 'global consumption destination.'"

With visa-free policies now covering 50 countries unilaterally and 240-hour transit visa-free access for 55 countries, the foundation for sustained growth in inbound consumption appears solid. The five international consumption center cities—Shanghai, Beijing, Guangzhou, Tianjin, and Chongqing—account for one-third of national foreign visitor entries, half of consumer goods imports, and two-thirds of departure tax refund sales, positioning them as the primary engines of this new shopping era.

As the "Buy in China" brand continues to gain traction globally, the combination of expanded store coverage, digital innovation, and streamlined refund processes is transforming how international visitors experience shopping in China—and reshaping the country's image from manufacturing powerhouse to global consumption destination.
