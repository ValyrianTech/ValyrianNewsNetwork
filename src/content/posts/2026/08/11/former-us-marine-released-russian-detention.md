# Former US Marine Released From Russian Detention

Russia has released Robert Gilman, a 32-year-old former U.S. Marine and English teacher from Massachusetts who had been detained in Russia for more than four years on disputed charges, according to [AP News](https://apnews.com/article/russia-us-marine-released-robert-gilman-4c14fea91552117ffd7fd4c893c3768e). Gilman, who was reportedly in grave medical condition, was freed on humanitarian grounds on August 11 and flown to a U.S. military hospital in Texas for medical and psychological evaluation.

## A Case That Drew International Attention

Gilman was arrested in January 2022 in Voronezh, Russia, after allegedly kicking a police officer while drunk on a train to Moscow. He was initially sentenced to 3.5 years in prison in 2022 for beating a police officer, and his sentence was later extended to 10 years in December 2025 after he was found guilty of assaulting prison guards, as [AP News reported](https://apnews.com/article/russia-us-gilman-sentence-american-detained-1398c5f86100f6f9be7e493b099262ca).

His family and advocates have consistently disputed the charges. According to his father's October 2024 Boston Globe op-ed, Gilman had fallen ill when the incident occurred and had accidentally kicked the police officer. The family said a "series of vague and escalating charges" were lodged, and in prison he was "goaded" into additional actions and related charges "through forced drugging, provocations, and torture."

## Deteriorating Health and Urgent Advocacy

In late June 2026, Gilman was transferred from prison to a civilian hospital in Voronezh, reportedly in a catatonic-like state. He was in a "dissociative stupor" for approximately 47-50 days, hooked up to a feeding tube and handcuffed to a bed, and was also diagnosed with a severe case of pneumonia. His mother flew to Russia to see him but was denied access by hospital officials.

[BBC News](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cvgj4m8e1ryo) reported that Senator Ed Markey said Gilman had been "essentially catatonic" and battling a "severe case of pneumonia" before his release. "Approximately 50 days ago, Robert was diagnosed with a dissociative stupor - a condition where he could not interact or eat," Markey said. "We do not know his current condition or the long-term impact on his recovery."

Kieran Ramsey, the Gilman family representative and former head of the FBI Hostage Recovery Fusion Cell, described the severity of the situation to [Fox News](https://www.foxnews.com/politics/us-marine-veteran-released-russian-prison-1666-days-detention): "Our main concern is that we think he's dying in a Russian hospital. And the Russians won't let his mother even visit him, let alone embassy staff."

## Humanitarian Release, Not an Exchange

The State Department, which had determined Gilman was wrongfully detained, confirmed he was released on "humanitarian grounds" and was not part of an exchange, with no other concessions provided. The release came after weeks of urgent advocacy by his family, Senator Ed Markey, and advocacy group Global Reach, amid growing international attention to his deteriorating health.

According to [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/aug/11/russia-frees-ex-us-marine-robert-gilman), Vladimir Putin granted the ailing American a pardon on humanitarian grounds. The release was seen by analysts as a signal of Moscow's eagerness to improve relations with the Trump administration, particularly after several weeks in which Trump had appeared to take a more supportive position towards Ukraine.

Gilman's sister, Lexie Hudson, credited President Donald Trump and Senator Markey with bringing about the former Marine's release. "There is no other reason that Robert is alive today other than that President Trump heard about the case and took action," Hudson said.

## Americans Still Detained in Russia

Gilman was one of at least 8-10 Americans who remained in Russian custody after a series of high-profile prisoner exchanges between the U.S. and Russia in recent years, including Trevor Reed, Brittney Griner, Paul Whelan, Evan Gershkovich, and Marc Fogel. [RFE/RL](https://www.rferl.org/a/russia-us-former-marine-robert-gilman-freed-medical-/33826753.html) reported that at least five American citizens remain in Russian detention, with other reports putting the number as high as 10.

Eric Lebson, Chief Strategy Officer of Global Reach, called on the administration to use the momentum of Gilman's release to bring home other Americans still detained in Russia, including Aleksandr Antonov, David Barnes, Olga Jezler, Andre Khachatoorian, and Chuck Zimmerman.

## What's Next

Gilman was flown to Washington, D.C. on a State Department plane, where he was greeted by U.S. envoy Steve Witkoff and Sebastian Gorka, deputy assistant to President Trump, before continuing on to a U.S. military hospital in Texas. His family has flown to Texas to be with him as he begins what advocates describe as "the long road to recovery."

Senator Markey said it was "a tragedy that it took Robert's near-death condition for his case to receive the attention that it required for his release to be negotiated." He added: "Throughout his detention, Robert was subjected to physical torture, forced medication, and provocations by Russian authorities. There is no doubt that his treatment by Russian authorities is the reason Robert is in this precarious state today."

As Gilman begins his recovery, the case serves as a stark reminder of the ongoing challenges facing Americans detained abroad and the complex diplomatic negotiations required to secure their release.
