# Trump Signs Order Overhauling Childhood Vaccine Schedule

President Donald Trump signed an executive order on Monday calling for sweeping changes to the U.S. childhood vaccine schedule, including reducing the number of recommended diseases from 18 to 11 and splitting the combined measles, mumps, and rubella (MMR) vaccine into three separate single-disease shots. The move directly contradicts the guidance of major medical organizations and comes despite decades of scientific research finding no link between vaccines and autism.

## The Executive Order

According to [AP News](https://apnews.com/article/vaccine-research-autism-trump-kennedy-rfk-d10f81f221c4ae9f5b2f83dd0ee98b29), the order, titled "Delivering Gold Standard Childhood Vaccine Recommendations for Americans," recommends that all childhood immunizations be administered at separate medical visits "to the maximum extent feasible." Trump called the announcement a "major victory for parents rights, religious and constitutional rights, and for the gold standard science."

The [executive order](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/08/delivering-gold-standard-childhood-vaccine-recommendations-for-americans/) creates three categories of vaccine recommendations: immunizations recommended for all children (11 diseases), immunizations for high-risk groups (including RSV monoclonal antibodies, hepatitis A, hepatitis B, meningococcal B, meningococcal ACWY, and dengue), and immunizations based on shared clinical decision-making (hepatitis A, hepatitis B, rotavirus, meningococcal disease, influenza, and COVID-19).

The order also directs the Attorney General to take legal action challenging state laws that conflict with parental authority, religious freedom, disability accommodations, and equal protection related to vaccine exemptions. It advises states with school vaccine mandates to consider updating their laws to reflect the administration's preferred schedule.

## Medical Groups Push Back

Public health experts and medical organizations have strongly condemned the order. Dr. Andrew Racine, president of the American Academy of Pediatrics, said the announcement spreads uncertainty, fear, and confusion where "there doesn't need to be any of that."

"Science about vaccines and their efficacy has not changed," Racine said, as reported by [BBC News](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/ce3q5vl581wo). "The distribution of viruses and other pathogens in our environment has not changed. Children in this country have not changed in the last 48 hours."

Racine called the executive order "dangerous" and not based on "gold-standard science," adding that "delaying or skipping shots is risky, especially as measles continues to spread and children go back to school."

Sen. Bill Cassidy (R-LA), the Republican chair of the Senate health committee and a physician, publicly condemned the order in a social media post. "I'm a doctor. This executive order is wrong. The President does not have the expertise to make these changes," Cassidy wrote. "Vaccines are overwhelmingly safe. Vaccines are effective. Vaccines DO NOT cause autism."

Dr. Jan K. Carney, president of the American College of Physicians, told [ABC News](https://abcnews.com/Politics/trump-executive-order-childhood-vaccine-schedule/story?id=135524851) that the order is "part of a troubling pattern by the administration to attempt to unilaterally change vaccine guidance, particularly for children, rather than relying on the transparent, scientific review that has guided the U.S. childhood vaccine schedule for decades."

## The Science Behind the Debate

The vaccine-autism link that Trump has long promoted originates from a 1998 paper by Andrew Wakefield that was later retracted and found to be fraudulent. Dozens of studies involving millions of children across multiple countries have found no link between vaccines and autism.

Racine noted that the question has been studied in "over 40 methodologically well-designed studies in over seven countries involving over 5 million children." He said, "I'm not sure that there is anything that has been as thoroughly looked at as this question. And the answer is unequivocally no."

Regarding the proposal to split the MMR vaccine, the CDC states there is "no published scientific evidence" showing "any benefit in separating the combination MMR vaccine into three individual shots." The American Academy of Pediatrics predicts it would take a decade to create a separate vaccine for each part of the MMR vaccine. Studies show that combination shots increase the likelihood of completing all recommended vaccinations by age 2.

A single dose of MMR is 0.5ml — about one-tenth of a teaspoon. Trump's claim that vaccines are the size of "a bottle of soda" poured into a child's body is not supported by any evidence.

## Legal and Operational Reality

Executive orders are guidance to federal agencies, not laws passed by Congress, and have no direct legal effect on the public. States, not the federal government, set school vaccination requirements. Twenty-nine states and the District of Columbia have decoupled their vaccination requirements from the CDC.

As [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/08/10/nx-s1-5927313/trump-rfk-jr-vaccines-autism-executive-order) reported, the order is an attempt to reinstate changes made earlier this year to CDC vaccination recommendations that were halted by a federal judge. In March 2026, U.S. District Court Judge Brian Murphy in Boston blocked the CDC's January changes to the childhood immunization schedule, ruling that the administration had made "arbitrary and capricious decisions" and had "disregarded those methods and thereby undermined the integrity of its actions."

The [NPR report on the judge's ruling](https://www.npr.org/2026/03/16/nx-s1-5749530/judge-blocks-rfk-jr-vaccine-changes) noted that the judge also stayed the appointments of 13 ACIP members appointed by Health Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. after he fired the entire 17-member vaccine advisory committee in June 2025.

Dorit Reiss, a vaccines expert at UC Law San Francisco, told [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/aug/10/trump-vaccines-executive-order-measles) that the order "should have no direct operational effects." However, "Trump has influence," she said, and the order may intensify "political pressure on states" to change school requirements.

## A Measles Outbreak Looms

The order comes as the U.S. is experiencing its worst measles outbreak in 35 years, with 2,134 confirmed cases. In 2025, the U.S. recorded its highest annual measles count since 1991. Two healthy children died from measles-related causes in the last year. Experts warn the U.S. could lose its measles elimination status.

Federal data shows U.S. kindergarten vaccination rates have been dropping for five consecutive years. About 3.6% of kindergarten students in the 2024-25 school year were exempt from at least one state-required vaccine, representing roughly 138,000 children. More than 286,000 kindergartners had not completed their MMR vaccine series.

Dr. Aaron Milstone, a pediatric infectious disease expert with the AAP, emphasized the stakes: "Why would we not give children a vaccine that is safe that prevents death? We had two healthy children die in the United States in the last year from measles virus."

## What's Next

The order directs the Secretary of HHS, through the HHS Task Force on Safer Childhood Vaccines, to present plans within 90 days to offer single-vaccine options for core childhood vaccines, assess timing and sequencing, develop alternative adjuvants to aluminum, and improve vaccine safety monitoring.

The order is likely to face legal challenges, as previous similar attempts to change the childhood vaccination schedule have been. Lawrence Gostin, a public health law expert at Georgetown University, noted that the newly confirmed CDC director, Dr. Erica Schwartz, faces a difficult position: "This is clearly the first test for Dr. Erica Schwartz. The President is placing her in an untenable position."

As children return to school and the measles outbreak continues, medical groups remain united in their opposition to the order. The American Academy of Pediatrics said it will continue to recommend the full vaccine schedule based on decades of scientific evidence.

"The science of vaccines and their efficacy has not changed," Racine said. "The distribution of viruses and other pathogens in our environment has not changed. Children in this country have not changed in the last 48 hours."
