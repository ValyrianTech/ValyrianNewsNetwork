# Rock Werchter Meadow Opens for Solar Eclipse Viewing

The iconic Rock Werchter festival meadow in Belgium will trade music fans for eclipse enthusiasts this Wednesday evening, as the site opens to the public for viewing the largest solar eclipse visible in the country since 1999. The moon is expected to cover approximately 90 percent of the sun at its peak, with the event running from 19:18 to 21:05 local time.

## A Festival Site Transformed

After hosting the Rock Werchter festival in early July, the festival park in Werchter has been cleaned up and the stage removed, making the expansive meadow available for the astronomical event. According to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/08/10/zonsverduistering-werchter-festivalpark-rotselaar/), the site is open to everyone — not just local residents.

"The stage has been removed, so we can open the festival park to visitors," said Mayor Jelle Wouters of Rotselaar. "Quite a lot of people asked for a place to watch the solar eclipse together with friends. The festival meadow can accommodate many people and you also have a good view."

The open meadow offers an unobstructed view of the western horizon, which is crucial since the sun will be low in the sky during the eclipse.

## Additional Viewing Locations

Beyond the festival meadow, the [Rotselaar municipality](https://www.rotselaar.be/zonsverduistering) is also opening the rooftop terrace of GC 'de Mena' — housed in the former MENA brewery building — from 19:00 to 21:30. At 24 meters high, the terrace provides a panoramic vantage point, though access is limited to residents of the municipality due to space constraints.

Eclipse glasses can be purchased for €5 at Egelhulpcentrum Avalon in Rotselaar, with proceeds supporting the center's operations.

## The Largest Eclipse Since 1999

According to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/07/29/grootste-zonsverduistering-in-belgie-sinds-1999-op-12-augustus/), the eclipse begins at 19:18 when the moon first touches the sun's disk, reaches its maximum at 20:13 with roughly 90 percent of the sun covered from Brussels, and ends at 21:05. A solar eclipse of this magnitude will not be visible in Belgium again until 2081.

The August 12, 2026 eclipse is part of the Saros 126 series and will be total in parts of Spain, Iceland, and Greenland — the first total solar eclipse on mainland Europe since 1999. As [HLN](https://www.hln.be/weer-en-wetenschap/grootste-zonsverduistering-sinds-1999-op-komst-op-12-augustus-wordt-zon-in-belgie-voor-zon-90-procent-bedekt~a5c1b46f/) reports, Volkssterrenwacht Urania notes that "for the real spectacle you have to go to Spain, Iceland or Greenland."

Interest in the event has been substantial. Marc Van den Broeck, director of Volkssterrenwacht Urania, reported that 80,000 people have already purchased eclipse glasses.

## Weather Outlook Positive

Weather forecasts are encouraging for the viewing event. According to [Weeronline](https://www.weeronline.nl/nieuws/9-8-2026-zonsverduistering-augustus-2026), only high cirrus clouds are expected, which should not obstruct viewing of the eclipse. The forecast calls for warm, mostly clear conditions Wednesday evening.

## Safety First

Experts emphasize that viewing a solar eclipse requires special eclipse glasses. Regular sunglasses, CDs/DVDs, or photographic filters are not safe — they block visible light but still allow harmful infrared and UV radiation through. The [eclipsen.be](https://www.eclipsen.be/) website, run by the Flemish observatories, provides extensive safety information and resources.

## What to Watch For

With the festival meadow open to all and the rooftop terrace reserved for residents, Wednesday evening promises to be a memorable occasion in the Rotselaar region. The [Volkssterrenwacht Mira](https://mira.be/artikels/de-zonsverduistering-van-12-augustus-2026) observatory in Grimbergen notes that while the partial eclipse is impressive, it won't compare to the total eclipse visible further south. For those unable to attend in person, [Spacepage](https://www.spacepage.be/nieuws/waarnemen/livestream-zonsverduistering-op-12-augustus-2026.html) will offer live streams of the event.

As the sun sets at 21:09, the eclipse will conclude with the moon slowly revealing the sun once more. For Belgians, it will be a once-in-a-generation celestial display — one that won't come around again until 2081.
