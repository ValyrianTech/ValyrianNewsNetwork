# Edwards Drops Re-Election Bid After Censure Recommendation

Rep. Chuck Edwards (R-N.C.) announced early Wednesday that he is withdrawing from his re-election campaign, two days after the House Ethics Committee recommended he be censured over sexual harassment allegations. The decision brings an abrupt end to the second-term congressman's bid for a third term representing North Carolina's 11th Congressional District.

"After much prayer and reflection, I've decided to withdraw from my re-election campaign. I will complete my current term," Edwards said in a [post on X](https://x.com/RepChuckEdwards/status/2084905422482178109). "Serving Western North Carolina has been the honor of my life. Thank you for your trust, prayers, and support."

## Ethics Committee Findings

The announcement came after the bipartisan House Ethics Committee released a 25-page report on Monday recommending censure, concluding that Edwards engaged in "persistent unprofessional and inappropriate conduct towards two young female staffers." The investigation, which included 16 witness interviews and reviewed over 1,500 pages of evidence, found "substantial reason to believe" Edwards violated House rules related to sexual harassment and maintaining a hostile workplace.

According to [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/politics-news/rep-chuck-edwards-drops-re-election-bid-censure-sexual-harassment-rcna590919), the committee found no evidence that Edwards engaged in sexual activity with or explicitly propositioned any staffers, and concluded the allegations "do not implicate quid pro quo sexual harassment." However, the panel said his "pattern of behavior would lead a reasonable observer to interpret it as such," citing lavish gifts, comments about staffers' appearances, invitations to intimate dinners and vacations, and notes expressing "effusive affection."

The [NPR report](https://www.npr.org/2026/08/03/nx-s1-5918341/chuck-edwards-ethics-report) detailed how the congressman showered the two young women with gifts including jewelry totaling over $1,000, designer purses, guns, shoes, flowers, a laptop and cell phone, and a KitchenAid mixer. The report also noted that some relevant messages were "seemingly deleted" from Edwards' phone, including a photo of 30 types of ice cream delivered to one staffer's home.

Edwards, 65, has denied wrongdoing. His campaign spokesman Paul Shumaker said the congressman "looks forward to having his name cleared," while his attorneys filed a 15-page rebuttal challenging the committee's findings.

## Political Fallout and the Race Ahead

The withdrawal dramatically reshapes a race that was already becoming competitive. Edwards had been endorsed by President Donald Trump last year and was set to face Democrat Jamie Ager, a farmer from Fairview, in November. Campaign finance filings through June 30 showed Ager had raised $3.05 million compared with $939,985 for Edwards, and forecasters at Sabato's Crystal Ball had shifted the race to a "toss-up" while the Cook Political Report rated it "Lean Republican."

Under North Carolina law, the 11th District Republican Executive Committee will select a replacement nominee. The [North Carolina Republican Party announced](https://www.nc.gop/north_carolina_republican_party_announces_process_to_fill_11th_congressional_district_ballot_vacancy) the committee will meet Monday, August 10, to choose Edwards' successor on the ballot.

"The people of Western North Carolina deserve a strong conservative voice on the ballot this November, and our Party is prepared to move through this process quickly and efficiently," NCGOP Chairman Jason Simmons said.

Ager responded to the news by expressing sympathy for the staffers involved. "My heart goes out to the young women who took these roles to serve our community," he said, adding that he remains confident about winning the race regardless of who the GOP nominates.

## Broader Context

The Edwards case comes amid a wave of sexual misconduct investigations in the House. Reps. Tony Gonzales (R-Texas) and Eric Swalwell (D-Calif.) both resigned earlier this year amid allegations of inappropriate relationships with staffers, and the Ethics Committee is also investigating Rep. Cory Mills (R-Fla.) over similar allegations. These cases have triggered a bipartisan push to overhaul how sexual misconduct is handled in the House.

Some Democrats have called for Edwards to resign from Congress entirely rather than serve out his term. "If Edwards is unfit to run for re-election, he is unfit to serve in Congress," said DCCC spokesperson Madison Andrus. Rep. Pramila Jayapal (D-Wash.) went further, saying "a censure is not enough, he should be removed from Congress."

## What's Next

The House is currently on recess, so any censure vote would not take place until at least September. Edwards will continue serving until his term ends in January 2027. The 11th District Republican Executive Committee's selection on August 10 will determine who carries the GOP banner in what is expected to be one of the most closely watched House races this November.
