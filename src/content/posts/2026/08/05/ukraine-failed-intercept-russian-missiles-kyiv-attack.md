# Ukraine Failed to Intercept Any Missile in Kyiv Attack

Russia launched a devastating overnight assault on Kyiv and the surrounding region on August 4-5, killing at least 17 people and wounding 44 others, according to [Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/5/russian-attacks-kill-17-exploiting-ukraines-lack-of-missile-interceptors). In a stark demonstration of Ukraine's air defense crisis, the Ukrainian Air Force confirmed it failed to intercept a single one of the 28 missiles fired — 24 ballistic and 4 hypersonic — while successfully shooting down 98 of the 115 drones launched.

![Rescuers work at the site of a Russian missile strike on a warehouse in Kyiv, August 5, 2026](https://storage.liga.net/images/general/2026/08/05/20260805062025-4144.jpg)

## A Night of Devastation

The attack, which began in the early hours of Tuesday night, targeted civilian infrastructure across Kyiv and the Kyiv region. According to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/liveblog/oekraine-kan-geen-enkele-russische-raket-onderscheppen-zeker-17~1781787179742/), strikes hit the Obolonskyi, Sviatoshynskyi, Desnianskyi, and Holosiivskyi districts of the capital, with fires breaking out in warehouses, office buildings, and residential areas.

The deadliest single location was near the Kvitneva railway station in the Brovary district, where eight people were killed while waiting for a delayed train on a platform adjacent to attacked logistics centers, according to [LIGA.net](https://news.liga.net/ua/war/news/rosiia-masovano-atakuvala-kyiv-ta-oblast-ie-zghybli-ta-desiatky-postrazhdalykh-foto-video). An ammonia leak also occurred in the Holosiivskyi district after the attack damaged a system containing about 300 liters of the substance, though rescuers localized the leak and said there was no threat to the population.

## Civilian Businesses in the Crosshairs

Zelensky said the main targets were warehouse facilities belonging to civilian businesses, infrastructure, and a railway station. "These facilities had nothing to do with the war — a brewing company, construction materials warehouses, and civilian logistics facilities," he said.

Several major Ukrainian companies were hit. Nova Poshta, the country's largest private postal company, reported three deaths at its sorting center in Kyiv — two partner carrier drivers and one contractor employee — with eight more injured. The company said cluster munitions were used in the strike, according to [Radio Svoboda](https://www.radiosvoboda.org/a/aslidky-ataky-rosiyi-po-ukrayini/33822402.html).

![Aftermath of Russian strikes on Kyiv, August 5, 2026](https://storage.liga.net/images/general/2026/08/05/20260805062605-8142.jpg)

Supermarket chain Silpo lost six employees when fires broke out at two of its distribution centers. Epicentr, the DIY/home improvement retailer, was forced to halt production at its Epicentr Ceramic Corporation plant after a direct missile hit destroyed all production furnaces, with full restoration estimated to take 1.5-2 years. One Epicentr employee was killed and three were wounded. ROZETKA, Ukraine's largest online retailer, also reported destruction of its warehouse complex in Brovary.

Ukraine's Prime Minister Sergii Koretskyi condemned the attacks in stark terms. "These strikes have no military purpose — this is terrorism," he said, according to [Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/my-europe/2026/08/05/ukraine-failed-to-shoot-down-a-single-russian-missile-launched-in-overnight-barrage). "Russia is expanding its campaign of terror by deliberately targeting businesses that serve millions of people."

## The Air Defense Crisis

The failure to intercept any of the 28 missiles underscores Ukraine's acute shortage of anti-ballistic missile interceptors, particularly Patriot PAC-3 missiles. The Patriot system is the only capability available to Ukraine that can intercept ballistic missiles.

The production gap is stark: US-based Lockheed Martin produces roughly 60-65 interceptors per month, while Russia produces around 120 ballistic missiles monthly. The US's five-month war with Iran has also greatly depleted the global Patriot stockpile, according to [Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/my-europe/2026/08/04/will-ukraine-get-the-patriot-licence-washington-sends-mixed-signals).

Serhii Beskrestnov, an adviser to Zelensky, described the escalating situation on Facebook: "The war of attrition has entered its next phase. The enemy is attacking everything in its path: retail warehouses, food warehouses, logistics hubs, enterprises, and construction material warehouses," as reported by Al Jazeera.

## Political Uncertainty Over Patriot Production

The attack comes amid ongoing uncertainty over whether the US will grant Ukraine a license to produce Patriot interceptors domestically. President Donald Trump first promised a production license at the NATO summit in Ankara on July 8, but backtracked on July 31, saying no final decision had been taken.

US Ambassador to NATO Matthew Whitaker said on August 3 that a long-term production agreement will not be concluded before winter, citing tight US export controls on PAC-3 interceptors. "We do not allow anybody but US manufacturers to make those in the United States," he said.

Zelensky directly linked the lack of interceptors to the deaths in the attack. "Ballistic missile interceptors could have saved the lives of those killed today," he said. "It is very important that our partners understand that delays in supplying them, or an unwillingness to provide anti-ballistic systems, lead directly to such horrific casualties and destruction."

## EU Announces Financial Support

On the same day as the attack, the European Commission announced €1.4 billion in funding for Ukraine from profits generated by frozen Russian assets. European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen condemned the strikes, saying, "Once again we wake up to the news of horrible atrocities by Russia through its aerial attacks on Ukraine. Russia must pay for the destruction it has caused," according to [Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/my-europe/2026/08/05/eu-releases-14-billion-to-ukraine-from-frozen-russian-asset-profits-after-kyiv-attacks).

Ninety-five percent of the funding — €1.33 billion — will go to the Ukraine Loan Cooperation Mechanism to help service loans from the EU and G7, while the remaining €70 million will be directed to the European Peace Facility for military assistance.

## What's Next

Russia's escalating attacks, combined with Ukraine's inability to intercept ballistic missiles, raise urgent questions about the trajectory of the war. Moscow has also received a missile unit from North Korea, potentially equipped with up to 120 missiles and 6 launchers, deployed to western Russia.

Zelensky has called on partners unable to supply interceptors to impose new sanctions on Russian ballistic missile production, urging the G7 and EU to act. "A significant part of Russian ballistic production is not under sanctions. Still," he said. "New steps are needed from the G7 and the EU, from all who support the protection of life."

The key questions in the coming weeks: Will the US finalize a Patriot production license before winter? Can allied transfers of surplus interceptors meet Ukraine's immediate needs? And will the EU and G7 impose the new sanctions Zelensky has requested? The lives lost in Kyiv on August 5 may depend on the answers.
