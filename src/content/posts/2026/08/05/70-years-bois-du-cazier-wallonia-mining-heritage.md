# Bois du Cazier: 70 Years of Wallonia's Mining Legacy

Belgium is marking the 70th anniversary of the Bois du Cazier mining disaster, the deadliest industrial catastrophe in the country's history, with a year-long program of commemorations, cultural events, and scientific milestones. The tragedy, which claimed 262 lives at the Marcinelle coal mine near Charleroi on 8 August 1956, continues to shape the identity of Wallonia and its relationship with the Italian immigrant community that helped build the region's industrial might.

## A Catastrophe That Shook a Nation

On the morning of 8 August 1956, 275 men descended into the Bois du Cazier mine. At approximately 8:10 AM, a fire broke out at the 975-meter level when a loaded wagon struck a metal beam, dislodging it and causing it to fall into the shaft, damaging oil and electricity lines. The resulting blaze trapped 262 miners, killing workers of 12 different nationalities, including 136 Italians and 95 Belgians. Only 13 men survived. King Baudouin visited the site on the day of the disaster, and the last bodies were not recovered until 23 August, as [RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/70-ans-du-bois-du-cazier-sur-les-traces-d-une-catastrophe-et-d-un-heritage-minier-en-wallonie-11766681) recounts in its anniversary coverage.

The disaster exposed the brutal dangers of the mining profession and accelerated the decline of Belgium's coal industry. Important safety measures were adopted from 1957 onward regarding fire prevention and fighting in mines. The tragedy also led to a global awareness of miners' working conditions, as [RTBF's historical retrospective](https://www.rtbf.be/article/il-y-a-65-ans-262-mineurs-y-perdaient-la-vie-le-bois-du-cazier-a-marcinelle-se-souvient-de-la-catastrophe-du-8-aout-1956-10820654) notes.

## The Coal-for-Men Agreement and Italian Immigration

The disaster cannot be understood without the context of the Belgian-Italian "coal for men" agreement signed on 23 June 1946. After World War II, Belgium needed to restart its economy, but Belgian miners refused to return to the dangerous, poorly paid work underground. The agreement provided that Italy would send workers to Belgian mines in exchange for 200 kg of coal per miner per day. Between 1946 and 1948, 75,000 Italians immigrated to Belgium, and nearly 100,000 Italian workers joined Belgian coal mines by 1956, as [RTBF's feature on the agreement](https://www.rtbf.be/article/du-charbon-contre-des-hommes-80-ans-apres-l-accord-charbon-l-immigration-italienne-faconne-toujours-la-belgique-11745726) explains.

After the 1956 disaster, Italy ended the official agreement, and Belgium turned to workers from Spain and Greece. But the Italian community had already become deeply woven into the fabric of Walloon society. As Colette Ista, director of the Bois du Cazier site, told [RTBF Charleroi Matin](https://www.rtbf.be/article/bois-du-cazier-charleroi-commemore-les-70-ans-du-drame-et-les-80-ans-du-protocole-d-accord-hommes-contre-charbon-11688346): "Italy has brought so much to our region. In many families, there are mixed marriages. It's part of our community."

## A Living Heritage: Wallonia's UNESCO Mining Sites

Today, the former mining sites of Wallonia stand as monuments to this industrial past. In 2012, UNESCO inscribed four major mining sites on its World Heritage list: Grand-Hornu, Bois-du-Luc, Bois du Cazier, and Blegny-Mine. In the 1950s, mines employed nearly 140,000 people in Wallonia and produced most of Belgium's coal. The last Belgian mine, Zolder, closed in September 1992.

Blegny-Mine is one of the few mines in Europe where underground galleries are still accessible through the original shaft. Visitors descend to depths where miners worked in temperatures up to 45 degrees Celsius, as [RTBF's profile of the site](https://www.rtbf.be/article/blegny-mine-un-voyage-immersif-au-c-ur-de-l-histoire-et-de-la-nature-11509765) describes. Guide Michel explains the grueling three-shift system: "We started in the morning at 7am, finished at 3pm. Another team started at 3pm until 11pm. And from 11pm to 7am." Workers of many nationalities used Walloon as a common language on the job.

Bois-du-Luc, near La Louvière, has preserved its original organization as a mining village, with workers' houses, workshops, shafts, and administrative buildings intact. As guide Pauline reflects: "It's true that today it's quite beautiful because it's peaceful, it's calm. But back then, everything was paved. There were many people working here, lots of activity, lots of smoke, noise from the machines." The site's [RTBF profile](https://www.rtbf.be/article/hommage-aux-mineurs-italiens-au-charbonnage-du-bois-du-luc-pres-de-la-louviere-11350755) highlights its unique preservation.

## Giving the Unknown Miners Their Names Back

One of the most remarkable developments in the disaster's legacy has been the ongoing effort to identify the 14 victims who remained anonymous for decades. Their bodies rested in the honor lawn of the Marcinelle cemetery until 2019, when Michele Cicora, son of victim Francesco Cicora, initiated a groundbreaking identification project.

With the support of about fifty volunteers including forensic doctors, dentists, anthropologists, and historians, the unidentified coffins were exhumed on 4 October 2021. Using modern DNA analysis conducted by the National Institute of Criminalistics and Criminology (INCC), six victims have now been identified, most recently in May 2026 when two more miners were named, as [RTBF reported](https://www.rtbf.be/article/marcinelle-deux-victimes-supplementaires-de-la-catastrophe-du-bois-du-cazier-identifiees-11731124).

The documentary "Les inconnus du Bois du Cazier" follows this extraordinary scientific and human operation. As funeral agent Denis Fontaine puts it: "It's important to give them a face and an identity." Michele Cicora's determination is captured in his own words: "The will to find him gives me the strength to support what I see." The documentary, directed by Moïra Leyder, airs on 7 August as part of a special evening on RTBF La Une, as [RTBF's documentary feature](https://www.rtbf.be/article/les-inconnus-du-bois-du-cazier-la-promesse-d-un-fils-qui-a-change-l-histoire-11734512) details.

## A Year of Commemoration and Transmission

2026 marks not only the 70th anniversary of the disaster but also the 80th anniversary of the coal-for-men agreement. The Bois du Cazier site has prepared an exceptional program combining memory, culture, and transmission, involving 17 local cultural partners. A major exhibition of photographs by Camille Detraux and Raymond Paquet opened in March, and the site recorded record attendance in 2025 with approximately 35,000 paying visitors and nearly 60,000 including free events.

As Colette Ista told RTBF Charleroi Matin: "It's true that the witnesses are beginning to disappear, but it's taken up by the 3rd and 4th generations, by enormous numbers of young people who come to visit the Bois du Cazier. I believe this collective history is more present than ever today."

The main commemoration ceremony will take place on 8 August, beginning at 8:30 AM with speeches and a visit to the cemetery. Italian Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni will not attend, though other Italian officials including Senate President Ignazio La Russa and Labour Minister Marina Elvira Calderone are expected, as [RTBF reported](https://www.rtbf.be/article/charleroi-giorgia-meloni-sera-absente-aux-ceremonies-de-la-catastrophe-du-bois-du-cazier-11766041).

## Innovation in Remembering

In a pioneering move, RTBF has used generative artificial intelligence to reconstruct the first hours of the disaster, creating a six-minute visual sequence based on authentic historical documents. The AI-generated images are clearly identified as reconstructions to avoid confusion with genuine archives. The sequence will be broadcast on 7 August in RTBF's television news, as [RTBF's announcement](https://www.rtbf.be/article/bois-du-cazier-la-rtbf-reconstitue-les-premieres-heures-de-la-catastrophe-du-8-aout-1956-grace-a-l-intelligence-artificielle-11764887) explains.

The special evening on RTBF La Une on 7 August will also feature the film "Marina" at 20:45, the documentary "Les inconnus du Bois du Cazier" at 22:55, and the theater piece "Cìncali" at 23:50, as [RTBF's programming announcement](https://www.rtbf.be/article/70-ans-de-la-catastrophe-du-bois-du-cazier-une-soiree-tres-speciale-se-prepare-sur-rtbf-la-une-11763629) details.

## A Legacy That Endures

Seventy years after the tragedy, the Bois du Cazier disaster remains far more than an industrial accident. It is a foundation of collective memory, a social and cultural heritage, and a permanent reminder of the human price paid to rebuild post-war Europe. As Bois du Cazier officials put it, the dual anniversaries of 2026 tell "the same story: that of work, exile, sacrifice, and solidarity."

In 2026, Wallonia is not merely commemorating the past—it is transmitting it to new generations, ensuring that the lessons of Marcinelle remain alive for decades to come.
