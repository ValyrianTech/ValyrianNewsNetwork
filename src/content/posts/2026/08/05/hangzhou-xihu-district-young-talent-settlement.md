# Hangzhou Xihu District Eases Young Talent Settlement

Hangzhou's Xihu District is rolling out a comprehensive suite of measures to help young professionals overcome housing and settlement challenges, transforming the difficulties of "finding a foothold" into lasting stability. The initiative, reported by [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/local/20260805/63c8a0b05bf349b3933d6549e55e97da/c.html), reflects a broader strategy by the eastern Chinese city to attract and retain the talent driving its technology-driven economy.

## A Full-Chain Talent Service System

At the heart of the effort is the upgraded "Xihu Talent Recruitment Project" version 2.0, which builds a full-chain youth talent service system. The Xihu District Talent Office has partnered with Xihu Investment Group to use the "Xitou·Yinli Port" platform as a carrier, providing intelligent, community-based long-term rental apartments on the housing side and shared offices, roadshow centers, and other functional modules on the entrepreneurship side.

The approach is designed to create an integrated "housing + entrepreneurship" full-cycle service ecosystem, allowing young talent to first "settle in" and then "put down roots."

## Affordable Housing: A Tangible Difference

For young professionals, the housing component has made an immediate impact. Luo Zhaowei, a young professional who recently moved into the Xitou·Xiyue Apartment in the district's youth talent community, described the transformation firsthand.

"Previously I stayed in hotels long-term — the cost was high and life was inconvenient," Luo said. "After moving into the Xitou·Xiyue Apartment, the rent is only half the market price, with complete gym and communal kitchen facilities, plus weekly community activities. The problems of housing, living, and socializing are all solved."

The district also operates the Qinghe Station program, which provides free accommodation for up to seven days (six nights) for eligible young people and new citizens coming to the district for job interviews, talent recruitment, or entrepreneurship competitions. According to [Hangzhou Bendibao](https://m.hz.bendibao.com/live/165949.shtm), the district currently has 17 Qinghe Station locations across sub-districts including Sandun, Zhuantang, Gudang, Lingyin, Liuxia, Cuiyuan, Jiangcun, Xixi, and Wenxin.

## From Laboratory to Production Line

Securing affordable housing is only the first step. The district's talent retention strategy also focuses on helping young professionals "do well" in their careers, particularly in technology entrepreneurship.

Hangzhou Logic Bit Technology serves as a flagship success story. The team of academicians, professors, and PhDs was selected for the "Xihu Talent" A-class project and received targeted support, successfully developing a 121-qubit quantum computing prototype machine with leading-edge technology.

"Xihu Science and Technology Venture Capital participated in every round of the company's financing, accompanying us from technology 'demonstration' to industrial application," said Wang Zhen, the company's founder.

The company's trajectory has been notable. Founded in 2022 by Wang Zhen, a Zhejiang University-trained researcher, Logic Bit has completed six rounds of financing, with the latest A-round of several hundred million yuan announced in July 2026. As [21st Century Business Herald](https://m.21jingji.com/article/20260709/herald/a47fe38942943037615b98d7ee9888ef.html) reported, the company also released a superconducting quantum computing cloud platform at its Quantum Day 2026 event, with 80% of its team members born after 1995.

Beyond individual projects, Xihu District has developed a mature technology commercialization mechanism. The district has invested in 56 university science and technology achievement transformation projects and linked "Xihu Talent" policies to invest in 65 high-quality talent projects, working closely with Zhejiang University, Westlake University, and other institutions to incubate laboratory projects and promote industrial application.

## Recruitment Through Community and Events

Making young talent "see" the district's sincerity and "feel" the opportunities is also a key part of the strategy. At this year's 2050 Conference — a non-profit technology event initiated by Wang Jian, academician of the Chinese Academy of Engineering and founder of Alibaba Cloud — the Xihu District Talent Office partnered with Datawhale, China's largest AI open-source learning community, to co-organize learning festivals and industry-talent forums.

As [Sina Finance](https://finance.sina.com.cn/wm/2026-04-16/doc-inhutnfy1426545.shtml) reported, the 2050 Conference, held annually in April at Hangzhou's Yunqi Town under the theme "Young people gather because of technology," has grown into a significant platform for talent recruitment. The Xihu District Talent Office's involvement included initiating industry-talent forums to discuss how government can provide "advisory-style" services for young people.

## A Model for China's Talent Competition

The Xihu District approach reflects a broader trend in Chinese urban governance: cities competing fiercely to attract and retain young talent, particularly in the technology sector. As [Dazhong News](https://m.dzplus.dzng.com/share/general/0/NEWS2138049GNXEMGEUGFZQD) noted in its analysis of Hangzhou's "Six Little Dragons" tech companies, most founders of these notable firms are not originally from Hangzhou but chose to start businesses there, drawn by forward-looking industrial policies and sincere talent recruitment efforts.

Hangzhou's "government provides sunshine and rain, enterprises grow" philosophy, combined with its strong university ecosystem and business-friendly environment, has made it one of China's leading innovation hubs. The Xihu District's comprehensive approach — integrating housing security, career development, community building, and active recruitment — represents a shift from simple financial incentives to full ecosystem building.

## What to Watch

As the district continues to upgrade its talent programs, key questions remain about scalability and long-term retention. Can other districts replicate this comprehensive model? How will these programs evolve as competition for tech talent intensifies both domestically and internationally?

For now, the Xihu District's efforts are delivering tangible results — from affordable apartments and free temporary housing to venture capital support and technology commercialization services. The measures are injecting sustained youthful energy into what the district describes as a "highland for new quality productive forces," and they signal a growing recognition among Chinese cities that winning the talent war requires more than just financial incentives — it requires building communities where young professionals can truly thrive.
