# FAA Investigates Why Plane Was Allowed to Take Off as Trump's Helicopter Approached

The Federal Aviation Administration is investigating why a commercial airplane was allowed to take off from Ronald Reagan Washington National Airport as President Donald Trump's helicopter, Marine One, was approaching on Tuesday afternoon — an apparent violation of safety procedures implemented after last year's deadly midair collision near the busy Washington, D.C. airport.

The incident occurred as Marine One departed the White House for Joint Base Andrews with the president aboard for the first leg of a West Coast trip. According to [AP News](https://apnews.com/article/marine-one-trump-reagan-airport-faa-midair-c3e7c86eff1eeec9b636cd2d8d89714a), the FAA decided after 67 people died on Jan. 29, 2025, when an airliner collided with an Army Black Hawk helicopter that all takeoffs and landings at Reagan would be halted anytime a helicopter passes by the airport.

## The Incident

Marine One lifted off from the Ellipse near the White House at approximately 2:33 p.m. ET on Tuesday, en route to Joint Base Andrews for the first leg of a trip to Los Angeles. Roughly one minute later, Envoy Air Flight 3742, an Embraer E170 operated by a wholly owned subsidiary of American Airlines, departed Reagan National Airport bound for Pensacola, Florida, according to flight tracking data.

Air traffic controllers had not halted commercial flights at Reagan National as required under the policy implemented after the January 2025 collision. FAA rules require that air traffic control receive at least three minutes' advance notice before Marine One departs so that commercial traffic at Reagan — about 4 miles from the Ellipse — can be halted.

The FAA confirmed a momentary loss of separation between Marine One and the commercial jet. FAA rules require aircraft to remain separated by at least 1.5 miles horizontally and 500 feet vertically around airports. Sources told [Reuters](https://www.jpost.com/international/article-904614) that the two aircraft did not maintain the required separation.

However, the airplane was climbing above Marine One as the helicopter approached, so the two aircraft were not on a collision course. The FAA said the aircraft continued to move away from one another after the loss of separation.

"Based on our preliminary safety review, there was momentary loss of separation after which the aircraft continued to move away from one another," the FAA said in a statement. "The air traffic controller was in contact with both the commercial pilot and Marine One pilot during the loss of separation. As the White House has said, the President was never in danger."

## Official Responses

The White House emphasized that President Trump was not in danger during the incident. "Marine One flights are piloted by some of the best aviators in the world, and at no point was the President in harm's way," White House spokesman Kush Desai said.

The Marine Corps also defended the actions of its helicopter pilots. "There were no 'close calls' with the routine Marine One movement Tuesday," Marine Corps spokesman Capt. Jacob Sugg said in an email. "Washington National Tower did not delay the helicopter crew, ask them to hold, or otherwise alter their flight profile."

A third aircraft, Republic Airways Flight 4700, was approximately 3 miles from Reagan at the time and was redirected as a precaution, looping around the airport before landing safely. Both Marine One and the commercial jet also landed without incident.

The Wall Street Journal first reported the incident. Two sources told Reuters that the FAA planned to convene a Safety Review Team to investigate — a significant formal step that examines underlying causes and produces recommendations for systemic change. The National Transportation Safety Board said Wednesday morning that it is still gathering information and hasn't yet decided whether to investigate.

## A Troubled Airspace

The incident comes amid heightened scrutiny of the congested airspace near the White House and Reagan National, which operates what the FAA has described as the single busiest runway in the United States. As [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/aug/05/trump-faa-marine-one-air-safety-incident) noted, the episode followed a tightening of proximity rules in the wake of the January 2025 collision that killed 67 people — the deadliest U.S. air disaster in more than two decades.

The NTSB's final report, released in January 2026, found that the collision resulted from deep-rooted systemic failures, including the FAA's flawed placement of helicopter routes in dangerously close proximity to Reagan's runway approach paths and an overreliance on the see-and-avoid principle.

Following the crash, the FAA imposed permanent restrictions on helicopter operations around Reagan, banned mixed helicopter and jet traffic, and required all military aircraft to broadcast their locations. In March 2026, the agency issued a nationwide notice suspending the use of visual separation between airplanes and helicopters in busy terminal airspace, citing an overreliance on pilot see-and-avoid operations.

## What's Next

The FAA's decision to convene a Safety Review Team signals that the agency is treating Tuesday's incident with significant seriousness. The main group of family members of last year's crash victims questioned why the government hasn't done more to prevent collisions in the crowded airspace around Reagan, urging Congress to pass the package of aviation safety reforms recommended by the NTSB.

As the investigation unfolds, the incident raises broader questions about whether the safety protocols implemented after last year's tragedy are being consistently enforced — and whether the nation's busiest airport can safely accommodate both presidential movements and the growing volume of commercial traffic that Congress has authorized.
