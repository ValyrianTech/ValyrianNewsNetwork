# China's 15th Five-Year Plans Center on Daily Life

China has unveiled a wave of "15th Five-Year" special plans that place people's daily necessities and livelihood at the heart of national economic strategy, signaling a shift toward consumption-driven growth anchored in tangible improvements to ordinary citizens' lives. The plans, released over the past month, span tourism, sports, health, and consumption expansion, collectively outlining how Beijing intends to translate strategic economic planning into everyday benefits.

## A Blueprint Rooted in Everyday Needs

The plans come as China's [People's Daily](https://www.news.cn/fortune/20260805/240da7965dfa4e53aadc7facf3bfb47c/c.html) frames the policy wave through the lens of "daily necessities" — the rice, oil, salt, and firewood that sustain households. This framing reflects a deliberate policy emphasis: the "15th Five-Year" Plan Outline, approved at the National People's Congress in March, sets 20 major indicators, with seven focused on livelihood — the highest proportion ever. These include targets for employment, income growth, education, healthcare, elderly care, and childcare.

According to [China Economic Net](http://district.ce.cn/newarea/roll/202604/t20260408_2888188.shtml), the seven livelihood indicators include keeping urban surveyed unemployment below 5.5%, ensuring per capita disposable income growth keeps pace with GDP, raising average education years of the working-age population to 11.7 years, and extending average life expectancy to 80 years by 2030. The plan also targets 73% of nursing-type beds in elderly care institutions and a 6-percentage-point increase in childcare enrollment for children under three.

## Consumption: The 60 Trillion Yuan Ambition

At the center of the policy push is the [Expanding Consumption "15th Five-Year" Plan](https://m.21jingji.com/article/20260713/herald/179235f36766ee18109e7543c6f85dd2.html), approved by the State Council on July 13. The plan targets total retail sales of consumer goods reaching approximately 60 trillion yuan by 2030, with significantly improved household consumption rates and a stronger role for consumption in driving economic growth.

The plan organizes its approach around four main lines: expanding service consumption, upgrading goods consumption, enhancing consumption capacity, and improving institutional policies. Service consumption takes center stage, with seven priority areas including daily life services, elderly care, childcare, culture and tourism, health, sports, and education.

"Achieving this goal will mean consumption's pulling power on growth is further strengthened, and the development pattern led by domestic demand becomes more stable," said Fu Yifu, a special researcher at Su Shang Bank, as quoted by 21st Century Business Herald.

New consumption models are also emphasized, including digital consumption, green consumption, first-release economy, experiential consumption, and inbound consumption. "AI + consumption" receives prominent attention, with plans to expand digital product offerings including AI-enabled smartphones, computers, smart wearables, and intelligent robots.

The plan also addresses the structural shift in Chinese consumption patterns. In the first half of 2026, service retail sales grew 5.3% year-on-year, significantly outpacing goods retail at 1.1%, according to [People's Daily](https://www.peopleapp.com/column/30052743982-500007614254). Tourism consulting and rental services grew 11.3%, while cultural and sports leisure services rose 10.4%. Inbound tourism has also surged, with visa-free entries up 30.6% year-on-year.

## Tourism: Building a Powerhouse

The [Tourism Powerhouse Construction "15th Five-Year" Plan](https://travel.cnr.cn/dsywzl/20260712/t20260712_527705385.shtml), released by the Ministry of Culture and Tourism on July 7, marks the first national-level special plan themed on building a tourism powerhouse. It targets significant progress in tourism high-quality development by 2030, with first-time goals for tourism inclusiveness and accessibility.

The plan emphasizes smart tourism, green development, and integrated development across sectors. It also introduces new focus on marine tourism space development, including cultivating marine tourism advantage regions, promoting island tourism, and improving cruise port infrastructure.

For inbound tourism, the plan calls for optimizing visa-free policies, opening new international routes and tourist trains, and streamlining departure tax refund procedures. These measures build on the momentum of the "China Travel" boom that has drawn increasing numbers of international visitors.

## Sports: A 7 Trillion Yuan Industry

The [Sports Powerhouse Construction "15th Five-Year" Plan](https://m.21jingji.com/article/20260724/herald/64615b2bb00259f7a710f6466f1741ac.html), officially released July 22, is the first sports plan included in the national special planning catalog. It targets a total sports industry scale exceeding 7 trillion yuan by 2030.

A key highlight is the establishment of a "sports event economy ecosystem centered on independent event IPs." The plan supports village-level events including the "Village Super League," "Village BA," and "Village Volleyball," alongside urban league competitions. It also plans 100 high-quality outdoor sports destinations.

"The events economy is a prominent highlight of this plan," said Bai Yufei, a professor at Beijing Sport University. "For the first time, the plan proposes building a sports event economy ecosystem centered on self-owned event IPs, emphasizing systematic development and endogenous growth capacity."

Jiang Yiyi, Dean of the Tourism College at Beijing Sport University, noted that the sports industry's value has "upgraded from single economic contribution to multiple values... becoming a core hub industry connecting multiple fields and chains."

## Health: A National Priority

The [National Health "15th Five-Year" Plan](https://5g.dahe.cn/news/202607142109556), approved by the State Council on June 29, sets ambitious targets including reducing the smoking rate among people aged 15 and above to 20% and raising average life expectancy to 80 years by 2030. The plan emphasizes comprehensive health improvement as the foundation for Chinese-style modernization.

According to Dahe Net, the smoking rate target builds on the "Healthy China 2030" framework, with current smoking rates at 23.2% according to 2024 data from the China CDC. The plan calls for strengthening smoke-free environment construction and expanding smoking cessation services.

## Infrastructure for Consumption: The Golden Outer Ring

The Ministry of Transport has announced plans to fully connect three major highway corridors — border highways G219 and G331, and coastal G228 — during the "15th Five-Year" period. According to [Yicai](https://www.yicai.com/video/103285561.html), Vice Minister of Transport Xu Chengguang described these routes as forming a "Golden Outer Ring" around China's borders and coastline, serving as major tourism, resource development, and border security corridors.

## Analysis: A Systematic Approach to Consumption

The coordinated release of these plans marks a transition from scattered, piecemeal consumption policies to a systematic, integrated approach. The 60 trillion yuan retail sales target represents both a blueprint and a mobilization order for using consumption as the anchor to stabilize economic fundamentals.

The emphasis on "daily necessities" signals that economic planning is focused on tangible improvements to ordinary people's lives, not just macroeconomic aggregates. This is reinforced by the unprecedented proportion of livelihood indicators in the Plan Outline and the "investment in people" approach that prioritizes social spending.

Peng Bo, a researcher at the Ministry of Commerce Research Institute, emphasized the need for a "trinity mechanism of strong guarantees, increased income, and optimized supply" to consolidate the foundation of endogenous consumption growth.

## What to Watch For

As these plans move from paper to implementation, several developments bear watching: the rollout of paid annual leave reforms that could unlock leisure time for tourism and recreation; the expansion of AI-enabled consumer products and services; the development of the Golden Outer Ring tourism corridor; and the effectiveness of income-boosting measures in sustaining consumption momentum through 2030.

For China's 1.4 billion consumers, the ultimate test of these plans will be whether they translate into better jobs, higher incomes, improved healthcare, and richer cultural and leisure experiences. The plans' emphasis on livelihood indicators suggests Beijing understands that economic strategy must ultimately serve the "rice, oil, salt, and firewood" of everyday life.
