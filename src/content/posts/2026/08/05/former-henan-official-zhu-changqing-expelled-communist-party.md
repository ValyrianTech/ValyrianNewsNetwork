# Henan Official Zhu Changqing Expelled from Party

Zhu Changqing, former director of the Environment and Resources Protection Committee of the Henan Provincial People's Congress, has been expelled from the Communist Party of China for serious disciplinary violations, according to an [announcement from Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/legal/20260805/51eaf973fb0d4328b7c0d2f8e5e5835e/c.html) published August 5, 2026. The decision was made following an investigation by the Henan Provincial Commission for Discipline Inspection, with approval from the Henan Provincial Party Committee.

## Investigation and Charges

The investigation into Zhu began on February 24, 2026, when the Central Commission for Discipline Inspection announced he was under review for suspected serious disciplinary and legal violations, as [reported by Henan Broadcasting](https://c.m.163.com/news/a/KMHU2OV70550B6IS.html). The five-month investigation concluded with findings that Zhu had committed serious violations of political discipline, organizational discipline, and integrity discipline, and is suspected of bribery crimes.

According to the official announcement, Zhu was found to have "lost his ideals and beliefs, abandoned his original mission, was disloyal and dishonest to the Party, and resisted organizational review." He also failed to report personal matters as required, failed to truthfully explain issues during inquiry, engaged in profit-making activities in violation of regulations, and used his position to seek benefits for others in land purchases and project construction, illegally receiving substantial amounts of property.

The announcement emphasized that Zhu "did not restrain himself or stop after the 18th Party Congress" in 2012, noting that the nature of his violations was serious and the impact adverse.

## Career and Rise Through the Ranks

Born in January 1959 in Fangcheng County, Henan Province, Zhu graduated from Zhengzhou University's History Department in 1982 before beginning a four-decade career in public service, according to his [biographical profile on Baidu Baike](https://baike.baidu.com/item/朱长青/42564). He rose through county and municipal positions in the Nanyang region, serving as deputy mayor of Nanyang City and later as a standing committee member and secretary-general of the Nanyang City Party Committee.

Zhu moved to provincial-level roles in 2011, serving as deputy secretary-general of the Henan Provincial Government. In 2013, he was appointed Party Secretary and Director of the Henan Provincial Food and Drug Administration. His most significant role came in January 2016, when he was appointed Party Secretary and Director of the Henan Provincial Land and Resources Department—a position he held until 2018, as confirmed by a [2017 article from the department's website](https://maolinsh.com/html/sitegrouproothtmlff8080814e03fe01014e04186744012071d41f6c50f3440fb1fae54fd25c07fa.html) documenting his leadership.

In January 2018, Zhu was appointed Director of the Henan Provincial People's Congress Environment and Resources Protection Committee, a role he held until his retirement in March 2022 at age 63. He also served as a member of the 10th Henan Provincial Party Committee and a representative of the 13th Henan Provincial People's Congress.

## Punishment and Legal Proceedings

Under the Party discipline regulations and relevant laws, Zhu has been expelled from the Party, had his retirement benefits cancelled, and his illegal gains confiscated. His case has been referred to prosecutors for criminal review and prosecution, with the involved assets transferred along with the case.

## Context: Anti-Corruption Campaign and Retired Officials

Zhu's case is part of China's ongoing anti-corruption campaign that has intensified since the 18th Party Congress in 2012. His investigation approximately four years after retirement highlights the campaign's reach into the ranks of retired officials, who are not exempt from scrutiny.

The case also draws attention to the land and resources sector, which has been a focus area for anti-corruption enforcement given the significant discretionary power held by officials in land allocation and construction approvals. Zhu's violations specifically reference "land purchases and project construction," areas central to his responsibilities as head of the provincial Land and Resources Department.

Henan Province has seen sustained anti-corruption enforcement in recent years. When a central inspection team was stationed in the province in April 2025, at least nine officials were announced as under investigation within a single week, reflecting the ongoing scrutiny of the province's political establishment.

## What to Watch

With Zhu's case now referred to prosecutors, the next phase will involve formal criminal proceedings on bribery charges. The outcome will be closely watched as another data point in China's continuing campaign against official corruption, particularly in the land and natural resources sector where enforcement has been especially active.
