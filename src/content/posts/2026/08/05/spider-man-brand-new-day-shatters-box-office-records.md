# Spider-Man: Brand New Day Shatters Box Office Records

"Spider-Man: Brand New Day" has made box office history, becoming the biggest domestic opening of all time with $360 million in its first weekend and crossing $1 billion globally in just six days. The Sony and Marvel Studios release, starring Tom Holland in his fourth solo outing as the web-slinger, has now grossed $1.155 billion worldwide, making it the [highest-grossing film of 2026](https://variety.com/2026/film/box-office/spider-man-brand-new-day-highest-grossing-movie-box-office-1236828413/).

## A Record-Breaking Debut

The film's final domestic tally of $360 million surpassed "Avengers: Endgame"'s $357.1 million opening from April 2019, according to [Deadline's comprehensive record tally](https://deadline.com/2026/08/spider-man-brand-new-day-box-office-records-1237016328/). The debut also set records for the biggest U.S. previews ($72 million), biggest Friday including previews ($169.8 million), and the largest opening weekend in U.S. admissions in the post-COVID era, with approximately 24.1 million attendees.

Internationally, the film collected $572 million from over 73,500 screens, with China contributing $121 million alone. That brought the worldwide debut to $932 million, second only to "Avengers: Endgame"'s $1.2 billion. The film also set all-time opening records in territories including France, India, Brazil, Spain, and the United Arab Emirates.

## The Biggest Weekend in Theater History

"Brand New Day" didn't achieve its record alone. Combined with Christopher Nolan's "The Odyssey," which earned $51 million in its third weekend, the two films helped propel the [biggest weekend of all time for North American theaters](https://variety.com/2026/film/box-office/spider-man-brand-new-day-box-office-opening-weekend-1236825953/), with approximately $430 million in total ticket sales.

What makes the achievement even more remarkable is that "Brand New Day" did not play in IMAX, as "The Odyssey" held the network's entire domestic footprint for three weekends. Despite this, the film dominated other premium large formats, setting new opening records for Dolby Cinema and 4DX.

## A Franchise at Its Peak

The film's success underscores the enduring power of the Spider-Man franchise. Holland's previous solo outings — "Homecoming" (2017), "Far From Home" (2019), and the colossal "No Way Home" (2021), which grossed $1.9 billion worldwide — have consistently performed at the highest levels. "Brand New Day" now ranks as the second-highest-grossing Spider-Man film ever.

Directed by Destin Daniel Cretton and written by Chris McKenna and Erik Sommers, the film follows Peter Parker four years after the events of "No Way Home," as he anonymously protects New York City while grappling with an evolution of his superpowers. It features Sadie Sink as Jean Grey, Jon Bernthal as Frank Castle/Punisher, and Mark Ruffalo as Bruce Banner/Hulk.

The film has received strong critical and audience reception, holding a [90% score on Rotten Tomatoes](https://editorial.rottentomatoes.com/article/weekend-box-office-spider-man-brand-new-day-climbs-to-spectacular-355-million-debut/) along with an "A" CinemaScore and a five-star PostTrak rating.

## Defying Superhero Fatigue

The record-breaking performance comes amid persistent talk of "superhero fatigue" in Hollywood. Following "Avengers: Endgame," Marvel experienced several high-profile disappointments, including "The Marvels" (2023), "Ant-Man and the Wasp: Quantumania" (2023), and "Captain America: Brave New World" (2025).

"Audiences are tired of seeing the same thing," analyst Jeff Bock of Exhibitor Relations told Variety. "There are too many connected storylines, and it's turning entertainment into homework. We need a fresh start."

"Brand New Day" was promoted as exactly that — a reset for the web-slinging series. The approach clearly resonated with audiences, particularly younger moviegoers. Ticket buyers were predominantly male (about 61%), with slightly more than half under age 25.

## What It Means for the Box Office

The film's success has broader implications for the industry. Paul Dergarabedian, head of marketplace trends for Rentrak, called it "perhaps the most important summer moviegoing season in the history of the business." The summer is now on pace to potentially surpass $4 billion for the first time since 2023's "Barbenheimer" summer.

With "Dune 3" and "Avengers: Doomsday" still on the horizon, Hollywood is hoping 2026 domestic revenues will eclipse $10 billion for the first time since 2019. The year has already produced four billion-dollar films, with "The Odyssey" expected to become the fifth.

## Looking Ahead

Marvel Studios president Kevin Feige called the debut "overwhelming, frankly, to find ourselves in this position on a Monday morning — in the very best way." The film's success bodes well for "Avengers: Doomsday," which reunites a smattering of superheroes and reintroduces Robert Downey Jr. as Doctor Doom in December.

As for Spider-Man's future, Holland has said a succession plan is already in the works for the role. But after a weekend like this, it's hard to imagine the studio will want to part with the 30-year-old actor anytime soon.

With "Brand New Day" now the [second-fastest movie to cross $1 billion](https://variety.com/2026/film/box-office/spider-man-brand-new-day-billion-dollars-six-days-1236826658/) after "Avengers: Endgame," and with minimal competition in the weekends ahead, analysts expect the film to climb rapidly toward $2 billion — a milestone only seven other movies have ever achieved.
