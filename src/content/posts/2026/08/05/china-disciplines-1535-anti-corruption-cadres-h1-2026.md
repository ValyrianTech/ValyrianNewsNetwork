# China Disciplines 1,535 Anti-Corruption Cadres in H1 2026

China's Central Commission for Discipline Inspection (CCDI) reported on August 4 that 1,535 discipline inspection and supervision cadres were disciplined in the first half of 2026, as the country's top anti-corruption body continues its campaign to root out misconduct within its own ranks. According to [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/government/20260805/b3fa24b8205b4df78f5136e7d69131ff/c.html), the CCDI placed 1,685 cadres on file for investigation during the period, with 138 transferred to judicial organs for prosecution.

## Internal Oversight Intensifies

The semi-annual report, released by the CCDI on August 4, details the scope of internal scrutiny within China's anti-corruption apparatus. National discipline inspection organs received more than 15,500 leads or reports involving discipline inspection cadres and processed over 14,200 of them. The commission also conducted 4,385 talks and correspondence inquiries with cadres under review.

Among those disciplined, 59 were bureau-level officials and 356 were county or division-level cadres, according to the [CCTV News report](https://m.21jingji.com/article/20260804/herald/3b9088d4db155207a2d82a17842ccd1a.html) carried by 21jingji.com. The report emphasizes that discipline inspection organs at all levels continued to strengthen the building of the cadre team "with higher standards and more practical measures," promoting the "Three Modernizations" Construction Year initiative aimed at standardizing procedures, enhancing legal compliance, and improving the quality of supervision and enforcement.

## The "Four Forms" System

The CCDI applied its graduated "four forms" disciplinary system to 5,999 person-times of discipline inspection cadres during the first half of 2026. The vast majority—4,397 person-times—received criticism and education under the first form, which addresses minor issues through talks and self-criticism. Another 1,219 person-times received light disciplinary action under the second form, while 222 received heavy disciplinary action under the third form. The most serious 161 cases fell under the fourth form, which involves transfer to judicial authorities for investigation and prosecution.

This distribution aligns with the CCDI's philosophy of "using the first form as the norm," emphasizing early intervention and correction over punitive measures wherever possible.

## Historical Context and Trends

The 2026 figures represent a decline from the first half of 2025, when [2,042 cadres were placed on file and 1,666 were disciplined](http://m.ahjjjc.gov.cn/p/127150.html), according to data published by the Anhui Provincial Discipline Inspection Commission. The numbers have fluctuated since the CCDI began publishing semi-annual internal oversight reports in 2023, when a nationwide education and rectification campaign drove figures to 2,482 placed on file and 1,647 disciplined in the first half of that year, as [Beijing News reported](https://m.bjnews.com.cn/detail/1692753531169734.html).

However, the number of cadres transferred to judicial organs has risen to 138 in H1 2026, up from 101 in the same period of 2025, indicating that serious violations continue to be detected and prosecuted. For the full year of 2024, the CCDI [reported](https://m.mp.oeeee.com/a/BAAFRD0000202504171070518.html) that 3,375 cadres were placed on file, 3,917 were disciplined, and 374 were transferred to judicial organs.

## Addressing the "Lamp Black" Problem

The CCDI's self-supervision efforts directly target what Chinese officials call the "lamp black" (灯下黑) problem—corruption within the anti-corruption agencies themselves. The commission has repeatedly emphasized that "discipline inspection organs are not naturally a pure land, and discipline inspection cadres do not have natural immunity."

At the 20th CCDI's 5th Plenary Session in January 2026, the commission called for consolidating the achievements of the "Standardization, Rule-of-Law, and Regularization Construction Year" initiative and extending it for another two years. The session's [communique](https://www.gov.cn/yaowen/liebiao/202601/content_7054741.htm) stressed the need to "perfect internal power constraint mechanisms and management supervision systems" and to "resolutely prevent 'lamp black'."

## What to Watch

As the CCDI continues its "Three Modernizations" initiative through 2027, observers will be watching whether the declining trend in internal discipline cases reflects genuine improvement in conduct among oversight cadres or a shift in enforcement priorities. The second half of 2026 will provide further data points on whether the current trajectory holds, and whether the increased referrals to judicial organs signal a tougher stance on the most serious violations within China's anti-corruption system.
