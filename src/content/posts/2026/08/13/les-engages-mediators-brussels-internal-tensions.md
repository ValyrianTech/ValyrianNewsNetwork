# Les Engagés Sends Mediators to Quell Brussels Internal Tensions

The centrist Les Engagés party has dispatched a mediator to address significant internal tensions in two Brussels locations, according to [La Libre Belgique](https://www.lalibre.be/belgique/2026/08/13/vives-tensions-chez-les-engages-a-bruxelles-une-bourgmestre-accusee-dautoritarisme-et-un-chef-cab-de-discrimination-raciale-SGKYWBPAJ5EXXLU5YGTEI36674/). The intervention follows accusations of authoritarianism against Jette's mayor Claire Vandevivere and allegations of racial discrimination against the chief of staff of Alderman Didier Wauters at the City of Brussels.

## Two Fronts of Conflict

The party's intervention targets two distinct trouble spots: the cabinet of Alderman Didier Wauters, who oversees Economic Affairs, Employment, Commerce and Communal Equipment at the City of Brussels, and the local party section in Jette, as [DH Net](https://www.dhnet.be/regions/bruxelles/2026/08/13/la-bourgmestre-de-jette-claire-vandevivere-dans-la-tourmente-les-engages-ont-envoye-un-mediateur-dans-la-commune-GGMAEX3ZFVDEBBJRKUDIVEGVJI/) reported.

At the Wauters cabinet, anonymous testimonies describe "recurring aggressiveness, public denigration, and repeated threats of dismissal," with women and racialized women said to be particularly targeted, according to [BX1](https://bx1.be/categories/news/autoritarisme-a-jette-et-management-toxique-a-la-ville-de-bruxelles-les-engages-depechent-un-mediateur-pour-apaiser-les-tensions/). Both the chief of staff and Alderman Wauters deny the allegations. Wauters acknowledges having observed "inadequate and unkind interactions between cabinet members" and that two groups were no longer speaking to each other, but says he never heard any racist or sexist remarks. The chief of staff dismisses the situation as a "storm in a teacup," denouncing "false rumors and accusations" from a single person and raising the possibility of filing a complaint.

In Jette, the situation is described as "more explosive and dangerous," with Vandevivere's management characterized as "divisive and authoritarian," potentially leading to the "implosion" of the local section.

## A Rocky Succession

The problems in Jette trace back to the succession of longtime mayor Hervé Doyen in spring 2022. Vandevivere, who had served as alderman for over 15 years, became the first woman mayor of Jette after Doyen's retirement following more than two decades in office, as [RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/jette-a-tous-les-atouts-selon-sa-nouvelle-bourgmestre-claire-vandevivere-10999796) reported at the time. Three close collaborators of the former mayor have since left the team under difficult circumstances.

The electoral picture has added to the malaise. In the October 2024 local elections, Vandevivere's list (LBJette/Les Engagés) remained the leading list but dropped from 16 to 11 seats — a significant loss of five seats compared to 2018. The decline has generated internal discontent within the local section, compounded by vocal opposition from socialist deputy Leila Agić and Fouad Ahidar at the municipal council.

## Defending Leadership

Vandevivere rejects the characterization of her leadership style. "I would simply say that, as a mayor, it's normal to be the subject of criticism," she told Le Soir, as quoted by La Libre Belgique. "However, I strive to combine leadership and participative management. One can always improve." She maintains that she was the one who contacted the party, defending her approach as intended to improve collective work and describing the criticisms as "subjective."

Yvan Verougstraete, president of Les Engagés in Brussels, sought to downplay the severity of the situation, telling Le Soir that "it is not abnormal for there to be tensions during a legislature" and framing the intervention as a matter of "active management in supporting teams."

## Broader Party Dynamics

The tensions come at a delicate moment for Les Engagés, which has been navigating significant political repositioning since its transformation from the Christian-democratic cdH to a centrist party in 2022. At the City of Brussels, Wauters was confirmed as alderman in November 2024, bringing Les Engagés-CD&V back into the majority after 12 years in opposition, as [L'Avenir](https://www.lavenir.net/regions/bruxelles/bruxelles/2024/11/27/communales-2024-prive-dun-siege-apres-recomptage-le-mr-renonce-a-lappel-maouane-le-recupere-pour-ecolo-les-engages-annoncent-wauters-echevin-KO7G735WQZDDDFKP2HP3CP4LNY/) reported.

The party has also faced other internal challenges in Brussels, including the Foyer Anderlechtois controversy earlier this year, and the broader political landscape in the Brussels-Capital Region remains fragile following a prolonged government formation crisis.

## What to Watch

The effectiveness of the mediator's intervention remains to be seen. Key questions include whether the chief of staff at the Wauters cabinet will face disciplinary action or legal consequences, whether leadership changes could occur at the Jette local section, and how these internal tensions will affect Les Engagés' position in Brussels politics. The allegations of racial discrimination, if substantiated, could carry significant legal and reputational consequences for the party. As [Newsy Today](https://www.newsy-today.com/brussels-les-engages-party-faces-crisis-over-authoritarianism-and-discrimination-claims/) noted, the party's decision to send mediators signals recognition of the seriousness of the situation — but whether the intervention can prevent the "implosion" feared in Jette remains an open question.
