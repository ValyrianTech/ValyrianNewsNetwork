# Belgian Discovery Could Rewrite Anatomy Textbooks Worldwide

Researchers at the University of Antwerp have made a groundbreaking anatomical discovery that challenges centuries-old teachings about the human groin region, potentially requiring the rewriting of anatomy textbooks worldwide. The finding, led by Dr. Thomas Mathieu, demonstrates that a muscle in the groin area does not insert on the pubic bone as previously believed, according to [La Libre Belgique](https://www.lalibre.be/planete/sante/2026/08/13/une-decouverte-belge-pourrait-elle-amener-a-reecrire-les-manuels-danatomie-du-monde-entier-2NOXNUZ5TFFU7DPX4JZ673N6YA/).

## The Discovery

Dr. Thomas Mathieu, a Belgian physician specializing in physical medicine, rehabilitation, and sports medicine, led the research team that conducted extensive anatomical dissection studies on cadavers. The research revealed that the musculotendinous attachments of the adductors and abdominal structures have close relationships with the pubic ligaments, and that different attachment muscles in the groin can be anatomically intertwined with each other.

"Since Andreas Vesalius in the 16th century, anatomical dissection studies have been conducted," Mathieu said. "But as a scientist, you sometimes have to question yourself and even the anatomy books to make progress."

The significance of the discovery cannot be overstated. As the University of Antwerp noted, new anatomical discoveries are extremely rare. The research redefines the anatomical organization of the inguinal region and constitutes the foundation of a new model for understanding this complex area of the human body.

## From Clinical Observation to Anatomical Breakthrough

The path to this discovery began with a clinical observation. In 2020, Mathieu first described a tear of the inferior pubic ligament (also called the arcuate pubic ligament), a condition that causes chronic groin pain in athletes. This previously unknown injury pattern has since become informally known as the "Mathieu syndrome" in the medical world, as documented on [Wikipedia](https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathieu-syndroom).

"I did my doctorate on groin injuries," Mathieu explained. "And in that context, I identified a new diagnosis, namely a tear of the pubic bone ligament. From there, we started an important anatomical research on cadavers. Until now, the groin region, and more particularly the adductors at the pubis, had not been much studied."

The clinical significance of this discovery is substantial. MRI analyses of athletes with pubic bone pain show that approximately one in three had a tear in this ligament. "The ligament plays a crucial role in the stabilization of the pubic symphysis and is therefore vulnerable to injuries, especially during intensive sports activities," Mathieu said. "Moreover, it is a poorly vascularized ligament that does not heal spontaneously."

## The Challenge of Groin Pain

The groin region has long been considered one of the most challenging areas in sports medicine to diagnose and treat. Chronic groin pain affects thousands of athletes annually in Belgium alone, and the complex anatomy around the pubic bone makes it difficult for doctors to identify the exact source of pain.

"The ligaments around the knee and ankle have been extensively studied," Mathieu said. "But less research was done on the ligaments of the pubic bone. For doctors, treating groin pain is a serious challenge. It is sometimes called the Bermuda Triangle of sports medicine."

The discovery builds on earlier work in the field. In 2017, Belgian orthopedic surgeon Ernest Schilders defined the PLAC (Pyramidalis-anterior pubic ligament-adductor longus complex) concept, which provided a new anatomical framework for understanding adductor longus injuries, as detailed on [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyramidalis–anterior_pubic_ligament–adductor_longus_complex). Mathieu's work extends this foundation with new insights from cadaveric dissection.

## Implications for Medical Practice

The new understanding has major implications for preventive strategies and treatment protocols. Mathieu has already successfully treated several patients with targeted injections, and the research describes a classification system so that doctors worldwide can recognize the problem.

Not only athletes benefit from this discovery. Many women who have recently given birth suffer from chronic groin pain, and a tear in the inferior pubic ligament is often the culprit. The discovery offers new hope for these patients as well.

The University of Antwerp emphasized the significance of the finding: "His historical discovery can mean a lot for athletes who have been struggling with pain for years," as reported by [Ortho-Rheumato](https://www.ortho-rheumato.be/nl/nieuws/beroepsnieuws/dr-thomas-mathieu-zorgt-voor-doorbraak-in-sportgeneeskunde.html).

## International Recognition

The discovery has earned Mathieu international recognition. Prestigious international universities and journals are seeking his research, and he was the laureate of the International Sports Medicine Leadership Award from the American Medical Society for Sports Medicine. His work has been published in leading scientific journals including the Annals of Anatomy and the Orthopaedic Journal of Sports Medicine.

Born in 1991 in Turnhout, Belgium, Mathieu works at AZ Rivierenland hospital and is affiliated with the University of Antwerp as a lecturer, according to his [Wikipedia biography](https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Mathieu). His journey from clinical observation to anatomical breakthrough demonstrates the importance of questioning established knowledge.

## A Rare Moment in Science

The fact that a new anatomical discovery is being made in the 21st century is extremely rare. Since Andreas Vesalius revolutionized anatomy with his publication "De Humani Corporis Fabrica" in 1543, the field has been built on centuries of accumulated knowledge. To find that fundamental teachings about the human body may need revision is a remarkable achievement.

The discovery was also covered by [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/kijk/2026/03/05/video-behandeling-liesblessure-journaal/), the Belgian public broadcaster, which highlighted how the finding could represent a breakthrough in the treatment of groin injuries. The [De Specialist](https://www.despecialist.eu/nl/nieuws/beroepsnieuws/dr-thomas-mathieu-zorgt-voor-doorbraak-in-sportgeneeskunde.html) medical publication also reported on the breakthrough, noting that the new knowledge has significant implications for both preventive strategies and treatment protocols.

## What's Next

As the research continues to gain international attention, the implications for medical education are becoming clearer. Anatomy textbooks worldwide may need to be updated to reflect the new understanding of the groin region's anatomical organization. For medical students, doctors, and researchers, this discovery serves as a powerful reminder that even the most established scientific knowledge should be open to questioning.

For the thousands of athletes and postpartum women who suffer from chronic groin pain, the discovery offers new hope for accurate diagnosis and effective treatment. As Mathieu's research continues to be validated and expanded upon by the international medical community, the full impact of this Belgian breakthrough is only beginning to be understood.
