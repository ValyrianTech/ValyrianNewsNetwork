# 2026 Shanghai Book Fair Opens with Record Activities

The 2026 Shanghai Book Fair officially opened on August 12 at the Shanghai Exhibition Center and Shanghai Book City, marking the start of a seven-day literary celebration that runs through August 18. This year's edition, formally titled the "2026 Shanghai Book Fair and 'Bookish China' Shanghai Week," features a record 1,420 cultural activities and more than 160,000 book titles on display, according to [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/culture/20260813/c8212febd813438ba4dbcd6590a79561/c.html).

## A Dual-Venue Format Expands the Fair's Reach

For the second consecutive year, the fair operates under a "dual main venue" model, with the Shanghai Exhibition Center and Shanghai Book City serving as twin anchors. The Shanghai Exhibition Center venue hosts daytime sessions from morning through late afternoon and evening sessions until closing, while the Shanghai Book City venue offers free entry throughout the fair, as detailed by the [Shanghai government](https://www.shanghai.gov.cn/nw17239/20260806/9fc2e8e2fe894bc199832b1b020f48dd.html).

Day tickets are priced at 10 yuan and night tickets at 5 yuan, with ticket sales having begun on August 5 through the Damai and Ticket Star platforms. The fair also continues its "Reading Night Market" (阅界夜市), which has been upgraded this year with cultural creative products, intangible cultural heritage crafts, specialty food, and performances.

## Heilongjiang Takes Center Stage as Guest Province

This year marks the first time Heilongjiang Province serves as the guest province of honor, bringing a curated selection of books including "Song of the Wusuli River" and "Character Recognition: Chinese Wisdom in Characters." The fair also welcomes first-time participation from both Guangdong and Heilongjiang publishing groups, with approximately 21 publishing groups and 380 publishing institutions from across China taking part.

The fair's theme, "The Fragrance of Books Travels Far" (最是书香能致远), reflects its ambition to connect readers with literature. As [People's Daily](http://sh.people.com.cn/n2/2026/0810/c138654-41663685.html) reported, the event has evolved from a simple book marketplace into a comprehensive cultural ecosystem over its more than two decades of history.

## Beyond Books: Community Engagement and Digital Innovation

A notable innovation this year is the fair's expansion into community spaces. On opening day, popular author Ma Boyong brought his new historical novel "The Second Emperor of Qin Must Die" to a community party-masses service center in Zhuanqiao Town, where he engaged directly with local readers in a packed session. According to [Kankanews](https://www.kankanews.com/detail/boy4A3nLaQ1), this marks the first time the fair has extended author events into neighborhood-level venues, allowing readers to meet their favorite writers closer to home.

The fair has also embraced digital transformation. A new "Shanghai Book Fair Service Cloud Platform" and an AI-powered "Shanghai Book Fair Intelligent Agent" provide visitors with real-time event schedules, book searches, and navigation assistance. Partnerships with MiniMax and Zhiyuan Robotics bring AI book recommendations, video generation from book content, and robot-assisted reading solutions to attendees.

## Industry Voices: A Cultural Benchmark

Publishing industry leaders have praised the fair's unique position in China's literary landscape. "Shanghai Book Fair is the best book fair," said Chen Yiwang, Director of China Publishing Group Shanghai Office, a sentiment echoed by many publishers nationwide. "This is actually the consensus of many publishers across the country."

Liang Guangyu, Executive Director and President of Unity Press, emphasized the fair's national and international reach: "The Shanghai Book Fair's influence radiates across the country and connects domestic and international markets. Its power to 'let good books meet readers, let readers encounter good books' is its irreplaceable core charm."

## Reading as a Social Institution

The fair takes place against the backdrop of China's newly implemented "National Reading Promotion Regulations," which institutionalize reading promotion as a societal priority. Yan Feng, Chairman of Fudan University Press, noted that the regulations have transformed reading from a "personal matter" to a "social matter," adding: "The book fair is the starting point, not the endpoint."

Jiang Li, Deputy Party Secretary of Shanghai Xinhua Media Chain and head of the Shanghai Book City venue, described the fair's evolving philosophy: "'Beyond books' will continue to be our theme. The charm of reading may not just be books. In this grand event, what we experience is interaction and exchange with authors, and the joy of participating in something together with like-minded people."

## Looking Ahead

The fair runs through August 18, with the 13th Shanghai International Literary Week running alongside it. Notable international guests include British writer Jeanette Winterson, Spanish writer Sara Mesa, French writer Paul Halter, and Portuguese writer Lídia Jorge. The literary week will connect with the 9th Lu Xun Literature Prize ceremony scheduled for late August in Shanghai.

As the fair continues to evolve from a book marketplace into a cultural ecosystem, it remains a barometer for China's reading culture and a testament to Shanghai's commitment to fostering literary engagement across all segments of society.
