# Russell Westbrook Retires After 18 NBA Seasons

Russell Westbrook, the NBA's all-time leader in triple-doubles and the 2017 MVP, announced his retirement on Wednesday after 18 seasons in the league. The 37-year-old guard shared his decision in a social media video narrated by actor Michael B. Jordan, in which he never says a word, according to [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/sports/nba/russell-westbrook-retires-nba-mvp-triple-double-leader-ends-career-18-rcna592224).

"Sometimes you don't even know when you've already watched the end," Westbrook wrote. "You had to be there. And now it's over."

## A Career of Historic Achievement

Selected No. 4 overall out of UCLA by the Seattle SuperSonics in 2008, just before the franchise relocated to Oklahoma City, Westbrook spent his first 11 seasons with the Thunder. He formed a legendary trio with Kevin Durant and James Harden that reached the 2012 NBA Finals, ultimately falling to LeBron James' Miami Heat in five games, as [USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/sports/nba/2026/08/12/russell-westbrook-nba-retirement-18-seasons/90979131007/) reported.

Westbrook's signature season came in 2016-17, when he became the first player since Oscar Robertson in 1961-62 to average a triple-double for an entire season, posting 31.6 points, 10.7 rebounds, and 10.4 assists per game. That historic run earned him NBA MVP honors and set a single-season record with 42 triple-doubles.

He went on to average a triple-double in three additional seasons, finishing his career as a two-time scoring champion, three-time assists leader, and two-time NBA All-Star Game MVP. Westbrook retires with 209 career triple-doubles, the most in NBA history, along with career averages of 20.9 points, 8.0 assists, and 6.9 rebounds across 1,301 games. He was also named to the NBA's 75th Anniversary Team in 2021 and won an Olympic gold medal with Team USA at the 2012 London Games, as [FOX17 Nashville](https://fox17.com/sports/content/russell-westbrook-retires-after-historic-18-season-nba-career-kevin-durant-james-harden-okc-thunder-sacramento-kings-denver-nuggets-los-angeles-clippers-lebron-james) detailed.

## A Journey Across Seven Franchises

After his Oklahoma City tenure, Westbrook played for the Houston Rockets, Washington Wizards, Los Angeles Lakers, Los Angeles Clippers, Denver Nuggets, and Sacramento Kings, where he finished his career. In his final season, he started 58 of 64 games for the Kings, averaging 15.2 points and 6.7 assists, according to the [Chicago Sun-Times](https://chicago.suntimes.com/nba/2026/08/12/russell-westbrook-retires-after-18-seasons-in-nba).

According to Shams Charania, Westbrook received offers from two teams during 2026 free agency before deciding to retire—a return to the Kings and an offer to join a revamped Washington Wizards team led by Trae Young and Anthony Davis, as [ClutchPoints](https://clutchpoints.com/nba/nba-stories/nba-news-russell-westbrook-received-offers-from-2-teams-before-announcing-retirement) reported.

## Tributes From Around the League

The announcement drew an outpouring of tributes from across the basketball world. LeBron James, who played alongside Westbrook with the Lakers, declared on Instagram: "HELLUVA CAREER BRODIE!! HOF next!!" as [ClutchPoints](https://clutchpoints.com/nba/nba-stories/nba-news-lebron-james-wants-russell-westbrook-hall-of-fame-induction) noted. Derrick Rose, Westbrook's 2008 draft classmate, posted a heartfelt message: "Congrats on the career gang.... Tell the family I said what's up!!!" per [ClutchPoints](https://clutchpoints.com/nba/nba-stories/nba-news-derrick-rose-message-russell-westbrook-retirement).

Giannis Antetokounmpo praised Westbrook's intensity, saying, "Incredible career, brother. You made people around the world feel your presence and love for the game. There are not a lot of people who played harder than you," as [Yahoo Sports](https://sports.yahoo.com/nba/article/lebron-james-giannis-antetokounmpo-among-many-in-nba-to-pay-tribute-to-russell-westbrook-after-retirement-030358212.html) reported.

Thunder executive vice president and general manager Sam Presti also honored Westbrook's impact on the franchise: "Russell Westbrook is the ultimate competitor. His internal belief and unbridled fearlessness helped establish the foundation of our organization from day one in 2008."

## Defending a Polarizing Legacy

In the latter half of his career, Westbrook faced significant criticism from fans and media about his shooting efficiency, perceived stat-padding, and fit with various teams. Golden State Warriors star Draymond Green addressed this on his podcast, calling out NBA fans for their treatment of Westbrook during that period.

"Basketball fans suck," Green said, per [ClutchPoints](https://clutchpoints.com/nba/golden-state-warriors/warriors-news-draymond-green-nba-fans-russell-westbrooks-retirement). "Because for the last 8 to 10 years, all this guy has heard is how bad he sucks, how he doesn't shoot it well, how he stat pads, and how he messes up teams. And when you're in this game and that's all you see, that's all you hear, you power through it."

## The Triple-Double Record Race

Westbrook's triple-double record may not stand as long as the one he broke. Nikola Jokić, who ranks second all-time with 198 triple-doubles, needs just 11 more to surpass Westbrook and posted 34 last season, according to [Yahoo Sports](https://sports.yahoo.com/nba/article/russell-westbrook-retires-as-nbas-triple-double-king-but-with-2-players-in-range-to-take-his-crown-021241072.html). Luka Dončić, with 90 career triple-doubles, is also within theoretical range but would need to significantly increase his current pace.

## What's Next

Westbrook will become eligible for the Naismith Basketball Hall of Fame in 2030, and multiple players, including LeBron James, have already declared him a certain first-ballot inductee. His number is widely expected to be retired by the Thunder, the franchise he helped define for over a decade.

Westbrook leaves behind a legacy as one of the most dynamic and statistically dominant players in NBA history—a relentless competitor whose impact on the game will be felt for generations. As he wrote in his retirement post: "You had to be there. And now it's over."
