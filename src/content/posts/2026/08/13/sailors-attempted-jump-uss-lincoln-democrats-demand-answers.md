# Sailors Attempted to Jump From USS Lincoln, Democrats Demand Answers

Democratic lawmakers are pressing the Trump administration for answers about conditions aboard the USS Abraham Lincoln after reports emerged that multiple sailors attempted to jump overboard during a record-breaking deployment supporting the U.S.-Iran war. The aircraft carrier has been at sea for more than 250 days with only two port calls, setting a modern-day record for consecutive days at sea and raising serious concerns about morale and mental health among its roughly 5,000 sailors and Marines.

## A Deployment Pushed to Its Limits

The USS Abraham Lincoln departed its home port of San Diego on November 21, 2025, on what was originally scheduled as a seven-month deployment to the South China Sea. In January 2026, the carrier was redirected to the Middle East to support U.S. operations in the war with Iran, and it has been engaged in combat operations since February as part of Operation Epic Fury.

The deployment, which was anticipated to end in May, has been extended multiple times with no publicly announced return date. The carrier has made just two port calls during more than eight months at sea — one in Guam in December 2025 and another in Oman in July 2026 — far exceeding the Navy's traditional deployment norms of six to seven months with regular port visits.

## Reports of Sailors Attempting to Jump Overboard

According to [Navy Times](https://www.navytimes.com/news/your-military/2026/08/11/multiple-uss-abraham-lincoln-sailors-have-tried-to-go-overboard-amid-extended-deployment-families-say/), at least two sailors had to be prevented from jumping overboard. Annabelle Loma, the wife of one sailor who attempted to jump, confirmed her husband is now on medical hold. Another sailor on watch intervened to stop a shipmate from going overboard, according to the sailor's wife, Maria Rodriguez.

"He said he hadn't felt adrenaline like that in his life, and he was just so thankful he was able to get to [the sailor] in time, to stop them from going overboard," Rodriguez told Navy Times.

Loma described her husband's fear about the consequences of his burnout. "He's scared," she said. "He thinks he'll get a dishonorable discharge, and just because he was burnt out, his 13-year career is ruined, just like that. That's not fair, that's not right."

[Stars and Stripes](https://www.stripes.com/theaters/us/2026-08-11/abraham-lincoln-middle-east-mental-health-town-hall-meeting-22506344.html) also reported that watchstanders stopped a crew member from jumping overboard, and that the crew was informed about the incident through a shipwide announcement. A sailor went overboard from the carrier in July and was quickly rescued and medically evaluated, according to a U.S. official.

## Democrats Demand Accountability

Sen. Richard Blumenthal (D-Conn.) sent a letter on August 12 to Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth and acting Navy Secretary Hung Cao, citing "widespread reports of shortages of basic supplies, water contamination, plumbing issues, deteriorating mental health, deck safety concerns, and disruptions in the mail system." The letter gave the Navy two weeks to respond.

"The men and women aboard the Lincoln have answered the call to serve their country," Blumenthal wrote. "The Department owes them not only adequate supplies, maintenance, and support during this deployment, but a sustainable force-generation model that does not rely on repeatedly extending sailors and ships to meet persistent operational demands."

Sen. Ruben Gallego (D-Ariz.), a Marine Corps veteran who served in Iraq, [called on X](https://x.com/RubenGallego/status/2087702863023833392) for a bipartisan congressional delegation to visit the carrier. "CODELs have visited war zones to inspect the conditions of our troops," he wrote. "There is no reason to be denied access."

Rep. Seth Moulton (D-Mass.), another Marine Corps veteran, said Democrats on the House Armed Services Committee will conduct an investigation into the reports. "Zero suicides is the only acceptable number," he said.

## Pentagon Moves to Replace the Carrier

Amid the mounting concerns, the Pentagon plans to replace the USS Abraham Lincoln with the USS George Washington, according to a U.S. official. [NBC News reported](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/national-security/uss-abraham-lincoln-replaced-poor-troop-conditions-rcna592141) that military leaders are privately raising concerns inside the Pentagon and the White House about low morale and widespread fatigue among service members deployed to support the Iran war. The USS Theodore Roosevelt carrier strike group is also being prepared to relieve the Lincoln, though no timeline has been provided due to operational security.

[Stars and Stripes](https://www.stripes.com/branches/navy/2026-08-13/uss-george-washington-middle-east-abraham-lincoln-22541038.html) confirmed the George Washington is headed to the Arabian Sea to replace the Lincoln, which has been deployed for over 250 days.

## Navy and Administration Responses

The Navy has pushed back against reports of a mental health crisis aboard the ship. "We have not observed an increase in suicidal ideations or attempts aboard the ship," a Navy official said. The Navy maintains that the Lincoln is "operating in a highly contested environment where traditional supply hubs in the Middle East were disrupted by combat actions."

Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth dismissed reports of deteriorating conditions, saying the situation has been "completely misrepresented" and that "some deployments are longer than others." He insisted that "we make sure every ship, every crew, every captain has everything we can provide them at every single moment."

However, family members who attended a town hall meeting in San Diego last week with acting Navy Secretary Hung Cao described intense concerns. More than 200 family members attended the meeting, where one spouse tearfully reported that her husband had texted her saying "he hopes he doesn't wake up tomorrow."

Vice Adm. Joseph Cahill, commander of Naval Surface Forces, acknowledged the strain at the meeting. "We hear you loud and clear [regarding] the impact this has on families, on service members and the long-term ability of us to stand, sustain our forces' health," he said.

## Broader Concerns About Navy Readiness

The Lincoln's extended deployment comes amid broader concerns about the Navy's ability to sustain its operational tempo. Earlier in 2026, the USS Gerald R. Ford broke the record for the longest carrier deployment since the Vietnam War, and Navy aviation personnel are leaving the service at a high rate. A 2025 study of deployed sailors found that almost half complained they were not receiving adequate help in coping with stress at sea.

Retired Navy Capt. Armen Kurdian noted that incidents of sailors wanting to jump overboard are not unprecedented during deployments, but the circumstances of this deployment have intensified the strain. "It's not uncommon that a strike group experiences somebody wanting to jump overboard or somebody wanting to commit suicide during a deployment," he said. "So the fact that it's happening during this deployment is not surprising."

## What's Next

Blumenthal has given the Navy until August 27 to respond to his letter. Democrats on the House Armed Services Committee are preparing to launch their own investigation, and Gallego is pushing for a congressional delegation to visit the carrier. The timeline for the Lincoln's replacement remains unclear, with officials citing operational security concerns.

As the Iran war enters its sixth month with no end in sight, the questions about the Lincoln's deployment raise broader concerns about how the U.S. military can sustain prolonged combat operations without sacrificing the well-being of the service members who carry them out. For the families waiting at home, the uncertainty is the hardest part.

"I don't want to worry that the circumstances of this deployment will get the best of my son, and he doesn't make it back home," said Manuel Guevara, whose son serves aboard the Lincoln. "That's an unbearable burden on my heart."
