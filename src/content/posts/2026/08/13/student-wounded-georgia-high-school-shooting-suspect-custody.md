# Student Wounded in Georgia School Shooting; Suspect Held

A ninth-grade student was shot in the leg at Warner Robins High School in central Georgia on Wednesday afternoon, and a 14-year-old fellow student was taken into custody about an hour later, according to [AP News](https://apnews.com/article/georgia-high-school-shooting-warner-robins-5018436511ae1ceec229ff4470e49f6d). The shooting occurred just one week into the new school year, prompting a massive law enforcement response and renewed concerns about school safety.

The incident unfolded around 2:15 p.m. inside a school bathroom, where a verbal altercation between two ninth-grade students escalated into a physical fight. One student brandished a firearm and shot the other, who was transported to a local hospital with non-life-threatening injuries. A school resource officer arrived quickly and applied tourniquets to the wounded student until emergency medical services arrived.

## Rapid Response and Apprehension

The suspect fled the school but was apprehended around 3:20 p.m. in the Huntington Hills neighborhood, about half a mile from campus. Houston County Sheriff Matt Moulton credited the school's training and emergency protocols for minimizing the incident's severity.

"Today a call that we train for every year happened to come to Houston County," Moulton said at an evening press conference. "The school threat protocol and our training was followed perfectly today. Fortunately what could have been a much-worse incident was minimized to us only having one student injured."

The response involved multiple agencies, including the Houston County Sheriff's Office, Warner Robins Police Department, Georgia State Patrol, Georgia Bureau of Investigation, and local fire departments. A Georgia State Patrol helicopter landed on campus during the operation, and nearby roads were temporarily closed.

## School Lockdown and Closure

Warner Robins High School was placed under Code Red, with students and staff secured in their classrooms. A controlled dismissal began around 4 p.m., with buses departing first, followed by car riders, then student drivers and walkers. According to [WGXA](https://wgxa.tv/news/local/developing-heavy-police-presence-at-warner-robins-high-school-as-police-respond-threat-law-enforcement-incident-school-district-campus-presence-officers), the school had conducted safety drills using its Centegix CrisisAlert System the day before the shooting.

Principal Brett Wallace announced that the school would be closed for students on Thursday and Friday, with teachers returning Friday and students returning Monday. Crisis counselors will be available for staff and students.

"Today was a hard day for our school and the entire community," Wallace said in a letter to parents. "We are grateful that the student who was injured is receiving care, and we are incredibly thankful for the quick response of law enforcement and first responders."

Houston County Schools Superintendent Richard Rogers echoed that sentiment, saying, "We're going to focus on healing."

## Community and Political Response

The shooting drew swift reactions from state and local leaders. [FOX 5 Atlanta](https://www.fox35orlando.com/news/shooting-reported-warner-robins-high-school-authorities-say) reported that Gov. Brian Kemp expressed gratitude for first responders, while U.S. Sen. Raphael Warnock offered prayers for the community. Warner Robins Mayor LaRhona W. Patrick praised the rapid response of public safety officers. According to [WGXA](https://wgxa.tv/news/local/wednesday-shooting-at-warner-robins-high-school-sparks-statements-from-local-leaders-mayor-larhonda-patrick-govenor-brian-kemp-senator-reverand-raphael-warnock-principal-brett-wallace-press-conference-sheriff-matt-moulton-houston-county-law-enforcement-i), former Atlanta Mayor Keisha Lance Bottoms expressed heartbreak over the incident.

Parents described the fear and confusion of the moment. "All I got was it's a code red, the school shut down," parent Crystal Weldon told reporters. "It was just heart throbbing to me, and I was scared because like, what if that was my child?"

## Broader Context: School Safety in Georgia

The shooting comes amid heightened scrutiny of school safety in Georgia, which experienced the deadliest school shooting in its history in September 2024. At Apalachee High School near Winder, 14-year-old Colt Gray killed four people and wounded seven others. In July 2026, Gray was [sentenced to life without parole](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/us-news/apalachee-high-school-shooter-colt-gray-sentenced-life-parole-rcna589406).

Houston County schools have also faced a series of security incidents in recent months. Between April and May 2026, at least six schools were placed under lockdowns for reported bomb and gun threats, most of which were determined to be hoaxes, according to the [Macon Telegraph](https://www.yahoo.com/news/articles/recent-threats-houston-county-schools-165438821.html). The district has reported an increase in lockdown-related responses over the past two years.

## What's Next

The 14-year-old suspect is expected to appear before a Superior Court judge, though details of potential charges have not been publicly released. Authorities have not disclosed how the suspect obtained the firearm. The investigation remains active, and the school community will begin the process of healing as students return to campus on Monday.
