# China's Anti-Graft Body Reshuffles in Mid-Year Plenum

The Communist Party of China's top disciplinary body convened an unprecedented mid-year plenary session in Beijing on August 22, electing two new deputy secretaries and signaling a firm commitment to enforcing discipline during upcoming leadership transitions. The sixth plenary session of the 20th Central Commission for Discipline Inspection (CCDI) was attended by 116 commission members — the lowest attendance of any plenary session since the 18th CPC National Congress in 2012, according to the [official communiqué](https://www.news.cn/politics/20260822/66896bd92e254592af1dc67477d5711b/c.html) released by Xinhua News Agency.

Chaired by the CCDI Standing Committee, the session was addressed by Li Xi, a member of the Standing Committee of the Political Bureau of the CPC Central Committee and CCDI Secretary. The meeting reviewed discipline inspection and supervision work from the first half of 2026 and set requirements for the remainder of the year, with particular emphasis on political supervision, strict enforcement of leadership transition discipline, and continued grassroots anti-corruption efforts.

## A Departure from Convention

This marks the first time since 2012 that the CCDI has convened a plenary session in mid-year, breaking from the traditional January schedule. [China Daily](https://usa.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202608/22/WS6a895a52a3106bc57421cea4.html) reported that the session's timing reflects the urgency of addressing personnel changes and disciplinary priorities mid-cycle. The unusual scheduling serves as a pivotal checkpoint for execution and personnel recalibration, according to analysts at [China Daily Brief](https://chinadailybrief.com/publications/ccdi-sixth-plenum-2026-leadership-appointments-anti-corruption-aqtv), an independent analysis publication.

Attendance has declined steadily across the 20th CCDI term, from 132 members at the first plenary session in October 2022 to 116 at this session. The CCDI currently lists 133 members, with one deceased. The drop of four members from the January 2026 plenary session reflects ongoing personnel changes within the commission.

## New Leadership Appointments

In accordance with the Party Constitution, the plenary session elected Zhao Shiyong as Deputy Secretary of the CCDI and Zhang Zhong as a member of the CCDI Standing Committee and Deputy Secretary. This marks the first adjustment of CCDI deputy secretaries since the 20th CPC National Congress in 2022.

Zhao Shiyong, 60, previously served as a CCDI Standing Committee member, National Supervisory Commission member, and Minister of the CCDI Organization Department. His career includes roles as Vice Governor of Jiangsu Province and senior positions within the Jiangsu Provincial Party Committee. Zhang Zhong, 58, previously served as Jiangsu Provincial Discipline Inspection Commission Secretary and earlier held positions in Jilin Province and with the Central Inspection Teams. Both appointees share Jiangsu work backgrounds, a detail noted by [Caixin](https://china.caixin.com/2026-08-23/102477079.html) as significant, and both have been promoted to full ministerial level.

[Lianhe Zaobao](https://www.zaochenbao.com/news/Chronicles/202608/2279367.html) reported that the new appointments fill vacancies created by the early retirement of former CCDI Deputy Secretary Xiao Pei, who was reassigned to a second-line position at the CPPCC National Committee in June 2026.

## Military Dimension and Declining Attendance

The plenary session highlighted notable developments within the military's representation on the CCDI. Thirteen military members were absent, including Vice Chairman of the Central Military Commission and CCDI Deputy Secretary Zhang Shengmin. Only nine military members attended the session, down from 22 at the start of the 20th CCDI term. [Epoch Times](https://www.epochtimes.com/gb/26/8/22/n14834759.htm) reported that the new Military Commission Discipline Inspection Commission Secretary, Zhang Shuguang, was not elected as CCDI Deputy Secretary at this session, breaking with the established convention that the military discipline inspection secretary concurrently serves as CCDI Deputy Secretary.

## Key Priorities and Policy Directions

The communiqué emphasized the study and implementation of Xi Jinping's Thought on Party Building, described as the "party-building chapter" of Xi Jinping Thought on Socialism with Chinese Characteristics for a New Era. The session called for strengthening political supervision, deepening political inspections, and strictly enforcing discipline in leadership transitions — a signal that the leadership is determined to maintain political order during upcoming personnel reshuffles.

According to [CGTN](https://news.cgtn.com/news/2026-08-22/China-s-top-anti-graft-body-vows-higher-standards-to-advance-its-work-1PP23c9gutO/p.html), the session also called for unswervingly improving conduct, enforcing discipline, and combating corruption, while continuing the concentrated rectification of misconduct and corruption at the grassroots level. The emphasis on grassroots corruption aligns with broader efforts to address issues directly affecting public services and daily life.

The [State Council of China](https://www.gov.cn/yaowen/liebiao/202608/content_7078902.htm) published the full text of the communiqué, which also stressed the importance of building a disciplined inspection team that is "loyal, clean, and responsible."

## Analysis and Implications

The mid-year plenum provides a snapshot of Beijing's disciplinary priorities at a critical juncture. Analysts at China Daily Brief note that by explicitly highlighting the enforcement of discipline during leadership transitions, the leadership signals "zero tolerance for political lobbying, factionalism, or misconduct" as regional and institutional rotations progress.

The personnel changes come amid a broader recalibration within China's anti-corruption apparatus. Zhou Liang, a former CCDI Deputy Secretary-General, was investigated in March 2026 while serving at the National Financial Regulatory Administration, and Li Xiaohong, former Director of the CPC Central Inspection Leading Group Office, was announced under investigation in June. Both were associated with former CCDI Secretary Wang Qishan, as was Xiao Pei, whose early retirement has been noted by observers.

The [People's Daily](http://paper.people.com.cn/rmrb/pc/content/202608/23/content_30176528.html) published the communiqué on its front page, underscoring the significance of the session in the party's official narrative of continued commitment to anti-corruption and discipline.

## What to Watch

As the CCDI moves forward with its new leadership team, several questions remain. The specific oversight portfolios assigned to Zhao Shiyong and Zhang Zhong will be closely watched. Whether the new military discipline inspection secretary Zhang Shuguang will eventually be elected as CCDI Deputy Secretary, or whether the convention has been permanently changed, remains an open question. The significance of the Jiangsu connection shared by both new deputy secretaries, and the potential for further personnel changes at the CCDI in the lead-up to the 21st CPC National Congress, will also merit attention.

The emphasis on "strictly enforcing discipline in leadership transitions" suggests that upcoming regional and institutional reshuffles will be conducted under heightened scrutiny, with the CCDI positioned as the guardian of political order during a period of significant personnel change within China's governing structures.
