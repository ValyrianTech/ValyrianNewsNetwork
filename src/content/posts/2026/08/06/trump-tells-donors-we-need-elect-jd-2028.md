# Trump Tells Donors 'We Need to Elect JD' in 2028 Signal

President Donald Trump privately told donors during an Oval Office meeting roughly two weeks ago that he wants Vice President JD Vance to win the 2028 presidential election, according to a source who confirmed the remarks to [Fox News](https://www.foxnews.com/politics/trump-told-donors-we-need-elect-jd-source-confirms-weighs-vance-rubio-2028). The comment — "At the end of the day, we need to elect JD" — offers the clearest signal yet of Trump's private thinking on the Republican succession contest, even as he continues to publicly weigh Vance against Secretary of State Marco Rubio.

The remark, first reported by The Washington Post and independently confirmed by Fox News Digital, marks a rare departure from more than a year of carefully maintained public ambiguity. Trump has repeatedly praised both men, floated a joint Vance-Rubio ticket as "unstoppable," and declined to specify who should lead it. Advisers caution that the private statement does not constitute a formal endorsement, and several told the Post they are not convinced Trump has made up his mind.

## The Succession Contest Takes Shape

The 2028 Republican presidential nomination race is shaping up to be one of the most consequential in modern American politics, determining who inherits the MAGA movement after Trump's constitutionally limited second term. While Vance remains the clear hypothetical frontrunner, Rubio has narrowed the gap in national polls and surveys conducted in key early voting states.

At the Conservative Political Action Conference in March, Vance won the 2028 Republican presidential nomination straw poll with 53% support, while Rubio surged to second place with 35% — up from just 3% a year earlier. A Big Data Poll conducted in late June showed Vance leading with 35.4% support versus Rubio's 16.5%, and a Verasight national survey from April found nearly four in ten Republicans preferred Vance.

Despite the polling gap, Trump has continued to signal both men remain in play. According to the Post, Trump questioned White House staff about the two potential contenders during a Marine One flight in late July — even after his private remark to donors. One adviser told the newspaper that Trump has privately resolved that Vance will be the GOP heir, but six other advisers and allies said they were not convinced the president had made up his mind.

## Vance's Structural Advantage

Vance enters the succession contest with significant structural advantages. As the Republican National Committee's finance chair — the first sitting vice president to hold the role — he has direct access to more than $128 million in party funds, according to the June Federal Election Commission filing. RNC Chair Joe Gruters praised Vance as "a fundraising powerhouse for the RNC, supercharging our war chest as we head into the midterms."

Vance's fourth child, a boy named Alec Neel Vance, was born on July 19 — the first child born to a sitting vice president in more than 150 years. People close to Vance said he intended to wait until after the birth before seriously considering a campaign, and that personal milestone has now passed.

At a Las Vegas donor event on Wednesday night, Trump made just one reference to Vance, hailing "the war on fraud led by our great Vice President JD Vance: He's doing a great job." The studied restraint in that public setting, against the backdrop of his private Oval Office remarks, captures the gap between Trump's public theater and his private leanings.

## Rubio Remains a Credible Alternative

Rubio, who ran against Trump in the 2016 Republican primary, has served as Secretary of State and former acting National Security Adviser — making him the first person since Henry Kissinger to hold both positions simultaneously. Trump has lavishly praised Rubio for his work in peacemaking efforts in Venezuela, Cuba, and the Middle East, declaring earlier this year that he would go down as "the greatest secretary of state in history."

Despite his rising poll numbers, Rubio has repeatedly said he would back Vance if the vice president enters the race. "If JD Vance runs for president, he's going to be our nominee, and I'll be one of the first people to support him," Rubio told Vanity Fair late last year. He does not have a comparable political operation ready, and Republican sources confirmed to Fox News that a group of GOP donors supporting Rubio is quietly exploring ways to boost his political profile — a development that has not sat well with some in Trump's political orbit.

## Trump's Deliberate Ambiguity

Political analysts note that Trump's public ambiguity serves a specific management purpose: keeping both potential heirs loyal and attentive while preserving his own influence during the remainder of his presidency. His aides broadly expect him to delay any formal endorsement, in part to avoid diminishing his own leverage.

The dynamic resembles Trump's 2024 search for a running mate, when Vance and Rubio were among the finalists. According to reporting from Maggie Haberman and Jonathan Swan's book "Regime Change," Trump asked Rupert Murdoch to compare the two men at a dinner, and held an informal applause vote for them at a Rose Garden dinner in May.

Vance, for his part, has dismissed suggestions of a rivalry with Rubio. "I think it's so interesting the media wants to create this conflict where there just isn't any conflict," he told Fox News' Martha MacCallum. "Marco is my closest friend in the administration."

## The Midterms as a Pivot Point

The upcoming midterm elections could dramatically reshape the succession contest. Republican losses could encourage additional candidates — including Florida Gov. Ron DeSantis, Sen. Ted Cruz, or Donald Trump Jr. — to enter the race and make it easier to criticize the administration's record. A strong performance, by contrast, could strengthen Trump's ability to shape the 2028 field.

As the [Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/international/global-trends/trump-considers-jd-vance-as-2028-presidential-candidate-as-he-weighs-successor-choices/articleshow/133007989.cms) reported, one adviser with direct knowledge of Trump's comment to donors said the president has privately resolved that Vance will be the heir to the GOP, but that it is "too early" to determine when Vance will announce his candidacy or when Trump will publicly endorse him. Another adviser cautioned: "Anyone assuming it's a lock is just getting way ahead of themselves, or just doesn't know the president very well."

## What to Watch For

Trump's private remark to donors represents the most explicit signal yet of his thinking on the 2028 succession, even though it falls short of a formal endorsement. The leak — confirmed by two independent sources — reveals a gap between Trump's public theater of ambiguity and his private leanings, consistent with his management style of maintaining suspense to keep both potential heirs loyal and attentive.

For now, Vance holds a significant structural advantage as RNC finance chair with deep donor relationships and Trump's apparent private preference. But Rubio's rising poll numbers, his foreign policy portfolio, and the unpredictable nature of the 2026 midterms keep the contest far from settled. Whether Trump makes a formal endorsement before the 2028 primaries begin — and how the midterm results reshape the field — will be the key questions in the months ahead.

As [TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/323352/20260806/trump-privately-endorsed-vance-2028-while-keeping-public-ambiguity-intact.htm) noted, a private lean is not a public commitment, and Trump has reversed himself before. But the signal to the donor class has been sent: the Oval Office furniture is beginning to point in one direction, even while the man at the desk keeps asking everyone around him which way they think it should face.
