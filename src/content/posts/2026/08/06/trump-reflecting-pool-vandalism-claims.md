# Trump Keeps Pushing False Reflecting Pool Vandalism Claims

President Donald Trump continues to insist that the Lincoln Memorial Reflecting Pool was vandalized, despite overwhelming evidence — including admissions from his own U.S. Attorney — that the damage was caused by a botched contractor installation. The controversy has exposed rare tensions within the Trump administration, as U.S. Attorney Jeanine Pirro, a longtime Trump ally, moved to dismiss charges against former Olympic canoeist David Hearn after court filings revealed the damage was due to shoddy workmanship, not vandalism.

## The Renovation and Its Failures

In April, Trump announced plans to renovate the historic Reflecting Pool, directing that the bottom be painted with what he called "American flag blue." Virginia-based Atlantic Industrial Coatings was awarded a $14.7 million no-bid contract to repaint and waterproof the pool's concrete floor, according to [AP News](https://apnews.com/article/trump-pirro-reflecting-pool-3703eaedddf580f32ff9aca33bc11851).

Within days of the project's completion in early June, problems emerged. A thick layer of green algae appeared on the pool's surface, and the blue coating appeared cracked, with sheets of paint flapping in the water, as [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/1/trump-administration-to-dismiss-reflecting-pool-charges-against-david-hearn) reported.

Trump blamed vandals, claiming they cut a "300-foot slit" in the pool's new liner with a knife or box cutter, and later claimed "thugs" cut a gash "300 yards long." He also claimed vandals threw fertilizer into the water.

## The Case Against David Hearn

On June 19, David Hearn, a 67-year-old former Olympic canoeist from Bethesda, Maryland, was detained by National Guard troops and U.S. Park Police for five hours after stopping by the pool during a bike ride. He said he reached in to examine the pool's newly peeled coating and briefly touched a chunk attached to the side of the pool, but obeyed a park worker who told him to let go.

On July 2, Pirro announced a grand jury indictment against Hearn on a single felony count of property destruction, carrying a maximum sentence of 10 years in prison. Pirro said Hearn "forcefully and violently" pulled up the bottom liner "with both hands," as [AP News](https://apnews.com/article/reflecting-pool-arrest-felony-trump-renovations-vandalism-d946ccf6bfc5207d4c5380b9001b7c26) reported.

## The About-Face

On July 31, Pirro's office moved to dismiss the charges against Hearn, admitting in a 20-page court filing that newly produced evidence from the Interior Department showed the damage was caused by a "botched installation" by the contractor and "the rush to complete the project prior to events associated with the America 250 celebration."

"Given all of this newly discovered information, it is difficult to attribute the widespread damage to the Reflecting Pool to vandalism, let alone to establish that fact beyond a reasonable doubt," Pirro wrote in the filing, according to [AP News](https://apnews.com/article/reflecting-pool-damage-olympian-hearn-trump-21d997b5b55e6adf01862d7c5292476d).

A National Park Service engineer had written an email on June 11 stating that the cause of the damage was contractor error via "overspray." A July 20 inspection confirmed "overspray and delamination had occurred, again pointing to contractor error," as [FactCheck.org](https://www.factcheck.org/2026/08/trumps-continued-claims-of-vandalism-at-reflecting-pool-disputed-by-u-s-attorney-memo/) documented.

Pirro blamed the Interior Department for providing "less than fulsome information at the outset of this case," and noted that had the DOI "been forthcoming with the information clearly in its possession, the government would not have sought a grand jury indictment."

## Trump's Public Rebuke

Trump responded with fury. On August 1, he posted on Truth Social: "I disagree 100% with Jeanine Pirro." He later said she "choked" and "folded like an umbrella," and insisted "everybody saw the vandalism," according to [AP News](https://apnews.com/article/trump-reflecting-pool-pirro-vandalism-criticism-c7aabcf59e87651dd7be0b322b5460b1).

"Well I just think she was totally wrong," Trump told reporters Tuesday night. "And everybody saw the vandalism."

Charges were also dropped against three other people charged with misdemeanors for allegedly removing pieces of paint from the Reflecting Pool — Justin Carreno, Cameron Thiers, and Sophie Dennison-Gibby.

Hearn's lawyers filed a 28-page motion urging a judge to permanently dismiss the case and foreclose the possibility of it being revived. They argued the Justice Department "knew or should have known" the case was flawed and that the government had "disregarded readily available evidence," as [AP News](https://apnews.com/article/reflecting-pool-charges-david-hearn-trump-pirro-3196c7e3d914ad9ef05562d586a8bff1) reported.

"From day one, the case against Mr. Hearn was motivated by political considerations and devoid of adequate factual support," defense lawyers wrote. "Those political pressures have not dissipated in the mere four weeks since the government saw fit to obtain the indictment."

## Broader Political Implications

The Reflecting Pool controversy comes as Trump faces low approval ratings, a war with Iran, and criticism over the economy ahead of pivotal midterm elections. The episode has highlighted the challenges his team faces in delivering hard truths he does not want to hear.

"He just blows it up like a keg of dynamite — it's a form of intimidating employees to be in group-think," said Rice University professor Douglas Brinkley, a presidential historian. "'If you work for me, you triple down.' Never disagree with the boss, even in a small fractional way."

By publicly rebuking Pirro, Trump sent a message to other administration officials that he is not to be contradicted, regardless of what the facts show. The administration had essentially declared in court that the president was wrong — a development that is difficult to dismiss as partisan opposition.

"It's difficult to discredit that by saying these are anti-Trump forces," said Kathleen Hall Jamieson, a professor of communication at the University of Pennsylvania.

## What's Next

Hearn's lawyers are urging a judge to permanently dismiss the case, arguing that the government's approach was "ready, fire, aim" and that "the administration owes Mr. Hearn an apology." Meanwhile, Interior Secretary Doug Burgum has continued to support Trump's claims that vandals damaged the pool, suggesting the conflict could continue to simmer.

Trump has said the Reflecting Pool would be "fixed" and reopened within the next two weeks, though the White House has not provided details on the timeline or additional costs. The episode underscores the broader tensions within an administration navigating between loyalty to the president and fidelity to the facts.
