# UNESCO Names Beijing 2029 World Capital of Architecture

UNESCO has designated Beijing as the 2029 UNESCO-UIA (International Union of Architects) World Capital of Architecture, announced the organization on August 5. The prestigious recognition, made upon the recommendation of the Joint Committee for the World Capital of Architecture chaired by distinguished architect Dominique Perrault, places the Chinese capital at the center of global architectural discourse for the coming years.

## A Recognition of Heritage and Innovation

According to [UNESCO](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/heritage-and-innovation-make-beijing-world-capital-architecture-2029), Beijing was selected for its "exceptional ability to bring together one of the world's richest architectural heritages with a forward-looking vision of sustainable urban development." The organization noted that the city's "historic urban fabric, successful regeneration projects and vibrant architectural culture demonstrate how heritage, innovation and community life can reinforce one another in shaping resilient and livable cities."

The World Capital of Architecture program was established jointly by UNESCO and the UIA to highlight the transformative role of architecture, urban planning, culture and heritage in advancing sustainable urban development. Every three years, UNESCO designates the host city of the UIA World Congress as the UNESCO-UIA World Capital of Architecture, as [Xinhua News](https://english.news.cn/20260805/8dc1cbd31d6b4976b1289e9bb657c3a8/c.html) reported.

## Beijing's Architectural Legacy

Beijing is one of the world's oldest capitals, with an architectural heritage spanning more than 3,000 years. The city is home to numerous UNESCO World Heritage Sites, including the Forbidden City, the Temple of Heaven, the Summer Palace, and the Great Wall of China. In 2024, Beijing's Central Axis was inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site, recognizing the city's unique urban planning tradition.

The city's architectural landscape represents a remarkable blend of ancient imperial architecture, traditional courtyard housing, and contemporary landmarks such as the CCTV Headquarters, the National Stadium (Bird's Nest), and the National Centre for the Performing Arts. Beijing has also undertaken significant urban regeneration projects, including the revitalization of historic neighborhoods and the transformation of industrial sites into cultural spaces like the 798 Art District.

## Theme: "Back to Balance"

Under the theme "Back to Balance," Beijing 2029 will promote architecture as a bridge between culture, nature and people. Through exhibitions, conferences, educational programs and public events, the city will foster international dialogue on how the built environment can contribute to more inclusive, sustainable and human-centered urban futures, according to UNESCO.

The 30th UIA World Congress of Architects is scheduled to take place in Beijing from June 18-21, 2029. Beijing last hosted the UIA World Congress in 1999 under the theme "Architecture of the 21st Century," which witnessed the adoption of the UIA Beijing Charter, as noted by [Arkitera](https://www.arkitera.com/etkinlik/uia-2029-beijing/).

## International Recognition

UNESCO Director-General Khaled El-Enany expressed confidence that Beijing's "extraordinary architectural heritage, dynamic urban transformation, and forward-looking vision will inspire new international dialogue on the power of architecture in improving people's lives and in safeguarding shared heritage for future generations."

Beijing joins a distinguished list of previous World Capitals of Architecture, including Rio de Janeiro (2020), Copenhagen (2023), and Barcelona (2026). The designation follows the recent conclusion of the UIA World Congress of Architects 2026 in Barcelona, the current World Capital of Architecture.

## Expert Perspectives

Chinese architect Ma Yansong told the [Global Times](https://www.globaltimes.cn/page/202608/1367640.shtml) that Beijing is a beautiful city because it seamlessly blends tradition with modernity. "Historic and contemporary architecture coexist in a very harmonious way, which is one of the reasons UNESCO selected Beijing," he said.

Describing the Temple of Heaven as the city's most beautiful landmark, Ma noted that "the proportions and composition are so perfectly balanced that they remind me of a symphony." He emphasized that "landmarks ultimately express a city's unique cultural identity," and in an era of globalization, preserving what makes each place unique has become increasingly important.

## Looking Ahead

The designation will bring significant international attention to Beijing's architectural landscape over the next three years. The city will host a year-long program of cultural, educational and professional activities alongside the 30th UIA World Congress of Architects in June 2029, providing opportunities for international collaboration, tourism promotion, and cultural exchange.

As confirmed by the [Beijing government](https://english.beijing.gov.cn/latest/news/202608/t20260806_4811093.html), this recognition signals UNESCO's confidence in the city's ability to contribute meaningfully to global architectural discourse. The theme "Back to Balance" reflects broader global concerns about sustainable development, heritage conservation, and human-centered urban design, positioning Beijing at the forefront of these conversations in the years ahead.
