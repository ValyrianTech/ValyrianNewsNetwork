# Belgium's Last Summer of Terrace Smoking: Mixed Reactions

As Belgium prepares to ban smoking and vaping on café terraces from January 1, 2027, café owners and customers in Namur are experiencing what could be their final summer with cigarettes on outdoor seating areas. The measure, approved by the Council of Ministers in September 2025, marks a significant shift for the hospitality industry and has sparked deeply divided opinions across the Walloon city.

The ban, announced by Federal Health Minister Frank Vandenbroucke, will prohibit smoking and vaping on café and restaurant terraces, as well as within a 10-meter radius around them. According to [RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/cigarette-en-terrasse-comment-les-cafetiers-et-clients-namurois-vivent-ce-dernier-ete-avant-l-interdiction-11761812), the measure is part of Belgium's broader goal of achieving a "smoke-free generation" by 2040.

## A Decade of Progressive Restrictions

The terrace ban is the latest step in Belgium's comprehensive anti-tobacco strategy. Smoking was banned inside cafés and restaurants in 2009 and 2011, followed by restrictions on vending machine sales in 2024 and an expansion to outdoor public spaces in January 2025. The April 2025 "Display Ban" further prohibited points of sale from prominently showcasing tobacco products. As [RTBF reported](https://www.rtbf.be/article/le-tabac-c-est-tabou-la-belgique-se-dote-d-un-nouveau-plan-anti-tabac-11116250), the federal government has been progressively tightening restrictions since 2022, when Vandenbroucke first outlined the "smoke-free generation" ambition.

The health rationale is stark: tobacco kills nearly two people every hour in Belgium, accounting for approximately 8% of all annual deaths. Passive smoking is responsible for 20-30% of lung cancers in the country, according to the [Horeca Focus Group](https://horecafocusgroup.be/fr/interdiction-de-fumer-sur-les-terrasses-a-partir-du-1er-janvier-2027/), which published a detailed analysis of the new measures.

## Divided Voices on Namur's Terraces

On the terraces of Namur, opinions are sharply split. Some customers welcome the ban as a relief from secondhand smoke, particularly those who have long complained about sharing their tables with a neighbor's cigarette smoke or watching children involuntarily inhale it. Others, like Hicham, a regular café customer, say the cigarette is an integral part of the terrace experience and that he will no longer drink coffee outdoors once the measure takes effect.

Café manager Adnan considers the measure disproportionate, arguing that citizens are capable of coexisting without a general smoking ban. "If smoking is such a scourge, the State's decision would simply be to ban it," he told RTBF. Damien Baert, a member of the Namur merchants' association, echoed this sentiment and proposed an alternative: letting café owners decide for themselves whether their establishment is smoking or non-smoking.

## Horeca Sector Raises Concerns

The hospitality sector has voiced significant apprehension about the ban's practical implications. Nathalie Laurent, president of Fédération Horeca Wallonie, told [DH/Les Sports+](https://www.dhnet.be/actu/belgique/2026/07/16/dernier-ete-ou-on-peut-fumer-en-terrasse-cest-la-disparition-de-milliers-de-cafes-LTUNSLFX3FEC3E6AJDKE2HQBJ4/) that "we're preparing rather poorly" and warned that the measure "will only displace the problem. The person who smokes will always smoke, just a bit further away."

Erik Beunckens, president of FedCaf, the federation of cafés, warns the ban could accelerate the decline of Belgium's café culture. The number of cafés has already dropped from about 20,000 fifteen years ago to fewer than 10,000 today, and Beunckens fears it could fall further to 5,000-6,000. "Café owners are not police officers," he said, referring to the requirement that establishments intervene with customers who light up.

## Health Advocates Support the Measure

Public health experts firmly back the ban. Nora Mélard, prevention expert at the Fondation contre le Cancer, told [DH/Les Sports+](https://www.dhnet.be/actu/sante/2026/07/16/fin-de-la-cigarette-en-terrasse-interdire-sans-mesure-pour-arreter-une-addiction-cest-rarement-efficace-SPFTPJ3IXVA5ZNXRK4LF5NGDXU/) that the foundation "firmly supports all the different measures aimed at reducing the accessibility and visibility of tobacco products." The goal, she explained, is to make it increasingly difficult to start smoking, "because it's much easier to discourage someone from starting than to help them quit."

## Implementation and Enforcement

Café owners will be required to display clear signage and intervene with customers who light up, under penalty of sanctions ranging from 208 to 8,000 euros. The smoker remains ultimately responsible. Temporary exceptions remain possible for certain events such as kermesses (village fairs) and Christmas markets. The ban also covers e-cigarettes and vaping, placing them on the same level as traditional tobacco. Smoking rooms, hookah bars, and cigar clubs will also be eliminated, making Belgium the second European country after Sweden to impose a total smoking ban in the hospitality sector.

The measure was initially planned for January 2026 but was delayed by one year to give establishments time to prepare. As [Le Dialogue Belge](https://ledialoguebelge.com/fumer-en-terrasse-cest-bientot-fini-ce-que-prevoit-vraiment-la-loi-de-2027/) notes, the 2011 indoor smoking ban precedent suggests that while there may be short-term revenue impacts, the feared wave of bankruptcies did not materialize.

## What to Watch For

As the January 2027 deadline approaches, the key questions remain: How strictly will the ban be enforced? Will the Horeca sector's worst fears materialize, or will the transition prove smoother than anticipated? And most importantly for Namur's café owners and their customers, will the terrace experience survive without the familiar haze of cigarette smoke? A non-scientific poll of approximately 400 readers by La DH found that 63% support the ban, suggesting that for many Belgians, the change may be welcome.

For now, the summer of 2026 continues — glasses clinking, conversations flowing, and, for a few more months, cigarettes burning on Namur's terraces.
