# Foreign Tourists Fuel 'Reverse Cross-Border Shopping' Boom in China

The "reverse cross-border shopping" trend is surging as foreign tourists increasingly choose to shop directly in Chinese markets, transforming China from a manufacturing hub into a global consumption destination. From cutting-edge electronics to traditional crafts, international visitors are discovering the appeal of buying goods at the source, according to [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/world/20260805/326f2b9939254bb28ea2a1a5bfad45d1/c.html).

## A New Consumer Wave

On overseas social media, hashtags like "bringing empty suitcases to China," "must-buy lists," and "departure tax refund guides" have become trending topics. In cities across China, foreign tourists are turning their trips into shopping expeditions, hauling luggage through commercial districts and stores while checking off items from carefully curated shopping lists.

The numbers tell a compelling story. In 2025, nationwide departure tax refund sales grew nearly 100% year-on-year, with one year's sales volume roughly equivalent to the total of the previous decade combined. The number of people processing tax refunds reached 270,000, a three-fold increase from 2024. The momentum has only accelerated in 2026: during the first half of the year, the number of overseas travelers processing departure tax refunds grew 366% year-on-year, while tax refund merchandise sales and refund amounts grew 69%.

## From Sightseeing to Shopping

The shopping list of foreign tourists has evolved dramatically. While tea, silk, and panda plush toys were once the standard purchases, today's visitors are gravitating toward foldable smartphones, AI glasses, drones, and smart wearables. At Beijing's Wangfujing pedestrian street, an Italian tourist named Luca purchased four Chinese-brand smart wristbands in one visit. At a Huawei flagship store in Shanghai, Spanish tourist Bernat marveled at new energy vehicles, exclaiming, "From a technological standpoint, this car is incredibly cool!"

Chinese cultural brands are equally drawing crowds. Brazilian tourist Chris visited Shanghai stores looking for designer bags from local brands, while Australian tourist Samantha made a special trip to explore trendy toy stores. Intangible cultural heritage products, blind box collectibles, and Chinese-style luggage have all become favorites among international shoppers.

Foreign tourists' consumption patterns are also shifting fundamentally. They are moving from "random buying" to "strategy shopping" — researching products on social media beforehand, then making targeted purchases and bulk orders for friends and family. They are also transitioning from "sightseers" to "experiencers," trying hanfu photography, traditional Chinese medicine treatments, and new energy vehicle test drives.

## Policy Open Doors, Services Win Hearts

The "reverse cross-border shopping" boom is not accidental. It is underpinned by China's high-level opening-up and a comprehensive upgrade of policies and services.

China's visa-free "circle of friends" has expanded significantly. The country has implemented unilateral visa-free policies for 50 countries and 240-hour transit visa-free policies for 55 countries. In 2025, 30.08 million foreigners entered China visa-free, accounting for 73.1% of all foreign entrants. In the first half of 2026, that number reached 17.815 million, representing 77.7% of all foreign entrants — a 30.6% year-on-year increase.

Total foreign arrivals have surged accordingly. In the first half of 2026, foreign arrivals to China reached 22.914 million, a 20.4% year-on-year increase, as [China Daily via Shaanxi Daily](https://www.sxdaily.com.cn/2026-08/03/content_20070286.html) reported. In 2025, total foreign visitors reached 35.17 million, up 30.5% from 2024, with foreign tourist spending up 39.2%.

The departure tax refund system has also been dramatically streamlined. From the 1.0 policy introduced in April 2025 to the 2.0 policy that took effect on July 1, 2026, key improvements include a lower refund threshold (from 500 yuan to 200 yuan), random sampling instead of item-by-item inspection for refunds under 10,000 yuan, paperless processing, cross-regional mutual recognition for "buy now, refund immediately" services, and an extended departure period of 28 days. The number of departure tax refund stores nationwide has reached 14,000, approximately four times the number at the end of 2024, as [CCTV](https://news.cctv.com/2026/05/19/ARTIeE9pSBuT6zKkWLpUXZCQ260519.shtml) reported.

## Manufacturing Power Meets Global Demand

The appeal of "shopping in China" is rooted in the country's manufacturing strength. According to the World Intellectual Property Organization, China has become the world's largest holder of AI patents, accounting for 60% of the global total. From drones to AI glasses, from foldable phones to smart wearables, China's robust industrial chain and innovation capacity have shifted its products from "price-driven" to "value-driven," making them sought-after items for global consumers.

At Shenzhen's Huaqiangbei — known as China's "First Electronics Street" — the influx has been remarkable. During summer 2025, the district received an average of over 7,000 foreign visitors per day, according to [Phoenix News](https://news.ifeng.com/c/8uX8u9hfdL7). One Egyptian tourist's shopping bags were filled to the brim, while a European visitor rushed between stalls with a suitcase, noting, "The products here are more complete and more affordable than in Europe."

## A Deeper Significance

The trend represents more than just commerce. As Foreign Ministry spokesperson Lin Jian said at a regular press conference on July 28, "The reverse cross-border shopping trend continues to heat up, allowing the world to share the quality and creativity of Chinese design and Chinese manufacturing." He added that behind foreign friends' preference for China travel and China shopping is China's commitment to empowering the world through high-quality development and connecting the world through high-level opening up.

Wang Jian, deputy director of the Institute of World China Studies at the Shanghai Academy of Social Sciences, offered a similar perspective: "What's in foreign tourists' shopping carts is not just goods, but recognition of China's innovation, Chinese culture, and lifestyle."

Zheng Rong, a researcher at the National Academy of Opening-up at the University of International Business and Economics, framed the shift in broader terms: "China is accelerating its transformation from the 'world's factory' to a 'global consumption destination.'"

## What to Watch For

The "reverse cross-border shopping" phenomenon shows no signs of slowing. European bookings for China travel grew 275% year-on-year, with ticket orders growing over 20-fold. Russia accounted for 53.5% of European orders (up 553%), while Western European countries — the UK, France, Germany, Italy, Spain, and the Netherlands — together accounted for nearly 42% of orders (up nearly 150%).

As the tax refund 2.0 policy continues to roll out and more services become digitized and streamlined, the shopping experience for international visitors is expected to improve further. The question is not whether the trend will continue, but how far it can go as China completes its evolution from the world's factory to a destination where the world comes to shop — and stays to experience.

Foreign Ministry spokesperson Lin Jian captured the sentiment best: "China, committed to peaceful development, full of innovation vitality and cultural charm, is gaining more and more trust and recognition. We welcome more foreign friends to come and experience the fiery charm of summer China up close."
