# Canadian Woman Ordered Deported After NJ Boardwalk Assault

A Canadian woman who attacked a teenage girl over clothing bearing "Trump" and "ICE" messaging on a New Jersey boardwalk has been ordered deported to Canada, the Department of Homeland Security confirmed. An immigration judge in Texas issued the removal order on August 4, following Kaitlyn Tracey's guilty plea to a misdemeanor simple assault charge in connection with the July 3 incident at Point Pleasant Beach.

## The Incident

According to [Fox News](https://www.foxnews.com/us/canadian-accused-attacking-teen-trump-ice-pants-ordered-deported-dhs), Tracey, 33, approached a group of four teenagers on the boardwalk during the Fourth of July holiday weekend and began yelling at them over their "patriotic colored sweatpants with political wording." Surveillance video captured Tracey striking one girl twice with an open hand—once to the body and once to the face—before fleeing the scene. The victim was wearing a "USA 250" tank top, and the sweatpants displayed the words "Trump" and "ICE."

Tracey was initially charged with endangering the welfare of a child, simple assault, harassment, and obstruction. She surrendered to police on July 13 and was held at Ocean County Jail before being transferred to ICE's Delaney Hall detention facility in Newark, New Jersey.

## Immigration Proceedings

DHS officials said Tracey entered the United States on April 14, 2024, on a visa that expired on September 6, 2024, and remained in the country beyond that date. A DHS spokesperson said Tracey "overstayed her visa and failed to depart," adding that "she received full due process and was ordered removed on Aug. 4 by an immigration judge to her home country of Canada."

According to the [New York Post](https://nypost.com/2026/08/04/us-news/canadian-who-battered-patriotic-teen-on-nj-boardwalk-ordered-deported-as-she-downplays-caught-on-camera-attack/), Tracey appeared by phone from an ICE detention facility in Texas for her court hearing in Toms River, New Jersey. She pleaded guilty to one count of simple assault as part of a plea deal and was sentenced to one day behind bars, with credit for time served.

Judge Guy P. Ryan, who presided over the criminal hearing, read portions of the victim's family impact statement, noting that the parents were "thankful for cameras and cell phones to have documented this incident." The judge said Tracey's banishment was expected to last "for a very long time."

## DHS Response

DHS publicly posted the surveillance footage and dubbed Tracey the "Maple Leaf Menace," using the case to illustrate its immigration enforcement priorities. A DHS spokesperson stated: "Under President Trump and Secretary Mullin, ICE is targeting the worst of the worst. Nearly 70% of ICE arrests are of illegal aliens charged or convicted of a crime in the U.S. Our message is clear: if you come into our country illegally and break our laws, we will find you, we will arrest you, and we will deport you."

The case drew attention beyond the United States. According to [IBTimes UK](https://www.ibtimes.co.uk/canadian-woman-arrest-cross-border-controversy-1809127), many Canadians online expressed that they did not want Tracey back in Canada, with some suggesting she should be prosecuted and imprisoned in the U.S. before any return.

## Legal Defense and Fundraising

Tracey's defense attorney, Francis Hodgson, argued during the hearing that his client "maintained employment and was a productive member of society" while in the U.S. and that she had "taken responsibility for her role" in the incident.

An online fundraiser for Tracey's legal expenses raised only $4,857 of its $10,000 goal, according to the [New York Post](https://nypost.com/2026/08/06/us-news/anti-trump-canadian-lunatic-who-slapped-teen-suffers-another-embarrassing-blow-as-she-was-ordered-deported/). A previous GoFundMe campaign was taken down by the platform for violating its policy against raising money for people accused of violent crimes.

## What's Next

Tracey is expected to be returned to Canada by ICE "as soon as possible," according to DHS. The timeline for her removal and whether she will ever be permitted to return to the United States remains unclear. The case has become a reference point in the ongoing national debate over immigration enforcement and politically motivated violence, highlighting how federal authorities are prioritizing the removal of foreign nationals who commit crimes while residing in the country without legal status.
