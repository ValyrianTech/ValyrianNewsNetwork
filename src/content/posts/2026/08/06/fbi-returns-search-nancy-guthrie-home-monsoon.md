# FBI Returns to Search Near Nancy Guthrie's Home After Monsoon Shifts Landscape

A neighbor of Nancy Guthrie in the Catalina Foothills of Tucson, Arizona, reported seeing FBI agents return to search the desert brush behind the missing 84-year-old's home days before her unsolved disappearance reached the six-month mark, according to [Fox News](https://www.foxnews.com/us/nancy-guthrie-tucson-neighborhood-searched-again-monsoon-shifts-landscape-neighbor-says).

The neighbor, who lives just a few doors down from Guthrie's home, said she recognized faces from earlier law enforcement activity in the neighborhood connected to the case. While a spokesperson for the Pima County Sheriff's Department stated that no task force investigators had returned to the area recently, the FBI did not immediately respond to a request for comment.

## Monsoon Season Alters the Landscape

The renewed search comes as Arizona's monsoon season has dramatically transformed the terrain around Guthrie's home, potentially exposing or moving evidence that investigators may not have previously seen. Bob Krygier, a retired Pima County Sheriff's Department lieutenant and former SWAT commander, told Fox News Digital that heavy rains could have shifted rocks, brush, and other environmental features.

"About a month ago, monsoon season started here in Tucson and a lot of water has come down — a lot of water at once, and it pushes things," Krygier said. "So it's moved the environment. Rocks have been pushed away."

Depending on the area, floodwaters can rise from just a few inches to as much as 12 feet, with enough force to sweep away vehicles, trees, rocks, and even people. Krygier noted that the Santa Cruz riverbed can reach 20 to 30 feet deep at peak flow and 50 to 60 feet across.

Krygier said there are several reasons investigators might return to previously searched areas, including new leads, viable tips, or a fresh set of eyes on the case six months into the investigation.

## A Case That Has Captivated the Nation

Guthrie, the mother of "Today" co-host Savannah Guthrie, was abducted from her home in the early morning hours of February 1, 2026. She was last seen when her son-in-law dropped her off at approximately 9:50 p.m. on January 31. A doorbell camera at her home was disconnected by a masked and gloved individual at 1:47 a.m., and bloodstains found at the scene were confirmed to be Nancy's through DNA testing.

The FBI released footage on February 10 showing an armed individual tampering with the doorbell camera. The suspect is described as approximately 5 feet 9 inches to 5 feet 10 inches tall, with an average build, a black mustache, and carrying a 25-liter Ozark Trail Hiker Pack backpack.

Pima County Sheriff Chris Nanos told Fox News Digital last week that investigators are still following up on active leads, reviewing tens of thousands of videos, and do not consider the case cold. In an interview at the six-month mark, Nanos said, "We are not giving up. We never will," as reported by [KY3](https://www.ky3.com/2026/08/02/we-are-never-giving-up-sheriff-says-progress-is-being-made-nancy-guthrie-case/).

## Ransom Notes Released to the Public

On July 31, the Pima County Sheriff's Office released a pair of purported ransom notes in the case, hoping someone in the public could help identify the sender's unique linguistic style. One note demanded millions in cryptocurrency and claimed Guthrie was alive but scared, while another claimed she had died shortly after being taken and was "buried in nature now," according to [AZFamily](https://www.azfamily.com/2026/07/31/redacted-ransom-notes-tied-nancy-guthries-disappearance-released/).

The FBI determined that three of the ransom notes received were fakes, but investigators believe one note claiming Guthrie died may be legitimate. The combined sum of rewards in the case is over $1.2 million and remains unclaimed.

Savannah Guthrie has made multiple emotional pleas for information, writing on Instagram at the six-month mark: "We are begging for help. We are desperate. We need someone to come forward. Someone knows something."

## What's Next

As the investigation continues, authorities are urging anyone with information to come forward. The case has drawn international attention, with the FBI, Pima County Sheriff's Department, and federal agencies all involved in the search for answers.

According to [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disappearance_of_Nancy_Guthrie), Guthrie is presumed deceased as of February 6, 2026. However, investigators have not confirmed her fate, and the family continues to hold out hope.

The renewed search near Guthrie's home, prompted by the shifting monsoon landscape, represents the latest chapter in a case that has gripped the nation. As [FOX23](https://fox23.com/news/nation-world/neighbor-of-nancy-guthrie-says-investigators-searched-neighborhood-again) noted, it is not uncommon for authorities to return to areas they have already searched upon identifying new leads or receiving new tips.

Anyone with information on the case is asked to call 1-800-CALL-FBI. Tipsters can remain anonymous by calling 520-88-CRIME.
