# House Declared Uninhabitable After Fire in Brussels

A house in the Neder-over-Heembeek neighborhood of Brussels has been declared uninhabitable following a fire early Thursday morning, according to [RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/une-maison-inhabitable-apres-un-incendie-a-bruxelles-11767145). The blaze, which broke out in the early morning hours on Rue des Faînes, sent the elderly resident to the hospital after he inhaled smoke. No fatalities were reported.

## The Incident

Firefighters from the Brussels fire department (SIAMU) were called to the scene shortly after dawn and encountered a violent fire that had started on the ground floor of the terraced house. The elderly occupant managed to escape the building on his own but required medical attention for smoke inhalation.

"The elderly resident was able to get to safety, but he had inhaled smoke and was transported to the hospital to receive care," Walter Derieuw, spokesperson for the Brussels fire department, told [La DH/Les Sports](https://www.dhnet.be/dernieres-depeches/2026/08/06/une-maison-inhabitable-apres-un-incendie-a-bruxelles-EODXVPL5QBD2FIDUAX2F7E6WKQ/).

Derieuw confirmed that crews were able to extinguish the fire, which had started accidentally in the kitchen, but the damage was extensive enough to render the property uninhabitable. Connections to public utility networks were also cut off as a precaution.

## Missing Smoke Detector Raises Concerns

A notable detail emerged during the response: the home had no smoke detector installed, despite such devices being mandatory by law in all Belgian dwellings, including in the Brussels-Capital Region.

The absence of a smoke detector is a recurring concern for fire departments across Belgium. Fire safety advocates have long emphasized that early warning systems are critical for giving residents—particularly the elderly—enough time to evacuate safely during a fire.

## A Neighborhood Familiar with Fires

Neder-over-Heembeek, a former municipality incorporated into the City of Brussels in 1921, has seen several fire incidents in recent years. In January 2024, a fire in an apartment building on the same street left a unit uninhabitable. Months later, an explosion followed by a fire on Rue François Vekemans injured three people. More recently, in July 2025, a roof fire in the neighborhood left a building uninhabitable and one person lightly injured.

These incidents underscore ongoing fire safety challenges in the area, though authorities have not suggested any connection between the events.

## Investigation and Aftermath

The fire was determined to be accidental in origin, with the kitchen identified as the point of ignition. While the cause has been established, the incident raises broader questions about compliance with smoke detector regulations.

Belgian law requires smoke detectors in all homes, whether rented or owner-occupied, with regional variations in enforcement. Fire departments have repeatedly urged residents to install and maintain these devices, noting that they dramatically increase the chances of surviving a residential fire.

The elderly resident is receiving hospital care for smoke inhalation. The house remains uninhabitable while utility connections are restored and repairs are assessed.

## What to Watch

Authorities are expected to continue monitoring the resident's recovery. The incident also serves as a reminder for homeowners across Belgium to verify that their smoke detectors are installed and functioning properly—a simple step that can make the difference between a close call and a tragedy.
