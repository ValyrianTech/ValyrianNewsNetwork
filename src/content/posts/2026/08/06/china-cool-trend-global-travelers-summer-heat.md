# 'China Cool' Trend Draws Global Travelers Amid Summer Heat

As extreme heatwaves grip much of the Northern Hemisphere, a new social media buzzword has emerged from the influx of foreign travelers seeking relief in China: "China Cool." The phenomenon, which combines China's naturally cool summer destinations with the perception among young international visitors that the country is uniquely attractive, has sparked a dramatic surge in inbound tourism and reshaped how the world views China as a travel destination.

## A Dual-Meaning Phenomenon

The term "China Cool" carries a dual significance, as [Xinmin Evening News](https://www.163.com/dy/article/L3IQCOG10512DU6N.html) explains. On one level, it refers to China's abundant naturally cool summer retreats—from the high-altitude plateaus of Tibet and Yunnan to the coastal breezes of Beidaihe and the forested mountains of the northeast. On another, it captures the genuine enthusiasm of international visitors who find China's modern infrastructure, technological innovation, and cultural richness genuinely "cool."

Foreign netizens have captured this sentiment in a widely shared phrase: "The world is in a sauna, but coolness lies in the East."

## Record-Breaking Inbound Tourism

The numbers tell a compelling story. According to [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/politics/20260806/ef3006ceef574478b8796936a6d92fa6/c.html), tourism platform data shows that among the top 20 source countries and regions for summer inbound travel, European countries account for 30 percent, with related travel orders surging 275 percent year-on-year and scenic area ticket orders increasing more than 20-fold. The UK, France, Germany, and other Western European nations together contribute nearly 42 percent of European inbound orders.

Russia has emerged as a particularly significant market, contributing 53.5 percent of European inbound orders with a year-on-year increase of 553 percent. Shanghai, a key international gateway, received 5.31 million foreign visitors in the first half of 2026, up 27.82 percent year-on-year, with Russian visitor numbers growing 132.05 percent.

Overall, China received 22.914 million inbound foreign visitors in the first half of 2026, a 20.4 percent increase year-on-year, according to [China Daily](https://www.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202608/05/WS6a726fa1a310986e2b468f83.html). Online payment platform data shows that since June, inbound tourist consumption has increased 72.9 percent year-on-year.

## Policy Opening as a Catalyst

Behind this surge lies an aggressive expansion of visa-free access. As of June 2026, China has implemented unilateral visa-free policies for 50 countries, full mutual visa exemption with 29 countries, and a 240-hour transit visa-free policy covering 55 countries at 65 entry ports, as [Reference News](https://www.163.com/dy/article/L3LK24410514BQ68.html) reports. Nearly 80 percent of inbound visitors now rely on these policies to enter China.

The impact is visible across the country. Yunnan province, known for its plateau climate and cultural diversity, surpassed 990,000 inbound visitors by August 1, with over half of the 81,000 foreign visitors processed at Kunming port in July being heat-escape tourists, according to [Xinhua's Yunnan report](https://www.163.com/dy/article/L3LGD9IF05346RC6.html). In northern Hebei province, Qinhuangdao received 70,800 foreign visitors from January to July, up 55.26 percent year-on-year, with the Beidaihe Airport international passenger route restarting after a six-year suspension.

## Infrastructure and Technology Appeal

Beyond natural geography, China's infrastructure provides a distinct advantage. In 2024, Chinese households averaged 150.6 air conditioners per 100 households, ranking first globally in air conditioner ownership. A stable national power grid has avoided the widespread blackouts and power rationing that have plagued some European cities during heatwaves.

"European tourists who have endured sweltering heat without AC and power rationing discover that in China they can escape into cool spaces anytime, anywhere," said Yang Yong, an international travel agency worker.

The appeal extends to technology. Visitors in Shenzhen interact with robot dogs at the world's first robot 6S store, while AI-powered translation glasses at Shanghai's China Art Museum help visitors explore ancient artifacts. German tourist Jonathan captured the sentiment while dining in Chongqing: "Eating hotpot here is so cool!"

## From Sightseeing to Immersive Living

A notable shift is underway in how foreign visitors experience China. Travelers are moving from checklist tourism to deep cultural immersion—learning Bai ethnic tie-dyeing in Dali, shopping at local markets and cooking Yunnan cuisine, practicing Tai Chi in Beijing parks, and attending Chinese-Russian cultural summer camps in Beidaihe.

Belgian tourist Marie, on her sixth visit to China, enrolled in a Yunnan cooking class. "Understanding local culture through buying and cooking food—this feeling is fantastic," she said. In Lijiang, over 50 percent of foreign visitors now stay four or more days, according to Yin Liangqin, deputy director of the Lijiang Municipal Bureau of Culture and Tourism.

This trend has spawned a new buzzword: "Chinamaxxing," a fusion of "China" and the internet slang "maxxing," meaning to maximize the Chinese experience by deeply integrating into local daily life.

## Expert Analysis: A Sustainable Trend?

Experts see the phenomenon as more than a seasonal fad. "This summer's inbound heat-escape boom is not driven by a single climate dividend, but is the result of the multi-dimensional resonance of four advantages: natural scenery, cultural heritage, digital convenience services, and opening-up policies," said Professor He Jianmin of Shanghai University of Finance and Economics.

Yin Ping, a professor at Beijing Jiaotong University's School of Economics and Management, echoed this view in China Daily: "The trend is not fleeting. As China continues to open up and improve its inbound travel services, 'China Cool' will gradually evolve from a seasonal buzzword into a regular feature of cross-border tourism."

## What's Next

As [People's Daily](https://en.people.cn/n3/2026/0806/c90000-20485826.html) notes, the "China Cool" hashtag reflects a growing global appreciation for a country whose appeal is rooted in its natural beauty, cultural charm, openness to the world, and commitment to peaceful development. With the Ministry of Culture and Tourism's new "15th Five-Year Plan" for tourism development emphasizing improved inbound travel facilitation, the infrastructure for sustained growth is being put in place.

The question now is whether the momentum can be maintained beyond the summer season. Experts suggest that as China continues to enhance its inbound travel services and cultural offerings, "China Cool" is poised to evolve from a seasonal phenomenon into a lasting feature of global tourism—one that brings the world closer to understanding contemporary China through direct experience rather than secondhand narratives.
