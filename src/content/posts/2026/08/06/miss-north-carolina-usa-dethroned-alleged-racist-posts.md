# Miss North Carolina USA Dethroned Over Alleged Racist Posts

Miss North Carolina USA 2026, Brittany Boltinhouse, has been stripped of her crown just weeks before she was scheduled to compete at the 75th annual Miss USA pageant. The decision, announced Wednesday by pageant organizer A Blaize Productions, followed a "thorough review of recently surfaced information" that reportedly included racial slurs in old social media posts.

Miss USA Chairman and CEO Thom Brodeur condemned the conduct in a statement, saying the organization "does not tolerate racism, homophobia, transphobia nor language that strips any person of their dignity — whoever they are," as [Fox News reported](https://www.foxnews.com/us/miss-north-carolina-usa-loses-crown-organizer-condemns-racism-homophobia-transphobia).

## A Historic Reign Cut Short

Boltinhouse, a 27-year-old entrepreneur from the Wilmington area, was crowned Miss North Carolina USA on June 28 in Spartanburg, making history as the first Honduran woman to win the title. She was scheduled to represent North Carolina at the Miss USA pageant on August 27 in Miami.

According to [NCBeat](https://thencbeat.com/miss-north-carolina-usa-old-tweets/), the North Carolina news outlet that first reported on the resurfaced posts, Boltinhouse used the N-word and other offensive language under the username "Sosa the Stallion" between 2017 and 2019. The outlet also reported that Boltinhouse posted a Facebook message in November 2024 endorsing Donald Trump for president.

Brodeur said the decision was not based on a single incident but reflected "conduct over an extended period of time," as [BBC News](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/c8jnwd7w19yo) reported. He also stated that Boltinhouse has prepared a statement showing "ownership, accountability and apology."

## A Nuanced Response

Brodeur's statements carefully balanced accountability with compassion, emphasizing that the organization's standards must be upheld while acknowledging the human cost of public scrutiny.

"There is a young woman at the center of this," Brodeur said. "She has been named, examined, and judged in public in a way that most of us would not survive, and she is still a human being with a family, a future, and a life ahead of her that is longer than this part of her journey."

He added: "I have never believed that accountability requires cruelty, or that holding a line means enjoying the fall of the person who crossed it."

A Blaize Productions echoed this sentiment in its own statement, saying, "We believe that people are capable of growth, that grace and accountability can coexist, and that one mistake does not have to define an entire life. We also believe that leadership carries a unique responsibility."

## New Titleholder Steps Forward

Myla Hadley, 24, the first runner-up in the state competition, has assumed the title of Miss North Carolina USA 2026 and will represent the state at the national pageant in Miami.

"I understand the seriousness and level of prestige that this title carries, and I can confidently say that both my team and I are ready to take this on," Hadley said, according to [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/aug/06/boltinhouse-miss-north-carolina-racist-posts). "While this may seem like a two-week turnaround given the circumstances, I've been preparing to represent at Miss USA since I first competed with this organization. I am ready to represent North Carolina."

Hadley, a business broker, also expressed gratitude for the support she has received, as [the New York Post](https://nypost.com/2026/08/06/us-news/new-miss-north-carolina-addresses-taking-crown-from-brittany-boltinhouse-who-was-dethroned-over-conduct/) reported.

## The Weight of a Digital Footprint

The controversy highlights the growing reality that past online behavior can have significant consequences for public figures. Jeff Phillips, a longtime pageant judge at local and state levels, noted that accepting a pageant title means accepting the scrutiny that comes with being a public figure, as [WWAYTV3](https://www.wwaytv3.com/miss-north-carolina-usa-reign-ends-after-past-social-media-posts-resurface/) reported.

"Once you step into a position, you've got to be aware that you are going to be, and always be, a public figure," Phillips said. "That takes on an entirely different type of scrutiny and spotlights that are put on you."

Phillips also pointed to historical precedent, recalling Vanessa Williams, who resigned as Miss America in 1984 after nude photographs taken before she won the title were published in Penthouse magazine. Williams remains the only Miss America to have resigned.

## What's Next

The 75th annual Miss USA pageant is scheduled for August 27 at the Adrienne Arsht Center for the Performing Arts in Miami, with the winner qualifying to compete at Miss Universe on November 24. Hadley will now carry North Carolina's hopes onto the national stage with only about three weeks of preparation time.

Meanwhile, Boltinhouse has not yet publicly responded to the decision. Brodeur indicated that her forthcoming statement will be the beginning of what he hopes will be a path toward growth and change.

"I believe people can change," Brodeur said. "Change is not a statement. It is a record. It is built over time, in the open, through conduct rather than apology alone."

As the pageant world watches, the episode serves as a reminder that in the age of social media, a crown carries more than prestige — it carries the weight of everything a titleholder has ever said or done online.
