# Belgium's 'Super Minister' Covers for 12 Colleagues on Holiday

While most of Belgium's federal ministers are enjoying their summer holidays abroad, one minister has stayed behind to keep the government running. Rob Beenders, Minister of Consumer Protection, Social Fraud, Persons with Disabilities and Equal Opportunities, is currently covering for **12 of his 14 federal government colleagues** during the summer break, earning him the nickname "super minister" from [La Libre Belgique](https://www.lalibre.be/belgique/2026/08/01/rob-beenders-le-super-ministre-qui-fait-le-boulot-de-12-autres-pendant-les-vacances-PMPWQ7UK3JED5JZC25OCBQXDUQ/).

## A One-Man Government

Beenders' cabinet confirmed to La Libre that he is handling urgent files for all federal ministers except two: Jean-Luc Crucke (Les Engagés), Minister of Mobility, Climate and Environmental Transition, and Prime Minister Bart De Wever (N-VA). The substitution period runs until August 15, 2026.

This is not the first time the Vooruit minister has taken on this role. He also served as substitute minister during the summer of 2025, a pattern that has drawn attention to the government's staffing arrangements during the holiday period.

## Administrative Continuity, Not Policy Decisions

The role is primarily administrative rather than political. As [BruxellesToday](https://www.bruxellestoday.be/politique/ministres-vacances-qui-remplace.html) reports, "It is not about making all the big decisions during the summer, but about handling administrative tasks that often require a government member's signature without waiting for the new term."

Beenders told De Standaard that there are more files this year than last: "There are more files than last time. Probably because we had a very productive last Council of Ministers... I have to read everything, especially when it comes to other people's files. But I was well prepared."

The workload involves signing documents, handling urgent administrative matters, and ensuring the government can formally function even while most ministers are abroad. In a previous interview with [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2025/08/01/rob-beenders-bart-de-wever/) during his 2025 stint, Beenders described the unusual nature of the work: "Within the federal government, this is done on paper rather than digitally, and sometimes even on parchment. That has something to it, I think. It gives the documents a certain authenticity and there is always proof of an important decision."

## Leadership During the Break

While Beenders handles the bulk of ministerial substitution, Deputy Prime Minister Jan Jambon (N-VA) is leading the country during Prime Minister De Wever's vacation in Japan. According to [Business AM](https://fr.businessam.be/jan-jambon-prend-les-renes-en-belgique-tandis-que-le-premier-ministre-de-wever-part-en-vacances-au-japon), Belgian political protocol requires official replacements at the head of government during ministerial holidays.

Beenders has stated he does not plan to travel abroad this summer, preferring to stay in Belgium to handle the substitution duties. His commitment to remaining available reflects the practical realities of keeping a government operational during the holiday season.

## A Minister's Unusual Path

Beenders' current role is notable given his relatively recent return to politics. After serving in the Flemish Parliament (2014-2019) and the Senate (2016-2019), he left politics in 2019 due to health reasons related to Ménière's disease, which caused hearing problems. He then worked in the private sector for an international hearing implant company before making a political comeback in February 2025 when he was appointed to the De Wever government, as [La Libre Belgique](https://www.lalibre.be/belgique/politique-belge/2025/02/03/le-come-back-politique-de-rob-beenders-vooruit-six-ans-apres-sa-mise-en-retrait-N2PI6VTU4JERNAAUWMO4BO3FME/) reported at the time.

His appointment was confirmed by Vooruit leader Conner Rousseau, with [DHNet/Belga](https://www.dhnet.be/dernieres-depeches/2025/02/02/accord-de-gouvernement-federal-rob-beenders-sera-lautre-ministre-vooruit-XDC6T7N2OZFJ7OVOCK34GCZZGM/) reporting that Beenders would take on the portfolios of Consumer Protection, Social Fraud, and Equal Opportunities.

## What This Means for Belgian Governance

The "super minister" phenomenon highlights several aspects of Belgian governance. First, it demonstrates the importance of administrative continuity: even during vacation periods, the government must remain able to sign documents, process urgent files, and formally represent the state. Second, it raises questions about workload concentration, as one minister handles the portfolios of 12 colleagues simultaneously.

The arrangement also comes at a time when the De Wever government faces significant challenges, including budget negotiations and various legislative reforms. The summer substitution ensures that urgent files can continue to be processed without waiting for September.

## Looking Ahead

The substitution period is expected to end on August 15, when ministers return from their holidays. Whether Beenders will continue to serve as the government's designated substitute in future summers remains to be seen, but his willingness to take on this responsibility twice in a row suggests a pattern that may continue.

As the summer break draws to a close, the question of whether this concentration of responsibility is sustainable - and whether it reflects broader staffing concerns in the Belgian federal government - is likely to remain a topic of discussion in political circles.
