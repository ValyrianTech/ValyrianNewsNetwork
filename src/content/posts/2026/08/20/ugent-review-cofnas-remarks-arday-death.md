# UGent to Act on 'Race Realist' Remarks After Arday Death

Ghent University (UGent) has announced it will take "appropriate follow-up action" regarding controversial statements made by postdoctoral researcher Nathan Cofnas, a self-described 'race realist,' following the death of Cambridge professor Jason Arday. The university's response, signed by rector Petra De Sutter, marks a significant shift from its earlier defense of Cofnas's appointment.

## Context: A Tragedy Shakes Academia

Arday, 41, was found dead at his home in Battersea, London on August 14, just over a week after resigning from Cambridge University following plagiarism allegations. Police described his death as "unexpected" but said they do not suspect foul play, according to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/08/14/voormalig-cambridge-professor-jason-arday-dood-teruggevonden-na/).

Arday had been celebrated as an academic trailblazer - the youngest Black professor in Cambridge's history when he was appointed in 2023 at age 37. His inspiring life story, overcoming autism and developmental delays that left him unable to speak until age 11, made him a symbol of what was possible in academia.

However, in late July, Cofnas published a blog post accusing Arday of plagiarism in his doctoral thesis. This was followed by a [Guardian investigation](https://www.theguardian.com/education/ng-interactive/2026/aug/01/playbooks-plagiarism-pigs-head-new-claims-surrounding-cambridge-professor-jason-arday) that raised questions about Arday's academic work, charity fundraising claims, and personal anecdotes. Arday resigned on August 5, citing "unrelenting public attention."

## UGent's Response

In its statement, UGent said it was "shocked by the death of Professor Arday" and that his passing "hits hard within our own academic community." The university affirmed its commitment to "respect for human dignity" and stated it opposes "discrimination, hatred, and racism."

"We value academic freedom and open academic debate, even when positions are controversial," the university said. "But that freedom is not unlimited. That freedom comes with responsibility and can be limited to protect the rights of others."

The university said it takes Cofnas's statements "very seriously" and has decided to give "appropriate follow-up within its powers and the applicable framework." Because the matter concerns a personnel file, UGent said it would not comment on possible decisions in advance, as [reported by VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/08/19/ugent-jason-arday-nathan-cofnas/).

## Who Is Nathan Cofnas?

Cofnas is an American philosopher and ethicist who describes himself as a 'race realist,' believing that IQ is genetically linked to race. His views have been described as pseudoscience by many experts, contradicting the broad scientific consensus that racial differences in IQ test scores are explained by environmental factors such as socioeconomic status, education, and healthcare access.

In a 2024 blog post, Cofnas argued that in a meritocracy there would be "close to zero" Black professors at Harvard, and that "Black people would disappear from almost all prominent positions, except in the sports and entertainment world." He called for "the war against nature" to stop and described DEI (diversity, equity, and inclusion) as "the devil," as [profiled by VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/03/17/filosoof-nathan-cofnas-portret-rassenrealisme-aanstelling-ugent/).

Cofnas was dismissed from Emmanuel College, Cambridge in 2024 over these blog posts. Cambridge University's investigation concluded his views, "while seen by many as offensive, did not breach the law and did not contravene university regulations designed to uphold free speech." Cofnas is now suing Cambridge over his dismissal.

## A Controversial Appointment

Cofnas's appointment at UGent's Department of Philosophy and Moral Sciences in early 2026 has been controversial from the start. In March, 300 staff and students sent an email asking the university to review his appointment, arguing his statements violate UGent's deontological code, which prohibits spreading ideas about the superiority of a race.

"The UGent deontological code is clear," said Pieter Beck, a science philosopher at UGent and leading critic of Cofnas's appointment. "One of the provisions is that freedom of expression is limited. It is not permitted to spread ideas about the superiority of a race. That is precisely what Cofnas clearly does in his writings," as [reported by VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/03/12/personeel-studenten-academici-aanstelling-rassen-racisme-deontol/).

About 60 students protested at the start of a UGent Board of Governors meeting in March, and in May, students presented a petition with more than 2,000 signatures to rector De Sutter. In April, Cofnas was pelted with beer and yogurt during a lecture at KVHV, a right-wing student association, and later filed a police complaint, according to [VRT NWS coverage](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/03/27/ugent-nathan-cofnas-aanstelling-omstreden-onderzoeker-protest-ra/).

Rector De Sutter had previously defended the appointment as legally correct, stating that "dissenting opinions may exist" and that it is primarily up to experts and colleagues to test and critique ideas.

## The Arday Tragedy

Arday's death has intensified scrutiny on the role Cofnas played in the events leading up to it. His plagiarism allegations triggered a media storm that contributed to Arday's resignation and subsequent death.

In his resignation statement, Arday said: "The persistent accusations, speculation and public commentary have taken a heavy toll on me and on those I love." He added: "This is not the end of my work. It is only the end of this chapter. I need time to recover," as [reported by VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/08/06/jason-arday-professor-ontslagen-camebridge/).

Tributes poured in following Arday's death. London Mayor Sadiq Khan said: "Jason Arday was the victim of a pernicious public shaming that other people in his position simply wouldn't have faced. His death was a tragedy, but once again it should be a wake-up call for all of us," as [reported by The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/uk-news/2026/aug/15/jason-arday-tributes-paid-to-former-cambridge-professor).

Daniel Kebede, general secretary of the National Education Union, drew a distinction between legitimate scrutiny and a "sustained public campaign that turns an individual into a symbol of everything the right believes is wrong with DEI."

## Academic Freedom vs. Institutional Responsibility

UGent's latest response represents a significant evolution from its earlier position. The university now explicitly acknowledges that academic freedom has limits and that Cofnas's statements need to be reviewed within its deontological framework.

Koen Bogaert, a UGent professor, wrote in [De Wereld Morgen](https://www.dewereldmorgen.be/artikel/2026/08/17/heksenjacht-op-zwarte-cambridge-professor-jason-arday-hoe-media-iemand-maken-of-kraken) that the campaign against Arday was a "witch hunt" that showed how media and social media can "make or break" individuals. He argued that the case exposed systemic problems in academia, including "publish or perish" pressures that make plagiarism a common temptation.

Cambridge University has agreed to hold an independent inquiry into Arday's appointment, with vice-chancellor Deborah Prentice acknowledging the affair has damaged the institution, as [reported by The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/education/2026/aug/12/cambridge-independent-inquiry-jason-arday-appointment).

## What's Next

The key question now is what specific action UGent will take regarding Cofnas. Options range from a formal review of his statements against the university's deontological code to potential disciplinary measures, though the university has said it will not comment on personnel matters in advance.

The case has broader implications for how universities across Europe balance academic freedom with institutional values and responsibilities. It also raises fundamental questions about the treatment of Black academics and the broader backlash against diversity, equity, and inclusion policies in higher education.

As [Wikipedia's profile of Cofnas](https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathan_Cofnas) notes, his appointment at UGent and the subsequent controversy have become a flashpoint in these debates. The outcome of UGent's review could set a precedent for how Belgian universities handle researchers with controversial views.

For the academic community, Arday's death serves as a sobering reminder of the human cost of public campaigns against individual scholars. The question now is whether institutions will learn from this tragedy or whether the cycle will repeat itself.
