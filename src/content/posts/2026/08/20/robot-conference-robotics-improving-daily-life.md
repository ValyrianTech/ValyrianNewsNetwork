# Robot Conference Showcases How Robotics Improves Daily Life

The 2026 World Robot Conference opened in Beijing on August 19, bringing together more than 373 exhibitors and 3,000 innovative products under the theme "Human-Robot Symbiosis, Production-Demand Integration." Running through August 23 at the Beiren Yichuang International Convention and Exhibition Center in Beijing's Yizhuang district, the five-day event demonstrates how robotics technology is moving from spectacle to practical applications in healthcare, household assistance, education, and food service, according to [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/tech/20260820/3dd5ad94332342289a7d7a7a65823281/c.html).

## A Growing Industry at a Pivotal Moment

The conference arrives at a transformative period for China's robotics sector. Industry data shows that from January to May 2026, robotics enterprises above designated size achieved revenue exceeding 90 billion yuan, up 26.9 percent year-on-year, with average annual growth of over 20 percent in the past five years, as reported by [Poster News](https://hb.dzwww.com/p/p4Cu5Hjs1G9.html).

The event's scale reflects this momentum. The [official World Robot Conference website](https://www.worldrobotconference.com/news/3599.html) reports that the expo features four major pavilions—Zhichuang, Zhihe, Zhizao, and Zhiqu—covering the full robotics industry chain, with 311 new product launches and 70-plus concurrent events. For the first time, 49 central state-owned enterprises are exhibiting with 12 application scenarios, and the State-owned Assets Supervision and Administration Commission announced the establishment of the "Central Enterprise Robot Innovation Consortium."

Beijing Mayor Yin Yong said at the opening ceremony that "robot technology is an important representative of new quality productive forces, changing the way humans produce and live," adding that Beijing is committed to treating robots as a key cultivation direction for high-precision industries.

## Robots Entering Daily Life

Beyond the exhibition halls, robots are increasingly embedded in everyday consumer experiences across Beijing. A robot chef at the Robot Flame Institute 2.0 in Yizhuang can precisely cook about 50 classic Chinese dishes, with each seasoning accurate to within 0.1 grams, according to [China News Service](https://m.cyol.com/gb/keji/articles/2026-08/19/content_enPjlaCBa2.html). At a JD Seven Fresh unmanned coffee shop in Beijing's Galaxy SOHO, robots make coffee in under 30 seconds, setting a Guinness World Record with 202 cups in one hour on opening day.

Haier's humanoid household robot can pick up dirty clothes and operate a washing machine via brain control, while Pudu Robotics' quadruped robots are designed to work in hazardous environments. Wu Xiang, Pudu Robotics' general manager, told [Global Times via 163.com](https://www.163.com/dy/article/L4OND4PQ0514R9OJ.html?clickfrom=w_tech) that these robots "can replace manual labor in high-pressure, high-temperature, hazardous gas, and remote cold environments," noting that the company's products have been sold to 85 countries and regions with cumulative shipments exceeding 130,000 units.

Xiaomi also unveiled its new-generation humanoid robot "Tieda" at the conference, standing 169-171 cm tall and targeting automotive factory applications. Xiang Diyun, general manager of Xiaomi's Robot Business Unit, explained that "for standardized tasks of 3-5 seconds per process, the robot already has the conditions for stable deployment," as reported by [Time Weekly via 163.com](https://www.163.com/dy/article/L4N3LVAC0519APGA.html?clickfrom=w_money).

## Landmark IPO Marks Industry Milestone

The conference coincided with a landmark moment for China's robotics industry: Unitree Robotics listed on the STAR Market on August 19, becoming the first humanoid robot manufacturer to go public on mainland China's A-shares market. Shares priced at 150.80 yuan opened at 1,100 yuan and closed at 845 yuan, surging over 460 percent. According to [BBC Chinese](https://www.bbc.com/zhongwen/articles/c5yrnedq47go/simp), the listing "marks an important step in China's ambitious robotics industry development plan."

Morgan Stanley raised its 2026 China humanoid robot shipment forecast to 50,000 units from 28,000, and estimates China's humanoid robot market will grow from $2 billion this year to $15 billion by 2030.

## International Recognition and the Road Ahead

International experts at the conference expressed strong confidence in China's robotics capabilities. Jeff Bernstein, president of the Association for Advancing Automation, noted that "China is currently in a leading position in the robotics industry because China has a national strategy dating back more than a decade," adding that China installs nearly 10 times more robots annually than the United States.

However, analysts caution that the industry still faces significant challenges. Zhu Keli, founding dean of the Guoyan New Economy Research Institute, told Global Times that "China's robotics industry has already passed the stage of 'can't build them,' but has not yet fully crossed the threshold of 'cost-effective to use.' The real test ahead is converting mass production capability into scenario value."

As the conference continues through August 23, industry observers will be watching whether the showcased innovations can translate into sustained commercial success—and whether robots can truly deliver on their promise to improve daily life at scale.
