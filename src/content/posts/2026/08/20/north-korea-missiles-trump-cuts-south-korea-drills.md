# North Korea Fires Missile Barrage After Trump Cuts Seoul Drills

North Korea launched approximately 10 short-range ballistic missiles toward the sea on Thursday, South Korea's military confirmed, just a day after Pyongyang dismissed President Donald Trump's decision to scale back joint military exercises with the United States. The launches, detected from the Pyongyang capital region around 5 p.m. local time, flew about 300 kilometers (185 miles) each toward North Korea's eastern waters, according to [South Korea's Joint Chiefs of Staff](https://apnews.com/article/south-north-korea-projectile-launch-military-exercises-b3af05067e4258f90f67023c7b2b3e9a).

The missile barrage came after a week of escalating tensions on the Korean Peninsula, triggered by Trump's surprise order to the Pentagon to "substantially reduce" the Ulchi Freedom Shield exercises just before they began on Monday. The 11-day drills, a central pillar of the U.S.-South Korea alliance dating to the 1950-53 Korean War, were shortened by about half, ending Friday instead of August 27, with some field training exercises canceled.

## Trump's Overture and North Korea's Dismissal

Trump cited what he said was a good relationship with North Korean leader Kim Jong Un and South Korea's refusal to support him over the war in Iran as reasons for the reduction. In a social media post, Trump described the exercises as "not only costly" but sending "a signal that is totally inappropriate and hostile" to North Korea, which he said "has been unthreatening and respectful" during his presidency, as [NPR reported](https://www.npr.org/2026/08/17/g-s1-138969/trump-exercises-south-korea).

However, Kim Yo Jong, the influential sister of North Korean leader Kim Jong Un, dismissed Trump's overture on Wednesday, saying "the provocative, aggressive nature of the drills won't change even though their duration and size were reduced," according to [AP News](https://apnews.com/article/korea-us-military-drills-313e01e4ee305b4c9b1b8bf12643fb17). She also denied Trump's claim that Kim Jong Un had responded to his request for a conversation.

"If the U.S. calculates that it can propagate its recent measure as the one of so-called good faith, they will not get the desired answer," Kim Yo Jong said in a statement carried by state media.

## Missile Launches and Regional Response

The launches on Thursday prompted South Korea's presidential national security council to hold an emergency meeting, after which it issued a statement urging North Korea to halt ballistic missile launches, which are banned under U.N. Security Council resolutions. The Japanese prime minister's office also detected the suspected launches, as [The Guardian reported](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/aug/20/north-korea-fires-10-missiles-day-after-trump-curtails-south-korea-joint-drills).

South Korea's military said it has bolstered its surveillance posture and was closely coordinating information on the launches with the U.S. and Japan.

## The Iran War Connection

A key analytical thread running through this crisis is that Trump's decision appears more focused on pressuring South Korea over the Iran war than on genuine outreach to North Korea. Trump said he asked South Korean President Lee Jae Myung if South Korea would join the U.S. in the "denuclearization" of Iran, "and they said, 'No thanks!'", as [CBS News reported](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/north-korea-missile-launch-trump-kim-jong-un-nuclear-weapons/).

Yang Uk, an analyst at Seoul's Asan Institute for Policy Studies, told AP that "Trump's message is that if you don't cooperate with us on Iran, we're not cooperating with you on the drills either. It's like he's asking South Korea to show it's helping him over Iran."

## Expert Concerns About the Drill Reduction

Experts across multiple sources warn that the reduction in joint military training carries significant risks for regional security and readiness. Leif-Eric Easley, a professor of international studies at Ewha Womans University in Seoul, told [DW](https://www.dw.com/en/trump-north-korea-kim-jong-un-south-korea-geopolitics-security-allies/a-78413321) that "the cost savings from downsizing drills are marginal, while the diplomatic benefits for relations with North Korea are doubtful."

Easley added that scaling back combined defense exercises "can damage alliance coordination, slow the process of South Korea taking on greater responsibility for its own defense and weaken deterrence against possible conflicts in Asia."

David Silbey, a professor of military history and defense policy at Cornell University, said the move reflects "the continuing neglect of American allies," noting that Taiwan, Japan and the Philippines are all watching closely.

Senator Jack Reed (D-RI), the top Democrat on the Senate Armed Services Committee, called the decision "another inane, haphazard decision by President Trump and his chaotic administration," adding that "Kim Jong Un has won a propaganda victory and South Korea, one of our strongest allies, has been punished for no reason beyond Trump's ego."

Senator Mark Kelly (D-AZ), a former Navy pilot, said "hollowing out these joint exercises is shortsighted and a mistake."

## South Korea's Push for Military Independence

The crisis has also accelerated South Korea's push for greater military independence. President Lee Jae Myung has renewed his call to regain wartime operational control (Opcon) of South Korean forces from the U.S., a goal Seoul has pursued for more than two decades, as [The Guardian reported](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/aug/18/south-korea-president-trump-military-drills-control-opcon-kim-jong-un).

"A strong alliance makes the foundation of security stronger, and strengthening our own capabilities increases our value and necessity as an ally," Lee said in a cabinet meeting.

This week's now-curtailed drills were meant to help certify South Korea's readiness to finally take over wartime command from the U.S., a process Lee has pushed to accelerate since taking office.

## North Korea's Strengthened Position

North Korea appears to be in a significantly stronger position than during Trump's first term. Kim has forged a solid alliance with Russia, which is providing military technology, energy supplies and economic assistance. Pyongyang is providing troops, artillery ammunition and ballistic missiles to support Moscow's war against Ukraine, and South Korea's intelligence service estimated last year that North Korea sent about 15,000 troops to support Russia's war effort.

Kim Sang-woo, a former politician and member of the board of the Kim Dae-jung Peace Foundation, told DW that "right now, Kim is in a very comfortable position and you could argue that he no longer needs the US."

Trump claimed Wednesday that North Korea has 57 nuclear weapons, while South Korean officials estimate the arsenal at between 80 and 120 warheads, according to [Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/2026/08/20/north-korea-says-trumps-scale-back-of-joint-military-exercises-with-south-korea-changes-no).

## What's Next

Trump has said he expects to meet with Kim Jong Un later this year, and Kim Yo Jong's restrained response—avoiding typical fiery rhetoric while saying relations between her brother and Trump are "still excellent"—suggests North Korea may be waiting for bigger concessions before returning to talks.

However, experts caution that Kim, now emboldened by his advancing nuclear program and expanding cooperation with Russia, won't likely embrace Trump's overture and return to talks anytime soon unless he's promised bigger concessions. Kim suggested last year that he could only return to talks if the U.S. drops its demand for North Korean denuclearization as a precondition for diplomacy.

The missile launches serve as a stark reminder that despite Trump's diplomatic overtures, North Korea remains committed to advancing its weapons programs. As the region watches for further developments, the question of whether Trump's gamble on personal diplomacy with Kim will pay off—or further destabilize the Korean Peninsula—remains unanswered.
