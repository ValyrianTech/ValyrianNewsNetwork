# Club Brugge Stadium Battle Intensifies as Six Neighbors Appeal

The legal battle over Club Brugge's new stadium has escalated once again, with six neighbors of the Jan Breydelstadion filing a cassation appeal to the Belgian Council of State against the environmental permit for the proposed 40,116-seat stadium on the Olympiasite. The appeal comes just weeks after the Council for Permit Disputes rejected the objections of 20 neighborhood residents on July 9, 2026, in what was seen as a decisive victory for the club after more than two decades of legal wrangling.

## The Final Legal Remedy

Toon Van Moerbeke, the leading opponent of the stadium project, confirmed that the six remaining residents are determined to pursue every available avenue. "This is the last legal remedy and we're going to use it," Van Moerbeke told [Het Laatste Nieuws](https://www.hln.be/brugge/grootste-tegenstander-van-nieuw-stadion-club-brugge-reageert-nu-6-buren-naar-cassatie-trekken-straks-moeten-ze-twee-stadions-afbreken-gaan-ze-dat-risico-nemen~a405bca1/). "Our lawyer has written more than 60 pages."

The cassation appeal, filed within the 30-day window following the July 9 ruling, does not have suspensive effect. This means Club Brugge can still begin construction this year as planned, despite the ongoing legal challenge. However, Van Moerbeke issued a stark warning about the potential consequences if the appeal succeeds.

"With the risk that they'll soon have to demolish two stadiums," he said, referring to the possibility that both the new stadium and the existing Jan Breydelstadion could be affected if the permit is ultimately overturned.

## A Costly Legal Battle

The appeal represents the final legal remedy available to the opponents. The Council of State will not review the substance of the case but will only check whether all procedures and legal rules were correctly applied. If procedural errors are found, the case would return to the Council for Permit Disputes for reconsideration.

Van Moerbeke noted that the high cost of cassation procedures—ranging from €30,000 to €50,000—has significantly reduced the number of opponents from the original 20 residents. "That only six neighborhood residents remain has to do with the cost of cassation procedures," he explained. "It's 30,000 to 50,000 euros! That's not very democratic. People dropped out for financial reasons, not because they gave up their resistance."

## City Officials Respond

Bruges alderman for Spatial Planning Mathijs Goderis acknowledged that the city had hoped the legal saga had finally come to an end. "Ultimately, we no longer expected a cassation appeal," Goderis told [Het Laatste Nieuws](https://www.hln.be/brugge/dan-toch-nog-zorgen-voor-blauw-zwart-zes-buren-gaan-in-cassatieberoep-bij-raad-van-state-tegen-nieuw-stadion-club-brugge~a0b56ea8/). "That appeal has no suspensive effect. Club Brugge can therefore perfectly put the spade in the ground."

Goderis expressed confidence that the Council of State would uphold the earlier ruling. "It's a pity the saga continues, but we live in a rule-of-law state," he said. "With the city, we have good hope that the Council of State will not annul the ruling of the Council for Permit Disputes. In our view, no procedural errors have been made."

## A Two-Decade Saga

The stadium project has been one of the longest-running legal battles in Belgian football history. First announced in January 2007, the project has weathered three different proposed locations, multiple annulled spatial plans, two annulled environmental permits, and persistent opposition from residents, environmental groups, and even business rivals of Club Brugge chairman Bart Verhaeghe.

The current Jan Breydelstadion, built in 1975, is over 45 years old and has long been considered inadequate for a club of Club Brugge's stature. The new stadium, designed with enclosed covered stands, a low roofline, and a white facade, will be embedded in a park environment on the Olympiasite, southwest of the current stadium.

According to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/08/19/stadion-club-brugge-dossier-beroep-cassatieberoep-raad-van-state/), the procedure at the Council of State is expected to take at least one and a half years. Club Brugge's lawyer Pieter-Jan Defoort previously noted that such procedures take considerable time, with the worst-case scenario stretching to 18 months before a decision is reached.

## What's Next

Club Brugge plans to start construction in autumn 2026 and hopes to play its first match in the new stadium during the 2028/2029 season. The club has confirmed that the appeal has no impact on its construction timeline.

For Van Moerbeke and the six remaining opponents, the fight continues regardless of the odds. "Even if everyone else agrees, I'll keep resisting until the last breath," he said in an earlier interview with [Sporza](https://sporza.be/nl/2026/07/09/is-er-nog-beroep-mogelijk-en-wanneer-kan-club-brugge-beginnen-te-bouwen-9-vragen-en-antwoorden-over-het-stadiondossier~1783589998163/).

The coming months will reveal whether Club Brugge proceeds with construction while the legal challenge unfolds, and whether the Council of State finds merit in the neighbors' procedural arguments. For now, the saga—now in its third decade—shows no signs of reaching a quiet conclusion.
