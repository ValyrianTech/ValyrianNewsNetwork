# Wang Yi's Seoul Visit Signals China-ROK Diplomatic Thaw

Chinese Foreign Minister Wang Yi arrived in Seoul on August 19 for a two-day visit, holding talks with his South Korean counterpart Cho Tae-yul in the first solo visit by China's top diplomat to South Korea in approximately five years. The meeting, which included a formal dinner lasting about four hours at the ROK Foreign Ministry, came at a critical juncture as regional security dynamics shift amid the Trump administration's reduction of US-ROK joint military exercises and renewed American outreach to North Korea.

## A Visit Marked by Strategic Timing

Wang Yi's visit — his first solo trip to South Korea since September 2021 — took place just days before the 34th anniversary of China-ROK diplomatic relations on August 24, and against a backdrop of significant regional developments. According to [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/politics/leaders/20260819/f4af02ae62b54ca58dc11210a5f720f0/c.html), the talks covered bilateral relations, the Korean Peninsula situation, and regional and international issues of mutual concern.

The visit came as President Trump ordered a significant reduction in US-ROK joint military exercises, with the Ulchi Freedom Shield (UFS) exercises ending six days early. Trump has also indicated he wants to resume diplomacy with North Korean leader Kim Jong-un, claiming to have received a response from Pyongyang. As [DW](https://www.dw.com/zh/美韩军演瘦身之际-中国外长访韩/a-78429191) reported, Wang Yi's visit was widely seen as Beijing's effort to maintain influence over Korean Peninsula affairs during this period of shifting regional security.

## Restoring Bilateral Relations

The meeting between Wang Yi and Cho Tae-yul was their second face-to-face encounter, following their talks in Beijing in September 2025 — approximately 11 months prior. According to [Asia Today](https://www.ajudaily.com/view/20260819073912428), the visit was part of a broader trend of improving China-ROK relations under the Lee Jae-myung administration, following the successful mutual visits of President Xi Jinping and President Lee Jae-myung in November 2025 and January 2026.

Wang Yi praised the trajectory of bilateral relations, stating that "China and South Korea face each other across the sea, and should naturally have frequent exchanges, enhance mutual trust, and deepen cooperation." He emphasized that the two countries should "demonstrate strategic vision" and work toward the "healthy, stable, and sustainable development of bilateral relations."

In a notable remark, Wang Yi expressed hope that the ROK would "independently and in parallel develop its foreign relations" from its own fundamental interests — a statement widely interpreted as encouragement for Seoul to maintain balanced diplomacy between China and the United States, as [RTHK](https://gbcode.rthk.hk/TuniS/news.rthk.hk/rthk/ch/component/k2/1866776-20260820.htm) reported.

## Economic Cooperation and People-to-People Ties

Economic cooperation featured prominently in the talks. Wang Yi highlighted that "China's super-large market provides South Korea with the huge opportunity of 'getting the moon first from the waterside pavilion'" (近水楼台先得月), underscoring the deep economic interdependence between the two countries.

Both sides agreed to strengthen cooperation in several key areas, including FTA services and investment negotiations, supply chain stabilization, cultural exchanges, introduction of giant pandas, and management and protection of Korean independence movement sites in China. According to [RFI](https://www.rfi.fr/cn/中国/20260819-王毅-中方积极考虑安排中韩领导人11月在深圳会晤), the two countries also noted that annual personnel exchanges are expected to exceed 10 million people in 2026.

Cho Tae-yul reaffirmed the ROK's consistent adherence to the One-China policy since the establishment of diplomatic relations, and both sides discussed plans for the 35th anniversary of diplomatic relations in 2027.

## Korean Peninsula: A Central Focus

The Korean Peninsula situation was a major topic of discussion. Cho introduced President Lee Jae-myung's "Korean Peninsula Peaceful Coexistence" blueprint from his Liberation Day (August 15) speech, which proposed multilateral discussions on ending the war and transforming the unstable armistice system into a peace system.

According to [RTHK](https://gbcode.rthk.hk/TuniS/news.rthk.hk/rthk/ch/component/k2/1866756-20260819.htm), Wang Yi reiterated China's consistent position on the peninsula, emphasizing that "China, as a responsible major country, maintains continuity and stability in its foreign policy, and is always committed to playing a constructive role for peace and stability on the peninsula." He expressed China's willingness to see North and South Korea gradually move toward peaceful coexistence.

Cho called on China to play a constructive role in getting North Korea to return to dialogue as soon as possible. When asked whether China would mediate US-North Korea summit talks, Wang Yi said this should be decided by the US and North Korea themselves, emphasizing that "especially the US needs to change its long-standing hostile policy toward North Korea."

## APEC Summit and High-Level Exchanges

A key outcome of the meeting was the discussion of arranging a meeting between President Xi Jinping and President Lee Jae-myung during the APEC Leaders' Meeting in Shenzhen in November 2026. Wang Yi confirmed that China is "actively considering" arranging such a meeting, according to RFI.

Wang Yi also extended an invitation to Cho Tae-yul to visit China, which the ROK Foreign Minister accepted. Cho expressed his expectation that Lee Jae-myung's attendance at the Shenzhen APEC summit and the 35th anniversary of diplomatic relations next year would further strengthen high-level exchanges.

## Analysis: A Strategic Recalibration

The visit reflects several converging dynamics in Northeast Asian geopolitics. First, it signals the consolidation of improved China-ROK relations under the Lee Jae-myung administration, which has pursued a policy of restoring ties with Beijing following years of strained relations under the previous Yoon administration.

Second, the timing of the visit — coinciding with Trump's military exercise reductions and outreach to Kim Jong-un — suggests Beijing's intent to remain a key player in Korean Peninsula affairs. Wang Yi's comments on US-North Korea relations, declining to take on a mediation role while urging Washington to change its approach to Pyongyang, reflect China's careful positioning.

Third, the emphasis on economic cooperation and people-to-people exchanges underscores the deep economic interdependence between China and South Korea, which remains a stabilizing factor in the bilateral relationship despite broader geopolitical tensions.

## What's Next

Following his visit to South Korea, Wang Yi was scheduled to travel to Indonesia to chair the first China-Indonesia Comprehensive Strategic Dialogue mechanism meeting and attend the second China-Indonesia Foreign and Defense Ministers' 2+2 Dialogue.

The successful arrangement of a Xi-Lee meeting at the Shenzhen APEC summit in November would further consolidate the improvement in bilateral relations. With the 35th anniversary of China-ROK diplomatic relations approaching in 2027, both sides appear committed to institutionalizing and deepening their bilateral relationship, even as the regional security landscape continues to evolve.

As the Chinese Foreign Ministry [confirmed](https://www.fmprc.gov.cn/web/wjbz_673089/xghd_673097/202608/t20260819_12006915.shtml), the talks marked an important step in maintaining the momentum of high-level exchanges between the two countries, setting the stage for what both sides hope will be a new chapter in China-ROK relations.
