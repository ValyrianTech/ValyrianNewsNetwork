# Media, Military Members Held at Gunpoint at Vandenberg Space Force Base

Five news reporters and two members of the military were held at gunpoint by security officers at Vandenberg Space Force Base in California on Monday, despite having proper escort, identification, and invitations to an official ceremony, according to [AP News](https://apnews.com/article/vandenberg-space-force-base-california-journalists-detained-4815c999cbcb9ea60c918429c0e0ade4). The incident occurred ahead of an invitation-only groundbreaking for a new training facility for missile operators at the base near Santa Barbara.

The reporters and military members were part of a convoy being escorted onto the base by uniformed public affairs staff in a government minivan when security personnel from the 30th Security Forces Squadron converged on them, believing the group were "gate runners" — unauthorized entrants attempting to breach the installation.

## A Routine Arrival Turns Into a Standoff

As is standard procedure, the media convoy followed a public affairs escort vehicle that pulled over just past the main gate to regroup before proceeding to the ceremony site. That pause triggered an alert, and within moments, [Noozhawk](https://www.noozhawk.com/media-military-members-held-at-gunpoint-at-vandenberg-sfb-despite-escort-invitation/) reported, multiple security forces members raised metal barricades along California Boulevard and ordered each vehicle to pull forward one by one.

With firearms pointed at the group, occupants were ordered to exit their vehicles individually with hands raised. The two military members — one enlisted and one junior officer — were handcuffed and placed in base vehicles, while the civilians were told to sit on the curb nearby. The standoff lasted more than 30 minutes, causing all media members to miss the ceremony they had been invited to cover. [KEYT/News Channel 3-12](https://keyt.com/vandenberg-space-base/2026/08/18/invited-members-of-the-media-held-at-gunpoint-by-vandenbergs-30th-security-forces-squadron/) reported that sirens were sounded and barricades went up at the gate entrance.

Those detained included reporters from Noozhawk, KEYT/News Channel 3-12, KSBY-TV, and the Santa Maria Times/Lompoc Record. All were eventually cleared and released without further incident, and one security forces member later apologized for the interaction.

## Official Response: A "Breakdown in Communication"

In a statement to Noozhawk, a Space Launch Delta 30 Public Affairs spokesperson called the incident a breakdown in communication, citing an increased number of unauthorized entry attempts at the base.

"Vandenberg Space Force Base is experiencing an increased number of unauthorized entry attempts," the statement said. "When media entered the installation, there was a breakdown in communication and movement continued prior to verification. Every potential unauthorized presence must be treated as a legitimate threat until proven otherwise. While we recognize this may have been unsettling, the security of the base and its personnel is our priority."

Tom Bolton, Noozhawk's editor in chief, was sharply critical of the response. "We respect the military's need to provide security for the base," Bolton said in a statement. "But clearly what occurred was unfortunate and unnecessary, and potentially dangerous, as firearms were drawn." Bolton added that the incident was "a failure on multiple levels, and the base owes apologies to the affected journalists."

## The Sentinel Program and the Missed Ceremony

The groundbreaking the journalists were invited to cover marked the official construction kickoff of the Air Education and Training Command's Sentinel Formal Training Unit — a 160,000-square-foot facility designed to train missile operators and maintainers for the [Sentinel intercontinental ballistic missile program](https://www.ksby.com/lompoc-valley/vandenberg-space-force-base-breaks-ground-on-113-million-sentinel-training-facility). The Sentinel system will replace the aging Minuteman III missiles that have served as part of the U.S. nuclear deterrent since the Cold War.

The project, expected to cost approximately $113 million, will train 300-400 students per year and is the base's largest Sentinel military construction project to date. Col. James Horne, Space Launch Delta 30 commander, said the expansion could bring 300-500 additional full-time personnel to the base, representing a significant economic impact for the surrounding community.

## Security vs. Press Access

The incident highlights a fundamental tension between heightened security protocols at military installations and the need for media access to cover official events. The fact that two of the base's own military members — both carrying valid passes and listed for entry — were handcuffed underscores the severity of the communication failure, as [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/aug/19/reporters-held-gunpoint-california-space-force-base) noted in its coverage.

Bolton said he hopes to have a conversation with base leadership to review what happened and prevent similar occurrences. The base has not publicly stated what corrective steps, if any, will follow.

## What to Watch For

As construction on the Sentinel training facility proceeds, questions remain about how the military will balance security requirements with the need for transparent media access. The incident could have lasting implications for trust between journalists and military institutions, and for how future media events at Vandenberg and other installations are handled.

The base's acknowledgment of a communication breakdown suggests an openness to review, but whether concrete procedural changes follow will determine whether this was an isolated incident or a symptom of deeper challenges in military-press relations.
