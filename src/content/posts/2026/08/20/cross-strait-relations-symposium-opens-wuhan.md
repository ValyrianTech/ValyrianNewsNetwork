# Cross-Strait Relations Symposium Opens in Wuhan Amid Tense Environment

The 2026 Cross-Strait Relations Symposium opened Thursday in Wuhan, bringing together more than 160 experts, scholars, and officials from both sides of the Taiwan Strait for a two-day discussion on the future of cross-strait relations, according to [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/tw/20260820/676594253ca1427ca4ae2dd879dcfd56/c.html). The event, themed "Uphold National Integrity, Work Together for National Rejuvenation," marks the annual gathering's return to its traditional August schedule.

## Context and Significance

Hosted by the Cross-Strait Relations Research Center, an institution affiliated with China's State Council Taiwan Affairs Office, and organized by the Hubei Province Cross-Strait Exchange Promotion Association, the symposium serves as one of the most significant annual academic platforms for cross-strait dialogue. This year's event is particularly notable as it is the first symposium organized under the center's new director, Long Hu, who previously served as deputy director of the Research Bureau of the Taiwan Affairs Office.

The symposium unfolds against a complex political backdrop. It follows the historic April meeting between Chinese leader Xi Jinping and Kuomintang (KMT) Chair Cheng Li-wen — the first such meeting between the two party leaders in a decade — and comes ahead of Taiwan's "nine-in-one" local elections scheduled for late 2026.

## Key Addresses and Themes

Peng Qing'en, Deputy Director of the CPC Central Committee Taiwan Affairs Office and State Council Taiwan Affairs Office, delivered the keynote address at the opening ceremony. Citing Xi Jinping's speech at the celebration of the 105th anniversary of the founding of the Communist Party of China, Peng called for Taiwanese compatriots to "uphold national integrity, recognize the historical trend of development, and join hands with mainland compatriots to resolutely oppose 'Taiwan independence' separatist activities and external interference." Peng was promoted to his current position in February 2026, as [reported by Lianhe Zaobao](https://www.zaobao.com.sg/news/china/story20260228-8652934).

Zhuge Yujie, Deputy Secretary of the Hubei Provincial Party Committee, emphasized the province's commitment to deepening cross-strait exchanges, stating that Hubei is "accelerating its construction as an important strategic fulcrum for the rise of the central region, creating broad space for deepening exchanges and cooperation between Hubei and Taiwan in various fields."

Participants will discuss two sub-topics over the course of the event: "Firmly Establish Correct Identity, Guard Our Common Home" and "Deepen Exchange and Integration, Cultivate Kinship and Well-being." In their collective remarks, attending scholars affirmed that "the mainland has successfully embarked on a path of Chinese-style modernization, with strong momentum and vitality in economic development," while calling for resolute opposition to "Taiwan independence" separatism.

## Taiwanese Media Excluded

A notable departure from previous years is the exclusion of Taiwanese media from this year's event. Taiwan's Central News Agency and China Times had previously sent reporters to cover the symposium, but according to [Lianhe Zaobao](https://www.zaobao.com.sg/news/china/story20260820-9546320), Taiwanese media were not invited this year. The [United Daily News](https://udn.com/news/story/7331/9702094) characterized the decision as reflecting the "tense cross-strait environment" and the "low-key" nature of this year's exchanges, noting that several Taiwanese scholars were still invited to attend in person.

## Broader Cross-Strait Context

The symposium is part of a broader pattern of cross-strait engagement efforts in 2026. In February, the KMT-CCP Think Tank Forum was held in Beijing, marking the resumption of institutionalized exchanges between the two parties after a decade-long hiatus. The April Xi-Cheng meeting produced four points from Xi on cross-strait relations, emphasizing "correct identity," peaceful development, exchange and integration, and opposition to Taiwan independence. Following that meeting, Beijing released ten policy measures to promote cross-strait exchanges and cooperation.

Just last week, the 11th Beijing-Taiwan Scholars Symposium brought together over 130 experts to discuss the impact of US-China relations on the cross-strait situation and Taiwan's upcoming elections. At that event, Long Mingbiao, Executive Vice President of the Association for Relations Across the Taiwan Strait, noted that "peace, development, exchange, and cooperation are also the mainstream public opinion in Taiwanese society," as [reported by Lianhe Zaobao](https://www.zaobao.com.sg/news/china/story20260812-9508333).

## Analysis and Outlook

The decision to exclude Taiwanese media while still inviting Taiwanese scholars suggests Beijing is seeking to maintain academic and political channels of communication while limiting public exposure during a period of heightened tension. The symposium's theme and sub-topics align closely with the framework Xi articulated in his April meeting with Cheng Li-wen, indicating continuity in Beijing's approach.

With Taiwan's local elections approaching at the end of 2026, the symposium's discussions on identity, integration, and cross-strait cooperation carry particular weight. The KMT, under Cheng's leadership, has completed nominations in 19 counties and cities and has formed alliances with the Taiwan People's Party in some areas — developments that could reshape Taiwan's political landscape and, by extension, cross-strait dynamics.

## What to Watch

Observers will be watching for concrete outcomes from the symposium, including any new policy signals or proposals for expanded cooperation. The upcoming Cross-Strait Youth Exchange Season, set to begin in Shanghai on August 24, will provide another test of whether Beijing's push for grassroots engagement can proceed despite the tense political environment. As Taiwan's elections draw closer, the interplay between mainland outreach efforts and Taiwan's domestic political dynamics will remain a critical factor in determining the trajectory of cross-strait relations.
