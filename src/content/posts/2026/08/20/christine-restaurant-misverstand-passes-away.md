# Christine (66) from 'Restaurant Misverstand' Passes Away

Christine Goemaere (66), a beloved participant from the fourth season of the popular Belgian television show 'Restaurant Misverstand', passed away on Tuesday at her home, surrounded by family. The show, which features people with young-onset dementia working in a restaurant setting, had made Christine a familiar and cherished figure to viewers across Belgium.

According to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/08/20/christine-restaurant-misverstand-overleden/), Christine "colored the fourth season of the TV series with her smile and warmth." Her family confirmed the news, with VRT 1 paying tribute on Facebook: "Christine was a sweet, caring and humorous woman who shone when she spoke about her children and grandchildren."

## A Life of Caring for Others

Before her Alzheimer's diagnosis in 2020, Christine worked as a nurse in the operating theater at AZ Sint-Lucas hospital in Ghent—a career defined by caring for others. [HLN](https://www.hln.be/tv/christine-66-uit-restaurant-misverstand-overleden~a096dc7f/) reported that as a former nurse, Christine was accustomed to caring for others her entire life. After her diagnosis, she found it difficult that her environment took over tasks from her, but in the program she blossomed.

Christine lived in a multi-generational home (kangoeroewoning) in Oostakker, East Flanders, with her daughter Irina, son-in-law Jason, and grandchildren Otis and Nova. On weekends she stayed with her other daughter Marijke, an arrangement they affectionately called their 'co-daughterhood.'

## Her Role in Season 4

Christine was one of eight participants in the fourth season of 'Restaurant Misverstand,' which aired on VRT 1 in spring 2026. The season was filmed at a renovated farm in Diksmuide, West Flanders, as [VRT's official announcement](https://www.vrt.be/nl/over-ons/nieuws-over-vrt/restaurant-misverstand-opent-deuren-acht-mensen-met-dementie-op-jonge-leeftijd-aan-de-slag-in-westhoekse-boerderij) detailed. Participants worked in the restaurant and on the farm, and also lived together in a holiday home near the IJzer river.

A particularly touching moment in the series was a special episode dedicated to Christine, in which presenter Dieter Coppens took her back to the operating theater where she had worked her entire career. The episode highlighted her lifelong dedication to nursing and the profound connection she maintained to her professional identity.

## A Show with a Powerful Mission

'Restaurant Misverstand' is based on the British format 'The Restaurant That Makes Mistakes,' which itself originated from a Japanese experiment by Shiro Oguni first held in Tokyo in June 2017, as documented on [Wikipedia](https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Restaurant_Misverstand). The Belgian version, produced by Roses Are Blue for VRT in collaboration with Alzheimer Liga Vlaanderen and other dementia organizations, has become a cultural touchstone.

In November 2024, Season 2 of the show won the prestigious International Emmy Award in the non-scripted entertainment category, as [VRT NWS reported](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2024/11/26/restaurant-misverstand-van-dieter-coppens-wint-emmy/). Presenter Dieter Coppens described the award as "a recognition for the courage that all participants and caregivers of the 3 seasons of 'Restaurant Misverstand' have shown by letting people look into their hearts and minds at one of the most vulnerable moments in their lives."

## A Community in Mourning

Christine's passing is part of a tragic pattern within the 'Restaurant Misverstand' community. Several other participants from various seasons have also passed away, including Lode (53) from Season 4 who chose euthanasia after the recordings, and Anne Moerman (57) from Season 3 who passed away in November 2025, as [VRT NWS reported](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2025/11/10/restaurant-misverstand-anne-moerman-overleden/).

The show's format highlights the reality of young-onset dementia, which typically begins between ages 40 and 65. Unlike common perception, the first symptoms are often behavioral and personality changes rather than memory problems. The program's mission is to show what people with dementia can still do after their diagnosis, challenging stereotypes and fostering understanding.

## A Legacy of Warmth

Christine's impact extended far beyond the television screen. As [OpenNieuws](https://www.opennieuws.be/nieuws/christine-van-restaurant-misverstand-overleden/) noted, those who knew her speak with great admiration of her character. She was known for her warm personality, her care for others, and her sense of humor—qualities that shone through in every episode she appeared in.

Her story, like those of the other participants, serves as a powerful reminder of the dignity, capability, and humanity of people living with dementia. Through her participation in the show, Christine demonstrated that a diagnosis does not define a person, and that there is still much joy, purpose, and connection to be found in life.

## What's Next

As the 'Restaurant Misverstand' community mourns yet another loss, the show's legacy continues to grow. The program has not only entertained but has also educated Belgian audiences about young-onset dementia, fostering greater empathy and understanding. Christine's warmth and smile will be remembered by viewers across Belgium, and her family's loss is shared by a nation that came to love her.

The cause of her death has not been publicly disclosed. Her family has asked for privacy during this difficult time.
