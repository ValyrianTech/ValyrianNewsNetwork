# Shi Yuqi's Shock First-Round Exit Rocks Badminton Worlds

Defending champion and world No. 1 Shi Yuqi suffered a stunning first-round elimination at the 2026 BWF World Championships in New Delhi on Monday, falling to India's 21-year-old rising star Ayush Shetty in a dramatic three-game battle. The defeat, which ended Shi's title defense in the Round of 64, marks one of the biggest upsets in recent World Championships history.

## A Historic Upset in New Delhi

Shetty, ranked 22nd in the world, defeated the 30-year-old Chinese star 21-14, 13-21, 21-19 in a match lasting one hour and 13 minutes at the Indira Gandhi Indoor Stadium. According to [Xinhua News](https://www.xinhuanet.com/sports/20260818/d0263bf56ed94be3b5ff648b8cd15571/c.html), the Indian youngster recorded his first career victory over Shi in four meetings, having previously lost all three encounters—including the Asian Championships final in Ningbo in April, where Shi triumphed 21-8, 21-10.

The match unfolded as a tale of momentum swings. Shetty, buoyed by a raucous home crowd, raced to an 11-5 lead and took the opening game 21-14. Shi responded in the second, finding his rhythm with precise net play to level the match at 21-13. In the decisive third game, Shetty surged to a commanding 17-6 lead before Shi mounted a furious comeback, saving four match points at 15-20 before ultimately falling 19-21.

"Miracles don't happen often, but I still tried my best," Shi said after the match, as reported by Xinhua. "When I was far behind, I tried everything to come back and turn the match around. I actually did quite well; I just fell short on the key points."

## Shetty's Breakthrough Moment

For Shetty, the victory represents the crowning achievement of a remarkable rise. The 1.95-meter (6'5") shuttler from Karnataka has been widely compared to Danish Olympic champion Viktor Axelsen for his height, powerful smashes, and wide reach. He won the 2025 US Open for his first BWF World Tour title and became the first Indian in 61 years to reach the men's singles final at the Asian Championships in April.

"Definitely it was an energy-booster for me," Shetty said of the home crowd's support, according to [AFP via France24](https://www.france24.com/en/live-news/20260817-number-one-shi-suffers-shock-first-round-exit-at-badminton-worlds). "The way they were standing and supporting at the end as I was getting nervy."

Shetty's coach, Sagar Chopda, credited his player's improved defense and tactical preparation for the breakthrough. "Today his defence was the key, because of that, he won quite a few points, and strategically he was better prepared," Chopda told [myKhel](https://www.mykhel.com/badminton/ayush-shettys-stunning-shi-yuqi-upset-at-bwf-world-championships-2026-coach-reacts-450497.html). "He was ready for Shi Yuqi's downward shots. And when he got to attack, he wasn't very erratic. He was very calm and played those half smashes and stick smashes really, really well."

## China's Men's Singles Crisis

The defeat compounds a difficult day for China's men's singles contingent. According to [BadmintonPlanet.com](https://www.badmintonplanet.com/badminton-news/ayush-shetty-shi-yuqi-2026-world-championships.html), 16th seed Weng Hongyang was also eliminated in the opening round, losing to Japan's Yushi Tanaka 17-21, 14-21. Eighth seed Li Shifeng was the only Chinese men's singles player to advance, defeating Kazakhstan's Dmitriy Panarin 21-7, 21-10.

Shi's early exit raises questions about his physical condition. The 30-year-old has been struggling with injuries since helping China win the Thomas Cup in May, where he battled acute gastroenteritis during the group stage before returning to win all his knockout matches. As [Yangtze Evening News](https://www.yzwb.net/news/ty/202605/t20260504_349364.html) reported, that Thomas Cup triumph marked his third title in the event, making him China's second men's singles player after Lin Dan to achieve the feat. However, he has withdrawn from multiple tournaments since then due to ongoing physical issues.

## Historical Significance

The 2026 World Championships carry added historical weight as the final edition to use the traditional 3-game 21-point scoring system. A new 3-game 15-point format takes effect on January 4, 2027, as noted in a [background article from Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/sports/20260817/62893495b9474b29a4f816d7298f649e/c.html). India has historically been a venue for upsets—at the 2009 Hyderabad World Championships, several top players including Lee Chong Wei and Taufik Hidayat suffered early exits.

For India, Shetty's victory validates the country's growing investment in badminton. As [NetEase](https://m.163.com/dy/article/L4IPF1Q905299I58.html) noted, India sees badminton as a key medal sport in its Olympic ambitions, and Shetty is viewed as a potential contender for the 2028 Los Angeles Games.

## What's Next

Shetty will face Sri Lanka's Dumindu Abeywickrama in the Round of 32, while Li Shifeng now carries China's men's singles hopes alone. The tournament continues through August 23, with the new 15-point scoring era looming on the horizon. For Shi Yuqi, the defeat raises significant questions about his path forward as he navigates injuries and prepares for the next Olympic cycle.
