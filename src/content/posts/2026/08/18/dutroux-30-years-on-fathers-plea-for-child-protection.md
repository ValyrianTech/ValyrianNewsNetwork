# Dutroux 30 Years On: Julie Lejeune's Father Issues Emotional Plea for Better Child Protection

Thirty years after the discovery of his daughter's body in the garden of Marc Dutroux's property in Sars-la-Buissière, Jean-Denis Lejeune has issued an emotional plea for stronger child protection measures in Belgium. In a social media post marking the grim anniversary, the father of eight-year-old Julie Lejeune called on politicians to do more, declaring that "there is still very, very much work to be done to better protect children."

"Dearest, today is again a special day," Lejeune wrote on August 17, 2026. "Thirty years ago we were told that I would never be able to hold you in my arms again." But he emphasized that his message was not a request for sympathy. "If we are still talking about this thirty years later, it is not to ask for pity, but to make clear to our politicians that there is still very, very much work to be done to better protect children. Children are not objects and not sexual toys," he said, signing the post simply as "Julie's Dad." [Het Laatste Nieuws](https://www.hln.be/binnenland/vader-van-dutroux-slachtoffer-julie-lejeune-in-emotioneel-bericht-er-is-nog-heel-heel-veel-werk-om-kinderen-beter-te-beschermen~a4ed3ba6/) reported on the emotional message.

## A Nation's Darkest Chapter

The Dutroux case remains one of the darkest chapters in modern Belgian history. Julie Lejeune and Mélissa Russo, both barely eight years old, were abducted on June 24, 1995, in Grâce-Hollogne near Liège. They were held for months in a hidden space in Dutroux's home in Marcinelle before dying of starvation in the spring of 1996. Dutroux moved their bodies to another property in Sars-la-Buissière, where they were discovered on August 17, 1996.

Dutroux kidnapped, abused, and held hostage a total of six girls: Julie Lejeune, Mélissa Russo, An Marchal, Eefje Lambrecks, Sabine Dardenne, and Laetitia Delhez. Four of them were murdered. Dutroux was sentenced to life imprisonment in 2004 following a trial at the Arlon assize court. [RTL Info](https://www.rtl.be/actu/belgique/justice/il-y-30-ans-marc-dutroux-etait-arrete-marcinelle/2026-08-13/article/797219) detailed the case's timeline and the subsequent reforms.

The case exposed severe dysfunctions in Belgium's police and judicial systems, triggering the largest public demonstration in the country since World War II. More than 300,000 people participated in the "Marche Blanche" (White March) in Brussels on October 20, 1996, demanding justice and systemic reform. [Paris Match Belgique](https://www.parismatch.be/actualites/societe/2026/08/12/30-ans-plus-tard-laffaire-dutroux-nest-toujours-pas-finie-soit-on-a-affaire-a-des-policiers-incompetents-soit-on-ne-veut-pas-nous-dire-la-verite-W7U7EX3C6RDOLCOIKPEX7Q52SU/) chronicled how the affair led to major institutional changes, including the creation of Child Focus, an integrated two-level police force, the Franchimont law strengthening victims' rights, the High Council of Justice, and sentencing courts.

## The Legacy of Parental Overprotection

Beyond the institutional reforms, the 30th anniversary has prompted deeper reflection on how the case reshaped Belgian parenting. The fear of the "man in the white van" fundamentally changed how children were raised, with many parents restricting outdoor play and unsupervised activities almost overnight.

Dr. Sophie Maes, a child psychiatrist, told [RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/julie-et-melissa-30-ans-plus-tard-la-surprotection-parentale-en-heritage-11771707) that the case brought an important focus on sexual abuse of children, which had been a taboo subject. "Parents quickly became very worried, realizing that the outside world is not so safe and that children could be sexual prey. One can truly speak of a wave of panic at the time," she said.

However, experts caution that the threat perception may have been skewed. Stijn Van Peteghem, a psychologist and FNRS researcher at ULB, noted the paradox: "We all have in mind this idea of the threat coming from outside, this idea of an unknown person who will kidnap children. But it's a bit paradoxical because the majority of physical and sexual violence against children is most often committed by people known to the family, or even within the family itself."

Child Focus data supports this assessment, showing only four reports of child abductions by unknown persons between 2016 and 2025. Dr. Maes emphasized that "predators like Marc Dutroux are the exception in child abuse, unlike intra-family violence."

The intergenerational impact of this overprotection remains visible today. Mathilde Kervyn, who was 13 in 1996 and is now a mother of four, described the lasting anxiety: "This case marked me so much that I can draw from memory the faces of Julie and Mélissa. There's really a knot in my stomach when they're out of my field of vision." Van Peteghem confirmed that "certain parental practices like overprotection can explain certain intergenerational effects. This can still color the way we educate our children today."

## A Memorial to Bridge Past and Future

As Belgium marks this somber anniversary, plans are moving forward for a permanent memorial at the site where Julie and Mélissa's bodies were found. The commune of Lobbes acquired the former Dutroux property in Sars-la-Buissière, demolished the house in 2023, and has been consulting with residents and Jean-Denis Lejeune on the design.

The memorial will take the form of a pathway "that bridges the past and the future," according to Mayor Steven Royez. "The monument is a priority for us," he told [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/08/14/julie-en-melissa-slachtoffers-van-dutroux-krijgen-gedenkteken/). Building permits are expected in the coming weeks, after which the commune will seek a contractor. [La Libre](https://www.lalibre.be/regions/hainaut/2026/08/14/un-lieu-de-memoire-pour-julie-et-melissa-verra-le-jour-a-sars-la-buissiere-JKUP6UW3QFCJBMNEB7MWNHVO5I/) and [L'Avenir](https://www.lavenir.net/regions/mons-centre/lobbes/2026/08/14/bientot-un-memorial-en-hommage-a-julie-et-melissa-a-sars-la-buissiere-SWQEEAAMANGE3IZHGNJ3L7TJ3Q/) both reported on the memorial project.

## An Investigation That Continues

Even three decades later, the case continues to generate news. In July 2024, approximately 200 photos of naked women—some possibly depicting minors—were found in Dutroux's cell at Nivelles prison. The investigation remains ongoing, a delay that has frustrated Lejeune. "Either we are dealing with incompetent police officers, or they don't want to tell us the truth," he told [DH](https://www.dhnet.be/actu/belgique/2026/08/13/de-dutroux-on-nattend-plus-rien-mais-toute-la-verite-na-pas-ete-faite-jean-denis-lejeune-evoque-les-30-ans-de-laffaire-dutroux-VKUAFWNZ7ZCDRIOXYYMMBMCUBI/), adding: "I have the impression of reliving the same kind of situation as when the girls were kidnapped in June 1995."

## The Changing Face of Threats

As Belgium reflects on three decades since the tragedy, experts point to a new frontier in child protection: the digital world. Dr. Maes told RTBF that "the impact of digital technology today is much more important than the Dutroux case thirty years later," noting that online predators are "extremely present, very organized, and able to circumvent defenses and barriers."

For Lejeune, the message remains clear. "I want everything you had to endure to give meaning to your suffering," he wrote. "I hope that one day we can set a powerful example with these pedophiles, so that they can never again leave prison to ruin the lives of victims again."

As the memorial takes shape in Sars-la-Buissière and the investigation into Dutroux continues, the case's legacy endures—both as a cautionary tale about institutional failure and as a reminder of the work still needed to protect the most vulnerable. [L'Avenir](https://www.lavenir.net/actu/belgique/2026/08/14/certains-ne-savaient-meme-pas-qui-il-etait-30-ans-apres-le-traumatisme-de-laffaire-dutroux-sestompe-t-il-34PCAZBEQND5VDMWLJ6SEFMP6Q/) noted that while the trauma fades for younger generations, those who lived through it carry its weight still.
