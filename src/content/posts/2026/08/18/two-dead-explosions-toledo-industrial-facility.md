# Two Dead After Explosions Rock Toledo Industrial Facility

Two workers were confirmed dead Tuesday morning after multiple explosions and a massive fire tore through an industrial recycling facility in Toledo, Ohio, according to [AP News](https://apnews.com/article/explosion-toledo-ohio-1651144114894733d2706e5a79e8c4d0). The blasts occurred shortly before 5 a.m. during a shift change at Brent Industries, located in the 2900 block of South Avenue.

Toledo Mayor Wade Kapszukiewicz confirmed the two deaths, with Fire Chief Allison Armstrong identifying the victims as a man and a woman. Twelve workers were inside the building at the time of the explosions, with most escaping with minor injuries.

## The Incident

The explosions sent flames more than a hundred feet into the air and caused at least half of the building to collapse. Fire crews were able to largely contain the blaze by 6:15 a.m., but smoke continued billowing from the site for hours afterward, [13abc News reported](https://www.13abc.com/2026/08/18/explosions-rock-south-ave-industrial-facility/).

"We're working to contain the fire and account for all the employees right now," Armstrong said. "They've got hazardous materials in there, so we're dealing with chemicals and runoff and tanks that exploded."

Residents felt the blast from a significant distance. Demetrius Langston, who was half a mile away when the explosions occurred, described the experience: "The shock waves came down the block. Then we all heard it again. Boom! You could feel it."

The building contained multiple tanks of mineral spirits, a petroleum solvent used for cleaning, according to fire officials. The facility specializes in recycling and disposing of textiles containing hazardous and non-hazardous materials.

## Victims Identified

Family members identified one of the victims as Mary Allen, a mother and grandmother who loved to fish and was always the first one in for her shift at the facility. Her daughter, Lenore Bangura, expressed her grief and frustration to 13abc News.

"My mom was amazing and I'm going to miss her," Bangura said. "I'm mad as hell she came to work on time. I'm mad as hell she's so dedicated to this company that she was the first one in and the last one out."

The name of the second employee who died has not yet been released.

## A Troubled Safety History

This is not the first deadly incident at a Brent Industries facility. In March 2015, an explosion and fire at the company's Brent, Alabama, plant killed one worker and injured several others, [AP News reported](https://apnews.com/511d5feb74b940a3a4b5a3d5a7d23f88). OSHA later determined that flammable vapor from mineral spirits ignited in an industrial washing machine. The company was cited for a half-dozen violations and fined $27,000.

In 2004, OSHA also cited Brent Industries for two serious violations at its Toledo plant, including failing to protect workers from death or serious harm when exposed to a 207-degree Fahrenheit steam release from a washing machine.

Founded in Alabama in 1977, Brent Industries has operations in both Brent and Toledo. The company offers textile reconditioning and industrial recycling services aimed at reducing waste and saving money, according to its website.

## Investigation Underway

The federal Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA) has launched an investigation into Tuesday's explosions. The agency has six months to conduct its investigation and will not release further information until it is completed.

State environmental officials were also on the scene monitoring for potential impacts. Authorities encouraged people near the site who could see or smell smoke to avoid exposure by keeping windows and doors closed and avoiding the area.

Fire Chief Armstrong noted that the priority for responders was life safety and fire mitigation, with arson investigators also on scene. The cause of the explosions has not been determined.

## What's Next

As crews continue to battle the remnants of the fire and investigators begin their work, questions are emerging about the safety record of industrial recycling operations and the hazards posed by facilities that handle volatile chemicals. The deaths at Brent Industries mark a tragic reminder of the risks faced by workers in industrial settings, particularly at facilities with a documented history of safety violations.

OSHA's investigation, which could take up to six months, will be closely watched for findings on what caused the explosions and whether any safety failures contributed to the deaths of the two workers. For the families of the victims and the community in Toledo, the immediate focus remains on mourning those lost and supporting the workers who survived the ordeal.
