# China Court Sentences Father-Son Duo for Illegal Chemical Sales

A Chinese court has sentenced a father and his son to prison terms for illegally selling controlled stimulant substances overseas, in a case that originated from a tip provided by the US Drug Enforcement Administration (DEA). The Wuhan Jiang'an District People's Court in Hubei Province delivered the first-instance verdict on August 14, sentencing Xia to six years imprisonment and his son to five years and six months, both with fines, according to [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/politics/20260818/df635a69cc3b4587bff67b2c8673330d/c.html).

## Case Origin and Investigation

The case began in June 2023 when the US DEA notified China's Ministry of Public Security about a Chinese citizen arrested for trafficking sensitive chemicals. The ministry swiftly deployed Hubei provincial public security authorities to investigate, and intelligence analysis identified Xia and his son as highly suspicious individuals, [China News Service reported](https://www.chinanews.com.cn/gn/2026/08-18/10679444.shtml).

Investigators discovered that since May 2021, the father-son duo had registered multiple shell companies and hired employees to fabricate their enterprises' production, operation, and transportation capabilities on domestic and international websites. They mass-published advertisements for non-scheduled precursor chemicals to attract overseas buyers, then sourced the chemicals domestically and shipped them through international freight forwarding companies once orders were placed.

Police formed a special task force that overcame significant challenges, including concealed criminal methods and a long time span. After approximately two years of intensive investigation, authorities confirmed that the chemicals being trafficked included substances controlled under Chinese law.

## Court Findings and Sentencing

The court found that between November 2021 and July 2024, Xia and his son organized others to illegally sell controlled stimulant substances in 123 transactions, with total sales amounting to over RMB 1.69 million (approximately SGD 320,000), as [reported by China.com.cn](http://news.china.com.cn/txt/2026-08/18/content_118652730.shtml).

The defendants were convicted under Article 225 of China's Criminal Law, which addresses illegal business operations. The verdict was announced publicly on August 18, with the court imposing a six-year prison sentence and fine on Xia, and a five-year-six-month sentence and fine on his son.

## Significance of the Case

This case highlights the practical outcomes of US-China law enforcement cooperation on drug-related crimes. The DEA's initial notification to China's Ministry of Public Security predates the formal US-China Anti-Drug Cooperation Working Group established during the November 2023 San Francisco summit, yet it reflects the ongoing operational coordination between the two countries' law enforcement agencies.

The case also underscores China's evolving regulatory approach to "non-scheduled" precursor chemicals — substances not yet formally listed as controlled but with potential for diversion to drug manufacturing. The National Narcotics Control Commission has issued four warning notices since August 2019, with the most recent in May 2026 identifying eight specific chemicals at risk of diversion.

## Police Warning

Following the verdict, police reminded companies and individuals engaged in chemical business and export trade to strictly comply with the warning notices issued by the National Narcotics Control Commission in August 2019, November 2023, November 2025, and May 2026, as [Lianhe Zaobao noted](https://www.zaobao.com.sg/news/china/story20260818-9535054). Authorities urged enhanced legal awareness and strict adherence to both domestic and international regulations to prevent legal risks.

## What's Next

While the first-instance verdict represents a significant legal milestone, questions remain about whether the defendants will appeal. The specific chemicals involved and their destinations were not publicly disclosed, and the exact amounts of the fines imposed were not detailed in the reporting.

The case may signal increased scrutiny of chemical export companies in China, particularly those advertising on international platforms, and could lead to further regulatory tightening around non-scheduled precursor chemicals. The successful US-China cooperation demonstrated in this investigation may also encourage continued bilateral law enforcement collaboration on drug-related crimes.
