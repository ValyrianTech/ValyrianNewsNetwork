# China Mourns Former Premier Zhu Rongji with National Flag Ceremony

Tiananmen Square flew the Chinese national flag at half-mast on Tuesday as the nation mourned the passing of Zhu Rongji, the former Premier who served from 1998 to 2003 and was one of the most consequential economic reformers in modern Chinese history. The flag-lowering ceremony coincided with the cremation of Zhu's remains in Beijing, according to [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/photo/20260818/12bab76bde6f4dab8c373c02d6a3ea2f/c.html).

Zhu died on August 12 at the age of 98, following an illness. The official obituary, issued jointly by the CCP Central Committee, the National People's Congress Standing Committee, the State Council, and the National Committee of the Chinese People's Political Consultative Conference, described him as an "outstanding member of the CCP, tested loyal communist fighter, outstanding proletarian revolutionary and politician, and outstanding leader of the Party and state," as reported by [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/politics/20260812/4c2c72e299ef4561915d2e507393a81f/c.html).

## A Nationwide Tribute

Flags were lowered at Tiananmen, Xinhua Gate, the Great Hall of the People, the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, all provincial and municipal government buildings, the Hong Kong and Macau Special Administrative Regions, border crossings, sea and air ports, and Chinese embassies abroad. The Hong Kong SAR Government flew the National Flag and Regional Flag at half-staff at the Central Government Offices, Government House, all boundary control points, and Hong Kong International Airport, as [Bastille Post](https://www.bastillepost.com/global/article/6084861-hong-kong-to-flow-flags-at-half-staff-in-mourning-for-the-late-mr-zhu-rongji-on-august-18) reported.

Security was tightened across Beijing, particularly around Tiananmen Square, which lies along the route to the Babaoshan crematorium where top leaders are typically cremated. [Reuters](https://www.yahoo.com/news/world/articles/china-lowers-flags-tightens-security-013901678.html) reported that public expressions of grief unfolded under heightened security, scrutiny, and censorship.

## A Reformer's Legacy

Zhu Rongji's career spanned China's transformation from a planned economy to a market-oriented system. As Vice Premier (1991-1998) and Premier (1998-2003), he curbed runaway inflation, tightened tax and banking systems, and pushed through a sweeping state-sector overhaul that cost millions of jobs at unprofitable state firms. His reforms also helped clear the way for China's accession to the World Trade Organization in 2001, deepening its integration into the global economy.

Known for his direct speaking style and anti-corruption stance, Zhu famously told officials in Shanghai in 1988: "My defining trait – my guiding principle – is independent thinking. I believe I should say what I truly think." He also issued a stark warning about corruption in 1998, saying he had prepared "100 coffins - 99 for corrupt officials, and one for myself."

Zhu had his party membership revoked in the late 1950s for an incautious three-minute speech, and it was not restored for roughly two decades. Despite this early setback, he rose to become one of the most powerful economic policymakers in China's modern history.

## Public Mourning and Complex Legacy

Zhu's death prompted widespread mourning on Chinese social media, with related topics accumulating over 190 million reads within hours. Citizens spontaneously visited his ancestral home "Tian Garden" in Changsha, Hunan Province, to lay flowers. One tribute read: "Farewell, the people's good Premier, you will forever live in people's hearts."

However, his legacy remains complex. While many remember him for his reform-era dynamism and willingness to "dare to speak the truth," his state enterprise reforms caused massive job losses, with millions of workers laid off. The [RFI](https://www.rfi.fr/cn/中国/20260816-朱镕基遗体定于周二火化-天安门等地将下半旗志) reported that authorities carefully managed public mourning, reflecting the political sensitivity surrounding popular former leaders.

The deaths of popular former leaders have long presented Chinese authorities with a potential trigger for public emotion. In 1976, following the death of Zhou Enlai, tens of thousands of people gathered in Tiananmen Square, and their mourning became a protest against the excesses of the Cultural Revolution.

## International Condolences

The widespread international condolences underscored Zhu's global significance. Japanese Prime Minister Takaichi Sanae sent condolences, and the Japanese Embassy in Beijing lowered its flag to half-mast - a rare diplomatic gesture. Leaders from Australia, Singapore, Malaysia, Pakistan, Cambodia, South Korea, Saudi Arabia, Vietnam, and other nations also expressed their condolences.

## What's Next

As China concludes its period of national mourning, the flag-lowering ceremony at Tiananmen Square marks the final chapter in the state funeral for one of the most consequential leaders of China's reform era. The nation now looks to how Zhu Rongji's complex legacy - of economic transformation, institutional reform, and global integration - will be remembered and interpreted in the years ahead.
