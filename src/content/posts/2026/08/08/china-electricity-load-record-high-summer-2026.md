# China's Electricity Load Hits Record High Again This Summer

China's national electricity load reached a historic record high of 1.557 billion kilowatts on August 7, marking the fourth time this summer that peak power demand has set a new all-time record, according to [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/20260807/8452ad100bcd4e3abc5e8f587e0b3d78/c.html). The new peak represents an increase of approximately 4.8 million kilowatts over the previous record, reflecting surging energy demand amid ongoing heatwaves and accelerating economic activity across the country.

## A Summer of Records

The latest milestone continues a pattern of unprecedented electricity demand that has characterized the 2026 summer season. On July 10, national electricity load first broke its historical ceiling, reaching 1.518 billion kilowatts, up 10 million kilowatts from the previous extreme, as [IT之家 reported](https://www.ithome.com/0/975/305.htm). Within days, on July 14, the load climbed further to 1.551 billion kilowatts, with the Central China region and multiple provincial grids including Hebei South, Sichuan, Hubei, and Hunan setting new records.

By July 30, the national electricity load had reached 1.553 billion kilowatts, approximately 45 million kilowatts higher than last year's extreme, according to [IT之家](https://m.ithome.com/html/983583.htm), which covered the National Energy Administration's press conference. Now, just over a week later, the August 7 record of 1.557 billion kilowatts underscores the sustained pressure on China's power grid during the peak summer demand season.

Since July 29, national electricity load has remained at persistently high levels, with daily maximum load—excluding weekends and holidays—exceeding last year's peak values. A total of 25 provinces, autonomous regions, and municipalities, including Beijing, Liaoning, Jiangsu, and Jiangxi, have seen their electricity loads set records a cumulative 78 times.

## Drivers of Surging Demand

The National Energy Administration attributes the sustained demand growth to two primary factors: economic improvement as the main driver of electricity consumption growth, and hot weather as the direct factor pushing up load levels. China's industrial transformation and upgrading is accelerating, and new growth drivers continue to grow and strengthen, the agency stated via Xinhua.

In the first half of 2026, total social electricity consumption grew 5.3 percent year-on-year, with the primary industry up 4.9 percent, the secondary industry up 5.1 percent, and the tertiary industry up 8 percent. Guangdong province's July electricity consumption exceeded 100 billion kilowatt-hours, making it the first province in China to surpass that threshold in a single month.

**New growth drivers are reshaping the demand landscape.** Jiang Debin, Deputy Director of Statistics and Digital Intelligence at the China Electricity Council (CEC), noted that China has accelerated the development of new quality productive forces, with new growth drivers represented by high-end manufacturing and the digital economy continuing to expand. Electricity consumption in these sectors shows strong growth momentum, becoming the core engine driving overall social electricity consumption growth, as reported by [21财经](https://m.21jingji.com/article/20260731/herald/9c33d5d50ed16bf2f267a6f554120c40.html).

AI and data centers have emerged as particularly significant contributors. Explosive growth in AI applications drove first-half 2026 internet data service electricity consumption to 49.4 billion kilowatt-hours, approaching the full-year 2024 level. National daily token calls surged from 100 billion in early 2024 to 140 trillion by March 2026—a 1,000-fold increase. Charging and swapping services for electric vehicles also grew rapidly, reaching 81 billion kilowatt-hours in the first half, with June alone hitting 14.8 billion kilowatt-hours, a 57.1 percent growth rate.

High temperatures have compounded the demand surge. Air conditioning cooling load now accounts for nearly 30 percent of national electricity load, exceeding 40 percent in some provinces. In certain cities, air conditioning load has reached approximately 44 percent of total load.

## Power Supply Response

Despite the record-breaking demand, China's power supply system has maintained stability. The country's total installed power generation capacity exceeded 4 billion kilowatts as of end-June 2026, up 10.8 percent year-on-year. New energy installed capacity surpassed 1.9 billion kilowatts, providing strong support for midday peak demand. At the moment of the record-breaking load, new energy generation output exceeded 600 million kilowatts, contributing over 40 percent of peak output.

Coal and natural gas supplies remain sufficient, with coal stocks at regulated power plants exceeding 30 days of usage. The national maximum load adjustment capacity exceeds 90 million kilowatts, representing over 6 percent of maximum load. More than 530 virtual power plants have been built, with maximum adjustment capacity exceeding 18 million kilowatts, as [21财经](https://www.21jingji.com/article/20260711/herald/a27527774d974db34b650af2921756ba.html) reported on the National Energy Administration's response to the first record.

The National Energy Administration is implementing a "one province, one policy" approach to power supply assurance, strengthening monitoring and early warning systems, and coordinating with meteorological departments. The agency has committed to "resolutely guarding the bottom line of not cutting power or limiting electricity." As [Beijing News](https://m.bjnews.com.cn/detail/1784024086129154.html) noted, the CEC's Zhang Lin stated that the 2026 national power supply-demand balance is expected to remain generally balanced, though some regions and provinces may face tight supply-demand conditions during the peak summer period.

## Outlook and Implications

The National Energy Administration previously forecast that 2026's maximum national power load would reach 1.575-1.6 billion kilowatts, potentially exceeding 1.6 billion kilowatts in case of widespread extreme heat. Liu Mingyang, Deputy Director of the Electricity Department at the National Energy Administration, said at a July 30 press conference that if widespread sustained hot weather occurs, the national maximum electricity load is expected to potentially reach around 1.6 billion kilowatts, an increase of about 90 million kilowatts compared to last year.

The CEC forecasts second-half 2026 electricity consumption growth of 5-6 percent, with maximum system load projected at 1.57-1.63 billion kilowatts. As the summer peak season continues, the repeated record-breaking loads highlight the growing challenge of managing peak electricity demand in an era of rapid electrification, AI-driven data center expansion, and EV adoption.

Electricity consumption is widely considered a "barometer" of economic activity in China. The sustained demand growth reflects the country's ongoing economic recovery and industrial upgrading, with new growth drivers becoming increasingly important contributors to overall consumption. The situation also underscores the need for continued investment in grid infrastructure, virtual power plants, energy storage, and demand-side management to ensure reliable power supply in the face of evolving demand patterns.

As China navigates the remainder of the summer peak season, the question of whether the national electricity load will exceed 1.6 billion kilowatts—as forecast under extreme heat scenarios—remains a key indicator to watch. The response to this challenge will test the resilience of China's power grid and the effectiveness of its policy coordination across provinces and regions.
