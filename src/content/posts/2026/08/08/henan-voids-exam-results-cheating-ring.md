# Henan Voids Exam Results After Confirming Cheating Ring

Chinese authorities in Henan Province have confirmed that criminal cheating occurred during the written examination for the 2026 "Three Supports and One Assistance" recruitment program, voiding all results from the July 11 exam and scheduling a re-examination for August 22. According to [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/local/20260807/77f6ab932d7c4a9cac24d5408f0a3fb4/c.html), suspects colluded with exam personnel before the test to obtain the actual exam papers, organized people to prepare answers, and then transmitted responses via pre-prepared electronic devices point-to-point to pre-arranged candidates.

## Background: The "Three Supports" Program

The "Three Supports and One Assistance" initiative is a Chinese government program that recruits college graduates for two-year grassroots service positions in rural areas, including teaching support, agricultural support, medical support, and rural revitalization assistance. The program carries significant career advantages: provinces must allocate approximately 10 percent of civil service recruitment positions for program veterans, and those who complete service receive a 10-point bonus on the national postgraduate entrance exam.

In 2026, Henan Province planned to recruit 3,082 college graduates through the program, with approximately 141,000 candidates registering for the written exam on July 11. The exam tested "Public Basic Knowledge," covering law, current affairs, literature, history, geography, and technology.

## How the Cheating Was Uncovered

The cheating was not initially detected by official monitoring systems. Instead, it was uncovered by netizens manually analyzing published score data. According to a [China News Weekly investigation](https://news.ifeng.com/c/8v8OrAk50GH), several anomalies emerged in cities including Anyang and Jiyuan:

- **"High score gaps"**: Single-person positions had exactly one candidate scoring above 80, and two-person positions had exactly two candidates scoring above 80, with the next highest score dropping to the 60s—creating an unusual 20-plus point gap.
- **Suspicious score jumps**: Three candidates in Jiyuan scored between 58.75 and 59.05 in a June 27 exam, then scored between 79.12 and 87.31 in the July 11 exam—increases of 20 to 30 points in just 13 days.
- **Unusual distribution patterns**: Among 69 candidates entering interviews in Jiyuan, the top three scores were the anomalous high scorers, with the fourth highest at only 69.48.

Multiple candidates told China News Weekly that scoring above 80 on the exam was "extremely difficult." The 2026 exam was notably harder than previous years, featuring new indefinite-choice questions and a stricter scoring rule for multiple-choice items that eliminated partial credit.

## The Investigation and Confirmation

On August 2, the Henan human resources department reported suspected exam cheating to police. The Henan "Three Supports" Coordination Office issued a notice the same evening confirming initial findings. The head of the Henan Provincial Personnel Examination Center was suspended pending investigation, and a special task force was established.

Police subsequently confirmed large-scale organized criminal cheating. As [reported by China.com](https://news.china.com/socialgd/10000169/20260807/49661316.html), the cheating ring colluded with internal exam personnel to obtain the complete original test papers before the exam, organized professional answerers to prepare standard answers, and transmitted responses in real time to candidates who had paid in advance and received miniaturized electronic receiving devices.

Public security authorities have arrested the main members of the cheating organization and other suspects involved in the criminal activities. The case remains under further investigation.

## Official Response: Results Voided, Re-exam Scheduled

On August 7, the Henan "Three Supports" Coordination Office announced that all results from the July 11 written exam would be declared void to maintain the integrity and fairness of the recruitment process. A new written exam has been scheduled for August 22 from 9:00 to 11:00 AM, testing on "Public Basic Knowledge" with a maximum score of 100 points.

According to [People's Daily](https://m.sohu.com/a/1060098753_120578424), the re-registration period runs from August 10 to 13 via the Henan Personnel Examination Center website. Admission tickets can be printed from August 19, and results will be published on August 31.

To mitigate the burden on candidates, the government has established a special transportation subsidy program. Candidates who take both the July 11 and August 22 exams will receive 300 yuan for out-of-province candidates and 200 yuan for in-province candidates, distributed by local human resources departments within one month after the exam. Total subsidy costs are estimated at approximately 30 million yuan, according to [analysis published on Sohu](https://m.sohu.com/a/1060342062_122851279).

Candidates confirmed to have cheated in the July 11 exam are banned from re-registering.

## The Scale of the Re-Examination

With approximately 141,000 candidates needing to retake the exam, this represents the largest exam re-administration in Chinese history, surpassing the 2010 Guangxi civil service exam leak that affected approximately 90,000 candidates. The financial and logistical undertaking is substantial, with the government funding both the re-exam logistics and the travel subsidies for affected candidates.

## Systemic Concerns and Public Trust

Commentators have raised significant concerns about the incident. As [analysis on Sohu](https://m.sohu.com/a/1060047065_581294) noted, the case highlights that external security measures—including metal detectors, signal jammers, and electronic device screening—are ineffective if internal personnel leak exam papers.

The fact that the cheating was detected by netizens rather than official monitoring systems has raised questions about proactive oversight. The "high score gap" patterns were visible in published score data, yet no official mechanism flagged them. Experts have suggested adopting AI-assisted scoring quality monitoring, similar to systems used in the national college entrance exam, along with improved risk detection for grassroots recruitment exams.

The incident also challenges public confidence in the fairness of government recruitment exams. As one [Tencent News report](https://news.qq.com/rain/a/20260729V04BK900) noted, the pattern of "one person per position scoring above 80" in multiple cities appeared suspiciously precise, as if scores were "tailor-made."

## What's Next

The re-exam on August 22 will be a critical test of the government's ability to restore trust in the recruitment process. The Henan "Three Supports" Coordination Office has stated that the recruitment process will maintain strict qualification review throughout registration, written exam, interview, physical examination, and background checks. Officials have also warned candidates to be wary of fraudulent training scams, noting that the program does not designate study guides or authorize training institutions.

The final recruitment list is expected to be published in mid-September, with recruited personnel beginning service in late September. The outcome of the police investigation and any subsequent prosecutions will be closely watched as a measure of accountability.

For the 141,000 candidates preparing to retake the exam, the August 22 re-examination represents not just another chance at a career opportunity, but a test of whether the system can deliver on its promise of fairness. As the investigation continues, the broader question remains: how many other exams across China may be vulnerable to similar schemes, and what systemic reforms will follow to prevent them?
