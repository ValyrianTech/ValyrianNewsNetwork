# Zunyi Official Liu Dongming Probed in Guizhou Anti-Corruption Push

Liu Dongming, deputy director of the Standing Committee of the Zunyi City People's Congress and Secretary of the Honghuagang District Committee in Guizhou Province, is under investigation for suspected serious violations of discipline and law, according to an announcement from the [Guizhou Provincial Commission for Discipline Inspection](https://www.news.cn/politics/20260807/933d9c9109de44f890f0b92d0d4b4d62/c.html). The investigation was announced on August 7, 2026, and marks the latest development in China's ongoing anti-corruption campaign targeting officials at various levels of government.

## A Pattern of Successive Investigations in Zunyi

Liu's case is the third major investigation of a senior Zunyi official in 2026, following the probes of Zunyi Mayor Huang Wei in February and Vice Mayor and Public Security Bureau Director Wu Rong in March. Huang Wei, who served as mayor for six years, was [reported](https://m.mp.oeeee.com/oe/BAAFRD0000202602031519170.html) to have been active in official meetings just days before his investigation was announced. Wu Rong, who was appointed vice mayor in August 2021 and took charge of the public security bureau in March 2022, was [investigated](https://h5.ifeng.com/c/vivoArticle/v0028w6gpZwDVNjla36--7lM9xTLDd0occrzzJG5AjK7hOdQ__) on March 20.

The successive investigations suggest a coordinated anti-corruption effort targeting Zunyi's leadership. Liu holds a dual role—serving both at the city level as deputy director of the People's Congress Standing Committee and at the district level as party secretary of Honghuagang District—making him a significant figure in the city's governance structure.

## Historical Precedent in Honghuagang District

The investigation carries particular weight given the history of corruption cases connected to the Honghuagang District. Jiang Lingchun, who previously held the same positions as Liu, was investigated in December 2021 and subsequently expelled from the Party and public office in May 2022. In February 2023, Jiang was sentenced to 12 years in prison for bribery and fined 1.5 million yuan. A [documentary](https://m.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_29029987) released by the Guizhou Provincial Commission for Discipline Inspection in October 2024 detailed Jiang's corruption, highlighting his obsession with golf and how his expensive hobby led him into a web of bribery and abuse of power.

Wang Jinjiang, another former Honghuagang District Party Secretary who served from 2006 to 2012, was also sentenced to 11 years in prison for corruption. With Liu's investigation, at least three former or current Honghuagang District Party Secretaries have now faced corruption probes.

## Broader Anti-Corruption Campaign in Guizhou

Guizhou Province has experienced a significant anti-corruption crackdown throughout 2026. Between March 27 and April 29, four female department-level officials fell in quick succession, spanning the transportation, judicial, disciplinary inspection, and women's federation systems. The [wave of investigations](https://www.zaobao.com.sg/news/china/story20260430-8977070) has been described as a rare and intense anti-corruption push in the province.

## Analysis and Implications

The investigation of Liu Dongming reflects the ongoing nature of China's anti-corruption campaign at the local government level. The pattern of successive investigations targeting Zunyi officials—particularly those connected to the Honghuagang District—suggests that disciplinary authorities are conducting a thorough review of the city's leadership structure.

The dual-position nature of Liu's role means his investigation could have wide-ranging implications for both the city's legislative body and the district's executive governance. As the investigation unfolds, it may reveal further details about corruption within Zunyi's local government and potentially lead to additional probes of officials connected to the Honghuagang District.

## What to Watch For

The investigation is still in its early stages, and several questions remain unanswered. The specific allegations against Liu have not been publicly detailed, and it is unclear whether his case is connected to the earlier investigations of Huang Wei and Wu Rong. Observers will be watching for potential outcomes of the ongoing probes into Zunyi's leadership, as well as whether the anti-corruption campaign in Guizhou will expand further.

For now, the case demonstrates the continued commitment of China's disciplinary authorities to investigating officials at various levels of government, even as the campaign enters its second decade. The investigation of Liu Dongming serves as a reminder that no level of the political hierarchy is immune from scrutiny in China's ongoing anti-corruption efforts.
