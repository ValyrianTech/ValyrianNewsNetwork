# Beijing Named UNESCO-UIA World Capital of Architecture 2029

UNESCO has officially designated Beijing as the UNESCO-UIA World Capital of Architecture 2029, making the Chinese capital the fourth city to receive this prestigious honor. The designation, announced on August 5, recognizes Beijing's exceptional architectural heritage and its forward-looking approach to sustainable urban development, according to [UNESCO](https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/heritage-and-innovation-make-beijing-world-capital-architecture-2029).

## A Global Recognition of Architectural Excellence

The World Capital of Architecture is a joint initiative established by UNESCO and the International Union of Architects (UIA). Every three years, UNESCO designates the host city of the UIA World Congress as the World Capital of Architecture, following a rigorous selection process. Beijing follows Rio de Janeiro (2020), Copenhagen (2023), and Barcelona (2026) in receiving this distinction.

The designation was made upon the recommendation of the Joint Committee for the World Capital of Architecture, chaired by distinguished architect Dominique Perrault. UNESCO Director-General Khaled El-Enany expressed confidence that "the city's extraordinary architectural heritage, dynamic urban transformation, and forward-looking vision will inspire new international dialogue on the power of architecture in improving people's lives and in safeguarding shared heritage for future generations."

UNESCO selected Beijing for its "exceptional ability to bring together one of the world's richest architectural heritages with a forward-looking vision of sustainable urban development," as reported by [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/world/20260807/1b87231e26574a22bede3cd37d112b7e/c.html). The organization highlighted the city's historic urban fabric, successful regeneration projects, and vibrant architectural culture as evidence of how heritage, innovation, and community life can reinforce one another.

## Beijing's Unique Architectural Heritage

Beijing's architectural culture is described as "unique and complete" by Shi Xiaodong, Chief Planner of the Beijing Municipal Commission of Planning and Natural Resources. Rather than a collection of individual landmark buildings, the city's architectural identity is formed by an interlocking spatial order encompassing the Central Axis, the Forbidden City, hutong courtyards, and the Three Hills and Five Gardens.

With more than 3,000 years of urban development history and 800 years as a capital city, Beijing offers a distinctive model of urban development. As Shi Xiaodong explained, the city can present "how a megacity with more than 3,000 years of city-building history and 800 years of capital history can continue to respond to contemporary life and future challenges while protecting historical civilization."

Naim Fayez, UNESCO Assistant Director-General for Culture, noted in an interview with Xinhua that Beijing's historic district renewal, urban regeneration, ecological restoration, and public space construction demonstrate "how architecture can contribute to sustainable urban development while preserving cultural identity."

## A Historic Moment for Chinese Architecture

The designation coincides with a historic milestone for Chinese architecture on the global stage. Zhang Li, Dean of Tsinghua University's School of Architecture, was elected President of the International Union of Architects on July 4, 2026 — the first Chinese architect to hold this position, as reported by the [Architectural Society of China](http://www.chinaasc.org.cn/news/129630.html).

Beijing will host the 30th World Congress of Architects in 2029 under the theme "Back to Balance," promoting architecture as a bridge between culture, nature, and people. This marks Beijing's second time hosting the World Congress, following its first in 1999 when the city hosted the 20th Congress and produced the landmark Beijing Charter.

The flag handover ceremony took place on July 2, 2026, at the closing ceremony of the 29th World Congress in Barcelona, with Beijing Vice Mayor Tang Wenhong extending a sincere invitation to global architects: "In 2029, let's meet in Beijing."

## What Beijing Will Show the World

According to [Beijing Daily](https://xinwen.bjd.com.cn/content/s6a4a1dece4b03fa51a80e763.html), the city plans a year-long program of exhibitions, conferences, educational programs, and public events centered on green low-carbon development, urban renewal, and historical heritage preservation.

Cui Kai, Academician of the Chinese Academy of Engineering and Chairman of the Beijing Architects Society, said Beijing will present how traditional urban wisdom enters contemporary life, showing how architects respond to people's real needs in historical preservation, urban renewal, green development, and public space construction.

Dong Gong, Vice Chairman of the Beijing Architects Society, emphasized the broader significance: "Our goal is not only to let the world see Beijing's architecture, but also to let the world understand the culture, life and innovative power behind Beijing's architecture."

## Looking Ahead to 2029

As Beijing prepares for its three-year countdown to 2029, the designation represents more than a title — it is an opportunity to showcase a unique urban development experience on the world stage. The city aims to demonstrate how a megacity can protect historical civilization while responding to contemporary life and future challenges, offering a model for sustainable urban development that resonates far beyond China's borders.

With the World Congress approaching, the international architecture community will be watching closely to see how Beijing translates its rich heritage and innovative vision into a global conversation about the future of our built environment.
