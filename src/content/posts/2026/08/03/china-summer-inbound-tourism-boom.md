# China's Summer Inbound Tourism Boom Draws Record Visitors

China is experiencing a record-breaking summer inbound tourism boom, with international visitors flocking to experience the country's diverse attractions, cultural offerings, and increasingly, its technological innovations. According to China's National Immigration Administration, the country recorded 22.914 million inbound trips by foreign nationals in the first half of 2026, up 20.4 percent year on year, as reported by [People's Daily](https://english.henan.gov.cn/2026/07-28/3380101.html).

## Visa-Free Policies Fuel the Surge

A key driver of this growth has been China's aggressive expansion of visa facilitation measures. In the first half of 2026, 17.815 million foreign visitors entered China visa-free, accounting for 77.7 percent of all foreign arrivals and representing a 30.6 percent year-on-year increase. China now offers unilateral visa-free entry to nationals of 50 countries, has signed mutual visa exemption agreements with 29 countries, and allows nationals from 55 countries to utilize the 240-hour visa-free transit program through 65 designated ports in 24 provincial-level regions, according to [CGTN](https://www.adnkronos.com/immediapress/cgtn-why-china-has-become-a-hot-summer-destination_4ziLQJC3doOWD534zBAN88).

Direct flights now connect overseas destinations with 160 Chinese cities, and more than three-quarters of inbound travelers enter the country visa-free. The upgraded departure tax refund policy, widespread mobile payment systems, and multilingual services at major attractions have further lowered barriers for international visitors.

## Yunnan Emerges as Top Summer Destination

Yunnan Province has emerged as a standout destination, leveraging its unique highland climate to attract travelers seeking relief from summer heat. The province received 4.256 million inbound tourists in the first half of 2026, generating tourism foreign exchange income of 2.084 billion USD, up 8.6 percent and 21.9 percent respectively year on year, according to [Xinhua](http://www.yn.xinhuanet.com/20260718/fae1193cde7b40038d06cde3b59b1c00/c.html).

Kunming Changshui International Airport processed over 2 million border crossings in 2026 as of June 26, up 16.66 percent year on year, with 681,000 foreign nationals crossing and 245,000 visa-free entries. Summer inbound hotel orders in Yunnan grew 41 percent year on year, with European source market orders up 30 percent, as detailed in [Yunnan Daily](https://news.sina.com.cn/zx/gj/2026-07-29/doc-iniknrrc1623058.shtml).

The province's appeal extends beyond its climate. Vietnamese tourist Nguyen Thi Ngoc Mai, visiting Kunming, said: "Yunnan's weather is cool, people are very warm, I really like it here," as reported by [Yunnan Daily via Phoenix News](https://news.ifeng.com/c/8vGTw8UlkFe). The original [People's Daily Overseas Edition](http://paper.people.com.cn/rmrbhwb/pc/content/202608/03/content_30172669.html) article featured photos of tourists from Indonesia, Malaysia, and Israel experiencing local culture in Yunnan, from tasting guoqiao rice noodles in Mengzi to trying woodblock printing in Dali's Fengyu Ancient Town.

## European Travelers Lead the Trend

European countries now account for nearly 30 percent of China's top 20 inbound source markets for summer 2026. Travel bookings have surged 275 percent year on year, while attraction ticket orders have increased by 20 times. Visitors from Britain, France, and Germany alone account for nearly 42 percent of all European arrivals.

Destinations across China are seeing remarkable growth. Qinhuangdao received 43,000 inbound visits in the first half of 2026, up 50 percent year on year, with the average stay of overseas tourists doubling from 7 to 15 days. At the Zhangjiajie Grand Canyon Scenic Area, daily visits have approached 10,000 since early July, with inbound tourists accounting for over 50 percent of the total.

## Tech Tourism: A New Frontier

The "Chinamaxxing" trend, which has gone viral on overseas social media particularly among Western Gen Z, is driving interest in Chinese culture, lifestyle, and travel, as noted by [Global Times](https://www.globaltimes.cn/page/202602/1355100.shtml). This fascination is now extending into technology tourism, with visitors touring NEV manufacturing plants, drone facilities, and AI companies.

British content creator Simon Phillips, who has lived in China since 1998, told the [Global Times](https://www.globaltimes.cn/page/202608/1367364.shtml) that "one of the great advantages of living in China is that you often get to experience tomorrow's innovations before the rest of the world." Indian business consultant Ram Niranjan Sharma added: "In the past, people mainly travelled to China for its culture and history. Today, many foreign visitors come specifically to see what the 'world of the future' looks like."

## Ambitious Targets for 2030

The boom comes as China sets ambitious goals for its tourism sector. A new five-year tourism development plan approved by the State Council targets 190 million annual inbound tourist visits by 2030, with inbound tourism spending of more than 150 billion USD annually, according to [China.org.cn](http://www.china.cn/info/2026-07/08/content_118590968.shtml). This builds on 2025's strong performance, when China recorded more than 150 million inbound tourist visits, up over 17 percent year on year, with spending surpassing 130 billion USD.

Global tourism expanded by 4.1 percent in 2025, while China's tourism economy grew by 9.9 percent, more than double the global average. The plan calls for expanding visa-free entry programs, increasing international air and rail connections, and improving multilingual visitor services with AI-powered translation devices.

## What to Watch

As China's summer travel peak continues through August, industry observers expect inbound tourism numbers to keep climbing. The convergence of visa facilitation, cultural appeal, and technological innovation positions China as one of the world's most dynamic tourism markets. With the new five-year plan setting ambitious targets, the question is not whether China can sustain its tourism boom, but how quickly it can scale up to meet the growing global demand for Chinese experiences.
