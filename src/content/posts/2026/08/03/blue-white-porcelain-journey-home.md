# A Blue and White Porcelain's Journey Home

A blue and white porcelain piece has completed a remarkable journey home to Jingdezhen, China's fabled Porcelain Capital, after decades abroad. The story, shared by Jad Tabet, former representative of Lebanon to the UNESCO World Heritage Committee, unfolded during the 48th session of the World Heritage Committee in Busan, South Korea, and coincides with the successful inscription of the Jingdezhen Handicraft Porcelain Industry Sites on the UNESCO World Heritage List on July 25.

## A Gift Across Decades

In the 1950s, a Chinese friend gifted a blue and white porcelain piece to Tabet's father in Lebanon. Years later, Tabet rediscovered the piece and made the decision to return it to its place of origin. When he offered it to Jingdezhen, experts identified it as an original piece from the late 18th century. It now resides in a Jingdezhen museum.

"It completed a full cycle: it experienced a long journey in the world, and finally returned to its hometown," Tabet said, as reported by [People's Daily Online](http://ent.people.com.cn/n1/2026/0803/c1012-40772559.html).

The story serves as a powerful symbol of cultural exchange and heritage preservation, echoing the broader significance of the World Heritage inscription. As the [People's Daily Overseas Edition](http://paper.people.com.cn/rmrbhwb/pc/content/202608/03/content_30172690.html) noted, the porcelain's journey reflects the unique value of world heritage: transcending time and space to connect the shared memories of all humanity.

## A Historic UNESCO Inscription

On July 25, the Jingdezhen Handicraft Porcelain Industry Sites was formally inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List during the 48th session of the World Heritage Committee in Busan, South Korea, bringing China's total number of World Heritage sites to 61. The inscription marks the first Chinese industrial heritage site to receive World Heritage status and the first World Heritage site globally themed on porcelain, filling a long-standing gap in UNESCO's register for ceramic heritage.

According to [Xinhua via People's Daily Online](https://en.people.cn/n3/2026/0725/c90000-20481764.html), the heritage property comprises five component parts covering the entire handcrafted porcelain production chain: the Town Area Porcelain Production Center, the Hutian Ancient Kiln Site, the Gaoling Porcelain Clay Mining Site, the Changling Porcelain Stone Mining Site, and the Jiaotan Firewood Production Area. Together, these sites span 1,979 hectares with a 5,545.7-hectare buffer zone, totaling 7,524.7 hectares.

The [UNESCO World Heritage Committee](https://www.unesco.org/en/world-heritage/committee-2026) recognized the site as meeting criteria 2, 3, 4, and 6, noting that since the 16th century, Jingdezhen became the world's handcrafted porcelain production center. The committee acknowledged the property's portrayal of the evolution of handicraft porcelain production from the 10th to 19th centuries, with profound industrial, artistic, and trading influences in China and beyond.

## The Porcelain Capital's Global Legacy

Jingdezhen's porcelain heritage is deeply rooted in cultural exchange. Blue and white porcelain itself was born from the meeting of Persian cobalt pigment, imported to China in the early 14th century, with Jingdezhen's egg-white porcelain. As the [China Economic Net](http://district.ce.cn/newarea/roll/202607/t20260731_3120149.shtml) reported, the "binary formula" of mixing kaolin clay with porcelain stone was a revolutionary innovation that enabled firing temperatures above 1,300 degrees Celsius, creating high-temperature hard porcelain that would transform global ceramics.

The global reach of Jingdezhen's porcelain was immense. American historian Robert Finley wrote in *The Story of Blue and White Porcelain* that from the 16th century Portuguese arrival in China, at least 300 million Chinese porcelain pieces landed in Europe over the following three centuries. The binary formula eventually spread to Europe in the 18th century, influencing the development of European high-temperature hard porcelain technology.

"The Jingdezhen site highlights another important dimension that was not really represented before concerning the artisan production," Alessandro Balsamo, head of the Nominations Unit at the UNESCO World Heritage Centre, told [China Daily](https://www.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202607/25/WS6a649f35a310986e2b4674a7.html). "China's contribution to the World Heritage List is absolutely outstanding."

## An 11-Year Journey to Recognition

The World Heritage application process spanned 11 years, from 2015 to 2026, going through four distinct stages. According to the [Beijing News](https://m.bjnews.com.cn/detail/1784952528129264.html), these included initial protection (1982-2014), rescue and protection (2015-2022), application breakthrough (2023-2024), and quality improvement (2025-2026).

The scope of the application expanded significantly over time. Initially focused on the Imperial Kiln Factory, it grew to encompass the entire handcrafted porcelain production chain, from raw material extraction to kilns, workshops, and market towns. As [Guancha.cn](https://www.guancha.cn/politics/2026_07_25_825033.shtml) reported, the final nomination comprised 5 major parts, 15 categories, and 45 component elements, covering more than 900 years of continuous development across the Song, Yuan, Ming, and Qing dynasties.

Wang Tongmao, director of the Jingdezhen Municipal Leading Group Office for World Cultural Heritage Nomination, explained the significance of the interconnected heritage sites: "Together, these five interconnected sections preserve an extraordinarily intact record of how porcelain was crafted and traded between the 10th and 19th centuries, and the heritage extends far beyond physical kiln ruins; it captures the intangible soul of the craft."

## Living Heritage and Future Legacy

The inscription recognizes not just physical structures but also living traditions. UNESCO officials specifically highlighted the importance of preserving knowledge and skills alongside physical sites. As the [Beijing Review](http://www.bjreview.com.cn/China/202607/t20260731_800443284.html) noted, Jingdezhen has established the world's first Ancient Ceramic Gene Bank, with over 20 million porcelain shards excavated from kiln sites since the late 1970s, and is developing AI-powered artifact restoration technology.

Zhan Shaolin, a national-level master craftsman and official inheritor of the manual throwing technique, emphasized the living nature of the heritage: "The traditional 72 steps are divided among distinct specialists. Each step represents a lifetime commitment for the artisan practicing it."

Tabet echoed this sentiment, noting that "traditional techniques are still continuing, young people are also working hard to inherit and innovate, continuously creating new ceramic products, and these products will also become future heritage."

## What's Next

The inscription is expected to significantly boost cultural tourism to Jingdezhen, with local artisans anticipating a surge in visitors. As the [Global Times](https://www.globaltimes.cn/page/202607/1366751.shtml) reported, the city has already become a magnet for artists from around the world, with thousands of foreign "Jingdezhen drifters" drawn to its creative atmosphere.

For the blue and white porcelain that returned from Lebanon, its journey home is complete. But as Tabet observed, the piece's story is part of a larger narrative about how cultural heritage transcends borders while ultimately finding its way home. The inscription of the Jingdezhen Handicraft Porcelain Industry Sites ensures that this legacy will be preserved and celebrated for generations to come.
