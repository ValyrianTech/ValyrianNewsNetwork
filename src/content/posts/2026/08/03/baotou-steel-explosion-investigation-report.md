# Baotou Steel Explosion Report Reveals Systemic Safety Failures, 62 Held Accountable

The Inner Mongolia Autonomous Region Emergency Management Department has published its investigation report into the "1.18" major container explosion at the Baotou Steel Plate Factory, confirming that the January 18 incident that killed 10 people and injured 84 others was a major production safety liability accident. According to [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/local/20260803/56a4c98055964c63aa85ef5e4b0009bb/c.html), the explosion caused direct economic losses of 67.23 million yuan (approximately $9.4 million) and resulted in accountability measures against 62 individuals.

## The Explosion and Its Immediate Aftermath

At approximately 15:01 local time on January 18, a 650-cubic-meter saturated water/steam spherical tank in the steelmaking operations department of Inner Mongolia Baotou Steel Rare Earth Steel Plate Co., Ltd. in Kundulun District, Baotou City, exploded without warning. The blast killed six workers from Shandong Qunqi Installation Engineering Co., Ltd. who were working on site, two safety management personnel from the plate factory, and two workers passing near the tank. Eighty-four people in nearby office buildings and the factory area sustained injuries, with five in critical condition.

The [investigation report](http://www3.xinhuanet.com/politics/20260802/ae9c8f60808b49f0a1b58fe9b2732b2d/c.html) revealed a detailed timeline of events leading up to the explosion. At 13:35, a business representative discovered leakage from the No. 1 spherical tank and immediately reported it. Over the next 86 minutes, multiple personnel inspected the leak, discussed disposal measures, and even closed and then reopened the electric inlet valve. At 14:51, warning tape was set up at the site. Just ten minutes later, at 15:01, the tank exploded.

## Direct Cause: Corrosion and Failed Response

According to the investigation, the direct cause of the explosion was a combination of long-term media corrosion and local stress coupled with alternating load effects during operation. The tank's inner wall developed irreversible "alkali stress corrosion cracking + corrosion fatigue" composite damage that eventually penetrated the full wall thickness, causing saturated water to leak from the north side of the lower circumferential weld.

Critically, [no effective on-site disposal measures were taken](https://www.guancha.cn/ChengShi/2026_08_02_825890.shtml) after the leak was discovered. Approximately 1.5 hours later, the pressure exceeded the ultimate load-bearing capacity of the defective container, causing the weld near the leak to rapidly tear along the entire circumference. The tank disintegrated, triggering instant flash evaporation of the large amount of saturated water inside and causing the explosion.

The investigation identified systemic issues beyond the immediate cause, including design deficiencies in the original equipment, manufacturing defects, non-standard inspection and testing, inadequate daily maintenance, chaotic safety management, and improper on-site disposal. It also found that both Baotou city and Kundulun district party committees, governments, and relevant departments failed to adequately perform their duties.

## A Deadly Prioritization of Production Over Safety

Perhaps the most troubling finding emerged months before the full report was released. At a February 27 press conference, Li Haowen, Director of the Investigation and Statistics Department of the Ministry of Emergency Management, revealed that the steam tank had been severely leaking for a full hour and a half before the explosion. Yet, as [Southern Metropolis Daily reported](https://m.mp.oeeee.com/a/BAAFRD0000202602271530498.html), the two on-site supervisors stationed by the enterprise ordered construction to continue to avoid stopping production.

"In the one and a half hours before the incident, the steam tank had already been severely leaking, but in order to avoid stopping production, the two on-site supervisors stationed by the enterprise still ordered construction to continue, resulting in the deaths of these two supervisors and related personnel at the site," Li said. "The supervision work was not done well - it harmed others and also harmed themselves."

Both supervisors were among the 10 people killed in the explosion.

## Accountability: 62 Individuals and 4 Companies

According to [China News Service](https://www.chinanews.com.cn/gn/2026/08-02/10670793.shtml), the investigation led to comprehensive accountability measures. Seven people from the plate factory have been approved for arrest by the Kundulun District Procuratorate on suspicion of criminal offenses, while six additional people have been recommended for transfer to judicial authorities for investigation.

Four companies received administrative penalties: Baotou Steel Union, Beijing Jingcheng Kelin Environmental Protection Technology Co., Ltd., Anshan Huaxin Heavy Industry Machinery Co., Ltd., and the Baotou Special Equipment Inspection Institute. The Kundulun District Party Committee and Government were required to submit written self-criticisms to the Baotou City Party Committee and Government, while the Baotou City Party Committee, Government, and Baotou Steel Group were required to do the same to the Autonomous Region Party Committee and Government.

Party and administrative discipline measures were applied to relevant personnel across Baotou Steel Group and its subsidiaries, related units, and multiple levels of government oversight bodies, including the Market Supervision Administration, Emergency Management Bureau, and State-owned Assets Supervision and Administration Commission.

## A Troubling Safety Record

The "1.18" accident is the 21st safety incident at Baotou Steel Union in nearly a decade. According to the Ministry of Emergency Management's notification on Baotou Steel Group's production safety situation, from 2016 to 2022, Baotou Steel Union experienced 20 production safety accidents causing 28 deaths, including one major accident and 19 general accidents.

Baotou Steel is one of the earliest steel industry bases established after the founding of the People's Republic of China, with construction beginning in 1954 and production starting in 1959. The company operates two listed entities - Baotou Steel Union (600010.SH) and Baotou Steel Rare Earth - and is China's largest plate production base in the northwest region, with an annual production capacity of 9.7 million tons.

The rare earth steel plate subsidiary had been in financial difficulty, reporting a net loss of 1.306 billion yuan in 2024 and a 491 million yuan loss in the first half of 2025. The company had been pursuing aggressive "extreme energy efficiency" initiatives, raising questions about whether cost-cutting pressures contributed to safety compromises.

## Financial Impact and Recovery

Following the explosion, [Baotou Steel Union's stock hit limit-down](https://cj.sina.com.cn/articles/view/1641561812/61d83ed400101bbg2) at market open on January 19. The company's share price later recovered somewhat, trading at 2.23 yuan with a market cap of approximately 100.9 billion yuan as of August 2.

According to [Daily Economic News](https://m.163.com/dy/article/L3BUDUE00512B07B.html), the affected production lines have fully resumed operations as of August 2. Insurance claims have been filed with insurers covering property all-risk insurance, production safety liability insurance, and public liability insurance. The company has stated it will strengthen safety inspections, upgrade old equipment, strictly regulate outsourcing, and improve emergency response plans.

## Broader Implications for China's Industrial Safety

This accident underscores several critical challenges in China's industrial safety landscape. The failure of a pressure vessel due to corrosion fatigue and stress corrosion cracking highlights the importance of regular inspection and maintenance of industrial equipment. The decision to continue production despite a severe leak - driven by the desire to avoid production stoppage - illustrates the dangerous prioritization of output over safety that can occur when financial pressures mount.

The investigation's findings of failures at multiple levels of government oversight, from local to autonomous region level, suggest that regulatory gaps remain a concern. The comprehensive accountability measures demonstrate China's approach to holding both individuals and organizations responsible for industrial accidents, but the recurring nature of safety incidents at Baotou Steel Union raises questions about whether systemic improvements are keeping pace.

As the company works to recover from this tragedy and implement the investigation's recommendations, the "1.18" explosion serves as a stark reminder that in industrial operations, safety cannot be sacrificed for efficiency - a lesson that carries profound human and financial costs when ignored.
