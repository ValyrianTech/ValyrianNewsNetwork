# China Activates Level IV Flood Response for Yunnan

The National Flood Control Headquarters and the Ministry of Emergency Management activated a Level IV flood control emergency response for Yunnan Province at 9:00 AM on August 3, 2026, according to [CCTV News](https://news.cctv.com/2026/08/03/ARTIcHCqyrijApErWWRJswOb260803.shtml). The decision follows meteorological forecasts predicting persistent heavy rainfall across the southwestern province from August 3 to 6.

## Persistent Rainfall and Rising Risks

Meteorological departments forecast that Yunnan will experience continued rainfall over the coming days, with some areas expected to receive heavy to torrential rain and locally extreme downpours. The [Xinhua News Agency](https://www1.xinhuanet.com/politics/20260803/d7b9e30c08f647a9a377955e65d656b6/c.html) reported that the activation was based on the "National Flood Control and Drought Relief Emergency Plan" and relevant regulations, citing the Ministry of Emergency Management as the source.

The response was triggered by elevated risks of mountain torrents, geological disasters, and flooding of small and medium-sized rivers across the province.

## Forecast Details

According to the [Yunnan Daily](https://yndaily.yunnan.cn/html/2026/20260801/20260801_003/20260801_003_10.html), the rainfall event is driven by a low vortex shear and monsoon trough, with heavy rain expected to move from north to south across the province from the night of August 2 through August 5. Affected areas include Zhaotong, Qujing, Kunming, Yuxi, Chuxiong, Lijiang, Dali, and other prefectures and cities across the province.

Cumulative rainfall is expected to reach 40-70 millimeters, with locally 120-160 millimeters possible. Maximum hourly rainfall intensity could reach 50-70 millimeters. Thunderstorms, short-duration heavy rainfall, local 8-10 level gusty winds, and hail are expected across most of the province.

Meteorological experts noted that the heavy rainfall areas in the western and southern parts of this event highly overlap with previous rainfall events, significantly increasing the risk of small and medium-sized river flooding, mountain torrents, geological disasters, and urban waterlogging.

## Coordination and Preparedness

On the morning of August 2, the National Flood Control Headquarters Office and the Ministry of Emergency Management held a joint consultation meeting with the China Meteorological Administration, Ministry of Water Resources, Ministry of Natural Resources, and other departments to assess the recent heavy rainfall situation, as reported by [Yunnan News](https://news.yunnan.cn/system/2026/08/02/034106300.shtml). The meeting emphasized the need to strengthen monitoring and early warning systems, particularly in remote mountainous areas, small and medium-sized rivers, and mountain torrent gullies.

Officials stressed the importance of implementing layered risk assessments and ensuring that flood warnings are communicated effectively from upstream to downstream areas. The consultation also called for comprehensive risk inspections of tourist attractions, construction sites, nursing homes, tunnels, bridges, and critical infrastructure.

## Yunnan's Flood Vulnerability

Yunnan Province is one of China's most disaster-prone regions, with 94% of its territory consisting of low-latitude, high-altitude plateau and mountainous terrain. According to the [Yunnan Emergency Management Department](https://yjglt.yn.gov.cn/html/2026/tjfx_0204/4032489.html), the province has over 20,000 geological hazard risk points and 22,000 monitoring points staffed by 27,000 monitors. The province's steep slopes, deep valleys, and terrain conducive to water flow concentration make it highly susceptible to landslides and mudflows.

The 2026 annual disaster risk forecast identified the flood season as a period of heightened concern, noting that Yunnan experiences virtually all types of meteorological disasters except sandstorms. The July forecast specifically warned of high flood and geological disaster risks during the main flood season, with particular attention needed for the "three periods overlapping" of flood season, summer vacation, and holiday travel.

## A Challenging Flood Season

The 2026 flood season has been particularly severe across China. From April to mid-July, national average precipitation was 8% above the long-term average, with nearly 500 meteorological stations recording daily precipitation exceeding historical extremes. Major rivers experienced 25 numbered floods, with 609 rivers exceeding warning levels—35% more than the five-year average.

This is not the first Level IV flood response activation for Yunnan in 2026. The province has experienced multiple activations during the current flood season, including national-level responses on May 31, June 8, and July 28, as well as several provincial-level activations covering various prefectures and cities.

## What to Watch

The Level IV response, the lowest in China's four-tier flood control emergency system, signals a moderate level of flood risk requiring monitoring and preparedness. It triggers coordination among relevant agencies and deployment of monitoring and response resources.

With rainfall expected to persist through August 6, authorities will be closely monitoring river levels, geological hazard risks, and the potential for urban waterlogging. The overlapping of heavy rainfall areas with previous events increases the likelihood of compounded impacts, making continued vigilance and proactive disaster prevention essential in the days ahead.
