# Jingpo Sword Dance Goes Viral: A Cultural Phenomenon

The Jingpo sword dance, a centuries-old tradition of the Jingpo ethnic group in southwest China's Yunnan Province, has exploded across Chinese social media in recent months, captivating millions with its powerful, masculine movements and rhythmic intensity. The phenomenon, which began with an AI-enhanced version of the traditional "Munao Zongge" melody, has now spread from China's border regions to audiences worldwide.

## A Viral Moment Rooted in Tradition

Since the first half of 2026, the "Munao Zongge" song from Dehong, Yunnan has been trending across social media platforms, accumulating over 4 billion views by late July, according to [Yangzi Evening News](https://www.yzwb.net/news/wy/202607/t20260729_377369.html). The viral track, adapted from a traditional Jingpo sacrificial melody composed by ethnic artist Shi Legan in 1991, was reimagined by music producer Zhen Zhen using AI technology to synthesize vocals and add electronic elements.

The song's popularity was amplified when Yuan Zhiping, a principal dancer and first-class performer at the Yunnan Provincial Song and Dance Theatre, choreographed a Jingpo sword dance routine set to the viral track. His performances, including an impromptu appearance on CCTV's "Let's Dance!" program in May, have been shared millions of times, with netizens praising the dance's "masculine beauty" and raw energy.

## The Sword Dance: More Than Entertainment

The Jingpo sword dance, known in the Jingpo language as "Shanqian Ge" or "En Tu Quan," is a traditional male dance that evolved from combat sword techniques. Characterized by vigorous chopping, slashing, and sweeping movements, it is performed to the rhythm of double-faced drums, elephant-foot drums, and gongs. The dance was listed as one of Yunnan Province's first batch of provincial-level intangible cultural heritage items in 2006.

Yuan Zhiping emphasized the dance's cultural significance in an interview with [Beijing Daily](https://news.bjd.com.cn/2026/05/26/11767710.shtml): "Many of our Yunnan dances come from life. The long sword is both a production tool and a weapon for the Jingpo people. In my view, the sword dance has a certain aggressive and defensive quality in its movements, and its aesthetic style is definitely masculine - it wasn't created for entertainment."

## A Cultural Phenomenon Explained

The viral success of the Jingpo sword dance is not accidental. As [China News Service](https://www.chinanews.com.cn/cul/2026/05-01/10614554.shtml) reported, the Munao Zongge festival, from which the dance originates, has been a national intangible cultural heritage item since 2006. In 2012, 18,604 dancers simultaneously performed the Munao Zongge dance at Mangshi Square in Dehong, setting a Guinness World Record.

Music producer Zhen Zhen, who created the viral AI-enhanced version, explained his creative process: "Only when I resolutely decided to start over, delved into historical materials and documents, and infused the burning patriotic sentiment and the ethnic emotion of 'clinging together like pomegranate seeds' did the creative blockage gradually disappear." His comments were reported by the [National Ethnic Affairs Commission](https://www.neac.gov.cn/seac/xwzx/202605/1189937.shtml).

## The Dance Goes Global

The phenomenon has transcended China's borders. Ni Mengxiao, director of the Nigeria Huaxing Art Troupe, has obtained performance rights to bring the song to Africa, noting that "as overseas Chinese, we have the obligation and responsibility to spread and promote China's excellent traditional culture."

Meanwhile, [Kunming Information Port](https://www.kunming.cn/news/c/2026-05-06/14038862.shtml) reported that the song has accumulated over 20 billion views on Douyin alone, with netizens across the country creating their own dance videos. Dong Jianbin, director of the Dehong Jingpo Cultural Work Troupe, observed: "Although there are no traffic stars or capital backing, this song, dance, and related videos went viral under the collective effort of everyone joining in and creating."

## Cultural Identity in the Digital Age

For Yuan Zhiping, the viral fame is about more than personal recognition. "I've discovered that people really love our Yunnan ethnic minority culture, which gives me an inexplicable cultural confidence," he told [Kunming Information Port via 163.com](https://www.163.com/dy/article/KUO4AP0E0550069O.html). "Without this ethnic group, without this land, there would be no us."

Liu Yan, director of the Dehong Prefecture Culture and Tourism Bureau, emphasized the broader significance: "The connotation of Munao Zongge goes far beyond song and dance itself - it is not only an intangible cultural heritage treasure, but also carries the profound expression of the Chinese national community consciousness."

## What's Next

As the phenomenon continues to grow, Yuan Zhiping is set to bring his team to perform at Dianchi Lake's east bank, as reported by [China.com](https://ent.china.com/movie/newszh/11005281/20260802/49649165.html). The dance's journey from a border-region ritual to a global cultural sensation demonstrates how traditional heritage can find new life through digital innovation, while remaining rooted in authentic cultural expression.
