# Dazu's Heritage Shows Ignite Night Economy in Chongqing

As dusk settles over Chongqing's Dazu District, a mist-laden water curtain rises from the lake surface, projecting images of the thousand-year reclining Buddha and Thousand-Armed Guanyin rock carvings onto the night sky. The "Yinian Dazu" water curtain show marks the opening of an ambitious cultural initiative that is transforming how this UNESCO World Heritage site engages visitors after dark.

![Visitors watching the Yinian Dazu water show at Changzhou Ancient City](https://xdz.txdzw.com/UploadFiles/image/20260725/20260727093002197508.jpg)

## Heritage Meets Nightlife

Since the start of summer 2026, Dazu has deeply integrated intangible cultural heritage (ICH) performances with nighttime consumption scenarios, according to [Guangming Daily](https://epaper.gmw.cn/gmrb/html/content/202608/03/content_21071.html). Heritage items featured include Dazu Rock Carvings, the Zhong'ao Fire Dragon, Carp Lantern Dance, and Sichuan Opera — traditions that have moved from festival stages into the fabric of everyday street life.

At Changzhou Ancient City, the district's core venue, visitors gather around the lake to watch the water curtain show before being drawn into a series of traditional performances. The Zhong'ao Fire Dragon, a Chongqing municipal-level ICH item with roots in the late Ming dynasty, sees performers weaving through flying sparks and iron flower showers as the 20-meter-long dragon undulates through the crowd. Nearby, the Carp Lantern Dance — a 200-year-old tradition from Wangu Town — brings bamboo-framed fish lanterns to life, their colorful scales shimmering as dancers reenact the "carp leaping over the dragon gate" legend.

"Enjoying the cool breeze and watching good shows is more interesting than staying home with air conditioning!" one local resident told Guangming Daily. A tourist from outside the region added: "Visited several attractions during the day, and it's a different feeling at night. Coming to Dazu to see two kinds of scenery is definitely worth it!"

## A Platform for Everyday Culture

Dazu has established regular performance platforms at Changzhou Ancient City and Xiangguo Park, where residents and visitors congregate. The Changzhou Theater presents Sichuan Opera excerpts, face-changing, and fire-spitting performances daily, while Baixing Square hosts regular Zhong'ao Fire Dragon and Carp Lantern Dance parades. The heritage performances follow a schedule of "weekly activities, nightly highlights," ensuring there is always something to see.

![The spectacular iron water throwing performance at Changzhou Ancient City](https://xdz.txdzw.com/UploadFiles/image/20260725/20260727093003414795.jpg)

The initiative builds on momentum from earlier events. During the 2025 National Day holiday, Changzhou Ancient City hosted drone fireworks shows and heritage performances, with the 31.6-meter reclining Buddha image projected across the night sky. The district's first Spring Festival Lantern Festival in early 2026 attracted 230,000 visitors and established the "Changzhou Night Banquet" brand, as [Chongqing Daily](https://www.cqrb.cn/quxiantoutiao/2026-07-07/2717614_pc.html) reported in its in-depth feature on the district's cultural tourism strategy.

## Economic Impact and Regional Significance

The sustained popularity of regular heritage performances has driven comprehensive growth in nighttime consumption, according to the Dazu District Culture and Tourism Committee. "Ancient heritage blending with the bustle of everyday life has both kept visitors' footsteps and extended the consumption chain," an official said.

Dazu is building an integrated "night tour, night performance, night dining, night shopping" immersive consumption scene, centered on Changzhou Ancient City and connected to Xiangguo Park and other areas. The results are visible in the numbers: in the first half of 2026, Dazu welcomed approximately 27 million visitors — up about 10 percent year-on-year — with total tourist spending reaching 20 billion yuan, a 20 percent increase.

![The Yinian Dazu real-scene water show at Miaoshan Lake](https://xdz.txdzw.com/UploadFiles/image/20260725/20260727093003476400.jpg)

Dazu's growing prominence in regional cultural tourism was underscored in December 2025, when the district hosted the 10th Joint Meeting of the Bashu Cultural Tourism Corridor, which spans Sichuan and Chongqing. The corridor has created 31 nighttime consumption clusters across the two regions, as the [Chongqing government](https://www.cq.gov.cn/ywdt/zwhd/bmdt/202512/t20251222_15260009.html) reported.

## A Model for Cultural Preservation and Growth

The Dazu initiative represents a broader trend across China, where cities are increasingly using intangible heritage to differentiate their night economies. Chongqing, where approximately 60 percent of consumption occurs at night, has long championed its "Unsleeping Chongqing" brand.

For Dazu, the approach serves a dual purpose: it keeps ancient traditions alive by embedding them in daily life, while creating new economic opportunities. The district targets 60 million annual visitors and 60 billion yuan in tourism spending by 2030, positioning itself as a cultural living room for the Chengdu-Chongqing region.

As the water curtain rises each evening over Changzhou Ancient City, Dazu offers a compelling example of how heritage and modernity can coexist — and thrive — in China's rapidly evolving cultural tourism landscape. The question now is how far this model can scale, and whether other heritage-rich cities will follow suit.
