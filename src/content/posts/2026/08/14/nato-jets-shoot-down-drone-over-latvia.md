# NATO Jets Shoot Down Drone Over Latvia Amid Ukraine Strike

NATO fighter jets shot down a drone that entered Latvian airspace in the early hours of Friday, marking the second such interception over the Baltic state this year and underscoring the dangerous spillover effects of Russia's war in Ukraine. The drone, which crossed into Latvia's north-eastern Balvi region, was intercepted as part of the alliance's Baltic air defence mission after an 'orange' airspace alert was declared across five municipalities.

The Latvian National Armed Forces (NBS) said the drone entered Latvian territory "as a result of Russia's use of electronic warfare equipment," according to [LSM](https://eng.lsm.lv/article/society/defence/14.08.2026-nato-fighters-shoot-down-drone-in-north-east-latvia.a658766/). The interception took place in Balvi municipality, in the territory of Rugāji parish, at a relatively high altitude, meaning fragments may be scattered over a wide area. The approximate location was near Lake Boževa, roughly 20 kilometers from the nearest city.

"We know that the drone was shot down in Balvi municipality, in the territory of Rugāji parish, but since the shooting down occurred at a relatively high altitude, fragments of the drone may be scattered over a wide area," NBS spokesperson Maija Zviedre said.

## A Night of Mass Drone Warfare

The incident occurred against the backdrop of one of the largest Ukrainian drone attacks on Russian territory since the war began. Russia's Defense Ministry announced that air defense units had shot down **553 Ukrainian drones overnight across 18 Russian regions and Crimea**, according to [Meduza](https://meduza.io/en/news/2026/08/14/nato-fighter-jets-shoot-down-drone-over-latvia-ukrainian-attack-sparks-fire-at-a-major-russian-baltic-sea-port).

The attack sparked a fire at the Ust-Luga port in Russia's Leningrad region — Russia's second-largest port after Novorossiysk and the largest on the Baltic Sea, and one of the five largest ports in Europe. The fire was later extinguished with no casualties, according to Leningrad region governor Alexander Drozdenko, who added that 54 drones were shot down over the region during the attack.

More than 10 additional drones were headed toward Moscow, according to Mayor Sergey Sobyanin, who claimed they were shot down with no damage or casualties. The scale of the attack represents a significant escalation in Ukraine's long-range strike capability and its willingness to target strategic infrastructure deep inside Russian territory.

The drone that entered Latvian airspace was likely Ukrainian, involved in the mass four-hour strike on the Leningrad region, but was disrupted and redirected by Russian electronic warfare and air defences, as [Metro](https://metro.co.uk/2026/08/14/fighter-jets-scrambled-shoot-done-entered-nato-airspace-29374841/) reported. The Ust-Luga port is a vital oil-export and sanctions-evasion hub with revenues sustaining Russia's war economy.

## NATO's Response and Escalating Threat

The Baltic Air Policing mission, which has stationed NATO interceptors in Estonia and Lithuania on a rotational basis for over two decades, was [redesignated as an air defence mission](https://eng.lsm.lv/article/society/defence/09.07.2026-baltic-air-policing-mission-switches-to-air-defence-mission.a654448/) on August 1, 2026. The change reflects NATO's recognition of the growing threat from drone incursions along its eastern flank. The Turkish Air Force is currently deployed in Estonia, having taken over the mission at Ämari Air Base, while Italy leads the mission in Lithuania.

The redesignation gives the Supreme Allied Commander Europe more flexibility to make timely decisions, according to Baltic defence ministers. As [MIG Flug](https://migflug.com/jetflights/turkish-f-16-baltic-air-defence-amari-estonia-2026/) noted, "air policing" was by design a monitoring role, while "air defence" signals a more operational posture — one that plugs the fighters into NATO's integrated air and missile defence system so the Alliance can actively counter an aerial attack rather than merely escort an unannounced visitor.

NATO allies gathered on Wednesday to discuss the recent drone incursions, signalling their "full solidarity with and support for the affected Allies." The alliance stated that "Russia bears full responsibility for the airspace violations, which are dangerous and unacceptable, and demonstrate Russia's increasing tolerance for risk," adding that it "stands ready to deter and defend against any aggression," as [Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/my-europe/2026/08/14/nato-baltic-air-policing-jets-shoot-down-drone-over-latvia) reported.

## A Pattern of Incursions

This is the second time NATO jets have shot down a drone over Latvia in 2026. On June 8, French Air and Space Force Dassault Rafale fighters shot down a drone that entered Latvian airspace between Rēzekne and Kārsava, though its wreckage was never found. The first drone incident of the year occurred on March 25, when a Ukrainian drone crashed in the Krāslava region after entering from Russia.

The pattern extends beyond Latvia. In May, a Romanian Air Force F-16 shot down a drone over southern Estonia. In July, Romanian fighter jets shot down three drones over just three days, with at least one identified as a Shahed-type used by Russian forces. In June, a Ukrainian sea drone exploded in the Romanian port of Constanta after veering off course due to Russian electronic warfare interference.

Finland has also been affected. The country temporarily restricted aviation and maritime traffic in the eastern Gulf of Finland amid security concerns over the incursion, as [AeroTime](https://www.aerotime.aero/articles/nato-fighters-drone-latvia-finland-airspace) reported. This followed a similar precautionary measure on August 3.

## Civilian Impact

The airspace threat directly affected civilians. Around 1,000 pilgrims at Aglona Catholic Grammar School sought shelter in a basement during the alert, as [LSM reported](https://eng.lsm.lv/article/society/society/14.08.2026-pilgrims-in-aglona-took-shelter-in-basement-during-drone-threat.a658778/). Aglona is hosting the Feast of the Assumption of the Blessed Virgin Mary on August 14-15, the largest religious event in Latvia, bringing together thousands of pilgrims and worshippers every year at Aglona Basilica.

Mobile phone broadcasting alerts were sent to residents in the affected areas — Augšdaugava, Preiļi, Rēzekne, Balvi and Alūksne — giving them an early morning wake-up call. The alert was declared shortly before 04:00 local time and lifted about an hour later after the drone was successfully intercepted.

The NBS has deployed additional air defence units to Latvia's eastern borders and continues to monitor its airspace. The military warned that "as long as Russia's aggression in Ukraine continues, a recurrence of cases where foreign drones enter or approach Latvian airspace is possible," according to the [Latvian National Armed Forces](https://www.mil.lv/lv/zinas/latvijas-gaisa-telpa-sabiedroto-iznicinataji-notrieca-arvalstu-bezpilota-lidaparatu-0).

## Looking Ahead

Latvia is taking additional measures to counter the growing drone threat. The country is considering the acquisition of Embraer A-29 Super Tucano light attack aircraft to provide a less expensive way of intercepting slow-moving drones than using frontline NATO fighters. Latvia's eastern border drone defences are expected to be ready by the end of September 2026, and an international drone defence exercise is scheduled to take place in the country.

The precise origin of the drone shot down on Friday has yet to be definitively established, though all evidence points to it being a Ukrainian drone deflected by Russian electronic warfare. Russian claims that Ukrainian drones are launched from Latvian territory have been dismissed by Latvian media as false.

As the war in Ukraine continues to generate dangerous spillover effects, NATO's eastern flank remains on high alert. The alliance's ability to respond swiftly and decisively to airspace violations will be tested again — the question is not whether, but when.
