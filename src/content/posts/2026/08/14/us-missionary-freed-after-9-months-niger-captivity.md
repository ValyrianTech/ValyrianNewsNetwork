# US Missionary Freed After 9 Months in Niger Captivity

An American missionary kidnapped in Niger more than nine months ago has been released, his organization announced Friday, ending a harrowing ordeal that drew international attention to the escalating security crisis in the Sahel region.

Kevin Rideout, a 50-year-old missionary pilot with [SIM International](https://www.sim.org/stories/kevin-rideout-release/), "is in good health in the care of US officials," the Christian NGO said in a statement. The organization confirmed he will soon be reunited with his wife, Krista, their children, and extended family.

## Release Confirmed

President Donald Trump confirmed the release on his Truth Social platform, writing that Rideout was "back in United States Custody." "Kevin, the United States of America looks forward to welcoming you HOME. Glad to have helped!" Trump posted, according to [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/aug/14/american-missionary-kevin-rideout-niger-released-kidnapping).

[BBC News](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/ckg40jvvkp7o) reported that Rideout was traveling to the US accompanied by FBI officials. However, details of how and where he was released have not been disclosed, and the identity of the group holding him at the end of his captivity remains unclear.

## The Kidnapping

Rideout was abducted on October 21, 2025, from outside his home in the Chateau 1 neighborhood of Niamey, Niger's capital, near the presidential palace. Three armed men drove him toward the western Tillaberi region, an area known as a hotbed of militant activity, according to AP News.

No armed group claimed responsibility for the abduction. [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/14/us-missionary-kidnapped-in-niger-released-after-nine-months-in-captivity) reported in March 2026 that Rideout was believed to be held in neighboring Mali by al-Qaeda affiliate Jama'at Nusrat al-Islam wal-Muslimin (JNIM), though other reports suggested Islamic State Sahel Province or criminals connected to that group. Reports indicate he may have been transferred between groups during his captivity.

Rideout had been working in Niger as a Christian missionary for 19 years and served as a pilot for SIM International, which operates in more than 70 countries. He and his wife also worked on community development projects, including drilling wells for clean water, helping refugees, teaching literacy, and supporting widows through microfinancing enterprises, as documented by [Christianity Today](https://www.christianitytoday.com/2025/11/missionary-pilot-kidnapped-niger-naimey-sim/).

## A Deteriorating Security Environment

The kidnapping was the first of an American in Niger since 2020, when Philip Walton was abducted and later freed by US special forces. It occurred against a backdrop of escalating violence and instability across the Sahel.

Niger has long faced attacks from armed groups including jihadis linked to al-Qaeda and the Islamic State organization. The security situation deteriorated significantly following the July 2023 military coup that ousted democratically elected President Mohamed Bazoum. The junta expelled Western military partners, including US forces, and forged new security ties with Russia.

Kidnapping of foreign workers in Niger has intensified dramatically in recent years. Jihadists abducted at least 15 foreign nationals between July 2024 and April 2025, and terrorism-related deaths in the country increased by 94 percent in 2024, according to the Institute of Economics and Peace.

In June 2026, more than 30 people were killed when gunmen attacked the main airport in Niamey — the second attack that year at the strategic hub. Cities and urban centers across the Sahel have become increasingly targeted by armed non-state actors.

## Broader Implications

The release comes as the US has sought to recalibrate its approach to the Sahel. After withdrawing troops from Niger in 2024, Washington donated $2.3 million in military equipment to the country in 2026, citing a "shared commitment to security cooperation," according to [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/08/14/nx-s1-5931225/kidnapped-us-missionary-released). The US has also shifted its focus to coastal West African states including Benin, Côte d'Ivoire, and Ghana.

Experts have warned that the kidnapping economy poses a growing threat to Western interests in the region. "Kidnapping is a multimillion-dollar business for jihadis," Ulf Laessing, head of the Sahel program at the Konrad Adenauer Foundation, told [AP News](https://apnews.com/article/niger-kidnapping-jihadists-niamey-american-missionary-d480c1587c5b72a157d0613023cc9daf) following Rideout's abduction. "It also means Western donors and aid groups will pull out staff and suspend operations in the Sahel which will lead to more poverty, making it easier for jihadis to recruit."

For the Christian missionary community in Niger, Rideout's kidnapping underscored the dangers facing foreign workers in a country where Christians make up less than 2 percent of the population. "We always thought Niamey was safe because it is the capital," Moussa Djibo, a Nigerien missionary, said after the abduction. "But after the kidnapping, we realized we are not safe. They can kidnap us too."

## What's Next

SIM International has asked for privacy for the family as they "reconnect after this time apart." The organization expressed profound gratitude for the prayers and support offered worldwide throughout Rideout's captivity.

While Rideout's release brings relief to his family and supporters, significant questions remain unanswered — including who ultimately held him, how his release was secured, and whether any conditions or payments were involved. The resolution of this case, however, offers a rare moment of hope in a region where kidnapping of foreigners has become increasingly common and Western influence continues to recede.
