# Marble Sand Frog Intercepted at Chinese Port in Biosecurity Crackdown

Chinese customs authorities intercepted a live marble sand frog (Tomopterna marmorata) at a port in Tianjin, marking the first recorded discovery of the African species in mainland China and underscoring the country's ongoing efforts to prevent invasive species from entering its borders. The frog was found during inspection of an imported shipment of chrome concentrate at Tianjin Dongjiang Customs, according to [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/politics/20260814/e567acfa69fb4938a3a6926f1b2c0173/c.html).

## A Camouflaged Stowaway

The marble sand frog, measuring approximately 39 millimeters in length, was identified by the Tianjin Customs Animal and Plant and Food Testing Center. Customs inspection personnel noted that the frog's slender brown body, which bears no significant markings, made it highly camouflaged against the surrounding ore sand environment.

"Without careful inspection by the customs officers, it would be very easy to overlook," inspection personnel said, as reported by Xinhua.

The marble sand frog is native to East and southern Africa, with its natural range spanning Botswana, Kenya, Malawi, Mozambique, South Africa, Zambia, and Zimbabwe, according to [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marbled_sand_frog). The species typically inhabits tropical savannas and subtropical shrubland areas, along with rivers, freshwater marshes, and agricultural lands.

## Ecological and Health Risks

Authorities expressed concern that the frog's strong adaptability could disrupt local ecological balance if it were to establish a population in China. According to research from the World Organisation for Animal Health, wild frogs in tropical regions are susceptible to chytrid fungus and may carry various parasites and other pathogenic microorganisms. If the frog carried harmful pathogens, it could potentially spread diseases and threaten native flora, fauna, and human health.

The intercepted frog was subjected to harmless treatment, and the container and cargo were comprehensively disinfected, customs officials confirmed.

## Broader Biosecurity Campaign

This interception is part of a wider effort by Chinese authorities to strengthen border biosecurity. In July 2023, the Customs General Administration launched a "Three-Year Special Action Plan to Strictly Prevent Invasive Species Invasion," as reported by [Kunming Radio and TV](https://radiokm.com/life/212.html). The initiative has intensified port quarantine measures and cracked down on illegal introduction of alien species.

More recently, a "List of Key Controlled Alien Species at Ports" jointly issued by six government departments, including the Customs General Administration and the Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Affairs, took effect on May 1, 2026, establishing a regulatory framework for managing high-risk species at entry points.

The scale of the challenge is significant. According to the China Invasive Alien Species Database, 669 species of alien organisms have invaded China's agricultural and forestry ecosystems, with over 100 species causing significant damage, as [Dute News](https://www.dutenews.com/n/article/7064654) reported. Biological invasions cause direct and indirect economic losses of approximately 200 billion yuan (about $28 billion) annually.

Beijing Customs reported intercepting 735 species and 2,432 instances of alien species as of May 2026, ranking among the top customs offices nationwide. Customs authorities across the country have intensified efforts to intercept invasive species smuggled through postal and passenger channels, uncovering increasingly sophisticated concealment methods. Recent cases include live beetles hidden in toy packages, centipedes concealed in gum containers, and tortoises strapped to passengers' bodies, as reported by [Southern Metropolis Daily](https://m.mp.oeeee.com/a/BAAFRD0000202607221630517.html).

## Official Warnings on Exotic Pets

The interception comes amid growing official concern about the illegal trade in exotic pets. Customs General Administration Director Sun Meijun warned at a State Council Information Office press conference on July 22 that "exotic pets should not be pampered; cute pets are not always cute," urging the public to resist illegal importation of live animals, as reported by Southern Metropolis Daily.

Customs authorities reminded that under China's Biosecurity Law and the Law on Entry and Exit Animal and Plant Quarantine, no unit or individual may introduce, release, or discard alien species without approval. Import companies are required to strictly fulfill quarantine declaration obligations and implement preventive treatment for goods and transport vehicles.

## Significance and Outlook

The discovery of the marble sand frog in a bulk commodity shipment highlights that invasive species can enter through channels beyond passenger luggage and postal parcels, which are more commonly highlighted in media coverage. The frog's presence in chrome concentrate demonstrates the need for continued vigilance across all import pathways.

As China's three-year special action plan approaches its conclusion, the interception serves as a reminder of the persistent threats posed by biological invasions and the importance of maintaining robust biosecurity defenses at the country's borders. The case also reinforces the need for international cooperation in addressing the global challenge of invasive species spread through trade networks.
