# Chassepierre Street Arts Festival Draws 25,000 Expected Visitors

The International Street Arts Festival in Chassepierre, Belgium, is set to welcome approximately 25,000 visitors this weekend for its 52nd edition. The festival, which runs from Friday 14 through Sunday 16 August in the small village of Florenville, will feature around 50 professional companies and between 250 and 300 artists from Belgium, France, Germany, and the Netherlands, according to [RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/25-000-personnes-attendues-au-festival-international-des-arts-de-la-rue-de-chassepierre-11769073).

## A Village Transformed

For 363 days a year, Chassepierre lives at the peaceful rhythm of one of Wallonia's most beautiful villages — a handful of stone houses clinging to the Semois river valley, with gîtes, two restaurants, and forest paths. But for one weekend each August, the village of roughly 400 inhabitants is transformed into a giant open-air theater, as [L'Avenir](https://www.lavenir.net/lifestyle/2026/08/03/pourquoi-25-000-personnes-envahissent-ce-petit-village-de-wallonie-chaque-ete-FEO5KL3EO5C2ZLCUI5RPOSYTXM/) describes. Prairies fill with spectators, gardens become dressing rooms, orchards host trapeze artists, and narrow lanes turn into stages under the open sky.

This year's edition is built around the theme "Se Ressouvenir" (Remembering), inviting audiences to consider memory as a source for imagination. The festival, which is recognized as one of the oldest street arts festivals in Europe, traces its origins to 1974, when poet Marie Fizaine invited 49 artist friends to the village for a poetry festival. In 1980, under the leadership of Alain Schmitz, it took a decisive turn toward international performances and street theater.

## Program Highlights

More than 200 performances will unfold across 17-18 venues — streets, fields, tents, and even the waters of the Semois river. Among the standout offerings this year is "Mizu," a performance by Cie Furinkai & Théâtre de l'Entrouvert featuring a life-size puppet made entirely of ice that melts as the show progresses. As festival director Charlotte Charles-Heep explained to [RTBF Musiq3](https://www.rtbf.be/article/le-festival-des-arts-de-la-rue-de-chassepierre-quand-le-theatre-le-cirque-et-la-musique-font-revivre-les-souvenirs-le-temps-d-un-week-end-11768888), the piece explores impermanence through a relationship between the ice puppet and a live actress.

The program also includes "Le Virevoltant" by Girotopia, an authentic 1912 flying chair carousel restored and set in motion, and "Le Dernier Carrousel," a new aquatic carousel created by Cie s'Enfabrik on the Semois. Brussels artist Guillaume Slizewicz presents "Aussi léger que le soleil sur l'eau," a digital work imagining what would have happened if a 1974 hydroelectric dam project at Chassepierre had been built. Music features prominently as well, with performances from Belgian duo Hills of Belgium, Balkan brass band Pufulet Orkestar, and Floris and the Flames, among others.

"From one edition to another, it's never the same program," Charles-Heep told RTBF. "We'll find theater, dance, puppets, music, and sideshows." The festival maintains its family-friendly spirit while offering shows with different levels of reading — some more dynamic, others more poetic or intellectual.

## Weather and Logistics

Organizers have been preparing for the event for over a week, marking out locations, setting up stands, bars, dressing rooms, and tents. With summer heat expected, the festival has implemented several adaptation measures: shaded areas accessible to the public, free water for refilling bottles, and an increased number of stewards equipped with misters. Some shows have been scheduled for evening hours to avoid the peak heat of early afternoon.

"One of the strengths of street arts is that they are shows that are supposed to be able to adapt to the weather, whether it rains, snows, blows, or is hot," Charles-Heep noted. A meteorologist monitors conditions throughout the weekend, able to predict changes to within half an hour.

## The Chassepierre Spirit

What draws 25,000 people to this tiny village each summer? Festival director Charlotte Charles-Heep attributes it to what she calls the "Chassepierre spirit." "It's a very family-friendly festival where you find all disciplines: circus, theater, dance, puppets, music, processions," she said. The festival's unique character also comes from its integration with the setting. "We think about the shows by integrating them into the location. The technique is almost invisible. The setting fully participates in the experience. We're in a bubble."

The festival is supported by more than 300 volunteers from the region and even from France, and operates under a contract-program with the Fédération Wallonie-Bruxelles that requires only professional companies, with 25% being Belgian. The event is also certified Access-i for accessibility to people with reduced mobility, and maintains eco-responsible practices including waste sorting.

For those planning to attend, Charles-Heep offers practical advice: "Coming thinking you'll just let yourself be carried along rarely works. Spectators often settle in three-quarters of an hour before shows. Better to spot a few appointments, then let yourself be surprised in between."

An opening evening on Friday 14 August features a performance by ADK followed by a concert from Ambassadeur Savon et son Orchestre, before the official festival launch on Saturday at 2 PM.
