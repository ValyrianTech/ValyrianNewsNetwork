# Belgium's Largest Flea Market Draws 200,000 Visitors

The 47th edition of Belgium's largest flea market in Temploux, near Namur, attracted approximately 200,000 visitors over the weekend of August 22-23, according to organizers. The annual brocante, recognized as the biggest of its kind in the country, featured more than 700 stallholders spread along a 6-kilometer route through the village streets, as [VRT NWS reported](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/08/23/brocante-temploux-rommelmarkt/).

![Fireworks cancellation notice at Temploux brocante](https://www.temploux.be/brocante/_media/img/large/2-2.jpg)

## A Village Transformed

For one weekend each August, the small village of Temploux — home to just over 2,000 residents — is transformed into what many describe as the "capital of Belgian flea markets." The scale is staggering: roughly 1,200 stands operated by 700 to 800 stallholders line the streets, staffed by some 900 volunteers drawn from village associations, according to the [official Temploux website](https://www.temploux.be/brocante/index-nl.html).

What sets Temploux apart from other markets is its strict quality standards. As [RTBF noted](https://www.rtbf.be/article/temploux-la-plus-grande-vraie-brocante-de-belgique-ou-le-neuf-est-banni-9383961), the brocante bans new items, clothing (except vintage), and militaria. Patrol teams of volunteers walk the aisles throughout the weekend to ensure vendors comply. "We make it a point of honor that this is a true flea market," former organizer Isabelle Beudels told RTBF in 2016. "We only sell things that have already lived and to which we give the possibility of living another life."

## A Magnet for Collectors

The event's reputation extends well beyond Belgium's borders. About 20 percent of visitors come from abroad — the Netherlands, France, Germany, and even Switzerland — drawn by the promise of authentic antiques and rare finds. The [City of Namur](https://www.namur.be/fr/loisirs/grands-evenements/ete/brocante-de-temploux) describes the event as "unique in Europe" for its size and quality.

Stéphane Vanhandenhoven, an antique dealer and buyer on the RTBF television show "Affaire conclue," was on hand to appraise objects for visitors. "There are 6.5 kilometers of flea market, with aisles on both sides," he told [RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/pourquoi-la-brocante-de-temploux-est-elle-a-ce-point-incontournable-la-reponse-de-stephane-vanhandenhoven-11772517). "There is nothing new, no clothes. It's not a second-hand clothing sale, but a quality flea market."

## More Than Just a Flea Market

Beyond the stalls, the weekend offered a rich program of side attractions. A comic strip festival took place under a marquee on the village square, while the Brick Mania Lego exhibition filled the sports hall. Sunday featured a parade of air-cooled classic Volkswagens, including the iconic Cox. Live radio broadcasts from VivaCité and Viva+ studios added to the festive atmosphere on the Place Madelin.

The traditional Saturday night fireworks were cancelled this year due to drought and fire risk, as confirmed by the [organizers](https://www.dhnet.be/dernieres-depeches/2026/08/10/plus-de-180000-personnes-attendues-les-22-et-23-aout-a-la-47e-brocante-de-temploux-DJSKT4KZE5CBJHATDGAWMZHBOA/).

## A Growing Legacy

The Temploux brocante has come a long way since its humble beginnings in 1978, when members of the village fancy-fair organized a small market on the Place Madelin. Over nearly five decades, it has grown into one of the most prestigious flea markets in Europe, earning the "Francis Laloux 1999" prize and the Gaillarde d'Argent 2018 from the Comité Central de Wallonie.

The economic impact on the region is substantial. The event consumes 5 tonnes of fries and 4.5 tonnes of meat, along with 30,000 liters of beverages — a third of it beer, as [RTBF detailed in 2023](https://www.rtbf.be/article/temploux-bien-plus-qu-une-simple-brocante-11229928). Profits are redistributed to village associations, making the brocante a vital community institution.

## What's Next

Organizers had anticipated between 180,000 and 200,000 visitors ahead of the event, and the final turnout appears to have met those expectations. The [RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/plus-de-150-000-visiteurs-pour-la-brocante-de-temploux-un-rendez-vous-incontournable-pour-les-collectionneurs-11774167) reported between 150,000 and 200,000 attendees, with Sunday drawing the largest crowds after Saturday's weather kept some visitors away.

With its 48th edition already scheduled for August 21-22, 2027, Temploux's transformation into Belgium's flea market capital shows no signs of slowing down. For collectors, bargain hunters, and curious visitors alike, it remains an unmissable date on the cultural calendar.
