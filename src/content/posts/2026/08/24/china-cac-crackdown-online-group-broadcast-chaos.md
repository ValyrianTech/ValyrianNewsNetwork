# China's CAC Cracks Down on Online Group Broadcast Chaos

China's Cyberspace Administration (CAC) has announced significant progress in its nationwide crackdown on chaotic group broadcast practices in the online entertainment sector, disposing of more than 7,200 irregular live rooms and punishing over 2,200 accounts since the special action was launched in July. The [Xinhua News Agency](https://www.news.cn/politics/20260824/f52545768be24f0dbb98f503323aba99/c.html) reported that the regulator has also guided platforms to clear out problematic group broadcast MCN institutions, published 17 governance announcements, and publicly exposed typical cases to deter further violations.

## The Special Action

The "Clear and Bright · Online Entertainment Group Broadcast Chaos Rectification" special action was launched nationwide on July 3, 2026, as part of the 2026 "Clear and Bright" series of regulatory campaigns. According to the [official CAC notice](https://www.cac.gov.cn/2026-07/03/c_1784735652192477.htm), the two-month initiative targets six categories of problems: irregular account registration, improper PK (player kill) tipping stimulation, manipulative gameplay inducing tips, vulgar content, infringement of minors' rights, and mismanagement by MCN institutions.

[ITHome](https://www.ithome.com/0/993/378.htm) reported that the enforcement represents a significant escalation from earlier actions. As of August 3, the CAC had already disposed of more than 1,840 irregular group broadcast accounts and reported typical cases. The latest figures, announced on August 24, show the enforcement has more than tripled in scope.

## Platform Cooperation

During the special action, the CAC has emphasized both enforcement and standardization, working with major platforms to refine review mechanisms and strengthen account management. [CCTV News](https://news.cctv.com/2026/08/24/ARTIdaDYieb4OQYkd2c8zz7T260824.shtml) detailed the platform-specific measures:

- **Douyin** is promoting MCN institution information display on group broadcast account homepages and organizing training for group broadcast institutions
- **Kuaishou** has refined group broadcast review standards and strengthened technical capabilities to improve recognition accuracy for vulgar gameplay
- **WeChat Video** launched identity verification for suspected minor group broadcast participants
- **Bilibili** removed multiplier points, gradient bonuses, and "ranking rush" tipping-stimulating mechanisms
- **Xiaohongshu** is taking measures like traffic restriction and strictly managing improper behaviors

## Industry Self-Regulation

The regulatory push coincides with broader industry efforts toward standardization. In January 2026, the China Performance Industry Association released the first group broadcast management standard, the *Network Performance Group Live Streaming Operation Management Requirements* (T/ZGYC 011—2026). As [Yangcheng Evening News](https://wap.ycwb.com/ikimvkjtic/content_53932610.htm) reported, the standard covers institutions, personnel, content, technology, costumes, safety, and social responsibility.

At an August 18 symposium jointly hosted by the association and Douyin Live, Pan Yan, Vice President and Secretary-General of the China Performance Industry Association, described the standard as "the first domestic special standard targeting the group broadcast format, filling the standard gap in the group broadcast field, and an important step for the group broadcast format to move toward standardization, professionalization, and high-quality development." The [China Economic Net](http://district.ce.cn/newarea/roll/202608/t20260820_3159035.shtml) reported that Wu Fangmei, Director of the Network Division at the Ministry of Culture and Tourism's Market Management Department, noted that group broadcast is "accelerating its shift from 'traffic-driven' to 'content-driven,' entering a value transformation stage."

## Analysis and Implications

The scale of enforcement underscores the extent of the problem in China's rapidly growing group broadcast sector, which has seen explosive popularity since 2024. The format—where multiple hosts perform together in a single live stream—has created new economic opportunities but has also been associated with vulgar content, manipulative tipping schemes, and exploitation of minors.

The multi-stakeholder approach—combining CAC enforcement, platform cooperation, and industry self-regulation—signals that Chinese regulators view group broadcast as a priority area requiring sustained oversight. Platforms face continued pressure to improve content moderation capabilities, while MCN institutions operating in this space face heightened compliance requirements.

The CAC has stated it will continue to maintain a high-pressure enforcement posture, increase the investigation and punishment of irregular accounts and institutions, and guide platforms to enhance technical governance capabilities. The regulator aims to promote "a healthy, orderly, and clean group broadcast ecosystem."

## What to Watch

As the special action approaches its September 3 conclusion, key questions remain: Will the initiative be extended beyond its initial two-month period? What long-term regulatory framework will emerge for group broadcasts? And how will the industry balance commercial viability with the push toward "content-driven" rather than "traffic-driven" models? The answers will shape the future of one of China's most dynamic—and most scrutinized—digital entertainment sectors.
