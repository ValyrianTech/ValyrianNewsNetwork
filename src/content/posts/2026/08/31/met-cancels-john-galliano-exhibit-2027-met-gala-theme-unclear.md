# Met Cancels John Galliano Exhibit, 2027 Met Gala Theme Unclear

The Metropolitan Museum of Art and fashion designer John Galliano jointly announced Monday that the planned exhibition "John Galliano: Horizons" will not proceed as scheduled, leaving the theme for the 2027 Met Gala uncertain. The decision follows weeks of intense backlash from Jewish community leaders and others over Galliano's past antisemitic and racist statements, according to [AP News](https://apnews.com/article/met-gala-exhibit-john-galliano-c7677f318cdd038b45804a8810ff94ef).

The exhibition, originally announced on July 31, was set to run from May 9, 2027, through January 9, 2028, and would have made Galliano only the third living designer to receive a solo show at the Costume Institute, following Yves Saint Laurent in 1983 and Rei Kawakubo in 2017. The museum confirmed that information on a new spring Costume Institute exhibition and the 2027 Met Gala will be announced at a later date.

## A Joint Decision After Mounting Pressure

In a statement posted on Instagram, Galliano said: "After much reflection and discussion with The Met and all those involved, I have decided, with great sadness, that it is best for the exhibition not to take place at this time." He added that he remains "fully accountable for the pain caused by my words in the past, particularly the hurt I caused to the Jewish and Asian communities."

"I understand that meaningful atonement is not achieved through an exhibition, institutional recognition or a single public gesture," Galliano continued. "It is a continuing responsibility, demonstrated through listening, learning and one's conduct over time."

Max Hollein, director and CEO of The Metropolitan Museum of Art, said in a [statement](https://www.metmuseum.org/press-releases/spring-ci-2027-announcement): "Following thoughtful discussions with John Galliano, we have together decided not to proceed with the exhibition."

Anna Wintour, Vogue's global editorial director and a Met trustee who has been a longtime friend and supporter of Galliano, praised the designer's decision. "John's decision that the exhibition devoted to his work should not take place at this time is very courageous and a measure of the man he is," she said. "I believe this is the right decision, and both he and the Museum have my full support."

Andrew Bolton, curator in charge of the Costume Institute who organized the planned exhibition, also expressed support for the decision, saying he believes "deeply in the responsibility of museums to examine difficult histories with rigor, candor, and care."

## A History of Controversy

Galliano was at the pinnacle of the fashion world for nearly 15 years as creative director of Christian Dior before a series of antisemitic and racist comments from 2010 and 2011 surfaced and went viral, including a video of him saying "I love Hitler" in a Paris bar. He was fired from Dior and his eponymous label, and in September 2011 was convicted by a French court for public insults based on race, religion, ethnicity, or origin.

After several years of rehabilitation for alcohol and drug addiction, Galliano made a comeback in 2014 when he was hired by Paris fashion house Maison Margiela, where he remained for a decade. The 2024 documentary "High & Low - John Galliano" by Kevin Macdonald examined his redemption arc, and in 2026 he sat front row at Dior and announced a creative partnership with Zara.

Despite these efforts at rehabilitation, the planned Met exhibition drew immediate criticism. The museum had held discussions with Jewish community leaders before announcing the show, hoping to ward off anticipated backlash, but opposition only intensified. According to [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/fashion/2026/aug/31/met-gala-john-galliano-cancelled), some Met donors reportedly threatened to withdraw gifts to the museum if the exhibition went ahead.

## Timing and Symbolism

The controversy was compounded by the timing of the 2027 Met Gala, traditionally held on the first Monday in May. In 2027, that date falls on May 3, which coincides with Yom HaShoah, Israel's Holocaust and Heroism Remembrance Day. The prospect of honoring a designer convicted of antisemitic hate crimes on a day of Jewish remembrance proved particularly fraught.

New York City Council Speaker Julie Menin, who is Jewish and the daughter of a Holocaust survivor, was among the most vocal critics. "After my numerous and lengthy meetings and conversations with The Met, I am pleased that they have agreed to not honor John Galliano," Menin said in a statement. "From the beginning, I have told them that I strongly disagreed with this decision, and at this rising time of antisemitism, it had served as a gut punch to the Jewish community."

Brad Lander, a Democratic congressional candidate from Brooklyn, was similarly direct: "This seems very straightforward to me. They should cancel the show and change the Met Gala honoree."

However, not all Jewish leaders opposed the exhibition. Jonathan Greenblatt, CEO of the Anti-Defamation League, told The New York Times: "We believe John Galliano has genuinely worked through the issues that led to his antisemitic outburst years ago in Paris, and we have long since accepted his apology."

## Institutional Dilemma

The cancellation raises broader questions about how cultural institutions should handle artists with troubled pasts. The Met's original approach was to structure the exhibition in three parts, including one that would "directly address the rupture caused by his antisemitic, racist, and anti-Asian conduct." But the museum ultimately concluded that the ethical concerns outweighed the artistic merits.

As [Variety](https://variety.com/2026/film/news/john-galliano-met-gala-canceled-2027-racism-backlash-1236847252/) reported, the Met's board of trustees had been scheduled to meet Monday morning to discuss the exhibition before the cancellation was announced. The decision was reached jointly, suggesting a negotiated resolution between the museum and the designer.

WWD noted that the cancellation comes at a particularly sensitive moment, with antisemitism at crisis levels in New York and across the country. The Anti-Defamation League's Greenblatt said: "With antisemitism at crisis levels, The Met honoring John Galliano at this moment was misguided, and even Galliano ultimately recognized what the Met should have done from the start."

## What's Next

With the Galliano exhibition cancelled, the Met must now identify a new theme for the 2027 Met Gala, one of the most anticipated events on the fashion calendar and a critical fundraising opportunity for the museum. The museum has said details will be announced at a later date, but the search for a replacement comes with significant logistical challenges.

For Galliano, the cancellation represents another chapter in a complex legacy. In his statement, he expressed gratitude to the Met and its leadership, saying he remains "profoundly indebted" to those who guided him through "fifteen years of sobriety and recovery from alcoholism and addiction." He is scheduled to return to the fashion spotlight this fall with his first collection as part of a two-year creative partnership with Zara, with a launch event planned for September 30 during Paris Fashion Week.

As Dana Thomas, author of "Gods and Kings: The Rise and Fall of Alexander McQueen and John Galliano," observed: "The fashion business has a short memory. It is also remarkably forgiving of bad behaviours." Whether the broader cultural world shares that forgiveness remains an open question.
