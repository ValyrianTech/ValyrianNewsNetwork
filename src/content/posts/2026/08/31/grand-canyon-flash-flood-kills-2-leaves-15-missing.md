# Grand Canyon Flash Flood Kills 2, Leaves 15 Missing as More Storms Loom

A major flash flood swept through Bright Angel Canyon and the Phantom Ranch area of Grand Canyon National Park on Saturday, killing at least two people and leaving approximately 15 missing or unaccounted for, according to [AP News](https://apnews.com/article/grand-canyon-flash-flood-bright-angel-arizona-b50ddf9b2f058cb23ad12087f0d938e4). The flood struck at approximately 2:30 p.m. MST on August 29, forcing the evacuation of more than 60 people from the canyon depths and causing extensive damage to critical infrastructure.

The [National Park Service](https://www.nps.gov/grca/learn/news/significant-flash-flooding-closes-phantom-ranch-and-north-kaibab-trail.htm) confirmed that the body of a 46-year-old man was recovered Sunday evening near Crystal Rapids along the Colorado River, with a second body confirmed recovered on Monday. Search and rescue operations continue as authorities brace for additional severe weather that could trigger further flooding.

## A Sudden Wall of Water

Witnesses described a terrifying scene as four "waves of rainfall" over two creek drainages caused water levels to rise more than 5 feet within 10 minutes. About half an inch to 1 inch of rain fell within 20-30 minutes on the steep, rocky terrain, according to National Weather Service meteorologists.

Parth Desai, a hiker rescued from the flood, told [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/aug/31/grand-canyon-storms-flash-flood) that he and fellow hikers saw "the creek flood, waterfalls coming up out of nowhere, boulders, mud, landslides happening everywhere." As a 4-foot debris wall headed toward his group, a colleague shouted "Up, Up, Up, Up!" and they scrambled into a patch of cacti before being rescued by helicopter.

Erica Viola, camping at Bright Angel Campground, described the chaos: "We just heard people yell, 'Run, run, run!' And there's 40 of us running through this deep, muddy water, trying to make it to the bridge. Once we got to the bridge, that's when this 15-foot wall of water came through the campground in Phantom Ranch and just wiped out all the bridges in the creek."

David Gregory, evacuated from Phantom Ranch, said the experience was harrowing: "It was like a whirlpool in there, with all the debris circling around, cabins and other equipment from the maintenance work site. I don't think we would have made it out alive … I think it would have been game over."

## Widespread Infrastructure Damage

The flood destroyed nearly all footbridges spanning Bright Angel Creek, eliminating hiker access across the creek, and forced the closure of Phantom Ranch, Bright Angel Campground, the North Kaibab Trail, and the Black and Silver Bridges until further notice. The Colorado River was also closed to river traffic due to metal structures and debris entering the waterway.

Most critically, the flood caused significant damage to the Transcanyon Waterline — the only water pipeline serving the park. According to [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/grand-canyon-national-park-flood-evacuation-helicopter-rescue-phantom-ranch/), preliminary assessments indicate approximately 40% of the pipeline was damaged or destroyed. The pipeline serves 6 million visitors per year and roughly 1,600 residents of Grand Canyon Village.

## Water Restrictions and Hotel Closures

With the waterline out of service, the park implemented Stage 4 water restrictions on the South Rim beginning Monday. All overnight hotel accommodations at park concessions — including El Tovar, Bright Angel Lodge, Maswik Lodge, Phantom Ranch, Yavapai Lodge, and Trailer Village — were halted until further notice, as reported by [ABC News](https://abcnews.com/US/20-missing-unaccounted-grand-canyon-after-flash-flood/story?id=136074360). Only dry camping is permitted, and fires are prohibited in the inner canyon and South Rim areas.

"These measures are crucial for ensuring the safety and sustainability of water resources," the park said in a statement. Halting overnight stays due to lack of water is rare, although the pipeline has frequently failed over the years.

The damage is a major setback to a $208 million rehabilitation project of the pipeline that began in 2023 and was expected to be completed next year. Jack Schmidt, director of the Center for Colorado River Studies at Utah State University, expressed concern about the financial implications: "Heaven help us if the whole thing down there at the bottom has to be reconstructed. Where are they going to get the money?"

## Search and Rescue Efforts Continue

The [National Park Service](https://www.nps.gov/grca/learn/news/public-assistance-requested-to-account-for-individuals-following-grand-canyon-flash-flood.htm) is asking the public for information about approximately 15 individuals who may be missing or unaccounted for following the flood. Anyone who knows of hikers or backpackers who were in the inner canyon along the Bright Angel Creek corridor on August 29 is urged to contact the NPS Investigative Services Branch.

More than 60 people have been airlifted from the canyon depths by helicopter, with the Arizona Department of Public Safety assisting in evacuation efforts. The American Red Cross is coordinating assistance at the Maswik Lodge Cafeteria on the South Rim.

Arizona Governor Katie Hobbs expressed her condolences: "My heart is with the families and loved ones of every person unaccounted for after the flash flooding at the Grand Canyon."

## Monsoon Season and Fire Scar Raise Concerns

Visiting the Grand Canyon can be particularly dangerous in the summer because of the risk of flash flooding that accompanies the monsoon season. The area receives less than 10 inches of rain per year on average, but clear days can give way quickly to thunderstorms and downpours.

Adding to the concern, rain also fell on part of the Dragon Bravo fire scar — a burn area left by a July 2025 wildfire that torched more than 227 square miles. The fire destroyed vegetation and dried the land into an impermeable surface incapable of absorbing water.

"With it being in the location that it is, it's only going to add to our concern with flash flooding," said Paige Konieczny, a meteorologist with the National Weather Service in Flagstaff.

## What's Next

Weather forecasters warn that additional thunderstorms and showers are possible, which could trigger more flash flooding, debris flows, and rockfall. A flash flood watch remains in effect for northern and central Arizona.

The incident follows a similar tragedy in August 2024, when a woman was swept away in a flash flood while hiking in the Havasupai region of the Grand Canyon. As search and recovery efforts continue, park officials stress that there is no estimated reopening date for the affected areas, and damage assessments are ongoing.

The Grand Canyon is the fourth-most visited national park in the U.S., with more than 4.4 million recreational visits in 2025. The full extent of the damage — and the timeline for recovery — remains uncertain as the region braces for more potential storms.
