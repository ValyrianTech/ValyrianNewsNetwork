# Police rescue boy (8) from sea in Bredene, Belgium

Police officers in the Belgian coastal town of Bredene rescued an 8-year-old boy from the sea on Sunday evening after he slipped away from his home unnoticed. The child was brought safely back to shore and reunited with his parents the same night, according to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/08/31/jongen-ontsnapt-uit-huis-en-komt-in-problemen-in-zee-politie-ka/).

## A narrow escape

Around 9:15 PM, officers from the Bredene/De Haan police zone spotted the young boy walking alone past a beach bar on the Bredene seafront. Moments later, the child walked into the sea, prompting officers to act immediately.

"The boy had left his parental home shortly before without his parents noticing right away," said Stijn Biebauw, spokesperson for the Bredene/De Haan police zone. "Fortunately, our people were able to notice the boy and intervene immediately."

The officers entered the water without hesitation and brought the child safely back to shore. The "drowning victim procedure" was briefly initiated but was called off once the police rescue was confirmed successful, as [KW (Krant van West-Vlaanderen)](https://kw.be/nieuws/ongevallen/jongetje-8-uit-zee-gered-door-politie-nadat-hij-eerder-ouderlijk-huis-ontglipt-was/) reported.

## Unharmed and reunited

Ambulance personnel who had been dispatched to the scene examined the boy, but he was found to be unharmed. According to [HLN](https://www.hln.be/bredene/jongetje-8-staat-s-avonds-laat-plots-alleen-in-zee-in-bredene-gelukkig-hebben-enkele-agenten-dat-opgemerkt~a57c277f/), Biebauw noted that the boy "was wet and had it a bit cold, but could immediately go back home."

Later that evening, the child was returned to his parents in good health. The boy's name has not been released, consistent with privacy protections for minors in Belgian media.

## A dangerous time on the beach

The incident occurred well after the 6:30 PM cutoff for supervised swimming on Belgian beaches, when lifeguards are no longer on duty. The North Sea can be particularly hazardous in the evening hours, with strong currents and changing conditions posing significant risks even to experienced swimmers.

Bredene's mayor, Steve Vandenberghe, who is himself a former lifeguard, has repeatedly warned about the dangers of entering the sea outside supervised hours. "There was a treacherous wind last night and then the sea turns into a murder machine," he said following a previous incident.

## A pattern of concern

This is the second incident involving an 8-year-old from Bredene in just ten days. On August 21, another boy of the same age was rescued from the Spuikom, a water basin in nearby Ostend, after falling in during a holiday camp. A surfer pulled him to safety, and the boy's parents had gone looking for him when he didn't return home.

The back-to-back incidents highlight ongoing concerns about child safety near water in the coastal region. In August 2023, a 16-year-old boy drowned in Bredene after getting into trouble in the sea while swimming outside supervised hours. Strong currents made rescue attempts by bystanders unsuccessful that day.

## The importance of police patrols

This latest rescue underscores the critical role that police patrols play on Belgian beaches, even outside regular hours. The officers' alertness and rapid response prevented what could have been a tragedy.

"Fortunately, several alert officers noticed that," Biebauw said, reflecting on the outcome.

## What to watch for

The incident may prompt renewed discussions about child safety awareness and supervision in coastal communities. Local authorities have previously organized large-scale rescue exercises, such as "Seacoast 2026" held in nearby De Haan in July, which tested coordination between police, fire brigade, ambulance services, and beach lifeguards in sea rescue operations.

As the summer season draws to a close, the message from officials remains consistent: the sea demands respect, and vigilance is essential—especially when children are involved. The quick actions of the Bredene police officers serve as a reminder that safety on the coast is a shared responsibility.
