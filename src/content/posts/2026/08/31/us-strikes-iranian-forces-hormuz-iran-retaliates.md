# US Strikes Iranian Forces in Hormuz, Iran Retaliates

The US military conducted "limited precision" strikes on Iranian Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) mine-laying forces on Larak Island in the Strait of Hormuz on Sunday, according to [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/aug/30/us-strikes-iranian-launchers-on-larak-island-amid-strait-of-hormuz-tensions). The strikes targeted two rocket launchers that IRGC personnel were preparing to use to deploy sea mines into the strategic waterway, marking the first US military action against Iran in over a month.

## A Return to Kinetic Action

US Navy Captain Tim Hawkins confirmed the operation, stating: "Earlier today, US forces struck two Iranian launchers on Larak Island. Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps forces were observed preparing to launch rockets with sea mines into the Strait of Hormuz." US Central Command (CENTCOM) described the action as "limited, precise action against IRGC minelaying forces posing an imminent threat in the Strait of Hormuz," adding: "In essence, Iran created the threat and the US military eliminated it to protect civilian mariners, commercial shipping, and the free flow of global commerce."

The strike breaks a lull in fighting that had lasted since late July, when President Donald Trump paused a two-week US military campaign and shifted toward economic pressure rather than military action. According to [Fortune/AP](https://fortune.com/2026/08/30/battle-for-hormuz-us-forces-iran-rocket-launchers-sea-mines/), the turn to sanctions came as the administration weighed diminished munitions stockpiles after months of war.

## Iranian Retaliation

The IRGC responded swiftly, launching ballistic missiles at US-operated King Hussein and Al-Azraq airbases in Jordan in an operation it called "Punishment of the Aggressor." [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/liveblog/2026/8/30/iran-war-live-iran-pledges-to-stand-firm-against-unjust-us-sanctions) reported that Jordan's military intercepted eight missiles that breached its airspace, while US sources said nearly all incoming missiles were intercepted with no significant impact.

The IRGC said the US attack killed and wounded several fighters and civilians, with preliminary unofficial reports citing two dead and two injured. IRGC spokesman Brig. Gen. Hossein Mohebi called the strike a "strategic and fatal error" by the Trump administration that would carry "severe repercussions," warning: "The adversary will face the ramifications of this faulty calculus across both the economic and military arenas."

## The Battle for Hormuz

The Strait of Hormuz, through which roughly a fifth of the world's oil and liquefied natural gas shipments normally pass, has been at the center of the conflict since Iran restricted commercial passage in early March. [DW](https://www.dw.com/en/iran-strikes-strait-of-hormuz-larak-island/live-78566992) reported that the US military had announced just days earlier that it had completed clearing Iranian sea mines from the strait's international shipping routes.

President Trump had warned last week that any ship or boat placing new mines would be "immediately and systematically destroyed," declaring a "Zero Tolerance policy on mine placement in full force and effect." CENTCOM Commander Adm. Brad Cooper stated: "International shipping lanes are open and momentum is building."

However, Iran insists the strait remains closed. Iranian Deputy Foreign Minister Kazem Gharibabadi said: "The Strait of Hormuz is closed... Why should we always wait for America to attack? We can take preemptive action." [The National](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/us/2026/08/30/us-strikes-iran-near-strait-of-hormuz-in-first-military-action-in-a-month/) reported that commercial traffic through the strait remains at a fraction of pre-war levels, with about 6,000 seafarers still stranded in the Gulf.

## Escalating Rhetoric and Economic Impact

Hours after the attacks, Trump claimed on social media that Iran's energy hub of Kharg Island was being "blown to smithereens," though no confirmation was immediately available. If confirmed, attacking Kharg - which handled 90% of Iran's oil exports before the war - would severely disrupt the Islamic Republic's energy trade.

Oil prices jumped more than 2 percent following the strikes, with Brent crude climbing to $90.32 a barrel, according to Al Jazeera. CENTCOM also reported it had redirected 83 commercial vessels, disabled three, and boarded two to enforce the naval blockade of Iran.

The IRGC said its air defenses shot down a US MQ-9 drone over the strait, causing it to crash into Gulf waters. Separately, a tanker ship was struck by an unknown projectile on Saturday north of Khasab, Oman, with no immediate claim of responsibility.

## A Six-Month Conflict

The latest exchange comes as the US-Iran war passes its six-month mark. The conflict began on February 28 when the US and Israel launched large-scale strikes on Iran, killing Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei on the first day. Iran retaliated with missile and drone strikes against Israel and Gulf states hosting US military bases, turning the confrontation into a regional war.

As [The Guardian's interactive timeline](https://www.theguardian.com/world/ng-interactive/2026/aug/28/how-the-iran-war-unfolded-every-attack-and-oil-price-change-visualised) documents, the conflict has been marked by repeated ceasefire attempts and failed negotiations. An April ceasefire brokered by Pakistan collapsed, a June memorandum of understanding unraveled within weeks, and July saw the largest exchange of fire since the interim deal. Eighteen US service members have been killed in the conflict.

## What's Next

The strike represents a significant escalation that breaks a month-long pause in US military action and comes just days after the administration announced a shift to economic pressure. Iranian officials have vowed retaliation, with senior Iranian MP Ebrahim Azizi warning: "Undoubtedly, no crime, at any level, goes unanswered; a response that is devastating, more painful, and instructive, which will complete the chain of your failures. Wait in fear and terror!"

Reports on Monday of another explosion on Larak Island and Iranian missiles being fired at ships in the Strait of Hormuz suggest the conflict may be intensifying. The renewed fighting threatens to derail ongoing mediation efforts by Qatar, Pakistan, and Oman, while oil markets remain on edge over supply concerns through the critical waterway.

As both sides trade threats and strikes, the central question remains whether this escalation represents a temporary spike or a return to sustained conflict - and whether diplomacy can resume before the situation spirals further.
