# Chinese military academies see record applicants and quality

BEIJING — China's 22 military academies admitted more than 17,500 candidates this year from about 192,000 applicants, the [Ministry of National Defense](http://www.mod.gov.cn/gfbw/xwfyr/lxjzh_246940/16476759.html) said, describing both application enthusiasm and student quality as record highs.

At the ministry's regular press conference on July 30, a reporter noted that military academies are "always the ideal choice for many students and parents" during the college application season. Spokesperson Colonel Jiang Bin responded that approximately 192,000 people from 31 provinces, autonomous regions, and municipalities registered for this year's admissions cycle. The 22 academies admitted more than 17,500 candidates, and their college entrance examination, or gaokao, scores ranked on average in the top 6 percent of their home province.

"Both application enthusiasm and student quality have reached record highs," Jiang said, according to the ministry's transcript. He described the academies as "the cradle for cultivating high-quality, professional, new-type military talent, the battlefield for tempering courage and fighting spirit, and the stage where youthful dreams bloom."

Because provinces administer the gaokao separately, ranking within each province is the standard way to measure performance. An average ranking in the top 6 percent places admitted students among the highest-performing test-takers in their province, a clear sign of how competitive military academy admission has become.

Both record claims are related but distinct. The application total measures how many students were willing to enter a demanding selection process, while the score ranking measures the academic quality of those ultimately chosen. Together, they suggest that military academies are attracting both a larger and a stronger applicant pool.

## Context

The announcement, made two days before China marks Army Day on August 1, received prominent coverage in state media. [People's Daily](http://edu.people.com.cn/n1/2026/0731/c1006-40771608.html) carried the item on page 11 of its July 31 print edition; the paper's online education section published it the same morning. [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/politics/20260730/14606dfd2dce4c6aab611b0bf2713ec8/c.html) included the figures in a broader dispatch on the press conference, which also covered naval exchanges, joint air exercises, and other defense matters. [China News Service](https://www.chinanews.com.cn/gn/2026/07-30/10669277.shtml), meanwhile, published a standalone report focusing on the military admissions announcement.

Provincial announcements offered more granular detail ahead of the admissions window. Hebei's state broadcaster [reported](https://www.hebtv.com/0/0rmhlm/qy/hbxwlb/lbhj/12380144.shtml) in June that the 22 academies planned to enroll 1,206 students in the province, one of several regional recruitment plans released over the summer.

## Analysis

Jiang framed the admissions record within China's broader military modernization drive. He told incoming cadets that choosing a military academy means "integrating one's personal ideals into the great endeavor of building a strong country and a strong military," and he urged them to contribute to "advancing national defense and military modernization with high quality and accelerating the building of a world-class military."

The ministry's announcement did not include a detailed national comparison with previous years, but its characterization of the figures as record highs highlights the growing appeal of military careers among Chinese students. The academic strength of this year's pool — with admitted candidates scoring, on average, in the top 6 percent of their province — underscores how selective military higher education has become. The timing of the announcement, just ahead of Army Day, also gave the record patriotic resonance in official coverage.

Beyond the numbers, the announcement signals the continuing appeal of a military career as a pathway to national service and professional development in China. The ministry's language tied individual ambition to the collective project of building a world-class military, and the strong applicant pool suggests that framing is resonating with young people and their families.

## What's Next

The record intake gives China's 22 military academies a deep pool of academically strong candidates as they train the next generation of People's Liberation Army officers. With Army Day celebrations on August 1, the announcement also serves as a symbolic marker of the military's emphasis on talent development. For incoming cadets, the work now shifts to military training and academic study that will shape their careers for years to come. For prospective applicants and their families, the numbers provide a benchmark for just how competitive military academies have become. Whether application enthusiasm can hold at these levels in future cycles remains an open question, but this year's figures suggest that military academies have solidified their place as a top choice for high-achieving students.
