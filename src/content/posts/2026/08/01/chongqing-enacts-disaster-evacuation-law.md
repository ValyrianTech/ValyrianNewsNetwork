# Chongqing Enacts Disaster Evacuation and Response Law

A new local regulation standardizing how residents are evacuated when natural disasters threaten took effect in Chongqing on August 1, empowering township and street governments to order immediate transfers without waiting for higher-level approval. The Chongqing Municipality Regulation on Evacuation and Transfer of Personnel in Natural Disasters, passed on July 30 by the Standing Committee of the Municipal People's Congress, is the sprawling southwestern metropolis's first dedicated law on disaster evacuation, [People's Daily Online](http://society.people.com.cn/n1/2026/0801/c1008-40771948.html) reported.

## Context: A Geography Built for Disasters

Chongqing is often described by officials as combining a large city, big rural areas, big mountainous areas, and big reservoir areas. Deep valleys, numerous geological hazard points, major rivers such as the Yangtze, Jialing and Wu, and extensive forests — compounded by intense summer heat and drought — make floods, landslides and forest fires recurring threats. Yet previous local regulations on evacuation were fragmented and principle-level, leaving unclear who takes the lead, how to transfer people and where they should go, [CQNEWS](https://www.cqnews.net/web/content_1532774991175434240.html) reported.

The 30-article regulation, organized into six chapters, closes that gap by systematizing the complete workflow: pre-disaster preparation, monitoring and early warning, evacuation implementation, resettlement, and legal liability. It also codifies practices developed through years of disaster response, including the "44NN" progressive warning-and-response mechanism and the "three musts" transfer standards — transfer when warning thresholds are reached, when danger signs appear, and when danger cannot be accurately assessed.

## Eight Situations, One Rule: Move Everyone

Under [the regulation's full text](https://www.cq.gov.cn/ywdt/jrcq/202608/t20260801_15881276.html), townships and street offices must immediately organize the evacuation of all threatened people — reporting to higher authorities at the same time rather than awaiting step-by-step approval — when any of eight emergency situations arises: abnormal signs of collapse, landslide, ground collapse, ground fissure or land subsidence; sudden cracking or worsening deformation of buildings; short-duration torrential rain in mountainous areas that may trigger flash floods or debris flows; sudden rapid rises in river water levels; reservoirs, mountain ponds, tailings ponds or embankments at risk of collapse or breach; sudden waterlogging in urban tunnels and culverts; forest fires threatening nearby residents or too complex to assess accurately; and other abnormal natural-disaster conditions.

Village and neighborhood committees must immediately organize self-rescue and mutual rescue and report to the township, street office or relevant department. Where a person refuses to evacuate after persuasion and the situation is especially urgent, the township or street government may lawfully take decisive protective measures to move the person to safety. Evacuated residents may not return to threatened areas without authorization before the danger has passed.

## Warnings Designed to Reach and Wake People

A central challenge in disaster-prone Chongqing has been ensuring warnings actually reach those at risk. Jiang Jin, deputy director of the Municipal Disaster Prevention, Reduction and Relief Office and deputy director of the Chongqing Municipal Emergency Management Bureau, said the city has built a segmented, graded, categorized and layered progressive warning-and-response mechanism — the "44NN" system — under which warnings are issued in stages from one-to-three-day trend forecasts to real-time graded alerts, and responses are activated across four levels from city down to village and grid, [CQNEWS reported](https://www.cqnews.net/web/content_1532789699857170432.html).

"For imminent-disaster warnings, being able to reach people and wake them up is a particularly important key," Jiang said. Warnings are delivered through the "141" grassroots smart-governance platform, phone calls, text messages, emergency broadcasts, alarms, gongs and whistles, and door-to-door visits. Broadcasters and telecom operators are required by the law to transmit warnings free of charge through a fast green channel.

## A Four-Level Responsibility Chain

The law also establishes a four-level responsibility system: the municipal government coordinates overall; district and county governments organize and deploy; township and street governments execute on the front line; and village and community bodies implement down to individual households, according to [Chongqing Daily's interpretation](https://www.cq.gov.cn/ywdt/jrcq/202608/t20260801_15881270.html). Evacuees are to be settled through a combination of government-led and self-arranged placement, centralized and dispersed options, and in-place and off-site arrangements. Residents in high-risk zones such as geological impact areas, cliff edges and low-lying land beside rivers are encouraged to relocate permanently, with district and county governments providing relocation subsidies and homestead adjustment support.

The regulation also strengthens cross-regional cooperation, requiring the sharing of water-regime information with neighboring Sichuan, Guizhou and Hubei and advance notification of reservoir discharges during flood season.

## Analysis: Closing the 'Dare Not Transfer' Gap

Legislators say the law tackles long-standing pain points in disaster response: officials who "dare not transfer," teams that "do not know how to transfer," and situations that are "difficult to control." Chen Jie, director of the Social Affairs Committee of the Chongqing Municipal People's Congress, said the regulation clearly divides responsibilities among city, district and county, township and street, and village and community levels, details the duties of industry regulators, and strengthens binding constraints on personnel transfer — helping ensure evacuation work is timely, standardized and orderly, [Xinhua reported](https://www.news.cn/20260801/57868c53d8c740b593f4a0bb04b64c5b/c.html).

By granting on-site decision power to the grassroots officials closest to the danger, the law seeks to remove the hesitation that can cost lives when minutes matter. It also fits a broader pattern in Chinese provincial legislation: Hebei enacted rules on flood-control evacuation in 2024, and Shaanxi has restricted unauthorized returns to evacuated areas since 2021.

## What's Next

Officials say implementation will focus on three tracks: refining emergency plans and responsibility systems to match the new legal requirements; upgrading support networks, including developing a paid-reporting system for natural-disaster risk hazards and digital tools to help frontline cadres make rapid decisions; and training officials and the public on hazard identification, warning signals, escape routes and self-rescue skills.

The regulation takes effect at the height of Chongqing's flood season — a demanding first test. The real measure of the law will be whether its new legal tools translate into faster, more decisive evacuations when extreme weather next hits the mountain city.
