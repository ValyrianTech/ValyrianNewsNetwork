# Tan Quan Appointed Chongqing Vice Mayor and Police Chief

Tan Quan, until recently an assistant minister of public security, has been appointed vice mayor of Chongqing and director of the Chongqing Municipal Public Security Bureau, filling a key post left vacant for more than two months. The decision was made on July 31 by the 25th session of the Standing Committee of the 6th Chongqing Municipal People's Congress, according to [The Paper](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_33692563).

## Context

The police chief position in Chongqing — described in Chinese political discourse as the city's "knife handle," a metaphor for the state's armed and police power — has drawn intense public attention since Vice Mayor and Public Security Bureau Director Zhang Anjiang died suddenly on May 7 at age 55. Days before the new appointment, [Lianhe Zaobao](https://www.zaobao.com.sg/news/china/story20260801-9454084) noted that a successor to the closely watched post had been expected for two months.

The appointment is also the latest step in a broad restructuring of Chongqing's municipal government following the March 2026 corruption investigation into Mayor Hu Henghua, who resigned in May.

## Key Developments

At the July 31 session, the standing committee also appointed Wu Shulin, a senior official from the National Development and Reform Commission, as vice mayor, and accepted the resignation of Vice Mayor Ma Zhen, to be reported to the next plenary session of the municipal people's congress. In the same session, the congress issued a notice stating that Zhang Anjiang's deputy qualification had automatically terminated following his death, as [Caixin](https://china.caixin.com/2026-07-31/102470248.html) reported.

Tan Quan, 55, is a native of Xuanhan County, Sichuan Province, and holds a doctorate in engineering mechanics from the University of Science and Technology Beijing. He spent most of his career in the Beijing public security system, serving as deputy chief and political commissar of the Beijing Public Transport Security Corps before becoming deputy district head of Haidian and director of the Haidian District Public Security Bureau in 2018. He later headed the Ministry of Public Security's Cybersecurity Bureau and was appointed assistant minister in October 2025, as [Guancha](https://www.guancha.cn/politics/2025_10_23_794286.shtml) documented. The State Council removed him from that post on July 16; a day later, he appeared as a Chongqing city leader at a video dispatch meeting chaired by Party Secretary Yuan Jiajun on rescue operations after the Pengshui County mountain collapse.

Wu Shulin, the other new vice mayor, has spent his career in regional economic policy at the NDRC, most recently as director of its Regional Coordinated Development Department, where he was involved in the Beijing-Tianjin-Hebei integration strategy and the Qianhai and Hengqin development plans.

## Analysis

Tan Quan's appointment restores a public-security career profile to the Chongqing police leadership. Zhang Anjiang, the previous officeholder, was the first Chongqing police chief since 1997 without a background in the public security or political-legal system, coming instead from the NDRC and local government. Tan's experience leading the ministry's cybersecurity bureau also aligns with Beijing's emphasis on cyber governance and social stability — priorities likely to shape his tenure in one of China's largest municipalities.

The reshuffle is part of a wider leadership overhaul in Chongqing. Mayor Hu Henghua was placed under investigation in March 2026, becoming the sixth senior Chongqing official investigated since the 20th Party Congress, as [Chinanews](https://www.chinanews.com.cn/gn/2026/03-20/10589945.shtml) reported. Chen Xinwu, 58, became acting mayor in late July; Liu Shangjin was appointed vice mayor in June. With two new vice mayors added and one resignation accepted on July 31, the municipal government team has been substantially reconfigured amid an ongoing anti-corruption campaign.

## What's Next

Attention now turns to the formal confirmation of Chen Xinwu as mayor and possible further personnel moves in Chongqing. For Tan Quan, the immediate challenges include managing the aftermath of the Pengshui disaster and maintaining public order in one of China's largest cities, as the central leadership continues to prioritize stability.
