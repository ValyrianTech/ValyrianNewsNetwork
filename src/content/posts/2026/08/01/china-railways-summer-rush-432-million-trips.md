# China Railways Log 432 Million Trips in Summer Rush

China's national railway network carried 432 million passenger trips in the first month of the summer travel rush, [CCTV News reported](https://news.cctv.com/2026/08/01/ARTIeaglm4kVh3btc62lRIFb260801.shtml) on August 1, citing China State Railway Group. The figure covers July 1–31 and keeps passenger flow at high levels halfway through the 62-day rush, which runs through August 31. Family study tours, summer resort travel, returning students and business visits have all contributed to the sustained traffic.

The summer rush is an annual peak for China's rail network, coinciding with school holidays and the hottest travel weeks of the year. Before it began, China State Railway Group projected 1.01 billion trips for the full July–August period, equivalent to about 16.29 million a day, according to [China Daily](https://global.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202606/30/WS6a43aa9ba310986e2b462be4.html). Early data show the season started strongly: in the first nine days, railways recorded 123.66 million trips, [ECNS reported](https://www.ecns.cn/cns-wire/2026-07-10/detail-ihfhemcv3617747.shtml). The previous summer rush ended with a record 943 million trips, [up 4.7 percent year on year](http://www.china-railway.com.cn/xwzx/ywsl/202509/t20250903_148270.html), according to China Railway.

New infrastructure is adding significant capacity this year. The Xi'an–Shiyan high-speed railway, part of the Wuhan–Xi'an corridor, opened on June 30 with Xi'an East Station entering service the same day; trains on the line carried more than 2.33 million passengers in July. Foshan Station on the Guangzhou–Zhanjiang high-speed railway began operations on July 1, running 98 trains daily to Guangzhou, Shenzhen, Changsha and Wuhan, according to [China News Service](https://www.chinanews.com.cn/cj/2026/08-01/10670647.shtml). The Lanxi–Jiande section of the Jinhua–Jiande high-speed railway and a new section of Beijing's suburban railway city sub-center line, Beijing West–Liangxiang, also entered service before the rush, CCTV reported.

China News Service said the overlapping demand has defined the season: "Since the summer rush began, multiple passenger flows — family study tours, summer resort travel, returning students, and business/family visits — have interwoven." Railway departments have strengthened station and train services and implemented travel safeguards to keep operations safe, stable and orderly.

Traffic is especially dense in the Chengdu–Chongqing region, the Beijing–Tianjin–Hebei area, the Yangtze River Delta and the Guangdong–Hong Kong–Macao Greater Bay Area, according to CCTV and China News Service. China Railway Beijing Group handled 38.774 million trips by July 31, a daily average of 1.251 million. China Railway Guangzhou Group reported 58.925 million trips, with a daily average of 1.901 million and a single-day peak of 2.468 million.

Capacity has been expanded through scheduling and data-driven planning. From July 1, a dedicated summer train diagram came into effect alongside the third-quarter schedule. Railway authorities analyzed 12306 ticketing big data to add passenger trains, couple EMU trainsets and attach extra carriages on popular routes, sections and time windows, CCTV reported. Ahead of the rush, China Daily noted plans to add 480 inter-regional passenger trains and run about 12,000 passenger trains daily.

The rail–tourism link has grown stronger in recent years. China Railway's review of the 2025 season emphasized tourist trains, study-tour charters and the fusion of rail with summer consumption, noting that the network spans more than 160,000 km of track. That expanded network, combined with new lines and stations, gives operators more flexibility to match supply with demand.

With 432 million trips in July, the network has completed about 42.8 percent of the 1.01 billion full-season projection. August typically peaks mid-month, driven by return travel and late-summer holidays, so the remaining weeks will determine whether China Railway can beat last year's record. Sustained rail volume is also read by state media and analysts as a sign of resilient domestic consumption and mobility.

The second half of the rush will be closely watched, particularly the performance of newly opened lines such as the Xi'an–Shiyan high-speed railway and Foshan Station. Service upgrades, new train diagrams and demand-driven capacity additions will all play a role in whether China's summer rail season sets another record.
