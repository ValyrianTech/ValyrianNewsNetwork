# China Launches Campaign to Curb Marriage Agency Misconduct

Five Chinese government departments have launched an eight-month nationwide campaign against misconduct in the country's marriage agency industry, targeting seven categories of problems from fabricated matchmaking profiles to personal data misuse. The action runs from July 2026 to February 2027 and is coordinated by the Ministry of Civil Affairs, according to [People's Daily Online](http://society.people.com.cn/n1/2026/0801/c1008-40772117.html).

## Context

The campaign follows years of high-profile consumer complaints and media investigations documenting induced consumption, false advertising and predatory contracts in the matchmaking market. In a December 2024 investigation, [Jiefang Daily](https://www.jfdaily.com/wx/detail.do?id=827374) detailed how agencies used aggressive sales tactics, fabricated "high-quality" client profiles and buried contract clauses to push customers into costly packages — often leaving them unable to obtain refunds. Chen Yinjiang, deputy secretary-general of the Consumer Protection Law Research Association under the China Law Society, told the outlet that fragmented regulation across multiple agencies had created gaps that unscrupulous firms exploited.

## Key Developments

The work plan was jointly issued on July 28 by the Ministry of Civil Affairs, the Cyberspace Administration of China, the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology, the Ministry of Public Security and the State Administration for Market Regulation. [China News](https://www.chinanews.com/sh/2026/07-31/10670099.shtml) reported that the campaign covers all market entities and social service institutions providing matchmaking services nationwide, including online dating platforms and offline agencies, while excluding free platforms purely for friendship or social purposes.

The seven targeted problem areas are: illegal registration or unlicensed service; false marketing, including "guaranteed success" promises and fabricated success stories; "marriage broker" fraud, in which fake clients are sent to meet paying consumers and fees are repeatedly charged under names such as "meeting fee" or "deposit"; unfair contract clauses that evade refund obligations; chaotic fee management, including hidden charges and refusal to return prepayments; perfunctory verification of clients' identity, marital status, education or occupation; and leakage or abuse of personal information.

The Ministry of Civil Affairs confirmed the campaign at its third-quarter press conference on July 30. [Legal Daily](http://www.legaldaily.com.cn/index/content/2026-07/31/content_9433772.html) quoted spokesperson Li Wanli as saying the ministry would strengthen supervision of the matchmaking service industry, promote a multi-department collaborative supervision mechanism and support the healthy, orderly development of the sector. The ministry also linked the effort to its broader push to build a "marriage- and childbirth-friendly society."

## Implementation

According to [Jiemian](https://www.jiemian.com/article/14859263.html), the campaign proceeds in five stages: mobilization this month, agency self-inspection from August to September, problem mapping from October to November, concentrated rectification from December to January, and summary and consolidation in February 2027. During self-inspection, agencies must complete declaration forms signed by their legal representatives; those that conceal or falsify information will be marked as priority enforcement targets. Confirmed violations are to be referred to market regulation and cyberspace authorities, and suspected criminal cases transferred to public security for investigation. The plan also encourages the use of model contract texts and a "sign contract first, pay later" model.

## Analysis

This is the first known nationwide, multi-department special campaign specifically aimed at marriage agency misconduct, signalling a shift from piecemeal local measures toward coordinated central enforcement with a fixed timeline. It also carries political significance: with officials focused on supporting marriage and childbearing amid demographic concerns, a predatory matchmaking industry has become a salient consumer issue. The concentrated rectification phase is expected to produce publicly named enforcement cases, and the February 2027 wrap-up could lay the groundwork for new national rules governing the sector.

## What's Next

The coming months of self-inspection and problem mapping will reveal how widespread the abuses are. Consumers and industry watchers will be watching whether the campaign produces concrete refund relief, how online platforms are held accountable compared with offline agencies, and whether the lessons from this crackdown translate into lasting national regulation.
