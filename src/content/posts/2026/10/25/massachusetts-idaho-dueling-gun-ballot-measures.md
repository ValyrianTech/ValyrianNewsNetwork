# Massachusetts, Idaho Put Dueling Gun Measures on Ballot

Two states roughly 2,000 miles apart will ask voters to render sharply contrasting verdicts on firearms this November. On November 3, [Massachusetts](https://apnews.com/article/gun-registry-firearms-massachusetts-montana-b47732768b4ec073d08f789e87f943c5) voters will decide whether to repeal a sweeping 2024 gun-control law, while Idaho voters will pick an official "state gun" from a list of six historic firearms. The paired votes offer a split-screen gauge of public opinion on guns at a moment of federal gridlock.

## A referendum forced by gun-rights activists

Massachusetts Question 9 exists because opponents of the 2024 law gathered petition signatures to force a referendum. The law — passed overwhelmingly by the Democratic-led Legislature — established a state firearms registry, banned "ghost guns" and devices that make guns fire rapidly, expanded the ban on assault-style weapons, and broadened the state's "red flag" law so that healthcare providers and school administrators, not just police, can ask courts to temporarily remove guns from people deemed a risk.

Because a referendum petition normally suspends a law until the election, but [Democratic Gov. Maura Healey signed an emergency provision](https://apnews.com/article/massachusetts-ghost-guns-new-law-healey-a180d51cf82c313dbc75014337467b90) putting it into effect immediately in 2024, gun owners face an October 28 deadline to register their firearms — six days before they vote on whether to keep the requirement.

"It's a total violation of people's rights to require this before they have their chance to vote on it," said Toby Leary, chair of The Civil Rights Coalition, which leads the repeal effort. Leary also warned that a government database of gun owners could endanger them if the information is hacked, calling it "a wish list for nefarious actors."

Supporters defend the law as balanced. "This legislation both preserves a legal pathway to gun ownership while also putting guardrails in place that keep people safe," said Ruth Zakarin, who chairs the campaign committee backing the law and leads the Massachusetts Coalition to Prevent Gun Violence.

## Idaho's symbolic exercise in civic pride

In Boise, the Republican-led Legislature placed a nonbinding advisory question on the ballot asking voters which firearm should become the official state gun of Idaho. The options, [according to Ballotpedia](https://ballotpedia.org/Idaho_Official_State_Gun_Question_(2026)), are the Winchester Model 1894, the Winchester Model 1873 "Gun that Won the West," the 1873 Colt Single Action Army revolver, the M1 Garand rifle, the Colt M1911 pistol, and the Remington Model 700 rifle.

State Rep. Jason Monks, the Republican House majority leader who sponsored the measure, framed it as a celebration of heritage and a turnout driver for the midterms. "There's not much more patriotic in Idaho than guns — and the importance of guns in helping to found our country and settle the West," he said.

Not everyone is convinced the options resonate locally. Neill Goodfellow, a board member of the Idaho State Rifle & Pistol Association, said "none of them really have a significant place in the history of Idaho" and predicted many voters won't know which to choose. He plans to vote for the M1 Garand.

## Why the contrast matters

The two questions capture how states are increasingly making gun policy directly at the ballot box. Massachusetts has one of the nation's lowest gun-ownership rates; Idaho, where about three in five adults live in households with guns, has among the highest. A 40-year-old federal law bars a national gun registry, so states have filled the void — Massachusetts became the third jurisdiction, after Hawaii and the District of Columbia, to enact a comprehensive registry, according to the Giffords Law Center to Prevent Gun Violence.

"These laws help law enforcement solve crimes, track down stolen and trafficked guns," said Lindsay Nichols, the center's policy director. "They also discourage illegal gun sales, and they help protect law enforcement by providing them information about what guns might be present."

Idaho's question, by contrast, is largely symbolic. Utah and Arizona became the first states to adopt official firearms in 2011, and about a dozen states now have one.

## What to watch

The Massachusetts result carries real legal weight: a federal lawsuit filed by gun advocates already challenges the law's licensing and registration requirements, citing the Supreme Court's 2022 Bruen decision, so the measure's fate may be settled in court regardless of the vote. In Idaho, a clear winner would likely prompt lawmakers to formally designate the firearm in their 2027 session — but Monks cautioned that an evenly split vote could prove "too controversial for lawmakers to settle." Either way, the twin ballots will offer an unusually direct read on where Americans stand on guns.
