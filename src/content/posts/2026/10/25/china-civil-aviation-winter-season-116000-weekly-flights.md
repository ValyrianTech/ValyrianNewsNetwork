# China Aviation Enters Winter Season With 116,000 Flights

China's civil aviation sector began operating its 2026/2027 winter-spring flight season on October 25, with 216 domestic and foreign airlines planning to run roughly 116,000 passenger and cargo flights per week nationwide, according to the Civil Aviation Administration of China (CAAC). The new season runs through March 27, 2027, [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/politics/20261024/6952cb35fe334683893e7c0a97f5ba2c/c.html) reported, citing the regulator.

The twice-yearly schedule change, known in Chinese as "huanji" (换季), reallocates routes, airport slots, and capacity to match seasonal demand patterns and international traffic flows.

## Domestic Network Expansion

On the domestic side, 61 airlines plan to operate about 94,000 flights per week, comprising 91,454 passenger flights and 2,812 cargo flights. A total of 34 airlines will open 288 new domestic routes, concentrated on feeder connections linking the Northwest, Northeast, and Southwest regions with the East China and Central-South regions.

Xinhua described the aim as advancing a "cross-regional, multi-tier aviation network" — part of Beijing's broader effort to improve connectivity between less-developed western and northeastern regions and the more prosperous eastern seaboard.

## International Flights Grow

Internationally, 191 airlines plan about 21,000 flights per week. Passenger services will reach 84 countries with 16,654 weekly flights, while cargo flights will serve 48 countries with 5,158 weekly flights.

Flights to Central Asia, South Asia, North America, Europe, Southeast Asia, and Latin America increased compared with the previous winter-spring season. Notably, flight volumes with 17 countries — including Kazakhstan, India, Canada, and Belgium — grew by more than 40%, according to the CAAC figures carried by Xinhua.

## Slightly Lower Overall Volume

While the airline count rose to 216 from 210 a year earlier, the aggregate weekly total edged down from prior seasons. During the 2025/2026 winter-spring season, which began on October 26, 2025, 210 airlines planned about 119,500 weekly passenger and cargo flights, [World Journal](https://www.worldjournal.com/wj/story/121474/9095118) reported at the time. The 2024/2025 season had 194 airlines and roughly 118,000 weekly flights.

Xinhua did not provide a year-on-year comparison for the total this season, so the shift should be read with caution: the growth in airline participants alongside a slightly smaller aggregate may reflect consolidation of capacity or changes in how flights are counted.

## Why It Matters

The winter-spring switch is a routine administrative event, but the composition of the schedule offers a signal about the direction of China's aviation recovery. The emphasis on expanded passenger links to Europe, Central Asia, and Latin America reflects Beijing's post-pandemic push to rebuild and diversify international air connectivity, including along Belt and Road corridors.

On the domestic side, the focus on feeder routes linking the country's interior west to its coastal economic hubs aligns with the "dual circulation" strategy, which seeks to strengthen internal demand while maintaining external trade links.

## What to Watch

The new routes will need to demonstrate sufficient demand through the winter travel period, typically anchored by the Spring Festival holiday in early 2027. The international expansion also depends on reciprocal traffic rights and continued recovery in outbound travel demand. The next schedule change takes effect on March 27, 2027, when the summer-autumn season begins.
