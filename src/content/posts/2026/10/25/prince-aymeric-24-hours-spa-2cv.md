# Prince Aymeric Takes on 24 Hours of Spa in a 2CV

Prince Aymeric of Belgium is making his endurance-racing debut at the 24 Hours 2CV of Spa-Francorchamps this weekend, taking the wheel of the iconic Citroën 2CV for an Ardennes team — and admitting he is "quite stressed" ahead of the challenge. The 20-year-old, a nephew of King Filip and second son of Prince Laurent and Princess Claire, started the race on Saturday at 18:00, with the finish scheduled for Sunday at 17:00, [VRT NWS reported](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/kijk/2026/10/25/video-francorchamps-prins-aymeric/).

The race takes place at the Circuit de Spa-Francorchamps, the celebrated track in Belgium's Ardennes region, and pits a large field of the French mass-market cars against the clock over a full day and night.

## A Family Legacy, 32 Years On

Aymeric's entry carries a particular symbolism. Exactly 32 years ago, his father, Prince Laurent, competed in the same race — partnering with Eric Van de Poele and Pascal Witmeur — before their team was forced to retire with an engine problem, [according to VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/09/21/prins-aymeric-spa-francorchamps/).

The parallel is one the young prince has openly embraced. "I am very happy to be able to take part in the 24 Hours 2CV, a race that is part of Belgium's motorsport heritage, and that 32 years after my father," he said in a team press release when his participation was announced in September.

His father's effort offers both inspiration and a note of caution: Prince Laurent's 1994 campaign ended not with a podium but with mechanical failure, a reminder of the attrition that defines endurance racing.

## A Structured Climb Into Motorsport

Aymeric's route into racing has been gradual and deliberate rather than improvised. He is affiliated with the M3M Endurance Academy, a Belgian structure that trains young drivers, which he joined several months ago and where he presented his racing programme in July 2026.

Before Spa, he tested himself in the Fun Cup, competing in the 8 Hours of Castellet in southern France in early October. Driving car #13 — his lucky number — he steadily improved his lap times, and his team climbed from 56th on the grid to finish 29th. He described the outing as a "dream weekend."

At Spa he drives car #14 for The Golden Horse, a team based in Malmedy, one of the towns in the Ardennes close to the circuit. In the same press release, the prince said he felt "very honoured" to make his debut "in the best possible context, within a leading team," adding that he adapted quickly during the test days and "felt at ease behind the wheel."

## The Weight of a Famous Name

For all the structured preparation, the step up to a 24-hour endurance race is a demanding one for any driver, let alone a 20-year-old making his first appearance in the discipline. Aymeric did not hide his nerves on the eve of competition: "I am quite stressed," he said, as the field prepared for the overnight running.

The event itself has a devoted following. The 24 Hours 2CV is regarded as part of Belgium's motorsport heritage and draws a large, enthusiastic 2CV community, a world away from the high-budget GT3 racing for which Spa is perhaps better known. That distinction matters for a young royal entering the sport: the 2CV race is a test of mechanical sympathy, teamwork and endurance rather than sheer horsepower, and it has long been a proving ground for amateur and semi-professional drivers alike.

Belgian media have followed his debut closely, treating it as both a human-interest royal story and a genuine sporting undertaking — a combination that has made the prince one of the more closely watched debutants in the field.

## What to Watch

The most immediate question is the result. The race finishes at 17:00 on Sunday, and the outcome — finishing position, any incidents, and whether car #14 survives the distance — was not yet known when this article was compiled.

Beyond the chequered flag, the bigger story is whether this weekend marks the beginning of a longer racing career for Aymeric — a path that would follow a small but notable tradition of Belgian royals taking to the track. For now, his own words capture the moment better than any analysis: a young driver, carrying his father's legacy onto the same circuit three decades later, and candid enough to admit he is nervous.
