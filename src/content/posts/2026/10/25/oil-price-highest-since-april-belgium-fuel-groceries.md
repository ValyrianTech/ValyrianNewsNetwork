# Oil Hits Highest Since April, Squeezing Fuel and Groceries

The price of Brent crude oil has climbed to 116.72 US dollars a barrel, its highest level since April 2026, as renewed tensions in the Middle East push up energy costs. According to [Het Laatste Nieuws](https://www.hln.be/binnenland/niet-alleen-slecht-voor-je-tankbeurt-maar-ook-je-boodschappen-olieprijs-op-hoogste-niveau-sinds-april~a5b328fa/), the surge will hit Belgian consumers twice — directly at the fuel pump and indirectly through higher grocery prices — because transport and agriculture both depend on diesel.

Energy specialist Thijs Van de Graaf framed the outlook in stark terms. "The fact that the price of crude oil is rising is, in any case, not good news," he told the paper, warning that the market expects regional unrest to persist. "What is certain is that we feel rising prices twice: at the pump and while shopping."

## A Volatile Year for Oil

The current level caps a turbulent twelve months for energy markets. Crude hit a 52-week low of 59.93 dollars a barrel in December 2025, then spiked to a peak of 138.21 dollars on 7 April 2026 amid the escalating US–Israel–Iran conflict. Prices eased to around 83–85 dollars over the summer before renewed unrest around the Strait of Hormuz and Bab el-Mandeb pushed them back up. The current price is roughly 79 percent higher than in October 2025, when a barrel cost about 63 dollars.

[De NRC](https://www.nrc.nl/nieuws/2026/04/30/olieprijs-bereikt-hoogste-niveau-sinds-begin-oorlog-midden-oosten-a4926683) reported earlier in the conflict that oil had already breached 125 dollars a barrel — 107 euros — as US President Donald Trump signalled he would continue a blockade of the Strait of Hormuz until Iran abandoned its nuclear programme. The chokepoint, through which roughly 20 percent of the world's daily oil consumption passed before the war, has been repeatedly disrupted.

## Why Prices Are Rising Again

Several forces are converging to keep upward pressure on prices. Iran is attacking oil tankers, vessels are striking sea mines, and the Houthis have bombarded Saudi oil fields and refining capacity. Fears that the US and Israel might act around the US midterm elections or Tuesday's Israeli election are adding to the risk premium, and Washington is sending a third aircraft carrier to the region — a move Van de Graaf said could signal a new escalation.

Even more oil leaving the Gulf has not eased the market. Shipping companies and captains are taking greater risks, and that extra supply is being absorbed by higher demand in Asia, where countries have relaxed strict consumption measures. Higher insurance premiums for shipowners are also lifting prices, even as more crude moves.

Perhaps most concerning, the buffers that once cushioned shocks are shrinking. OECD countries have already released a third of their strategic reserves, and commercial inventories — including at airports — are low worldwide. [NOS](https://nos.nl/artikel/2612423-olieprijs-stijgt-naar-hoogste-niveau-in-vier-jaar) noted that the price surge is only gradually filtering through to the pump, with advisory prices for petrol and diesel adjusting by cents per litre over days rather than instantly.

## The Outlook: Uncertainty Rules

Forecasting has become treacherous. Goldman Sachs predicted in late February that prices could reach 150 dollars a barrel, but in September the bank told clients it would stop making projections altogether. "Even they don't know anymore," Van de Graaf said. "The keyword is uncertainty. In two weeks, when it becomes clear whether Trump will do anything after the midterms, we will know more."

The path lower, he suggested, runs through de-escalation — Iran attacking fewer tankers and the Houthis halting their offensive against Saudi Arabia. A breakthrough in the Russia-Ukraine war could also help, though an announced energy-infrastructure ceasefire unravelled after Kyiv and Moscow revealed they had not agreed to it.

"But a pessimistic scenario, in which things escalate either in the Middle East or in the war between Ukraine and Russia, is not excluded, and then prices shoot up again," Van de Graaf warned. "Uncertainty rules."

For Belgian households, the mechanism is clear even without owning a car. Goods are mainly transported by truck and agricultural products are harvested with machines — both of which run on diesel. As Van de Graaf put it, higher energy prices are felt "several times over." With crude at its highest since April and geopolitical risks unresolved, consumers should brace for continued pressure on both their fuel bills and their shopping baskets.
