# Beijing Debuts First Comfort-Class Tourism Train

Beijing will launch its first "comfort-class" tourism train on December 22, a 10-day, 9-night loop through northeast China that pairs hotel-grade private cabins with cultural programming, according to [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/20261024/821a5b9f0a284eb8bb7bc6072af969c6/c.html). The "New Oriental · Xiake Train" is a joint venture between China Railway's Beijing bureau and New Oriental Culture & Tourism Group, and marks the capital's entry into a fast-spreading new tier of premium rail tourism.

## A Train Built for Comfort

The service will use 18 carriages configured for just 190 passengers, with the entire train laid out in soft-sleeper three- and four-person compartments, each equipped with its own private bathroom. A dining car and a multi-function carriage will house a culture lecture hall, a tea room and a traditional Chinese medicine therapy space—an amenity mix aimed squarely at families, parent-child travelers and leisure and wellness seekers.

"The whole train is fitted with soft-sleeper three- and four-person compartments, each equipped with an independent bathroom," Yan Lei, party secretary of the tourist-train subsidiary of China Railway Travel Group, said in the Xinhua report.

The maiden itinerary departs Beijing Station on December 22 and travels via Tianjin, Changchun, Yichun, Harbin, Dunhua, Yanji and Shenyang, returning to Beijing on December 31. The route is designed to showcase the northeast "between ice and snow and culture," Xinhua reported.

## Part of a National Push

The Beijing train is the latest regional entry into a "comfort-class" category that has expanded rapidly across China in 2026. In May, the Yangtze River Delta launched its own train, the "Yangtze River Delta Star," from Shanghai West Station with nearly 200 tourists bound for an 18-day Xinjiang tour, according to [China News Service](https://www.chinanews.com.cn/sh/2026/05-19/10624163.shtml). That train featured all-private compartments with independent bathrooms, elderly-friendly touches such as corridor handrails, anti-slip carpet, bathroom grab bars and shower chairs, plus an onboard clinic with a medical refrigerator and an AED.

The rollout follows a June 2026 policy push: eight central departments, including the Ministry of Commerce, the Ministry of Culture and Tourism and China Railway Group, issued measures to promote integrated rail-tourism development and expand service consumption. China Railway Group has said it will focus on expanding the tourism-train fleet, diversifying products and carrying out tourism-friendly retrofits of stations and trains.

## Why It Matters

The Xiake Train is notable for more than its itinerary. It is a private-public partnership: China Railway supplies rolling stock and operating authority while New Oriental provides brand, tour design and cultural content. The education giant, known for its tutoring business and the East Buy livestreaming unit, launched its culture-and-tourism arm in 2023 and has said it intends to pursue a separate initial public offering for the division.

The product is also calibrated to China's aging population and a broader shift toward slow, high-service travel. Independent en-suites, on-board wellness spaces and no-shopping, unhurried itineraries speak to demand from silver and family travelers who value comfort over speed. New Oriental founder Yu Minhong has framed the travel business as tourism pursued "with the logic of education, content production and community operation"—selling knowledge and experience rather than efficiency. The on-board lecture hall and tea room are direct expressions of that philosophy, testing whether consumers will pay a premium for curated culture woven into a hotel-grade rail journey.

The economics behind the push are real but still modest. New Oriental's tourism segment reported roughly 1.2 billion yuan in revenue in fiscal 2025 alongside a loss of about 100 million yuan, underlining both the ambition and the early-stage costs of the bet. Beyond any single train, officials have framed rail-tourism integration as a way to stimulate service consumption—a category China has been working to expand as domestic travel demand matures.

## What to Watch

Ticket prices and the on-sale date for the Xiake Train have not been disclosed. Whether the service adopts the same "travel by night, tour by day" model and butler service seen on the Delta train remains unconfirmed. The Beijing debut will be closely watched as a template for similar public-private rail-tourism tie-ups in other provinces—and as a test of whether travelers will pay a premium for culture and knowledge woven into a hotel-grade journey.
