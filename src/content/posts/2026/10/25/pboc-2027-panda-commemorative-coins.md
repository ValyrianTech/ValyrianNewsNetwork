# PBOC to Issue 2027 Panda Gold, Silver and Platinum Coins

The People's Bank of China will issue the 2027 edition of its Panda precious metal commemorative coins on October 30, 2026, the central bank announced — continuing a series that has run without interruption since 1982 and remains China's longest-standing and most widely issued precious metal coin program.

According to [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/fortune/20261024/32fd3bfa75d54d30b8241606e4a1b70f/c.html), the set comprises 15 coins: seven uncirculated, or bullion-type (普制), coins and eight proof-type (精制) coins. All are legal tender of the People's Republic of China. The central bank published the issuing announcement on October 23, and the state news agency reported it the following day.

## A Familiar Design, a Broader Metal Mix

Every coin in the set shares a consistent design language. The obverse bears the Hall of Prayer for Good Harvests at the Temple of Heaven in Beijing, along with the national name and year. The reverse depicts two giant pandas nestled together, accompanied by the face value, weight and purity.

What distinguishes the 2027 set is its metal composition. Beyond the traditional gold and silver, the lineup includes three platinum coins — a 100-gram bullion coin and 30-gram and 3-gram proof coins. Platinum returned to the Panda series with the 2022 edition after an extended absence, and its expanded presence in the 2027 set underscores the program's diversification beyond the two metals that built its reputation.

## The Investment Core

The bullion line anchors the series' appeal to investors. The flagship 30-gram round gold coin — 99.9% pure, 32 mm in diameter, face value 500 yuan and a maximum mintage of 500,000 — is the series' most closely watched investment product. Its low face value relative to the metal's market value is typical of bullion coinage, where legal tender status is nominal and value tracks the metal price.

Below it sit 15-gram (200 yuan), 8-gram (100 yuan), 3-gram (50 yuan) and 1-gram (10 yuan) gold coins, each with mintages between 200,000 and 500,000. The 30-gram silver coin, at a face value of 10 yuan and a maximum mintage of 5 million, is the mass-market entry point for collectors. A 100-gram platinum bullion coin (3,000 yuan, 10,000 mintage) rounds out the bullion line.

[Guangming Daily](https://m.gmw.cn/2026-10/25/content_1304579140.htm), citing the PBOC announcement, published the full specifications and mintage for all 15 coins.

## Scarcity at the Top

The proof line, aimed at collectors, is defined by larger weights and far tighter mintages. The rarest coin in the set is the 1-kilogram gold proof — 90 mm in diameter, face value 10,000 yuan and a maximum mintage of just 2,000. It is followed by a 150-gram gold proof (2,000 yuan, 5,000 mintage), a 100-gram gold proof (1,500 yuan, 30,000) and a 50-gram gold proof (800 yuan, 40,000).

The silver proof coins — 1 kilogram (300 yuan, 10,000 mintage) and 150 grams (50 yuan, 30,000) — sit alongside two platinum proofs: 30 grams (1,000 yuan, 10,000) and 3 grams (100 yuan, 10,000).

## Distribution and Market Context

China Gold Coin Group Co., Ltd. is the sole general distributor for the set, with sales channels listed on the China Gold Coin website at www.chngc.net/qd, according to the Xinhua report. As reported by [National Business Daily](https://www.nbd.com.cn/articles/2026-10-23/4615209.html), the set will be issued on October 30, confirming the composition of seven bullion and eight proof coins.

The Panda series occupies a singular place in modern numismatics. Issued annually since 1982, it is widely regarded as the benchmark of China's precious metal commemorative coins — a "national treasure-level" product and one of the most stable categories in coin collecting, frequently recommended as an entry point for new collectors. The series has also served a dual role: bullion coins with low premiums meet investment demand, while proof coins with limited mintages cater to the collector market.

## What to Watch

Two questions will shape the set's reception. First, retail pricing for the bullion coins — the central bank typically announces it closer to or at issuance — will determine how attractive the gold and platinum coins are against spot metal prices. Second, demand for the platinum pieces will be watched closely, given that the metal remains a smaller and more volatile segment of the precious metals market than gold or silver.

For collectors and investors alike, the October 30 launch keeps a four-decade tradition intact — and, with three platinum coins in the mix, extends it a little further into new territory.
