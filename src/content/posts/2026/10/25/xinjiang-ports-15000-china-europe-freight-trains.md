# Xinjiang Ports Hit 15,000 China-Europe Trains in 2026

The two major railway ports of Alashankou and Horgos in China's northwestern Xinjiang region have handled more than 15,000 China-Europe and Central Asia freight train trips so far in 2026, with combined port freight throughput reaching 30.58 million tons, according to figures from China Railway Urumqi Group Co., Ltd. reported by [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/world/20261025/d8897b5dc7194177b19194b08e5480b8/c.html).

The milestone, reached as of October 23, underscores Xinjiang's continued rise as the land gateway for overland trade between China and Eurasia — a corridor Beijing has cast as a core component of its Belt and Road Initiative.

## A Capacity Boost Behind the Numbers

Railway authorities have deliberately expanded the throughput of the westward corridor. According to Xinhua, the Urumqi railway group raised the maximum train composition from 50 to 55 cars and increased the per-train loading weight from 2,500 to 3,000 tons — roughly a 10% gain in the freight moved per train.

The route network served by the two ports has also grown. Alashankou now handles 130 train routes reaching 21 countries, including Germany and Poland, while Horgos operates 91 routes connecting to 18 countries and 46 cities and regions abroad.

Earlier in the year, the dual ports had already crossed the 10,000-train mark, as reported by [China National Radio's Xinjiang channel](https://xj.cnr.cn/gstjxj/20260730/t20260730_527736519.shtml), which noted that the two ports were then handling an average of more than 50 China-Europe and Central Asia trains per day.

## Customs Reform Speeds the Flow

Behind the rising volume is a sustained push to shorten clearance times. Urumqi Customs has rolled out a "smart railway port plus local fast clearance" reform, cutting overall import transit time from two to three days to within 16 hours, and reducing local export clearance from six hours to one hour, according to the CNR report.

At Horgos, customs authorities have embedded oversight directly into rail operations, establishing a "priority marshalling plus fast release" green channel and deploying smart document filing, intelligent inspection and shared rail-customs data. At Alashankou, the customs office offers 7×24 appointment clearance and advance declaration, using smart imaging equipment to keep trains' port dwell time to a minimum.

The difference is tangible for traders. "Now customs clearance for trains is basically arrive-and-go; our goods clear from declaration to release several times faster than before, giving strong support for expanding into European markets," said Yang Yajie, a customs declarant at Xinjiang Alashankou Jie'an Logistics Co., Ltd., in remarks carried by CNR.

The trains carry more than 200 categories of goods, including auto parts, machinery, electronics and daily consumer products — a shift toward higher-value manufactured exports that has characterized the network's evolution.

## Why the Western Corridor Matters

China-Europe freight trains run along three domestic corridors: the Western Corridor through Alashankou and Horgos, the Central Corridor through Erenhot, and the Eastern Corridor through Manzhouli and other northeastern border crossings. Alashankou is China's largest single China-Europe freight train port by volume and ranks among six outbound gateways nationwide.

The Western Corridor's significance is structural. Analysis of the network has found that the western route accounts for more than half of national China-Europe rail volume in recent reference periods, reflecting Xinjiang's position as the principal overland bridge between China's manufacturing heartland and European and Central Asian markets.

The strategic logic extends beyond logistics. As global shipping faces periodic disruptions and rising costs, an efficient overland alternative gives Chinese exporters a degree of resilience and route diversification that maritime-only trade cannot offer. The growth also serves Beijing's domestic agenda of developing Xinjiang as a "bridgehead for westward opening" and an "Asia-Europe golden corridor."

## Official Data, Official Framing

An important caveat accompanies these figures: the primary data comes from China Railway Urumqi Group, a state operator, and was disseminated through state media. The numbers are authoritative in the sense that they reflect official railway records, but they have not been independently audited by a third party. Route counts also shift over time — Alashankou was reported as serving 129 routes in late July and 130 by late October — reflecting continued additions rather than any inconsistency.

The cumulative scale of the network provides context for the milestone. China-Europe freight trains surpassed 120,000 cumulative trips around late November 2025, carrying cargo valued at more than $490 billion, with the range of goods expanding to tens of thousands of product types.

## What to Watch

The next milestone to track is whether the dual ports can push past their full-year 2025 totals and sustain the more than 50 trains per day they now average through the winter months, when weather can slow rail operations in the region. Continued upgrades to the Jinghe–Alashankou second line, which reportedly lifted Alashankou's receiving capacity, will also determine how much more volume the corridor can absorb.

For now, the 15,000-train figure marks a clear statement: Xinjiang's role as the overland spine of China-Eurasia trade is not just holding steady — it is expanding.
