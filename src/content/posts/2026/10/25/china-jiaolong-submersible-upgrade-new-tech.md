# China's Upgraded Jiaolong Submersible Debuts New Tech

China's flagship crewed deep-sea submersible, the Jiaolong, has completed its first 7,000-metre-class sea trial following a second round of iterative upgrades and the domestic substitution of key components, according to [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/tech/20261025/b704a61683fe4e818701f6cb2826ba8c/c.html). Operated from the mother ship Shenhai Yihao, the vessel conducted three test dives in the Philippine Sea Basin, reaching a maximum depth of 7,056.9 metres — the deepest point for the entire second leg of China Ocean Voyage 100.

The milestone, reported on 25 October 2026, showcases both hardware and software advances: a new high-energy-density battery pack, domestically built thrusters, ultra-high-definition cameras, an AI-powered seabed detection system, and a fault-diagnosis decision-support tool now deployed aboard the submersible.

## From Zero to One: The Jiaolong Program

The Jiaolong is China's first independently developed large-depth crewed submersible, marking the country's leap "from zero to one" in crewed deep diving. It set a historic maximum depth of 7,062 metres in 2012 and won the 2017 National Science and Technology Progress Award, First Prize.

China now fields what officials describe as the world's most complete deep-diving equipment matrix. Alongside the Jiaolong sit the 4,500-metre-class Shenhai Yongshi (Deep Sea Warrior), with more than 95 percent domestic content, and the Fendouzhe, which reached the bottom of the Mariana Trench at 10,909 metres. Uncrewed vessels include the Haidou and Qianlong series, supported by the Tanjiao and Shenhai Yihao research ships.

## Three Keywords: Deeper, Longer, Clearer

Sun Yongfu, a researcher at the China National Deep Sea Center and head of the National Key R&D Program project on the Jiaolong's operational capability, described the upgraded vessel using three phrases, as Xinhua reported.

**"Dives deep."** The Jiaolong completed 94 dives in 2025, which Sun called "a milestone breakthrough for the year" and the foundation for this year's 7,000-metre trials. According to [CCTV reporting via CNR](https://news.cnr.cn/native/gd/20260103/t20260103_527481457.shtml), annual dive counts have risen sharply — from roughly 20 per year between 2013 and 2017 to 68 in 2024 and 94 in 2025.

**"Runs long."** A new 7,000-metre-class high-energy-density oil-immersed lithium battery pack extends a single underwater operation to as long as 10.8 hours. When the battery was introduced, [CCTV reported via Guangming Online](https://tech.gmw.cn/2025-04/09/content_37955345.htm) that recharge time fell from about 10 hours to three or four, enabling high-frequency diving. Domestically produced direct-drive low-noise DC thrusters have raised propulsion, pushing underwater speeds above 2.5 knots.

**"Sees clearly."** Wide-angle and high-magnification ultra-HD cameras allow the Jiaolong to shoot 4K footage, providing clear imagery even in the dark deep sea and supporting species identification and large-scale habitat mapping. Earlier CCTV coverage noted a 36x zoom 4K camera capable of resolving the fine hairs on sea anemone tentacles, and a video system upgraded to 10 channels of 4K high definition.

Other upgrades include expanded emergency hydraulic system interfaces, a deep-water hydraulic buoyancy adjustment system, a ship-borne 5G private network, and a deep-sea vessel monitoring system.

## AI Adds an "Intelligent Brain"

Beyond hardware, the Jiaolong now carries software designed to reduce the workload on its crew. Li Jinpeng, a postdoctoral researcher at the China National Deep Sea Center, developed a deep-sea seabed intelligent detection system based on a multimodal large model.

"With AI algorithms it can achieve real-time interpretation of the footage shot by the Jiaolong," Li said, according to Xinhua. Users can also "dialogue" with the system, which returns corresponding answers.

Li said he has "fed" the system more than 800,000 images of deep-sea benthic organisms and substrates. The model identifies typical organisms and substrates in video, automatically takes screenshots, and tags them with name, latitude and longitude, time, and depth. "Previously this information required operators to manually interpret and record it; after this system is applied it will greatly reduce their workload," he said.

A human correction loop allows users to edit incorrect screenshots and feed the data back so the system can learn and upgrade. The system also records dive tracks in 3D, analyzes organism counts and relative abundance, and can quickly generate a seabed ecological environment analysis report.

## Safety First: A Fault-Diagnosis Companion

Jiaolong pilot Zhang Yi demonstrated a fault-diagnosis auxiliary decision system developed to project requirements and operational needs. During dives, if the submersible malfunctions, the system promptly provides troubleshooting plans, historical fault solutions, and associated technical drawings and documents to aid pilot decisions.

"Promptly eliminating hidden dangers, resolving risks during dives, and guaranteeing the lives and safety of operating personnel — this is a very important matter aboard the Jiaolong," Zhang said.

The system is also intended to fill gaps in pilots' knowledge amid rapid hardware and software iterations, helping crews handle potential accidents promptly.

## An Open Door to Global Partners?

Sun framed the upgrades as a foundation for international cooperation. "The Jiaolong's completion of its iterative upgrade means we now have more stable and reliable conditions for large-depth deep-sea scientific research; in the future we can open and share with global scientific research partners, joining hands to deepen understanding of the deep-sea world," he said.

Li added that he will further develop the AI system to provide more intelligent data-processing tools supporting deep-sea international cooperation.

## What to Watch

This mission's 7,056.9-metre dive is a leg-specific maximum, not an all-time record — the Jiaolong's historic peak remains 7,062 metres, set in 2012. The distinction matters: the story here is not about breaking a depth record but about mature, repeatable capability. A support team that has shrunk from about 17 people in 2017 to roughly 10 today, and domestic thrusters that logged zero maintenance and zero faults across the 2025 season, are arguably more telling indicators of progress than any single number.

Still unanswered are the exact dates of the three Philippine Sea Basin dives, the full scale of China Ocean Voyage 100, and — perhaps most consequentially — whether the pledge to "open and share" the Jiaolong with foreign researchers translates into concrete, named partnerships. For now, the technical advances are clear. The international dimension remains a promise rather than a program.
