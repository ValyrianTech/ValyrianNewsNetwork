# NY AG James to Lead Cornell Rape Case Inquiry

New York Attorney General Letitia James will oversee a criminal investigation into allegations that a former Cornell University student was drugged and gang-raped by fraternity members in 2024, Gov. Kathy Hochul announced — an extraordinary intervention that strips a sitting local prosecutor of a closely watched case. James said Friday that her office is treating the inquiry as a criminal investigation and could pursue charges, according to [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/10/01/nx-s1-5987703/cornell-fraternity-rape-case-updates).

"We will review the facts, gather evidence, interview witnesses, and if warranted, pursue prosecutions," James said at a joint press conference with Hochul on Friday morning.

## Why the Governor Stepped In

Hochul appointed James as special prosecutor on Thursday evening, citing a loss of confidence in Tompkins County District Attorney Matthew Van Houten's handling of the case. At Friday's press conference, she sharply criticized both campus police and the local prosecutor for failing to conduct a thorough investigation, NPR reported.

"In what world would the police learn from a victim that she'd been literally gang raped while intoxicated by five men in a fraternity house and not elevate those allegations to the district attorney?" Hochul asked. The woman alleged seven men were involved; it is unclear why Hochul mentioned five.

Hochul also faulted Van Houten for not interviewing the woman or requesting a full transcript of her statements to police at the time. She said the discrepancies that have since surfaced "undercut my faith, and the public's faith, in the district attorney's ability to effectively investigate and prosecute the case."

"If someone is too intoxicated to consent, it should not matter whether they chose to use drugs or alcohol. Voluntary intoxication is not a license for sexual assault or gang-rape. Period," Hochul said, [according to The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/oct/02/new-york-cornell-alleged-gang-rape-kathy-hochul), which reported the governor described the alleged victim as having been "failed" by the system.

## The Allegations

The alleged assault occurred on October 19, 2024, at the Chi Phi fraternity house on or near the Cornell campus in Ithaca, N.Y., when the accuser — identified in court papers as "Jane Doe" — was 20 years old. She alleges she was drugged and raped by seven men over several hours.

In mid-September 2026, she filed a 101-page civil lawsuit in the Supreme Court of the State of New York against the seven men, the fraternity, Cornell University, her sorority (Delta Delta Delta), and a nightclub, Moonies, which she says negligently served her alcohol as a minor. Cornell has said its Title IX investigation resulted in expulsions and suspensions, though it has not specified how many students faced which consequences, citing federal privacy law. The Xi chapter of Chi Phi was banned from campus and has since been suspended indefinitely by the university.

## A Central Dispute Over the Evidence

The case has turned on a conflict between what the accuser told campus police and what the local prosecutor says he reviewed. Van Houten has said his 2024 decision not to prosecute rested on a signed six-page written statement that described the conduct as "voluntary, conscious, and consensual."

But [CBS News New York](https://www.cbsnews.com/newyork/news/cornell-university-rape-case-jane-doe-police-interview-transcript/) obtained a transcript of the accuser's interview with Cornell University Police, conducted over two days in November 2024. In it, she told an investigator, "I can say with 100 percent confidence that I was raped," and described being "completely and totally incapacitated" and subject to coercion.

Van Houten told NPR member station [WSKG](https://www.wskg.org/regional-news/2026-09-30/tompkins-county-da-says-if-factual-reported-police-transcript-with-jane-doe-would-have-changed-investigation) that he was in the process of obtaining the interview transcript from Cornell and did not know when he would receive it — "or if Cornell will resist turning it over."

"If it's factual, we absolutely should have been informed about what Jane Doe said to police that was not in her six-page statement," Van Houten said. He has also reopened the case and said a senior prosecutor has been assigned.

## Questions Over Cornell's Conduct

Cornell has defended its handling of the case, saying campus police investigated thoroughly and that the university held a months-long Title IX process. It has, however, agreed to bring in outside counsel for an independent review of how it handled the matter.

The university has also come under scrutiny for edits to a public FAQ page about the allegations. As WSKG [reported](https://www.wskg.org/regional-news/2026-10-01/cornell-quietly-edits-faq-page-about-fraternity-sexual-assault-allegations-amid-public-backlash), Cornell quietly removed a sentence stating that its police department had "shared all investigatory information with the Tompkins County District Attorney's Office" — a change that critics say deepens questions about what the prosecutor was told. Cornell did not respond to questions about the change.

The structure of the investigation itself has drawn criticism: the case was handled by Cornell University Police, a unit of the university, rather than municipal police. The Ithaca Police Department has said it was not informed of the case and learned of the allegations through the media.

"Any suggestion that the university did not impose consequential punishments for those involved is false," Cornell Vice President for University Relations Kyle Kimball told NPR, though students have expressed outrage and demanded "institutional accountability" at a public hearing.

## Legal and Political Implications

Hochul's decision to appoint a special prosecutor is a rare step that signals a breakdown of confidence in the local justice system. It also carries broader significance for how campus sexual assault cases are handled at elite universities, a perennial flashpoint in the post-#MeToo era.

The governor has said she is considering new legislation to close a "gap in state law" that makes it difficult to bring sexual-crime charges when an alleged victim was voluntarily intoxicated — even if incapacitated. New York already has among the toughest campus sexual assault laws in the country, but Hochul said she believes more can be done.

## What to Watch Next

The central questions now are whether James's office will convene a grand jury and bring charges, and against whom. Much may hinge on whether Cornell produces the full transcripts of the accuser's 2024 interviews, and on whether evidence gathered two years ago can still support a prosecution.

At the same time, Cornell's independent external review, the civil lawsuit, and any legislative push in Albany will unfold in parallel — each adding pressure on institutions that have struggled to explain how the case was handled.

"Every New Yorker deserves to know that when they report a crime, they will be investigated fully and fairly," James said. "That they will be seen and heard. I stand ready to lead a process guided by the facts and the law."
