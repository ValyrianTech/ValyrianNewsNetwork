# US Sends Third Carrier to Middle East in Iran Buildup

The United States is dispatching a third aircraft carrier strike group and thousands of additional troops to the Middle East, a significant military buildup that comes as President Donald Trump weighs escalating strikes on Iran after the November midterm elections. The deployments could bring three US carriers near Iran by the end of October and push total US forces in the region above 50,000, according to [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/world/20261002/4844d7f66ea94c44ae4faeac2c49694b/c.html), which relayed an initial report by The Wall Street Journal citing US officials.

## What Is Being Deployed

The USS Theodore Roosevelt carrier strike group departed San Diego on Sunday, Sept. 27, on a scheduled deployment, while the USS Makin Island Amphibious Ready Group — three ships carrying the 13th Marine Expeditionary Unit — left a day later. According to [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/10/2/new-aircraft-carrier-10000-us-troops-is-the-iran-war-about-to-escalate), a US official said the combined force would position three aircraft carriers and two amphibious landing groups near Iran by the end of November.

The group of ships now en route carries more than 7,000 sailors and 2,000 Marines, [The Guardian reported](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/oct/02/us-third-aircraft-carrier-middle-east-iran). The full buildup could involve an additional 9,000 to 10,000 troops in total, including sailors and Marines assigned to the carrier strike group and other ships.

Two US carriers are already in the region — the USS George H.W. Bush and the USS George Washington — along with the USS Boxer amphibious readiness group. If the George Washington remains after the Roosevelt arrives, it would mark the first time since April 2026 that the US has had three carrier strike groups in the Middle East. The Washington, typically deployed to the Pacific and ported in Japan, was moved to the region to relieve the USS Abraham Lincoln after its crew spent more than 260 days at sea straight.

## Readiness Concerns After a Record Deployment

The new deployment has been shaped in part by the strain of that earlier rotation. According to the [New York Post](https://nypost.com/2026/10/01/us-news/trump-sends-3rd-aircraft-carrier-to-middle-east-with-10k-more-troops-as-he-weighs-post-midterm-iran-assault/), reports of weary sailors attempting to jump overboard amid shortages of critical supplies — after Iranian strikes disrupted supply lines, including a key port in Bahrain — prompted the Pentagon to prepare the Theodore Roosevelt for a long stay. Chief of Naval Operations Adm. Daryl Caudle said in a video announcement that the reforms include “new entertainment equipment on the mess decks, more durable fitness equipment, spare cardio machines ready for our placement, and even holiday materials purchased ahead of time.”

## A War at a Stalemate

The buildup unfolds as the 2026 Iran war enters its eighth month. The conflict began Feb. 28 with joint US-Israeli strikes and has since settled into a costly stalemate, with oil prices remaining above pre-war levels and indirect US-Iran talks stalled. Last week, Trump rejected an Iranian proposal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz within seven days in exchange for sanctions relief, the lifting of a US naval blockade, and a regional ceasefire.

In an interview with [Time magazine](https://time.com/article/2026/10/01/donald-trump-2026-interview/) published Oct. 1, Trump said it was “possible” he would ramp up bombing in Iran after the Nov. 3 midterm elections, though he declined to give details. He also said he had rejected the latest ceasefire proposal because it included terms he “wouldn't have approved a year ago” and “wouldn't have today.”

The diplomatic track has shown little progress. As [CNBC reported](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/02/us-iran-war-trump-hormuz-.html), Secretary of State Marco Rubio ordered Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi's delegation to leave New York earlier than planned after Qatari-mediated indirect talks failed to produce a breakthrough. The same day, US Treasury expanded sanctions on Iran's auto and rail sectors. The war has also spilled into the wider region: a Saudi-led coalition accused the Iran-backed Houthis of a drone attack on a power station supplying the Prophet's Mosque in Medina, and a tanker was struck by an unknown projectile while leaving the Strait of Hormuz.

## Analysis and Implications

On its face, the deployment gives Trump a wider menu of military options. Al Jazeera reported that the Pentagon is not currently planning to withdraw any assets even if an agreement with Iran is reached, and Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth has said forces are “ready to go” should Trump order new attacks. Some analysts have suggested the buildup could also serve as a negotiating lever, pressuring Tehran ahead of any future talks.

The timing is politically charged. The war is deeply unpopular in the United States, and the November midterms could punish Trump's Republican Party for his handling of it. Trump has countered that the conflict should help his party because Iran will not obtain a nuclear weapon — a framing he repeated in the Time interview, arguing that “by annihilating Iran, we've created peace in the world.”

## What to Watch

Several questions remain unresolved. It is not yet clear whether the new deployment is meant to relieve another carrier or add to the force posture, and whether the George Washington stays or returns to Japan. The precise timeline for the full buildup — as early as late October or by the end of November — varies by source. And with three carriers and two amphibious groups potentially massing near Iran, the central question is whether the buildup is a bargaining chip in stalled diplomacy or the opening move of a larger campaign after the midterms.
