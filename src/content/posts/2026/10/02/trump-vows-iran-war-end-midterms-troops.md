# Trump Vows Iran War to End as Midterms Loom

President Donald Trump is vowing that the seven-month U.S. war with Iran will end "right after the election" — the November 3 midterm elections — if not sooner, even as Washington is dispatching roughly 9,000 additional troops and a third aircraft carrier strike group to the Middle East, according to [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/live-updates/trump-iran-war-us-midterm-elections-troops-deployment/). The dual message — a promise of peace paired with a major military buildup — has tied a stalled foreign conflict to mounting domestic political stakes.

Speaking Thursday in Denton, Texas, Trump said Iran "is ready to fold up" and that the United States would win the war "right after the election," adding, "maybe before the election." He also declared, "I'm ensuring that Iran will be very quickly ended." Iran's leadership has publicly rejected that characterization.

## A War With Two Clocks

The conflict is now in its seventh month. U.S. and Israeli strikes on Iran began February 28, 2026, per [Iran International](https://www.iranintl.com/en/202609194831). Since then, the war has become inseparable from the American political calendar, with both sides facing electoral deadlines: the U.S. midterms on November 3, and a general election in Israel this October, as [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/9/10/trumps-iran-war-now-has-a-midterm-election-problem) reported.

Iran, by contrast, faces no imminent national vote. Its unelected Supreme Leader and its next legislative elections — not due until 2028 — grant Tehran a longer runway, a dynamic analysts say has shaped its strategy of endurance.

"Iran has prepared for a long war," Sanam Vakil, MENA director at Chatham House, told Al Jazeera. "It may be that part of that was a secondary plan to hold out long enough to destabilise the US midterm elections once it realised that negotiations with the current administration were not going anywhere."

## The Military Buildup

The U.S. is deploying the USS Theodore Roosevelt Carrier Strike Group and the USS Makin Island Amphibious Group to the Middle East — roughly 7,000 sailors and 2,000 Marines, about 9,000 forces in total. The [Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/iran-war-trump-ships-deployment-middle-east-42c23e9af0333f4bc3b4f419cc3a603e) reported that the move would bring U.S. naval presence in the region to roughly 20,000 sailors and Marines.

According to [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/oct/02/us-third-aircraft-carrier-middle-east-iran), the buildup could place three carrier strike groups in the region at once — the first time at that level since 2003. The USS George H.W. Bush and USS George Washington are already in the area, along with the USS Boxer amphibious readiness group. Forces could arrive by the end of October, just days before voters head to the polls.

Two additional U.S. Patriot missile batteries were reportedly sent to Saudi Arabia and Qatar to protect oil and gas facilities, according to [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/10/02/patriot-missiles-iran-war-saudi-arabia-qatar). The deployments reflect persistent concern that Iran could retaliate against Gulf energy infrastructure.

## Iran Pushes Back

Tehran has rejected Trump's suggestion that its theocracy is on the verge of collapse. On Friday, Mohsen Rezaei, secretary of Iran's Supreme National Security Council, mocked the president on X, calling him "the worst president in American history" and writing, "You can win every day in your own imagination. But reality is shaped by Iran — by its people, its soldiers, and its missiles."

In a [TIME magazine interview](https://time.com/article/2026/10/01/donald-trump-2026-interview-transcript/) published Thursday, Trump said it was "possible" he would ramp up bombing in Iran after the midterms, and rejected Iran's latest ceasefire proposal as "not good enough." Asked whether the unpopular war threatened Republican midterm chances, he replied, "It's possible... It should help, because Iran will not have a nuclear weapon."

Iran's conditions for negotiations — conveyed through Qatari and Pakistani mediators — are ending the war on all fronts, releasing seized Iranian assets, and lifting the naval blockade, Rezaei said. As of late September, the U.S. response had been delivered via Qatar and described as "feedback" rather than acceptance or rejection, Iran International [reported](https://www.iranintl.com/en/202609302992).

## Economic Pressure on Both Sides

The war's economic toll is being felt in Washington and Tehran alike. U.S. gasoline prices have climbed to a national average of $4.27 per gallon, up nearly 30% year over year. Trump's approval rating has fallen from the high-40s in January 2025 to the low-to-mid 30s. A Marquette Law School poll conducted September 2–9 found that 79% of respondents said the war had not been worth its cost, 73% disapproved of Trump's handling of the conflict, and only 20% approved of his handling of gasoline prices.

Democrats have seized on the issue ahead of the midterms. Representative Ro Khanna of California introduced an "Illegal War Refund Act" proposing $1,700 payments to taxpayers, arguing the war "has cost Americans in food and gas prices an estimated $230 billion." The political scientist H.A. Hellyer of the Center for American Progress told Al Jazeera that the decisive question was turnout: "It's not that many members of the Republican base will suddenly switch to supporting the Democrats, as much as they'll stay home and not vote."

Iran, meanwhile, is under severe strain. Its currency crossed 2.5 million rials to the dollar in late September, losing roughly 44% of its value in six months. Oil exports have fallen from about 2 million barrels per day before the war to roughly 220,000–255,000 barrels per day in August. Inflation runs above 60–70%, and medicine prices have risen roughly 103% this year.

To blunt the global energy shock, G7 leaders agreed Friday to a "coordinated release" of 100 million barrels of oil, including a front-loaded diesel release within the first 20 days, following an emergency video conference, according to an [Élysée Palace statement](https://www.elysee.fr/emmanuel-macron/2026/10/02/g7-leaders-statement-on-global-energy-security-and-market-stability).

## The Hormuz Lever — and Its Limits

Iran's principal lever remains the Strait of Hormuz, the chokepoint through which a fifth of the world's oil passes. Its parliament has advanced a bill to formalize Iranian "management" of the strait, including fees on commercial vessels and bans on ships linked to Israel or hostile states. The measure still requires full parliamentary approval and Guardian Council review.

That leverage, however, is eroding. Regional Persian Gulf oil exports have recovered to nearly 80% of pre-war levels, with Saudi Arabia, Iraq, and the UAE exporting about 13 million barrels per day, according to The Wall Street Journal, as cited by Iran International. The recovery undercuts Iran's ability to squeeze global markets through the strait.

## What to Watch

Trump's framing — a quick victory tied to Election Day — offers voters a near-term horizon. Whether that horizon is achievable is another matter. Iran shows no sign of capitulating, diplomacy via Qatar and Pakistan remains stalled, and Tehran has looked increasingly to Beijing as a potential bridge to Washington. China and Russia recently vetoed a U.S.-backed UN resolution extending the mandate of a panel monitoring sanctions on Iran.

With roughly 9,000 more American troops heading to the region and a third carrier group on the way, the coming weeks will test whether the promise of an end to the war is a negotiating position, an electoral message, or a genuine turning point. For now, both the battlefield and the ballot box remain unresolved.
