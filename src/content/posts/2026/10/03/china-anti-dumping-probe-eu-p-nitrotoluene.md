# China Opens Anti-Dumping Probe into EU P-Nitrotoluene

China's Ministry of Commerce (MOFCOM) announced on Saturday that it has formally launched an anti-dumping investigation into p-nitrotoluene (PNT) imported from the European Union, opening a new front in the rapidly escalating trade-remedy confrontation between Beijing and Brussels.

The probe, effective October 3, follows an application filed on September 14 by Jiangsu Huaihe Chemicals Co. and Hubei Dongfang Chemical Industry Co. on behalf of China's domestic PNT industry, according to the state-run [Xinhua News Agency](https://www.news.cn/world/20261003/a8a7dacbd2fc44fbba5f2512271dd71e/c.html).

## A Key Chemical Intermediate

P-nitrotoluene, also known as 4-nitrotoluene, is an organic compound used to produce p-toluidine, DSD acid, p-nitrobenzoic acid, toluene diisocyanate (TDI), and tolidine. Those derivatives feed into the dyes, pigments, pesticides, and pharmaceuticals sectors, making PNT a strategically important upstream intermediate for multiple value chains.

MOFCOM said it reviewed the applicants' qualifications, the product under investigation, the domestic like product, the impact on domestic industry, and relevant country conditions before deciding to open the case under China's Anti-Dumping Regulations.

## Dumping Margin Alleges Over 100%

Citing the applicants' petition, the [Global Times](https://www.globaltimes.cn/page/202610/1371662.shtml) reported that preliminary evidence shows the alleged dumping margin on EU-origin PNT exports to China exceeding 100 percent. The Chinese producers alleged that EU exporters adopted a low-price dumping strategy to maintain or expand their share of the Chinese market.

MOFCOM also cited industry data showing that EU-origin PNT imports remained at high levels between 2022 and 2025 while prices fell by nearly 60 percent cumulatively over the period.

"The dumped imports from the EU had caused damage to the production and operations of China's domestic industry," the ministry said in its statement.

The investigation will examine alleged dumping from July 1, 2025 to June 30, 2026, and is expected to conclude by October 3, 2027, with a possible six-month extension under special circumstances.

MOFCOM stressed that the application met the conditions for initiating an anti-dumping investigation under Chinese law and World Trade Organization rules, and that China would conduct the probe in accordance with laws and regulations and make an objective and impartial ruling.

## A Tit-for-Tat Trade Environment

The PNT probe is the latest in a widening series of trade-remedy actions between the two economies. MOFCOM noted that the EU has launched 28 trade-remedy investigations against China since 2025, around 50 percent of which involved chemical products, and that the bloc had recently opened three investigations into Chinese chemical products in succession, including polyvinyl chloride.

The EU has moved aggressively against Chinese chemicals and other goods through 2025 and 2026. On July 28, 2026, Brussels published four anti-dumping regulations against Chinese products in a single day, targeting polyamide yarn, sodium benzoate, benzyl alcohol, and ferrosilicon and combining definitive duties, provisional duties, and expiry-review continuations, according to [ChemNet](https://news.chemnet.com/toutiao/detail-76561.html).

The broader context is a record EU goods-trade deficit with China of roughly €359.9 billion in 2025, up nearly 18 percent from the prior year, alongside Chinese retaliatory probes into European pork, brandy, and dairy. As [Peacock Tariff Consulting](https://www.peacocktariffconsulting.com/left-with-few-choices-inside-the-eus-trade-fight-with-china/) noted in a June 2026 briefing, EU officials have described the economic relationship with Beijing as "no longer sustainable" while conceding that China will almost certainly retaliate.

China has consistently framed its own trade remedies as restrained. "China has repeatedly stated that the EU needs to squarely address the economic and trade issues it faces and resolve concerns between the two sides through dialogue and consultation," a MOFCOM spokesperson said.

## What's Next

The investigation remains at its initiation stage, and any provisional or final measures would not take effect until 2027. The applicants argue that timely anti-dumping action would help restore what they describe as distorted market competition, safeguard China's PNT producers, and support the stability of broader industrial and supply chains.

Notably, EU-origin PNT has drawn similar scrutiny elsewhere. India's Directorate General of Trade Remedies has pursued its own anti-dumping cases against EU-origin PNT in recent years, suggesting the alleged pricing behavior has attracted complaints across multiple major markets.

How Brussels responds — and whether the PNT case becomes another round in an accelerating spiral or a prompt for renewed dialogue — will be a key test of whether the world's second- and third-largest economies can still manage their differences without a broader rupture.
