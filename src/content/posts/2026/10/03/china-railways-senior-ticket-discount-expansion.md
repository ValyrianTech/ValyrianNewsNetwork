# China Railways Expands Senior Ticket Discounts

China State Railway Group (China Railway) announced on October 3, 2026 that it is expanding its ticket discount program for elderly passengers, removing a weekday-only restriction and extending the eligible travel window in a move aimed at making rail travel more affordable for seniors. Under the new measure, passengers aged 60 and above can receive a "discount on discount" (折上折) of 10 percent off the already-reduced fare on designated trains marked with the character "敬" (jìng, meaning "respect"), for travel between October 18 and December 30, 2026, according to [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/politics/20261003/cd4cbef523a64982abe56ef032c2a335/c.html). Discounted tickets go on sale progressively from October 4.

## Context: A Pilot Becomes a Broader Policy

The expansion builds on a pilot program introduced earlier this year. In April 2026, China Railway announced a limited senior discount tied to the May 19 "China Tourism Day" travel period, offering 10 percent off the executed fare on select bullet trains — but only during non-holiday weekday hours, defined as Monday noon to Friday noon, for travel between May 30 and June 30, as [IT Home reported](https://www.ithome.com/0/943/144.htm). That first iteration was framed as a public-benefit measure tied to off-peak travel demand.

The October revision marks a clear broadening. The discount now covers an off-peak autumn and early-winter window that includes weekends, rather than being confined to the middle of the work week. The eligible trains are identified by the "敬" character on the 12306 booking platform, a symbol the railway operator has used to signal senior-friendly services.

## Key Details of the Expanded Measure

According to [IT Home](https://www.ithome.com/1/009/404.htm), the discount applies to passengers aged 60 and above who purchase tickets using a range of accepted identity documents, including the PRC Resident Identity Card, Hong Kong and Macau Residence Permits, the Taiwan Residence Permit, Home Return Permits, Mainland Travel Permits for Taiwan residents, the Foreigner's Permanent Residence ID, Temporary Identity Certificates, and Household Registration Booklets.

The savings are described as "折上折" — a discount applied on top of the executed fare, meaning the 10 percent reduction stacks on any existing promotional pricing rather than replacing it. Qualifying tickets are labeled "长者优惠" (Senior Discount) in order details and on the passenger's ticket page.

Two additional benefits reinforce the program. Seniors who hold a Disabled Military Personnel Certificate, Disabled People's Police Certificate, or National Comprehensive Fire and Rescue Team Disabled Personnel Certificate can receive a further 10 percent off on top of their existing preferential fares. And elderly frequent-traveler members who buy and complete their journeys continue to earn three times the normal loyalty points, redeemable for tickets or seat upgrades.

## Analysis: A Policy Wired Into China's Aging Strategy

The discount is more than a fare adjustment. It sits within a widening set of policies tied to China's rapidly aging population and its push to develop the "silver economy." Under the 15th Five-Year Plan, Beijing has explicitly called for expanding age-friendly products and services, and rail travel is a designated growth area. China Railway has set targets to design more than 100 senior-focused tourism routes and build 160 sets of age-friendly train cars by 2027.

The timing also reflects operational logic. The eligible window runs from mid-October through late December — a period after the National Day holiday peak when railways typically have spare capacity. By extending the discount to weekends, the operator may be trading a small amount of peak revenue for higher off-peak utilization, while also answering growing expectations that public services accommodate older citizens. The September 2026 optimization of discounts for children, disabled military personnel, police, and firefighters suggests a broader, systematic recalibration of fares across passenger categories.

Notably, the policy has drawn little critical commentary in mainstream sources. Coverage has been largely descriptive and positive, with state media framing it as a "惠民举措" — a people-serving measure — and part of ongoing "适老化服务" (age-friendly service) improvements.

## What to Watch

Several questions remain open as the program rolls out. It is not yet clear how many trains and seats carry the "敬" designation, what the fiscal cost or ridership impact will be, or whether the discount will become a permanent, year-round feature after the December 30 cutoff. How effectively older passengers — some of whom face barriers with digital booking apps — can access the discount will also shape its real-world impact.

For now, the expanded measure offers a concrete example of how China is attempting to fold the needs of its growing senior population into everyday public services — one train ticket at a time.
