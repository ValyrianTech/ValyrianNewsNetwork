# China's Golden Week: Record Travel, Muted Spending

China's National Day "Golden Week" is under way, and by most visible measures it is a resounding success: packed high-speed rail stations, sold-out long-haul flights and a record wave of travelers heading abroad. But beneath the bustle lies a more complicated story. As [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/fortune/20261003/a3f81ff9db5f40a6806480b1293b64ad/c.html) presents it, the holiday showcases the "world's consumption vitality" — yet economists caution that soaring travel volumes do not necessarily translate into equally strong spending.

## A Historically Long Holiday

This year's break is unusually generous. The Mid-Autumn Festival fell on Sept. 25–27, and National Day runs Oct. 1–7. While not an official 13-day holiday, authorities explicitly encouraged workers to combine the two with paid annual leave, producing a continuous break approaching two weeks. That structure shifted a single Oct. 1 travel crush into three distinct waves: long-haul departures on Sept. 25–26, family trips around Oct. 1 and a shoulder wave of shorter, off-peak trips in between.

The National Immigration Administration forecast daily port crossings would exceed 2 million, with peaks on Oct. 1 and Oct. 6. Outbound travel demand has been strikingly strong. A survey by Dragon Trail International found that 54 percent of respondents planned overseas trips this holiday, up sharply from 35 percent a year earlier, according to [DW's Chinese-language service](https://www.dw.com/zh/%E4%B8%AD%E5%9B%BD%E9%BB%84%E9%87%91%E5%91%A8%E6%97%85%E6%B8%B8%E7%83%AD%E4%BA%86%E6%B6%88%E8%B4%B9%E8%BF%98%E6%98%AF%E5%86%B7/a-79472743). Ctrip data cited in the same report showed bookings for hotel stays of seven nights or more up 123 percent year-on-year, with average outbound trips exceeding nine days.

## The Volume-Spending Gap

The central tension is what analysts call a "K-shaped" consumption pattern: travel participation is high, but spending per traveler is under pressure. "The enthusiasm for travel is high, but consumers remain very cautious about how much they spend," Ailsa Liao, a senior analyst at Forthright Securities, told DW. "This creates a K-shaped consumption pattern: the number of travelers may be high, but per-capita spending remains under pressure."

The numbers bear this out. Over the 2025 eight-day National Day holiday, China recorded 888 million domestic trips and total spending of 809.006 billion yuan — roughly 911 yuan per capita, a near-three-year low. That pattern appears to be persisting into 2026. Domestic tourist spending per trip fell 3.3 percent in the first half of the year, and Sienna Parulis-Cook, marketing and PR director at Dragon Trail International, noted that "tourists are more focused on whether the travel experience they get is worth the money."

## A Macroeconomic Backdrop of Caution

The holiday's vibrancy sits against a backdrop of subdued domestic demand. According to China's National Bureau of Statistics, retail sales rose just 1.1 percent year-on-year in the January–August period, and only 0.4 percent in August alone. Big-ticket categories were notably weak: auto sales fell 18.5 percent, furniture 7.9 percent and building materials 11.8 percent.

The property sector, a key drag on household confidence, has yet to stabilize. Real estate development investment fell 19.9 percent year-on-year through August, while sales of new commercial housing dropped 13.0 percent. Against that backdrop, households are prioritizing cash flow and balance-sheet repair over discretionary spending.

The analysis from the Chinese financial commentator Xu Sanlang, writing for [Tencent's Huoxing Hongguan](https://news.qq.com/rain/a/20261001A07DAD00), captures the mismatch. Xu writes that China's retail sales grew only 1.1 percent in the first eight months of 2026, and August growth was a mere 0.4 percent, with large-ticket consumption under sustained pressure. "More people does not mean more spending," he wrote, adding that "tourism first requires free time, while consumption truly requires income." He cautioned that "excitement can be manufactured by a holiday, but sustained consumption prosperity ultimately depends on income."

## Where the Growth Really Is

Still, the holiday has generated genuine momentum in specific segments. The most striking is outbound long-haul travel. According to an [H33 Research report](https://www.h33.top/report/20261002-74d0) on the 2026 National Day outbound market, bookings for overseas trips exceeding 10 days rose 62 percent quarter-on-quarter, while demand for visa-free long-haul destinations surged roughly 300 percent. Deep, immersive experiences — cultural tours, research travel and outdoor exploration — climbed around 50 percent.

Crucially, 2026 has seen historic visa liberalization. As of this year, Chinese citizens can travel visa-free or with visa-on-arrival to 90 countries and territories, a record high. Turkey's visa-free arrangement took effect on Jan. 2, and Brazil's on May 11, which reportedly triggered a tenfold jump in flight searches. These policy shifts have turned once-marginal destinations into viable options for time-pressed Chinese travelers. Europe captured roughly half of outbound bookings, while Japan — traditionally a top destination — saw a sharp contraction, with Chinese visitor numbers down 60.4 percent year-on-year in May and for six consecutive months, amid only 53.5 percent recovery in route capacity.

## What to Watch

The picture that emerges is one of a holiday that is both vibrant and strained — a window revealing the shape of Chinese consumer demand rather than resolving its contradictions. Travel is real, and for many households it remains an affordable priority. But the household decisions that drive broader economic recovery — large purchases, discretionary spending, commitments tied to confidence about future income — are still waiting for the conditions that made this Golden Week look so busy.

The official post-holiday figures from the Ministry of Culture and Tourism, the Ministry of Commerce and the NBS will be the real test. If per-capita spending again fails to keep pace with volumes, the gap will reinforce the view that China's consumer recovery is real but uneven — strong where it is cheap to participate and cautious where it requires a commitment. For now, the holiday has proven that Chinese travelers are ready to move. Whether they are ready to spend is a different question.
