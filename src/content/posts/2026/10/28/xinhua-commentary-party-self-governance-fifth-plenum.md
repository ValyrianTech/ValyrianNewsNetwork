# Xinhua Commentary Frames Party Self-Governance Agenda

Xinhua News Agency published the third and final installment of a commentary series on Xi Jinping thought on Party building on 27 October 2026, framing the ruling Communist Party's capacity for "self-purification, self-improvement, self-innovation and self-enhancement" — a doctrine of internal renewal timed to coincide with a major Party gathering in Beijing. The piece, [titled](https://www.news.cn/politics/leaders/20261027/855f3ab636f842d39ca0014a3c313724/c.html) *"Continuously Achieving the Party's Self-Purification, Self-Improvement, Self-Innovation and Self-Enhancement,"* was written by Xinhua reporter Xiong Feng and released at 22:40 local time.

## Context: Messaging During a Plenum

The single most important fact behind the commentary is its timing. The 20th Central Committee's Fifth Plenum is convening in Beijing from 26 to 29 October 2026, and its stated agenda is to "study major issues concerning persistently advancing full and rigorous Party self-governance," according to a [People's Daily theory piece](https://paper.people.com.cn/rmrb/pc/content/202610/27/content_30187812.html) published on page 9 of the 27 October edition.

The three-part series — published on consecutive days from 25 to 27 October — thus functions as strategic communication issued while the Plenum is in session, designed to frame the meeting's agenda and build ideological momentum for what observers expect to be a formal decision or resolution on Party building. A Politburo meeting on 21 September had already signaled the theme, calling for "deeply recognizing the importance and urgency of persistently advancing full and rigorous Party self-governance."

Parts One and Two addressed the Party's overall leadership and its ties with the public, respectively. Part Three turns to the institutional machinery of discipline, anti-corruption and regulation.

## The Mechanics of Self-Governance

The commentary anchors its argument in a leadership quotation from Xi Jinping: *"Advancing Party building as a great project, and consistently upholding the principles and policies of the Party governing itself and governing itself strictly, is a great creation of our Party and a great magic weapon for building the Party and the country and rejuvenating the Party and strengthening the country."*

It cites Xi's speech at the 20th Central Commission for Discipline Inspection's Fifth Plenary Session in January 2026, which deployed what the piece calls a "three mores" formulation: implementing Party Central Committee decisions "more resolutely and effectively," locking power into the "cage of institutions" "more scientifically and effectively," and advancing the anti-corruption struggle "more clearly and firmly."

To illustrate the campaign's scale, the commentary reports that in the first half of 2026 disciplinary inspection and supervision organs nationwide filed 538,000 cases and disciplined 412,000 people, including 74 officials at provincial-ministerial level or above. Those figures are consistent with broader nationwide statistics for January to September 2026, reported separately, which showed higher cumulative totals — 800,000 cases filed and 680,000 people disciplined, including 105 at provincial-ministerial level or above.

The piece also highlights institutional innovations. At the end of 2025, the General Offices of the CCDI and the National Supervisory Commission issued "Evidence Guidelines for Investigating New-Type and Hidden Corruption," aimed at solving difficulties in detecting, gathering evidence on and characterizing emerging forms of graft. In 2026, a Party-wide education campaign on establishing a correct view of political performance targeted leadership teams and cadres at county-division level and above, with particular attention to top officials.

On regulation, the commentary traces an accelerated pace of intra-Party rule-making, citing the first central document specifically devoted to deploying intra-Party regulation construction, two national conferences on the subject, and three successive five-year plans. It marks the 4 December 2012 Eight-Point Regulation as the first major intra-Party regulation adopted by the Politburo after the 18th Party Congress.

## Analysis: The "Self-Revolution" Doctrine

The title's "four selfs" belong to the canonical "self-revolution" framework, which positions the Party as capable of reforming itself without external pressure. In an official genre that blends reporting with political messaging, the emphasis on institutionalization — what the Party calls "rule-based Party governance" — is significant. It suggests the Plenum's expected outcome will lean toward new rules and mechanisms rather than a change in campaign intensity.

The commentary also acknowledges the challenge facing that project. It describes the "four major tests" and "four dangers" as persisting long term, and warns that the anti-corruption situation "remains severe and complex." Quoting Xi, it urges the Party to "pursue the exhausted bandits with the remaining courage," maintaining strategic resolve while continuing to "root out and clean up."

The piece closes by invoking Xi's 22 October speech at the Great Hall of the People marking the 90th anniversary of the Long March victory, in which he called for persistently advancing full and rigorous Party governance to ensure the Party "always remains the strong leadership core of the cause of socialism with Chinese characteristics."

## What to Watch

The Fifth Plenum concludes on 29 October. The key question is whether it produces a formal communiqué or a dedicated decision on Party governance — documents that would translate the commentary's framing into binding policy. Also worth watching is how the anti-corruption campaign's intensity carries into the 15th Five-Year Plan period, given the historically high number of senior officials disciplined in 2026.

For now, the Xinhua series stands as a deliberate ideological marker: a statement of continuity, issued mid-meeting, that the Party intends to keep its self-imposed discipline at full strength.
