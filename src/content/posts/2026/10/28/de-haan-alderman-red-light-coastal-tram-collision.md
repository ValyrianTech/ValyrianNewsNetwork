# De Haan alderman runs red light, hits coastal tram

A first alderman in the Belgian coastal town of De Haan drove through a red light at high speed and collided with the coastal tram, newly surfaced CCTV footage has revealed, prompting scrutiny of a public official's conduct behind the wheel. Rudi Cattrysse, the town's first alderman for culture and civil affairs, walked away unhurt and no one on board the tram was injured — but his car was badly damaged.

The collision occurred on 9 October at the intersection of the Koninklijk Plein and the Koninklijke Baan, beside De Haan's famous art-nouveau tram shelter, [VRT NWS reported](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/10/28/schepen-de-haan-betrokken-bij-ongeval-met-kusttram-pure-verstr/). The incident only became public on 28 October, when camera footage of the crash emerged.

## 'Pure absent-mindedness'

Cattrysse attributes the accident to what he called pure verstrooidheid — sheer distraction — after a hectic day. According to the alderman's account, he had closed his bicycle shop and was due to give a speech at a season-presentation event at the Wielingen cultural centre in Wenduine. He had forgotten his text and was driving back to his shop to retrieve it.

"At the spot where the accident happened, there are traffic lights both for the tram tracks and for the actual intersection with the Koninklijke Baan," Cattrysse told VRT NWS. "I saw the second pair of traffic lights on green and wanted to just make it, but meanwhile the lights for the tram tracks turned red and I no longer saw that. Before I knew it, I collided with that tram."

The alderman stressed that the crash was handled by the book. "We immediately called the police. My breath and saliva tests were negative," he said, adding that he was well supported — including by the tram driver, who was himself shaken by the collision. Damage to his car is being settled between insurers.

## Mayor closes ranks

Mayor Wilfried Vandaele publicly defended his colleague, ruling out immediate political consequences. "This can happen to anyone," the mayor said. "Rudi barely drinks alcohol and was sober then too. He is himself enormously shocked by what happened."

The mayor then explained why he felt personally involved: Cattrysse had been due to pick up the mayor's own wife and bring her to the season presentation in Wenduine. "It was in fact the intention that he would pick up my wife to go to that presentation," Vandaele said. "She would have liked to attend, but I had other obligations myself. So I am also somewhat embarrassed by it. I am above all relieved that no one was injured."

According to the municipality of De Haan's [official college listing](https://www.dehaan.be/college-van-burgemeester-en-schepenen), Cattrysse serves as first alderman with responsibility for the library, civil affairs, culture, the music and art academy, and foreigner affairs. Both he and Vandaele belong to Lijst Burgemeester, the localist group around the mayor.

## A tricky intersection

The crash shines a light on a genuinely confusing piece of coastal infrastructure. At the Koninklijk Plein, drivers encounter separate signals for the tram tracks and for the road intersection — a dual configuration that can catch out even local road users.

The Kusttram itself is a Belgian institution. Running roughly 67 kilometres along the entire coast from Knokke to Adinkerke and operated by De Lijn, it dates back to 1885 and carried about 16.5 million passengers a year as of 2017, according to [Wikipedia's account of the line](https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kusttram). Much of it runs on segregated track but crosses roads at level crossings equipped with dedicated tram signals — precisely the setup Cattrysse described.

## Accountability questions

The story's late emergence — nearly three weeks after the fact — is what gave it public traction. The footage, rather than the collision itself, turned a minor traffic accident into a question about how elected officials conduct themselves on public roads.

The mayor's swift intervention appears designed to draw a line under the episode. There has been no indication of disciplinary, legal or political consequences beyond the insurance settlement, and the coalition around Lijst Burgemeester is closing ranks.

Still, the case raises open questions. Opposition parties on the De Haan council could press for clarity, and the dual-signal layout at the Koninklijk Plein may invite renewed scrutiny of whether the crossing is safely designed for the volume of traffic and tourism it handles.

For now, the immediate facts are modest: a hurried alderman, a red light, a damaged car and no injuries. But in a small coastal town where local politics is personal, the image of an official running a red light into the coastal tram is likely to linger.

## What to watch

Whether the episode prompts formal questions in the De Haan council, and whether the intersection's signal configuration comes under review, will determine if a private lapse becomes a public issue. The mayor has made clear he hopes it will not.
