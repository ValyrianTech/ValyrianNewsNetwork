# Nicotine Pouches: Banned, Yet Everywhere Among Youth

They are illegal to sell in Belgium, yet they are turning up in night shops, on foreign websites and in school classrooms. A joint investigation by [VRT NWS Pano and Knack](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/10/26/vrt-nws-pano-onderzoekt-snus-nicotine-in-een-zakje/) published this week finds that nicotine pouches have become a widespread, discreet and highly addictive habit among young people — a full three years after Belgium moved to ban them.

## A Ban That Did Not Bite

Belgium banned the sale of nicotine pouches through a Royal Decree of 14 March 2023, effective 1 October 2023, as part of the federal anti-tobacco plan. The rationale was clear: the small white pouches — placed under the upper lip for a quick "nicotine rush" — contain no tobacco but are infused with nicotine and appealing flavourings, from mint to watermelon.

Yet the investigation found the products remain easily accessible. Users told VRT NWS they buy them in night shops, order them online from Czech webshops, or through informal dealers — at roughly €8 per 20-pouch can behind a counter versus €3 to €4 online. Worldwide, sales have nearly tenfolded in five years, from 4 billion to 37 billion pouches.

A survey of 500 young people by three VUB journalism students, part of a master's thesis, found that 93 percent knew what nicotine pouches were and 10 percent had tried them.

## Discreet, Addictive and Harsh on the Body

What makes the pouches so hard to police is how inconspicuous they are. "You don't have to go outside for it and it doesn't smell as strongly," one user told VRT NWS. Unlike a cigarette or vape, a pouch can be used unnoticed — including during class.

The physical toll is immediate. One anonymous user described "literally taking pieces of gum out of your mouth because nicotine pouches have such a burning effect." A 16-year-old at Atheneum Lier recalled: "The world starts spinning hard, you become extremely nauseous. It only went away after half an hour." Teacher Sven van Belle said he had twice seen a pupil begin vomiting during class because of pouch use.

Addiction sets in fast. Ex-footballer Frank Boeckx described withdrawal as "3 days of cold sweats... I felt like a heroin addict who can't get his fix." He claims "1 in 3 footballers uses snus," an estimate not backed by rigorous data.

## 'Poison for Your Blood Vessels'

Health experts warn the long-term consequences could reverse decades of declining smoking rates. "Nicotine is a cardiovascular toxic substance. It is poison for your blood vessels," said cardiologist Paul Dendale of UHasselt. Over time, he warned, it causes hardening of the arteries that leads to heart attacks — "you lay the foundation for heart attacks in 20 or 30 years."

Filip Lardon, a tobacco-prevention expert at UAntwerpen, called nicotine "one of the most addictive drugs in the world" that "intervenes in the nervous system in a deceptive way." Dendale was blunt about the stakes: "We know that anyone who keeps using will, unfortunately, eventually become a client of the cardiologists."

## Why It Matters

The case exposes a widening gap between well-intentioned public health policy and real-world enforcement. Because there is no legal domestic market to track, authorities struggle even to measure how many young people use the products. The original health rationale — outlined by the [Stichting tegen Kanker](https://kanker.be/resource/verbod-nicotinezakjes-belgie/) — was that the pouches have no proven role in helping smokers quit, while being cheap, brightly packaged and attractive to non-smoking youth.

The confusion is compounded by terminology: Belgian youth call the products "snus" or "snabba," though true snus is a Swedish tobacco product banned across the EU except in Sweden. What young Belgians actually use are tobacco-free nicotine pouches — a distinction that has allowed the products to slip through regulatory cracks.

## What to Watch

Belgium is far from alone in grappling with the issue. The Netherlands banned sales from 1 January 2025, Denmark is phasing in flavour restrictions in 2026, and Sweden has fiercely defended its snus tradition against a French ban. The EU is weighing tighter rules, setting up a lobbying battle in which the tobacco industry promotes pouches as a smoking-cessation tool.

For Belgium, the immediate question is enforcement. The Pano documentary "Nicotine in een zakje" airs Wednesday on VRT 1 and VRT MAX, and pressure is likely to build on policymakers to close the gap between a ban on paper and a ban in practice. Whether that translates into stricter inspections, advertising limits or new EU-wide rules remains to be seen — but for a generation of young users, the addiction is already here.
