# Midnight Maas: Romantasy Fans Turn Out for ACOTAR Release

Thousands of readers flooded big-box stores and independent bookshops across the United States on the night of Monday, October 26, for midnight release parties celebrating *A Court of Splintered Harmony*, the sixth installment in Sarah J. Maas' best-selling *A Court of Thorns and Roses* series, [NPR reported](https://www.npr.org/2026/10/27/nx-s1-6011109/acotar-a-court-of-splintered-harmony-sarah-j-maas). The book went on sale the following day, Tuesday, October 27, and the turnout underscored the surging commercial and cultural force of "romantasy"—the fantasy-romance crossover genre that has become one of publishing's most powerful franchises.

At Politics and Prose, an independent bookstore in Washington, D.C., dozens of fans in tight leather costumes, pointy faerie ears and sparkly tiaras browsed the shelves while waiting for the clock to strike midnight. The event, which ran from 10 p.m. to midnight, drew about 70 people—more than organizer Nicole Davidow had planned for when she set aside just 50 tickets.

## A Community, Not Just a Purchase

For many attendees, the appeal was less about getting the book early than about experiencing the moment alongside fellow readers. Dalia Luque said she could have simply preordered the novel online but chose to attend in person.

"I just want to have fun with the community and get to share with my friends as we read along," Luque said. She had her face painted to represent the Summer Court, one of seven territories that make up the ACOTAR world—each ruled by a High Lord and stamped with its own color palette and "vibe," a system fans compare to astrological signs or the factions in Veronica Roth's *Divergent*.

"I've always enjoyed stepping into a new reality, or a different version of reality," Luque added. "And so I think these books create a whole new world, and that's pretty exciting."

Ivana Wong, whose black dress adorned with sequin stars, silver headpiece and sheer, flowy cape won the night's costume contest, said the wait had been long in the making. "I've been waiting for this since the very first book came out," she said, adding that she planned to take a couple of days off work to read.

Chastity Mayhaus, a recent transplant to the capital, came with a more practical goal: meeting like-minded fans and scouting local book clubs.

"I'm hoping to make some friends, just talk to some people," Mayhaus said. "I'm looking around to see any good book clubs that are like in the area, so maybe I can scout out some new ones."

## Five Years in the Making

The enthusiasm reflects a lengthy drought for the series. The first ACOTAR book was published in 2015, and Maas released new installments fairly regularly until she stepped away from the saga in 2021—leaving readers with nearly a five-year gap before *A Court of Splintered Harmony* arrived.

The series follows a cast of attractive faeries and humans as they battle, embark on quests and, of course, fall in love. The new novel arrives as romantasy has moved from a niche curiosity to a retail powerhouse, with bookstores programming major events and merchandise tie-ins around releases.

## The BookTok Engine

Much of the ACOTAR fanbase was built online, particularly on BookTok, the book-focused corner of TikTok where readers share reviews, fan art and recommendations. But as the midnight crowds showed, that digital community is increasingly eager to gather in person.

"I think that online community wants to be in a room together," Davidow said. "In general, we find that with all of our events, people are craving a community."

Monday's party was the first midnight release event Davidow had organized in her five years at the bookstore—a sign of how thoroughly the genre has reshaped expectations for book retail.

The romantasy boom has drawn growing attention beyond the sales floor. NPR has explored the genre's cultural footprint, including [the voice actors behind its audiobooks](https://www.npr.org/2026/03/30/nx-s1-5759214/romantasy-audiobooks-acotar-anthony-palmini) and [the politics of romance and fantasy fiction](https://www.npr.org/2026/01/28/nx-s1-5690372/yes-romance-fantasy-novels-are-political).

## What's Next

Once midnight struck, fans hurried to the checkout line, and readers clutching the pink and yellow hardcover flowed out of Politics and Prose—presumably to disappear into the book's pages. For publishers and booksellers, the message is clear: a devoted, socially connected readership can turn a single release into a national event. The question now is whether romantasy's momentum can be sustained as other authors and franchises compete for the same passionate, community-minded fans.
