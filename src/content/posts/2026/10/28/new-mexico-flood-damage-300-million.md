# New Mexico Flood Damage Could Hit $300 Million

Infrastructure damage in a single New Mexico county could reach $200 to $300 million after a week of historic, El Nino-driven flooding swept the Four Corners region, Governor Michelle Lujan Grisham said, as officials warned Tuesday that the costly recovery is only beginning.

The estimate covers San Juan County alone, according to [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/10/27/nx-s1-6019352/southwest-flood-damage-estimates-are-hundreds-of-millions-for-new-mexico-alone), underscoring the scale of a disaster that has battered Colorado, New Mexico, Utah and Arizona and left at least one person dead.

## A river that grew 13-fold in 36 hours

The floods were set off by intense rainstorms tied to a highly active, El Nino-driven Pacific hurricane season, which forecasters have tied to the unusually heavy rainfall across the region.

At the center of the destruction was Farmington, a city of roughly 46,000 in northern New Mexico. Torrential rains over the San Juan Mountains in Colorado sent floodwaters surging down the San Juan River. According to [river-gauge data](https://water.noaa.gov/gauges/frmn5#hg=official) from the National Weather Service, the river in Farmington swelled 13 times larger in just 36 hours, rising from about 3.5 feet early Thursday to 14 feet by Friday - possibly a record for the city.

"This is a catastrophic flood of historic proportions," San Juan County public information officer Devon Neeley said. "This river got wider than we have ever seen it in our lifetimes."

Small towns along the river also saw historic flooding, including Pagosa Springs, Colorado; Bloomfield, New Mexico; and Bluff, Utah. Surges into Farmington came from two normally dry tributaries, Gobernador Canyon and Canon Largo.

## Residents rescued from rooftops

San Juan County fire crews, police, National Guard troops and out-of-state swift water teams rescued between 200 and 300 residents, according to [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/weather/floods/flood-warning-four-corners-storms-death-tornado-rcna605774), using rooftop helicopter rescues and amphibious vehicles.

In Bloomfield, resident Zach Flatt described the scramble to escape. "In 30 or 40 minutes it had jumped about 4 feet," he said. "After I saw that big jump, and how close it was to going over the banks, I said we've got to get out." The family watched via webcams as more than 18 inches of water inundated their property, washing away 22 of their 24 chickens.

The storms also spawned a tornado that touched down in Santa Fe on Saturday night. At least one flooding death has been confirmed: a female passenger was swept away when a family tried to cross a flooded arroyo in their vehicle and was found dead about a mile away.

## A growing health hazard

As waters recede, officials are warning of contamination. Raw sewage, gas and diesel, pesticides and fertilizer have mixed into the floodwater, according to [Weather.com](https://weather.com/2026/10/25/news/weather/still-more-rain-evacuations-remain-in-place-from-four-corners-region-flooding). Sewage spills occurred from flooded treatment plants in Sedona, Arizona, and releases flowed into the San Juan River from damaged wastewater facilities in Shiprock on the Navajo Nation.

"The water is not healthy. Stay out of the water," said San Juan County Fire Chief David Vega. "Gas, diesel, pesticides, fertilizer, these are not things that you want on your clothes."

## A partial federal response

The White House approved a partial emergency declaration covering San Juan, Rio Arriba, Cibola and Sandoval counties, along with several pueblos and tribal nations, the [Governor's Office](https://www.governor.state.nm.us/newsroom/feds-partially-grant-emergency-declaration-for-northern-nm-flooding-2169/) said. It authorizes direct federal assistance, including Army Corps of Engineers debris removal, U.S. Geological Survey flood monitoring, EPA drinking-water testing and FEMA sheltering support.

But the federal government did not grant the state's full request for reimbursement of debris removal and emergency protective measures. Lujan Grisham said she will again seek a Major Disaster Declaration once damage is fully assessed, with Santa Fe and Socorro counties expected to be added.

"The White House approved our request for an emergency declaration quickly, but this is only the first step," the governor said. "New Mexico is facing its second major flood in a month, and our counties and Tribal nations have spent everything they have keeping people safe. They shouldn't have to carry those costs alone."

## What to watch

The fiscal pressure is mounting. A separate Major Disaster Declaration request remains pending for flooding in southern New Mexico from Sept. 20 to Oct. 2, seeking help for an estimated $27.1 million in public infrastructure damage across 14 counties and three Tribal nations.

Contamination, damaged gas and power lines, and structural hazards are expected to keep thousands of evacuees from returning home for days. For a region long dependent on coal, oil and gas - and a hub for the sprawling Navajo reservation - the road back will be measured not just in dollars but in months.
