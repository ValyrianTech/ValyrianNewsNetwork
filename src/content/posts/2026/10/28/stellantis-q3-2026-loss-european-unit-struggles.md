# Stellantis Posts €453M Loss as European Unit Struggles

Stellantis, the automotive group behind Opel, Peugeot, Fiat and Citroën, reported a net loss of €453 million in the third quarter of 2026, according to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/10/28/rode-cijfers-voor-autobouwer-stellantis/). Yet the quarter told two stories at once: a headline loss that deepened the narrative of a carmaker in crisis, and a set of operating numbers that sent the group's shares sharply higher as investors bet the worst may be behind it.

The loss itself came despite modest revenue growth. Net revenues rose 1% year-over-year to €37.6 billion in the quarter, the company [reported](https://www.stellantis.com/en/news/press-releases/2026/october/third-quarter-2026-financial-results), driven by higher volumes in Enlarged Europe and stable volumes with favourable currency effects in South America.

## A loss that looks better than it is—or worse

The €453 million net loss represents a striking 90% improvement over the €4.53 billion loss posted in the same quarter a year earlier, a period weighed down by heavy one-off charges. More telling for the underlying business was the group's adjusted operating income, which more than doubled to €256 million from €90 million a year earlier, with the adjusted margin widening to 0.7% from 0.2%.

That beat the €191 million that analysts had expected in a Reuters poll, and shares rose more than 9% on the Milan exchange by mid-afternoon. "This is the only clear support for the recovery of Stellantis's adjusted operating margin," Citi analysts wrote in a note quoted by [Reuters](https://be.marketscreener.com/beursnieuws/stellantis-boekt-bescheiden-bedrijfsresultaat-in-derde-kwartaal-maar-verbrandt-a-4-miljard-ce785cd2db89f027), pointing to the company's cost-cutting drive as the decisive factor.

## Europe remains the sore spot

Beneath the improved headline numbers, the regional breakdown reveals where Stellantis is still bleeding. Every region delivered a positive adjusted operating margin except Enlarged Europe—the home market for the Opel, Peugeot, Citroën and Fiat brands most familiar to Belgian buyers. There, the margin fell 340 basis points year-over-year, the only negative reading in the group.

Belgium's direct exposure to the carmaker is limited: the country accounts for roughly 1.5% of Stellantis revenue, and mass vehicle assembly on Belgian soil ended when the Opel Antwerp plant closed in 2010. But the country remains tightly bound to the fate of these brands through dealerships, suppliers and the symbolic weight of Opel's industrial past. Belgian financial daily De Tijd framed the story bluntly, writing that the crisis at Stellantis "hits hardest in Belgium."

## The cash burn is the real concern

For all the improvement in operating profit, the most striking figure in the quarterly report was the negative €4.0 billion in industrial free cash flows—a modest improvement on the €4.3 billion burned a year earlier, but still a staggering sum to consume in a single quarter. The group's chief executive, Antonio Filosa, reaffirmed guidance for 2026 and repeated that Stellantis expects positive industrial free cash flows in 2027.

"During the quarter, we continued to make progress in the early execution of our FaSTLAne 2030 plan, launching new products, accelerating partnerships as planned, and delivering meaningful improvements in manufacturing and quality," Filosa said in a statement. He added that the group remains on track to implement at least 40% of a targeted €6 billion in run-rate cost savings by the end of the year.

## Three forces squeezing European carmakers

The quarterly loss needs to be read against the structural pressures bearing down on the entire European auto sector. The first is intense competition from Chinese manufacturers, which VRT NWS explicitly cited as a drag on Stellantis and its peers. The second is the costly, halting transition to electric vehicles—Stellantis took more than €22 billion in special impairments related to EVs in February 2026 and has had to scale back its electric ambitions in the United States. The third is trade policy: the group expects net tariff costs of roughly €1.0 billion for the full year.

Adding to the complexity, Stellantis is deepening its partnership with Chinese automaker Leapmotor, whose sales within the group's European operations grew nearly four-fold year-over-year. That boosts volumes and electric-vehicle share, but it also embeds a Chinese competitor inside Stellantis's own portfolio—a double-edged strategy emblematic of the sector's dilemma.

## What to watch

The group's share price remains down roughly 57% year-to-date, and investors will scrutinise two milestones ahead. The first is the promised positive free cash flow in 2027, which Filosa has staked his credibility on. The second is 10 December, when Stellantis will present a new business plan for its loss-making Maserati brand at a dedicated event in Modena, Italy.

For now, the quarter offers a rare thing for Stellantis: a beat, a rising share price and a reaffirmed outlook. Whether that marks the start of a genuine recovery or merely a pause in a longer decline—one that for Belgium echoes the closure of a plant that once defined a region—remains the open question.
