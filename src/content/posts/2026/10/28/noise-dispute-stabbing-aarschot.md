# Noise Dispute Turns Violent: Man Stabbed in Aarschot

A dispute over loud music between neighbours in the Flemish town of Aarschot escalated into a violent stabbing that left a 42-year-old man with life-threatening injuries, Belgian prosecutors have revealed. The case, which only became public on 28 October, highlights how a routine neighbourhood nuisance complaint can spiral into serious violence — and has left both the injured man and his alleged attacker facing charges.

## What Happened

According to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/10/28/incident-met-mes-in-aarschot/), police were called to a home on the Diestsesteenweg in Aarschot, in the province of Flemish Brabant, at around 10 p.m. on Friday 16 October, following reports of a fight. A quarrel had broken out between the 55-year-old female resident and a 55-year-old male friend from Diest on one side, and their 42-year-old neighbour on the other, after a discussion about music that was too loud.

The 42-year-old man reportedly went to the couple's home — at the request of the two residents, according to [HLN](https://www.hln.be/aarschot/man-42-uit-aarschot-krijgt-meer-dan-tien-messteken-na-ruzie-over-geluidsoverlast~af55dc1f/) — carrying a knife. Once inside, a scuffle broke out in which the neighbour was stabbed more than ten times in his leg. He was left in life-threatening condition and taken to hospital. The 55-year-old man from Diest suffered an injury to his hand.

## Reclassification of the Case

Because the exact circumstances were initially unclear, police detained all three people. The Leuven public prosecutor's office opened a judicial investigation into attempted manslaughter and brought in an investigating magistrate.

That magistrate ultimately reassessed the facts, reclassifying the case as intentional assault and battery causing incapacity for work, rather than attempted homicide. The 55-year-old man from Diest was charged with intentional assault and battery, in circumstances where the facts were "provoked by the victim" — a qualification that serves as a mitigating factor under Belgian criminal law. He was released under conditions.

After the 42-year-old man left hospital, he too was brought before the investigating magistrate. He was charged with intentional assault and battery causing incapacity, as well as with using an object not designed as a weapon to cause injury and threats — referring to the knife. He was also released under conditions. The 55-year-old woman was questioned by police and released without charge.

## Both Sides Now Suspects

The case is notable for its unusual legal posture: the seriously injured victim is himself now a suspect. That both men face charges underscores how claims of self-defence and provocation will be central to determining the eventual outcome. All three involved are required to remain available to authorities during the investigation, which is being conducted by the detective unit of the Aarschot police zone under the direction of the investigating magistrate.

## A Broader Pattern of Neighbourhood Tension

Noise complaints over *burenhinder* — neighbourhood nuisance — are a common source of friction in Flanders. Belgian law offers residents several remedies, from reporting the matter to police and the local municipality to civil action before a justice of the peace. Most such disputes are resolved through mediation or civil channels. The Aarschot case illustrates what can happen when a relatively ordinary disagreement is instead resolved through violence.

## What to Watch

The investigation is ongoing, and the precise allocation of blame will depend on how the magistrate weighs the competing claims of provocation and self-defence. For now, both men remain free under conditions, and the case stands as a reminder of how quickly a mundane complaint about noise can escalate into life-altering harm.
