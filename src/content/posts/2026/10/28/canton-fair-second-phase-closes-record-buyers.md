# Canton Fair's Second Phase Closes With Record Buyer Turnout

The second phase of the 140th China Import and Export Fair—better known as the Canton Fair—concluded in Guangzhou on October 27 after a five-day run, drawing 278,000 overseas buyers from 218 countries and regions, a 13.4% increase over the same phase of the previous edition, according to [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/fortune/20261028/4e4e37f8e12c4b1c90663ba995f323d6/c.html).

Themed "Quality Home," the phase occupied a 515,000-square-meter exhibition space and featured nearly 11,000 exhibiting companies displaying 2.376 million products—positioning the fair as a showcase for what Chinese state media bill as the pairing of "China Smart Manufacturing" and "Eastern Aesthetics."

## Context: A Barometer of Chinese Exports

The Canton Fair, held biannually in Guangzhou since 1957, is China's oldest and largest trade fair and a closely watched proxy for global demand for Chinese goods. The 140th edition runs from October 15 to November 4, 2026, structured across three phases. Phase One, dedicated to advanced manufacturing, ran October 15–19; Phase Two, the consumer-facing "Quality Home" segment, covered household goods, gifts and decorations, building materials and furniture. Phase Three, themed "Better Life," is scheduled for October 31 to November 4.

The fair's scale underscores its role in Chinese trade diplomacy: the full event spans 1.55 million square meters, 75,700 booths and 54 specialized exhibition zones, and organizers continue to market it as the world's largest exhibition.

## Key Developments

Beyond headline attendance, several indicators pointed to a deliberate shift up the value chain. More than 3,600 "specialized, refined, distinctive and innovative" enterprises and "single champion" firms exhibited in the phase's export section—roughly 7.6% more than a year earlier—a signal that higher-margin, design-led manufacturers are increasingly central to the fair's lineup.

The phase hosted 178 new-product launch events, with the number of debut products up about 15% over the last edition. Organizers also leaned into green manufacturing as an export selling point: nearly 640,000 green, low-carbon products were displayed, accounting for 26.9% of all exhibits, a reflection of China's broader "dual carbon" agenda being packaged for international buyers.

Matchmaking was a major feature. By October 27, nearly 20 supply-and-purchase sessions had been held, including a "Trade Bridge—Asia-Pacific" event that connected 15 buying enterprises from Vietnam, Russia, the United States and Kazakhstan with 48 exhibitors. In total, 152 overseas business organizations and 384 leading procurement enterprises attended as organized delegations.

## Analysis: From Low-Cost Goods to Integrated Solutions

A recurring theme among buyers and exhibitors was the transformation of China's export proposition—away from low-price commodity single items and toward design innovation, intangible cultural heritage turned into wearable products, and turnkey "integrated solutions."

Modular housing emerged as a standout category. According to customs data cited by the China News Service, China's prefabricated-building export value rose from $1.472 billion in 2015 to roughly $4.339 billion in 2025, nearly tripling in a decade, with first-quarter 2026 growth of 45%. Firms reported selling container-sized modular homes—about 40 square meters, expandable, assembled by four to five workers in half an hour—priced between 100,000 and 150,000 yuan per unit.

The emphasis on drawing global buyers aligns with Beijing's push to stabilize foreign trade and attract foreign business amid global trade tensions and supply-chain diversification pressures. The fair's slogan—"Canton Fair connects the world, mutual benefit for all"—frames openness as central to the event's identity.

Organizers also extended the fair's reach beyond the exhibition halls, introducing an "exhibition plus overseas livestream" mode during the middle days of each phase and offering overseas-buyer membership services for the first time, aimed at buyers unable to attend in person.

## What's Next

Attention now turns to Phase Three, the "Better Life" segment covering textiles, apparel, footwear, health and food products, which runs October 31 to November 4. How that phase performs—particularly in buyer attendance and order volumes—will offer a fuller picture of global appetite for Chinese consumer goods heading into 2027.

A caveat worth noting: all attendance and exhibitor figures originate from China's official trade-fair apparatus, which has an interest in presenting strong numbers. Independent third-party verification of the fair's attendance and transaction data is not readily available, so the figures should be read as official tallies rather than independently audited results.
