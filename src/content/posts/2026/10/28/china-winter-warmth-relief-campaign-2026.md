# China Launches Winter Relief Campaign for the Homeless

China's Ministry of Civil Affairs has launched its annual "Winter Warmth" special relief campaign, a nationwide operation running from now until March 15, 2027, aimed at ensuring that destitute and homeless people can survive the cold season safely, [People's Daily reported](http://society.people.com.cn/n1/2026/1028/c1008-40822791.html). The notice, issued by the ministry's General Office, directs local authorities to coordinate multi-department street patrols, build grassroots reporting networks, and set up temporary relief points for people with no means of support.

## Context: A Recurring Seasonal Safety Net

The campaign is not a crisis response but a routine, institutionalized program launched every autumn as part of the government's broader winter-spring livelihood protection. The 2026-27 edition follows a near-identical operation the previous year, when the ministry ran the campaign until March 15, 2026, with the Northeast and Northwest regions extending it to March 31, [according to Guangming Daily](https://gy.youth.cn/gywz/202511/t20251101_16324168.htm).

This year's window again allows the Northeast and Northwest — regions with longer, harsher winters — to extend the campaign "as appropriate," reflecting a seasonal logic that aligns relief efforts with the coldest months and major holidays, including the Spring Festival, when outdoor exposure is most dangerous.

## Key Measures: Proactive Outreach

The ministry's notice emphasizes an active-outreach model rather than waiting for people to seek shelter. Local civil affairs departments are required to establish a meteorological early-warning linkage mechanism and to coordinate joint patrols with public security, urban management, health, and emergency management departments.

According to People's Daily, authorities must conduct grid-style sweeps of locations where destitute people may sleep rough — train stations and docks, underground passages, bridges and culverts, and abandoned buildings — and increase patrol frequency before extreme weather and at night.

The campaign also mobilizes an unusual corps of frontline spotters: sanitation workers, couriers, and bus and taxi drivers are encouraged to report relief leads. Temporary relief points may be set up at sites such as welfare-lottery stations so that people in difficulty can seek help nearby and conveniently.

## Expanded Services Beyond Food and Shelter

This year's notice extends support well beyond basic provisions. For those who refuse institutional shelter after persuasion, authorities are instructed to provide food, clothing, and warm supplies, leave contact and help methods, and notify local township or sub-district offices for follow-up tracking.

For critically ill patients and those with mental disorders, the notice enshrines a "treat first, assist later" principle, requiring that they be sent to designated hospitals promptly.

Services also reach people pushed into difficulty by domestic violence or migrant workers who have lost employment, who are to receive emergency and transitional livelihood support along with coordination for psychological counseling, legal aid, and employment consulting. For recipients unable to provide personal identification, authorities will use "Internet+" methods to trace families and help them return home.

## Analysis: Routine Governance With a Broader Mandate

The campaign's significance lies less in novelty than in what it reveals about China's relief machinery. The emphasis on street patrols, night sweeps, and mobilizing ordinary workers as spotters reflects a proactive philosophy aimed at people who are hard to reach through conventional shelter intake.

The expanded menu of services — psychological counseling, legal aid, family reunification — signals a shift toward a more service-oriented conception of relief, one that treats homelessness as a social condition demanding multi-agency response rather than a simple sheltering problem.

The program is also embedded in top-level national policy. The campaign is explicitly named in the Communist Party and State Council's notice on year-end and Spring Festival work, placing winter livelihood protection within the country's political agenda for social stability during the holiday period.

## What's Next

Local civil affairs bureaus and relief-management stations across the country will now implement the notice, with provincial authorities adapting details to local conditions. The campaign will run through mid-March, with the northern regions potentially extending operations into late March. How effectively the grassroots reporting networks and temporary relief points function in practice — and whether vulnerable people who refuse shelters can be reached and supported — will be the measure of the program's success this winter.
