# i-Police Leak Case: Verlinden and Prosecutor Spokespeople Are Suspects

A spokesperson for Belgian Justice Minister Annelies Verlinden (CD&V) and a spokesperson for the Brussels public prosecutors office have been formally placed under suspicion by a Brussels investigating magistrate for breach of professional secrecy, deepening the scandal over the failed i-Police police digitalisation project. The news, first reported by De Standaard and Le Soir and confirmed to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/10/27/woordvoerder-verlinden-in-verdenking-gesteld-na-lekken-in-zaak-r/) by one of the lawyers involved, raises uncomfortable questions about confidentiality at the highest levels of government and the judiciary.

According to VRT NWS, the Dutch-speaking spokesperson of the Brussels prosecutor's office allegedly "spied" by passing confidential information about the i-Police investigation to the Dutch-speaking spokesperson of Verlinden. Whether that information reached the minister herself remains unclear.

## Context: A 299 million euro failure

i-Police was a flagship programme intended to digitalise police work, consolidate disparate data systems into a single information platform and enable faster detection of risks and criminal networks. Announced in 2017 by then-Interior Minister Jan Jambon (N-VA) in the aftermath of the terrorist attacks, the project was launched at the end of 2021 under Verlinden, who signed a 299 million euro contract with the French IT consultancy Sopra Steria.

In December 2025, her successor as Interior Minister, Bernard Quintin (MR), cancelled the contract after years of billing totalling some 75.8 million euros had produced, in his words, "no tangible result" — a decision the [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2025/12/26/digitalisering-politie-mislukt-monstercontract-verbroken/) reported at the time. The Brussels prosecutor's office then opened a preliminary investigation in January 2026 into possible embezzlement of public funds and conflict of interest in the dossier, which according to De Standaard has since become a full judicial investigation.

The facts that led to the spokespeople being placed under suspicion emerged during that investigation. The Brussels prosecutor's office spokesperson has been suspended since June 2026. Her lawyers declined to comment for now.

## Verlinden stands by her spokesperson

Verlinden has publicly backed her spokesperson and denies any wrongdoing, emphasising respect for the secrecy of the investigation and the presumption of innocence. "As Justice Minister I attach great importance to respect for the secrecy of the investigation and the presumption of innocence. These are two fundamental principles of our rule of law. That is why I will not make substantive statements about an ongoing investigation," she told VRT NWS.

She added: "I myself and my former cabinet staff have always acted correctly and in accordance with the law. Moreover, I have made myself available to the competent authorities to provide all necessary explanation. I have nothing to hide in this."

Mounir Soudi, the lawyer for Verlinden's spokesperson, rejected the characterisation of the allegations. "It is particularly hard to have to read in the newspapers about my client's alleged role in the reported case," he said. "It even mentions espionage, which gives the articles a deliberately intended pejorative charge, but is otherwise rather ridiculous." Soudi also questioned who might have an interest in leaking from the dossier.

## Opposition demands an inquiry

The revelations have reignited opposition pressure over a project that has become a byword for public waste. Vlaams Belang MPs Ortwin Depoortere and Alexander Van Hoecke are again demanding a parliamentary inquiry committee, arguing it should hear all those involved under oath and lay bare the full decision-making process. "The questions about this millions fiasco keep piling up," Depoortere said, adding that a spokesperson is "a direct confidant of the minister" and that whether Verlinden herself was aware must be clarified — while she "of course also bears political responsibility for the functioning of her cabinet."

Groen faction leader Matti Vandemaele said: "How much taxpayers' money must be squandered before the government takes this case seriously? How much conflict of interest is needed before Prime Minister Bart De Wever intervenes?" Anders MP Arthur Orlians stopped short of backing a full inquiry committee but called for an extra Justice committee hearing. "All transparency is lacking," he said. "A spokesperson is not suspended just like that. Then the question is: what did the minister know and what not?"

## Analysis: a crisis of two dimensions

The case now cuts in two directions at once. On the political side, it is a reckoning over the stewardship of a massive public IT contract — and over Verlinden's prior role as a lawyer for Sopra Steria immediately before she became Interior Minister in 2020. On the judicial side, the leak allegations strike at the confidentiality of the investigation itself, suggesting that sensitive case material may have flowed from the prosecutor's office to a minister's cabinet.

The distinction between what a spokesperson knew and what a minister knew will be central to any parliamentary scrutiny. A spokesperson is, as Depoortere noted, a trusted intermediary — which is precisely why the question of whether information stopped at the cabinet's edge or reached the minister matters both legally and politically.

## What to watch

Parliament's previous refusal to establish a full inquiry committee is now under renewed pressure, with Vlaams Belang and Groen both pushing to reverse that decision. Key open questions remain: What exactly was leaked, to whom, and for what purpose? Did the confidential information reach Verlinden herself? Will the De Wever government take any action against the justice minister? And can any of the 75.8 million euros paid to Sopra Steria be recovered?

For now, Verlinden is holding firm, invoking the presumption of innocence — a principle that now applies to her own spokesperson as much as to anyone else in the case.
