# Coal Falls Below Half of China's Power Output for First Time

Coal-fired generation supplied less than half of China's electricity for the first time on record, falling to 49.7% of total output in the first half of 2026, as the country's breakneck build-out of wind and solar reshapes its power system. The milestone, disclosed in the Ministry of Ecology and Environment's annual climate report, came as Beijing prepares to head into next month's COP31 summit with an unusually strong story to tell on the energy transition.

The ministry's report, reported by [Caixin Global](https://www.caixinglobal.com/2026-10-26/coal-falls-below-half-of-chinas-power-output-for-first-time-102497427.html), also confirmed that by the end of July, installed solar capacity had surpassed coal capacity — making photovoltaics China's single largest source of installed generation capacity. The figures were first announced by the National Energy Administration in late July and re-anchored this week within the broader climate policy picture ahead of the U.N. climate talks.

## A Threshold Long in the Making

The headline number is the culmination of years of acceleration. In the first half of 2026, renewable generation reached nearly 2 trillion kWh, up about 9% year-on-year and exceeding 40% of total generation for the first time, according to state broadcaster [CCTV, as carried by China Youth Daily](https://news.cyol.com/gb/news/articles/2026-08/01/content_5xE9pMF0dm.html). Coal plants still generated roughly 2.5 trillion kWh over the period — a vast absolute volume — but their share of the mix has now fallen below the halfway mark that defined China's grid for generations. In 2025, renewables had already supplied all of the country's growth in electricity demand while thermal output declined for the first time in nearly a decade.

## What Is Driving the Shift

Three forces have combined to produce the crossover, according to analysis published by the [Economic Daily and republished by Sina](https://news.sina.cn/znl/2026-08-13/detail-inincnuy3443755.d.html). First is the collapse in clean-energy costs: over the past decade, the levelized cost of wind and solar power in China fell by more than 60% and 80% respectively, with solar generation now cheaper than the benchmark coal tariff in many regions. Second is industrial scale — China now accounts for more than 70% of the global market in wind, solar and energy-storage equipment. Third is system support: the world's largest AC/DC hybrid grid, new-type energy storage capacity of 153 million kW, and a national unified power market that is gradually smoothing the intermittency of renewable output.

The Economic Daily commentary framed the shift as renewables shedding their status as "supplementary energy" and "moving to center stage." In the first half of the year, renewable additions totaled 117 million kW, or 73.9% of all new capacity installed, and by the end of June renewables made up more than 60% of China's total installed capacity.

## A Broader Climate Picture

The coal milestone landed alongside a sweeping progress report on China's climate commitments. Vice Minister of Ecology and Environment Li Gao said the country remains on track toward the pledges it made under the Paris Agreement: peaking carbon dioxide emissions before 2030, cutting carbon intensity by more than 65% from 2005 levels, lifting non-fossil energy to about 25% of primary energy consumption, and installing over 1.2 billion kW of combined wind and solar capacity.

In 2025, CO2 emissions per unit of GDP were 17.7% lower than in 2020; non-fossil sources reached 21.7% of energy consumption, up from 16%; and new-energy vehicles accounted for 50.8% of new vehicle sales. By the first half of 2026, total renewable installed capacity reached 2.455 billion kW, or 60.7% of the national total. China's national carbon market, now more than five years old, covers about 8.3 billion tonnes of CO2 — over 65% of national emissions — and was expanded in March 2025 from power generation to steel, cement and aluminum.

## The COP31 Test

Beijing heads to COP31, scheduled for Nov. 9–20 in Antalya, Turkey, with a domestic record that lends credibility to its diplomatic push. China has said it will press for implementation of existing commitments across the roughly 100 negotiation issues on the table and for progress on climate finance — including road maps for developed countries to meet a $300 billion annual financing target by 2035 and to triple adaptation funding.

Li also warned that climate change had become "an immediate crisis" rather than a future risk after severe summer heat waves caused major casualties and losses, and pointed to expanded adaptation efforts: 39 cities are running climate-adaptive urban development pilots, and all 31 provincial-level regions have issued adaptation plans.

## What to Watch

Two questions will determine whether this first half-year proves a genuine turning point. The first is whether the coal share keeps falling as the 15th Five-Year Plan (2026–2030) — which seeks a 17% reduction in carbon intensity from 2025 levels by 2030 — is implemented. Renewables are now entering what planners call a phase of "scaling up, improving quality and reliable substitution," but integrating a high share of intermittent wind and solar still strains grids and markets.

The second is whether China can reconcile its rapid decarbonization of the power sector with its continued role as the world's largest coal consumer and producer. Coal's share of total energy consumption — not just electricity — stood at 51.4% in 2025, a reminder that the power-generation milestone, however historic, is one marker on a much longer road.
