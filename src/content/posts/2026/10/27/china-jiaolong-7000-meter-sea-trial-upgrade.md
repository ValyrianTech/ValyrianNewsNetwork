# China's Jiaolong Completes 7,000-Meter Sea Trial After Upgrade

China's Jiaolong crewed submersible has completed a 7,000-meter-class sea trial following its second iterative upgrade and the domestic substitution of key components, according to state news agency [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/tech/20261027/765df09750c54f068354dc057958e835/c.html). The vehicle carried out multiple deep dives in the Philippine Sea Basin and returned safely, with two of three trial dives exceeding 7,000 meters and a maximum depth of 7,056.9 meters.

## Context and Background

Jiaolong is China's first domestically developed crewed deep-sea submersible, best known for reaching 7,000 meters during its landmark 2012 Mariana Trench trials. Over the past decade it has shifted from experimental testing to routine scientific application, operating in the South China Sea, the Indian Ocean and the Mariana Trench area, and it now serves as a high-frequency workhorse alongside newer systems such as the 10,000-meter-class Fendouzhe.

The latest trials were conducted under the National Key R&D Program project "Jiaolong Key Operating Technology Capability Enhancement and Scientific Application," and drew on the second leg of China's Dayang 100 expedition. According to [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/tech/20261025/b704a61683fe4e818701f6cb2826ba8c/c.html), the deep dives were the first 7,000-meter-class trials completed after the upgrade and domestic substitution work, and represented the maximum depth reached across the entire second leg of the voyage.

## Key Developments

The trial campaign involved three dives in the Philippine Sea Basin, two of which passed the 7,000-meter mark, with the deepest reaching 7,056.9 meters, Xinhua reported. All test indicators met the requirements for the 7,000-meter-class sea trial, and the vessel was supported by its mother ship, Shenhai Yihao ("Deep Sea No. 1").

"This fully verified the reliability and safety of the Jiaolong after its iterative upgrade and the indigenization of key components, and consolidated and raised our country's technological advantages in the field of crewed deep-diving equipment," said Sun Yongfu, a researcher at the China National Deep Sea Center and head of the project.

The upgrade list published by Xinhua covers several core systems: 7,000-meter-class high-energy-density oil-immersed lithium battery packs for power, domestically produced direct-drive low-noise DC thrusters for propulsion, an expanded emergency hydraulic system interface with large-depth seawater hydraulic buoyancy adjustment, and wide-view, high-zoom ultra-high-definition camera systems capable of 4K imagery.

Sun summarized the gains with three keywords. "Dives more" — operating efficiency has jumped, with 94 dives completed in 2025 alone, described as a milestone annual breakthrough. "Runs longer" — a single underwater operating time of up to 10.8 hours and a deep-sea cruising speed of more than 2.5 knots. "Sees clearly" — 4K high-definition imagery even in the dim deep-sea environment, supporting species identification and habitat mapping.

## Analysis and Implications

The upgrades extend beyond hardware. According to Xinhua, researchers have added a deep-sea intelligent detection system built on a multimodal model that was trained on more than 800,000 images of deep-sea benthic organisms and substrates. The system can identify organisms and seabed types in real time on video, automatically capture frames with metadata such as name, latitude and longitude, time and depth, and generate seabed ecological environment reports. A separate fault-diagnosis decision-support system gives pilots troubleshooting guidance and historical fault solutions, with technical drawings, to safeguard operations.

The framing of the announcement reflects a broader strategic push. State media consistently tie deep-sea capability to China's "maritime power" strategy and national key R&D programs, emphasizing indigenous innovation and international openness. Sun said the upgraded platform would allow China to "open and share" deep-sea research capabilities with global partners in the future, adding that the dives would further deepen international ocean cooperation.

The dives took place in the Philippine Sea Basin and western Pacific high seas — areas of both scientific interest, such as biodiversity and seabed geology, and strategic attention. Chinese expeditions in international waters are presented as legitimate scientific activity. The Jiaolong milestone also sits alongside a widening Chinese submersible portfolio: in late 2025, Fendouzhe and Jiaolong conducted joint underwater operations during China's 15th Arctic scientific expedition, when Jiaolong completed China's first dive in an Arctic ice zone, according to [China.com.cn](http://photo.china.com.cn/2025-10/27/content_118144125.shtml).

## What's Next

The immediate question is whether the upgraded Jiaolong moves into routine operational service and how quickly scientific results from these dives are published. Sun's comments suggest further announcements on international sharing and open access to the platform are likely. The trial also reinforces a pattern worth watching: China is modernizing its existing fleet at the same time as it expands the reach of newer, deeper-diving vehicles across the Pacific and the Arctic — activity that is scientific in purpose but increasingly visible in geopolitically sensitive waters.
