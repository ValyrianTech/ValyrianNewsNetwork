# China Industrial Profits Up 15.5% as AI Boom Masks Weakness

Profits at China's large-scale industrial enterprises rose 15.5% year-on-year in the first nine months of 2026, reaching RMB 6.03 trillion, according to data released Tuesday by the [National Bureau of Statistics](https://www.news.cn/fortune/20261027/76a7397c615a47cc96e567ca3de6b964/c.html). The headline figure — a continuation of double-digit cumulative growth throughout the year — was powered overwhelmingly by an electronics boom, even as several legacy sectors contracted sharply and the overall pace cooled from earlier in the year.

September alone saw industrial profits grow 14.4% year-on-year, a rebound from the near-stall of July, when single-month growth slumped to just 2.6%. Operating revenue for the January–September period climbed 6.6% to RMB 106.21 trillion, while the operating revenue profit margin improved to 5.68%, up 0.44 percentage points from a year earlier.

## The AI and chip engine

The story within the story is electronics. Profits in computer, communications and other electronic equipment manufacturing surged 103.8% year-on-year, contributing a striking 9.6 percentage points to the overall 15.5% headline growth, according to NBS Chief Statistician Yu Weining.

"In Jan–Sep, electronics industry profits grew 1.0-fold, contributing 9.6 percentage points to overall industrial profit growth — an important pillar for the relatively rapid profit growth," Yu wrote in the bureau's [official interpretation](https://www.news.cn/fortune/20261027/a3bd22523ec047e8a4a4e709d37f7387/c.html).

The surge reflects surging demand for AI computing power, advanced memory and semiconductors. Electronic specialty materials profits jumped 2.2-fold, semiconductor discrete devices rose 51.7%, optoelectronic devices climbed 69.8% and electronic circuits gained 62.5%, Yu noted. High-tech manufacturing more broadly posted profit growth of 52.0% — 36.5 percentage points faster than all above-scale industry — and now accounts for 25.5% of total industrial profit, up 6.2 percentage points year-on-year. That shift is central to Beijing's "new quality productive forces" policy push.

Raw materials were the second engine. Non-ferrous metal smelting and rolling profits jumped 79.7%, chemical raw materials and products rose 49.2%, and oil and gas extraction gained 21.3%, as AI and new-energy industries stoked demand for copper and aluminum. Mining profits overall rose 38.2%, and manufacturing gained 17.0%.

## A tale of diverging sectors

Beneath the aggregate, the picture is far more uneven. While a narrow set of high-tech and commodity-linked winners carried the headline, several major industries posted steep declines.

Ferrous metal smelting and rolling profits collapsed 73.3%, non-metallic mineral products — a proxy for cement and construction materials tied to the property sector — fell 46.9%, and automobile manufacturing dropped 22.8%. Electrical machinery and apparatus slipped 5.9%, agricultural and sideline food processing fell 17.3%, and electricity and heat production and supply declined 17.9%, pulling the broader utilities category down 14.3%.

The auto decline underscores the persistent margin squeeze from China's EV price war, while the weakness in steel and cement reflects the ongoing property-sector slump. Only petroleum, coal and other fuel processing turned from loss to profit.

By enterprise type, shareholding enterprises led with profit growth of 20.8%, followed by state-controlled enterprises at 13.1% and private firms at 8.7%. Foreign, Hong Kong, Macao and Taiwan-invested enterprises barely grew at all, posting just 0.5%.

## Cooling momentum and cash-flow caution

The 15.5% cumulative pace marks a deceleration from the 18.7% recorded for the first half of 2026, a peak for the year's cumulative growth, as [Securities Times](https://www.p5w.net/roll/cj/202607/t20260728_6488760.htm) reported in July. The moderation was foreshadowed by a sharp slowdown in July's single-month figures, before September's rebound to 14.4%.

Even as profits rise, balance-sheet indicators point to working-capital strain. Accounts receivable climbed 10.0% year-on-year to RMB 29.77 trillion, and the average receivables collection period lengthened to 71.6 days, up 1.3 days. Finished-goods inventory rose 11.6%, and inventory turnover time increased to 21.0 days. Total liabilities grew 7.4%, outpacing the 6.7% growth in total assets, nudging the asset-liability ratio to 58.6%.

On the cost side, the picture was more encouraging: cost per RMB 100 of revenue fell 0.45 yuan to 85.02 yuan, continuing a year-long trend of declining unit costs.

## What to watch

Yu framed the outlook around policy support and structural transition, calling for continued efforts to "leverage macro-policy effectiveness, step up domestic-demand expansion, optimize supply, accelerate the transition between old and new growth drivers, and strengthen endogenous economic momentum."

The data lands amid Beijing's broader push to boost domestic consumption and manage a prolonged property downturn. For investors and analysts, the key question is whether the AI-driven electronics boom can broaden enough to offset the drag from autos, steel, cement and utilities — or whether the headline 15.5% will continue to flatter an economy in which the median industrial firm's experience diverges sharply from the aggregate. The trajectory of electronics profits, the depth of the property-linked slump, and the pace of receivables build-up in the months ahead will be the figures to watch.
