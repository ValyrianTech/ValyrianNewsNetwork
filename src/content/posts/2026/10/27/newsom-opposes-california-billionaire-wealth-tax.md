# Newsom Opposes California Billionaire Wealth Tax

California Governor Gavin Newsom is actively opposing Proposition 40, a November ballot measure that would impose a one-time 5% wealth tax on the state's roughly 200 billionaires, even as the California Democratic Party has endorsed it. According to [KQED](https://www.kqed.org/news/12106033/the-political-calculus-behind-newsoms-opposition-to-californias-billionaire-tax), the governor's stance is a deliberate political calculation as he weighs a 2028 presidential run, disappointing progressives while courting centrists and wealthy donors.

## What Prop 40 Would Do

Proposition 40, proposed by the healthcare union SEIU-UHW, would levy a one-time 5% tax on the total wealth of California's roughly 200 billionaires. It would apply to any billionaire who lived in the state on January 1, 2026, with about 90% of the revenue directed toward Medi-Cal, the state's healthcare program for low-income residents covering roughly 15 million Californians. The measure is framed as a way to backfill deep federal budget cuts to Medicaid under the Trump administration.

Newsom argues such a tax should be imposed nationally so billionaires don't flee the state, warning that a state-level levy would ultimately hurt the budget by driving wealthy entrepreneurs elsewhere.

## A Counter-Measure Funded by Brin

Newsom has gone further than mere opposition: on October 9, he endorsed Proposition 42, a counter-measure funded by Google co-founder Sergey Brin. According to [CalMatters](https://calmatters.org/politics/2026/10/newsom-tax-propositions-november-election/), Prop 42 would cancel Prop 40 by prohibiting retroactive taxes and banning new taxes on personal property, including pensions, 401(k)s, investments, business holdings, and art collections.

"We all agree that Californians deserve to have their retirement and life savings protected from new state taxes so those savings are there when they need them," Newsom said in a statement announcing the endorsement.

Prop 42 is one of two Brin-backed counter-measures. Proposition 41 would subject any Prop 40 revenues to spending limits and audits; Newsom has not taken a position on it.

## Splitting the Democratic Party

Newsom's opposition has opened a rift within his own party. The California Democratic Party endorsed Prop 40 despite the governor's stance, and labor leaders have sharply criticized his position. "There are a lot of elected officials who are so afraid of this billionaire class," said Lorena Gonzalez, president of the California Federation of Labor Unions.

Dave Regan, president of SEIU-UHW West and the architect of the billionaire tax, was more pointed. "He is not living in the same world, and he is trying to raise hundreds of millions of dollars from these folks... he is acting like a politician with a campaign problem and not a governor with a healthcare problem."

Progressive critics see donor influence at work. Rep. Ro Khanna, a California Democrat and potential 2028 contender, told [Democracy Now](https://www.democracynow.org/2026/10/20/california_billionaire_tax): "You can't be anti-Trump and then do the bidding of Trump donors." Saikat Chakrabarti, president of the progressive think tank New Consensus, argued Newsom opposes the tax because "he needs those billionaires on his side."

## The Electability Bet

Supporters of Newsom's stance argue it signals the kind of moderation that matters in a national primary. "That might help him, even in a Democratic primary, because the top issue for Democratic primary voters in presidential elections is, is this person electable?" said Jim Kessler of the center-left think tank Third Way. "No one believes that California is an under-taxed state."

Fernando Guerra, a political science professor at Loyola Marymount University, said opposing the tax "defuses him being the total lefty from California." He predicted the measure will be defeated—giving Newsom a "voters have spoken" argument to carry into a national race.

The progressive backlash is already reaching beyond California. The Yes on Prop 40 campaign has launched television and digital ads calling out Newsom in early presidential primary states—South Carolina, Nevada, New Mexico, Virginia, and New Hampshire—rather than in California, where the No side is heavily outspending supporters.

## What to Watch

Polling shows a slim majority of likely voters support the tax, setting up a close November contest with national implications. Billionaires are already positioning: Meta struck a new deal with CEO Mark Zuckerberg, creating a new non-voting share class to keep him in California after he warned he might leave if Prop 40 passes, while tech billionaire Chris Larsen has donated more than $12 million to oppose the measure.

Notably, former Vice President Kamala Harris and former House Speaker Nancy Pelosi have stayed silent on Prop 40. How the ballot measures resolve—and whether a Prop 40 defeat becomes an asset or a liability for Newsom—will shape the opening moves of the 2028 Democratic primary.
