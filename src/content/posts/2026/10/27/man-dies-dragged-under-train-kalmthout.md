# Man dies after being dragged under train in Kalmthout

A 34-year-old man has died in hospital after he was dragged under a departing passenger train at Heide station in Kalmthout, in the Flemish province of Antwerp, the Antwerp public prosecutor's office has confirmed. The man, who lost a leg in the incident on Monday evening, died of his injuries on Tuesday.

The accident happened at around 17:15 on Monday 26 October, when the man and another person were removed from the train by railway police. As the train pulled out of the station, the man was caught and dragged onto the tracks.

## What happened

According to [The Brussels Times](https://www.brusselstimes.com/belgium/2379995/man-kicked-off-train-in-antwerp-dies-after-being-dragged-underneath), the man and a companion had been put off the train shortly before the accident, reportedly after he had allegedly harassed other passengers. As the train left the station, the man is said to have continued protesting from the platform and banged on the carriage windows.

Flemish broadcaster [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/10/27/slachtoffer-trein-heide-kalmthout-overleden/) reports that, according to witnesses, the victim was caught by his backpack as the train departed and was dragged along before falling onto the tracks. He suffered life-threatening injuries and was taken to hospital in critical condition, where he died the following day.

The train was a service on the busy line running from Antwerp toward the Dutch border town of Essen, near Roosendaal. Rail traffic between Kapellen and Essen was disrupted for a considerable time after the incident.

## Investigation under way

The Antwerp prosecutor's office has opened an investigation to establish the exact circumstances of the death. Officials have not yet released the victim's name or nationality, and it remains unclear precisely why the two men were removed from the train and by whom.

Investigators are expected to examine the conduct of the train at departure, the role played by the backpack and the sequence of events on the platform. The second person who was put off the train, who was not reported injured, is also likely to be questioned as part of the inquiry.

## A recurring rail-safety concern

The case highlights the risks that arise at the platform edge when passengers interact with moving trains. Incidents in which people are dragged under departing carriages after altercations, or while attempting to board or alight, have recurred on Belgium's rail network. Heide, a stop on the historic Antwerp-Essen line, is a commuter-belt station near the Dutch border.

The death also raises questions about how disruptive passengers are handled by railway police and about platform safety at smaller stations, where staff presence and surveillance may be more limited.

## What's next

The prosecutor's office has not indicated when it expects to conclude its investigation. For now, the death is being treated as a tragic accident rather than a crime, though the inquiry will determine whether any further action is warranted.

The case has drawn attention across Belgium's Dutch-, French- and English-language media, with outlets largely relying on the prosecutor's statement and agency reporting. What remains to be established is the full account of how a confrontation over alleged harassment on board ended in a fatal accident on the platform minutes later.
