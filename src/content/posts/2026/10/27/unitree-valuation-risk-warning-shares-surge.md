# Unitree Warns of Valuation Risk After 65% Share Surge

Chinese humanoid robot maker Unitree Robotics has warned investors of valuation risks after its Shanghai STAR Market shares surged more than 65% over seven trading days, a rally driven by a double upgrade from J.P. Morgan and mounting optimism about "physical artificial intelligence," [Caixin reported](https://www.caixinglobal.com/2026-10-27/unitree-warns-of-valuation-risks-after-shares-surge-more-than-65-102497621.html). The company's own caution, issued in an after-hours filing, carries unusual weight: it signals that management views the stock price as increasingly decoupled from the near-term commercial reality of the humanoid-robot business.

## A Rally Built on Optimism, Not Earnings

The advance began around Oct. 15, when Unitree traded near 408 yuan. The turning point came on Oct. 25, when J.P. Morgan upgraded the stock two notches, from "underweight" to "overweight," and lifted its December 2027 price target to 995 yuan — more than triple its previous target and implying roughly 48% upside from the Oct. 26 close. The upgrade followed the 2026 World Physical AI Conference in Hangzhou.

The catalyst was not an earnings beat but a technological narrative. On Oct. 23, the stock hit its daily trading limit, closing up 20% at 624 yuan, after founder Wang Xingxing demonstrated how OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra model — released Sept. 3 — could directly operate Unitree robots without prior training, [Caixin noted](https://www.caixinglobal.com/2026-10-26/unitree-robotics-surges-as-ai-models-promise-to-unlock-robot-potential-102497443.html). The demonstration addressed a long-standing bear case: that Unitree's robots are hardware-heavy but software-weak, dependent on remote control or preset programs. If general-purpose AI models can serve as a ready-made "brain," hardware leaders gain without building world-class software themselves.

On Oct. 26, the stock gained another 7.7%, touching an intraday high of 737.5 yuan (about $110) on trading value of 7.47 billion yuan and a turnover rate of 37.7%. That evening, Unitree filed its warning, flagging a cumulative price deviation of 30.5% over three trading days.

## The Numbers Behind the Warning

The most striking figure is Unitree's trailing price-to-earnings ratio: 465.2x, against an industry average of 38.5x. Even after the run-up, the stock remains below its first-day opening level of 1,100 yuan, though it still trades at roughly four-and-a-half times its 150.8-yuan IPO price. Rather than a fresh breakout, the rally is a partial recovery from a steep post-listing decline.

That decline was dramatic. Unitree completed a blockbuster STAR Market IPO in August 2026, opening at 1,100 yuan on debut before plunging 44% within a week, as [Caixin's earlier analysis](https://www.caixinglobal.com/2026-08-28/analysis-unitree-shares-slide-as-investors-reassess-humanoid-robot-valuations-102478761.html) described. Analysts attributed the volatility in part to a tiny free float — only 7.4% of shares were available for trading — which amplified retail speculation.

## Smart Money Sells Into the Rally

Institutional flows suggest that professional investors are treating the surge as a liquidity event rather than a vote of conviction. On Oct. 26, two institutional seats recorded net purchases of 249 million yuan, while the five largest selling seats offloaded 816 million yuan. The major sellers were associated with China International Capital Corp., Goldman Sachs and GF Securities. Notably, J.P. Morgan Securities (China) was recorded as an institutional seller during the Oct. 23 limit-up session — meaning the bank's research arm was upgrading the stock even as its trading desk was selling.

## Commercialization Still Years Away

The valuation debate ultimately hinges on how quickly humanoid robots become commercially viable. The company's founder has been candid about the timeline. At the World Robot Conference in August, Wang said factory adoption remains limited by weak efficiency and generalization, and that embodied AI may still be two to ten years from a "ChatGPT moment." That caution sits uneasily with a 465x multiple.

The operational picture is mixed. Revenue climbed from 159 million yuan in 2023 to 1.7 billion yuan in 2025, a compound annual growth rate of 226.8%, and net profit rose to 278 million yuan in 2025. But first-half 2026 revenue growth slowed sharply to an estimated 35.6%–45.4% year-on-year, weighed down by a higher base, cooling industry fervor and aggressive entry by electric-vehicle and consumer-electronics makers. Hardware still accounts for nearly 99% of revenue.

Competition is also intensifying. At the World Robot Games, Unitree won two silvers and no golds, while rivals Tiangong and AgiBot took five and four golds respectively. Unitree had produced roughly 11,000 G1 bipedal robots by June 2026, against estimated global humanoid shipments of about 19,100 units in the first half of the year.

## What to Watch

The structural backdrop remains supportive. China's Ministry of Industry and Information Technology said the country produced more than 40,000 humanoid robots in the first half of 2026, with full-year output expected to exceed 100,000 units, and Beijing has approved its first national humanoid-robot standards, effective April 1, 2027.

Yet for investors, several risks loom. A 12-month lock-up for pre-IPO shareholders and most strategic investors expires in August 2027, potentially creating a supply shock if earnings disappoint. AI startup DeepSeek, a Unitree shareholder, has committed to a longer 36-month lock-up on its holdings — a sign of confidence, but not a guarantee. And the stock's fate is now tightly bound to a single thesis: that physical AI matures fast enough to justify a valuation far above its peers. If that narrative cools, the company's own warning may prove prescient.
