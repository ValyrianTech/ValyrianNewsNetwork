# Belgian Diesel Hits Record €2.51 as Energy Crisis Deepens

Belgium's maximum price for diesel will rise to a record €2.51 per litre on Wednesday, the country's Federal Public Service for Economy announced on Tuesday, as an energy crisis driven by conflict in the Middle East tightens its grip on European households and businesses. The 6.6-cent increase eclipses the previous all-time high of €2.501 per litre set barely a month earlier, on 22 September, according to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/liveblog/dieselprijs-klimt-naar-nieuw-record-251-euro-per-liter~1774343778229/).

The record comes with a striking twist: petrol is getting cheaper. The maximum price of petrol 95 (E10) will fall 1.4 cents to €1.988 per litre — dropping below the €2 mark for the first time since early September — while petrol 98 (E5) slips to €2.149. Heating oil, however, moves in the opposite direction, rising roughly 2.5 cents to €1.6108 per litre for orders of at least 2,000 litres.

## A Crisis With Deep Roots

The diesel milestone is not an isolated event but the latest symptom of a European energy emergency that has been building for eight months. Since the United States and Israel attacked Iran in late February 2026, and Iran's Revolutionary Guard closed the Strait of Hormuz, the global energy market has been under sustained strain. The closure removed roughly 11 million barrels of oil per day from circulation — more than the 1973 and 1978 oil crises combined, according to VRT NWS energy analysis.

Unlike the supply shocks of the 1970s, this crisis involves destroyed infrastructure rather than merely blocked shipping. Iran reportedly struck almost 40 oil and gas installations, while Qatar says it has lost more than 17% of its gas production for years to come. Even if the Strait were to reopen fully, rebuilding global inventories could take up to two years, warned Amin Nasser, chief executive of Saudi oil giant Aramco.

The price pressures are visible across the board. Brent crude traded around $113.98 a barrel in late October, up from roughly $101 earlier in the month, while European gas prices on the benchmark TTF exchange climbed above €97 per megawatt-hour — the highest level since the December 2022 energy crisis. Before the Iran war broke out, European gas cost about €32 per megawatt-hour.

## Households on the Front Line

The impact on Belgian families has been severe. The average annual gas bill for a household with average consumption rose from €1,319 in March to €1,898 in September — an increase of 44%, according to figures cited by the Gezinsbond, the Belgian family association. Households on variable contracts feel such increases immediately.

A study by the Université libre de Bruxelles for the federal energy regulator CREG found that 11.8% of Belgian households — about 10% of the population — were "energy vulnerable" in 2024, meaning they struggle to pay energy bills, cannot adequately heat their homes, or spend more than 7.5% of disposable income on energy. The burden falls disproportionately on single young people, single elderly people, single-parent families and renters, with more than one in five single young people and single-parent households classified as vulnerable.

"We are not asking for general support, but targeted help for families in energy poverty," said Gezinsbond chairman Ivo Mechels, calling for a strengthened social heating fund and a temporary expansion of the social tariff so that vulnerable families do not end up without power or heating.

## A Storm on the Horizon

The strain is already reaching the retail energy market. Smaller supplier Ecofix has told customers that gas advance payments will rise by about a quarter from November — a move echoing the 2022 crisis, when many suppliers sharply increased prepayments.

Energy expert Luc Pauwels of VRT NWS said the decision should set off alarm bells. "A storm is coming," he warned, noting that Ecofix operates variable contracts whose prices already track the market. "Apparently they expect gas prices to remain high all winter, and that gas price is now almost three times as high as last winter. Gas reserves in Europe are historically low, and the markets are reacting to that in panic."

"We are in a serious energy crisis," Pauwels concluded. The numbers bear him out: Belgium's only gas storage facility, at Loenhout, is just over 60% full — compared with 93.9% a year earlier — while European gas reserves overall stood at about 70%, down from 82% a year ago.

## Why Diesel and Petrol Are Moving Apart

The divergence between diesel and petrol is one of the more puzzling features of the current squeeze. Diesel supply remains especially tight, linked to refinery constraints and a global diesel shortage that prompted the International Energy Agency to prioritise diesel in its reserve releases. In early October, the G7 announced it would release up to 100 million barrels of crude and diesel from strategic reserves over four months, coordinated by the IEA, with diesel given priority because of market tightness.

Petrol, by contrast, has seen some supply easing, allowing pump prices to retreat below the psychologically important €2 threshold — cold comfort for the many Belgian motorists and hauliers who rely on diesel, and for a freight sector already squeezed by high operating costs.

## Europe's Policy Puzzle

The crisis has forced European leaders into difficult choices. At an emergency summit in Brussels on 15 October, heads of government debated the EU's ETS2 carbon tax on heating and transport, scheduled to start in 2028. Italy, Poland, Czechia, Slovakia and Hungary pushed to delay it to 2030, while Sweden, Denmark and Finland argued for keeping it intact. The Council conclusion asked the Commission to update its impact assessment rather than delay or scrap the scheme.

"It is indeed coming," Belgian Prime Minister Bart De Wever said at a late-night press conference. "Dropping it now would be pointless."

EU Energy Commissioner Dan Jorgensen warned in late September that the winter ahead could be brutal. "During a normal winter, almost 50 million people in Europe cannot properly heat their homes," he said. "This winter, that could become even worse." The IEA's executive director, Fatih Birol, has described the situation as the biggest energy crisis in decades, while Equinor chief executive Anders Opedal cautioned that Norway — Europe's key alternative supplier — has "no spare capacity for oil and gas."

## What to Watch

With Brent near $114, European gas above €97 per megawatt-hour and winter heating demand just beginning, the pressure on diesel prices shows little sign of easing. Analysts warn that inventories are dangerously low and could take up to two years to rebuild even in a best-case scenario. The first real test will come with the first sustained cold snap of the season.

For Belgian consumers, the immediate questions are practical: how high bills will climb this winter, whether the EU will expand social tariffs or delay ETS2, and whether diesel — the fuel that powers the country's freight and farming sectors — will break further records before the year is out. On current trends, few are betting against it.
