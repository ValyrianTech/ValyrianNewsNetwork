# Tibet Quake Left 20-Kilometer Surface Rupture, Team Finds

Field investigators have determined that the magnitude 7.0 earthquake that struck Baqing County in the Tibet Autonomous Region tore a surface rupture running roughly 20 kilometers between its start and end points, China's Xinhua News Agency [reported](https://www.news.cn/local/20261027/2607ab1bcb19496ca65fc0485937c87b/c.html). The finding, released by a field team from the China Earthquake Administration (CEA), offers the clearest picture yet of the fault movement behind the temblor and will help define the zone of extreme damage as reconstruction begins.

## What Investigators Found

The rupture zone trends nearly north to south. According to the CEA field team, surveyors first located the surface rupture on 25 October at a site roughly 15 kilometers east of the epicenter and about 20 kilometers from Maru Township. The next day, the team pinpointed its two endpoints — on a hillside by a summer pasture in Gangqionga Village and on a slope northwest of Gelonggai Village, both in Maru Township.

Li Feng, a researcher at the China Earthquake Disaster Prevention Center, described the character of the break. "Both the north and south ends of this earthquake fault cut through the ridgeline," Li said, according to Xinhua. "The main rupture zone is dominated by normal-fault rupture with a component of left-lateral strike-slip, slipping about 0.9 meters; the highest intensity was IX (9 degrees)."

On China's seismic intensity scale, which gauges the severity of shaking and damage rather than the energy released, intensity IX denotes severe destruction. The combination of a shallow rupture, roughly 0.9 meters of surface slip, and intensity-IX shaking helps explain the concentrated devastation in the townships near the epicenter.

The survey was aimed at on-site intensity assessment and seismic-damage distribution mapping — work that establishes the rupture's length and character, supports the delineation of the extreme-damage zone, and provides a scientific basis for rebuilding.

## The Earthquake and Its Aftermath

The mainshock struck at about 22:33 on 22 October at 32.40°N, 93.79°E at a depth of just 10 kilometers — shallow enough to produce strong surface effects. The epicenter lies within the Qiangtang Block of the Qinghai-Tibet Plateau, close to the Baqing-Leiwuqi fault about 2 kilometers away. Scientists described the event as an extensional, or normal-fault, rupture consistent with the region's tectonics.

The quake killed 16 people and injured 76, according to figures reported in the days after the event, while displacing thousands from their homes in a remote, high-altitude region where villages are scattered across steep terrain. More than 12,000 people were relocated to safety and hundreds of households saw their homes collapse or suffer damage.

The disaster also triggered a series of strong aftershocks. A magnitude 5.3 aftershock shook Zaduo County in Qinghai Province on 24 October, and a magnitude 5.7 event followed on the evening of 25 October near the main epicenter, the largest to date, [according](https://news.sina.cn/znl/2026-10-26/detail-iniwqhxr2064951.d.html) to China Earthquake Networks Center measurements cited in state coverage. The two main epicenters lay about 19 kilometers apart.

## A Race Against Winter

Rescue and relief operations have been complicated by altitude, cold, and wind. Nighttime temperatures drop below freezing, and gusts in some areas have reached 13 meters per second, adding urgency to winterization efforts. Crews worked through the night to complete the first batch of 60 prefabricated resettlement houses by 02:10 on 25 October, and power was restored to a resettlement site in Maru Township after an eight-hour push, state media reported.

Authorities have moved to assess damaged homes village by village, tagging buildings on a three-tier scale — green for usable, yellow for suspended use, and red for prohibited — to guide repairs and reconstruction. In Gangqie Township, one of the hardest-hit areas, 17 resettlement sites have taken in more than 900 households, and additional prefabricated housing is being erected.

## Why the Rupture Matters

The precise mapping of a surface rupture is more than a scientific curiosity. The length and orientation of the break help seismologists understand how stress released along the fault, and it tells engineers and planners exactly where the ground itself failed — information that is critical for siting new homes, roads, and infrastructure away from the most hazardous ground.

China's official response has spanned national, regional, and local levels, with thousands of rescue personnel, including military and armed police units, deployed to the disaster zone. Central authorities have allocated tens of millions of yuan in disaster relief and reconstruction funds, alongside emergency supplies, heaters, and clean-water equipment.

## What to Watch

Several questions remain open. The 20-kilometer figure is the straight-line distance between the two confirmed endpoints; the actual trace of the rupture may be longer once fully mapped. The final casualty toll has not been formally updated since the initial figures were released, and continued aftershocks — along with the risk of rockfalls and landslides on already-cracked slopes — remain a concern as winter closes in.

For the people of Baqing, the immediate priority is safe shelter before the cold deepens. For scientists, the rupture data will feed into a longer effort to understand the restless tectonics of the Qiangtang Block — and to prepare for the next event on a plateau where the ground is always, slowly, on the move.
