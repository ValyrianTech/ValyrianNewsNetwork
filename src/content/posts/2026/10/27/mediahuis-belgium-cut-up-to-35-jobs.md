# Mediahuis Belgium to Cut Up to 35 Jobs as Costs Bite

Mediahuis België, one of the country's largest media groups, announced on 27 October 2026 that up to 35 jobs are at risk as it launches a restructuring plan aimed at keeping the company financially healthy. The publisher of *De Standaard*, *Het Nieuwsblad*, *Gazet van Antwerpen* and *Het Belang van Limburg* disclosed the plan at a special works council and has signalled its intention to proceed with a collective dismissal, [VRT NWS reported](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/10/27/tot-35-jobs-op-de-tocht-bij-mediahuis-belgie/).

## What the plan involves

According to a company statement quoted by VRT NWS, the proposed plan "could lead to a reduction of 29 full-time equivalents (FTEs), meaning that an estimated maximum of 35 employees could be affected." That figure represents roughly 4 percent of the group's Belgian workforce of just over 850 jobs.

Mediahuis attributes the move to two converging pressures. Distribution costs have risen by approximately €25 million over the past three years, even as print volumes decline, while wage costs have climbed by around 30 percent in five years. At the same time, advertising revenues remain under strain from competition with international digital platforms.

"To remain financially healthy in the longer term and to preserve room for investment, Mediahuis België considers structural measures necessary," the company said. Negotiations with the trade unions have now begun.

## A recurring pattern

The announcement extends a series of incremental restructurings at Mediahuis België. In 2019, the company eliminated 82 jobs; in 2024, a further 20 positions were cut. Last year, jobs also disappeared at the newsrooms of *Gazet van Antwerpen* and *Het Nieuwsblad*. The latest round follows the same logic of trimming costs to protect margins in a shrinking print market.

*De Standaard* independently reported the same figures under the headline "Transformatieplan van Mediahuis België bedreigt maximaal 35 werknemers" ("Mediahuis België transformation plan threatens a maximum of 35 employees"), [noting](https://www.standaard.be/economie/bedrijven/transformatieplan-van-mediahuis-belgie-bedreigt-maximaal-35-werknemers/163662441.html) that the plan caps the number of affected staff at 35.

## A profitable group, not a distressed one

The cuts are striking because they come from a position of financial strength rather than crisis. When Mediahuis published its full-year 2025 results in February, it reported consolidated revenue of €1.258 billion, up 2 percent, and a stable operating result of €151.8 million. The group closed 2025 with a net cash position of €84.4 million, having turned around a net debt position of €72.4 million a year earlier, according to [the results announcement](https://www.emerce.nl/wire/mediahuis-boekt-stabiele-resultaten-2025-overschrijdt-kaap-n-miljoen-digitale-abonnees).

Digital growth has been a bright spot: the group surpassed one million digital subscribers in 2025, an 8 percent increase, with nearly 60 percent of all subscribers now fully or predominantly digital. The company itself flagged the cost squeeze, noting that distribution costs in Belgium nearly tripled compared with 2023.

That combination — a debt-free balance sheet and continued job reductions — points to a structural realignment rather than an emergency. The Belgian cuts are best read as a margin-protection and investment-capacity measure in a market where fixed distribution and wage costs are largely volume-independent, while print advertising and circulation continue to erode.

## The wider Belgian picture

Mediahuis is far from alone. Belgian employers announced collective dismissal intentions at 63 companies in the first half of 2026, threatening 5,115 jobs, while all of 2025 saw 8,428 jobs involved in announced collective dismissals, [Business AM reported](https://businessam.be/dossier-collectief-ontslag-hoe-bescherm-je-je-inkomen-wanneer-je-job-op-de-tocht-staat/), citing federal employment data. Flanders accounted for the majority of threatened jobs.

Belgium's collective dismissal rules, anchored in the so-called Wet-Renault adopted after the 1997 Renault Vilvoorde closure, oblige employers to inform and consult employee representatives before proceeding and to set up re-employment support. An announced intention is not the same as definitive dismissals: in completed procedures in the first half of 2026, roughly 4,072 workers were ultimately dismissed against 4,539 initially involved — a difference of about 10 percent — and the consultation phase ran a median of around 63 days.

## What to watch

The final number of job losses at Mediahuis België may well fall below 35. Past restructurings in Belgium have seen announced figures shrink after consultation through voluntary departures and redeployment, and the company has not yet specified which departments or roles are affected. Union reaction and any threat of industrial action have not yet been reported.

For now, the story is a familiar one for European newsrooms: digital subscribers are growing, but the cost of putting papers on doorsteps and paying staff is outpacing the advertising revenue that once underwrote the business. How Mediahuis balances those forces — and how its Belgian workforce fares through the talks — will be the test in the weeks ahead.
