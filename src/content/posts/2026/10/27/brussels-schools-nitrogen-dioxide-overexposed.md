# One in Four Brussels Schools Overexposed to NO2 Pollution

More than a quarter of Brussels' primary schools are exposed to nitrogen dioxide at levels at least 50 percent above the World Health Organization's recommended limit, according to a new ranking by the air-quality campaign group Les Chercheurs d'air, as [reported by Le Soir](https://www.lesoir.be/775532/article/2026-10-08/un-quart-des-ecoles-bruxelloises-sont-surexposees-au-dioxyde-dazote). The findings place children's health and traffic pollution at the centre of a widening debate over air quality in the Belgian capital — and show that the burden falls heaviest on its poorest neighbourhoods.

## The findings

The study, published on 8 October 2026, used the SIRANE air-dispersion modelling software to analyse all 841 Brussels schools — primary and secondary — for the reference year 2024. According to the [campaign group's own report](https://www.leschercheursdair.be/2026/10/08/combien-decoles-bruxelloises-sont-trop-polluees/), every one of the 841 establishments exceeds the WHO's annual-mean NO₂ recommendation of 10 µg/m³.

The headline "one in four" figure refers specifically to fundamental schools — nursery and primary combined. Of the 618 analysed, 167 (27.1 percent) face NO₂ concentrations of more than 15 µg/m³, which is 50 percent above the WHO guideline. Those are the institutions the group classifies as "overexposed."

"We are in a situation that is genuinely dangerous for children's health," Renaud Leemans, campaign coordinator for Les Chercheurs d'air, told Le Soir.

The WHO, quoted in the group's report, warns that "exceeding air-quality guideline levels is associated with significant public-health risks." Nitrogen dioxide is a respiratory irritant produced mainly by combustion, chiefly vehicle engines; road traffic accounts for roughly 60 percent of NO₂ emissions in the Brussels Region. Exposure is linked to asthma, reduced lung function in children and cardiovascular disease.

## A double injustice

The exposure is not spread evenly across the capital. In Saint-Josse-ten-Noode, all schools face concentrations at least 50 percent above the WHO threshold. Seven in ten schools are affected in Schaerbeek, and one in two in Ixelles and the City of Brussels.

Those figures map closely onto income. Saint-Josse, Schaerbeek and the City of Brussels all post poverty-risk rates above 20 percent — reaching 33 percent in Saint-Josse, the highest in Belgium. Because economically precarious populations are more vulnerable to air pollution, the group describes the overlap as "a double injustice."

## A problem that predates this report

The 2026 ranking is the latest in a series documenting systematic NO₂ exceedances around sensitive sites in Brussels. In February 2022, [La Libre reported](https://www.lalibre.be/regions/bruxelles/2022/02/11/qualite-de-lair-bruxelles-tousse-les-ecoles-suffoquent-6MRPSSMRDZFJXHQJGG55SAIFXA/) on an earlier study showing that all schools measured exceeded WHO recommendations, while [RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/qualite-de-l-air-une-soixantaine-d-ecoles-bruxelloises-en-zone-trop-polluee-10931961) covered a passive-sampler campaign that found around 60 schools in zones too polluted. A [2024 report](https://www.leschercheursdair.be/2024/09/03/plus-dune-centaine-ecoles-fondamentales-bruxelloises-surexposees-au-no2-rapport/) from the same group found more than a hundred fundamental schools overexposed to NO₂.

## Improvement, but not enough

The picture has nonetheless improved. The campaign group notes that between 2022 and 2024 fewer schools were exposed to the highest pollution levels, crediting measures such as the Low-Emission Zone (LEZ) alongside the gradual renewal of the vehicle fleet. The group cautions, however, that its SIRANE-based figures are conservative — real concentrations facing children are "very probably higher" than those modelled.

Underpinning that improvement is one of Brussels' most contested policies. On 1 January 2026, after the Constitutional Court annulled a planned postponement, the LEZ tightened to ban Euro 5 diesel and Euro 2 petrol vehicles from the capital, a move [RTBF reported](https://www.rtbf.be/article/de-nombreux-vehicules-interdits-a-bruxelles-des-ce-1er-janvier-2026-qui-est-concerne-comment-cela-va-t-il-se-passer-11647108) affects some 400,000 vehicles. Brussels Environment says the zone has already cut nitrogen oxide levels by 55 percent, fine particles by 33 percent and black carbon by 60 percent.

## What's next

The ranking sharpens a difficult trade-off for Brussels policymakers. The legal EU limit for annual-mean NO₂ remains 40 µg/m³ — four times the WHO's health-based recommendation — meaning many schools can be fully compliant while still exposing children to levels the WHO considers harmful. For Les Chercheurs d'air, that gap is precisely the point: compliance is not the same as safety.

The immediate questions are where the region goes next on traffic and school siting. The group has called for stronger measures to limit pollution near educational sites, and its data gives campaigners a commune-by-commune map of where the need is greatest. With a quarter of the capital's primary schools still overexposed — and the worst cases concentrated in its poorest districts — the study makes clear that reducing traffic emissions remains as much a matter of equity as of environmental policy.
