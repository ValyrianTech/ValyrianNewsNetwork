# Magnitude 6.2 Quake Strikes off Papua New Guinea

A magnitude 6.2 earthquake struck beneath the Solomon Sea off the coast of Bougainville, Papua New Guinea, early Monday, according to the [United States Geological Survey](https://earthquake.usgs.gov/earthquakes/eventpage/us6000u1kv). The quake occurred at a depth of 53.5 kilometers, about 79 kilometers south-southeast of Panguna, and authorities issued no tsunami warning.

There were no immediate reports of damage or casualties. The USGS assessed the event at a green alert level, indicating a low likelihood of fatalities and economic loss.

## What Happened

The earthquake struck at 14:07 UTC on October 11 (1:07 a.m. local time on October 12), with its epicenter at 7.006°S, 155.702°E, under water roughly nine miles offshore. The USGS detected the event at 47 seismic stations and classified it with a maximum Modified Mercalli Intensity of V, corresponding to moderate shaking and very light damage.

China's state news agency Xinhua [reported](https://www.news.cn/20261011/ab477d6749a3401abfca05f42815181a/c.html) that the quake struck near Panguna, with a focal depth of 53.5 kilometers, citing the USGS. The report was later syndicated by [People's Daily](http://world.people.com.cn/n1/2026/1012/c1002-40812681.html).

## Limited Impact Predicted

According to [Earthquakelist.org](https://earthquakelist.org/news/2026/10/11/m6-2-earthquake-papua-new-guinea-1344775/), an earthquake-tracking service, shaking may have been felt by roughly 320,000 people across two countries. The majority — about 243,500 people — experienced light shaking at intensity level IV, while about 78,380 were exposed to moderate level V shaking. Papua New Guinea accounted for around 310,000 of those exposed, with residents of the Solomon Islands also affected.

The service noted that USGS models put the likelihood of between zero and one fatalities at about 90 percent, with a similarly high probability of economic damage between US$0 and US$1 million. No significant foreshocks or aftershocks were recorded within 100 kilometers of the epicenter.

## A Seismically Active Region

The epicenter lies within one of the world's most seismically active zones, at the junction of the Pacific and Indo-Australian tectonic plates. Earthquakelist.org noted that this was the strongest earthquake in the region since a magnitude 6.4 event on September 1, 2024. A magnitude 7.9 quake struck 44 kilometers west of Arawa in January 2017, and another magnitude 7.9 event hit the region in December 2016.

The reference point near the epicenter, Panguna, is home to a dormant copper and gold mine that was at the center of the Bougainville civil conflict between 1988 and 1998. The region is also pursuing independence from Papua New Guinea following a 2019 referendum, a process still pending ratification by the national parliament.

Because the quake's magnitude fell below 6.5, it was considered unlikely to trigger a destructive tsunami, though it occurred at a shallow depth under the sea.

## What to Watch

No tsunami statement was issued by any authority, and the USGS green alert assessment pointed to minimal impact. Outstanding questions remain over whether isolated structural damage in remote settlements near South Bougainville went unreported, and whether any aftershocks materialize in the coming days.
