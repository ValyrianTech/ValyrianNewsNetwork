# Reconstructed Huanggang Port Opens With One-Stop Clearance

The reconstructed Huanggang Port, the busiest passenger land crossing between Shenzhen and Hong Kong, officially opened at 6:30 a.m. local time on Oct 12, introducing a first-of-its-kind "cooperative inspection, single release" clearance model that allows travelers to complete both jurisdictions' immigration procedures after queuing just once, according to [People's Daily / Xinhua](http://gba.people.cn/n1/2026/1012/c42272-40812504.html).

Positioned as the world's largest single-structure land port complex, the rebuilt terminal consolidates a "one place, two checks" arrangement with the new cooperative model, cutting overall clearance time from roughly 30 minutes to about five minutes. It remains the only 24-hour passenger land port between the two cities.

## Context: From 1989 Landmark to Reconstruction

The original Huanggang Port opened in 1989 as China's first large land port offering 24-hour clearance. Over more than three decades it served hundreds of millions of cross-border passengers, with daily passenger traffic growing from several thousand to a peak of more than 60,000, and vehicle traffic rising from just over a hundred to about 10,000 a day, Xinhua reported. Mounting clearance demand led to an in-situ reconstruction that began in June 2019.

For years, travelers and drivers endured a "two places, two checks" regime — queuing separately at Shenzhen's Huanggang Port and Hong Kong's Lok Ma Chau Control Point, and riding across the Lok Ma Chau bridge in between. The new port removes that friction entirely by concentrating all inspections within a single joint building.

## Key Developments: A New Clearance Model

The signature change is the combined "one place, two checks" and "cooperative inspection, single release" model — the first such deployment at a Shenzhen-Hong Kong land crossing. As detailed by [China News Service](https://www.chinanews.com.cn/dwq/2026/10-12/10711878.shtml), the port features 134 cooperative-inspection express lanes and 68 manual counters. The express lanes use a three-gate integrated design: the first gate reads the travel document and verifies lane eligibility, the second collects fingerprint and facial biometrics for identity comparison, and the third releases the traveler. Manual counters are set side-by-side along the boundary line, so travelers queue once and present documents to both the exiting and entering sides.

Vehicle clearance has been overhauled in parallel. The port operates 28 "five-in-one" vehicle cooperative-inspection lanes that integrate five agencies — Shenzhen Customs, Shenzhen Border Inspection, Hong Kong Customs, the Hong Kong Department of Health, and Hong Kong Immigration. An intelligent all-in-one machine collects vehicle, document, face, and fingerprint data simultaneously, turning the traditional two-step inspection into what officials call "one stop, one inspection, coordinated release." Both sides' inspection departments achieve backend information interconnection and parallel vetting, so travelers need not re-queue or re-verify.

The difference was immediate for the first passengers through. "Previously, entering via Huanggang Port required two checkpoints, with cumbersome queuing and layered verification," said Hong Kong resident Ms. Huang, the new port's first inbound traveler, according to China News Service. "Today, one queue completes both sides' checks — it's really much faster." Cross-border bus driver Mr. Tan, the first inbound cross-border driver, said a single stop now completes the entire inspection process, cutting clearance time by more than half.

The new port is designated a pure passenger crossing; cross-border cargo inspection facilities have been removed to focus on passenger efficiency. Designed near-term daily clearance flow is 200,000 passengers, rising to 300,000 in the longer term.

## Analysis: Governance Under 'One Country, Two Systems'

The project's legal architecture took shape over 2026. The NPC Standing Committee authorized Hong Kong Special Administrative Region jurisdiction over the Hong Kong Port Area and related extension areas on June 26, followed by State Council approval on July 8 defining its coordinates and area. The "Huanggang Port Hong Kong Port Area Ordinance" (Cap. 659) was gazetted July 20 and took effect July 31.

The governance framework was formalized on July 30, when the HKSAR Government and the Shenzhen Municipal Government signed a cooperation arrangement on implementing "one place, two checks" at the port, alongside a land-use rights agreement. Under the terms, Shenzhen bears the design and construction cost of the entire reconstruction — including the Hong Kong Port Area — while the HKSAR pays a symbolic rent of RMB 1,000 per year, with the lease running until June 30, 2047. As [People's Daily Overseas Edition](http://hm.people.com.cn/n1/2026/0731/c42272-40771625.html) reported, HKSAR Secretary for Security Tang Ping-keung thanked the Central Government for its support, calling the signing "an important milestone in the preparatory work for the port's opening."

![60-second look at the Huanggang Port reconstruction process, produced by China News Service](https://www.chinanews.com/ga/shipin/cns-d/2026/10-12/news1071548.shtml)

The rebuilt complex spans 689,700 square meters across five floors above ground and four underground, with a vertical layout that separates outbound and inbound passenger flows and keeps pedestrian and vehicle traffic independent, according to a [Southern Metropolis Daily](https://m.mp.oeeee.com/a/BAAFRD0000202608051638556.html) feature. Its design concept, "Foundation of the Bay Area, Pillar of the Nation," draws on Shenzhen's city flower, the bougainvillea, for the L4 ceiling. Five rail lines are planned to converge at the site, including the existing Shenzhen Metro Line 7, which already has a Huanggang Port station.

## Implications: A Hub for the Greater Bay Area

Analysts see the port as more than a faster checkpoint. "Beyond everyday exchanges such as Hong Kong residents traveling north to shop and Shenzhen residents visiting Hong Kong for tourism, the commute of sci-tech talent and business people between the two cities will also become more convenient, further deepening livelihood and industrial coordination," said Yang Qiurong of the China Development Institute (Shenzhen), as quoted by Southern Metropolis Daily.

Chen Yanyan of the Renmin University of China Shenzhen Research Institute framed the shift as a geographic upgrade. "The change this brings is upgrading Huanggang Port from a Shenzhen-Hong Kong crossing into a hub port radiating across the Greater Bay Area," she said, noting that a researcher from Dongguan or Guangzhou could one day take an intercity train to the port and connect directly to the Hetao Shenzhen park and Hong Kong park.

That hub function will roll out in phases. The Guangzhou-Dongguan-Shenzhen intercity railway's Qianhai-Huanggang section is under construction, and the MTR Northern Link spur line is planned to connect to the port — expected, per earlier Hong Kong-side information, by 2034 or earlier. A planned Shenzhen-Guangzhou central-axis intercity also envisions a station there. Chen cautioned that the pace of these rail projects will determine how quickly the port's radiating capacity materializes.

## What's Next

The new port abuts the Hetao Shenzhen-Hong Kong Sci-Tech Innovation Cooperation Zone, a developing research hub under a "one river, two banks; one zone, two parks" model, and links the Guangzhou-Shenzhen-Hong Kong-Macao sci-tech innovation corridor to support a "half-hour research circle" and "one-hour industry circle." The joint opening announcement, issued Oct 9 by the Shenzhen Port Office and the HKSAR Security Bureau and covered by [Lianhe Zaobao](https://www.zaobao.com.sg/news/china/story20261009-9808989), confirmed the Oct 12 start and the suspension of passenger services at the temporary inspection buildings that had handled traffic during construction.

For now, the immediate test is operational: how smoothly a facility designed for 200,000 daily passengers handles the mix of commuters, shoppers, business travelers, and researchers expected through its doors. Day-one throughput figures were not immediately available. What is clear is that a crossing that once symbolized the physical separation between two cities has been rebuilt around the idea of moving people across it as if the boundary were barely there.
