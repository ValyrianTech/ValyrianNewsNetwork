# China Unifies Tax Penalty Standards Nationwide

China's State Taxation Administration (STA) has issued nationally unified benchmarks for the exercise of discretion in tax administrative penalties, a move aimed at standardizing tax enforcement, curbing arbitrary decision-making, and guaranteeing fair and consistent treatment of taxpayers across every region of the country, [Xinhua News reported](https://www.news.cn/fortune/20261006/01599a6d31b24212aee1da75a3bd1ac7/c.html).

The National Tax Administrative Penalty Discretion Benchmarks (2026 Edition) were publicly released on September 30 and take effect November 1, 2026. It marks the first time such benchmarks have been unified nationwide, replacing a patchwork of regional standards that had emerged over the past several years.

## A Shift From Regional to National Standards

The benchmarks cover 9 categories and 66 items of tax administrative penalty matters, each with defined discretion tiers ranging from "minor" to "serious," applicable conditions, and specific penalty standards, according to the [STA's official interpretation](https://guangdong.chinatax.gov.cn/gdsw/wzjd/2026-09/30/content_1ce5cf9755914e0f84eeae9b01b2b7ff.shtml) published by the Guangdong tax service.

The nine categories span violations of tax registration management (items 1-10), account books and voucher management (items 11-15), tax declaration and filing management (items 16-18), tax and fee collection management (items 19-30), tax inspection (items 31-33), invoice and voucher management (items 34-59), tax payment guarantee (items 60-62), tax-related professional services (items 63-65), and tax-related information reporting (item 66).

The effort follows a deliberate progression the STA has described as moving from "provincial unification" to "regional unification" and finally to "national unification." By early 2025, tax bureaus across six regions - Northeast, Southwest, Northwest, Central-South, North China, and East China - had each jointly issued their own regional benchmarks. The 2026 Edition was built, the agency said, "on fully absorbing the results of prior regional practice."

## How Penalties Are Tiered

The benchmarks refine existing statutory penalty ranges rather than create new offenses. In a [detailed report](https://www.yicai.com/news/103383156.html), the financial outlet Yicai illustrated the approach using tax evasion as an example.

Under existing law, tax evasion carries confiscation of unpaid or underpaid tax plus late fees, along with a fine of 50% to five times the evaded amount. The 2026 Edition breaks this into four discretion tiers based on three quantified factors: the number of penalties within five years, the amount of unpaid or underpaid tax, and the proportion of that amount to tax payable.

A first penalty within five years involving under RMB 500,000 in underpaid tax - or less than 30% of tax payable - falls into the "lighter" tier and draws a fine of 50% of the evaded amount. At the other end, two or more penalties within five years involving RMB 500,000 or more and at least 30% of tax payable fall into the "serious" tier, carrying a fine of three to five times the amount.

Yicai noted the benchmarks use a "fixed amount plus range" approach that preserves reasonable discretion for individual cases, preventing the simplistic, one-size-fits-all enforcement that can produce unfair outcomes.

## A Companion List for Minor Violations

Alongside the benchmarks, the STA issued a "Minor Violation Not Punished" item list covering eight penalty matters distilled from the benchmarks. The measure complements the agency's existing "First Violation Not Punished" framework, introduced in 2021, which already covered 14 items.

The official interpretation clarified a key definitional point: under the benchmarks, "first violation" means the first offense within a single calendar year - from January 1 to December 31 - a change intended to improve enforcement certainty.

An STA official in the Policy and Regulations Department said the comprehensive optimization of discretion tiers, applicable conditions, and standards for the 66 items would promote "more reasonable classification of tax violations, more scientific setting of discretion factors, and more precise control of discretion margins," helping to unify enforcement standards and improve the certainty of tax enforcement.

## Broader Significance

The benchmarks support China's wider "unified national market" initiative, which seeks to dismantle regional barriers and standardize enforcement across jurisdictions to reduce business uncertainty and compliance risk. The State Council's Regulation on Optimizing the Business Environment requires the state to improve discretion benchmarks and reasonably determine the scope and magnitude of enforcement discretion.

The Shanxi tax service emphasized that the benchmarks "refine and quantify" discretion under existing tax laws, administrative regulations, and departmental rules - they do not create new penalty items or change statutory ranges. A separate set of rules for complex, high-stakes, or disputed penalties requires collective deliberation before a decision is issued.

Local tax authorities have been instructed to implement penalties strictly in accordance with the law, make decisions within the benchmark ranges, and safeguard the lawful rights and interests of taxpayers and fee payers, [China Economic Net reported](http://www.ce.cn/xwzx/gnsz/gdxw/202610/t20261006_3248383.shtml).

## What's Next

With the benchmarks set to take effect on November 1, the STA said it will pursue training, interpretation, and public guidance, and conduct timely dynamic updates as laws and regulations evolve. The agency framed the effort as part of a broader push to optimize the tax business environment and improve enforcement effectiveness.

Key questions remain, including how uniformly the new standards will be applied across regions in practice and whether the accompanying spreadsheet annexes detailing all 66 items will translate into the consistent, predictable enforcement the agency has promised.
