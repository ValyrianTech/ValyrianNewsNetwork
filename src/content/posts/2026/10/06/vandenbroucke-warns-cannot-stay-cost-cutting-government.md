# Vandenbroucke Warns He Cannot Stay in Cost-Cut Government

Belgian Deputy Prime Minister Frank Vandenbroucke (Vooruit) has issued a stark warning to his own coalition partners, declaring he cannot remain in a government that, in his words, "stomps on everyone who is vulnerable." The remarks, delivered on Tuesday during an opening lecture at Ghent University, land days before an unofficial budget deadline and lay bare the tensions running through the federal government as it hunts for roughly 10 billion euros in savings.

"I cannot work and live in a government in which the message is: we are going to stomp on everyone who is vulnerable," Vandenbroucke said during the traditional *openingscollege* of the political science programme, according to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/liveblog/-frank-vandenbroucke-vooruit-waarschuwt-kan-niet-leven-in-re~1790599460224/). "I did not enter politics for that."

The lecture, postponed a week on doctor's orders, was expected to be dominated by Vandenbroucke's recent illness. Instead, the health minister turned it into a pointed policy intervention.

## A Two-Front Argument on Welfare Abuse

Vandenbroucke paired his defence of the vulnerable with an unusually symmetric critique of welfare abuse. "All abuse or profiteering is wrong — at the bottom of society, but also at the top," he said, arguing that a debate on responsibility that targets only the weakest is not a debate on responsibility at all.

His chosen target at the top was the proliferation of management companies (*managementvennootschappen*) — personal corporate structures through which high earners invoice clients rather than drawing a conventional salary, often yielding a much lower effective tax burden. According to [HLN](https://www.hln.be/binnenland/frank-vandenbroucke-tijdens-openingscollege-aan-ugent-elk-misbruik-is-fout-aan-de-onderkant-van-de-samenleving-maar-ook-aan-de-bovenkant~a7205876/), Vooruit's study service estimates that these structures cost the treasury between 4.2 and 4.7 billion euros a year, calculated using four different methods.

Vandenbroucke conceded the figure is not official, and that the structures are perfectly legal and often legitimate. "If you can abuse a benefit for another purpose, you can also abuse a company for another purpose — namely to stash a salary in it and pay very little tax on it," he said. "You have to point the finger at the politicians who do not want to change that."

## The Budget Endgame

The intervention comes at a delicate moment. The so-called Arizona coalition — N-VA, MR, Les Engagés, Vooruit and CD&V — has been locked in negotiations over Prime Minister Bart De Wever's budget plan, which seeks about 10 billion euros in savings to rein in a deficit that puts Belgium among the worst fiscal performers in the eurozone. Some economists cited by local outlets argue the real gap is closer to 20 billion euros.

Talks broke up on Sunday evening without agreement, with positions described as too far apart. De Wever is expected in parliament on 13 October for his policy statement and to submit the budget to the Chamber — two days before the plans go to the EU. The inner cabinet reconvenes on Wednesday.

Two sticking points dominate. The first is a planned VAT reform raising the top rate from 21 to 22 percent and merging the 6 and 12 percent reduced rates, a proposal fiercely resisted by MR. As [Knack](https://www.knack.be/nieuws/belgie/politiek/asiel-beperken-en-remgeld-verhogen-20-voorstellen-om-10-miljard-euro-te-vinden-voor-de-begroting/) reported, health-care savings and asylum restrictions are among roughly 20 measures on the table. The second is health-care austerity — reducing the growth norm, raising patient co-payments and narrowing eligibility for the increased allowance — which the left-leaning coalition partners resist.

That second front is where Vandenbroucke is personally exposed. As [La Libre](https://www.lalibre.be/dernieres-depeches/2026/03/24/vandenbroucke-refuse-toute-nouvelle-economie-dans-les-soins-de-sante-IH7DN3BNPZA77LE7FTBKZP2QIU/) reported earlier this year, he has consistently refused new savings in health care. In December, MR chair Georges-Louis Bouchez attacked him directly, arguing that asking a 42-billion-euro health budget to absorb a 300-million-euro saving was, per [Redactie24](https://www.redactie24.be/politiek/bouchez-haalt-stevig-uit-naar-frank-vandenbroucke-186834), "small change." Bouchez has also warned the government stands or falls by the coalition's internal 80/20 split between savings and new revenues.

## Vooruit as the Internal Conscience

Vandenbroucke's lecture is best read as the opening bid of the left within an essentially centre-right government. Vooruit is the only left-wing party in the coalition, and its leader has described it as a small left party in a much more right-wing whole. By reframing the debate — from benefit recipients to wealthy tax-avoiders — he shifts the moral burden of the savings drive onto the top of society, just as his party puts concrete proposals on the table.

The timing reinforces the political intent. It follows Vooruit's earlier act of resistance, when it withdrew its agreement to the Flemish budget after Budget Minister Ben Weyts admitted a calculation error. The message to coalition partners is that the party's cooperation is conditional.

It is also a personal reset. Vandenbroucke had been absent from negotiations the previous week, and rumours about the severity of his illness circulated widely. On Tuesday he was emphatic: "I had some health issues, but I have fully recovered," he said, describing himself as "alive and kicking." He nonetheless acknowledged "major substantive concerns about the budget."

## A Well-Funded State as a Foundation

Beneath the tactical manoeuvring, Vandenbroucke offered a broader argument about the welfare state itself. "A well-organised welfare state is not a cost item," he said, "but the foundation of many of our freedoms, because it takes care of us." That framing directly challenges the cost-cutting logic now dominant in the coalition — and sets up the fight to come.

## What to Watch

The coming week is decisive. The inner cabinet reconvenes on Wednesday, and De Wever must present a budget by 13 October if Belgium is to meet its European obligations. Whether Vandenbroucke's warning translates into concessions on health care — or into a genuine confrontation — will determine the shape of the final package.

What is already clear is that the moral language of the budget debate has changed. A sitting deputy prime minister has publicly set a limit on what he will accept, and he has chosen the language of social protection to do it. The question now is whether his coalition partners will adjust their plans — or call his bluff.
