# Dutchman (21) Charged Over Berchem Fireworks Bombing

Belgian authorities have confirmed that a 21-year-old Dutch national is being held over an attack with heavy fireworks on a home in the Berchem district of Antwerp, an incident first reported in May. The Antwerp council chamber (raadkamer) decided on Monday to keep the suspect in custody, according to an official [press release from the Antwerp prosecutor's office](https://www.om-mp.be/nl/artikel/persbericht-berchem-nederlander-aangehouden-voor-aanslag).

## The Attack

On the night of 15 May 2026, an explosion occurred at the front door of a residence on the Oogststraat in Berchem. The device used was Cobra fireworks, a powerful and illegal type of pyrotechnic. The blast caused fire and explosion damage to the house and to a car belonging to the residents. No one was injured.

As [VRT NWS reported](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/10/06/nederlander-aangehouden-aanslag-berchem/), the suspect is believed to have placed or thrown the device at the property before fleeing the scene.

## The Investigation

Investigators established that shortly after the blast, a black BMW with Dutch license plates left Antwerp and drove toward the Netherlands via the E19 motorway. That same night, Dutch police stopped the vehicle, which contained three occupants, two of them minors.

The adult occupant, aged 21, was extradited from the Netherlands last week and placed under arrest by an investigating judge. On Monday, the Antwerp council chamber confirmed his continued detention. The charges relate to causing an explosion, vandalism, and participation in a criminal organization, according to the prosecutor's office.

## Context: A Wider Pattern

The Oogststraat case fits a well-documented pattern of explosive attacks in and around Antwerp, many of them tied to the drug trade. When the attack was first reported, [VRT NWS noted](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/05/16/explosief-naar-woning-gegooid-in-berchem/) that such attacks in Antwerp are often linked to the drug milieu, though at that time it was not yet clear whether that was the case here.

The weapon of choice, Cobra fireworks, is central to that pattern. As [background on the device](https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cobra_(vuurwerk)) explains, Cobra fireworks are classified as professional-grade category F4 pyrotechnics, containing flash powder that is more powerful than ordinary gunpowder. In practice, they are widely trafficked illegally and used as weapons or intimidation tools in conflicts within the organized crime underworld. Because they are relatively cheap and easily obtained, they have become a common means of carrying out bombings against homes, businesses, and vehicles.

The cross-border dimension of the case is also characteristic. The suspect's Dutch nationality and the vehicle's rapid flight toward the Netherlands reflect the close links between drug-related violence in Belgium and the Netherlands, particularly around the port of Antwerp.

## A Busy Week for Antwerp Investigators

The arrest comes amid heightened activity by the Antwerp prosecutor's office on drug-related explosive attacks. In a separate case, prosecutors announced on 2 October that a second suspect had been detained over an attack at the Charlottalei in Antwerp, where an explosive device detonated outside a building. In that case, a 20-year-old man from Borgerhout admitted involvement, and an 18-year-old from Deurne was identified as an intermediary; the [prosecutor's office stated](https://www.om-mp.be/nl/artikel/persbericht-antwerpen-tweede-verdachte-aangehouden-voor-aanslag-charlottalei) that the motive appeared to fit the drug milieu.

Together, the two cases illustrate the persistence of the phenomenon despite sustained police attention.

## What's Next

The 21-year-old suspect remains in custody pending further investigation. It is not yet clear when the case will come to trial, nor whether the two minors who were in the BMW will face any measures. The link between the Oogststraat attack and the drug milieu has not been officially confirmed, but the method, the flight toward the Netherlands, and the broader context in Antwerp all point in that direction.

What is certain is that the case has moved from an unsolved attack in May to a concrete prosecution in October, a reminder of how long investigations into such bombings can take, and how often the trail leads across the Belgian-Dutch border.
