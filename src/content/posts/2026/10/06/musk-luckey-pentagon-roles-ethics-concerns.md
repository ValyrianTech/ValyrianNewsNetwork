# Musk, Luckey Pentagon Roles Raise Ethics Concerns

Elon Musk and Palmer Luckey are set to co-lead a new Pentagon study on the future of warfare, an arrangement that is drawing scrutiny because both men run companies that hold billions of dollars in U.S. defense contracts that the study's recommendations could shape.

Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth announced "Project Meridian: The Future of Warfare" in a "State of the Force" address at Marine Corps Base Quantico, Virginia, on Sept. 30, according to [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/10/06/nx-s1-5991899/elon-musk-palmer-luckey-pentagon-drones-ai). The study of automated and AI-powered weaponry is led by former House Speaker Newt Gingrich alongside Musk, the SpaceX chief executive, and Luckey, the co-founder of defense technology firm Anduril Industries. It is expected to run 120 days and to produce a public report with a classified annex.

## A Study Tied to the Contractors It Could Reward

The ethics question at the center of the arrangement is structural. SpaceX has secured more than $8 billion in U.S. defense contracts in 2026 alone, per government contract announcements, and its products include Starshield, a military-grade satellite network, and the Grok AI chatbot, which the Pentagon uses. Anduril builds military drones for land, sea and air, and received its initial funding from tech billionaire and conservative activist Peter Thiel.

"They all want to sell stuff," said Richard Painter, a professor of corporate law at the University of Minnesota and a former chief White House ethics lawyer in the George W. Bush administration. "So if we go into it understanding that [Musk], like all the other contractors, he wants to make a lot of money selling AI to the Defense Department. Whatever he proposes, but it's not like he's doing it as a neutral public service."

A former senior Defense Department official, who requested anonymity out of fear of reprisal from the Trump administration, put it more bluntly. "It's a massive conflict of interest," the official said, noting that a study recommending more drones or space-based assets could imply fewer traditional systems like tanks — a zero-sum shift in which the co-leaders' companies stand to gain.

## The Rules Depend on a Classification the Pentagon Won't Disclose

How much the conflict-of-interest rules constrain Musk and Luckey turns on a question the Department of Defense has declined to answer: whether the two men are serving as executive branch employees or as contractors.

Executive branch employees are bound by federal criminal law barring them from participating in government decisions that affect their own finances or those of their family and business. Contractors are not subject to that law. Musk previously served as a special government employee while leading the Department of Government Efficiency, but the Pentagon has not said which category applies to the Project Meridian roles, and it did not respond to questions about potential conflicts. SpaceX and Anduril likewise did not respond to requests for comment.

A Department of Defense spokesperson said the work is managed through the MITRE Corporation, a nonprofit that oversees federally funded research, which is "responsible for the analysis and recommendations, and will evaluate all inputs with technical rigor while applying appropriate conflict-of-interest and information safeguards." Hegseth directed the Pentagon's chief technology officer, Emil Michael, to commission the project through MITRE. Few details were released in Hegseth's one-page memo.

## Beyond Meridian: Six Initiatives and a Broader Pattern

Project Meridian was one of six initiatives Hegseth unveiled at Quantico, [Breaking Defense](https://breakingdefense.com/2026/09/hegseth-announces-plans-for-new-4-star-drone-command-further-general-officer-cuts/) reported. They also included a new Autonomous Warfare Command, or AUTOWARCOM, a four-star command dedicated to scaling drones, robotics and AI across the joint force; a 20% cut to generals and admirals by Jan. 1, 2027; plans for a new military base, the first in roughly 40 years; a push for installations to generate their own power independent of the civilian grid; a new corps of cadets; and an office of religious affairs reporting directly to the secretary.

The announcement reflects a broader emphasis on accelerating autonomous warfare, driven by lessons from drone use in Ukraine and attacks on U.S. forces during the war with Iran. Hegseth framed the study as an exercise in looking outward. "As we scan that horizon of an unknown future, we should have the humility to recognize that the best forecasters of future conflict do not solely reside inside the Pentagon," he said, adding that the effort is "futuristic on purpose."

Critics say the arrangement fits a pattern in the second Trump administration of appointing industry figures to influential roles. Painter contrasted it with the Bush administration, where officials were required to divest defense stocks. "I never saw anywhere near as many financial conflicts of interest in any other administration," he said. "Even the first Trump administration wasn't as bad as this."

## A Watchdog's Caution — and a Dissent

Not everyone sees an emergency. A senior Government Accountability Office official, speaking anonymously because they are not authorized to discuss unpublished matters, said safeguards are supposed to keep panel members away from procurement activities. "That doesn't mean that we shouldn't be paying attention to what the project comes out with," the official said. But the official also questioned the need for the effort at all: "I don't know why we would need a new task force to talk about something since we've had many folks thinking about this issue for a while. What would the point of this be?"

A second former Pentagon official, also speaking anonymously out of fear of retribution, was more sanguine, noting that such panels routinely make recommendations that Congress may accept or reject, and that there is a difference between recommending a requirement and writing it and then bidding on it. [Stars and Stripes](https://www.stripes.com/theaters/us/2026-09-30/hegseth-state-of-the-force-quantico-23016908.html) reported that Hegseth gathered roughly 600 rank-and-file troops for the address.

## What to Watch

Project Meridian's findings are due around late January 2027 — a public report plus a classified annex — giving watchdog groups and lawmakers a defined moment to weigh whether the study's recommendations align with the national interest or with the balance sheets of its authors. The Department of Defense's refusal to say whether Musk and Luckey are employees or contractors leaves a key accountability question open, and it is one that Congress, which must ultimately fund and authorize any procurement shift, is positioned to press. President Trump was expected to speak at an Anduril facility in Baltimore in the days after the announcement, underscoring how closely the administration's defense-technology agenda and the co-leaders' businesses now run in parallel.
