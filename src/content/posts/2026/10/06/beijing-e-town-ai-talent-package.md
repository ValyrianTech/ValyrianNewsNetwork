# Beijing E-Town Rolls Out AI Talent Package

Beijing's flagship economic development zone has unveiled a package of measures aimed at attracting, cultivating and retaining so-called AI-native talent, as the region pushes to cement its status as a national hub for artificial intelligence. The Beijing Economic-Technological Development Area (Beijing E-Town, also known as Beijing Yizhuang) released the Several Measures on Supporting the Development of AI-Native Talent at the 2026 Beijing Yizhuang Talent Development Conference on August 18, according to [People's Daily](http://edu.people.com.cn/n1/2026/1004/c1006-40809182.html).

Dubbed the "AI Talent 8 Measures," the policy spans three dimensions—entrepreneurship, youth employment and ecosystem cultivation—and builds a full-chain system covering everyone from student interns to world-class scientists.

## Who the Policy Targets

According to [Guangming Daily](https://epaper.gmw.cn/gmrb/html/content/202610/03/content_26563.html), the measures focus squarely on the AI field, singling out world-class scientists, technical leads of leading global large-model teams, technical backbones with both technical and market experience, serial entrepreneurs and top young talent. Crucially, officials said the policy leaves room for cross-disciplinary innovators alongside AI-native talent.

In a notable shift from the previous "one-time assessment, lifelong benefit" approach, the package adopts a dynamic model described as "support one as it matures, assess one, support one" (成熟一个、评定一个、支持一个). This embeds support into a company's journey from technology validation through industrialization.

The financial incentives are substantial. Top global talent receive generous startup capital and development rewards, while industry elites may receive up to 5 million yuan (roughly $700,000). Recipients are incorporated into the Yicheng Talent System, gaining access to compute clusters, customized R&D space and industry scenario matching, according to the official policy release reported by [Beijing News](https://m.bjnews.com.cn/detail/1787061261129799.html).

## Betting on 'Super Individuals'

The policy also makes a novel bet on a category it calls Super Individuals, or OPCs—lightweight, founder-driven operations that are increasingly common at the frontier of AI. A newly created review committee that includes OPC representatives will assess applicants on real creativity, closed-loop delivery capability and development potential, awarding individuals up to 500,000 yuan. The zone is also exploring transitional social-insurance continuation and a startup-insurance backstop for people moving from employment into entrepreneurship.

The youth component is equally distinctive. Beijing E-Town launched what it describes as the city's first AI-specific internship support measure, covering AI competition winners, students from top-university AI specialty classes, talent with deep large-model R&D internship experience, and even liberal-arts-background talent skilled in prompt engineering. Participants receive internship subsidies and employment rewards.

The zone is also promoting Frontier Deployment Engineers (FDE)—a role blending model understanding, engineering implementation and scenario adaptation—and supports school-enterprise-research collaboration to cultivate it.

"The AI-native talent policy must address more than bringing talent in—it must answer which talent to support, how to accompany their growth, and how to connect different talents," an unnamed Beijing E-Town official said in the policy release.

## Backing It Up With Infrastructure

The measures sit within a broader, rapidly expanding AI policy suite. Beijing E-Town is now home to more than 800 core enterprises across the AI industry chain, with industry scale exceeding 100 billion yuan, according to [Beijing Time](https://item.btime.com/f4u6uct9oce9lgap5g1vmpfqpt4). That is up sharply from roughly 600 core enterprises and an 80 billion yuan scale at the end of 2025. The zone has built what officials describe as Beijing's largest AI public computing platform, with 5,000P of high-performance computing in use.

The talent package arrived alongside a series of related announcements at the same August conference, including an upgraded "Talent 10 Measures 2.0+" policy backed by a 1 billion yuan annual talent development fund, an industry-education integration plan covering 100 schools and 100 enterprises, and a million-square-meter International Talent Community.

## Analysis

The policy reflects a maturing understanding among Chinese policymakers that winning the AI race is not only about compute and capital but about people—and that the profiles of those people are changing. By formally recognizing Super Individuals and prompt engineers from liberal-arts backgrounds, Beijing E-Town is acknowledging a workforce that is younger, more cross-disciplinary and organized in lighter-weight forms than traditional industrial hiring assumes.

The framing also carries strategic weight. Shi Wei, deputy secretary of the zone's Party Working Committee, noted that the area now hosts nearly 400,000 workers and cited a talent contribution rate of 69.3%, phrasing designed to cast talent as the foundation of industrial upgrading.

At the same time, the emphasis on retention and "accompanying" talent through company formation suggests officials have learned that recruitment alone is insufficient. The transitional social insurance and startup insurance backstops are small but telling interventions aimed at de-risking the leap from employee to founder.

## What to Watch

Much will depend on implementation. The policy carries a "trial" (试行) designation, and the dynamic assessment model will need transparent criteria to avoid becoming a discretionary grant system. The real test of the AI Talent 8 Measures will be whether the zone can convert generous subsidies into durable companies—and whether other regions adopt similar playbooks as China enters its 15th Five-Year Plan window.

The zone's stated ambition is to build a "full-domain AI city" with global influence. With 800-plus enterprises already clustered and public computing in place, Beijing E-Town appears to be betting that the next competitive edge in AI will be the ability to attract and keep the people who build it.
