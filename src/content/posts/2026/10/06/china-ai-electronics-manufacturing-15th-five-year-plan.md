# China Ties AI to $4.2T Electronics Manufacturing Push

China has unveiled a sweeping industrial blueprint that places artificial intelligence at the center of its electronic information manufacturing sector, targeting an ambitious RMB 30 trillion (about US$4.2 trillion) in revenue from large-scale enterprises by 2030. The Ministry of Industry and Information Technology (MIIT) and the National Development and Reform Commission (NDRC) jointly issued the *Development Plan for the Electronic Information Manufacturing Industry during the 15th Five-Year Plan Period* on 10 September 2026, with public coverage following on 15 September, according to [21st Century Business Herald](https://www.21jingji.com/article/20260915/herald/9c3957cf6a74eeb4a22ac1be1647f438.html).

The Plan marks the first major sectoral strategy built explicitly around AI as the driving force reshaping chips, terminals, computing infrastructure, and industrial applications — a shift that analysts say could redefine how China competes in global technology supply chains.

## A Sector Already at Global Scale

China's electronic information manufacturing sector is not starting from a small base. According to the official plan text reproduced by the [Hubei Provincial Department of Economy and Information Technology](https://jxt.hubei.gov.cn/bmdt/rdjj/202609/t20260915_6013920.shtml), the industry's revenue has ranked first among China's 41 major industrial categories for 13 consecutive years. China produces more phones and computers than any other country, accounts for over 75% of global display panel shipment area, and now counts integrated circuits as its single largest export commodity by value.

The numbers reinforce the scale of the ambition. In 2025, large-scale electronic information manufacturing enterprises generated RMB 17.4 trillion in revenue, up 7.4% year-on-year. In the first half of 2026 alone, revenue reached RMB 9.41 trillion, up 18.5% year-on-year — a pace that puts the 2030 target within reach.

## Four Pillars, Seventeen Tasks

The Plan is organized around four themes — "building foundations, improving quality, cultivating new growth, and governance" — and deploys 17 key tasks across nine special columns. The first and most prominent priority is advancing full-chain breakthroughs in integrated circuits, including high-performance processors, high-density memory, and high-reliability analog and mixed-signal chips. It also calls for refining mature manufacturing processes, raising advanced-process capability, promoting wide-bandgap and ultra-wide-bandgap semiconductors such as gallium oxide and diamond, and developing domestic EDA tools and IP cores.

As Ao Li (敖立), Vice President of the China Academy of Information and Communications Technology (CAICT), told [Economic Daily](http://paper.ce.cn/pc/content/202610/06/content_339874.html): "Converting scale advantage into systemic advantage, and upgrading 'leadership in quantity' into 'victory in quality,' is the major proposition for the development of electronic information manufacturing during the 15th Five-Year Plan period."

The Plan also makes intelligent terminals a central vehicle for AI adoption. It calls for iterative upgrades to AI phones, AI computers, smart glasses, VR/AR/MR devices, wearables, in-car terminals, industrial terminals, and commercial terminals — positioning these products as the entry point through which AI reaches hundreds of millions of users.

## AI as the Decisive Variable

At the heart of the strategy is a bet that AI and electronics manufacturing will become inseparable. Ao Li described the deep integration of AI and electronic information manufacturing as "the decisive variable reshaping the industrial form" over the next decade. Liang Linjun (梁林俊), Chief Engineer at CAICT's Institute of Integration of Informatization and Industrialization, framed the relationship as symbiotic: "Electronic information manufacturing provides an irreplaceable material foundation for AI development. Each leap in AI capability requires synchronized support from hardware capabilities such as compute, storage, interconnect, and sensing."

The market signals are already visible. As of end-June 2026, China's intelligent compute scale was up 177% year-on-year. In 2025, shipments of AI phones, AI computers, and other intelligent terminals exceeded 100 million units, and 2026 sales of AI phones and computers are expected to surpass non-AI products — a shift from a replacement cycle into a new "intelligent upgrade" cycle.

Industry voices have echoed the Plan's framing. A Bu Likerim (阿不力克木), Vice President of Lenovo Group and Executive Chairman of Lenovo China's Technology Management Committee, said the Plan accurately captured two keys to industrial upgrading: terminals as the gateway for intelligent technology, and industry digital-intelligent integration as the main battlefield for AI to penetrate the industrial end. "For AI to truly grow into new quality productive forces, it is not enough to have only large models and compute — it must land in hundreds of millions of intelligent terminals and the real scenarios of thousands of industries," he said, per Economic Daily.

## Standards, Governance, and Guardrails

The Plan pairs its growth targets with an emphasis on governance. It calls for coordinating capacity planning and early-warning across sub-sectors and for "comprehensively rectifying 'involution-style' competition" — a reference to the destructive price wars and redundant low-level construction that have plagued sectors such as solar and EV batteries.

Standard-setting is another pillar. In May 2026, MIIT, the Ministry of Commerce, and the State Administration for Market Regulation jointly launched the *AI Terminal Intelligence Grading* national standard series, which defines intelligence concepts, tier classifications, and testing methods. Li Hongwei (李宏伟), Chief Engineer at the China Center for Information Industry Development, said the standards give AI terminals a unified "health check standard," covering scenarios like office, learning, and design — regulating the industry while helping consumers choose with confidence.

## Self-Reliance and Opening Up

The Plan reflects China's dual imperative: reducing exposure to technology containment while maintaining international engagement. It calls for building up anti-sanction and anti-long-arm-jurisdiction capabilities in key electronic information fields, even as it pledges to participate actively in international standardization and improve cross-border cooperation.

[Phoenix News](https://news.ifeng.com/c/8wRjQxlP8j1), citing CCTV's *Xinwen Lianbo*, reported that the Plan positions China to "play a pivotal role in the global industrial landscape" by 2030, with industry R&D investment intensity reaching 3.5% and the emergence of world-class enterprises in integrated circuits, advanced computing, consumer electronics, basic electronics, and energy electronics.

## What to Watch

The Plan's success will hinge on execution — particularly on whether China can translate investment into genuine leadership in advanced semiconductors. Its emphasis on countering "involution" also signals that Beijing is wary of the overcapacity and margin erosion that have undercut returns in other strategic industries.

At an August 2026 press conference on the 15th Five-Year Plan, MIIT Information and Communication Development Department Director Liu Yulin (刘郁林) struck a tone of measured resolve, noting the period is "critical for the quality upgrade" of the sector. "We will maintain strategic resolve and calmly respond to the uncertainty of rapidly changing external environments with the certainty of high-quality domestic industrial development," he said, according to [IT之家](https://www.ithome.com/0/994/822.htm).

How that certainty is built — and whether AI can indeed become the engine the Plan envisions — will define China's technology trajectory for the rest of the decade.
