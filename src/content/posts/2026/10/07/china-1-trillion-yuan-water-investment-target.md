# China Pushes 1 Trillion Yuan Water Investment Target

China's Ministry of Water Resources (MWR) has reaffirmed its commitment to complete 1 trillion yuan (approximately US$140 billion) in water conservancy construction investment in 2026 and to break ground on 40 major new water projects, reinforcing infrastructure spending as a pillar of the country's economic strategy. The announcement, reported by [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/fortune/20261007/c57ff3e7faea4faaa4adfec949649c66/c.html) on Oct. 7, restates a target first set out publicly by MWR Planning Department Director Li Ming at a State Council Information Office (SCIO) press conference on Sept. 30.

## Robust Progress Through Three Quarters

According to Xinhua, the first nine months of 2026 saw 41,400 water projects implemented nationwide, 28 major water projects newly started — already surpassing the total for all of 2025 — and 795 billion yuan (about US$111 billion) in construction investment completed. Water project construction also absorbed roughly 2.01 million jobs, underscoring the sector's role as a labor-intensive engine of employment.

"There are still three months until the end of the year; the Ministry of Water Resources will further intensify its work to ensure the achievement of the full-year goal of starting 40 major new water projects and completing 1 trillion yuan in water conservancy construction investment," Li Ming said, as reported by the [Shanghai Securities News](https://field.10jqka.com.cn/20260930/c680418765.shtml).

## A Maintenance of High Investment, Not a New Peak

The 1 trillion yuan figure is significant but not unprecedented. China has completed more than 1 trillion yuan in annual water investment for four consecutive years, since 2022. During the 14th Five-Year Plan period (2021–2025), total water construction investment reached 5.68 trillion yuan, of which 2.05 trillion yuan — 36.1 percent — came from local government special bonds, bank loans, and social capital.

In 2025 alone, the sector drew 1.28 trillion yuan in investment, absorbed 3.15 million jobs, and started 27 major projects, including flood-zone adjustments on the Huai River's Fushan section, the Sichuan Yin Da Ji Min diversion, key embankment reinforcement at Hunan's Dongting Lake, and the Guangdong Huangmaoxia water hub.

## Li Ming's Three Keywords: Projects, Funding, Progress

Asked how the ministry intends to sustain such a high volume of investment, Li Ming pointed to three priorities: projects, funding, and progress.

On project preparation, the MWR will guide localities to deepen feasibility studies, optimize engineering designs, complete land, forestry, and environmental approvals, and push projects toward earlier approval. Many reserve projects already exist in national and local plans, he noted; the immediate task is to make them "shovel-ready."

On funding, the ministry aims to leverage government investment at all levels while diversifying sources. Measures include deepening reforms on water pricing and water-rights trading, cultivating more water REITs, piloting a new mechanism for trading cultivated-land indicators tied to water network construction, and attracting more local government special bonds, bank loans, social capital, and new policy-based financial instruments. During the 14th Five-Year Plan, such non-fiscal channels averaged 327.9 billion yuan per year — 2.6 times the average of the previous plan period — while social capital alone reached 548.5 billion yuan, up 86.4 percent.

On progress, Li Ming said the ministry will tighten the responsibility of participating units, strengthen scheduling and analysis, and track project progress regularly. "On the premise of ensuring project quality and safety, accelerate construction progress, complete as much physical work volume as possible, and better play the role of stabilizing investment, promoting employment, and benefiting livelihoods," he said.

## A Broader Strategy for Water Security

The Sept. 30 press conference also featured Water Resources Minister Li Guoying, who laid out the ministry's water security strategy for the 15th Five-Year Plan (2026–2030). Citing intensifying climate change and the 2026 breach of the Liulan Reservoir in Hengzhou, Nanning, Guangxi — which caused major casualties — Li Guoying said the ministry would pursue "the completeness, foresight, and certainty of the defense system to respond to the uncertainty of flood events."

He described a pattern of "one increase, three randoms": more extreme rainstorm and flood events, occurring at random locations, times, and intensities. Since May 2026, the equatorial central-eastern Pacific has entered an El Niño state that continues to strengthen, with a super El Niño expected to raise the likelihood of extreme rainfall, high temperatures, and drought.

By the end of the 15th Five-Year Plan, as reported by [Hong Kong Wen Wei Po](https://www.wenweipo.com/a/202610/01/AP6abd71e3e4b01d54a2857056.html), the ministry aims to add 15 billion cubic meters of reservoir capacity, raise the compliance rate of important embankments to 91 percent, extend national water network coverage to 82 percent of the country's territory, add 40 million mu of irrigated farmland, raise rural tap water coverage to 98 percent from 96 percent in 2025, and increase soil erosion control by 310,000 square kilometers. A National Water Network Dispatch Center, Big Data Center, and basin sub-centers are also planned.

## Analysis: Infrastructure as Counter-Cyclical Lever

The water investment target reflects a broader Chinese strategy of using infrastructure spending to sustain domestic demand and manage the property-sector downturn. Water projects are particularly attractive as a counter-cyclical tool: they are labor-intensive, geographically dispersed, and generate durable public goods — flood control, irrigation, and drinking water — that carry clear social returns.

The emphasis on financing innovation is equally telling. With local government finances under strain, the ministry is signaling that it cannot rely on fiscal transfers alone; it must crowd in private capital through REITs, water-rights trading, and land-indicator mechanisms. Whether these channels can scale fast enough to fill the gap will be a key test of the model's sustainability.

## What's Next

With about three months left in 2026, the ministry must complete roughly 205 billion yuan in investment to hit its 1 trillion yuan target — a pace consistent with the first three quarters. The larger question is the 15th Five-Year Plan itself: the Nov. 2025 reservoir breach at Liulan has given new urgency to flood-control spending, and the final allocation of projects and funding will shape China's water security — and its construction sector — for years to come.
