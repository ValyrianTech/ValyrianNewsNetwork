# Nevada's Safe GOP Seat Turns Competitive Over Valor Claims

RENO, Nev. — Nevada's 2nd Congressional District was drawn in 1981 to be reliably Republican, and a Democrat has never won it. But with less than a month until Election Day on Nov. 3, the seat has become unexpectedly competitive, weighed down by allegations that the GOP nominee exaggerated his military service and moved to the district purely to run for office.

Republican David Flippo, a retired Air Force lieutenant colonel endorsed by President Trump, faces Democrat Teresa Benitez-Thompson, a former Nevada Assembly majority leader. The race has drawn national attention, a Democratic Congressional Campaign Committee "Red to Blue" designation and six-figure union ad spending — while the retiring Republican incumbent refuses to endorse his own party's nominee.

## A Bronze Star Claim Under Scrutiny

The controversy centers on how Flippo has described his military record. The Washington Post first reported in late September that Flippo's campaign repeatedly described him as a recipient of the Bronze Star "for valor," a distinction reserved for heroism under enemy fire.

As [the Las Vegas Sun reported](https://lasvegassun.com/news/2026/sep/24/nevada-congressional-candidate-flippo-falsely-clai/), Flippo received a standard Bronze Star in 2004 during a 24-year Air Force career — an award for meritorious service in a combat zone, not the "V" device that denotes valor in battle. The campaign repeated the valor claim in a 2023 announcement video, at least two 2026 fundraising solicitations and more than a dozen press releases over four months.

Flippo has apologized, blaming a young staffer for mixing up the Bronze Star with an Outstanding Unit Award that his whole unit received. "Look, part of leadership is taking ownership," he told [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/10/07/nx-s1-5984671/nevada-midterm-election-valor-carpetbagger-reno). "It was just an honest oversight."

## The Hat That Drew Veterans' Attention

The dispute intensified over a campaign hat. The conservative outlet [Nevada News & Views](https://nevadanewsandviews.com/two-valor-pins-one-valor-award-the-hat-david-flippo-stopped-wearing-after-his-bronze-star-claims-unraveled/) first noted that photographs on Flippo's campaign website and social media showed him wearing a veterans cap bearing a Bronze Star pin next to two "V" pins — symbols veterans immediately recognize as valor awards. The campaign removed the photo shortly after NPR asked about it.

Flippo deflected when pressed on the pins. "You know if you've lived with grandkids," he told NPR, adding that his grandchildren "ended up doing that."

Military honors experts say the distinction is anything but trivial. "It amounts to a lie," Doug Sterner, a two-time Bronze Star recipient, told the Post. "It's a falsification, a misrepresentation of the award that he received. He did not get an award for heroism." Jason Dempsey, a retired Army officer at the Center for a New American Security, said such claims "denigrat[e] everybody's service."

Bill Conrad of Combat Vets for Congress defended Flippo, calling the reporting a "hit piece" that "slandered a decorated military veteran whose staff mistook which of his medals had a valor device."

## 'Carpetbagger' Accusations

Flippo's second vulnerability is his path to the district. He was originally running for Congress in Nevada's 4th District, in the Las Vegas area, until Rep. Mark Amodei announced his retirement in February. Flippo then switched to the open Northern Nevada seat — roughly seven hours north by car.

[As The Nevada Independent reported](https://thenevadaindependent.com/article/does-teresa-benitez-thompson-stand-a-chance-in-deep-red-northern-nevada), the move grated on voters. "I was absolutely not going to vote for someone who moved here just a month or two before the primary," said Susan Fisher, a registered Republican and retired lobbyist. "I view him as a carpetbagger."

Benitez-Thompson has leaned into her deep local roots. A former social worker, one-time Miss Nevada and chief of staff to the state attorney general, she has lived in Northern Nevada for four decades and is raising four children in Reno. "This is home," she told NPR.

## A Financial Disclosure Fight

Flippo's campaign has also been dogged by his failure to file a personal financial disclosure required by federal law — a form that shows voters the source of a candidate's wealth and potential conflicts of interest. Flippo has loaned his campaign more than $1.6 million this cycle.

Fisher filed a four-page complaint with the Department of Justice Public Integrity Section in September asking for an investigation, [KOLO TV reported](https://www.kolotv.com/2026/09/10/reno-voter-requests-investigation-congressional-candidate-david-flippo/). The campaign said Flippo requested an extension while moving his holdings into a blind trust.

The disclosure finally surfaced in late September. [The Nevada Independent reported](https://thenevadaindependent.com/article/we-finally-have-david-flippos-financial-disclosure-heres-what-we-found) that Flippo listed a net worth between $3.2 million and $14.5 million, including large holdings in Arizona land ventures. But the filing came roughly five weeks before Election Day, and there is no record that he filed one during his 2024 congressional run.

## An Incumbent's Rebuke

Perhaps the most striking development is Amodei's refusal to back his would-be successor. "This seat is some kind of competitive like it has never been in the history since it was created," Amodei told NPR. "I attribute it to the candidate and the way he's run his campaign."

[In an interview with The Nevada Independent](https://thenevadaindependent.com/article/amodei-still-refuses-to-back-flippo-as-rift-between-the-two-republicans-grows), Amodei called Flippo "not my cup of tea," "arrogant" and "incredibly, incredibly disrespectful to the people whose votes you want." He said there was "no way" he would endorse him.

Gov. Joe Lombardo, by contrast, endorsed Flippo and has not withdrawn it. The National Republican Congressional Committee dismissed Democratic hopes, telling NPR that the party is "delusional" to think the race is competitive.

## The Numbers and What's Next

The math still favors Republicans. Registered Republicans outnumber Democrats by nearly 71,000, and Trump won the district by 14 points in 2024. The Cook Political Report rates it R+7.

But the warning signs are real. Republican super PACs have poured more than $1 million into TV and digital ads to shore up Flippo. The DCCC added Benitez-Thompson to its "Red to Blue" program in early October, and the national AFL-CIO launched a six-figure ad blitz featuring union veterans opposing Flippo — [likely the first time the union has targeted the deeply red seat](https://thenevadaindependent.com/article/it-disrespects-everybody-why-union-nevada-veterans-are-filming-ads-opposing-flippo).

Polling is sparse and mixed. A Strategies 360 survey gave Flippo a 45–42 lead, while a GBAO poll for a Democratic-aligned group found Benitez-Thompson ahead 38–37, with Independent American Party candidate Lynn Chapman at 14 — a potential outlet for Republicans unhappy with their nominee. Inside Elections shifted the district from "solid" to "likely Republican," its first move away from ruby red since before Amodei took office.

"This isn't a coin flip," Inside Elections Deputy Editor Jacob Rubashkin cautioned. But as statistician Nate Silver noted, "This seat flipping would be a surprise, but there are usually a few surprises in wave elections."

With affordability dominating voter concerns and no recent independent public polling, the central question remains whether the valor and carpetbagger controversies have broken through to the broader electorate. Both campaigns are watching where the national environment lands — and whether outside money floods in during the final weeks.
