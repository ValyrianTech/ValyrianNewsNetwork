# Tropical Storm Isaias Forecast to Become a Hurricane

Tropical Storm Isaias formed early Wednesday in the warm waters off eastern Mexico and is forecast to strengthen into the first hurricane of the Atlantic season before reaching the U.S. Gulf Coast late this week, according to [The Associated Press](https://www.nbcnews.com/weather/storms/tropical-storm-isaias-forms-forecast-become-hurricane-hit-gulf-coast-w-rcna602035). The storm has prompted hurricane and storm surge watches, a Florida state of emergency covering 25 counties and widespread coastal preparations.

As of Wednesday morning, Isaias was located about 285 miles west of Progreso, Mexico, and about 580 miles southwest of the mouth of the Mississippi River, the National Hurricane Center reported. It packed maximum sustained winds of 40 mph (65 kph) and was moving east-northeast at about 8 mph.

## A Storm Set to Rapidly Intensify

The National Hurricane Center expects Isaias to reach hurricane strength by Thursday, with forecasts suggesting it could then intensify into a Category 2 storm. [BBC Weather](https://www.bbc.com/weather/articles/cw146l6pnvx0o) reports the storm could peak near 110 mph over the Gulf before possible weakening to Category 1 just before landfall as it encounters increasing wind shear.

Forecasters say the storm could make landfall late Friday or early Saturday somewhere between eastern Louisiana and the western Florida Panhandle, with guidance highlighting the corridor from Biloxi, Mississippi, to Panama City, Florida. Isaias is expected to grow larger as it approaches the coast, meaning significant impacts could extend well away from the storm's center.

A Hurricane Watch covers the northern Gulf Coast from Bay St. Louis, Mississippi, to Indian Pass, Florida, while a Storm Surge Watch extends from the mouth of the Mississippi River east to Yankeetown, Florida.

## Hazardous Impacts Expected

According to [FOX10 in Mobile](https://www.fox10tv.com/2026/10/07/isaias-strengthening-mobile-pensacola-braces-significant-friday/), the current forecast calls for 5 to 7 feet of storm surge inundation above normally dry ground from Ocean Springs, Mississippi, east to Indian Pass, Florida, including Mobile Bay. Rainfall of 3 to 6 inches is expected from southeast Louisiana into the Florida Panhandle, with isolated totals reaching up to 10 inches and the potential for flash, urban and isolated river flooding.

Beyond wind and water, forecasters warn of tornadoes near and east of the storm's track, dangerous surf and life-threatening rip currents, and the possibility of long-duration power outages. "A hurricane that begins weakening before landfall can still reach the coast as a dangerous hurricane," FOX10's Matt Barrentine warned.

## Florida Mobilizes Ahead of the Storm

Florida Governor Ron DeSantis signed Executive Order 26-202, declaring a state of emergency in 25 counties and allowing the state to stage preparedness resources, according to [Saint George Insurance Brokerage](https://www.sgibinc.com/en/florida-emergency-hurricane-storm/). "This order will allow the Florida Division of Emergency Management to stage critical preparedness resources and ensure Florida is ready to respond," DeSantis said.

The order covers Baker, Bay, Calhoun, Columbia, Dixie, Escambia, Franklin, Gadsden, Gilchrist, Gulf, Hamilton, Holmes, Jackson, Jefferson, Lafayette, Leon, Liberty, Madison, Okaloosa, Santa Rosa, Suwannee, Taylor, Walton, Wakulla and Washington counties. The declaration is not a statewide evacuation order, but it enables the state's Emergency Response Team to stage generators, fuel, flood barriers, bottled water, meals, tarps and sandbags in Tallahassee and the Santa Rosa area. State officials warned that recent rainfall has already saturated soils in parts of Florida, increasing the risk of flash flooding.

## The End of a Record-Quiet Season

Isaias would be the first hurricane of a 2026 Atlantic season that has been record-breakingly quiet, suppressed by a developing, record-strong El Nino in the Pacific. The resulting wind shear over the Caribbean and Atlantic has torn storms apart before they could develop.

"Isaias will bring an end to the lack of hurricanes that has led to a record-breakingly quiet Atlantic season so far with development suppressed by the growing El Nino pattern," BBC Weather presenter Ben Rich wrote.

If Isaias reaches hurricane strength on Thursday, it would end one of the longest waits on record for a season's first Atlantic hurricane. According to [WPDE ABC15](https://wpde.com/weather/abc-15-weather-authority-blog/tropical-storm-isaias-forms-in-the-gulf-could-be-the-first-atlantic-hurricane-of-2026-tropics-rain-wind-historic-october-7-2026), the gap for the record-latest first hurricane continues to widen, and the 2026 season has already become the first satellite-era season to reach October with no hurricanes. The storm managed to form in an area of limited wind shear and unusually warm sea surface temperatures of 30 to 31 degrees Celsius, supplying the fuel for rapid development.

## What to Watch For

The key milestone will be whether Isaias reaches hurricane strength on Thursday, determining whether the season's historically late start for a first hurricane finally ends. The storm's final landfall location and intensity will decide the severity of surge and wind damage along the central Gulf Coast.

Residents from Louisiana to the Florida Panhandle have been urged to complete preparations before conditions deteriorate Friday. As the storm moves inland, it is expected to spread heavy rain, gusty winds and continued tornado risk across the Deep South and into the Carolinas over the weekend. The next name on the 2026 Atlantic list is Josephine.
