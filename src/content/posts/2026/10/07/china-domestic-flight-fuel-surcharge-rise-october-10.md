# China Domestic Flight Fuel Surcharges to Rise Oct 10

Fuel surcharges on domestic routes across mainland China will increase starting October 10, with the standard set at 50 yuan per segment for routes of 800 kilometers or less and 90 yuan per segment for routes over 800 kilometers, according to [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/fortune/20261007/4261439e9c8c4103bbdb67e516fc4142/c.html).

The increase adds 10 yuan to short-haul tickets and 20 yuan to longer routes compared with the current standard, which has been in effect since August 5.

## What Changes for Travelers

Multiple airlines confirmed the adjustment to Xinhua on October 7. The new rates apply to tickets issued from 00:00 on October 10, 2026, meaning passengers who book before that cutoff will pay the lower existing surcharge - 40 yuan for routes of 800 kilometers or less and 70 yuan for longer routes.

[Several carriers](https://goodsfu.10jqka.com.cn/20261006/c680444938.shtml), including the low-cost operator 9 Air, have already published formal notices. Under the rules, infants are exempt from the fuel surcharge, while children, disabled revolutionary servicemen, and people's police disabled in the line of duty pay half the standard - 20 yuan for short routes and 40 yuan for long routes.

One detail matters for flexible travelers: the surcharge is levied according to the original ticket issue date. If a ticket is rescheduled, the surcharge is neither refunded nor supplemented. That rule creates a clear incentive to buy early.

## Why the Surcharge Is Rising

Domestic air passenger fuel surcharges in China operate under a linkage mechanism tied to the comprehensive procurement price of domestic aviation kerosene. When that cost exceeds 5,000 yuan per tonne, airlines may charge a surcharge, with the 5th of each month serving as the fixed adjustment window, [according to industry coverage](https://m.163.com/dy/article/KP51A89O0550EB0R.html).

Aviation kerosene is the most rigid core cost for airlines, accounting for 30% to 40% of total operating expenses for domestic carriers. Rising international crude oil prices have pushed procurement costs higher, triggering the latest adjustment. Airlines cite National Development and Reform Commission documents governing the mechanism - known as Fa Gai Jia Ge Nos. 2879 (2009), 2219 (2011), and 571 (2015) - as the regulatory basis for the change.

The move fits a volatile pattern this year. The surcharge fell to its 2026 low on January 5, when it was set at 10 yuan for short routes and 20 yuan for long routes. It jumped in April amid a surge in international fuel prices, driven by shipping restrictions in the Strait of Hormuz and escalating Middle East tensions, before being lowered again on July 5 and August 5 as fuel costs eased.

Data from the industry platform Flight Master show the current standard of 40 yuan and 70 yuan that has held since early August will be replaced by the higher rates announced this week, as [reported](https://www.eeo.com.cn/2026/1006/1056758.shtml) by Economic Observer and Shanghai Securities News.

## Consumer Response and Market Impact

Surcharge increases have repeatedly prompted ticket stockpiling by Chinese travelers, who buy in advance to lock in lower rates before effective dates. During the April 2026 increase, consumers reported purchasing tickets as far ahead as December, with some buying tickets for the entire year.

Analysts at the travel data firm Qunar noted that expectations of surcharge hikes tend to pull forward booking demand, particularly around peak travel periods such as Golden Week and the summer holidays. That same dynamic may now play out ahead of the October 10 effective date.

## What to Watch

The immediate question is whether the increase holds or reverses. Because the surcharge is recalculated monthly against aviation kerosene procurement costs, future movement will depend largely on international crude oil prices and the pace of any easing in the Middle East.

Airlines, meanwhile, face a dual squeeze: higher fuel costs compress margins even as the surcharge partially offsets them, a burden most acute for low-cost carriers whose competitiveness rests on cost control. For travelers, the practical takeaway is straightforward - booking before October 10 avoids the new surcharge, and the original-issue-date rule means those savings stay locked in even if a trip is later rescheduled.
