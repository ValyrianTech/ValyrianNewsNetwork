# Kim Jong Un Visits Dandong as Wang Huning Hosts

North Korean leader Kim Jong Un made a one-day visit to the Chinese border city of Dandong on Saturday, accompanied by Wang Huning, one of China's most senior political figures, in a high-profile display of Beijing-Pyongyang diplomatic engagement.

According to [Xinhua](https://english.news.cn/20261024/37420bc7fdc741aaac87a13c61cc155b/c.html), Wang — a member of the Standing Committee of the Political Bureau of the Communist Party of China Central Committee and chairman of the National Committee of the Chinese People's Political Consultative Conference — conveyed cordial greetings from President Xi Jinping to Kim. Foreign Minister Wang Yi also took part in the day's activities.

Kim crossed into China by train from Pyongyang to Sinuiju and over the newly opened New Yalu River Bridge, as [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/oct/24/kim-jong-un-crosses-newly-opened-bridge-into-china) reported, citing South Korean government sources. It was Kim's first known trip abroad in more than a year and his sixth visit to China.

## A Ceremony Rooted in Shared History

The visit's centrepiece was the commemoration of the 76th anniversary of the Chinese People's Volunteers' entry into the 1950–53 Korean War, which China marks on Sunday. Beijing's official readout, published by [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/politics/leaders/20261024/97c35c0d27bd421283c7d30a32592100/c.html), detailed a wreath-laying ceremony at the Memorial Hall of the War to Resist US Aggression and Aid Korea, where both national anthems played and honour guards carried a wreath bearing a banner in Korean reading: "Forever remember the martyrs of the Chinese People's Volunteers."

Kim adjusted the ribbon himself and joined a moment of silence before touring the memorial hall, where he donated a historic photograph bearing his handwritten inscription. In a later meeting, Kim said he had come "specially to Dandong" to honour the Chinese volunteers who "supported Korea with their lives and blood," expressing the DPRK's "heartfelt thanks and eternal respect."

Wang framed the visit as a continuation of the momentum from recent leadership exchanges. "Last September and this June, General Secretary Xi Jinping and General Secretary [Kim] realized historic mutual visits and reached a series of important consensuses, leading traditional China-DPRK friendly and cooperative relations into a new historical period," Wang said, according to the Xinhua readout.

## An Economic Dimension and a New Bridge

The symbolism of the trip is difficult to separate from its practical backdrop. The New Yalu River Bridge — a roughly 3km, four-lane cable-stayed span linking Dandong to Sinuiju — officially opened to traffic on 15 October, just nine days before Kim's arrival. China finished building it around 2014, but it sat unused for about 12 years amid delays on the North Korean side.

Wang explicitly tied the relationship's future to the new crossing. "Taking the opening of the New Yalu River Bridge as an opportunity, we will expand personnel exchanges and economic and trade cooperation to better benefit the peoples of both countries," he said. In the afternoon, Wang accompanied Kim on a tour of a Dandong automobile factory, inspecting the production workshop and receiving a briefing — a nod toward the North's emphasis on regional economic development.

## A Two-Bridges Balancing Act

Kim's timing reflects a broader strategic calculation. His crossing came less than two months after North Korea and Russia opened their first road bridge across the Tumen River, leaving Pyongyang newly road-linked to both of its principal backers — China and Russia.

As [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/oct/24/north-korea-new-yalu-river-bridge-russia-china) noted in its analysis, the new bridges carry both economic and geopolitical weight. Leif-Eric Easley, a professor at Ewha University in Seoul, told the newspaper that Kim is "leveraging his windfall from Moscow for additional gains from Beijing," while China "may seek to recover lost influence by increasing trade and people-to-people exchanges." The same analysis cited Fyodor Tertitskiy of Korea University, who argued that Kim is "trying to play the two against each other" rather than presiding over a straightforward rivalry for influence.

## A Frosty Neighbourhood

The warmth on display in Dandong contrasts sharply with North Korea's relations with South Korea. Kim's sister, Kim Yo Jong, has issued roughly 10 statements criticising Seoul since late September and has announced plans to resume loudspeaker propaganda broadcasts at the border.

Observers will also watch whether Kim's China trip feeds speculation about a possible meeting with US President Donald Trump on the sidelines of the APEC summit in Shenzhen on 18–19 November. Reuters reported on Friday, however, that US and South Korean officials see little chance of a Trump-Kim meeting this year.

## What to Watch

For Beijing, the visit consolidates a party-to-party channel of diplomacy — Wang, rather than Premier Li Qiang, was the named host in Xinhua's account, a detail that diverges from earlier South Korean reporting. For Pyongyang, the trip underscores a deliberate choice to lean on its northern neighbours while turning away from the South. Whether the newly open bridge translates into measurably deeper trade, or remains chiefly a political symbol, will be the test of what Wang described as a relationship entering a "new historical period."
