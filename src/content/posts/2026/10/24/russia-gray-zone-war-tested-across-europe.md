# Russia's Gray-Zone War Tested Across Europe

A cross-border investigation coordinated by the European Broadcasting Union has documented roughly 100 Russia-linked hybrid incidents across 17 European countries between 1 April and 15 October 2026, painting a picture of a coordinated campaign of sub-threshold pressure on the continent. The findings, published by Belgian public broadcaster [RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/sabotage-drones-et-violations-de-l-espace-aerien-suivi-des-menaces-hybrides-liees-a-la-russie-dans-toute-l-europe-11804795), draw on open-source research and fact-checking by journalists from 17 public-service media outlets working through the EBU's [Eurovision News Spotlight](https://www.ebu.ch/groups/eurovision-news-spotlight) network.

The investigation compiles incidents including airspace violations, sabotage attempts, the use of low-level "disposable" agents recruited online, the instrumentalization of migration flows, and overt Russian military activity near European borders. According to RTBF, the figure is a "very conservative estimate" that excludes classified incidents known to military and intelligence agencies but never made public.

## A widening gray zone

The European Centre of Excellence for Countering Hybrid Threats (Hybrid CoE) defines hybrid threats as actions by state or non-state actors that combine military and non-military, overt and covert means to undermine a target. The centre sorts such operations into three escalating tiers: hybrid influence (information operations and electoral interference), hybrid threat operations (attacks on critical infrastructure or cybercrime), and hybrid warfare (incidents involving the use of force for political and strategic ends).

European leaders have increasingly spoken in stark terms. In September, French President Emmanuel Macron declared "no one is safe" before convening an unprecedented meeting at the Elysée on Russia's hybrid war against Ukraine's allies, attended by 2027 presidential candidates and political parties. "This is a very serious situation," Mikael Frisell, director-general of the Swedish Civil Contingencies Agency, told SVT. "Russia has a strategy to destabilize our societies and erode Western support for Ukraine. We must respond by taking proactive measures and preparing for the worst."

## The Black Sea becomes a front

The most recent incidents pointing to an intensification have clustered around the Black Sea, where repeated drone attacks have targeted commercial ships off the coasts of Romania and Bulgaria. The human toll is not yet fully established: two deaths were confirmed by mid-October, and at least ten other crew members were reported missing and presumed dead, per RTBF.

On 13 October, European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen [declared](https://x.com/vonderleyen/status/2109900758854373525) that the Black Sea had become "another front of escalation of the Russian war in Ukraine." In her State of the Union address, she warned: "We must not delude ourselves about what is happening here: the hybrid threats Europe faces are less and less hybrid and less and less threats." EU foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas, speaking on the sidelines of the UN General Assembly, said Moscow is trying to intimidate Europe into reducing its support for Ukraine.

Moldova offers a striking example of the pressure. Defence Minister Anatolie Nosatîi said in late September that the government had recorded 84 drone incursions in 2026, accusing Russia of endangering the lives of Moldovan citizens.

## Where is the red line?

As these operations take on an increasingly physical dimension, a fundamental question arises: at what point could an incident cross a threshold and qualify as full-fledged military aggression?

Dr Teija Tiilikainen, director of the Hybrid CoE, told Eurovision News Spotlight that new states are being targeted and new technologies are increasingly used. "We could assume that, in the event that a hybrid threat operation or hybrid war attack caused human casualties, there would be strong pressure to take more significant countermeasures," she said. She added that EU and NATO members are generally reluctant to define where that red line sits. "Strategic ambiguity is considered an effective way of deterring hostile states," she noted.

In its annual threat update on 13 October, MI5 Director General Sir Ken McCallum said Russia "remains aggressor number one" and that the Russian military and intelligence services are "once again escalating their attempts at hybrid warfare — being careful to stay below the NATO Article V threshold it'd be foolish for them to cross," according to the [official transcript](https://www.mi5.gov.uk/mi5-director-general-delivers-annual-threat-update).

## A sign of weakness, or of resolve?

Not everyone reads the escalation the same way. NATO Secretary-General Mark Rutte, speaking at a press conference in Tallinn on 14 October, cautioned against overreaction. "Russia is not in a favorable position," he said. "These hybrid attacks and all that they entail are a sign of weakness. What they want is for us to talk about it all day, to be disturbed, to hold press conferences about it. We won't do that. We will react where we need to react."

Yet Rutte acknowledged the campaign continues, adding that Putin is trying to weaken Western determination and prevent support for Ukraine. Asked about an official database of hybrid incidents in Europe, NATO's press office said there is "no public or unclassified document" it could share on the subject.

Russia has repeatedly denied involvement in the dozens of incidents documented across Europe and described as hybrid attacks, asserting instead that Western capitals have declared a real hybrid war against Russia, in the words of Foreign Ministry spokesperson Maria Zakharova in May 2026. The framing of the investigation is inherently adversarial toward Moscow, and RTBF itself stresses that some incidents remain "suspected" rather than confirmed, and that some initial attributions have later been disproved.

## What's next

The recent escalation is seen not only in rising numbers but in the changing nature of incidents, Tiilikainen said. "Operations are becoming increasingly dynamic and even military forms are being used. We can say that the gray zone between peace and war — and the zone of hostile activity below the threshold of an armed attack — is widening. New tools are being tested, as is the tolerance of Euro-Atlantic countries, along with their reactions and countermeasures."

The open question for European capitals and for NATO is how much of that widening gray zone they can absorb before ambiguity stops being a deterrent and starts inviting escalation. The EBU's earlier investigation, ["Playing With Fire"](https://spotlight.ebu.ch/p/playing-with-fire-are-russias-hybrid), documented the same sabotage-and-recruitment model in 2025. With the incidents now increasingly physical, the tolerance being tested is not just Russia's — it is Europe's own.
