# Nine-day rail closure on line 161 to finish RER works

Rail traffic between Ottignies and Brussels-Luxembourg is suspended in both directions from the morning of Saturday 24 October until late evening on Sunday 1 November, as Infrabel and SNCB carry out the final phase of RER construction on line 161. The nine-day closure, [announced jointly by the two operators](https://www.rtbf.be/article/le-trafic-des-trains-sera-interrompu-entre-ottignies-et-bruxelles-luxembourg-fin-octobre-11804284) on Thursday 22 October, forces thousands of commuters in the Walloon Brabant belt onto replacement buses or diverted intercity services.

## What is happening on the line

The closure allows Infrabel's teams to complete the four-tracking of line 161 between Watermael and the junction towards Louvain-la-Neuve — the "ultimate phase" of a project relaunched in 2018. During the nine days, crews will install the last two switches at the exit of La Hulpe station towards Brussels and connect them to tracks laid in an earlier phase, while adapting the tracks and the catenary, the overhead power system.

In parallel, other teams will demolish old platforms at Rixensart, install canopies at La Hulpe and fit out various equipment at Profondsart. According to [Infrabel's RER project documentation](https://infrabel.be/fr/projet/rer), four-tracking doubles capacity on the RER lines, taking them from two to four tracks so that express trains are fully separated from slower stopping services.

## How commuters are affected

SNCB's service adaptations are substantial. According to the [operator's official works page](https://www.belgiantrain.be/fr/news/works-bruxelles-ottignies), the S8 trains linking Zottegem, Brussels and Ottignies or Louvain-la-Neuve are replaced by buses between Brussels-Luxembourg and Ottignies, calling at Etterbeek, Watermael, Boitsfort, Groenendaal, Hoeilaart, La Hulpe, Genval, Rixensart and Profondsart.

Certain direct trains are diverted via Louvain and will not serve Bruxelles-Schuman or Bruxelles-Luxembourg. On weekdays, the IC services from Brussels-Midi towards Namur and onwards to Arlon, Luxembourg, Liège and Dinant are rerouted or shortened, with journey times extended by 20 to 30 minutes. The S4 line between Alost, Brussels and Louvain-la-Neuve terminates at Brussels-Luxembourg, and several peak-hour P trains are shortened, rerouted or cancelled altogether — including the P services to Namur and Rochefort-Jemelle and the 21:41 S8 from Louvain-la-Neuve to Ottignies. SNCB is urging passengers to consult its journey planner before travelling.

## A project with a long history

The RER — Réseau Express Régional, a regional express rail network designed to improve public transport within 30 kilometres of Brussels — has become something of a byword for delays in Belgian transport policy, and is still routinely referred to in the local press as a "dossier maudit", or cursed dossier. Construction on the Ottignies section began as early as August 2006 and on La Hulpe in March 2011, before the project was formally relaunched on a section-by-section basis in 2018 to spread costs and limit disruption. Work on Genval and Rixensart followed in 2021.

The finish line is now in sight. Four tracks have already been in service since November 2023 between Ottignies station and the Louvain-la-Neuve junction, and since mid-2025 between Brussels and the entrance to La Hulpe — roughly half the distance. Infrabel plans to complete the line 161 works between La Hulpe/Hoeilaart and Ottignies in December 2026, while works at Ottignies station itself, including a new station managed by SNCB, will continue into 2027.

## What it delivers — and what remains

The nine-day disruption comes with a payoff: from mid-December, SNCB expects to offer an enhanced service thanks to the full commissioning of the four tracks. Higher capacity on line 161, a key axis carrying both commuter traffic and long-distance services towards Luxembourg, should translate into more frequent trains and improved reliability for the Brabant Wallon towns that depend on it.

The comparison with the rest of the RER is instructive. On line 124 between Brussels and Nivelles, Infrabel does not expect the four-tracking between Uccle and Wallonia to be completed before December 2034, with the timetable dependent on the granting of permits. Line 161, by contrast, is among the most advanced sections of a network whose delivery has repeatedly slipped.

For the next nine days, though, the priority for regular passengers is simpler: check the planner, allow considerably more time, and expect buses.
