# Belgium Weighs €5.4bn BNP Paribas Stake Sale

The Belgian federal government is reportedly considering selling part of its stake in French banking giant BNP Paribas — a holding worth an estimated €5.4 billion — as it struggles to close a stubborn budget gap. But the government has offered almost no confirmation of the idea, and several coalition parties deny that a sale is genuinely on the table. The question, first raised by Bloomberg, has left markets and observers guessing as federal budget talks remain stalled.

## A stake born of crisis

Belgium's holding in BNP Paribas dates to the 2008-2009 financial crisis, when the state intervened to rescue its banking sector. At its peak, the state held up to 10% of BNP Paribas, the French parent of BNP Paribas Fortis. After a partial resale of shares in 2013, that stake fell to its current 5.7%, according to [RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/l-etat-belge-envisage-t-il-de-vendre-ses-parts-dans-bnp-paribas-beaucoup-de-questions-peu-de-reponses-11804821).

The sale idea surfaced in a tense moment. Federal budget negotiations were paused after coalition partner Vooruit called a halt on the evening of 21 October, asking for time to examine a series of new proposals. It was in that context that Bloomberg, generally well-informed, reported that "the idea of the Belgian state parting with part or all of its BNP Paribas shares was gaining ground within the government."

As [La Libre / Belga](https://www.lalibre.be/economie/entreprises-startup/2026/10/21/la-vente-des-parts-belges-dans-bnp-paribas-gagne-du-terrain-au-sein-du-gouvernement-KHLZLZZGXVFWPGGLMOMDYNA3WI/) reported, the state holds 5.7% of the group, worth an estimated €5.4 billion. The partial privatization of state-owned bank Belfius is already underway — a roughly 20% sale aimed at raising about €2 billion — and any BNP Paribas move would likely follow that process, per Bloomberg's anonymous sources.

## The case against a sale

Inside the "Arizona" coalition, caution is driven by more than one concern. Selling would mean executing what officials describe as a "one-shot" — a unique operation that is neither reproducible nor durable in its effects.

It would also mean forgoing the annual BNP Paribas dividends that traditionally help the federal budget: €465 million last year, and more than €500 million already this year. That figure includes €320 million in exceptional dividends on 2025 results, received in February 2026 through SFPIM, the federal financial arm, plus a €200 million advance dividend in July.

The most significant constraint is legal. Under EU budget rules, proceeds from such an asset sale cannot be booked as current revenue to plug the deficit; they would have to be applied to reducing Belgian debt. Experts note that even a €5 billion transfer against Belgium's roughly €581 billion federal debt would trim the debt by only about 1% — an effect RTBF described as "homoeopathic."

There is also market timing to consider: any sale would need to be executed at the right moment and the best price, in a context marked by frequent stock-market volatility.

## Silence and denials

For now, the federal government is saying nothing. The Finance Ministry's cabinet, which would be at the heart of any decision, has maintained total silence, while some parties have ruled out that a sale is genuinely being contemplated in its current form. The result, RTBF noted, is "denials on one side and total radio silence" on the other.

All reporting on the idea traces back to a single Bloomberg report citing anonymous sources. No official statement has confirmed that a sale decision has been taken.

## Why it matters

The dilemma captures a structural tension in Belgium's public finances. The state's bank holdings — acquired to stabilize the sector in 2008 — have become a reliable source of recurring income at a time when the federal budget badly needs it. Yet the same holdings are among the largest liquid assets the state could draw on to reduce a debt mountain that EU rules increasingly pressure Brussels to address.

The Belfius precedent is instructive. That partial sale has been framed as a way to raise cash while preserving a dividend, managed by SFPIM — an approach that avoids the "one-shot" problem of a full disposal. Whether a BNP Paribas sale would follow the same logic, or simply be a one-off cash injection, remains unresolved.

## What to watch

The decisive factors are likely to be the budget negotiations themselves and the pace of the Belfius process. If the coalition's budget gap proves too large to close through conventional measures, a partial BNP Paribas disposal could move from exploratory idea to live option — though EU rules would still limit its practical usefulness.

For now, the only certainty is the uncertainty. A €5.4 billion asset sits on the state's balance sheet, its future the subject of denials, silence and a single, unconfirmed report. The next move may depend less on financial logic than on how badly the government needs cash — and how much it is willing to give up in annual dividends to get it.
