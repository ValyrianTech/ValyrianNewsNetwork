# Tibet Quake Response Shifts to Relief and Recovery

Two days after a magnitude-7.0 earthquake struck Baqing County in the Tibet Autonomous Region, the operation has moved from the frantic first hours of search and rescue into a vast logistics effort: delivering tens of thousands of relief items, housing displaced residents in tent camps and treating the injured in a remote, high-altitude corner of the Qinghai-Tibet Plateau.

According to [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/20261024/d01bbbaa64d04ce7a00469df5cf22f1c/c.html), 52,450 items and sets of relief supplies allocated across national, regional, municipal and county levels had been dispatched to the disaster zone by 11:00 on 24 October, while more than 2,500 rescue personnel were committed to the effort. The quake, which struck at 22:33 on 22 October, had killed 16 people and injured 76 as of the latest official tally, with 339 homes collapsed or damaged.

## The quake and its toll

The earthquake's epicenter lay within the Qiangtang block of the Qinghai-Tibet Plateau, about 2 km from the Baqing–Leiwuqi fault, at a shallow depth of roughly 10 km. Han Yanyan, a senior engineer at the China Earthquake Networks Center, described the event as an extensional rupture broadly consistent with the region's tectonic characteristics. It was the largest earthquake in China so far in 2026 and the first magnitude-7 or above event within 200 km of the epicenter in the modern instrumental record.

Initial figures emerged quickly. In its first comprehensive report, [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/20261023/c61bb6628dd744328b5e539fd30c98e3/c.html) said the quake had killed 16 people and injured 32 — 11 seriously and 21 lightly — with 339 homes collapsed. By the following day the injury count had risen to 76, and no missing persons were reported after three rounds of house-to-house searches.

Aftershocks have remained dense, with 834 recorded by 09:45 on 24 October, the largest of them reported at around magnitude 4.3 to 4.5. The sequence even shook neighboring Qinghai Province, where a magnitude-5.3 tremor was felt in Zadoi County.

## A coordinated, top-down response

Within hours of the quake, President Xi Jinping issued instructions calling for all-out search and rescue and treatment of the injured, warning that the disaster zone's high altitude and cold weather demanded close monitoring of seismic and meteorological conditions and protection against secondary disasters. Premier Li Qiang followed with directions to verify the extent of the damage and to strengthen earthquake monitoring and prevention work, as [CCTV](https://news.cctv.com/2026/10/23/ARTIHYAN2UWIk3neGsrEqdC5261023.shtml) reported.

China's National Disaster Prevention and Reduction Committee and the Ministry of Emergency Management raised the national natural disaster relief emergency response to Level III. In addition to an initial 5,000 central relief items, another 5,000 were allocated, and 43,000 more were coordinated through social-sector mechanisms. The Central Organization Department allocated 25 million yuan from Party dues to support relief, while the China Red Cross and private firms including Anta Group and Pinduoduo each contributed millions of yuan or large shipments of supplies.

On the ground, Baqing County committed more than 1,600 people, the Nagqu Fire Rescue brigade deployed 347 personnel, 15 search dogs and 82 vehicles, and the Nagqu Public Security Bureau dispatched 530 officers and 100 vehicles. The Western Theater Command activated its emergency response, and by 16:00 on 23 October the People's Liberation Army and Armed Police had deployed more than 400 personnel and 50 vehicles, treated five injured people, erected 35 tents and set up two medical aid stations and two hot-food supply points. Specialized national rescue teams brought reconnaissance and heavy-lift drones, slope radar, satellite ad-hoc networks and life-detection equipment, and a large Wing Loong drone arrived on site.

## Shelter, supplies and medical care

Authorities have established 35 resettlement sites across the disaster zone. In Er Village in Maru Township, among the worst-hit areas, 51 blue tents housed all affected villagers by 18:00 on 23 October, with electricity restored to 36 of them at the time of reporting and the remainder to follow.

The supply manifest reflects the plateau's bitter cold. Alongside 4,651 tents and 2,000 prefabricated movable homes, the dispatch included 11,772 folding beds, 10,133 cotton mattresses, 5,715 quilts, 2,250 cotton outfits, 1,800 cotton trousers, 6,070 military greatcoats, 2,227 stoves and 694 Tibetan robes.

Medical treatment has proceeded in parallel. All 76 injured received timely care, and of the 11 initially critical patients, five improved to minor status while the remaining six were transferred to higher-level hospitals — three to Nagqu People's Hospital and one each to the Tibet Autonomous Region People's Hospital, the Tibet Hospital affiliated with Sichuan University's West China Hospital, and Lhasa People's Hospital. West China Hospital activated its emergency plan and dispatched a medical team from Chengdu at about 06:00 on 24 October. The National Health Commission deployed a working group and national experts, mobilized six health emergency teams and activated a blood emergency supply mechanism. China Mobile restored damaged base stations, returning communications capacity in the quake zone to pre-earthquake levels.

## The road ahead

Experts assess the risk of large-scale geological disasters as relatively low, but they caution that localized hazards persist. Fan Xuanmei, director of the State Key Laboratory of Geohazard Prevention at Chengdu University of Technology, warned that resettlement sites must avoid dangerous ground and make use of flat terrain, and that cracked or damaged houses must not be reoccupied. Tu Jienan, a senior engineer at the China Aero Geophysical Survey and Remote Sensing Center for Natural Resources, noted that steep slopes above villages around the epicenter carry a risk of small collapses, landslides or rockfalls that could block rescue routes.

For Baqing — a former national-level poverty-stricken county sitting above 4,500 meters, where winter is closing in — the hardest questions lie ahead. Chief among them is how and when to rebuild the 339 damaged homes, whether the dense aftershock sequence will cause further casualties or secondary hazards, and how long relief logistics can be sustained on the plateau as temperatures fall. The official framing emphasizes speed and coordination; the coming weeks will test whether that machinery can carry a remote county through a winter in tents.
