# China Exports Top 8 Trillion Yuan in Quarter for First Time

China's goods exports exceeded 8 trillion yuan in a single quarter for the first time, with third-quarter outbound shipments rising 22% year-on-year, according to customs data that underscores a deepening shift toward higher-value, technology-intensive goods. For the first nine months of 2026, exports totaled 23.2 trillion yuan, up 16.4% from a year earlier, [Xinhua News reported](https://www.news.cn/fortune/20261024/105d1a75b6174500ac94f532d061bb1e/c.html).

The milestone, announced at an October 14 press conference by the General Administration of Customs, marks a quarter in which the growth rate itself accelerated — the 22% third-quarter pace comfortably outran the 16.4% nine-month average. Total goods trade for January–September reached 33.61 trillion yuan, up 4%, while imports slipped 0.9% to 10.41 trillion yuan, according to [China Securities Journal](https://finance.eastmoney.com/a/202610143895367512.html).

## A Structural Shift Toward "New" Drivers

The headline number is significant less for its size than for what it contains. Machinery and electrical products — the backbone of China's industrial export basket — grew 23.7%, well above the overall export growth rate. New-energy products and computing-power-related goods were singled out as the leading growth drivers in the Xinhua analysis.

At the product level, the composition tells a story of industrial upgrading. Ship exports surpassed 350 billion yuan, up 24.6%, with 300,000-tonne tankers and large liquefied petroleum gas and liquefied natural gas carriers multiplying. Industrial robot exports reached roughly 10 billion yuan, up 16.9%. Electronic components jumped 70%, while electrical wire, cable and related equipment rose 25.3% — gains tied to global demand for AI computing and power infrastructure, [People's Daily reported](https://www.cnr.cn/mspd/sywzl/20261015/t20261015_527842287.shtml), citing customs data.

Chemicals and consumer goods rounded out the picture. Basic organic chemicals rose 24.4% and primary-form plastics 32.2% as Middle East instability tightened global energy and chemical feedstock supply. Digital camera exports climbed 40.5%, alongside niche consumer items such as portable air-conditioners and mini ice-makers adapted to global demand for personalised, scenario-specific products.

Xinhua framed the shift in structural terms: China's export mix has moved decisively from relatively labour-intensive goods toward technology-intensive goods, while export markets have re-oriented away from a long-standing US and Europe focus toward Belt and Road partner countries, ASEAN, the Middle East and Latin America — a diversification strategy designed to spread single-market risk.

## From Price Competition to Brand and Service

Economists interviewed by Xinhua described a competitive logic that is changing at the level of the firm, not just the trade statistics. Zhan Yubo, deputy director of the Institute of Economics at the Shanghai Academy of Social Sciences, noted that since 2023 outside observers had worried China's export contribution would decline amid global supply-chain restructuring. "But the fact is, from 2023 to 2025, even facing external shocks such as the tariff war, China's exports still maintained rapid growth," he said.

Ruan Qingsong, a professor at Tongji University's School of Economics and Management, pointed to two adjustments among exporters: pushing products up the value chain and diversifying market presence further. Beyond raising the technology content of goods, he argued, Chinese firms must export complete equipment sets bundled with supporting services, reinforced by local warehousing and localised operations.

He also flagged a less-discussed bottleneck — branding. "Sometimes with the same tech content, we are no worse than European or American products, but cheaper, because international market recognition of our high-end brands still needs improvement," Ruan said, arguing that closing this gap is a commercial and strategic challenge rather than a purely technical one.

## The Domestic Imbalance Behind the Headline

Even as exports shine, the analysis is careful to qualify the celebration. Viewed through the lens of consumption, investment and exports — the "troika" that drives Chinese growth — the balance remains uneven. Consumption is described as mild and stable, while investment appears to be declining.

Ruan singled out the fall in private investment as the most important concern. "The private economy is most sensitive to market conditions; if the decline in private investment narrows or even rebounds, that would be a very good signal," he said, adding that restoring business confidence and actual orders is the core prerequisite — once orders and profits improve, other problems ease.

Zhan argued the priority for future policy is sustainability, achieved through technological progress — particularly breakthroughs in core "chokehold" technologies — and ensuring the gains from that progress reach all parts of society. He called for policy tilted toward balance: guaranteeing even income growth, easing worries over healthcare and elderly care, and advancing livelihood projects. "Overall, this is systemic work," he said.

## What to Watch

Customs Deputy Director and Spokesperson Wang Jun said the fundamentals of exports have not changed, pledging to build a modern industrial system with advanced manufacturing as its backbone. But Ruan cautioned against blind optimism based on this year's high growth, urging preparation for potential demand fluctuations next year.

The 8-trillion-yuan quarter is a genuine marker of industrial upgrading — and a reminder that China's export story is increasingly one of robots, ships, electronic components and computing power rather than apparel and furniture. Whether that momentum carries into the fourth quarter and beyond will depend less on factory floors than on the confidence of private investors at home and the durability of demand in the diversified markets China has spent years cultivating.
