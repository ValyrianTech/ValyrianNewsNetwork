# Politburo Reviews Inspections as CPC Targets Universities

China's top decision-making body reviewed the results of the Party's latest round of internal inspections and, a day later, named the targets of the next wave—an almost exclusive focus on the nation's elite higher-education system.

The Politburo of the Communist Party of China's Central Committee met on 23 October, chaired by General Secretary Xi Jinping, to review the comprehensive report on the seventh round of central inspections of the 20th Central Committee, according to [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/politics/leaders/20261023/96c5296dfcc84c06a009d3e7e018e6ac/c.html). The following day, with Central Committee approval, the Party announced that the eighth round will conduct regular inspections of the Party organizations of 32 units—the Ministry of Education and 31 leading universities, [The Paper](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_34325730) reported.

## A Routine Sequence With Real Weight

The back-to-back announcements are procedurally standard: the leadership reviews the findings of one inspection round, then launches the next. But the substance carries signal. Concentrating an entire wave on China's most prestigious universities—the bulk of them Project 985 and "double first-class" institutions—points to sustained scrutiny of a sector the Party treats as central to ideological discipline as well as academic output.

## What the Seventh Round Found

The seventh round, deployed in April 2026, saw 15 Central Inspection Teams examine 36 central and state organs through regular inspection, with the window for public petitions closing on 23 June. The Politburo's readout offered an assessment that was positive but pointed: work in central and state organs had "made positive progress, but there are also some problems that must be highly valued and seriously resolved."

The meeting called on inspected organizations to "solidify rectification responsibility," strengthen oversight of corrective action, and "dare to touch hard issues and truly rectify." It also framed central and state organs as the "first phalanx" in practicing the "Two Upholds"—the principle of upholding Xi's core position and the authority of the Party's centralized leadership—and urged them to continuously improve their "political judgment, political understanding, and political execution."

The readout reiterated a "high-pressure posture in punishing corruption" and a commitment to "carry the strict tone through to the end," language that reaffirms the disciplinary campaign launched more than a decade ago. The meeting also "studied other matters," a standard catch-all phrase in official readouts.

## Why Universities, and Why Now

The eighth round's roster reads like a who's who of Chinese higher education: Peking, Tsinghua, Renmin, Beijing Normal, Fudan, Shanghai Jiao Tong, Xi'an Jiao Tong, the University of Science and Technology of China, Zhejiang, Nanjing, Wuhan, Sun Yat-sen, and more, alongside the Ministry of Education itself.

The sector focus is not unprecedented. During the 19th Central Committee, a comparable round in 2021 targeted 31 centrally administered universities, and earlier rounds examined universities after the anti-corruption drive intensified in 2013. Such sector-wide sweeps typically touch on admissions integrity, research funding, ideological work, and leadership conduct—areas where the center has sought to reset academic culture and governance.

## Analysis: Oversight as Political Alignment

The pairing of the two announcements illustrates how the inspection apparatus functions in Xi's governance model: as much an instrument of political and ideological alignment as a conventional anti-corruption audit. Calling central and state organs the "first phalanx" of the "Two Upholds" frames compliance in explicitly political terms, and extending the next round to universities extends that expectation into academia.

Neither readout disclosed which organs were found problematic or what specific issues surfaced in the seventh round—consistent with the opaque reporting style of the system. What the eighth round ultimately produces in findings, disciplinary actions, and rectifications will emerge only over the coming months.

## What to Watch

Three questions will shape how this round is read. First, whether any university leaders are investigated or disciplined as a direct result of the eighth round. Second, whether the round is framed around admissions fraud, academic misconduct, or ideological and loyalty concerns. And third, whether the inspections connect to broader anti-corruption efforts ahead of the next Party Congress cycle. For now, the message is clear: the Party's oversight machinery is turning its attention squarely on the institutions that train China's next generation of elites.
