# Misiorowski Sets World Series Velocity Record on First Pitch

Jacob Misiorowski stepped onto a World Series mound for the first time and, before the adrenaline had a chance to settle, made history. The Milwaukee Brewers right-hander opened Game 1 of the 2026 Fall Classic with a 103.5 mph fastball to Tampa Bay Rays leadoff hitter Yandy Diaz — the fastest pitch ever recorded in World Series play, according to [AP News](https://apnews.com/article/misiorowski-fastest-world-series-pitch-c5f72fd8fec1a1c79abf8907b98ae7ae).

The pitch was taken high out of the strike zone for a ball. But the record was already in the books, and Misiorowski went on to earn his first postseason win as a starter, allowing one run over five innings as Milwaukee beat Tampa Bay 3-2 on Friday night.

## A Record Broken on the First Delivery

The previous World Series record for any pitcher belonged to Aroldis Chapman, who threw 103.2 mph in the ninth inning of Game 2 in 2016 while pitching for the Chicago Cubs. The previous high for a Series starting pitcher was 101.1 mph by the New York Mets' Noah Syndergaard in 2015.

The 24-year-old's velocity was so extreme that the stadium scoreboard at American Family Field could not keep pace, flashing 103.4 mph — one-tenth of a mile slower than MLB's Statcast system recorded. Statcast's pitch-tracking era began in 2008, and all fastest-pitch records refer to that period.

"I had so much adrenaline built up," Misiorowski said after the game. "I don't even know how to tell you how I felt. I still don't know how I felt. I feel like I'm still trying to take everything in and understand what happened."

## More Than a Single Throw

Misiorowski's first pitch was the headline, but the volume of heat that followed was just as striking. He reached 100 mph with 38 pitches, topping the previous World Series high of 24 set by Chapman in Game 5 of 2016. He threw 30 pitches at 101 mph or higher, 11 at 102-plus and three at 103-plus. The prior high for 100 mph pitches by a starting pitcher in a Series was six, by Tampa Bay's Tyler Glasnow in 2020.

His line was efficient rather than overpowering: five innings, four hits, one run, one walk and a season-low three strikeouts on 78 pitches. Rays batters fouled off 20 of those pitches. According to the [AP's game recap](https://apnews.com/article/world-series-rays-brewers-game-1-049d8efa7d3d7c8fc8a4e4b88d8dfe77), his spin rate was down and he generated just seven swings and misses, five on the fastball — subtle signals of a long season.

"The adrenaline kind of took over, and I tensed up and threw it hard," Misiorowski said. "Then I settled down, and that was the game plan, to settle in and do me. Don't try and overdo it."

## Milwaukee's First World Series Game in 44 Years

The record came on a milestone night for the Brewers franchise, hosting its first World Series game since 1982. Milwaukee took a 2-0 first-inning lead and added an insurance run in the seventh, then survived a chaotic ninth. A game-ending double play was overturned on video review after replays showed shortstop Cooper Pratt's foot slightly off the bag; reliever Trevor Megill regrouped and struck out Jonny DeLuca with two runners in scoring position to seal the win.

Rays manager Kevin Cash offered a wry assessment of facing Misiorowski. "He's such a special pitcher," Cash said. "I felt like for him to be out of the game at five innings, there's a small win in that."

Brewers manager Pat Murphy tempered expectations about the strikeout totals. "What we're used to seeing in the regular season was Miz just mow through people and have two strikeouts an inning. That's unrealistic," Murphy said. "This is the best team in the American League."

## A Season of Unmatched Velocity

Misiorowski's record-setting night was the culmination of a season unlike any the sport has tracked. He threw 1,115 pitches of 100 mph or higher in 2026, shattering the previous single-season high of 673 by Jordan Hicks in 2018. His season-high fastball was 105.5 mph to the Cubs' Pete Crow-Armstrong on June 27, just shy of Chapman's regular-season record of 105.8 mph set in 2010.

His postseason run had already produced a milestone: a 104.8 mph first pitch to Fernando Tatis Jr. in the NLDS opener on Oct. 3, which at the time set the record for fastest postseason pitch. He reached 100 mph with 96 of 182 pitches during the Division Series and League Championship Series.

The workload is worth watching. Misiorowski has thrown 188 innings in 2026, up from 141 1/3 last year, and the dip in spin rate during Game 1 is a potential fatigue signal as the series wears on.

## What's Next

The Brewers hold a 1-0 series lead heading into Game 2 on Saturday night, with Drew Rasmussen (16-5, 2.67 ERA) slated for Tampa Bay against Milwaukee's Logan Henderson (11-3, 2.47). The team that has won Game 1 has gone on to win the World Series 62.8% of the time.

This is the first World Series between two teams seeking their first championship since 1980, and both clubs sit in the bottom half of MLB payrolls — Milwaukee in the 19th spot and Tampa Bay 25th as of Aug. 31. For the small-market Brewers, the night belonged to a 24-year-old whose first pitch of the Fall Classic will be replayed for years.

"It was crazy," Misiorowski said of reaching the Series. "It's everything I dreamed of."
