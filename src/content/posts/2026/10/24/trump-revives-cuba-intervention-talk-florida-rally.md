# Trump Revives Cuba Intervention Talk at Florida Rally

President Donald Trump revived the idea of possible U.S. military intervention in Cuba on Friday, telling a South Florida campaign rally that warships returning from the Iran war could stop off the island's coast — remarks that were followed within minutes by Secretary of State Marco Rubio urging Cubans to partner with the CIA.

According to [AP News](https://apnews.com/article/donald-trump-cuba-venezuela-florida-3b33aab6719558e289632e974c7a82ad), Trump made the remarks at a rally for candidates in Fort Myers, just days before the November 3 midterm elections and in an area where Cuban Americans are a significant voting bloc.

## What Trump Said

The remarks came not from Trump's prepared text but in response to a shouted question from the crowd about Cuba. "We'll do a little stopover," Trump said. "Actually, what we're going to do with Cuba is, we're going to come back from Iran. Those beautiful ships will pass Cuba. We'll have one of them stop about 50 yards off the coast. They'll raise up their hands. And then we're going to send (Secretary of State) Marco Rubio in to finish up the deal."

Trump had raised the idea earlier in 2026 but had not mentioned it in recent months before the rally.

The timing was notable. Voters have expressed concern about the economic fallout of the U.S. war on Iran — a conflict Trump has long forecast but which has not yet happened — and prices from gas to groceries have become a central concern ahead of the midterms.

## Rubio's Unusual Public Call

Moments after Trump spoke, Rubio posted on X that "now is the time" for action on Cuba, addressing his message "to those who seek to create positive change" and urging them "to seize this opportunity to create the change you desire through a discrete partnership with the CIA."

As [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/oct/23/marco-rubio-cuba-trump) noted, Rubio used "discrete" rather than "discreet" — apparently a mistake, though the substance of the message was a striking and unusually explicit public appeal.

The call was later amplified. According to [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/10/24/rubio-calls-on-cubans-to-partner-with-cia-to-create-positive-change), the CIA uploaded a video on X offering Spanish-language instructions for those who believe they have information of interest, encouraging them to visit a U.S. embassy in person if they can travel safely.

## Havana Mocks the Overture

Cuba's government responded not with alarm but with mockery. Deputy Foreign Minister Carlos Fernandez de Cossio wrote on X: "Now the United States is resorting to public recruitment for the CIA… There must be a great deal of desperation or misery within the agency for it to be forced to sell its services like a tube of toothpaste."

Fernandez de Cossio said last month that it would be an "atrocious international crime" for the U.S. to use military force against Cuba, describing it as a peaceful neighboring country.

## A Pattern of Escalating Pressure

The rally remarks are the latest turn in a pressure campaign that has been building all year. A U.S. fuel blockade on Cuba has been in place since January, following the U.S. military raid that captured former Venezuelan President Nicolás Maduro, and it has contributed to severe fuel shortages, blackouts, and an economic crisis on the island. [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/americas/trump-says-us-will-take-care-cuba-its-way-back-iran-2026-10-23/) reports that the Trump administration has stated its goal is to change Cuba's communist government.

In May, the administration announced criminal charges against former Cuban leader Raúl Castro, and Florida subsequently brought additional charges and, per Al Jazeera, issued an arrest warrant for him on October 19. Trump said in May that previous presidents had "considered intervening in Cuba" for decades but that "it looks like I'll be the one that does it."

Yet the administration has also sent mixed signals. In his address to the UN General Assembly last month, Trump said Rubio was "deep into negotiations with Cuba." Days later, he told reporters he anticipated the two countries "will make a deal," adding: "I don't think we'll need the military. Cuba is failing very badly." Earlier this week, the two countries clashed at the United Nations as Washington sought to build opposition to the annual vote condemning the decades-long U.S. embargo.

## Rhetoric or Intent?

The central question is whether these remarks reflect operational intent or are calibrated to a domestic political audience. Both readings have support.

On one hand, the remarks were extemporaneous, delivered in response to a shouted question at a campaign rally, and echo language Trump has used before without follow-through. On the other, Rubio's public call for Cubans to work with the CIA — and its amplification through an official agency video — is exceptionally explicit for a sitting secretary of state, suggesting active intelligence efforts rather than mere posturing.

Rights advocates have described the administration's threats as echoing an "imperialist approach," and UN rights experts have condemned the oil blockade as a violation of international law.

The Cuban-American community in South Florida is a politically potent bloc, making Cuba a resonant theme in the final stretch of a campaign dominated by economic anxiety. Whether the rhetoric translates into action — or was aimed chiefly at voters — may become clearer after November 3.

## What to Watch

Several questions remain unresolved: whether the administration takes any concrete military or intelligence steps after the remarks; how Florida voters respond at the ballot box; whether the CIA's public recruitment effort triggers a formal Cuban or international response; and what becomes of the reported U.S.–Cuba negotiations, which would presumably preempt any intervention. The trajectory of the Iran war — and the economic pain it is causing — will shape both the timing and the credibility of any move on Cuba.

For now, the episode underscores how Cuba has re-emerged as a live front in U.S. foreign policy, revived in a single exchange at a Florida rally.
