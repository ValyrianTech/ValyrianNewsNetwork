# Man Shot by ICE Says Agents Never Identified Themselves

A Dominican immigrant father of two shot in the neck by a federal immigration agent in New York City says the masked officers who confronted him never identified themselves as law enforcement, according to a new court petition that directly contradicts the Trump administration's account of the encounter.

The filing, submitted on behalf of 28-year-old Oscar Belgal in Brooklyn federal court on Oct. 14, offers the fullest picture yet of his version of the Oct. 8 shooting in the Marble Hill neighborhood — an incident that has become a flashpoint testing the limits of the relationship between Mayor Zohran Mamdani and President Donald Trump.

According to [Gothamist](https://gothamist.com/news/man-shot-by-ice-in-nycs-marble-hill-said-federal-officers-never-identified-themselves), Belgal did not know the men who ambushed and shot him were U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement agents until he woke up in a hospital bed. "The men did not identify themselves, show any documents or otherwise communicate with Oscar in any substantive way," the petition states.

## The Two Accounts

The competing narratives of the shooting diverge sharply.

Per the petition, Belgal had just picked up his 5-year-old son from school and taken him to McDonald's. He parked near his home and began pulling out when he realized the spot was in front of a fire hydrant. That is when an armed man in civilian clothing with a black face covering bolted toward his vehicle, followed by several other masked men with weapons drawn, per [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/oct/14/ice-shooting-new-york-city-oscar-belgal).

NYPD Commissioner Jessica Tisch said the city's surveillance footage did not capture the shooting itself, though police recovered at least seven shell casings at the scene and reported that no city officers were involved in the federal operation before shots were fired, according to an earlier [Gothamist](https://gothamist.com/news/person-shot-during-encounter-with-federal-agents-in-nycs-marble-hill-nypd-says) report.

One agent pointed a weapon inside the car and smashed the driver's-side window, glass exploding on Belgal's face and arm, according to the filing. The agent then fired at least seven shots into the vehicle while Belgal's son "cowered in the back seat." Belgal was struck once below his left ear.

The Department of Homeland Security tells a different story. Officials claim agents boxed in Belgal's vehicle, that he refused orders to exit, accelerated into two government vehicles and drove toward other officers — that he "weaponized his vehicle," in the words of Marcos Charles, the head of ICE's Enforcement and Removal Operations division. DHS says his arrest was justified because he was in the country without authorization.

The petition asserts that video footage "clearly shows" Belgal was trying to pull his car forward, where no officers were stationed, to escape the officer shooting into his vehicle, and that another car "careened from behind and blocked the path."

## Aftermath and Detention

After being shot, Belgal got out of the car and "pleaded for medical assistance, fearing that he was dying," the filing states. He alleges the officers threw him down, handcuffed him and left him slumped over as he faded in and out of consciousness — and that it was NYPD officers, not federal agents, who took him to receive care.

Belgal now remains detained at the Metropolitan Detention Center in Brooklyn. According to [CBS News New York](https://www.cbsnews.com/newyork/news/ice-agents-didnt-identify-themselves-marble-hill-shooting/), his attorneys say he is in urgent need of medical care, with the bullet still lodged in his neck.

"ICE officers shot him in the neck in front of his 5-year-old son, failed to provide him with immediate medical assistance, and now continue to hold him in a facility notorious for medical neglect," attorneys Perry McAninch and Elyssa Williams of The Legal Aid Society said in a statement. "With a bullet still lodged in his neck and his serious medical needs unmet, every additional day in custody puts him at further risk."

The Guardian reports that Belgal's lawyers say he has lost sensation on the left side of his face and has impaired hearing, and has yet to receive surgery to remove the bullet.

A federal judge last week barred his deportation and ordered that he not be transferred outside the New York City metro area or New Jersey while the court reviews the emergency petition. The government was given a Monday deadline to respond.

## A Test for Mamdani and Trump

The shooting has injected new strain into the unexpectedly cordial relationship between Mamdani, a 34-year-old democratic socialist, and Trump.

According to [City & State New York](https://www.cityandstateny.com/politics/2026/10/ice-shooting-tests-limits-mamdani-trump-relationship/416542/), Mamdani spent two hours on the phone with Trump and Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin seeking Belgal's release — negotiations that failed. The mayor said federal authorities then took Belgal out of the hospital and "whisked him away" in an unmarked vehicle.

At a press conference in Marble Hill, Mamdani demanded that ICE halt enforcement across the city. "This city wants ICE out," he said, per another [City & State New York](https://www.cityandstateny.com/politics/2026/10/mamdani-says-city-wants-ice-out-following-shooting/416521/) report. "There is no explanation for federal officers firing this many rounds into a car that has a 5-year-old."

Mullin, meanwhile, repeatedly characterized Belgal as "a thug" and the "worst of the worst," blaming the city's sanctuary policies for creating "an environment for criminal activity to thrive," and claiming the mayor's words condemning ICE are "dangerous and incite hostility."

Democratic strategist Trip Yang told City & State that access, not outcomes, may be the fair measure of the relationship. "Personal affection in politics often gets you access. It cannot guarantee results," he said.

## Competing Portraits

A separate narrative about Belgal has also taken hold. The [New York Post](https://nypost.com/2026/10/10/us-news/criminal-illegal-migrant-shot-by-ice-tormented-nyc-apartment-building-with-machete-neighbor-claims-menace-to-society/) reported that a former neighbor alleged Belgal once pulled a machete on another resident's nephew and called him "a menace to society." The Post also noted Belgal's criminal record: a 2023 robbery case in which he pleaded guilty to fourth-degree grand larceny and received five years' probation, and a pending 2025 drug-sale charge to which he has pleaded not guilty.

Belgal's attorneys say he "vehemently denies" any relationship to any gang. DHS and ICE officials have alleged he was part of the Trinitarios gang and was targeted for that reason.

Other Marble Hill residents offered a more mixed account. One told the Post she had "never heard anything like that about him," while another said he "was in the streets doing crazy things."

## What to Watch

The government faces a Monday deadline to respond to the petition, and a federal judge will weigh whether to order Belgal's release. The Bronx district attorney has opened an investigation into the agents' conduct, and none of the officers involved has been publicly named.

The episode also raises unresolved questions about how federal immigration operations are conducted in a city whose leaders have repeatedly clashed with the administration over enforcement. Whether the shooting reshapes — or merely strains — the Mamdani-Trump dynamic may become clearer in the days ahead.
