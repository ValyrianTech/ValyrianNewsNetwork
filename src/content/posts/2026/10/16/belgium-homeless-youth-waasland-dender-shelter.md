# Belgium: 104 Homeless Youths Sought Shelter in Two Years

More than 100 homeless young people sought shelter in the Waasland and Denderstreek regions of East Flanders over the past two years, but the organization tasked with helping them could start only about 30 housing trajectories because of limited capacity. The figures, released by youth homelessness organization Compañero Waas en Dender, highlight a growing housing crisis that is pushing vulnerable young Belgians out of the housing market.

According to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/10/16/companero-dakloze-jongeren-104-aanmeldingen-na-2-jaar-wooncrisis/), Compañero received 104 registrations from homeless or home-less young people aged 17 to 25 since it was founded about two years ago. The organization was able to start roughly 30 guidance trajectories, while in 74 other cases it could offer only "meaningful advice" or referrals to other services.

## 'We Don't Have Enough Resources'

"At least once a week we are called with the question of whether we can help someone. But we don't have enough resources for that," coordinator Tom Fruytier told VRT NWS. "In principle we can only start 12 trajectories a year, and that is far too little for such a large region."

Compañero Waas en Dender guides vulnerable young people between 17 and 25 who are homeless, home-less or at risk of becoming so. It is a project of CAW (the general welfare work centers) and vzw Lia. Each trajectory lasts about a year, with young people visited multiple times a week. Not everyone qualifies for help; the organization first tries to give advice or refer people elsewhere, starting a trajectory only when other care options are exhausted.

One of the young people helped was Quinten, 22, who approached the organization about a year ago. "I searched for a home of my own for a long time. I barely had hope left," he told VRT NWS. "At some moments I really just wanted to give up, but then I ended up at Compañero." He now lives in temporary housing and receives visits from the team three times a week. "It's one thing to stand on your own two feet, but you also have to be able to sustain it. You need help for that, and many young people don't know where to find it."

## Bumping Into the Housing Crisis

The difficulty of moving young people into permanent homes reflects a broader problem. "That's not easy, because we too run into the housing crisis," Fruytier said. "It's already difficult even for single people or young couples to find an affordable home. What must it be like for our young people?"

To bridge the gap, Compañero works with housing associations to use about ten vacant homes as transit housing, where young people can stay for three to six months. It is also building residential units in cooperation with the municipality of Hamme, where young people can live for about three years before moving to permanent accommodation.

Fruytier said the number of homeless young people is rising and that the help landscape is too fragmented for young people to navigate. "There are organizations, but they all have a different name. Sometimes I myself find it a tangle, let alone that young people can easily find their way in it."

## A Broader Flemish Picture

The local figures fit into a larger pattern. According to Flemish government agency [Opgroeien](https://www.opgroeien.be/kennis/themas/dak-en-thuisloosheid-aanpakken-bij-jongvolwassenen/de-belangrijkste-cijfers-over-dak-en-thuisloosheid), an estimated 21,428 people are homeless or home-less in Flanders, including 14,822 adults and 6,606 children. One in three is under 18. Between one in five and one in four of all counted people is aged 16 to 25.

Most do not sleep on the street. According to [Opgroeien](https://www.opgroeien.be/kennis/themas/dak-en-thuisloosheid-aanpakken-bij-jongvolwassenen/profiel-van-dak-en-thuisloze-jongeren), 45.9 percent of homeless young adults live in "hidden homelessness," staying temporarily with friends or family, compared with 30.8 percent of those over 26. Family or network conflict is the direct cause in 37.4 percent of cases among young adults. The agency identifies three main profiles: young people leaving youth care (24.2 percent), Belgian young adults without a care history (29.4 percent), and newcomers, mostly without Belgian nationality and often with a migration or flight history (30.4 percent).

The problem has drawn political attention. In February 2026, Flemish ministers Caroline Gennez (Welfare and Poverty Reduction) and Hans Bonte (Housing) presented a homelessness action plan for 2026-2029. As detailed by [Vooruit](https://nieuws.vooruit.org/minister-gennez-en-bonte-komen-met-plan-om-dak--en-thuisloosheid-aan-te-pakken-iedereen-verdient-een-dak-boven-zijn-hoofd), the plan rests on five pillars: prevention, sufficient sustainable housing, extra attention for young people leaving youth care, intensive multidisciplinary care, and emergency shelter. The government has committed to 56,000 additional social homes and 10.2 million euros for 154 emergency homes. The underlying decision was [recorded](https://www.vlaanderen.be/vlaamse-regering/beslissingen-van-de-vlaamse-regering/actieplan-ter-voorkoming-en-bestrijding-van-dak-en-thuisloosheid-voor-de-periode-2026-2029) by the Flemish government on 13 February 2026.

## What's Next

A new regional count was underway on the day the figures were published, and Fruytier expected the numbers to rise further. "These are harsh figures. The number of young people who are homeless is increasing," he said. "I fear the figures will shoot up again."

The episode underscores a persistent gap between policy ambitions and on-the-ground capacity. Even with a provincial action plan in place, organizations such as Compañero remain strained, and the young people they serve continue to face a housing market that is out of reach. For Quinten, the goal is modest but elusive for many of his peers: "I'm not asking for more. I just want to lead a simple life, have work and save some money."
