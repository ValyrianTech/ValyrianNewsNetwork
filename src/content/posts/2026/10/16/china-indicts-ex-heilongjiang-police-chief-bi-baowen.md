# China Indicts Ex-Heilongjiang Police Chief Bi Baowen

Chinese prosecutors have formally indicted Bi Baowen, a former vice governor of Heilongjiang province and longtime head of its public security department, on charges of accepting bribes and using his influence to accept bribes, the Supreme People's Procuratorate announced on October 16.

The case, investigated to conclusion by the National Supervisory Commission, has been filed with the Shenyang Intermediate People's Court in Liaoning province -- a designated jurisdiction outside the region where the alleged crimes occurred, a practice Chinese authorities use to limit local interference. According to [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/legal/20261016/91fd8164c61241799499807975d6ab49/c.html), the procuratorate informed Bi of his litigation rights, interrogated him, and heard the opinions of his defense counsel during the review stage.

## Who Bi Baowen Is

Bi, 66, spent four decades in China's public security apparatus, beginning in the Shandong Provincial Public Security Department and rising through posts in Rizhao and Binzhou before becoming deputy director of the Shandong department in 2006. He was transferred across provincial lines in 2016 to lead the Heilongjiang Provincial Public Security Department, and in 2017 took on the concurrent role of vice governor, according to [Dazhong News](https://m.dzplus.dzng.com/share/general/0/NEWS2917084RASCOIHGCXGYE). He stepped down from both posts in August 2020 and was placed under investigation in November 2025, roughly five years into his retirement.

## The Charges

Prosecutors allege that while holding a succession of senior policing posts in Shandong and Heilongjiang, Bi used his position to secure benefits for others and exploited the conveniences of his office to channel improper benefits through the official acts of other state functionaries, illegally accepting property. The indictment characterizes the amount accepted during his active career as "especially huge" (特别巨大).

After retirement, prosecutors say, Bi continued to leverage the influence of his former Shandong posts to obtain improper benefits for others through other officials' acts, accepting property in an amount described as "huge" (巨大). Under a joint judicial interpretation by China's top court and procuratorate, bribery of RMB 3 million or more constitutes an "especially huge" amount -- a threshold carrying a potential sentence of ten years to life imprisonment, or death, plus fines or asset confiscation, as noted by [Sohu](https://www.sohu.com/a/1088438530_122014422).

## The Disciplinary Record

Bi's prosecution follows his expulsion from the Communist Party on May 22, 2026, after a review by the Central Commission for Discipline Inspection and the National Supervisory Commission. That notice, carried by [Qianlong](https://dangjian.qianlong.com/2026/0522/8672086.shtml), accused him of losing his ideals, accepting banquets that could compromise impartial duty, securing benefits for others in cadre appointments, and tolerating relatives who used his authority for private gain.

Among the more unusual findings: Bi was cited for "irregularly issuing motor vehicle license plates" and, in the commission's words, for being "greedy and corrupt, enforcing the law while breaking it" -- turning police authority into a tool for personal gain in case handling and project contracting. The notice stated he did not "converge or stop" after the 18th Party Congress, a standard aggravating characterization in Chinese disciplinary language.

## A Pattern in Heilongjiang

Bi's case is the second involving a current or former provincial police chief in 2025, after Ye Hanbing, Sichuan's vice governor and public security director, was investigated on May 21. Closer to home, Bi's former subordinate Deng Chi -- once head of the Heilongjiang department's science and technology division and later a deputy director of the department -- was placed under investigation in August 2025 while in office and expelled from both the Party and public office in February 2026, according to [China Youth Daily](https://c.m.163.com/news/a/KLNVGGD80514CDBK.html).

The two investigations unfolded while the Central Third Inspection Team conducted a routine two-and-a-half-month inspection of Heilongjiang beginning in July 2025 -- a coincidence that Chinese anti-corruption observers often read as a signal of intensified scrutiny of a province or sector.

## What to Watch

No trial date has been announced, and the exact sums involved remain undisclosed beyond the legal categories. The "especially huge" characterization points to a potentially severe outcome: comparable corruption cases with the same designation have drawn sentences ranging from long fixed terms to death with reprieve.

For Beijing, the case reinforces the sustained tempo of its anti-corruption campaign, particularly within the public security system, where officials are entrusted with enforcing the law they are accused of subverting. For Heilongjiang, it leaves open the question of how far the fallout from Bi's tenure -- and the provincial apparatus he once led -- will extend.
