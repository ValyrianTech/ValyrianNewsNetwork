# Oregon School Shooting Kills 3 Children, Teacher in Bend

A 16-year-old gunman opened fire at a Bend, Oregon elementary school on Thursday afternoon, killing three children and a teacher before an armed community member pursued him into the building and shot him dead, authorities said. Two more victims — a 7-year-old child and a 54-year-old teacher — were hospitalized in critical condition, and the attack sent a tight-knit city of about 100,000 people into mourning.

According to [Oregon Public Broadcasting](https://www.opb.org/article/2026/10/15/bend-school-active-shooter/), the first 911 call came at 12:59 p.m. from a person who reported being shot at multiple times near Silver Rail Elementary School. A dozen more calls followed within minutes, reporting that a shooter had entered the building.

## How the Attack Unfolded

The school went into lockdown at 1:01 p.m., and the first officers arrived at 1:02 p.m., Bend Police Chief Mike Krantz said at a Thursday evening press conference. By then, someone else had already intervened.

"Moments later, an officer reported that the suspect had been shot by a community member who had seen the suspect enter the school, and pursued him into the school and engaged him," Krantz said. Police said the gunman forced entry by firing at locked doors and gates. The bystander's identity has not been publicly released.

Officials confirmed the deceased children were ages 5, 8, and 11. A teacher was also killed, and the suspect died at the scene — bringing the total death toll to five. Authorities have not released the victims' names, and the shooter has not been publicly identified beyond being a 16-year-old Bend resident.

Silver Rail Elementary serves kindergarten through fifth grade, with roughly 450 students and 40 staff members, according to [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/oct/15/oregon-elementary-school-shooting). It is part of the Bend-La Pine Schools district, the sixth-largest in Oregon. Children were bused to Journey Church on South Highway 97, which served as a family reunification center.

## A City Gathers to Grieve

By evening, hundreds of residents had filled Drake Park's amphitheater for a candlelight prayer vigil led by local clergy, [OPB reported](https://www.opb.org/article/2026/10/15/bend-reels-after-school-shooting/). People sang hymns and huddled together as darkness fell over the park.

Kevin Desrosiers, who attended with his two daughters, said he knew the grandparents and parents of the 5-year-old who died. "They're a great local family, been here for years," he said, choking back tears. "You never want to say goodbye to your kids in the morning and even assume that you wouldn't see them again at the end of the school day."

Others grappled with the violence in a place many described as safe. "You never think that something like this would happen in your hometown where you live," said Kyra Peterson, a Bend resident and new mother, who noted that family members had been working at a local Safeway during a 2022 deadly shooting there. "It's just very shocking."

Payton Stewart, a student at Mountain View High School, said school-shooting threats have become a normalized part of life for her generation. "Every kid has to have a conversation that their parents have to talk to them, 'Hey this might happen,'" she said. "We have to have drills about it."

## Officials Respond

Oregon Governor Tina Kotek praised the bystander who intervened, addressing the person directly. "Your actions carry a heavy emotional burden," she said. In a [statement](https://www.independent.co.uk/news/world/americas/crime/bend-oregon-school-shooting-gunman-identity-updates-b3067805.html) posted on X, Kotek wrote: "As we learn more about the tragic event at Silver Rail Elementary in Bend today, we must come together and support each other, neighbor to neighbor, Oregonian to Oregonian. No community should have to live through this day."

Bend-La Pine Schools Superintendent Steven Cook offered a message to families. "Please hold your children, hold your loved ones tight tonight. Let them talk. Let them cry," he said. "We will get through this — but not yet. We must grieve for the loss of our community members."

Bend Mayor Melanie Kebler said she was "struggling to make sense of a loss that feels unimaginable," adding that "a school should be a safe place where children can learn and feel secure." FBI Director Kash Patel confirmed that the bureau's Portland field office is assisting local authorities, who are being led by Bend Police with support from Oregon State Police.

Former congresswoman Gabrielle Giffords, a gun-control advocate who survived a 2011 mass shooting, said she was "heartbroken." "This cannot continue to be a reality in our country," she said. "Leaders at every level must step up and act."

## What's Still Unknown

Key questions remain unanswered. Police have not provided a motive or confirmed whether the shooter had any ties to the school. "This is going to be a long investigation," Krantz said.

Officials initially described the weapon as a "long gun," a category that includes rifles and shotguns. A law enforcement official briefed on the shooting told the Associated Press, speaking on condition of anonymity, that the attacker used a pump-action shotgun — a detail that has not been officially confirmed. The weapon's origin also remains unclear; unnamed law enforcement sources told CBS News that the gun was purchased by the shooter's mother, according to The Independent, though this has not been verified by officials.

OPB public safety reporter Troy Brynelson noted the gaps in the public record, telling [OPB's Morning Edition](https://www.opb.org/article/2026/10/16/oregon-school-shooting-bend-silver-rail/) that police had not yet identified the shooter or explained "what cause or motivation maybe led to the attack."

## A Recurring American Tragedy

The shooting is the latest in a grim pattern of gun violence on U.S. school grounds. According to Everytown for Gun Safety, there have been at least 100 incidents of gunfire at schools across the country in 2026, resulting in 33 deaths, The Guardian reported.

The attack unfolded in a state with some of the nation's tightest gun laws. Oregon requires gun owners to undergo a criminal background check and safety training before being approved for a firearm permit — a measure [upheld by the Oregon Court of Appeals](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2025/mar/12/oregon-appeals-court-rules-gun-control-law) in March 2025. At the Drake Park vigil, some attendees called on authorities to further harden those laws, and state Democratic lawmakers renewed calls for action.

"Once again we must ask how many schools must be shot up before Republicans agree to act to curb gun violence," Senator Ron Wyden wrote on X, as reported by The Independent.

## What to Watch

All 32 schools in the Bend-La Pine district canceled classes, sports, and extracurricular activities on Friday, and Oregon State University-Cascades and Central Oregon Community College campuses also closed. Authorities are expected to release further details as the investigation proceeds, including the identities of the victims and the shooter.

For now, a community known for being "not too small, not too big" — where, as resident Gracie Cromie put it, "every time you go out you're guaranteed to run into someone" — is left to grieve. "There is real evil in this world," Cromie said at the vigil. "But that doesn't mean that God isn't with us in these times."
