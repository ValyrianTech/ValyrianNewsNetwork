# China to Host Global Governance Forum in Xiongan

Organizers announced on October 16 that the 2026 Global Governance Forum will be held October 23–25 in Xiongan New Area, Hebei Province, bringing together participants from nearly 100 countries to discuss the reform and improvement of the global governance system. The forum, hosted by the Chinese People's Institute of Foreign Affairs (CPIFA), carries the theme "Rebuilding Confidence and Cooperation: The Centennial Changes and Reforming and Improving Global Governance," according to [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/politics/20261016/d32e083781474dfb83a57fbbcdef68fa/c.html).

## A Platform for Broad International Dialogue

According to the announcement, the forum will invite figures from strategic, political, academic, business and media circles, as well as young leaders, with particular emphasis on leading voices in United Nations affairs, climate change, artificial intelligence and multilateral trade. Confirmed foreign guests come from the Global South as well as Europe and the Americas. Roughly 70 percent are scholars, experts and notable figures, while about 30 percent are political figures.

A distinctive feature of the program is a field tour: participating foreign delegates and Chinese and international media will visit Xiongan New Area to experience firsthand what officials describe as a "future city." The CPIFA, which is hosting the event, is a Chinese people's organization specializing in people-to-people and public diplomacy rather than a formal government ministry.

## Rooted in China's Global Governance Initiative

The forum is positioned as a flagship platform for advancing the Global Governance Initiative that President Xi Jinping proposed on September 1, 2025, at the "SCO+" meeting in Tianjin. As the [Xi Jinping Thought on Diplomacy Research Center](https://cn.chinadiplomacy.org.cn/gdi/2025-09/11/content_118073013.shtml) explained, the initiative rests on five principles: sovereign equality, observance of international rule of law, multilateralism, a people-centered approach and action-oriented pragmatism.

It is the fourth in China's series of global initiatives, following the Global Development Initiative, the Global Security Initiative and the Global Civilization Initiative — together addressing the dimensions of development, security, civilization and governance.

China's ambassador to Belarus, Zhang Wenchuan, described the forum's purpose in a feature published in the Belarusian newspaper Minskaya Pravda and carried by the [Chinese Embassy in Belarus](https://by.china-embassy.gov.cn/sssgxwdt/202609/t20260917_12024405.htm). "The upcoming Global Governance Forum in Xiongan will focus on discussing concrete measures for implementing the Global Governance Initiative," Zhang said. He added that the initiative "upholds openness and inclusiveness, does not pursue closure or exclusion, and does not advocate confrontation or gaming."

## Why Xiongan?

Holding the forum in Xiongan is a deliberate choice. The new area is China's flagship national project to build a demonstration zone for modern development strategy, innovation, green growth and high-quality urbanization. For international participants, the field tour is intended to link the abstract language of governance reform to a visible model of China's development achievements.

The announcement follows earlier signals that the event was coming. At the 2026 Lujiazui Forum in June, officials indicated that China would host the first "Xiongan Global Governance Forum" in autumn 2026, inviting global figures to discuss building what they called a more just and reasonable global governance system.

## Broader Momentum — and Open Questions

Beijing portrays the initiative as part of a widening base of support. According to the embassy feature, roughly 160 countries and international organizations have expressed backing, and more than 60 countries have joined the "Friends of Global Governance Group." China has also rolled out nine areas of governance reform measures and established new platforms including the International Mediation Institute, the World Data Organization and the World AI Cooperation Organization.

Officials frame the initiative as complementary to the existing international system rather than a rival to it. Ambassador Zhang said China's approach "does not advocate confrontation or gaming," and the CPIFA has presented the forum as an open, inclusive public platform welcoming countries at different stages of development and with different political systems.

Still, the event arrives amid intensifying geopolitical competition over the rules and institutions of global governance. How the forum balances calls for reform with the interests of established powers — and whether it produces concrete commitments rather than declarations — will be closely watched. The composition of the guest list, its heavy weighting toward the Global South, and the decision to stage the event in a project city designed to showcase China's development model all signal Beijing's intent to tie its governance agenda to its own modernizing vision.

With the forum just a week away, attention now turns to what participating governments and experts will actually agree to — and whether the gathering can translate the theme of "rebuilding confidence and cooperation" into outcomes that outlast the three-day meeting.
