# Wang Yi Pushes Asia Security Vision at Baku CICA Summit

Chinese Foreign Minister Wang Yi used the 7th Summit of the Conference on Interaction and Confidence-Building Measures in Asia (CICA) in Baku on 15 October to press Beijing's case for a regional security order built by Asian states themselves, as the forum expanded its membership and issued a new declaration on peace, security and cooperation across the continent.

Wang, attending as Special Representative of President Xi Jinping, delivered a speech setting out a four-point proposal for regional cooperation and describing CICA as a platform "independently initiated and jointly built by Asian countries," according to the [People's Daily](http://politics.people.com.cn/n1/2026/1016/c1001-40815606.html), which carried the full text of his remarks as filed by Xinhua from Baku.

## A Four-Point Proposal for Asia

In his address, Wang put forward four priorities for CICA members: jointly safeguarding peace and stability, jointly promoting prosperity and revitalization, jointly advancing mutual learning among civilizations, and jointly improving global governance.

He framed the moment as a pivot point for the region. "The people of Asia love peace the most and desire development the most," Wang said. "Facing the accelerated evolution of changes unseen in a century, only by establishing a sense of a community with a shared future, upholding what is historically right, and adhering to unity and cooperation can Asian countries ride the tide, turn crises into opportunities, and jointly create a better future."

Wang also praised Azerbaijan's chairmanship, saying China "highly evaluates Azerbaijan's efforts during its chairmanship to advance CICA cooperation and build a 'stronger Asia,'" and pledged that Beijing would "continue to strongly support and actively participate in the CICA process." He linked China's domestic agenda to the region's stability, noting that 2026 marks the opening year of the country's 15th Five-Year Plan and that China would "firmly follow the path of peaceful development, provide more stability to the international situation through its own high-quality development, and provide a stronger driving force for global growth."

## Summit Outcomes: New Members and a New Declaration

The summit concluded with the adoption of the Baku Declaration and a Statement on Occupational Safety and Health, according to Azerbaijan's Ministry of Foreign Affairs, as [reported](https://report.az/en/foreign-politics/azerbaijan-mfa-highlights-key-outcomes-of-7th-cica-summit) by Report.az. The Chinese readout described the outcome document as the "Declaration of the 7th CICA Summit."

The gathering also delivered institutional changes. Laos was admitted as a full member, bringing CICA to 29 member states, and Azerbaijan's chairmanship was extended through 2028. Ambassador Sultan Hajiyev was appointed CICA Secretary General for 2026–2030, and Cambodia's ratification completed the process for the Convention on the Privileges and Immunities of the CICA Secretariat to enter into force.

"Azerbaijan remains committed to strengthening CICA as a platform for dialogue, confidence-building and collective action towards a more peaceful and prosperous Asia," the Azerbaijani foreign ministry said in a post on X.

## A Venue for Eurasia and Middle East Diplomacy

The Baku summit doubled as a hub for bilateral talks on some of the world's most sensitive conflicts. On the sidelines, Wang met separately with Israeli President Isaac Herzog, Russian Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov, Bahraini Foreign Minister Abdullatif bin Rashid Al Zayani, Turkish Foreign Minister Hakan Fidan and the Palestinian foreign minister, according to the People's Daily readout.

The Wang–Lavrov meeting, held a day before the summit, covered CICA cooperation as well as bilateral relations and the international agenda, with the two sides also discussing relations with the United States, the Russia–Ukraine conflict and the conflict involving Iran, according to [Report.az](https://report.az/en/other-countries/lavrov-wang-yi-discuss-cica-cooperation-during-meeting-in-baku), citing Russia's foreign ministry.

Other regional leaders were also in Baku. Tajikistan's President Emomali Rahmon attended the summit, where regional security and economic cooperation topped the agenda, [Asia-Plus](https://asiaplus.news/en/2026/10/15/emomali-rahmon-to-attend-cica-summit-in-baku/) reported, noting that the summit was also expected to address transport, digitalization and the organization's internal effectiveness.

## Türkiye and the Push for Institutional Depth

Türkiye, one of CICA's most active members, welcomed the Baku Declaration and the admission of Laos, [TRT World](https://www.trtworld.com/article/7056e8e1cb24) reported. In a statement, the Turkish foreign ministry said member states had "once again reaffirmed their commitment to preserving peace, prosperity and stability in Asia, in a spirit of common cooperation based on regional ownership."

Ankara also welcomed the extension of Azerbaijan's chairmanship for 2026–2028, saying it would help ensure institutional continuity as CICA moves toward becoming a fully fledged international organization. It thanked outgoing Secretary General Kairat Sarybay for his leadership and wished success to his successor, Hajiyev.

## What CICA Is — and Why It Matters

CICA is an inter-governmental forum founded on the idea that security in Asia is indivisible and that small and large states should interact on mutually beneficial terms, with its secretariat based in Astana, Kazakhstan. The process was first proposed by Kazakh President Nursultan Nazarbayev at the United Nations General Assembly in 1992, and its first summit was held in Almaty in 2002, where the Almaty Act charter was adopted, according to [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conference_on_Interaction_and_Confidence-Building_Measures_in_Asia). Counter-terrorism became a central theme after the 11 September 2001 attacks.

The forum's chairmanship has rotated among Kazakhstan, Türkiye, China, Tajikistan, Kazakhstan again, and now Azerbaijan, which took the helm in 2024. In 2014, China hosted the Shanghai Summit, where Xi Jinping announced the "New Asian Security Concept," whose core premise held that "Asian issues should be taken care of by Asians, and Asian security should be maintained by Asians." Beijing's continued emphasis on CICA reflects that outlook.

## Analysis: A Platform in Search of Weight

CICA's value to Beijing lies less in binding commitments than in framing. The forum gives China a multilateral setting to argue that Asian security should be organized regionally rather than through Western-led alliances — a theme Wang's remarks echoed by invoking the Five Principles of Peaceful Coexistence and multilateralism. The decision to keep the chairmanship in Baku through 2028, alongside a four-year term for a new secretary general, signals a modest institutionalization aimed at giving the body greater continuity and weight.

Yet CICA remains a consensus-based forum with a broad and politically varied membership — from Israel and Türkiye to Iran, Russia, India and China — and little enforcement power. Its expansion to 29 members, while symbolically significant, does not resolve the competition among its members over the region's security architecture. That internal diversity is precisely why the summit's value often lies in the bilateral meetings on its margins, from Baku to the next host city.

## What to Watch

With the Baku Declaration adopted and the chairmanship secured through 2028, attention now turns to implementation. Two questions will shape CICA's trajectory: whether its new connectivity and confidence-building mechanisms translate into concrete cooperation, and whether the forum can convert its widening membership into genuine diplomatic leverage. For China, the test will be whether its rhetoric about a "shared future" for Asia can coexist with the strategic competition that continues to define the region.
