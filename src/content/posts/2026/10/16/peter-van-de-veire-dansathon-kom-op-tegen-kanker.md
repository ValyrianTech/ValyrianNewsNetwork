# Van de Veire Dances 24 Hours for Cancer Charity

Radio 2 presenter Peter Van de Veire began a 24-hour non-stop dance marathon on Friday morning in the East Flemish town of Zottegem, raising funds for Kom op tegen Kanker, Flanders' leading cancer charity. The third edition of the Radio 2 Dansathon started at 11:00 and runs until 11:00 on Saturday, with Van de Veire presenting live radio and dancing throughout.

For the first time, the event is being held outdoors in a large dance tent on the Markt, Zottegem's central market square, rather than in an indoor venue. According to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/kijk/2026/10/16/video-dansathon-radio-2-peter-van-de-veire-kom-op-tegen-kanker/), listeners of Radio 2 can request their favourite dance songs during the broadcast, and the challenge concludes on Saturday at 11:00.

## A Cause Close to the Presenter

Van de Veire has spoken candidly about why he returns to the event year after year. In a [VRT press release](https://www.vrt.be/nl/over-ons/nieuws-over-vrt/radio-2-dansathon-peter-van-de-veire-danst-opnieuw-24-uur-tegen-kanker), he dedicated this edition to a friend who died of cancer.

\"Geert from Eeklo was 53 when he died of cancer,\" Van de Veire said. \"Geert was a lovely friend full of energy, who loved music and Life itself. But Geert will never turn 54, however hard he tried to recover. So for all the Geerts in the world, I happily dance for another 24 hours.\"

Writing on the Kom op tegen Kanker [blog](https://www.komoptegenkanker.be/blog/derde-keer-dansen-met-peter-van-de-veire), he reflected on the emotional weight of the broadcast. \"Each year, people tell us during the show that they are on the mend. Scientists announce new breakthroughs. Sadly, we also hear testimonies of loved ones who did not make it. Those hit hard,\" he said. He noted that the closing dances of previous editions were especially meaningful: in 2024 with his mother and in 2025 with his mother-in-law, both of whom had had cancer and are now doing well.

## How the Fundraising Works

Listeners can request a song via the website radio2dansathon.komoptegenkanker.be, optionally attaching a dedication or personal story. During the event, sending an SMS with a song request to 4342 donates one euro to Kom op tegen Kanker, and larger voluntary donations are also welcome. Proceeds support research into more effective treatments, cancer prevention, and better care for patients.

The event is broadcast live on Radio 2 and streamed via VRT MAX. The public is welcome to dance along on the Markt; if the tent fills up, an outdoor screen allows visitors to follow proceedings. Local clubs, schools, traders, the hospital, and volunteers are all involved, with Zottegem Winkelcentrum vzw running a bar for the duration.

David Vansteenbrugge, general director of Kom op tegen Kanker, framed the initiative as part of a broader movement. \"Music has the special power to connect people and to set them literally in motion,\" he said in the VRT release. \"Every step, every story, and every donation contributes to the same goal: a world in which we no longer lose anyone prematurely to cancer.\"

## A Growing Event

The Dansathon has expanded since its debut. The first edition in 2024 took place in a VRT studio in Brussels and raised 25,148 euro, while the 2025 edition at SingCity in Gent brought in 48,210 euro, according to the local outlet [NUUS.be](https://www.nuus.be/2026/10/13/zottegem-24-uur-radio-2-dansathon-tegen-kanker/). As [Radiotrefpunt](https://www.radiotrefpunt.nl/entry/9133-peter-van-de-veire-danst-24-uur-en-haalt-48210-euro-op-voor-kom-op-tegen-kanker/) reported, the 2025 event drew attention from across the country, with appearances by well-known figures and the closing dance performed by Van de Veire's mother-in-law.

Hosting the event carries a cost for the town. Zottegem has budgeted 30,000 euro for the occasion, including an 18,150 euro buy-out fee for cooperation with VRT and 5,100 euro for tent rental, with the remainder covering catering and security, NUUS.be noted.

## Why It Matters

Cancer remains a heavy burden in Flanders. On average, 130 people receive a cancer diagnosis every day, amounting to more than 47,341 new patients a year, or more than five per hour. The Dansathon's dual purpose is to raise money and to give a public platform to the stories of those affected.

The event arrived a day after Dag tegen Kanker (Day Against Cancer). A warm-up broadcast of the programme Oh la la, presented by Radio 2's Kim Debrie, was held on the Markt on Thursday evening with a performance and coincided with that annual observance.

## What's Next

Van de Veire is expected to complete his 24-hour stint on Saturday morning, when he will finally take off his dancing shoes. The final total raised will be announced at the conclusion of the broadcast. Whether the event returns for a fourth edition, and whether it will once again be held outdoors, remains to be seen, but organisers and the host town have framed the 2026 Dansathon as an affirmation of solidarity with everyone touched by cancer.

As the town of Zottegem put it in its [announcement](https://www.visit-zottegem.be/over-zottegem-en-haar-bestuur/publicaties/nieuws/zottegem-verwelkomt-radio-2-dansathon), the event brings one of Flanders' warmest and most unifying occasions to its historic Markt — a fitting setting, the city said, for a warm and welcoming town.
