# Cold Wave to Peak as Northern China Braces for Rare Snow

A powerful cold wave sweeping across China will drive temperatures to new seasonal lows and bring an unusually intense burst of rain and snow to the north, with the heaviest precipitation expected on October 17, meteorological authorities warned. According to [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/politics/20261016/1dcc363d63e44e3dba6a921896fc768c/c.html), the cold air will move west to east across most regions north of the middle and lower Yangtze River from October 16 to 19, pulling maximum temperatures down by 4°C to 8°C—and by more than 10°C in parts of western Gansu, central and eastern Inner Mongolia, and eastern Northeast China.

## Record Lows Across Major Cities

The temperature drop will redraw the country's weather map. Beijing, Taiyuan, Tianjin, Shijiazhuang, Lanzhou, Yinchuan, Urumqi, and Jinan are all expected to see their highest temperatures for the second half of the year replaced. Urumqi's high on October 16 was just 5°C, while Beijing, Taiyuan, and Shijiazhuang are forecast to fall to 16°C to 17°C on October 17.

Around October 19, the 10°C isotherm will push south to the Huanghuai region, and the 0°C line will reach northern North China and central Northwest China. Urumqi, Xining, Yinchuan, and Taiyuan are set to see lows at or below freezing for the first time this half-year, signaling the approach of meteorological winter for many northern areas. A second cold front is then forecast to sweep south from October 20 to 22, reaching the Jiangnan region, where Nanjing, Shanghai, and Hefei may also set new seasonal lows.

## Rare October Rainfall and Snow

The cold air is colliding with an unusually vigorous flow of warm, moist air, producing an October precipitation event that forecasters describe as rare for the time of year. "This round may be rare for the same period in terms of its scope, accumulated precipitation, and single-day intensity," China Weather Network analyst Lin Nan said, as quoted by [CCTV](https://news.cctv.com/2026/10/16/ARTILxfmTTI2znWYl2PAfoXS261016.shtml).

On October 16, snow and sleet developed across southern Xinjiang, eastern Tianshan, the Hexi region of Gansu, Qinghai, and northern and eastern Tibet, with heavy snow or blizzards at high elevations. Moderate to heavy rain fell across eastern Gansu, Ningxia, Shaanxi, Shanxi, Henan, Hubei, and northern Chongqing, with local rainstorms.

October 17 is expected to be the strongest phase of the event. Moderate to heavy snow or sleet—locally blizzard or severe blizzard—is forecast for northeastern Inner Mongolia and northwestern Heilongjiang. Heavy rain is expected across central and eastern Inner Mongolia, the Northeast, North China, Huanghuai, and Jianghuai, with local severe downpours in western and southern Liaoning, northeastern Hebei, the western mountains of Beijing, and parts of Shandong, Henan, Anhui, Hubei, and Hunan.

Lin Nan attributed the intensity to an active pattern of cold fronts converging with warm, moist air, compounded by a subtropical high that is strengthening out of season and running stronger than normal. "The warm, moist flow is vigorous, comparable to summer, transporting large amounts of water vapor northward," she said.

## Travel and Safety Warnings

Authorities urged residents to prepare for sharply colder and wetter conditions. The China Weather Network warned that rain and snow could make roads slippery and cause icing, disrupting travel, and noted that rainfall in some northern areas could break historical extremes. The agency advised the public to stay away from mountains, river valleys, and low-lying areas vulnerable to heavy rain or severe convective weather, as reported by [China News](https://www.chinanews.com.cn/sh/2026/10-15/10715011.shtml).

Coastal regions face an additional hazard. As a Huanghuai low-pressure system develops and moves out to sea, winds will strengthen markedly, especially on October 17, with gusts of 7 to 9 on the Beaufort scale possible over the Bohai Sea, the northern Yellow Sea, and the coasts of Shandong and Liaoning.

## A Double-Edged Event for Agriculture

The rainfall arrives during a critical window for autumn harvest and winter wheat planting, making it both a benefit and a risk. Precipitation across the winter wheat zone has been notably below normal since September, and the incoming rain is expected to improve soil moisture and support later wheat sowing, as well as to ease a meteorological drought in Shandong and Jiangsu.

At the same time, unharvested fields in North and Northeast China need accelerated harvesting, and harvested autumn grain should be covered promptly to prevent mold, authorities said. The heavy snow forecast for northeastern Inner Mongolia and northwestern Heilongjiang is expected to leave significant accumulation that could affect transport and facility agriculture.

## What to Watch

The first cold front is expected to weaken after October 18, though snow will persist in northeastern Inner Mongolia and northwestern Heilongjiang and moderate to heavy rain will continue along the middle and lower Yangtze. The key question will be whether a second front from October 20 to 22 pushes the cold further south into Jiangnan. For now, the message from forecasters is clear: northern China should brace for a sharp, early taste of winter—and for rainfall that would be notable even in summer.
