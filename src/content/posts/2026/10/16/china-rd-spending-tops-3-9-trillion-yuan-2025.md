# China R&D Spending Tops 3.9 Trillion Yuan in 2025

China's gross expenditure on Research and Experimental Development (R&D) surpassed 3.9 trillion yuan in 2025, reaching 3.92882 trillion yuan (about $550 billion), an increase of 8.2 percent year-on-year, according to figures released jointly on October 16 by the National Bureau of Statistics (NBS), the Ministry of Science and Technology, and the Ministry of Finance. The milestone, detailed in the *2025 National Science and Technology Funding Statistical Bulletin*, underscores Beijing's sustained push toward technological self-reliance and its ambition to lead in fields such as semiconductors, artificial intelligence, and green energy.

## A Symbolic Threshold Crossed

The most striking feature of the 2025 data is the rise in R&D intensity — the ratio of R&D spending to GDP — which climbed to 2.80 percent, up 0.11 percentage points from 2024. According to [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/tech/20261016/e4e8478a62b740939dbe16079e547440/c.html), this marks the first time China's research intensity has exceeded the average of the Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD) nations, which stands at roughly 2.72 percent.

In absolute terms, China remains the world's second-largest R&D spender behind the United States. The full statistical bulletin, [published](https://www.news.cn/tech/20261016/a64b7dff6375473390370f0f97276ad5/c.html) by the three government bodies, notes that China's total is 3.4 times that of Germany (ranked third) and 3.5 times that of Japan (ranked fourth). On R&D intensity, China ranks 12th globally.

## A Five-Year Sprint

The figures cap a period of rapid growth under China's 14th Five-Year Plan (2021–2025), during which R&D spending expanded at an average annual rate of 10.0 percent. As [Yicai](https://www.yicai.com/brief/103396054.html) reported, that pace outpaces the comparable averages of the United States (8.4 percent), Germany (6.2 percent), France (4.8 percent), Japan (5.8 percent), and South Korea (8.9 percent), placing China at the front of the world's major economies.

Per-capita R&D funding, measured by full-time-equivalent researchers, rose to 487,000 yuan, an increase of 7,000 yuan.

## Basic Research: Closing the "Chokepoint" Gap

Perhaps the most strategically significant number in the bulletin is the growth of basic research funding, which rose 13.3 percent to 283.46 billion yuan — 5.1 percentage points faster than overall R&D growth. Basic research's share of total R&D edged up to 7.21 percent, an increase of 0.33 percentage points, and China now ranks second globally in absolute basic research spending, behind only the United States.

Chinese officials have long acknowledged that the country's vulnerability in "chokepoint" (卡脖子) technologies — advanced semiconductors, precision instruments, and other critical inputs — stems from insufficient investment in foundational science. The NBS statistician Luo Qiushi framed the connection directly: "Many 'chokepoint' technology problems China faces stem from inadequate basic theory research — the source and foundational issues not being clarified." Science and Technology Minister Yin Hejun, writing earlier this year in the *People's Daily*, argued that "without strong basic research and original innovation capacity, high-level technological self-reliance cannot be achieved."

Applied research, by contrast, grew a more modest 3.2 percent to 444.16 billion yuan, while experimental development — the largest category — rose 8.4 percent to 3.2012 trillion yuan, accounting for 81.5 percent of all R&D.

## Enterprises Drive the Growth

The bulletin confirms the central role of corporate innovation. Enterprise R&D spending crossed the 3-trillion-yuan threshold for the first time, reaching 3.03944 trillion yuan, up 7.7 percent. Firms accounted for 77.4 percent of all R&D — above 77 percent for a fourth consecutive year — and contributed 73.7 percent of total R&D growth.

That corporate share now sits close to the United States (77.6 percent) and Japan (78.9 percent), and well above Germany (68.2 percent) and France (66.0 percent), signaling a structural shift from China's earlier state-institute-led model toward one in which companies are the primary engines of research. Government-affiliated research institutions spent 457.23 billion yuan (up 8.1 percent), while universities spent 340.73 billion yuan (up 11.1 percent), the fastest-growing performer category.

## Industry and Regional Concentration

In high-tech manufacturing among large firms, R&D reached 843.07 billion yuan, up 9.9 percent, for an R&D intensity of 3.38 percent of revenue. Eight major industry categories each exceeded 100 billion yuan in R&D investment, together accounting for 69.4 percent of all industrial enterprise research spending.

Regionally, innovation remains heavily clustered. Six provincial-level regions surpassed 200 billion yuan in R&D spending: Guangdong (551.04 billion yuan), Jiangsu (494.82 billion yuan), Beijing (369.61 billion yuan), Zhejiang (317.72 billion yuan), Shandong (282.57 billion yuan), and Shanghai (258.05 billion yuan). Three innovation hubs — the Beijing-Tianjin-Hebei region, the Yangtze River Delta, and Guangdong — together accounted for 58.8 percent of national R&D, up 0.4 percentage points.

Seven regions exceeded the national R&D intensity average, led by Beijing at 7.10 percent, Shanghai at 4.55 percent, and Guangdong at 3.78 percent. Together they represent 56.2 percent of national R&D.

## Fiscal Support and the Road Ahead

National fiscal spending on science and technology reached 1.31845 trillion yuan, up 4.4 percent, with local governments contributing 66.1 percent of the total — a reminder that regional and municipal budgets, not only central coffers, underwrite China's research ambitions.

Beyond the raw numbers, [China Daily](https://china.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202610/16/WS6ad18b5ee4b09a165c78f8ac.html) noted that the bulletin's release reinforces Beijing's messaging on technological self-reliance at a time of intensifying global competition over advanced chips, AI, and clean energy.

## What to Watch

The 2025 figures validate a decade-long strategy, but they also expose the challenge ahead. China's R&D intensity of 2.80 percent still trails the United States (roughly 3.5 percent), South Korea (about 5 percent), Japan (about 3.3 percent), and Israel (over 5 percent). The symbolic crossing of the OECD average — itself boosted by China's large GDP base — is a milestone, not a finish line.

The real test lies in whether the sharp rise in basic research funding translates into breakthroughs at the "source and foundation" that Luo and other officials describe as the missing link. As the 15th Five-Year Plan (2026–2030) takes shape, officials have signaled continued emphasis on lifting the basic research share, diversifying funding sources, and building a financial system aligned with innovation. Whether those commitments produce the foundational advances China seeks — or merely more of the same scale-driven growth — will define the next phase of the country's technological ascent.
