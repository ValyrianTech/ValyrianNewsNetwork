# Brewers Sweep Dodgers to Reach First World Series Since 1982

LOS ANGELES — After 44 years, the Milwaukee Brewers are going back to the World Series.

Milwaukee completed a four-game sweep of the two-time defending champion Los Angeles Dodgers with a 6-4 comeback victory in Game 4 of the National League Championship Series on Thursday night at Dodger Stadium, clinching the franchise's first NL pennant and its first World Series berth since 1982, [according to NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/10/16/nx-s1-6004787/brewers-sweep-dodgers-mlb).

"We weren't coming in to play patty-cake," Brewers manager Pat Murphy said. "We were hungry, and as long as it's an even playing field, we're going to come in and give it our best shot to win. That's exactly what we did."

## A Reversal of Fortune

The sweep was a direct reversal of the 2025 NLCS, when the Dodgers swept the Brewers on their way to a second straight title. [MLB.com](https://www.mlb.com/news/brewers-sweep-dodgers-in-nlcs-to-reach-2026-world-series) noted that Milwaukee became the first major league club to finish a postseason sweep after being swept by that same opponent the previous October — and the first team to sweep a best-of-seven series in which it trailed at some point in every game.

Milwaukee, which posted a franchise-record 103 wins during the regular season — the most in MLB — entered the series as a heavy underdog against a Dodgers club with more than double its payroll and a roster stocked with former MVPs.

"They may have a bunch of Hall of Famers, but we've got a bunch of gamers — and the gamers won this week," Murphy said.

## How Game 4 Unfolded

Shohei Ohtani's first home run of the postseason helped stake Los Angeles to a 4-0 lead in the second inning, a three-run shot following an Andy Pages RBI single before a crowd of 49,104.

But the Brewers chipped away. Milwaukee scored twice in the fourth, twice in the fifth to tie it, and took the lead for good in the sixth when center fielder Garrett Mitchell fell behind 0-2 before launching a 409-foot leadoff homer to center — the first home run of his career on an 0-2 count.

"It's absolutely surreal," Mitchell said. "You talk about the battle-tested Brewers. We went down early and the belief in that dugout got stronger and stronger."

No. 9 hitter David Hamilton added a two-out solo shot in the ninth for insurance, and reliever Aaron Ashby pitched three scoreless innings to earn the save.

"We got down early tonight and we were like, it's going to be a great story," Brewers captain Christian Yelich said. "We're going to come back and win this. Like, stay together, start chipping away. And that's exactly what they did."

## A Small-Market Blueprint

The victory capped a remarkable run that has made Milwaukee a model of analytics-driven success. As [Wisconsin Public Radio reported](https://www.wpr.org/news/brewers-heading-to-the-world-series), the Brewers have the 12th-lowest payroll in the majors at $143.5 million, while the Dodgers' $338 million payroll is more than double that. Milwaukee is also baseball's smallest media market.

Yet the Brewers tied for the second-fewest home runs in MLB during the regular season (154) while scoring the second-most runs, built on walks, stolen bases and relentless at-bats. Then, improbably, they out-homered the Dodgers 7-1 in the NLCS.

First baseman Jake Bauers, named the [NLCS MVP](https://www.mlb.com/news/jake-bauers-wins-2026-nlcs-mvp), embodied that resilience. Bauers collected at least one hit in all four games, working a 12-pitch walk in Game 2 and homering on the 11th pitch of an at-bat against Blake Snell in Game 3 — signature moments for a player who nearly quit baseball two years ago.

"There was never a doubt in that dugout what was going to happen tonight," Bauers said. "We knew we were going to win that game."

## A Dodgers Collapse and an Uncertain Winter

For Los Angeles, the sweep ended a bid for a three-peat never accomplished by a National League team. An aging, injury-hit roster fell short, and the Dodgers' offense went cold at the worst time.

Ohtani, who did not pitch this postseason, was candid about his struggles. As the [New York Post reported](https://nypost.com/2026/10/16/sports/shohei-ohtani-brewers-exposed-him-dodgers-nlcs-sweep/), the four-time MVP hit just .231 in the series and .192 across the postseason.

"The Brewers were overall strong, and a very good team," Ohtani said. "And [they] really exposed parts of me as a player, and got to give some credit to that."

Dodgers manager Dave Roberts was gracious in defeat. "I'm not used to those consolation speeches," he said. "Those guys played flawless. You got to sometimes tip your cap."

## What's Next

Milwaukee will host Game 1 of the World Series on Friday, Oct. 23, at American Family Field against the winner of the American League Championship Series between the Cleveland Guardians and the Tampa Bay Rays.

The Brewers franchise has never won a World Series. Its only previous appearance came in 1982 — as an American League team — a seven-game loss to the St. Louis Cardinals.

Reflecting on the moment, Brewers owner Mark Attanasio invoked two franchise icons: Bud Selig, the team's 92-year-old founder, and Bob Uecker, the beloved late broadcaster.

"It's so important for the community, the entire state of Wisconsin," Attanasio said. "We're standing on shoulders of Bud Selig and Bob Uecker."

For a small-market club four wins from a first title, the story is only getting bigger.
