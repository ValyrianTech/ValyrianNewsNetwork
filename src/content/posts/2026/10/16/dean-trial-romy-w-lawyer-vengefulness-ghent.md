# Dean Trial: Romy W.'s Lawyer Attacks 'Vengefulness'

Lawyers for Romy W. launched a blistering defence plea on Friday in the Ghent assize trial over the death of four-year-old Dean Verberckmoes, accusing prosecutors and the victim's family of "pure rancour, pure revenge" and demanding her full acquittal for the child's kidnapping, which ended in his death in January 2022.

"They want to repay one wrong with another," defence counsel Raan Colman told the jury, according to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/liveblog/advocaat-haalt-fel-uit-waarom-moet-romy-kapot-omdat-ze-hun-ei~1791388401335/). "What is happening here is pure rancour, pure revenge. And they are lying on top of it. I am disgusted by it."

The jury is expected to deliver a verdict on guilt on Friday, with sentencing anticipated on Monday.

## The Two Defendants

The trial before the Court of Assizes in Ghent concerns the death of Dean Verberckmoes in January 2022. Two defendants stand accused: **Dave De Kock (39)** and his then-girlfriend **Romy W. (29)**.

De Kock faces charges of manslaughter (*doodslag*) and kidnapping of a minor with fatal consequences. He admits responsibility for Dean's death and the possession of child abuse images, but disputes the kidnapping charge. Romy W. is charged as co-defendant for kidnapping with fatal consequences and denies any involvement.

Belgium's assize court is the only court in the country that tries serious crimes before a jury of lay citizens alongside professional judges. The distinction between manslaughter and murder — the latter requiring premeditation — is central to the proceedings, as it determines whether De Kock faces up to 30 years or a life sentence.

## How Dean Died

Dean was staying with De Kock and Romy W., friends of his mother Elke Verberckmoes, in mid-January 2022. He went missing after De Kock left the apartment following an argument about alcohol.

According to the prosecution, De Kock drove to the parking lot of a furniture store in Sint-Gillis-Waas and stayed there for two hours and 41 minutes, where he said he "cooled his anger on Dean." He then drove through the Western Scheldt Tunnel into the Netherlands. He claims Dean needed to urinate and then suffered an epileptic seizure, but experts rejected those accounts. Dean died in the Netherlands.

De Kock later abandoned the child's body at Neeltje Jans. On 17 January 2022, Dean was found dead under bushes after a days-long search, as [Omroep Zeeland](https://www.omroepzeeland.nl/nieuws/18814777/zo-verliep-de-eerste-dag-van-het-proces-over-de-dood-van-kleuter-dean) reported. Before the body was found, De Kock bought and used drugs in Koudekerke and kept two sexual appointments in Middelburg.

The case has painful parallels with De Kock's past: in 2010 he was sentenced to 10 years in prison for the severe abuse and death of two-year-old Miguel, the son of his then-partner, in Turnhout. A 2022 "white march" commemorated both children, [RTV](https://www.rtv.be/actualiteit-en-nieuws/dave-de-kock-voor-assisen-voor-moord-op-peuter-dean) noted.

## The Defence's Case

Colman argued that his client was asleep and did not know what De Kock planned, calling the alleged kidnapping "the stupidest kidnapping I have ever come across," [Omroep Zeeland](https://www.omroepzeeland.nl/nieuws/18862537/live-verdediging-aan-het-woord-in-zaak-dood-dean-4-romy-en-dave-brengen-het-slechtste-in-elkaar-naar-boven) reported.

"It happened on the parking lot, that is certain," Colman told the court. "That man did something there that she knew nothing about. That much is clear from the messages." He asked what motive his client could possibly have had, noting that Dave and Romy had taken Dean to a pharmacist and that phone records showed her concern for the boy.

Colman pointed to the emotional tenor of the case, arguing that the prosecution was projecting guilt onto his client. "Why must Romy be destroyed? Because they cannot cope with their own guilt. But he — pointing at Dave De Kock — is the only guilty one," he said. He described his client as "treated like a dishrag," noting she has spent "four years, eight months and 28 days" in detention.

"I ask you to administer justice and to be just," Colman concluded.

## The Prosecution and the Family

The defence plea followed pointed arguments from the prosecution and the lawyer for Dean's mother. Prosecutor Serge Malefason asked the jury to convict both defendants on all charges, describing De Kock as "a very black, cold man" who "beat a child to death" and carried "a four-hour death struggle for Dean" on his conscience.

Turning to Romy W., Malefason posed a hypothetical to the jury: "If there had been no Romy covering Dave's retreat, flight, kidnapping, would little Dean still have been alive? I ask you to take that into your deliberations."

Jef Vermassen, the veteran lawyer for Dean's mother, argued for a murder charge, citing what he described as a deliberate plan. "To me this is a murderer. There was premeditation," he said. He also sought additional guilt questions against Romy W., including criminal neglect, so the jury could convict her however it assessed the evidence.

"It cannot be that she goes free," Vermassen said. "For us, she is guilty."

## A Toxic Relationship

Lawyers for De Kock acknowledged his responsibility for the death but insisted it was manslaughter, not murder. "He beat Dean to death. We will take responsibility for that, admit guilt and bear the appropriate punishment," said defence counsel Nick Heinen.

"This is a textbook example of manslaughter: an explosion in the head, a blow. Without any form of premeditation," he told the jury. He described the relationship between the two defendants as toxic, noting that drug use by De Kock and alcohol use by Romy W. drove escalating conflict. "They bring out the worst in each other," he said.

## The Central Question

As the jury begins its deliberations, it must resolve a stark question: whether Romy W. knowingly helped conceal Dean's abduction, or whether she was, as her lawyers argue, an unaware bystander to a killer's crime. Under Belgian assize procedure, the jury must acquit if any reasonable doubt remains.

For the family of Dean — whose mother was described in court as losing "her dearest possession" through misplaced trust — the verdict is the culmination of a years-long wait for accountability. As Vermassen's co-counsel Emeline Vanhooren told the court, "Today an answer will be given to the question of who is guilty and why."

Whether the jury accepts that answer in the form the family seeks, or in the form the defence demands, will determine the fate of both defendants.
