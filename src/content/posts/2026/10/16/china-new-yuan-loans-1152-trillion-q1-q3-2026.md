# China New Yuan Loans Rise 11.52 Trillion Yuan in Q1-Q3

New yuan-denominated loans in China increased by 11.52 trillion yuan (about $1.71 trillion) in the first three quarters of 2026, the People's Bank of China (PBOC) said, as the world's second-largest economy continued to expand credit while steering lending toward technology and green industries. The data, released on October 14 and reported by [People's Daily Online](http://finance.people.com.cn/n1/2026/1016/c1004-40815815.html) on October 16, underscores a broader shift in how Chinese companies finance themselves — away from bank loans and toward bonds and equities.

## A slowing but restructuring credit engine

The headline loan figure masks a crucial nuance: total lending growth is slowing even as credit itself expands. According to the PBOC's official *Financial Statistics Report for the First Three Quarters of 2026*, loans to the real economy rose 11.5 trillion yuan — but that was 3.04 trillion yuan less than in the same period a year earlier. Overall social financing, the broadest measure of credit, reached an increment of 27.46 trillion yuan in the January-September period, down 2.63 trillion yuan year-on-year, while the stock of social financing stood at 468.17 trillion yuan at end-September, up 7.1% from a year earlier.

The composition of that credit tells the more important story. Household loans fell by 1.03 trillion yuan, dragged down by a 1.14 trillion yuan decline in short-term borrowing — a sign of weak consumer confidence and continued deleveraging in the property market. Corporate and institutional loans, by contrast, rose 12.55 trillion yuan, but leaned heavily on short-term lending (+4.64 trillion yuan) and bill financing (+1.24 trillion yuan), suggesting companies remain cautious about long-term investment.

## Direct financing gains ground

The clearest structural shift is the rise of direct financing. Corporate bond net financing climbed 1.18 trillion yuan year-on-year to 2.75 trillion yuan, while domestic equity financing rose to 535.9 billion yuan. At the same time, government bond net financing fell 1.16 trillion yuan as issuance paced differently compared with 2025.

By the first seven months of the year, bonds and equities together accounted for roughly 48% of new social financing — nearly matching the 46.5% share of loans to the real economy — according to [CCTV Finance](https://jingji.cctv.cn/2026/08/15/ARTIKLvKD8IqqfiOAvZRZLY1260815.shtml). "For a long time, China relied heavily on indirect financing dominated by bank credit," said Dong Ximiao, chief economist at Zhaolian, in comments carried by CCTV. "In recent years, the multi-tiered capital market has accelerated, and bond and equity financing channels have widened significantly."

"Slower-but-higher-quality loan financing will become the norm, and a rising share of bond and equity financing in broad credit may become a trend," Ming Ming, chief economist at CITIC Securities, said, as reported by the [National Business Daily](https://m.nbd.com.cn/articles/2026-07-15/4473777.html).

## Officials frame a "new normal"

PBOC officials have embraced the slowdown as a structural feature rather than a cyclical weakness. Xie Guangqi, director of the central bank's Monetary Policy Department, said the financing changes "may be long-term and trend-based, reflecting deep adjustment of China's economic structure and the transition between old and new growth drivers."

"Money and credit will continue shifting from extensive expansion to connotative development," Xie added. "Loan 'slower growth with improved quality' may become one of the new normal features of macro operations." He noted the PBOC is de-emphasizing quantity-based targets and moving toward price-based monetary policy.

The pivot is visible in the loan data: the PBOC's structural tools have channeled credit toward technology, green industries, and small businesses, with inclusive micro and small business loans and medium- and long-term loans to the services sector (excluding real estate) growing faster than overall lending, CCTV reported.

## Rates at record lows, demand still weak

Borrowing costs have fallen to historic lows. The weighted average interest rate on new corporate loans in September was just below 3% — about 0.2 percentage points lower than a year earlier — while the rate on new personal housing loans was around 3.1%. The interbank lending rate held at 1.4%.

Yet cheaper money has not translated into stronger demand. The gap between broad money (M2), which grew 7.2% year-on-year to 359.63 trillion yuan, and narrow money (M1), which grew just 3.2% to 116.76 trillion yuan, widened to 4 percentage points — a signal economists read as weak activation of corporate production and household consumption. "The current situation of weak endogenous demand has not yet been reversed," Ming said.

Deposits rose 20.76 trillion yuan in the first three quarters, led by a 9.39 trillion yuan increase in household savings — evidence that consumers are saving rather than spending.

## What's next

With the balance of 2026 approaching, attention turns to whether Beijing will step up stimulus. Wen Bin, chief economist at China Minsheng Bank, told the [Economic Information Daily](http://finance.ce.cn/bank12/scroll/202603/t20260316_2829551.shtml) that "countercyclical adjustment is expected to be stepped up" in the second half, with existing policies "fully utilized" and incremental measures prepared. Analysts such as Wang Qing of Orient Jincheng have predicted further rate and reserve-requirement cuts.

The bigger question is whether the shift toward direct financing and quality-driven credit can offset the drag from a cooling property sector and cautious households. For now, China's policymakers appear to be betting that a leaner, more market-oriented credit system will prove more resilient than the debt-fueled expansion of the past.
