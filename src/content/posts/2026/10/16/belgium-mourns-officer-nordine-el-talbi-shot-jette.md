# Belgium Mourns Officer Nordine El Talbi, Shot in Jette

Police officers from across Belgium gathered at the Jubelpark (Cinquantenaire) in Brussels on Friday morning to commemorate inspector Nordine El Talbi, the 41-year-old officer shot in the head in Jette earlier this week, in a ceremony that mixed public mourning with mounting demands for the country to confront gun violence and drug-related crime.

Thousands attended the 11 a.m. commemoration, which opened with the Belgian national anthem and featured a helicopter flyover, speeches from colleagues and officials, and a moment of silence broken by the wailing sirens of police vehicles. The event came three days after El Talbi was killed in the line of duty, and drew officers from every corner of the country who travelled to [pay tribute](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/liveblog/postuum-ereteken-helikoptervlucht-en-indrukwekkende-stilte-dui~1791870783608/).

## A Veteran Officer and a Father of Two

Nordine El Talbi, known among colleagues by the nickname "James," was a long-serving member of the Brussels-Capital/Ixelles police zone, which he joined as an inspector in 2009. In 2019 he achieved what his corps chief described as a lifelong ambition by joining the Anti-overvalbrigade (AOB), the specialized anti-robbery unit deployed in high-risk interventions.

"Nordine, 41 years old, joined the police zone in 2009," corps chief Michel Goovaerts said. "Ten years later, in 2019, he realised his dream: joining the AOB. He did so with great conviction and professionalism."

According to a [profile published by VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/10/13/wie-is-de-agent-die-omkwam-in-jette/), El Talbi spent a decade in frontline services in the city centre before moving to the specialized unit. Goovaerts described him as "a true Brussels guy" from a large family and deeply connected to the city. He was a father of two.

## The Night of the Shooting

The sequence of events began on the night of Monday 12 October, when a violent carjacking took place in Tubize in Walloon Brabant. Armed men seized a vehicle from a 35-year-old man. The stolen car's plate was later flagged by an ANPR camera on the Brussels Ring Road, and the vehicle was located in Jette.

Two AOB teams set up discreetly in the area. Shortly before 1 a.m. on Tuesday 13 October, El Talbi—an experienced plainclothes officer—was sent on foot to conduct a reconnaissance of the stolen vehicle. Colleagues, receiving no response from him, went to check and found him unconscious on the pavement near house number 80 on Avenue Carton de Wiart. He had been struck by a single bullet to the head and died shortly afterwards.

At the scene, investigators found three pistols and a large quantity of drugs inside the stolen vehicle. Several suspects were arrested, and an investigating judge was appointed for murder of a person with a public function—a charge carrying a maximum penalty of life imprisonment.

## A National Emergency, Leaders Say

The killing has intensified a debate that had already been building in Brussels over drug-related violence and the proliferation of firearms. At the ceremony, Brussels Mayor Philippe Close (PS) did not mince words.

"We are in a national emergency," Close said. "We must do more to protect our population, but also our police officers." He noted that a decade ago the situation was not comparable, referring to the growing availability of weapons.

Federal Interior Minister Bernard Quintin (MR) told the assembled officers that the country stood with them. "I want to tell you today that you are not alone," he said. "Your grief is also our grief. The whole country stands at your side." But he added a forward-looking note: "Today is a day of comfort, but tomorrow we must draw lessons." Quintin has called on both the governing majority and the opposition to support law enforcement.

The Brussels public prosecutor, Julien Moinil, who is leading the investigation, emphasised the human dimension of the loss. "They take risks in increasingly difficult conditions. They are people. Behind every officer stands a mother, a son, a brother, a sister."

## A Posthumous Honour and a Wall of Memory

During the ceremony, El Talbi's family received the Burgerlijk Kruis 1ste Klasse (Civil Cross 1st Class), an official Belgian honour whose citation references "courage, dedication and humanity." Goovaerts pledged that the force would not forget him. "What I can guarantee is that we will keep Nordine's memory alive."

El Talbi's name will be added to the "wall of memory" inaugurated in 2019 at the Polis Center, the largest police building in Brussels. According to the NSPV police union, he is the 36th Belgian police officer to die while on duty in the past 25 years—not all in shootings, but with violence accounting for a substantial share.

His funeral was held on Wednesday at the Al-Amal mosque in Anderlecht, where more than 2,000 people are reported to have attended. On Tuesday night, fellow officers gathered at the Poelaertplein in Brussels, illuminating their vehicles' blue emergency lights as a mark of respect. A condolence register was opened at Brussels City Hall.

## Weapons and a Prosecution Warning

The Brussels prosecutor-general, Frédéric Van Leeuw, has warned about the flow of dangerous weapons into the capital, pointing to conflict zones as a key source. "For years, many weapons came from another major conflict, in the former Yugoslavia, but now we are concerned about the consequences of the war in Ukraine," he said. "A lot of weapons were delivered there, and some risk coming back onto the market. Cheap weapons are also produced in Turkey and find their way to the European market."

Van Leeuw linked the availability of guns to the economics of the drug trade. "The value of drugs is enormously high, and that goes hand in hand with an increase in open violence," he said. "It is no longer about discreet criminal organisations, but more about groups that use open violence to intimidate rivals."

The prosecutor's office also issued a rare public rebuke of media coverage. Rebutting speculation—particularly reporting by HLN—it said leaked hypotheses were "based on unconfirmed and incorrect information, particularly regarding the course of events and the number of suspects," and warned that such reporting "endangers the normal course of the judicial investigation." The office said it would communicate only when the investigation allowed.

## Anger and Powerlessness

Beyond the official tributes, the killing has stirred raw emotions within the force. Sven Mary, a lawyer for the VSOA police union, captured the mood: "A policeman doesn't leave in the morning to get a bullet in his head." He argued that respect for the uniform and for authority has eroded over the past three to four years, particularly in Brussels.

Jurgen De Landsheer, corps chief of the Brussels-South zone, described a "mixture of anger and powerlessness" among his officers. "We do so much in the fight against drug violence and for the safety of citizens," he told VRT NWS, "but we also notice that drugs are not the only problem—it is also the easy availability of weapons, and the readiness to use them against anyone."

He also spoke of the toll on colleagues who witnessed the aftermath. "There are people who saw Nordine lying there and performed CPR on him, who had to move on immediately."

Among those attending the ceremony were police cadets, some confronting the realities of the profession for the first time. "It is important to know that this could happen to us later," said cadet Eowynn Goessens.

## What's Next

The investigation continues under the authority of an investigating judge, with prosecutors refusing to confirm widely circulated accounts of the suspects' identities and motives. The political aftermath is likely to focus on police training, equipment and protection, as well as on the broader question of weapons trafficking and drug-related violence that officials now describe as a national emergency.

For Belgium's police forces, Friday was a day of mourning. The harder debates—over resources, sentencing and the reach of organised crime—are only beginning.
