# Xi Congratulates Hunan University on 100th Anniversary

Chinese President Xi Jinping sent a congratulatory letter to Hunan University on 16 October 2026, marking the institution's 100th anniversary and praising its century of contributions to education, talent cultivation, and national development, according to a report by the state news agency [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/politics/leaders/20261016/acbfa1a451fd4baab76ca93856510d74/c.html).

In the letter, Xi extended congratulations to all faculty, staff, and alumni, and urged the university to keep serving national strategy as it enters its second century.

## A Dual Milestone: 1,000 Years and 100 Years

The celebration carries unusual historical weight. The university's campus in Changsha, Hunan Province, contains Yuelu Academy, a center of learning founded in 976 CE — making it the higher education institution with the longest continuous same-site operation in China, as [People's Daily](https://paper.people.com.cn/rmrb/pc/content/202610/16/content_30185968.html) noted. The 2026 occasion therefore combines two anniversaries: 1,050 years since the academy's founding and 100 years since the institution was named Hunan University in 1926.

Xi's letter drew directly on that lineage. He called on Hunan University to inherit its "glorious revolutionary tradition," continue the "academic lineage of the thousand-year academy," and carry forward the spirit of "seeking truth from facts and daring to be first," according to the [full text of the letter](https://www.news.cn/politics/leaders/20261016/ca8a136b225f4a4fbf5726083c41c8b1/c.html) also published by Xinhua.

## Serving the Nation

At the center of Xi's message was the theme of 经世致用 ("practical application for the benefit of society") — the idea that scholarship should serve the nation. Xi said he hoped the university would "strive to cultivate more outstanding talents of practical use to the world, and make new contributions to building an education powerhouse and advancing Chinese-style modernization."

The university has positioned its recent achievements to match that framing. Deng Wei, Party Secretary of Hunan University, said the school is working to "polish the brand of Yuelu Academy in the new era" and to build "a highland for cultivating leading talents of practical use to the world," according to People's Daily.

Academics echoed the theme. Luo An, an academician of the Chinese Academy of Engineering, said that doing research "requires the focus to grind a sword for ten years, and even more requires the ambition to tackle key problems for the country," People's Daily reported.

## Historical and Regional Ties

The university's history is interwoven with the political history of modern China. Hunan University became one of the country's 16 national universities in 1937; the following year it was bombed by Japanese aircraft and relocated westward to Chenxi, continuing its teaching in mountain shelters. In 1950, Mao Zedong personally inscribed the university's name, and Li Da, a co-founder of the Communist Party of China, became its first president, as [Hunan Daily](https://epaper.voc.com.cn/hnrb/html/2026-10/15/content_1804207.htm) chronicled.

Xi himself visited Yuelu Academy on the campus on 17 September 2020, quoting the academy's famous couplet — "Chu is noted for its talents, and here they abound" — in remarks to students.

Regional development is another pillar of the anniversary coverage. According to Hunan Daily, more than 40% of the university's postgraduate graduates now stay in Hunan, and the school has transferred more than 600 items of intellectual property worth at least 1.9 billion yuan, more than 80% of it within the province.

## Analysis: A Familiar Signal

Letters of congratulations to major universities are a recurring instrument in Xi's approach to higher education. A comparable letter in 2023 marked Yunnan University's centennial, and such messages typically link institutional identity to national goals — here, the drive to build an "education powerhouse" and to advance "Chinese-style modernization."

For Hunan University, the emphasis on revolutionary heritage and the millennium-old academy serves a dual purpose: it reinforces the university's distinctive cultural prestige while aligning it with the central leadership's broader campaign to pair education with the country's strategic and modernization priorities.

## What's Next

The anniversary places the university at a symbolic "new starting point," the phrase Xi used to open his appeal in the letter. As it enters its next century, the institution faces an explicit expectation from the highest level of leadership: connect tradition with national ambition. For observers of Chinese higher education policy, the letter's language — and the university's response — will be a signal of how that balance is being defined.
