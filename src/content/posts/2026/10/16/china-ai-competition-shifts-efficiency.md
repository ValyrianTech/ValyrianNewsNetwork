# China AI Race Shifts From Scale to Efficiency

China's artificial intelligence industry is entering a new phase in which competition is shifting from a race for scale and model size toward efficiency, cost-effectiveness and real-world deployment, according to an analysis published on October 16 in the Economic Daily, the country's flagship state-run economic newspaper.

The commentary argues that a recent surge in computing-power prices across the global AI supply chain is a warning signal: China's AI sector "cannot rely on merely scaling up, but must compete on efficiency."

## A Compute Price Shock as a Turning Point

The analysis points to a wave of price increases among Chinese AI chipmakers. According to the commentary, companies including Huawei, Cambricon, MetaX and Iluvatar CoreX raised product prices, with some models rising by 20% to 50%.

"Compute power is the foundational element and underlying support of the AI industry," the authors wrote. "This global compute 'price-increase wave' is a warning to AI development — we cannot rely only on 'scaling up,' we must 'compete on efficiency.' Deep transformation of the AI industry is imperative."

The shift reflects a maturing understanding that raw model size and headline benchmarks are becoming less decisive than the economics of running AI at scale, where training and inference increasingly drive enormous electricity and compute demand.

## Three Pillars of an Efficiency Model

As republished by [Guangming Online](https://guancha.gmw.cn/2026-10/16/content_39049720.htm), the commentary outlines three pillars of what it describes as a China-specific, intensive and efficient AI development model.

The first is **lightweight model research and development**. Leading domestic firms are advancing model compression, mixed-precision computing and inference optimization. DeepSeek is cited as an example of achieving internationally competitive performance with far lower resource investment than its global peers.

The second is **efficient software-hardware coordination**. The authors highlight the deep co-tuning of algorithms with domestic chips. They note that DeepSeek V4 launched first on Huawei's Ascend chips this year, binding the domestic software-hardware ecosystem together, with an inference cost claimed to be one-seventieth that of GPT-4 and a hardware cost one-third that of the Nvidia solution. An independent [technical analysis](https://juejin.cn/post/7632162180319379497) similarly frames the V4 launch on Ascend as the first frontier-level model trained largely outside Nvidia's CUDA ecosystem.

The third is **application-scenario driven deployment**. The authors argue that China's abundant data resources, complete industrial system, broad application scenarios and large market allow vertical applications to pull large models into real-world use, with scenario data then feeding back to improve model performance.

## Policy Recommendations

The commentary, syndicated by outlets including [Sina Finance](https://t.cj.sina.com.cn/articles/view/1663612603/6328b6bb0200248fi), goes on to offer a set of policy recommendations aimed at accelerating the efficiency transition.

These include concentrating resources on breakthroughs in high-end chips and basic software to build a self-controllable, coordinated AI base hardware-software system; accelerating construction of a National Integrated Computing Network and tightening coordination of compute scheduling; strictly controlling low-level, fragmented data-center construction; and establishing a compute-power coordination mechanism with the electricity sector, including pilots for "source-grid-load-storage integration" and "green power direct connection."

The authors also call for deepening the development and open sharing of data resources, building high-quality industry datasets and improving data property-rights and trading rules. They propose dynamically publishing a list of key "AI+" industries and vertical scenarios, focusing on areas such as smart manufacturing, bio-manufacturing and assisted healthcare, and offering equipment-renewal or interest subsidies to demonstration projects. To address the difficulty of deploying and scaling vertical models, they suggest including first-of-its-kind hardware and software in government procurement catalogs.

## What It Signals

The piece is notable less for its technical claims — which are the authors' assertions rather than independently verified metrics — than for its provenance. It was written by Wang Chenwei, a researcher at the Institute of Economic System and Management under the Chinese Academy of Macroeconomic Research, a think tank affiliated with the National Development and Reform Commission, China's top economic-planning agency. The Economic Daily's commentary section is widely read as a channel for signaling official economic-policy thinking.

The emphasis on efficiency, software-hardware self-reliance and green compute ties the AI transition to broader national priorities: consolidating the AI industrial chain, cultivating "new quality productive forces" and advancing high-quality development.

## What to Watch

Whether the efficiency pivot translates into durable advantage will depend on execution. Key questions include how quickly domestic chipmakers can mature their software ecosystems to rival established platforms, how the National Integrated Computing Network is implemented on the ground, and whether subsidy and procurement mechanisms can convert promising vertical models into scalable commercial deployments.

For now, the message from China's policy-adjacent commentary is clear: the era of competing on sheer scale is giving way to a contest over who can deliver AI most cheaply, efficiently and at scale.
