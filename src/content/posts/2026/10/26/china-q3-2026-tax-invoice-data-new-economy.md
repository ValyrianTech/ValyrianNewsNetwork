# China Touts Q3 'Toward New and Better' as Old Engines Stall

China's economy ran steadily through the first three quarters of 2026 and moved "toward new and better," with industry, emerging industries, interprovincial trade and corporate equipment investment all strengthening in the third quarter compared with the second, according to tax-invoice data released by the State Taxation Administration and carried by [People's Daily](http://finance.people.com.cn/n1/2026/1026/c1004-40821306.html) on 26 October.

The official narrative of acceleration rests on a high-frequency measure of business sales — value-added tax invoices, which track nominal revenue across virtually all commercial activity. It presents a materially brighter picture of the quarter than the headline GDP figures the [National Bureau of Statistics](https://www.stats.gov.cn/english/PressRelease/202610/t20261019_1965619.html) published a week earlier, which showed growth slowing and demand under strain.

## The New Economy Is Genuinely Booming

The tax data tell a consistent story of strength in advanced industries. Industrial enterprise sales revenue rose 7.4% year on year over the three quarters, and 7.8% in Q3 alone — a modest 0.1 percentage point pickup on Q2. Advanced manufacturing, spanning high-tech and equipment manufacturing, was the standout: Q3 sales revenue climbed 12.3% year on year, up 3.4 percentage points from the second quarter, with quarterly growth rising in each successive period.

The fastest growth clustered around artificial intelligence. Sales revenue in digital-economy core industries rose 9.1% over the three quarters and 9.9% in Q3, while patent-intensive industries grew 13.1% and 14.4% respectively. Within AI-related manufacturing, integrated circuits surged 77.9% in the third quarter and semiconductor-device specialty equipment jumped 35.9% — accelerations of 6.4 and 9.3 percentage points over Q2.

Corporate investment told a similar story. Nationwide purchases of machinery and equipment rose 12.6% over the three quarters and 15.9% in Q3, the latter up 4.6 percentage points on the prior quarter. Manufacturing equipment purchases climbed 17.6% in Q3, while information-industry equipment purchases jumped 30.4%, attributed to the accelerated build-out of computing-power and data centers.

"Sales revenue reflects the present; equipment investment points to the future," the Securities Daily commentator Han Yu wrote in a companion analysis. "That enterprises are willing to spend on machinery is the most direct 'vote' for market prospects."

The tax data also pointed to progress on the government's push for a unified domestic market. Interprovincial trade reached 41.4% of total sales over the three quarters, up 0.4 percentage points year on year, and 41.7% in Q3. Transportation and logistics revenue grew 7.3% over the three quarters and 9.9% in Q3.

## The Other Half of the Picture

The NBS figures confirm the strength in advanced industry — high-tech manufacturing value added grew 14.3% and equipment manufacturing 10.2%, with 3D-printing devices up 62.5%, lithium-ion batteries up 39.5% and industrial robots up 29.1%. But they also reveal an economy tilting sharply between its old and new engines.

GDP grew 4.6% year on year over the three quarters, decelerating from 5.0% in Q1 to 4.3% in Q2 before a modest rebound to 4.5% in Q3. Fixed-asset investment fell 7.2% year on year, dragged down by a 20.6% collapse in real-estate development investment and a 10.4% drop in private investment. Retail sales of consumer goods grew just 1.1%.

The NBS was candid about the strains. The economy advanced "despite mounting pressure," it said, but "the issue of insufficient domestic demand remains prominent, and the foundation for steady economic growth still needs to be consolidated." Trade was the clear bright spot, with goods imports and exports up 18.8% and September exports up 29.6%.

## Reading the Two Narratives Together

The apparent contradiction resolves once the measures are understood. Tax-invoice data capture nominal sales revenue — not inflation-adjusted and not a substitute for value-added GDP. The Q3 acceleration the tax data highlight is real, but it is concentrated in the new-economy segments the government has made its strategic priority. The traditional engines — property, broad fixed-asset investment and household consumption — remain in a visible contraction, a pattern consistent with a deliberate "old-for-new" structural rotation.

That framing aligns tightly with Beijing's economic doctrine. The official coverage explicitly invokes "new quality productive forces" to describe the digital economy and patent-intensive industries, and "toward new and better" has become a recurring slogan for 2026 — a signal that the government is emphasizing industrial upgrading over raw GDP maximization.

## What to Watch

The fourth quarter will test whether the Q3 momentum in AI, semiconductors and equipment investment can broaden enough to offset the property and consumption drag. Analysts will watch for monthly investment data to see whether the fixed-asset contraction eases, and for any new stimulus measures aimed at lifting household demand — the soft spot the tax-invoice story leaves untouched. For now, the official message is clear: the new economy is carrying the quarter, and the government is content to let that story stand.
