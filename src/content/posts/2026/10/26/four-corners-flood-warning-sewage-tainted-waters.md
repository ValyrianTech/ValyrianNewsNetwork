# Four Corners Flood Warning Lingers as Sewage Threatens

A flood warning remained in effect Monday across parts of the Four Corners region, where saturated communities from Utah to New Mexico were beginning the slow work of cleanup and damage assessment while confronting water that officials warned could be contaminated with chemicals and raw sewage, according to [AP News](https://apnews.com/article/four-corners-flooding-storm-river-evacuations-a91e683f54e41eb3fe8c03863a502d21). At least one flooding-related death has been reported, and rivers swollen by nearly a week of unusually heavy rain were still running high.

The affected area is the meeting point of Utah, Colorado, Arizona and New Mexico — the only spot in the United States where four states touch. Nearly a week of relentless rain beginning Wednesday, Oct. 21, drove rivers over their banks, flooded homes and forced hundreds of water rescues across the region.

## A Death in Santa Fe

The lone confirmed fatality occurred Saturday in Santa Fe, New Mexico, when a family attempted to drive across a flooded arroyo, the county sheriff's office said. A father and child managed to reach land, but a female passenger was swept away by the current and found dead about a mile away.

The same evening, a tornado touched down in Santa Fe around 7 p.m., and forecasters were working to confirm whether additional twisters struck elsewhere in the state. Michelle Vialpando, a lifelong Santa Fe resident, photographed the funnel from her patio in the Aldea de Santa Fe neighborhood.

"I was pretty surprised at what I was seeing and just started snapping photos," Vialpando recalled, according to an [earlier AP report](https://apnews.com/article/four-corners-flooding-storm-961459bac9d5db8dc9e5260cac61aec3). "I was literally in shock. I've lived in Santa Fe my entire life and have never seen anything like that before."

## River Cresting Near Record Levels

By Monday morning, the San Juan River stood about 1.5 feet above its 23-foot flood stage, the National Weather Service said. It was expected to crest near 31 feet later Monday before falling below flood stage by Tuesday morning — approaching but not matching the 32-foot crest recorded on Sept. 10, 1927.

Residents of Bluff, Utah, were evacuated over the weekend but allowed to return early Monday. Officials cautioned that several properties remained flooded and urged residents to avoid the river entirely.

"Stay out of the San Juan River," the weather service warned. "Water levels remain elevated, and possible sewage contamination poses additional health risks."

In the Farmington area of New Mexico, swollen rivers were receding, but many people remained evacuated and hundreds were without power. San Juan County officials did not have official figures on the number of homes damaged or people displaced.

"It's a pretty active disaster zone," county spokesperson Devin Neeley told the AP. "We're not on the downswing by any means."

## Rescues and a Saturated Landscape

At least a couple hundred people have needed rescue since the rains began — too many for busy officials to keep an accurate count. New Mexico National Guard helicopters carried out 11 hoist operations, each hauling as many as eight people to safety, some plucked from rooftops.

The flooding affected roughly 2,000 people in and around Farmington, a city of about 46,000. Zach Flatt, whose Bloomfield home sits on the San Juan River, described evacuating with his family Thursday evening as the water surged.

"In 30 or 40 minutes it had jumped about 4 feet," Flatt said. "After I saw that big jump, and how close it was to going over the banks, I said we've got to get out."

The family watched via webcam as at least 18 inches of water inundated their property, washing away 22 of their 24 chickens. "We watched stuff just float away, off the porch into the front yard," Flatt said.

## Contamination a Growing Concern

In the Shiprock area of the Navajo Nation in New Mexico, wastewater facilities and water holding ponds were damaged by flooding, causing sewage releases into the San Juan River, according to Wilfred Franklin Jr., district manager for the Navajo Tribal Utility Authority. Officials did not know as of Sunday how much sewage had entered the river.

Gas and propane lines may also be broken, and downed power lines remain a hazard. Undersheriff Cory Tanner said crews were going house to house to check for dangers — a process that could take days.

"The public is anxious to get back in," San Juan County Fire & Rescue Chief David Vega acknowledged at a news conference. "But I want the public to know there are still hazards out there."

## Colorado and the Broader Pattern

In Colorado, authorities in Archuleta County called the flooding "catastrophic." Teams conducted welfare checks and aerial scanning to gauge the destruction, and as of Sunday evening there were no reports of injuries or loss of life. Around Pagosa Springs, the San Juan River raged at a level unseen in over a century — well over 2 feet higher than a flood just over a year earlier that had been considered a 100-year event. Gov. Jared Polis added Conejos County to the list of places eligible for disaster help.

The disaster is the latest in two months of on-and-off heavy rain driven by tropical moisture coming ashore from the Pacific. After months of drought, other recent deadly flooding struck the Grand Canyon and other parts of New Mexico. Forecasters pointed to El Niño — the natural, temporary warming of parts of the Pacific — as the likely culprit for the unusually heavy rainfall.

"The soils are very saturated right now, so it's not going to take much rain for at least the small creeks and streams to overflow," said Kris Sanders, a meteorologist at the weather service office in Grand Junction, Colorado.

## What's Next

New Mexico's congressional delegation on Sunday urged President Donald Trump to declare an emergency for the state, which would release federal assistance funding. Gov. Michelle Lujan Grisham and Maj. Gen. Miguel Aguilar of the New Mexico National Guard planned to visit San Juan County on Monday to survey damage and meet residents.

Several major routes remained closed, including a four-mile stretch of New Mexico Route 371 south of Farmington, a two-mile section of U.S. Highway 64 west of Shiprock, and U.S. 160 northeast of Pagosa Springs. The Million Dollar Highway between Durango and Ouray has reopened after repairs.

With soils still saturated and rivers only just beginning to recede, officials warned that the danger is far from over.

"This is not over, and we need you to monitor alerts," Farmington Police Chief Steve Hebbe said. Sheriff Shane Ferrari, whose own home took on about 4 feet of water, struck a similar tone: "There's a lot of anxiety, and there's a lot of frustration. But please understand we are blocking those areas for your safety."
