# Microsoft Offers Buyouts to a Quarter of Its Workforce

Microsoft is offering voluntary buyouts to roughly 60,000 employees — about 27% of its global workforce — as the software giant redirects billions of dollars toward artificial intelligence infrastructure, according to [AP News](https://apnews.com/article/microsoft-buyout-ai-layoff-xbox-c5a639f53615be198db1799adf6837e1). The offer, announced Monday, covers eligible workers in the United States, Canada, India, and the United Kingdom.

The move is the latest step in Microsoft's drive to reshape its workforce for the AI era while funding a massive data-center build-out. The company is projected to spend roughly $190 billion on capital expenditures in 2026, largely on AI computing infrastructure.

## A New Kind of Buyout

The announcement came in an internal memo from Amy Coleman, Microsoft's executive vice president and chief people officer, which was [published on the company's official blog](https://blogs.microsoft.com/blog/2026/10/26/introducing-a-voluntary-exit-offer/).

"This offer gives eligible employees who may be considering a future outside the company a new choice to make that transition now with support," Coleman wrote. "It also gives participating organizations another way to adapt their workforce to changing needs."

The program is open to employees at experience level 70 and below, and extends up to the vice president level — higher than an earlier round that was capped at senior director. Not every team is participating. Employees must decide by early December, and those who accept will leave the company on December 31.

Microsoft said it does not have a specific headcount reduction target this time. According to [The New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/10/26/technology/microsoft-buyouts-ai-spending.html), only a sliver of the 60,000 eligible employees are expected to accept, and the company noted that between 3 and 5 percent of tech industry employees typically take such offers.

## Building on Earlier Cuts

The buyout builds on Microsoft's first-ever voluntary retirement program announced in April 2026, which offered early retirement to about 7% of U.S. employees. More than 30% of those eligible accepted. In July, the company [laid off 4,800 workers](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/06/microsoft-cuts-2point1percent-of-employees-as-xbox-unit-plans-to-spin-studios.html), about 2.1% of its global workforce, with substantial cuts at its Xbox gaming business.

The new offer is considerably broader in both geography and seniority. According to [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/technology/microsoft-is-offering-buyouts-around-60000-employees-business-insider-reports-2026-10-26/), staffers across the company would be eligible — including those in AI experiences, Copilot, finance, human resources, Xbox, and Windows + Devices — while units such as marketing, commercial business, and security are excluded.

Microsoft had approximately 223,000 full-time employees globally as of June 30, 2026, down slightly from a flat headcount of about 228,000 in prior years.

## The AI Spending Squeeze

The buyouts are best understood as a lever to offset the financial drag of Microsoft's enormous AI investment. The company has been ramping up spending on data centers to meet high AI computing demand, supplying cloud services to Anthropic, OpenAI, and many others.

Capital expenditures for the September quarter were expected to exceed $50 billion, up from $34.9 billion in the same quarter a year earlier. That spending has put a strain on Microsoft's finances even as its business booms: the company reported $332 billion in revenue in its most recent fiscal year, up 18%, with profit growing 31% to $134 billion.

According to [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/26/microsoft-msft-voluntary-buyouts.html), the strategy aims to make the shift without weakening Microsoft's financial profile. In its second quarter, cloud rivals Alphabet and Amazon both reported negative free cash flow, but Microsoft showed $19.64 billion in positive free cash flow — down 23% year over year.

The broader AI data-center investment boom — likely topping $700 billion across the industry this year — has also made memory chips, processors, and electricity more expensive, and economists expect it to keep pushing up inflation at least through the end of 2026.

## What to Watch

Microsoft reports September-quarter results on Wednesday, October 28, when investors will look for fresh guidance on capital spending and headcount. Shares of the Redmond, Washington-based company hit an all-time high earlier in October and rose about 1% on Monday.

Several questions remain: what severance terms the offer carries — the standard package includes a minimum of eight weeks' base pay, stepping up with tenure — how many employees actually accept by early December, and whether acceptance concentrates in specific functions or geographies. Microsoft has also signaled that further involuntary cuts could follow, particularly at Xbox.

For now, the message is deliberate: Microsoft is framing the reduction as choice, not coercion — a way to adapt its workforce to changing needs while preserving morale and building for an AI-first future.
