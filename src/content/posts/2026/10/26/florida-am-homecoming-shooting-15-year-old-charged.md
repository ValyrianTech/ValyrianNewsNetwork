# 15-Year-Old Charged in Florida A&M Shooting That Hurt 11

A 15-year-old boy has been charged as an adult with 11 counts of attempted second-degree murder in connection with the mass shooting that wounded 11 people near Florida A&M University's Bragg Memorial Stadium, authorities said Monday, unfolding just outside the Tallahassee campus moments after the school's homecoming football game ended.

The suspect, identified by authorities as Anthony Rivers III, was arrested Sunday and also faces one count of possession of a firearm by a delinquent. He appeared in a brief hearing Monday before Leon County circuit court Judge Anthony Sheeran, who ordered him held in secure detention pending arraignment. Rivers did not speak during the proceeding, according to [AP News](https://apnews.com/article/florida-am-shooting-arrest-tallahassee-e91b8d7e82eb8dee7a38df18771d0adf).

## What Happened

The gunfire erupted just before 8 p.m. Saturday near the intersection of Perry and Manatee streets, a crowded corner immediately outside the stadium, as thousands of fans streamed out following the homecoming game against Arkansas-Pine Bluff. Eleven people were struck by bullets, and a twelfth — a grandmother of one of the victims — injured an ankle while fleeing, according to the [Tallahassee Police Department](https://www.talgov.com/Main/News/6100).

Six victims were adults and six were juveniles, the youngest just 11 years old. At least three suffered serious injuries, and one remained in critical condition as of Sunday. Officers carried wounded victims through the dense crowd to waiting ambulances because emergency vehicles could not reach them.

"What should have been a weekend of celebration in our community instead became a night of fear and violence for the victims and their families," Police Chief Lawrence Revell said in a statement. "While this arrest marks a significant step forward, our work is far from over."

## A Dispute Among Acquaintances

Detectives say the shooting grew out of a physical altercation among people known to one another before shots were fired into the crowd. Investigators believe the incident was an isolated dispute and that none of the victims appear to have been intended targets. Surveillance footage captured part of the shooting, State Attorney Jack Campbell said, but police are still seeking additional video and witnesses.

"We know there are people who saw something, heard something or may have video that could help us piece together exactly what happened," Revell said. "No detail is too small."

The investigation remains open, and officials have said additional arrests are possible. Charging a 15-year-old as an adult is a significant legal step; Florida law allows juveniles to be "direct filed" into adult court for serious offenses such as attempted murder, as [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/oct/26/florida-famu-shooting-arrest) reported. Authorities publicly identified the juvenile suspect because of the severity of the charges.

## A Campus in Mourning

Homecoming is one of the defining rituals of Florida A&M, a historically Black university whose fall celebration draws alumni, students and the wider Tallahassee community. The violence struck at the heart of that tradition.

"As a neighbor and community partner, we are providing our full assistance to the Tallahassee police department," university President Marva Johnson said in a video message. "Our pride, our identity, and our future will not be defined by the challenges we face, but how we respond to them together."

Head football coach Quinn Gray struck a similar note. "Homecoming is about family," he wrote on Facebook. "No one should leave our celebration in an ambulance, and no child should ever be caught in gunfire."

The shooting carried painful echoes of a 2022 incident on FAMU's campus, when a bystander was killed and four others wounded in gunfire outside a student recreation center — a case in which two men were later arrested and charged.

## Broader Toll

The FAMU shooting was one of five mass shootings recorded across the United States over the weekend of Oct. 24–25, according to the nonpartisan Gun Violence Archive, which has tallied 386 mass shootings nationwide so far this year. The episode also underscored the recurring vulnerability of large public gatherings, where dense crowds can both obscure assailants and slow the emergency response.

Crowd-control experts have noted that stadium exits — narrow choke points where thousands of people funnel past one another at once — are notoriously difficult to secure without heavy visible policing that many event organizers resist. Saturday's scene, with officers physically carrying victims out, illustrated how quickly such bottlenecks can turn a chaotic end-of-game rush into a mass-casualty event.

## What's Next

Rivers remains in secure detention while prosecutors prepare to move his case into adult court. The State Attorney's office and Tallahassee police have urged anyone who witnessed the shooting or holds photos or video to contact authorities through the department's tip line and digital evidence portal.

For Tallahassee, the coming weeks will bring both a legal reckoning and the harder work of comforting victims — six of whom were children — and a university community confronting yet another act of gun violence at what should have been its most joyful gathering of the year.

"To all the victims, please know that we are with you and we are praying for your recovery, in body, mind and spirit," Johnson said.
