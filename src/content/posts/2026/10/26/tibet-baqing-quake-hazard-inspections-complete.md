# Tibet Quake: Hazard Inspections Near Epicenter Complete

Authorities have completed geological hazard inspections within 50 kilometers of the epicenter of the magnitude-7.0 earthquake that struck Baqing County in Tibet's Nagqu City two days ago, part of an effort to prevent secondary disasters as relief operations continue. The quake, which hit the high-altitude pastoral region on the night of 22-23 October, has left 16 people dead and dozens injured, according to [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/politics/20261026/c5e4535c22f64f77ae93aac993dae2cf/c.html).

## A Race Against Secondary Hazards

The Ministry of Natural Resources (MNR) said it launched a Level III geological hazard defense response and dispatched a seven-member expert team led by a bureau-level official to the quake zone, joining local crews. Rear expert teams used pre- and post-quake remote sensing imagery to analyze 80 glacial lakes and 145 glaciers within the 50-kilometer radius. Their assessment found the quake's overall impact on glaciers and glacial lakes was relatively small, with no glacial lake outburst hazards detected.

The Tibet Autonomous Region's natural resources department separately launched a Level II response, mapping hazard points within 100 kilometers and deploying drones and satellite navigation equipment for emergency inspections. According to the MNR, the system has so far committed 78 personnel, 21 vehicles, 13 drones and 18 teams, inspecting 16 prefabricated and centralized resettlement areas and 77 residential gathering areas. Crews have checked 157 geological hazard points—122 pre-existing and 35 newly identified—and deployed prevention measures at all high-risk sites. No casualties from secondary geological hazards have been reported, and inspections are now expanding to Biru and Sog counties.

## The 'Three Scenes' of Relief

State media framed the relief effort around the theme of "life first" (生命至上), with [People's Daily](http://society.people.com.cn/n1/2026/1026/c1008-40821238.html) organizing its coverage around three vignettes: the Party flag flying over resettlement sites, warmth on a cold night, and the lights of trucks carrying supplies toward the epicenter.

The hardest-hit area is Maru Township, where resettlement sites sit above 4,400 meters. At the No. 2 village site, 86-year-old Yangbeng told the newspaper that cadres visited several times a day and medical staff made regular checks. "They take very good care of us," she said. Zhao Hui, a medical worker from Nierong County People's Hospital and a 16-year Party member, said she set out the night the quake struck. "In such a critical moment, we must charge to the front," she said.

## Power, Communications and Shelter

Partial power outages knocked 628 base stations offline. Repair crews entered the disaster zone at 2 a.m. on 23 October and completed inspection of two main grid lines by 8 a.m., clearing faults one by one. China Mobile restored communications at Maru Township by 4 a.m. on 23 October and deployed a cloud-integrated base station on 24 October to fill coverage gaps in remote pastoral areas.

Resettlement is gathering pace. According to [China News Service](https://www.chinanews.com.cn/tp/hd2011/2026/10-25/1208846.shtml), 60 prefabricated housing units had been erected at the Maru Township No. 2 village site, with residents beginning to move in on the evening of 25 October. More than 12,400 people have been relocated. The State Council raised the national earthquake disaster response from Level IV to Level III, while the National Development and Reform Commission allocated 30 million yuan in central budget investment and the Ministry of Finance and Ministry of Emergency Management disbursed 50 million yuan in relief funds.

## A New Surface Rupture

On the science front, a field team from the China Earthquake Administration on 25 October first discovered a surface rupture zone roughly 15 kilometers east of the epicenter, [CCTV](https://news.cctv.cn/2026/10/26/ARTI1ICNI4d5brbW4w0Fhjir261026.shtml) reported. The rupture was dominated by normal faulting with left-lateral strike-slip movement, with a near-vertical rupture plane, a maximum depth of about 3.5 meters, roughly 0.9 meters of lateral slip and a rupture width of 8 to 30 meters. Investigators also found sand liquefaction and ground fissures, indicating a relatively large area of geological hazard impact.

## What to Watch

The immediate priority is shelter and warmth as winter approaches in one of the world's highest inhabited regions. The broader test will be whether expanded hazard inspections in neighboring counties keep pace with the risk of landslides and aftershocks. For now, official accounts stress that no secondary geological disaster has claimed a single life—a claim that will be measured, in the weeks ahead, against the difficult conditions on the ground.
