# Anthropic Files for Record IPO as CEO Warns of AI Risk

Artificial intelligence giant Anthropic filed paperwork Monday for what could be the largest initial public offering in stock-market history — a move that would turn the five-year-old maker of the Claude chatbot into one of the world's most valuable companies, even as its chief executive warns that advanced AI could pose an existential threat to humanity.

The San Francisco company's public S-1 with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission sets up a Wall Street debut next month that could raise as much as $100 billion, according to reports, potentially eclipsing the $86 billion record set by SpaceX in June, [the Associated Press reported](https://apnews.com/article/anthropic-ipo-shares-public-ca2f4c282bef3201a392e59e3df2300c). Anthropic plans to list on the Nasdaq Global Select Market under the ticker "ANT," with Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley leading a syndicate of at least 22 banks.

The filing lands at a moment of acute tension for the AI boom: investors have grown anxious about the enormous sums being poured into the technology, and Anthropic is racing to tap public markets this year while rival OpenAI waits until next year. Its offering may stand largely alone in what was once expected to be a crowded fall listing season, [Reuters noted](https://www.reuters.com/legal/transactional/anthropic-opens-books-ais-explosive-growth-staggering-costs-2026-10-26/).

## The Safety Paradox at the Center

The defining contradiction of the offering is that a company built around warnings about AI's dangers is now pursuing the most aggressive capital raise in market history. CEO Dario Amodei has spent years urging caution, and in a September essay titled ["We Must Pace the Frontier"](https://darioamodei.com/post/we-must-pace-the-frontier), he argued that the industry should deliberately slow the rate at which it improves AI models. "We must slow the pace at which we improve the capabilities of AI models," Amodei wrote. "Progress will still seem fast, and we must make wise use of the time we gain."

The 335-page filing itself dramatizes the tension. Its risk factors run nearly 100 pages and, as the AP put it, make for riveting reading — spanning everything from the quotidian (failure to retain key staff) to the horrific, including the prospect that AI models could go rogue and "pose catastrophic or existential risks to humanity."

According to [CNBC's coverage](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/26/anthropic-ipo-live-updates-ai-s-1-filing.html), the prospectus warns that sufficiently advanced AI systems could pose such risks "if not developed and deployed responsibly," and concedes that no single insurer — and potentially the industry in aggregate — may be able to underwrite the exposure from a catastrophic AI incident, leaving "potentially significant uninsured losses." In its own filing with the SEC, Anthropic acknowledges that some of its models, "including our own," have exhibited self-preserving behaviors.

## A Business Inflection, and a Staggering Cost

Beneath the philosophy sits a genuinely remarkable financial story. Anthropic's revenue topped $16 billion in the first half of 2026 — roughly 14 times the same period a year earlier. The company reported a net loss of $80.7 billion for the period, though that figure is overwhelmingly a non-cash accounting artifact: most of it stems from marking up convertible notes and derivatives as Anthropic's private valuation soared.

Operationally, the picture is brighter. Anthropic's first-half operating loss narrowed to $693 million, and its Q2 marked the company's first quarter of GAAP operating profit. Redpoint Ventures' Erica Brescia called it "an absolutely blockbuster business" on CNBC, adding that "the positive trend in profitability is extremely impressive, especially given how early it is in this AI transformation."

The ambition comes with a bill. Anthropic has committed to roughly $780 billion to $810 billion in compute and infrastructure obligations, much of it non-cancelable and payable "regardless of actual usage" — about six times its cash pile. Critics note that the company's suppliers double as its lenders, distribution channel, billing agents, and shareholders, a circular structure that skeptics compare to 2008-era financial engineering.

## Governance Engineered for Control

The filing also reveals an unconventional governance structure designed to insulate founders and the safety mission. A single share of special Class F stock, held by an LLC owned by the seven co-founders, will carry 50.1% of total shareholder votes — capping public Class A holders at 49.9%, effectively without a say over board seats. A Long-Term Benefit Trust, holding a single Class T share, elects a majority of the board, intended to ensure safety is not subordinated to financial interests. Anthropic will be a Nasdaq "controlled company."

For the founders, the payoff is extraordinary. Dario Amodei, 43, and his sister and co-founder Daniela Amodei, 39, are each worth $15.5 billion according to Forbes, and the filing suggests each could wind up holding 1.4% of the company — a windfall of as much as $28 billion apiece at the top expected valuation.

## Political Headwinds

Anthropic also carries unusually public baggage. In February, President Donald Trump and Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth designated the company a supply-chain risk, and Anthropic sued the Pentagon in March over what it called an "unlawful campaign of retaliation" for refusing to allow unrestricted military use of its technology. A California judge ruled in Anthropic's favor, while a Washington, D.C. judge sided with the government in a separate case; both sides are appealing.

## What to Watch

The offering's final size, valuation, and timing remain unconfirmed. Reports point to a valuation of $1.5 trillion to $2 trillion and a debut as early as the week of November 16. At $1.5 trillion, Anthropic would rank among the world's most valuable public companies. Whether public markets can — or should — price the kind of existential risk Anthropic's own CEO keeps warning about is the question the ANT listing will finally force investors to answer.
