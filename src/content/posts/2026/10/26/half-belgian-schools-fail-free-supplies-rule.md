# Half of Belgian Schools Fail Free Supplies Rule

More than half of schools in French-speaking Belgium do not fully comply with the requirement to provide school supplies free of charge, according to a new study by the Ligue des Familles and two parents' associations. The survey of 1,578 parents across more than 1,000 schools in the Fédération Wallonie-Bruxelles found that in 51% of cases, schools failed to fully respect the rules that took effect this 2026-2027 school year, [RTBF reported](https://www.rtbf.be/article/plus-de-la-moitie-des-ecoles-ne-respectent-pas-la-gratuite-des-fournitures-scolaires-selon-la-ligue-des-familles-11804994).

Only 44% of concerned parents — those with children from first maternelle to fifth primary — said their school provided all the necessary material. Meanwhile, 21% received a list of supplies to buy themselves, such as pencils, erasers, folders and calculators, and 19% had to purchase textbooks and exercise books used in class.

## A Right Eroded in Practice

The Ligue des Familles, working with UFAPEC and FAPEO — the parents' bodies for Catholic and official education respectively — frames the findings as an equity problem. "Free schooling is a fundamental right that is not only enshrined in the Constitution, but also in the international treaties Belgium has ratified," said Merlin Gevers, a research officer at the Ligue des Familles. "It is an obligation on our public authorities."

Gevers described the trend as "a degradation of the situation compared with previous years," noting that "today, only 44% of pupils receive all the necessary material — that's fewer than one pupil in two." A 2024 Ligue survey had found that eight in ten schools respected the free-supplies rules under the previous system.

## The Policy Paradox

The organisations point to a structural contradiction at the heart of the system. This year, the Fédération Wallonie-Bruxelles government extended the free-supplies obligation from fourth to fifth primary — with sixth primary to follow next year — while sharply cutting per-child funding. According to [RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/fournitures-scolaires-la-ligue-des-familles-demande-au-gouvernement-d-adapter-le-financement-par-eleve-juge-insuffisant-11771809), the subsidy was roughly €75 per child previously but has fallen to about €24 this year.

The Ligue argues this makes compliance structurally near-impossible. "How do you provide quality material to every pupil, everywhere and at every moment they need it to learn, when the means have been sharply reduced?" Gevers asked. "Schools try to manage as best they can, but this creates a lot of problems on the ground because it leads to different practices."

## Inside a School

The strain is visible at the school level. Patrick Ronsse, director of Saint-Henri primary school in Woluwe-Saint-Lambert, Brussels, estimates the establishment will lose €13,000 this year. "We are being asked to apply the principle of free schooling to more classes while the budget is being cut, which is peculiar," he said. "We had to offer lower-quality products... we used the school's own funds to compensate." The school also had to postpone renovation works.

The Ligue documented troubling practices, including schools preventing children from taking school-supplied material home — forcing parents to buy duplicates for homework — conditioning access to supplies on a formal parental request, and soliciting "solidarity contributions" or donations.

The study itself is politically contested. Education Minister Valérie Glatigny (MR) has previously questioned the Ligue's methodology and representativeness, with MR deputy Stéphanie Cortisse urging prudence in a tense parliamentary commission last year, [La Libre reported](https://www.lalibre.be/belgique/enseignement/2025/11/04/valerie-glatigny-a-t-elle-remis-en-question-la-legitimite-de-la-ligue-des-familles-lors-dune-commission-tendue-jai-repondu-de-facon-factuelle-UZT7HIP4C5B5DEDCDYW5SNEVN4/). The Ligue counters that an official Inspection survey also found widespread non-compliance, with three in four schools failing to respect all the rules, per its [rules guidance](https://liguedesfamilles.be/article/frais-scolaires-2026-2027-quelles-sont-les-regles).

## Three Demands

The Ligue des Familles, FAPEO and UFAPEC are calling on Minister Glatigny to ensure adequate funding for schools to meet the free-supplies obligation, clarify the rules to end ambiguous interpretations, and relaunch a systematic inspection of school fees — a scrutiny the associations say is essential to monitoring whether schools comply in practice.

## What to Watch

The dispute sits within broader austerity in the Fédération Wallonie-Bruxelles, where the government is targeting deep savings while extending an emblematic egalitarian measure. As an [earlier RTBF analysis](https://www.rtbf.be/article/fin-de-la-gratuite-des-fournitures-scolaires-en-federation-wallonie-bruxelles-enjeux-et-repercussions-11614795) noted, ending free supplies was estimated to add about €104 per child per year to family costs — and to risk stigmatising children as the system shifted toward targeting only the most precarious families, with criteria set by each school.

The coming months will test whether the government answers the call for funding and clearer rules — or whether families and schools continue to absorb the gap between the letter of the free-education principle and its practice.
