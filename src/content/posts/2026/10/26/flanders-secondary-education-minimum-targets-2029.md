# Flanders Sets Tougher Secondary Targets for Dutch, Maths

The Flemish Government has approved new minimum attainment targets for secondary education, to take effect in September 2029, [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/10/25/nieuwe-minimumdoelen-in-middelbaar-onderwijs/) reports. The reform, championed by Education Minister Zuhal Demir of the nationalist N-VA, raises the bar especially high for Dutch and mathematics and restructures the curriculum around individual subjects rather than broad cross-curricular competencies.

The decision, taken on Friday 24 October, follows the earlier overhaul of attainment targets for nursery and primary education. It marks a deliberate attempt to make Flemish schooling a single, continuous progression, from kindergarten to the final year of secondary school.

## A Shift from Competencies to Subjects

At the heart of the reform is a structural simplification. The current system is built around 16 broad key competencies that cut across subjects, such as spatial awareness and sustainability. These are to be replaced by concrete, subject-specific goals for disciplines such as Dutch, mathematics, history and science. The so-called overlapping goal frameworks that linked the competencies together will disappear.

"We have sometimes made education too complicated," Demir said. "We talk about 16 key competencies, while ultimately it is about something very simple: what must our children know and be able to do in different subjects like history, maths or Dutch? We simplify the rules without lowering the bar."

Some general goals, including citizenship and financial literacy, are retained, but they will now be anchored in the subject knowledge pupils are expected to acquire.

## Higher Bar for Dutch and Maths

The most visible change concerns language and numeracy. At the end of the first grade of secondary school, every individual pupil must reach the higher minimum targets for Dutch and mathematics, though the class council retains the final say. For other subjects, schools must ensure the targets are sufficiently achieved at the group level, a distinction whose practical meaning is reportedly not yet clear.

The reasoning is continuity. "It would be absurd that we raise the bar in primary education, only to let it drop again in secondary school," Demir said. "Education must form one logical whole. What you learn in one year must be the basis for the next. When the first pupils enter secondary school in September 2029 with their new knowledge, that education must be ready to build on it." The 2029 start date was chosen precisely because that is when the first cohort educated under the new primary targets moves up.

## Tailored Frameworks for Different Pupils

Separate goal frameworks are being created for pupils in the A-stream and the B-stream, the latter for students who did not obtain a primary certificate or who come from special primary education. A clear goal framework is also being introduced for special secondary education, with goals linked to daily life and independence in living, working and participating in society for pupils in training forms 1 and 2.

The concrete targets will now be developed per subject by scientists and education specialists under an independent expert commission, and must be specific enough to verify whether pupils have effectively mastered the material. The framework is currently a draft decree that still requires approval by the Flemish Parliament before the detailed targets are elaborated.

## Change Fatigue and Legal Scrutiny

The reform is not without risk. The current secondary targets were themselves only recently introduced, in 2023, after a turbulent process during which the Constitutional Court annulled a version of them for constraining freedom of education too severely.

Katrijn Denies, an education researcher at KU Leuven, told [De Morgen](https://www.demorgen.be/nieuws/demir-wil-vanaf-2029-nieuwe-minimumdoelen-in-het-secundair-met-focus-op-kennis-een-leraar-wiskunde-is-opgeleid-om-wiskunde-te-geven~b25735be/) that the content rationale is sound but the practical burden is significant. "From a content perspective it is very logical that work is being done on new minimum targets, but for teachers and schools this is again a major adjustment," she said. "They have already been working for three years to get to grips with the current targets and will soon have to start again."

Demir argues that the field will get more breathing room this time. "We have the time from now to 2029, that is much more than in primary education," she said. "A maths teacher is trained to teach maths. Over the years we have made a whole tangle of all kinds of objectives and rules. We want to get rid of that."

## What to Watch

Much still depends on the years ahead. The Flemish Parliament must approve the framework, and the concrete targets have yet to be written. Two open questions will shape the reform's success: what group-level achievement will mean in practice for non-core subjects, and whether schools receive enough support to implement a third set of targets within roughly a decade.

For now, the political message is clear. "Knowledge is not an old-fashioned concept," Demir said. "Those who know a lot can understand things better, think more critically and make independent choices. That is precisely what we want to give our young people." Whether that ambition translates into better outcomes, without overwhelming the teachers expected to deliver it, is the test that lies ahead.
