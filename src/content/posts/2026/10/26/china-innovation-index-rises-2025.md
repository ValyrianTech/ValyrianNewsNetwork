# China's Innovation Index Rises 6.4% in 2025

China's national innovation index reached 185.5 in 2025, a 6.4% increase over the previous year, according to the [National Bureau of Statistics (NBS)](https://www.stats.gov.cn/sj/zxfbhjd/202610/t20261023_1965677.html). The gain was 1.1 percentage points higher than the prior year's growth, and places measured innovation performance 85.5% above its 2015 baseline.

The index, compiled by the NBS Department of Social, Science and Technology Statistics under its "China Innovation Index Research" programme, aggregates 18 indicator indexes across four pillars: innovation environment, input, output, and effectiveness. All four sub-indices rose, led by output — a pattern that underscores both the breadth of China's research buildup and the persistent gap between how much the country invests and how much it converts into productivity.

## A four-pillar breakdown

The strongest pillar was the **Innovation Output Index, which climbed 8.0% to 233.0** — the fastest-growing area and a signal of China's expanding scientific footprint. Within it, all four indicators rose, led by high-value invention patents held per 10,000 R&D personnel, which grew 12.1% — the fourth consecutive year of double-digit gains. Drawing on more detailed figures [reported by People's Daily](https://www.cernet.edu.cn/rd/zui_jin_geng_xin/202610/t20261023_2785727.shtml), China held 2.292 million high-value invention patents at the end of 2025, equal to 43.1% of all valid domestic invention patents, or 16 per 10,000 people. The country ranks first globally in papers indexed by SCI and EI and second in CPCI; its 155,000 high-level international journal papers account for 39.2% of the world total, and it ranks first in both paper output and citations.

The **Innovation Environment Index rose 7.5% to 199.9**, with all five component indicators up. The share of enterprises benefiting from the R&D super-deduction tax credit jumped 19.1%, and science and engineering graduates as a share of the relevant age cohort rose 8.7%. In 2025, China produced 3.306 million STEM graduates at bachelor's level and above — 51.7% of all graduates, up 1.0 percentage point.

The **Innovation Input Index increased 5.9% to 172.2**, underpinned by total R&D spending of 3.92882 trillion yuan, up 8.2% year-on-year. Enterprises contributed 73.7% of that growth. Notably, basic research spending rose 13.3% to 283.464 billion yuan — 5.1 percentage points faster than overall R&D — lifting its share of total research funding to 7.21%. Policymakers have long identified basic research as a structural weak spot relative to applied work, and the continued two-digit rise is framed as a step toward closing that gap.

## The weak link: effectiveness

The **Innovation Effectiveness Index was the laggard, rising just 2.9% to 137.0**. Four of its five indicators advanced, but one — the share of patent-intensive industries in GDP — was flat. Labor productivity reached 184,413 yuan per person, up 6.1%. The clearest measure of commercial conversion, the **new-product sales revenue share of operating revenue, rose 4.5%** — well below the 8.0% gain in the output index. In absolute terms, above-scale industrial firms generated 36.4 trillion yuan in new-product sales, up 2.5% year-on-year and equal to 26.5% of operating revenue, while the "Three New" economy — new industries, business forms and models — grew its value-added 6.2% to 25.8 trillion yuan, or 18.39% of GDP.

That divergence is telling. As NBS statistician Lin Mei put it, the index reflects an innovation environment "continuously optimized," input "steadily increasing," output "growing relatively quickly," and effectiveness "continuously rising" — but the data show that quantitative output is outpacing commercial conversion. The effectiveness pillar, by far the weakest, fuels the familiar critique that China's strengths are concentrated upstream, in inputs and publication volume, more than downstream in productivity gains.

## Context and caveats

The index is a domestic composite measure base-lined to 2015, not an international ranking. It should not be confused with the WIPO Global Innovation Index, which in 2025 placed China 10th globally — its first time in the top ten, as [reported by the NBS](https://www.stats.gov.cn/zs/tjwh/tjkw/tjqk/zgxxb/202602/P020260228312118523231.pdf) and [People's Daily](https://paper.people.com.cn/rmrb/pc/attachement/202601/05/baa61884-92c1-4ca3-ba26-3afe76dc8ee3.pdf). The two are different measures: the NBS index is a self-referential time series, while the GII is a cross-country comparative metric. Reporting sometimes blurs the two.

Observers also caution that the 6.4% headline is a policy-aligned official statistic whose indicator weights and methodology are set by the NBS itself, limiting independent verification. It arrives as Beijing continues to emphasize "self-reliance and self-strengthening in science and technology," a priority reinforced since 2018 and embedded in successive Five-Year Plan frameworks.

## What to watch

China's innovation output is expanding faster than at any point in the past year, and the basic research momentum is real. The open question is whether the effectiveness pillar — the bridge between research and commercial productivity — can keep pace. A flat patent-intensive-industry share of GDP and a new-product sales share index growing at just 4.5%, against 8.0% output growth, suggest the conversion challenge remains the frontier to watch in coming index releases.
