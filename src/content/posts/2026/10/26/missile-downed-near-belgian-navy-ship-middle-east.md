# Missile Downed Near Belgian Navy Ship in Middle East

A ballistic missile was shot down near the Belgian Navy minehunter *Primula* in the Bab el-Mandeb strait in mid-October, a French escort frigate intercepting the projectile before it could reach the vessel. No injuries were reported among the Belgian crew, and military analysts say the ship was most likely not the intended target.

The incident, first reported by [De Morgen](https://www.demorgen.be/nieuws/nooit-meegemaakt-ballistische-raket-neergehaald-nabij-belgische-mijnenjager-in-midden-oosten~b0941dee/) and later confirmed to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/10/26/belgische-mijnenjager-primula-defensie-ballistische-raket-midden/), occurred on 14 October as the minehunter was transiting one of the world's most strategic maritime chokepoints. It marks a rare brush with a ballistic threat for a Belgian warship and underscores the risks facing European naval deployments in a region increasingly defined by missile and drone warfare.

## What Happened

According to [HLN](https://www.hln.be/buitenland/raket-neergehaald-nabij-belgische-mijnenjager-in-midden-oosten-dat-is-een-zware-bedreiging~aa9849ef/), the missile was detected near the *Primula* and neutralised by a French escort vessel. The minehunter, an older-generation ship designed to locate and destroy sea mines, carries no capability to intercept incoming rockets on its own — which is why it sails under escort in these waters.

The *Primula* has been deployed south of the Arabian peninsula since the summer, awaiting a possible demining mission in the Strait of Hormuz. It was heading toward that strait when the missile was intercepted near Bab el-Mandeb, the 32-kilometre-wide passage linking the Red Sea to the Indian Ocean.

A French investigation into the incident is ongoing, and Paris has kept details limited. Belgium's military has declined to comment, as has Defence Minister Theo Francken of the N-VA. Military operations are discussed in the federal parliament behind closed doors.

## Not the Intended Target, Analysts Say

Sources cited by De Morgen said there were "no indications" that the *Primula* and its escort were deliberately targeted, raising the possibility that the missile was a warning shot or a show of force. The missile was probably fired by the Houthis, the Iran-aligned rebel group that controls much of western Yemen, including the coastline along the Red Sea.

"It is very unlikely that the minehunter was the target of a ballistic missile attack," defence expert Jens Franssen told VRT NWS. "Such a missile flies very high and very fast, which makes it difficult to hit moving targets like a sailing ship. In such a case an anti-ship missile is usually used."

Even so, the event resonated among military professionals. "This is a very serious incident," said Colonel (ret.) Roger Housen, a military analyst. "In my entire career in the army I have never known a ballistic missile to be shot down near a Belgian ship. That is a serious threat."

## A Strategic Chokepoint Under Pressure

The Bab el-Mandeb strait has been volatile for years. As [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/07/22/houthi-blokkade-bab-el-mandeb/) has documented, the Houthis have repeatedly disrupted shipping through the passage — attacking dozens of cargo ships and tankers after the outbreak of the Gaza war in 2023 and, more recently, threatening a blockade targeting vessels carrying Saudi cargo. Ships that avoid the strait must take a far longer route around Africa via the Cape of Good Hope, adding 10 to 15 days and driving up transport costs.

The broader picture is one of overlapping crises. Iran has effectively controlled the Strait of Hormuz since early 2026, and the United States and Iran have been at war for roughly eight months. Saudi Arabia recently launched an offensive to push the Houthis back from the Red Sea coast, and the rebels responded with missile and drone strikes on Saudi targets.

Notably, the Houthis promised the European Union at the end of September that they would no longer attack European ships. It remains unclear whether the *Primula* incident puts that commitment at risk.

## Belgium's Thin Naval Capacity

Belgium fields only two frigates of its own, both outdated, according to De Morgen. New high-tech minehunters are entering service, but delivery of replacement frigates is years behind schedule. That gap leaves Belgian vessels dependent on allied escorts for protection in high-threat environments — a reality laid bare by this incident.

The deployment also carries political weight at home. Within the Belgian government, CD&V and Vooruit have voiced doubts about sending the navy to Hormuz, and any demining mission there depends on a US-Iran ceasefire.

## What to Watch

Three questions will shape what comes next. First, whether the French investigation confirms the missile's origin and intent — and whether the Houthis deliberately fired near the ship. Second, whether the incident strains the rebels' pledge to spare European vessels. And third, whether it hardens or softens Belgian political resistance to a Hormuz deployment.

For now, the *Primula* is back in safe waters. But the episode is a pointed reminder that even a minesweeper on a defensive mission can find itself inside the arc of a ballistic missile in today's Middle East.
