# Charleroi Named an Innovation City at UN Mayors Forum

The Belgian city of Charleroi has been designated an "Innovation City" by the World Intellectual Property Organization (WIPO) at the United Nations Forum of Mayors, the city announced. The recognition places the Walloon municipality alongside global peers and marks a symbolic milestone in its decades-long effort to reinvent its post-industrial identity.

The city confirmed the designation on Sunday evening in a statement, according to [RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/charleroi-designee-ville-de-l-innovation-lors-du-forum-des-maires-de-l-onu-11805887), Belgium's French-language public broadcaster.

## A Jury Drawn to a New Economic Model

According to the City of Charleroi, the WIPO jury was particularly impressed by local initiatives that combine training, research, and industrial entrepreneurship. Among the projects singled out were the A6K-E6K innovation and training hub, as well as the regional leadership the city has built in sectors including bioproduction, biotechnology, and aerospace.

The award is given to no more than three cities each year. It highlights metropolitan areas that foster innovation, creativity, and economic transformation. This year, Charleroi shares the honor with Liverpool and Shanghai, two far larger and more globally prominent cities.

## From the 'Black Country' to a Forward-Looking City

For Charleroi, the designation carries meaning beyond a trophy. The city is the largest in Wallonia and was historically one of Belgium's industrial heartlands, known for coal mining, steel, and manufacturing. In the 20th century it earned the nickname *Pays Noir*—Black Country—for its coal industry and heavy manufacturing.

The decline of heavy industry in the late 20th century brought economic hardship and a reputation for post-industrial decay. In recent years, however, Charleroi has pursued an ambitious program of urban renewal and rebranding, investing in culture, technology, and innovation. The city has become known for its distinctive industrial heritage, repurposed architecture, and a growing creative economy.

The WIPO recognition at the UN Forum of Mayors gives that transformation international visibility—placing Charleroi in a conversation with some of the world's most dynamic cities rather than in the shadow of its industrial past.

## Why the UN Forum Matters

The UN Forum of Mayors is a platform that brings city leaders together to discuss urban challenges, sustainable development, and innovative solutions to local governance. Recognition at this forum situates Charleroi alongside other innovative cities on the international stage—a notable shift in a reputation that was long anchored in the scars of deindustrialization.

## What to Watch

The designation is a marker of momentum rather than an end in itself. The key question is whether Charleroi can convert this international spotlight into sustained investment, partnerships, and jobs—particularly in the high-value sectors the jury cited. For a city that has spent decades redefining itself, the prize is both an endorsement of how far it has come and a test of what it does next.
