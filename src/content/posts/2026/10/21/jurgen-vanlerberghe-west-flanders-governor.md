# Vanlerberghe becomes West Flanders' first socialist governor

Vooruit has designated Jurgen Vanlerberghe as the new governor of West Flanders, making him the first socialist to hold the province's top administrative post. The 57-year-old, currently a provincial deputy, succeeds Carl Decaluwé, who has governed since 2012 and is retiring. According to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/10/19/jurgen-vanlerberghe-portret/), the nomination was confirmed on 20 October, with the formal oath of office expected around mid-November.

The appointment marks a notable political shift. The governorship of West Flanders had long been associated with the Christian-democratic CD&V, and Vanlerberghe's selection reflects Vooruit's strengthened position in the province.

## A career officer turned politician

Vanlerberghe was born in 1969 in Roeselare and grew up in Poperinge, where he still lives. He began his career in the military, training at the Royal Cadet School and the Royal Military School before serving as a professional officer in the Belgian army until 2000, rising to the rank of captain.

Around the turn of the century he moved into politics, becoming an adviser to then deputy prime minister Johan Vande Lanotte. He was elected as a municipal councillor in Poperinge in 2001 and later served as alderman. At provincial level he sat as a provincial councillor before succeeding John Crombez as a Flemish Member of Parliament in 2011, where he focused on agriculture, fisheries and rural policy.

After an unsuccessful run for the Chamber of Representatives in 2014, he worked on the sp.a research service and later became cabinet chief at the Province of West Flanders. Since December 2018 he has been a provincial deputy responsible for Environment, Nature, Tourism and ICT, and he chairs the provincial tourism agency Westtoer.

## A "green soul" and a small entrepreneur

As a deputy in a largely rural province, Vanlerberghe has placed considerable emphasis on the environment. "Creating more forest in West Flanders remains important to me as a deputy," he said. His green positions brought him into conflict with Flemish environment minister Jo Brouns (CD&V) over higher standards for triazoles in West Flemish drinking water — a dossier that will test how he positions himself now that he is expected to be politically neutral.

Beyond politics, Vanlerberghe works behind the scenes in his wife's clothing boutique, an experience he has said made the COVID-19 pandemic a confronting period for small entrepreneurs.

## "I didn't doubt for a second"

Reacting to his nomination, Vanlerberghe was emphatic. "I'm really looking forward to it, I didn't doubt for a second," he said, as [reported](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/10/20/nieuwe-gouverneur-west-vlaanderen-jurgen-vanlerberghe/) by VRT NWS. "I entered politics to get things moving, to have an impact. I see this as a new challenge that comes my way." He thanked Vooruit leaders Conner Rousseau and Melissa Depraetere for the opportunity.

Depraetere, a minister and the party's leading figure in West Flanders, described him as "a West Fleming through and through — someone who would rather stand with his boots in the mud than sit neatly in a suit behind his desk. A hard worker who knows every trade."

## Safety and crisis planning first

Vanlerberghe intends to begin with safety and emergency planning, drawing on his military background. "A governor has to be there when things go wrong. My background at Defence taught me what crisis management really demands," he said. "That's why I want to know the emergency plans before we need them. Especially with real risks like flooding and drought, water policy and emergency planning now go hand in hand."

Rousseau framed security as the thread running through Vanlerberghe's career. "From crisis management to the fight against flooding, he always puts protecting the people of West Flanders first. And that is exactly what the governor's job is about," he said.

## What's next

The formal procedure still has to run its course: the Flemish government must ratify the federal government's decision, a process expected to take about three weeks before the oath of office around mid-November. Until then, Vanlerberghe will wind up his current duties as a deputy while familiarising himself with the governor's files.

His rise also carries a wider political significance. The governorship had been widely expected to go to Vooruit amid coalition negotiations, even as speculation swirled around CD&V's Hilde Crevits — a dynamic that [De Tijd](https://www.tijd.be/politiek-economie/belgie/vlaanderen/alle-ogen-op-hilde-crevits-na-vertrek-van-carl-decaluwe-als-gouverneur-west-vlaanderen/10687886.html) noted when it reported that the post was "actually for Vooruit." Who will replace Vanlerberghe as provincial deputy remains unknown.

For a coastal, rural province exposed to real risks of flooding and drought, the choice of a crisis-tested, environmentally minded governor is unlikely to be a coincidence. The question now is how the first socialist governor in West Flanders' history will translate that profile into a politically neutral office.
