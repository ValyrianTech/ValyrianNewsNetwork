# Lukas Dhont pushes boundaries with WWI romance Coward

Belgian director Lukas Dhont has released his third feature film, Coward, a First World War romance that marks a deliberate departure into the war genre for the filmmaker behind Girl and Close. The film is now in Belgian cinemas, was the opening film of Film Fest Gent, and is Belgium's official submission for the 2027 Oscars in the Best International Feature category, according to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/10/19/lukas-dhont-verlegt-zijn-grenzen-met-coward-een-oorlogsfilm-v/).

## A new direction without abandoning intimacy

Coward keeps love at its centre, but this time against the explosive backdrop of the WWI front. Gunfire and bodies in the mud share the screen with dance and music. Dhont, who won the Caméra d'Or at Cannes in 2018 with Girl and the Grand Prix in 2022 with Close, has said war films are "the genre I like least" — yet he calls Coward his sweetest and warmest film to date.

His previous work explored a transgender girl's ballet ambitions in Girl and an intense teenage friendship in Close. Coward reworks that signature focus on tenderness between young men, now set within one of history's most brutal conflicts.

## A hidden history behind the front lines

The story began with a single black-and-white photograph: a Belgian soldier behind the front line, dressed as a woman. Fascinated, Dhont researched further, including at the Imperial War Museum in London, and found more such images — young men staging revues during the war to distract themselves and their comrades from the horror of the front. Such performances emerged in armies around the world, and Dhont even found photographs of German soldiers performing dressed as women.

That history inspired Dhont and screenwriter Angelo Tijssens to tell the story of Pierre, a young man sent to the front who deliberately wounds himself so he is reassigned to the "Verstotelingen," a troop of theatre-makers operating behind the lines. There he meets Francis, the flamboyant showmaster of the troupe, and a turbulent romance begins.

## First-time actors win at Cannes

The film competed for the Palme d'Or at Cannes, where its two leads — Emmanuel Macchia as Pierre and Valentin Campagne as Francis — jointly won the Best Actor prize, as [VRT NWS reported](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/05/24/coward-lukas-dhondt-wint-filmprijzen-in-cannes-reacties/). Both were first-time actors discovered by Dhont: Macchia was found during a casting at his school, where he studied garden and landscape architecture.

Dhont is known for casting talent-spotting in unconventional places — Victor Polster (Girl) was found at a ballet school, and Eden Dambrine (Close) was approached on a train. He deliberately chooses inexperienced actors because they embody the feeling of "the first time": the first time at the front, the first time far from home, the first time falling in love with another boy.

"You'll hardly find anyone more believable than Emmanuel Macchia and Valentin Campagne," VRT film journalist Lieven Van Gils wrote in his review of the film.

## Tenderness amid the horror

The film contrasts the horror of war — young men collecting and removing the bodies of fallen comrades — with moments of exceptional tenderness: soldiers caring for each other after an attack, helping each other back to their feet, and the slowly deepening intimacy between Pierre and Francis. Van Gils described "moments of exceptional tenderness" sitting among the brutality.

A central paradox drives the story. "Here we are free," says Francis, aware the freedom is temporary. Within the sheltered world of the theatre troupe their love can blossom, but the film asks what happens when the war ends and the two return to civilian life — one to a farm, the other to his parents' clothing shop. Do they dare choose each other then? And who, in the end, is the real coward?

Rights to the film were sold before its world premiere at Cannes, securing distribution in the US, Spain, Italy, Latin America and the UK.

## What's next

With strong festival momentum, a Cannes acting prize for its two newcomers and Belgium's Oscar nomination in hand, Coward is positioned as a major Belgian cultural export for the 2026-2027 awards cycle. Audiences can judge for themselves now that it is in cinemas.
