# China Issues Top-Level Plan to Fuse Science and Industry

China's two most senior administrative bodies — the General Office of the CPC Central Committee and the General Office of the State Council — jointly released an opinion on 21 October 2026 aimed at deeply integrating scientific and technological innovation with industrial innovation, according to [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/politics/20261021/a0b107f4625f417384f5b8ab492739a0/c.html). The document, a comprehensive national framework of eight parts and 17 specific measures, seeks to channel research breakthroughs directly into industrial application and economic growth.

## Context: A Plenum-Driven Priority

The opinion arrives just after the CPC's 20th Central Committee 4th Plenum, which designated the deep integration of sci-tech and industrial innovation as one of the main objectives for socioeconomic development during the 15th Five-Year Plan period (2026–2030). According to the [Ministry of Science and Technology (MOST)](https://www.most.gov.cn/xxgk/xinxifenlei/fdzdgknr/fgzc/zcjd/202610/t20261021_197541.html), the document breaks with the habitual logic of "producing results first and considering transformation later," embedding industrial application goals into the design, evaluation, and funding of national R&D projects.

The opinion states its core objective plainly: to develop "new quality productive forces" and build a modernized industrial system, with a focus on constructing an enterprise-led, multi-actor system of integrated industry-university-research-application innovation. Its stated ambition is to "open the channel from a strong science and technology to a strong industry, strong economy, and strong nation."

## The Six Key Task Areas

The full text, [published by Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/politics/20261021/a9cfacac1ab24b778c03b6f05012e99c/c.html), organizes its measures around six priority areas: seamlessly connecting the innovation chain with the industrial chain; supporting a modernized industrial system through high-quality sci-tech supply; strengthening enterprise-led collaborative innovation; improving the coordinated allocation of innovation resources; creating a sound ecosystem for integration; and expanding development space.

Several named technology domains signal where Beijing intends to concentrate its bets. The opinion calls for "decisive breakthroughs" in integrated circuits, industrial mother machines, high-end instruments, advanced materials, and biomanufacturing. In frontier fields, it targets quantum technology, hydrogen and fusion energy, brain-computer interfaces, embodied intelligence, and 6th-generation (6G) mobile communications.

## A Bet on Enterprises and the Private Sector

A central shift in the document is its emphasis on enterprises as the primary innovation actors. It encourages the National Natural Science Foundation (NSFC) to pilot programs open to private enterprises, calls for raising the R&D expense super-deduction ratio for tech SMEs, and urges state-owned enterprises to establish R&D reserve funds exempt from value-preservation assessment. Tech leading enterprises are encouraged to act as "chain leaders" and form innovation consortia.

The financial architecture is equally detailed. The opinion envisions a sci-tech finance system matched to the full lifecycle funding needs of tech enterprises, with government and state-capital venture funds directed to "invest early, invest small, invest long-term, and invest in hard tech." It also proposes an innovation points system, a sci-tech board in the bond market, and a sci-tech insurance policy system.

## Regulatory Balance and Local Pilots

On regulation, the opinion strikes a deliberately permissive tone for emerging sectors. It calls for piloting relaxed market access in new business formats, exploring "sandbox regulation," and improving laws for frontier fields such as artificial intelligence and biotechnology. It also mandates full implementation of the patent open licensing system and better fault-tolerance mechanisms for tech transfer and venture investment.

As [Science and Technology Daily](https://www.stdaily.com/web/gdxw/2026-10/21/content_600313.html) summarized, the document further supports localities in carrying out early and pilot trials of deep integration, strengthens central-local cost-sharing for sci-tech investment, and encourages national high-tech development zones and independent innovation demonstration zones to test policies first.

## Analysis: Operationalizing a Slogan

The opinion is best read as the operational blueprint for "new quality productive forces," a concept Xi Jinping has promoted since 2023 to describe productivity driven by technological breakthrough and high quality. By assigning enterprises leadership over applied national projects and reforming the ownership of job-related scientific achievements, the document directly targets China's long-standing "valley of death" between laboratory results and market application.

The measures also carry a reassurance signal for the private tech sector, following the regulatory crackdown of 2020–2022. Opening NSFC programs to private firms and shielding R&D reserves from state-asset assessment are calibrated to boost private investment confidence. At the same time, the emphasis on self-reliance in integrated circuits and industrial mother machines responds to external export controls by prioritizing bottleneck technologies.

## What's Next

As with most top-level Opinions, the document sets direction rather than detailed rules. The concrete test will come in follow-on implementing regulations, funding allocations, and the rollout of local pilot programs. The Ministry of Science and Technology is positioned as the lead implementing ministry, with coordination expected across the NDRC, MIIT, and finance, education, and human-resources authorities. How quickly enterprises respond — and whether the promised reforms to achievement commercialization reach the grassroots — will determine whether the framework delivers on its ambitious goals.
