# Brussels bans e-scooters in drug hotspots, opens door to curfews

Brussels minister-president Boris Dilliès (MR) signed a permanent regional police decree on Wednesday that bans electric scooters in 19 drug hotspots and gives mayors the power to impose emergency measures — including a curfew — when security deteriorates. The decree replaces temporary measures that had been renewed every six months since 2024, according to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/10/21/plan-voor-aanpak-drugsgeweld-brussels-gewest/).

The announcement lands against a year of sustained gun violence in the Belgian capital. Regional figures record 74 shootings between 1 January and 30 September 2026, leaving 2 people dead and 28 wounded — incidents the authorities link to conflicts in the drug milieu.

## From rolling renewals to a permanent framework

The region frames the shift as a bid for speed and coordination. Until now, Brussels' anti-drug security measures have operated on six-month cycles since the regional strategy launched in 2024, requiring repeated renewal. Under the new decree, the framework becomes permanent and — officials argue — easier to adapt to conditions on the ground.

The centerpiece is a ban on electric scooters in 19 designated hotspots, covering both shared and privately owned devices, and extending to their use and parking. The region justifies the measure with a statistic: between January 2025 and July 2026, an electric (shared) scooter featured in more than a quarter of drug-related shootings, either to fire weapons or to flee. As [BRUZZ](https://www.bruzz.be/actua/samenleving/boris-dillies-verbiedt-alle-steps-negentien-drugshotspots-2026-10-21) reported, the ban covers shared and private scooters alike.

The hotspot list spans the region. In Brussels-City and Saint-Gilles: Antwerpsesteenweg, Krakeel, Noordstation/Versailles, Hallepoort, Zuidstation and Sint-Antonius. In Sint-Jans-Molenbeek: Zwarte Vijvers, Ribaucourt, Bonnevie, Paal- en Kolomstraat and IJzer. In Anderlecht: Clemenceau, Peterbos and Aumale. Others include Marbotin (Schaarbeek), Matongé and Graystraat (Elsene/Ixelles) and Elisabethpark (Koekelberg). Mayors will review the list every three months and can request neighbourhoods be added or removed.

## Region-wide rules and heightened police powers

Some measures apply across the entire region. Brussels is banning pyrotechnic devices — fireworks that are regularly deployed against police and emergency services — and prohibiting the sale of nitrous oxide ("laughing gas") except for professional use.

Within the 19 hotspots, existing restrictions remain in force: a ban on entering a zone for the sole purpose of buying drugs; a ban on public drinking; a prohibition on takeaway alcohol sales between 22:00 and 06:00 (restaurants and cafés exempt); and permanent 24/7 police checks covering identity, administrative seizures and security.

The decree also hands mayors escalation powers when the situation worsens in a hotspot: restricting traffic to local residents, banning gatherings, closing hospitality venues at night, imposing specific speed limits, and — most controversially — a curfew.

The region's public-order architecture remains delicate. Mayors stay primarily responsible for order in their municipalities, but the law empowers the minister-president to intervene in their place when disturbances spread across multiple municipalities, when necessary measures are not taken, or when the general interest requires it, as BRUZZ detailed.

"My role is to get everyone pointing in the same direction: the mayors, the police districts, the public prosecutor's office and the prevention and security services," Dilliès said. "Everyone keeps their responsibilities. But when we are confronted with criminal networks that pay no heed to municipal borders, it is up to the Brussels-Capital Region to chart the course for all of Brussels. We will not abandon a single neighbourhood."

## A political battleground

The decree arrives as drug violence has become a fixture of Brussels politics. A late-August surge — six shootings in a single week, four of them in Saint-Gilles — prompted Dilliès to convene the Regional Security Council (CORES) alongside federal Interior Minister Bernard Quintin (MR), Brussels public prosecutor Julien Moinil, national drug commissioner Ine Van Wymersch and local mayors. That meeting set in motion the long-term approach now formalized. A regional "drugs commissioner" was created to coordinate an integrated strategy, with €68 million allocated to security and prevention in 2026.

Federal Interior and Justice ministers have been summoned before a joint parliamentary committee over the shootings, and Moinil has repeatedly warned of insufficient investigative capacity, saying there are too few investigators to fight drug violence.

## The questions the decree leaves open

The measures are far-reaching, and their justification deserves scrutiny. The central statistic — that more than a quarter of drug-related shootings involved an e-scooter — comes solely from the Brussels government and has not been independently verified. Whether a scooter ban actually reduces violence, or merely displaces it to other means of transport, remains unproven.

The region presented the plan largely on its own terms. A media-framing analysis by [Gekleurd.be](https://gekleurd.be/artikel/plan-voor-aanpak-drugsgeweld-brussels-gewest) scored the original VRT NWS coverage 84% objective but flagged "noticeable framing on some points," noting that critics — residents or human-rights organizations — were not given a voice despite measures as intrusive as a curfew and permanent ID checks. It also pointed out that the link between the shootings and the drug milieu is an assessment by authorities and police, not a raw fact.

The structural tension is real. Strengthening the regional minister-president's authority over municipal mayors touches a sensitive nerve in Belgium's layered federal system, where policing and public order are traditionally local competencies. How aggressively that override power is used will determine whether the decree is seen as coordination or centralization.

## What to watch

Brussels is betting that a permanent, flexible framework will outlast the reactive six-month cycle. Two things will test that bet: whether the shooting figures fall in the coming months, and whether the curfew and override powers are ever used — and challenged. Separately, shared e-scooters are already due to be banned region-wide from 2027, meaning the hotspot restriction is, in part, a preview of a broader change to come. For now, the region has made its position clear: it will not leave a single neighbourhood to its fate.
