# De Wever Bets on Thursday Budget Deal as Debt Mounts

Belgian Prime Minister Bart De Wever hopes to present a federal budget agreement to parliament on Thursday, telling lawmakers on Wednesday that his five-party coalition is running out of time to close a roughly €10 billion savings and revenue package through 2029. "We are already in injury time with the government," De Wever said in the Chamber of Representatives, according to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/liveblog/premier-de-wever-hoopt-morgen-begrotingsakkkoord-te-kunnen-voorl~1790599460224/). "I hope the majority factions will step over their shadows and make the agreement."

The remarks came after the inner cabinet — the so-called *kern*, made up of the premier and his deputy prime ministers — broke up after midnight on Tuesday without a deal, despite meeting from 19:00. A fresh round of talks was scheduled for Wednesday evening, with De Wever aiming to secure a deal in time for a parliamentary statement on Thursday, a debate on Friday and a vote on Saturday.

## A Government in Injury Time

De Wever's warning was blunt. "If we can't make an agreement tomorrow, we will bear the consequences," he said, in comments carried by VRT NWS. Speaking to French-language broadcaster [RTL Info](https://www.rtl.be/actu/belgique/politique/nous-sommes-deja-dans-le-temps-additionnel-de-wever-met-la-pression-sur-larizona/2026-10-21/article/804071), he was more resigned: "I have little to announce, and that is of course regrettable."

The arithmetic behind the urgency is stark. Belgium is seeking to find roughly €10 billion through a combination of spending cuts and new revenues to bring its public finances onto a sustainable path. The task has exposed the fault lines at the heart of De Wever's "Arizona" coalition — a five-party alliance of N-VA, Vooruit and CD&V on the Flemish side and MR and Les Engagés on the Francophone side — sworn in in February 2025 and named after the colours of the US state's flag.

The central divide pits the centre-right N-VA and the liberal MR, which favour spending cuts, against Vooruit, CD&V and Les Engagés, which argue that more must be raised from "the strongest shoulders" through higher taxes on wealth, high incomes and companies. Healthcare savings and new taxes remain the principal sticking points.

## The Debt Arithmetic

The negotiations are unfolding against a deteriorating fiscal backdrop. According to Eurostat data reported by VRT NWS, Belgium's public debt rose to 110.2% of GDP in the second quarter of 2026 — some €719.07 billion — up 0.8 percentage points from the first quarter. Within the eurozone, only Greece (140.8%), Italy (139.9%) and France (119.1%) carry a heavier burden. The budget deficit climbed to 5.3% of GDP over the same period.

The pressure is filtering down to the regions. Flemish MP Maurits Vande Reyde (DURF) said the Flemish government's debt will rise by €3.1 billion next year — a roughly 5% increase — based on the 2027 budget documents. "This runaway mountain of debt pushes annual interest charges up to €1.7 billion in 2027," said Flemish faction leader Stephanie D'Hose and MP Tom Ongena (Anders), according to VRT NWS. "That is a hallucinatory fivefold increase in barely six years."

De Wever has repeatedly framed the debate as an existential one. "I can understand your concerns about holiday pay and indexation, but the end of the world is a debt crisis," he told RTL Info, warning that Belgium must reassure financial markets to avoid a French-style reckoning. He has argued that public spending has risen by more than €60 billion since the start of the century, blaming debt-interest charges and rising pension costs on previous governments. "To claim that all of this is our fault is a responsibility I refuse to assume," he said.

## Market Warnings

The country's central bank has amplified the alarm. In an analysis published on 19 October, the National Bank of Belgium concluded that the country needs "a binding national budgetary framework" to bring its debt-to-GDP ratio onto a sustainable trajectory, [RTL Info reported](https://www.rtl.be/actu/belgique/politique/la-belgique-besoin-dun-cadre-budgetaire-national-contraignant-la-banque/2026-10-19/article/803826). The BNB found that the budget deficit has widened steadily since the start of the century, reaching 5.2% of GDP in 2025, while the primary balance swung from a surplus of nearly 7% of GDP to a deficit of close to 3% — a level unseen since the early 1980s.

The deterioration, the central bank said, is explained almost entirely by rising primary spending, with revenues remaining relatively stable — though in the past decade revenues have fallen even as spending climbed. Between 2016 and 2025, the budget balance worsened by 2.7% of GDP (€18 billion) and the primary balance by 3.3% of GDP (€21 billion), even as interest charges fell.

Not everyone reads the diagnosis the same way. The Christian trade union ACV argued that "the erosion of revenues, not the increase in spending, is at the heart of the budget problem," pointing to the growth of flexi-jobs, student work and the proliferation of company creation by individuals.

## The Cost of Failure

The stakes extend beyond Belgium's borders. Should the coalition fail to agree, it could be forced to run next year on "provisional twelfths" — a stopgap mechanism that freezes spending at the previous year's level — something De Wever has said he is determined to avoid. The parliamentary calendar adds to the pressure: the Chamber is in recess next week, and the end-of-December deadline for a 2027 budget is looming.

De Wever has also floated, and then shelved, a "debt brake" that could freeze spending if no multi-year deal is reached by year-end — a mechanism designed to reassure markets that it was later set aside after coalition partners judged it unbalanced. Deputy Prime Minister David Clarinval (MR) offered a measured assessment of the talks, describing them as "slow but steady" with "an awful lot of work" remaining, quipping that "the optimists will see it optimistically, the pessimists pessimistically."

Budget Minister Vincent Van Peteghem (CD&V) stressed the broader stakes. "Our country cannot afford immobility," he said in the Chamber. "The responsibility is too great to push it ahead of us." For his part, VRT NWS political journalist Bart Verhulst suggested that Wednesday's meeting could prove decisive. "Tonight could be D-day," he said. "Whether it succeeds substantively remains to be seen."

## What to Watch

Everything now hinges on the Wednesday-evening meeting of the inner cabinet. If a deal emerges, De Wever can present it to parliament on Thursday and aim for a vote by the weekend. If the talks stall again, the coalition will face difficult questions about whether it can govern at all — and whether a 2027-only budget, worth about €4 billion, becomes the fallback, at the cost of the premier's ambition to break with Belgium's tradition of one-year budgeting. For a country already among the eurozone's most indebted, the clock — and the markets — are watching.
