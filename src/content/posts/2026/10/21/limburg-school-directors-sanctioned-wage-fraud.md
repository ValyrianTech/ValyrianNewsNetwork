# Two Limburg school directors sanctioned over wage fraud

The Limburg provincial deputation has sanctioned two school directors following a disciplinary investigation into wage fraud uncovered by Audit Vlaanderen, provincial deputy for Education Laura Olaerts (N-VA) announced. The director of the APP school in Munsterbilzen has been dismissed, while the general director of the School Community of Provincial and Municipal Education Limburg has been demoted in rank and thus loses the position of director, [VRT NWS reported](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/10/21/directeurs-provinciale-scholen-ontslagen-na-tuchtonderzoek/).

Both individuals were involved in a system in which wages were paid for work that was never performed. The sanctions stem from a disciplinary procedure launched by the deputation, running in parallel with a criminal investigation that prosecutors say is now in its final phase.

## Context: a devastating audit

The case began with a damning report from Audit Vlaanderen, the Flemish internal audit agency, which focused on the provincial education service of Limburg and two provincial schools: the Provinciale Handelsschool Hasselt (PHH) and the APP school in Munsterbilzen.

According to the findings, the personnel manager of the two schools received wages for services never delivered and arranged for acquaintances to be paid wrongly. The manager also registered a friend as a teacher and administrative worker at both schools, despite that person never having worked there.

The audit further found that Flemish education funding had been misused. Provincial staff were entered into a fictitious course or onto the teaching hours of a teacher on sick leave, so that they were paid with money from the Flemish government rather than the province's own operating funds.

The severity of the situation brought the file to the office of Flemish Minister of Home Affairs Hilde Crevits (CD&V), who placed the province under supervision and called for swift action. The Limburg public prosecutor's office opened a criminal investigation, and the province declared itself a civil party.

## Key developments

As the case unfolded in late 2025, the deputation launched a disciplinary procedure against several members of the provincial education staff. In December 2025, the directors of PHH Hasselt and APP Munsterbilzen, along with the general director of the education service, were [preventively suspended](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2025/12/19/directeurs-provinciale-handelsschool-hasselt-en-app-school-munst/) pending the investigation.

At the time, the socialist education union ACOD Onderwijs Limburg called the suspension "the only possible and correct measure." Its provincial secretary, Yves Vannijlen, argued that it was implausible the provincial government had known nothing of the fraud, noting that any submission to the Education Department must be signed by a director.

Two of the three suspended figures have now been sanctioned. The director of PHH Hasselt has since retired, meaning no disciplinary punishment could be imposed. The personnel manager at the centre of the affair was not part of the disciplinary investigation at all; he had resigned several months before the Audit Vlaanderen report was finalised.

"The director of the APP school in Munsterbilzen is dismissed. The general director of the School Community of Provincial and Municipal Education Limburg is demoted in rank and thus loses his position as director," Olaerts said. She declined to elaborate on the specifics, citing the confidentiality of individual personnel files. Both sanctioned individuals can still appeal the decision.

Reacting to the outcome, Vannijlen said the misuse of funds was hard to comprehend. "Funds were unlawfully used for individual gain, while they should serve teachers and students. That defies all imagination," he said. He added that school staff had borne the consequences, as the schools went a long time without directors and personnel had to put in extra effort to compensate, expressing satisfaction that the file is now partly behind them.

## Analysis: accountability on two tracks

The case illustrates how public-sector fraud in Belgium is pursued along two parallel tracks. The disciplinary sanctions handed down by the deputation are administrative, not criminal: the dismissal and demotion settle the employment-accountability question, but they do not determine criminal liability. The Limburg prosecutor's office, which the province joined as a civil party, is still investigating, and its file is described as being in its final phase. The raadkamer, the council chamber that screens cases before trial, will soon decide whether the implicated individuals are referred to court, though no timing has been announced.

The sanctions also leave open questions about structural accountability. During the early stages of the affair, opposition provincial councillor Joost Venken (Groen) questioned how the deputation could not have known that people were working at the provincial service who were not on the payroll, arguing that "serious mistakes were made at all levels." The CD&V-led province expressed regret at the time, with first deputy Inge Moors stating that it deplored how a few individuals had abused their position at the expense of provincial education. Those political questions have not been formally resolved by the disciplinary outcome.

A notable asymmetry runs through the case. The personnel manager widely seen as the central figure escaped the disciplinary process entirely by resigning before the audit concluded, even as the directors who signed off on the arrangements faced its consequences. Whether he will face criminal liability remains to be determined.

## What to watch

Three things will determine how the affair concludes. First, the criminal track: the prosecutor's referral decision could produce convictions independent of the administrative sanctions. Second, any appeals by the two sanctioned directors, which could yet alter the disciplinary outcome. And third, the broader debate over oversight in provincial education, where unions and the opposition have argued that responsibility extends beyond the individuals directly implicated. For now, the schools can begin to move on from a period in which, as the union put it, staff carried an extra burden in the absence of permanent leadership.
