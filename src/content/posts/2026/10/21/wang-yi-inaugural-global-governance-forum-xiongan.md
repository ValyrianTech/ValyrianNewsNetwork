# Wang Yi to Open Inaugural Global Governance Forum in Xiongan

Chinese Foreign Minister Wang Yi will attend the opening ceremony of the 2026 Global Governance Forum (Xiongan) on October 23 and deliver a keynote speech, the Foreign Ministry announced on October 21, according to [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/politics/leaders/20261021/a650c618ee7a4ccf9f8b29898e507165/c.html). The event, the first of its kind, is designed to translate President Xi Jinping's Global Governance Initiative into concrete international practice and to showcase the Xiongan New Area as a stage for major global policy gatherings.

Wang, a member of the Political Bureau of the Communist Party of China Central Committee, will set out what officials describe as practical measures to further implement the Initiative and to build "a more just and equitable global governance system," spokesperson Mao Ning told a regular press briefing in Beijing. He is also expected to meet foreign guests attending the forum.

## A Forum Built Around a Theme

The gathering runs from October 23 to 25 under the theme "Rebuilding Confidence and Cooperation: Changes Unseen in a Century and Reforming and Improving Global Governance." Mao said the forum "invites people from all countries to gather in Xiongan to jointly discuss the major plans for reforming and improving global governance," as [Xinhua reported](https://www.news.cn/world/20261021/44fbff0b1d0d46049b174eb277b3f9b4/c.html).

“We believe Chinese and foreign guests will use this forum as an opportunity to help the international community jointly solve governance challenges and reform and improve global governance,” she added.

According to [CGTN](https://news.cgtn.com/news/2026-10-21/Chinese-FM-to-attend-2026-Global-Governance-Forum-Xiongan-opening-1RqgrvE93MY/p.html), Wang will use the keynote to outline concrete measures for implementing the Initiative, which China has positioned as a centerpiece of its multilateral diplomacy. Chinese officials have sought to present the forum not merely as a talking shop but as a vehicle for converting shared ideas into action.

## The Scale and Symbolism of Xiongan

The choice of venue is itself a statement. [Global Times](https://www.globaltimes.cn/page/202610/1372400.shtml) reported that more than 180 overseas guests from over 90 countries—spanning the Global South, Europe and North America—are confirmed, alongside more than 180 Chinese participants, with 22 seminars across five thematic tracks. Organizers say the forum also serves as a case study in urban governance, connecting Xiongan with the world and exchanging experience on how cities are run.

Xiongan, established in 2017, is a national "millennium project" conceived to relieve Beijing of non-capital functions and to drive integration across the Beijing-Tianjin-Hebei region. In 2026—the opening year of the 15th Five-Year Plan—the area has accelerated its development, and hosting a high-level international forum there projects it as a new symbol of Chinese modernization, distinct from traditional diplomatic venues such as Beijing or the Boao forum.

## One Year On From the Initiative

The forum falls almost exactly a year after Xi proposed the Global Governance Initiative on September 1, 2025, at an "SCO+" meeting during the Shanghai Cooperation Organization summit in Tianjin—a moment timed to the 80th anniversary of the founding of the United Nations. The Initiative calls for a fairer global order and is presented as complementing China's earlier Global Development, Global Security and Global Civilization Initiatives.

At the one-year mark, the Foreign Ministry said the Initiative had drawn support from nearly 160 countries and international organizations, with more than 60 countries joining a "Group of Friends of Global Governance," per [People's Daily](https://paper.people.com.cn/rmrbhwb/pc/content/202609/02/content_30178684.html). Beijing also released a concept paper and a white paper setting out nine priority areas for reform, and pointed to new institutional bodies—including the International Organization for Mediation, the World Data Organization and the World AI Cooperation Organization—as evidence that the vision is becoming operational.

Wang first signaled the forum's launch in June, telling a State Council Information Office briefing that China would host the inaugural gathering in autumn 2026 to "pool broader international consensus for building a more just and equitable global governance system," [China Daily](https://www.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202606/17/WS6a323b85a310986e2b4607fe.html) reported at the time. At that same briefing, he pointed to emerging-area governance—AI, sustainable development, green transitions and supply-chain stability—and to platforms such as the mediation body and a bid to host a marine biodiversity secretariat in Xiamen.

## Analysis: Messaging, Reform and the Global South

The forum is a diplomatic milestone that converts a Chinese proposal into a standing international venue. Its "rebuilding confidence" framing implicitly critiques what Beijing portrays as the shortcomings of the existing multilateral order, and positions China and the Global South as agents of reform at a time when Western-led institutions face strain.

For China, the gathering also carries domestic symbolism. Elevating Xiongan as an international stage reinforces the narrative that the country is building institutions for the future—and that its flagship new city is ready for the world's attention. It follows a busy autumn calendar of China-hosted events, from the Xiangshan security forum to the Canton Fair, underscoring Beijing's push to remain a hub of global convening.

Yet the forum's ultimate test will be substance. Whether it produces concrete funding, new mechanisms or durable commitments—rather than declarations alone—will determine whether it becomes an annual fixture with real weight.

## What to Watch

Three questions will shape how the forum is judged. First, which countries attend, and at what level: a strong showing of senior officials and ministers would lend credibility, while thin attendance would undercut the pitch. Second, whether Wang's keynote delivers tangible deliverables—financing, new cooperation platforms or institutional commitments that go beyond the June announcements. Third, how Western governments and analysts respond: engagement would signal the forum matters, while dismissal would frame it as messaging rather than machinery.

For now, the announcement sets the stage. When Wang rises to speak in Xiongan on Friday, he will be making a case not just for a forum, but for China's vision of how the world should be governed—and for the city that Beijing hopes will help prove it.
