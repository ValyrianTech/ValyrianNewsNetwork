# China's Q3 Growth Hits 4.5% as AI Exports Offset Weak Demand

China's economy grew 4.5% year-on-year in the third quarter of 2026, a modest rebound from the second quarter's 4.3% that keeps the country on track to meet its official full-year target — but the headline masks a widening gap between a booming export machine and a fragile domestic engine.

According to data released by the National Bureau of Statistics (NBS) on 19 October, GDP for the first three quarters reached 107.1 trillion yuan, up 4.6% on the same period a year earlier. The figures, reported by [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/oct/19/china-economy-grows-faster-than-forecast-after-ai-boom-boosted-exports), came in above economist expectations and landed near the lower bound of Beijing's 4.5%–5% growth target for 2026, the opening year of the 15th Five-Year Plan.

## A Production-Strong, Consumption-Weak Story

The quarter's defining feature is a sharp divergence between output and spending. Industrial production rose 5.5% year-on-year in September, up from 5.2% in August, while retail sales managed only a 1.1% increase over the same month. Exports surged by more than a third in September, lifted by shipments of AI-related electronics.

"This pickup in GDP growth mainly reflects ongoing export strength on the global AI/tech boom, more than offsetting drags from the Iran conflict and ongoing weakness in domestic demand," analysts at ABN Amro said, as cited by The Guardian.

Duncan Wrigley, chief China economist at Pantheon Macroeconomics, noted that hi-tech manufacturing is outpacing traditional sectors. "Manufacturing output growth remained robust at 6.0% year on year in September, buoyed up by stronger export demand for electronics, AI-related equipment and autos," he said.

## Official Framing: 'New Momentum' and the AI Bet

The state-run Xinhua news agency, in [a flagship All-Media Headline feature](https://www.news.cn/fortune/20261020/28bdb8c7296341aea6c56c82105c76c9/c.html), framed the data far more optimistically, casting the year as a strong start to the 2026–2030 planning cycle. The report emphasised "new quality productive forces" — the doctrine championed by Xi Jinping covering AI, humanoid robotics, new energy and advanced manufacturing.

The figures Xinhua highlighted are genuinely striking. New growth drivers — high-end manufacturing, the digital-intelligent economy and modern services — contributed more than 40% of growth. Value-added output of digital products manufacturing jumped 13.6% year-on-year. Clean energy sources accounted for 36.8% of large-scale industrial power generation, and new energy vehicle retail penetration exceeded 60% for a sixth consecutive month.

Xinhua also reported that 10.52 million new urban jobs were created, 87.7% of the full-year target, with the surveyed urban unemployment rate at 5.2%, unchanged from a year earlier. Consumer prices rose 0.9%, which the agency described as mild, and goods trade climbed 18.8% to a record high for the period.

"In the first three quarters the economy ran generally smoothly, maintaining a development trend of new momentum, better structure and improving quality and efficiency," said Mao Shengyong, deputy commissioner of the NBS, at a State Council Information Office press conference.

## The Numbers the Official Account Left Out

Yet the official narrative omitted the most difficult figures. Fixed-asset investment fell 7.2% over the first nine months, and property investment tumbled 20.6% — a contraction that appears nowhere in the Xinhua feature. The agency's only reference to real estate was Mao's assertion that the market is "moving in a positive direction" based on transactions, inventory and prices.

That claim sits awkwardly beside the property data reported the same week, and alongside the August jailing for life of Evergrande founder Hui Ka Yan after he pleaded guilty to fraud — the most visible symbol of a sector still working through an extraordinary debt crisis, [reported The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/business/2026/aug/20/evergrande-founder-jailed-life-fraud-hui-ka-yan).

To his credit, Mao acknowledged the pressure points more candidly than the surrounding spin. "The international environment is complex and changeable, the problem of insufficient domestic demand remains prominent, and the foundation for steady improvement still needs to be consolidated," he said. He also pointed to "strong supply and weak demand" (供强需弱) as a structural challenge.

The stimulus backdrop is telling. A Politburo meeting on 30 July called for macro policy to be "intensified and effective" and for domestic demand to be "effectively expanded," while the Ministry of Finance allocated 550 billion yuan in local government debt quotas and the central bank adjusted several structural monetary tools.

## Why It Matters

The Q3 report crystallises a structural risk at the heart of the 15th Five-Year Plan: an economy increasingly leaning on external demand and a cyclical global AI investment boom while its internal consumer engine stalls. Exports can power growth while the AI capex cycle lasts, but they cannot substitute indefinitely for household spending.

At 4.6% for the nine-month period, China remains a standout among major economies — the NBS estimated the increment of 5.7 trillion yuan as the largest for any comparable period in five years. But the composition matters as much as the total. Retail sales growing at roughly a fifth of the pace of factory output is not a sign of balanced rebalancing; it is a sign of an economy where production races ahead of the pay packets meant to absorb it.

## What to Watch

The decisive tests lie in the fourth quarter and beyond. First, whether export momentum holds if the global AI capital-spending cycle cools. Second, whether Beijing can engineer a genuine consumption recovery — the +1.1% retail figure is the single most important vulnerability in the data. Third, whether the property sector can keep contracting at more than 20% without triggering broader financial contagion.

Mao struck a confident note on the full-year outlook, saying "positive factors for stable, high-quality development are accumulating" and that there is "solid support for achieving the full-year growth target." With growth now at 4.6% — just above the 4.5% floor — the margin for error in the final quarter is thin, and the gap between official optimism and household reality will be the number to watch.
