# China Medical Insurance Fund Spending Tops 1.7 Trillion Yuan

China's basic medical insurance pooled fund spent 1.7515 trillion yuan (about $246 billion) in the first nine months of 2026, the National Healthcare Security Administration (NHSA) said on 21 October, a figure that underscores the vast scale of the country's public healthcare system and the pace of its spending. According to [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/fortune/20261021/23c0a985bfca45f7bc85397f36b9958e/c.html), the fund took in 2.3028 trillion yuan over the same January-to-September period, leaving a cumulative surplus of roughly 551 billion yuan.

The numbers cover the pooled portion of basic medical insurance, including maternity insurance, which covers about 1.33 billion people — roughly 95 percent of China's population. They are released periodically by the NHSA through state media as routine reassurance that the fund is running steadily.

## A Two-Pillar Split

The release breaks the figures into two structurally distinct funds. The employee basic medical insurance pooled fund collected 1.4474 trillion yuan and spent 1.0044 trillion yuan, producing a substantial surplus of about 443 billion yuan. Maternity insurance benefit payments alone reached 101.01 billion yuan.

The urban-rural resident basic medical insurance pooled fund — the more heavily government-subsidized pillar covering 942 million people — collected 855.47 billion yuan and spent 747.09 billion yuan, a narrower surplus of about 108 billion yuan.

That split matters because the employee scheme, funded by employer and employee contributions, is the financially stronger of the two. The resident scheme depends far more on state subsidies and serves a population for whom out-of-pocket healthcare costs remain a significant concern. Together they form the world's largest medical insurance scheme by covered population, and their balance is closely watched as an indicator of the broader social security system's health.

## Growth Moderate, But Maternity Costs Climb

Comparing the nine-month total with the previous data release offers a sense of the fund's pace. Data for the first eight months of 2026, reported by [Yicai](https://www.yicai.com/news/103384938.html) citing Xinhua, put revenue at 2.0728 trillion yuan and expenditure at 1.5504 trillion yuan. The September figures therefore added roughly 230 billion yuan in revenue and 201 billion yuan in expenditure.

That trajectory is broadly in line with historical patterns. According to [People's Daily](https://paper.people.com.cn/rmrb/pc/content/202607/17/content_30169328.html), reporting on the NHSA's 2025 statistical bulletin, the fund closed 2025 with total revenue of 3.59 trillion yuan, total expenditure of 3.01 trillion yuan and a current-period surplus of about 525.8 billion yuan. The first three quarters of 2026 have already produced roughly 551 billion yuan in surplus, consistent with a system that continues to collect more than it spends.

One line item stands out: maternity insurance benefits. At 101.01 billion yuan for the first nine months of 2026, such spending is already approaching three-quarters of the entire 2025 full-year figure of 139.4 billion yuan, as reported in the same People's Daily bulletin. Maternity insurance enrollment had reached nearly 260 million people by the end of 2025, with more than 35.74 million people receiving benefits that year.

The rapid growth reflects Beijing's broader push to encourage childbirth amid falling birth rates. Expanding maternity support has become a national policy priority, and the fund's figures show the fiscal commitment that accompanies it.

## Why the Numbers Matter

The release is routine, but its significance is not. The health of China's medical insurance fund bears directly on three overlapping concerns.

First is fiscal sustainability. China is aging rapidly, and the ratio of active contributors to retirees in the employee scheme continues to fall. Each new retiree generates far more healthcare spending than an active worker contributes, a structural pressure that grows with every year. The reserve level is a buffer against that trend, and a steady surplus is a signal of stability.

Second is household confidence. Out-of-pocket medical costs are a major driver of precautionary savings in China, and a well-funded insurance pool reduces the financial risk families face when illness strikes. In a period of cautious consumer spending, that matters for the broader economy.

Third is policy delivery. The government has staked credibility on expanding social welfare under the "common prosperity" framework. Medical insurance is among the most visible components of that promise, and its solvency is a measure of how well the state can deliver on it.

Spending growth has also appeared moderate in year-on-year terms. The first three quarters of 2024 saw pooled fund expenditure of 1.76 trillion yuan, reported by [CCTV via Beijing News](https://m.bjnews.com.cn/detail/1730423096129982.html), a figure very close to the 1.7515 trillion yuan recorded for the same period in 2026. Analysts attribute the flattening to strict fund supervision and anti-fraud enforcement — which recovered 34.2 billion yuan in 2025 and 118.2 billion yuan over the 14th Five-Year Plan period — alongside price cuts from centralized volume-based drug procurement.

## What's Next

The remainder of 2026 will show whether the current trajectory holds. Full-year spending will likely exceed the nine-month total by a wide margin, and maternity benefit costs could approach or surpass the 2025 full-year figure if the current pace continues.

Also worth watching is progress on provincial-level pooling of the fund, which the NHSA has promoted as a way to equalize risk and resources across regions with different demographic profiles. As China's population ages, how that redistribution is managed will determine whether the world's largest medical insurance scheme can remain, in the regulator's phrasing, "safe and sustainable." For now, the numbers tell a story of scale, steady surplus, and one line item — maternity support — that is growing faster than the rest.
