# Typhoon Chanthu Nears China as CMA Triggers Level IV

China's meteorological authority has activated a Level IV emergency response for a major meteorological disaster as Super Typhoon Chanthu — the 30th named storm of the 2026 Pacific season — advances toward the waters east of Taiwan, threatening wind and rain across southeastern China and the East China coast. The [China Meteorological Administration](https://www.news.cn/politics/20261021/478f0a571d7e4c118219eb61b8baccac/c.html) (CMA) said the decision followed a comprehensive assessment and emergency consultation on October 21.

At 14:00 local time, Chanthu's center was about 1,050 kilometers southeast of Taipei, moving northwest at 15 to 20 kilometers per hour toward the seas east of Taiwan, according to the CMA's Central Meteorological Observatory.

## A Super Typhoon Behind a Low-Level Alert

The contrast at the heart of the response is striking. While the CMA activated only a Level IV alert — the lowest rung on China's four-tier meteorological emergency scale — the storm itself is anything but modest.

Chinese maritime and provincial sources classify Chanthu as a super typhoon, with maximum sustained winds near its center above Force 17. As [Lianhe Zaobao](https://www.zaobao.com.sg/news/china/story20261021-9870668) reported, citing the Zhejiang Provincial Meteorological Observatory, the storm's center packed winds exceeding Force 17 as of 08:00 on October 21.

The relatively low response level reflects Chanthu's forecast track — projected to pass east of Taiwan and curve toward Japan rather than make a direct landfall on the Chinese mainland — not its raw intensity. China's four-tier system runs from Level IV to Level I, and a Level IV activation signals formal, coordinated vigilance rather than top-level mobilization.

## Forecast Track and Timing

The Central Meteorological Observatory expects Chanthu to turn northeast near the sea area northeast of Taiwan around October 24 — or possibly to graze the northeastern Taiwan coast before turning — then head toward southwestern Japan.

Before that turn, the storm is forecast to brush close to China's eastern seaboard. A forecaster at Zhejiang's climate center told local media that the likelihood of Chanthu closely skirting Zhejiang or approaching the province's coast remained quite high, according to [Tencent News](https://news.qq.com/rain/a/20261021A05PA000), which reported that the storm had entered China's 48-hour warning line. The Central Meteorological Observatory continued to issue a typhoon blue warning — the lowest of China's color-coded typhoon alerts — at 06:00 on October 21.

## Zhejiang and Fujian Mobilize Early

Even without landfall, authorities are moving pre-emptively. The Zhejiang Maritime Safety Administration activated a Level III anti-typhoon emergency response at 08:00 on October 21, entering what state media described as full battle readiness. By that morning, 36 passenger ferry routes had been suspended due to high winds and all 223 waterborne construction projects had been halted, Lianhe Zaobao reported.

Zhejiang's maritime authorities have issued more than 61,000 warning messages and conducted safety checks on some 23,000 vessel movements since the response began. The provincial flood-control command urged coastal localities to secure vessels, suspend maritime routes, close coastal tourist sites, and evacuate personnel from aquaculture and offshore construction sites.

Neighboring Fujian moved in parallel. The province ordered its ocean-going fishing vessels to return to port by 10:00 on October 21, with crew required to come ashore by 10:00 the following day. Fujian's marine forecast warned of waves six to nine meters high and winds of Force 13 to 14, gusting to Force 15 to 16, in fishing grounds near the typhoon's path. The passenger routes between Fujian and Taiwan's outlying islands — including the Mawei-Matsu and Huangqi-Matsu crossings — were suspended from October 21 through October 24.

## Taiwan Braces as Politics and Weather Collide

Across the strait, Taiwan is preparing for the storm's closest approach, expected between the evening of October 22 and the early hours of October 24. Taiwan's Central Weather Administration said it would likely issue a sea typhoon warning late on October 21 into early October 22, with a land warning possible on the afternoon or evening of October 22. Northern Taiwan, Yilan, and Hualien face heavy to torrential rain, with extremely heavy rain possible in northern mountain areas on October 23.

The storm has also become entangled in Taiwan's politics. President Lai Ching-te, speaking at a Democratic Progressive Party meeting on October 21, warned that while Chanthu is unlikely to strike Taiwan directly, Friday and Saturday would be the period when the storm's circulation is closest to Taiwan, with impacts especially pronounced in northern Taiwan and Hualien. He urged party officials and candidate teams to put public safety and disaster prevention first, and not neglect potential risks for the sake of campaign events, [Lianhe Zaobao](https://www.zaobao.com.sg/news/china/story20261021-9872217) reported.

His remarks came as Taiwan enters the campaign period for its 2026 local elections, with the typhoon's arrival coinciding with a consecutive-holiday weekend. The Executive Yuan has ordered government departments to complete typhoon preparations and coordinate with local governments.

## What to Watch

Three questions will shape the storm's impact over the coming days. First, whether Chanthu grazes northeastern Taiwan or turns northeast earlier — the CMA says either remains possible. Second, whether the track shifts west toward Zhejiang and Fujian, which could prompt mainland authorities to escalate their response beyond Level IV. And third, how shipping and aviation across the East China Sea and around Taiwan are disrupted as the storm passes.

For now, the message from authorities on both sides of the strait is one of vigilance without alarm: a super typhoon is coming close, and the machinery of preparation has been set in motion well before its nearest approach. As Xinhua noted, the storm will ultimately track toward southwestern Japan — making Chanthu a multi-country event that engages mainland China, Taiwan, and Japan alike.
