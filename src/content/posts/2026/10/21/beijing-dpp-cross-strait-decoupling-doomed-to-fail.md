# Beijing: DPP Cross-Strait Decoupling 'Doomed to Fail'

China's State Council Taiwan Affairs Office (TAO) said on Wednesday that the ruling Democratic Progressive Party's push to force cross-strait economic "decoupling and severing of chains" is doomed to fail, citing strong trade growth and warning Taipei against tightening semiconductor export controls aligned with Washington.

Speaking at a regular press conference in Beijing, TAO spokesperson Zhang Han pointed to customs data showing cross-strait trade reached USD 301.18 billion in the first nine months of 2026, up 30.3% year-on-year, as [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/tw/20261021/32d978935cfb4bb68a7290f1649a00c8/c.html) reported.

## Trade Data Undercuts Decoupling Push

According to the General Administration of Customs figures cited by Zhang, mainland exports to Taiwan rose 37.3% year-on-year to USD 84.9 billion, while mainland imports from Taiwan climbed 27.8% to USD 216.28 billion. Taipei runs a large and growing surplus in the bilateral goods trade.

"These data fully prove that cross-strait industrial and supply chains remain solid, that the two economies cannot be decoupled and their industries cannot be unchained," Zhang said, adding that integrated development is "the general trend and the will of the people."

The remarks echo a consistent TAO refrain. In January, the office said "any plot to weaken cross-strait economic ties is doomed to fail," and in July, spokesperson Zhu Fenglian criticized the DPP's promotion of a "non-red supply chain" — a push to reduce reliance on mainland-linked production — as harming Taiwan's future, [China News Service](https://www.chinanews.com.cn/gn/2026/07-15/10659666.shtml) reported at the time.

## Sanctions Extended Over Shen Boyang Case

Zhang also addressed the mainland's punitive action against enterprises linked to Shen Boyang, a prominent "Taiwan independence" figure. She said that after the TAO sanctioned Zhaoyi Co. Ltd. in June 2025 — a firm headed by Shen's father, Shen Tucheng — Shen Tucheng continued buying mainland goods through another company, VWAY, and reselling them abroad for profit.

"The facts are clear and the evidence is conclusive," Zhang said, accusing Shen Boyang of "endless sophistry and obfuscation" that "fully exposes his guilty conscience and base character." She rejected Taiwan's Mainland Affairs Council characterization that the measures were coercion aimed at interfering in elections, saying Beijing protects the legitimate rights of Taiwanese people but will not tolerate "diehard" independence advocates and their associated firms.

## Chip Controls in the Crosshairs

The sharpest exchanges came over semiconductors. Taiwan's economic affairs authority head recently said the island would cooperate with US policy to further restrict exports of AI chips and advanced semiconductors to the mainland — a move Zhang cast as economically self-harming.

"The DPP authorities, out of political self-interest, follow the US at every step, obstructing normal market operations and pressuring competitive firms to relocate capacity abroad," she said, arguing the policy harms Taiwan's industry and ordinary people while benefiting "independence speculators."

Zhang insisted external blockades have "never suppressed our progress, in the past or the future," pointing to the 15th Five-Year Plan and the drive for "new quality productive forces" as evidence of the mainland's push for technological self-reliance. She invited Taiwanese semiconductor firms to invest and innovate on the mainland, calling cross-strait cooperation in the sector "the correct path to mutual benefit."

Invoking a Chinese proverb about squandering an inherited estate, Zhang said the DPP was "selling off the family estate without a pang of conscience," consuming generations of Taiwanese effort and achievement.

## Analysis: Messaging Meets Market Reality

The timing is pointed. With cross-strait trade expanding at nearly a third year-on-year, Beijing is using hard commercial numbers to argue that political efforts to diversify away from the mainland are fighting the market — and to reassure Taiwanese businesses that the mainland remains open to them even as it tightens pressure on those it deems politically hostile.

Yet the two tracks sit uneasily together. The same press conference that touted interdependence also reaffirmed sanctions reaching into family business networks and shell or renamed companies, signaling that economic engagement and targeted punishment will continue in parallel. The semiconductor question is where these tensions are sharpest: Taiwan's chip industry has boomed on global AI demand, making any export restriction a high-stakes bet, while Beijing positions itself as the natural market for that output.

## What's Next

The immediate focus will be whether Taipei formalizes tighter AI-chip export controls and how Beijing responds. The Shen Boyang case may also see further enforcement, testing the reach of mainland sanctions against cross-strait business ties. For now, Beijing's message is clear: it will cite trade figures as proof of interdependence while warning that political decoupling carries a price.
