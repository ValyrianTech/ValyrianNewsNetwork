# China, Russia Premiers Chair 31st Regular Meeting

Chinese Premier Li Qiang and Russian Prime Minister Mikhail Mishustin co-chaired the 31st Regular Meeting of the Heads of Government of China and Russia in St. Petersburg on the morning of 21 October, signing a joint communique and a package of cooperation documents in trade, aerospace, science and technology, and education, according to [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/politics/leaders/20261021/dd9aca9a20fb456189cf61606628c337/c.html).

The meeting is the standing annual mechanism through which the two governments translate presidential-level consensus into sector-by-sector cooperation, and the readout — carried in identical terms by the [Chinese Ministry of Foreign Affairs](https://www.fmprc.gov.cn/zyxw/202610/t20261021_12044564.shtml) — underscored a partnership both sides describe as being at its highest point in history.

## What was agreed

Following the talks, the two premiers signed the Joint Communique of the 31st China-Russia Prime Ministers' Regular Meeting. The two sides also concluded cooperation documents spanning the economic and trade, space, science and technology, and education fields.

Both premiers received work reports from five bilateral sub-committees: the China-Russia Investment Cooperation Committee, the Energy Cooperation Committee, the Humanitarian (People-to-People) Cooperation Committee, the Prime Ministers' Regular Meeting Committee, and the Intergovernmental Cooperation Committee for China's Northeast Region and Russia's Far East.

The readout named economic and trade investment, energy and resources, connectivity, aerospace, finance and industry as priority areas. It also pointed to newer growth sectors — artificial intelligence, biotechnology and the digital economy — alongside a joint effort to run the "China-Russia Year of Education" and to expand tourism, cultural, youth and media exchanges.

## A partnership framed at its peak

Li said that in recent years, amid a "complex and turbulent international situation," the two countries had joined hands under the strategic guidance of their heads of state, using "vigorous and resilient cooperation" to hedge against external uncertainty. He said the China-Russia comprehensive strategic cooperative partnership in the new era was "entering a new stage of greater achievement and faster development," and that China was ready to turn the leaders' consensus into "practical actions and concrete results."

Mishustin noted that 2026 marks the 25th anniversary of the China-Russia Treaty of Good-Neighborliness and Friendly Cooperation, which was signed in 2001. He said the relationship was "at its highest level in history" and had "set a model for relations between states," adding that bilateral trade and economic cooperation maintained good momentum and that Russia was willing to deepen practical cooperation on the basis of mutual benefit, mutual respect and good-neighborliness.

Chinese State Councilor Wu Zhenglong also took part in the activities.

## Context: an institutionalized relationship

The premiers' regular meeting is the government-level counterpart to the annual head-of-state summits, operationalizing political agreements through standing sub-committees covering investment, energy, humanitarian affairs and regional development. This year's session follows the 30th meeting, held in Hangzhou in November 2025, at which the two premiers signed the 30th joint communique and witnessed cooperation documents on customs and satellite navigation, according to the [Chinese consulate in Marseille](https://marseille.china-consulate.gov.cn/zgyw/202511/t20251103_11746227.htm), which republished the official readout.

2026 is a milestone year for the relationship: it marks a quarter-century since the Treaty of Good-Neighborliness and the 30th anniversary of the strategic cooperative partnership established in 1996. Both premiers have repeatedly emphasized these anniversaries, and a major commemorative conference was held in Harbin in July.

Trade has been the economic anchor. According to a [Chinese Academy of Social Sciences analysis](http://euroasia.cssn.cn/kycg/lw/202609/t20260901_6065413.shtml) by Li Yonghui of its Institute of Russian, Eastern European and Central Asian Studies, bilateral commerce rose from about US$107.7 billion in 2020 to US$244.8 billion in 2024 — more than doubling over five years — after having surpassed US$240 billion in 2023, beating targets set by both governments. Energy remains what Chinese officials call the "ballast" of the relationship, though both sides increasingly stress quality over quantity — shifting from resource complementarity toward innovation cooperation and industrial integration, the same analysis notes.

People-to-people ties have also deepened. The same analysis records that mutual visa exemptions have driven tourism growth, with Russian visitors to China up roughly 40 percent year-on-year in the fourth quarter of 2025 and Chinese tourists to Russia up about 44 percent in the first quarter of 2026. The 2026–2027 China-Russia Year of Education is the current flagship of that cultural calendar.

## Analysis: continuity over breakthrough

This is a routine but high-level manifestation of a deeply institutionalized partnership. Its news value lies less in any single breakthrough than in the continuity and breadth of coordination at a moment of international turbulence — and in the forward-looking emphasis on technology and education.

Chinese officials and state media have repeatedly characterized the relationship as a model of major-country relations built on the principles of non-alignment, non-confrontation and not targeting third parties — a framing the CASS analysis describes as grounded in "strategic autonomy, mutual respect, and equality and mutual benefit." That framing is paired with arguments that deeper China-Russia coordination within frameworks such as the UN, the Shanghai Cooperation Organisation and BRICS contributes to a multipolar international order — a contrast, implicit but clear, with Western-led alignments.

The concrete deliverables this time are clear: the joint communique and cooperation documents in four named fields. The new growth areas and Year of Education initiatives are directional priorities stated in the readout rather than itemized commitments. The full text of the communique was not immediately published; itemized figures on specific energy contracts, trade targets or project lists would require that document to verify.

## What to watch

Attention now turns to the implementation of the newly signed documents and to the broader diplomatic calendar, including the two countries' coordination at upcoming multilateral summits. For observers, the key indicators will be whether the stated pivot toward artificial intelligence, biotechnology and the digital economy translates into concrete projects, and how the education and tourism exchanges evolve through 2027. What remains unmistakable is the steady institutional rhythm of the relationship — a machinery of annual meetings, sub-committees and documents that both governments clearly intend to keep running.
