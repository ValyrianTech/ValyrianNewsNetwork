# China Condemns Kevin Tsai Over Taiwan Rally Appearance

China's State Council Taiwan Affairs Office (TAO) said there is "no room for ambiguity" on matters of major principle after Taiwanese entertainer Kevin Tsai was seen at a campaign event for Shen Boyang, the ruling Democratic Progressive Party's (DPP) candidate for Taipei mayor, [according to The Paper](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_34247472).

Speaking at a regular press conference in Beijing on the morning of October 14, 2026, TAO spokesperson Chen Binhua responded sharply when asked to comment on Tsai's appearance at the October 4 launch rally for Shen's campaign headquarters.

## Chen Warns Against 'Two-Faced' Conduct

Chen reiterated that Taiwan is "an inalienable part of China's territory" and that adherence to the One-China principle is a "common political foundation" for people on both sides of the strait. He described Shen as a "diehard Taiwan independence separatist" whose actions are "well known to all," and said any form of separatist activity "seriously harms the fundamental interests of the Chinese nation."

"We always respect and protect the legitimate freedom of speech and expression of our Taiwanese compatriots, but on matters of major principle, there is no room for ambiguity," Chen said. "No person or force can reap the dividends of mainland development on one hand and collude and entangle with Taiwan independence separatist forces on the other, being two-faced people."

## The Rally That Sparked the Response

The rally that drew the TAO's attention took place on the afternoon of October 4 on Beiping East Road in Taipei, near DPP headquarters, [United Daily News (UDN) reported](https://udn.com/vote2026/story/124652/9793880). Organizers claimed more than 30,000 attendees, while some other reports put the figure at over 120,000. President Lai Ching-te, Vice President Hsiao Bi-khim, and Kaohsiung Mayor Chen Chi-mai were among the featured guests.

Tsai, one of the most prominent television hosts in the Chinese-speaking world, was spotted seated in the front-row "rock zone." According to UDN, he left about 30 minutes after the event began and was approached by reporters. He said he had come to "take a look," adding, "Many people came to participate; I also came to listen to the other side's views." Asked whether he would attend an event for incumbent KMT mayor Chiang Wan-an, he replied that "if I have the chance to interview every candidate, that would be great."

## A Proxy Battle Over Cross-Strait Policy

The rally underscored how the 2026 Taipei mayoral race has become partly a referendum on cross-strait policy. Shen, a DPP legislator known for his combative national-security and cross-strait profile, was formally nominated in May 2026 to challenge incumbent Chiang Wan-an of the Kuomintang.

At the rally, Lai rejected suggestions that Shen had been nominated merely to constrain Chiang and secure roughly 45 percent of the vote, calling the claim "cognitive warfare." [Lianhe Zaobao](https://www.zaobao.com.sg/news/china/story20261004-9783220) reported that Lai stressed: "I am the party chairman; I nominated Shen Boyang to make him win the election!"

Shen appeared in a "captain" outfit—a navy double-breasted jacket, white trousers, and a bright orange scarf—echoing his campaign's "ocean current" (yang liu) branding, a play on his name.

## Analysts Debate Whether Campaign Is Breaking Through

Scholars who observed the rally offered a mixed assessment of whether Shen's campaign is broadening his appeal beyond the DPP's base. Chen Shih-min, a political science professor at National Taiwan University, said that most standing attendees at the back stayed for a long time, suggesting they were "clearly self-motivated" rather than organized by party mobilization.

"The persuasiveness of the ocean current toward median voters is increasing," Chen said, though he cautioned that whether it can continue to expand support remains to be seen. Liu Chia-wei, a professor at National Taipei University, said that judging whether the campaign has truly broken out of its "echo chamber" depends on whether attendees were original DPP supporters or merely curious onlookers.

On-site interviews appeared to support the latter possibility. An attendee in his late 70s told Lianhe Zaobao he drove from Tamsui out of curiosity, saying he "didn't know who Shen Boyang was" before seeing him on television. A 60-year-old woman identified as Ms. Lin said she had previously voted for candidates from different parties and was drawn to Shen's positive, non-attacking style.

## Separate Sanctions on Family Business

At the same October 14 press conference, Chen announced fresh sanctions on VWAY CO., LTD., a company linked to Shen's father, Shen Tucheng. According to [a separate UDN report](https://udn.com/vote2026/story/7331/9813722), pro-Beijing Hong Kong outlet Ta Kung Wen Wei had reported a day earlier that after the elder Shen's earlier company, Zhaoyi, was sanctioned by China in June 2025, he used VWAY to continue doing business on the mainland, with trade volume of nearly US$700,000 in just over half a year.

Chen said the TAO would prohibit VWAY from any transactions or cooperation with mainland organizations, enterprises, and individuals.

"Shen Boyang on one hand agitates anti-China and anti-communist sentiment, obstructs cross-strait exchanges and cooperation; on the other hand, together with relatives, tries every means to make money from the mainland. The ugly conduct is despicable," Chen said. He added that it is "entirely futile" for such enterprises to try to evade sanctions, saying this "precisely exposes their panic at the sharp sword of punishing independence hanging high."

Asked about evidence and about Shen's late-night social media post questioning why anyone would focus on his family's business, Chen said the person in question "has always called white black, changes face faster than turning a page," and "what he should do now is prove his innocence and give Taiwanese society a responsible account."

## What to Watch

With Taiwan's nine-in-one local elections expected in late November 2026, the Taipei race is likely to remain a focal point of cross-strait tension. The mainland's willingness to respond directly to a celebrity's presence at a rally—and to tie it to sanctions on a candidate's family business—signals that Beijing intends to keep pressure on figures it designates as separatists as the vote approaches.

For Shen, the challenge is whether the "ocean current" momentum observed at the rally can translate into support among the median voters who will ultimately decide the race. For Tsai, whose broad popularity spans both sides of the strait, the episode has already drawn sharp criticism from mainland commenters—and placed a rare spotlight on the political calculations of celebrity appearances in Taiwan's elections.
