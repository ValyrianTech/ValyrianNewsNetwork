# NSV! Protests in Leuven Over Cancelled Dewinter Lecture

The nationalist student organisation NSV! staged a protest on the Grote Markt in Leuven on Wednesday evening after the city cancelled a planned lecture by Vlaams Belang figurehead Filip Dewinter, escalating a dispute over free speech and campus politics in the Flemish university city, [VRT NWS reported](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/10/14/nsv-organiseert-protest-op-grote-markt-in-leuven-na-geannuleerd/). Dewinter, a Flemish MP, was present at the demonstration, which he and organisers framed as a defence against censorship.

The lecture, on the controversial 'omvolking'—or 'Great Replacement'—theory, was to have taken place in the Romaanse Poort hall. The city, which owns the venue, cancelled the booking after concluding that NSV! had breached the rental agreement by publicising the event in advance.

## The Cancellation

According to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/10/12/lezing-filip-de-winter-afgelast/), the hall seats only about 40 people, and organisers had agreed not to advertise the event beforehand. Both NSV! and Dewinter nonetheless promoted it on social media. Leuven mayor Mohamed Ridouani (Vooruit) was blunt about the decision: "The conditions for renting the meeting room were not met, with cancellation as a result."

Police spokesperson Marc Vranckx told [HLN](https://www.hln.be/leuven/politie-in-leuven-op-scherp-nadat-lezing-met-dewinter-niet-door-kan-gaan-confrontatie-tussen-links-en-rechts-mogelijk~ae8cc6df/) that the issue was procedural, not ideological. "The agreement was that no advertising would be made for the lecture, given that it concerns a meeting room with limited capacity. Both Filip Dewinter and NSV! broke that agreement," he said. Vranckx stressed that the dispute "really concerns the conditions for renting the hall that were not met, and not the content of the lecture."

## Competing Narratives

NSV! rejected that explanation. In a social media statement, the group called the stated reasons a mere pretext. "We are convinced that the real reason lies with the content of the lecture and the pressure that was exerted following the threat of violence by left-wing counter-demonstrators," it said. The organisation added: "We regard this as an unacceptable infringement of freedom of speech. That is why we feel compelled to make our voice heard in the street. We will not let ourselves be silenced."

Dewinter echoed that framing, telling HLN, "Ridouani was simply looking for a rod to beat the dog with," and accusing the mayor of bowing to pressure from the extreme left. "It is not my problem if it gets out of hand," he said. "I remain someone elected by hundreds of thousands of Flemish people and I may express my opinion." He had floated legal action before the Council of State, though HLN reported that scenario appeared unlikely.

National NSV! president Arnt Hubert suggested the city government might be yielding to extreme-left pressure and would not rule out that activists on the left could use violence, saying "They are not averse to violence against those who think differently."

## Police on Alert

The prospect of a confrontation between left-wing and right-wing demonstrators prompted a significant police deployment. Counter-demonstrations had been announced, including by the Leuven anarchist organisation LAG, at the nearby Joris Helleputteplein. No permits had been requested for the demonstrations.

"We will be present on the ground, in uniform and in plain clothes," Vranckx said, adding that police would "do everything to prevent disturbances." There were no immediate reports that the protests had turned violent.

Elias Drijfboom, president of NSV! Leuven, declined to comment to VRT, and the city was not immediately reachable for further comment.

## A Recurring Flashpoint

The dispute fits a pattern in Leuven, where far-right events hosted by NSV! have repeatedly drawn legal and political challenges. In 2023, a court overturned KU Leuven's last-minute decision to ban a lecture by Austrian far-right figurehead Martin Sellner. A year later, a lecture by former MP Dries Van Langenhove—advertised as being about 'regenerative agriculture'—led to a hate-speech conviction over his remarks about Black Africans and Roma women.

The 'omvolking' theory at the centre of the cancelled lecture holds that Western countries' native white populations are being systematically replaced by non-Western immigrants. VRT has described the term as one that "works like a red rag to a bull" in Belgian political debate.

The episode also follows recent unrest elsewhere: earlier in October, a scuffle at Antwerp city hall involving Dewinter left him with a bleeding eyebrow.

## What's Next

At its core, the standoff turns on a familiar Belgian tension: whether a venue cancellation driven by contractual or security concerns is a legitimate administrative decision or de facto content-based censorship. The city insists it acted on procedure; NSV! and Dewinter insist the procedure was a cover story.

NSV! has said it would seek another venue or, if necessary, a public space for the lecture, and did not rule out further action. Whether the dispute remains a local flashpoint or feeds a broader national debate over free speech and the far right in Flanders may depend on how the coming days unfold.
