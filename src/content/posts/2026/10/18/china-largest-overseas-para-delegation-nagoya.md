# China Debuts Largest-Ever Overseas Para Delegation in Nagoya

China's sports delegation for the Aichi-Nagoya Asian Para Games made its formal appearance at a welcome ceremony in Nagoya on 17 October, opening a Games that run from 18 to 24 October and drawing the country's largest-ever overseas squad to the regional event. The delegation, first to enter the afternoon session, marched into the Athletes' Plaza at Nagoya Port Garden Pier with officials and athletes from wheelchair fencing and other sports, according to [Xinhua News Agency](https://www.news.cn/sports/20261017/6cd08f4886d34fbea60385e1988b0d8e/c.html), which covered the ceremony.

The Games bring more than 3,000 para-athletes from 45 countries and regions together across 18 sports and 502 events. China is contesting 17 sports and 335 events, and the ceremony served as a formal welcome ahead of the opening night.

## A Delegation at Record Scale

China's contingent totals 500 people, including 312 athletes — 146 men and 166 women — drawn from 25 provinces and regions and 13 ethnic groups, according to a delegation announcement [reported by CCTV](https://focus.scol.com.cn/zgsz/202610/83337099.html). The athletes have an average age of 28.8, ranging from 16 to 50. It marks China's fifth appearance at an Asian Para Games and its largest delegation ever sent to an overseas edition of the event.

The scale reflects the growing depth of China's para-sport program, which has become a fixture at the top of Asian and Paralympic medal tables in recent cycles.

## Pride on Display

For the athletes, the flag-raising and ceremony carried weight beyond protocol. Ao Lanzhu, a wheelchair fencing athlete competing in six events — two individual and four team — described the moment the Five-Star Red Flag was raised.

"At this moment I feel extremely proud and happy," Ao said, according to Xinhua. "These days we've had pre-competition training and adapted to the venue; everything feels okay. This time I'm competing in 6 wheelchair fencing events — 2 individual and 4 team. I'll try to do well in every match and strive to win championships!"

Across three days, from 15 to 17 October, welcome ceremonies were held three times daily on the plaza, with delegations applauding one another as they entered in formation — a display of the camaraderie that defines the multi-sport event.

## Flag Bearers Set the Tone

China named female para taekwondo athlete Li Yujie and male para powerlifter Yan Panpan as its flag bearers for the opening ceremony, [Xinhua reported](https://www.news.cn/sports/20261017/e05cec830eba4cbdbc91dcd30559e540/c.html).

Li, 25, won China's first-ever Paralympic taekwondo gold at Paris 2024 and was named 2025 World Para Taekwondo Female Athlete of the Year. Yan, born in 1990, is a dominant para powerlifter with multiple World, Asian Para Games, and Tokyo and Paris Paralympic golds to his name. Both are students or graduates of Beijing Sport University. Their selection signals a squad blending established champions with rising stars.

## Housing and Host Tensions Linger

The Games open against a backdrop of organizational scrutiny. The container-style housing built for the able-bodied Nagoya Asian Games earlier this year — which drew complaints from some competitors — is being reused to accommodate some para-athletes and to house delegation headquarters. Xinhua noted the arrangement neutrally, but it connects to prior concerns about athlete accommodation at the venue.

Hospitality has also been a sensitive theme. Aichi Governor Hideaki Ohmura drew a diplomatic flap after Thailand team officials posted social-media complaints about food, reportedly linked to the newly added teqball event. Ohmura said on 6 October he "didn't really want such people to come," remarks that triggered backlash in Thai media, as [Tokai TV reported](https://www.tokai-tv.com/tokainews/feature/article_20261008_48693). He later clarified that the remark applied only to the officials who posted, reaffirmed that Thai athletes were welcome, and said the region would provide proper hospitality for the Para Games.

## What to Watch

The opening ceremony on 18 October launches a week of competition across 18 sports. For China, the immediate questions are whether its record-sized delegation can convert depth into medals across 335 events, and how athletes like Ao — chasing titles in six wheelchair fencing disciplines — perform on the Nagoya stage. Beyond the podium, the Games will be watched for how organizers address accommodation and hospitality concerns that surfaced during the preceding Asian Games, and whether the para event delivers the welcoming atmosphere its host has promised.
