# China Eases Homebuying With Completed-Home Push, Subsidies

China has introduced a package of new housing measures designed to make homeownership more accessible and to stabilize its long-troubled property market. The policies — rolled out across August and September 2026 — encourage the purchase of completed, already-built homes over presale units, lower the entry threshold for buyers, and provide direct interest subsidies on mortgages, according to [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/fortune/20261017/9762181207b14d439b4caa480f4b94c7/c.html).

The measures mark one of Beijing's most ambitious attempts yet to reshape a real estate sector that has weighed on the world's second-largest economy for years.

## From Presale to Completed Homes

The centerpiece of the reform is a shift away from China's long-standing commercial presale system, under which buyers pay for homes that are delivered two to three years later — often exposing them to mismatched specifications, delayed delivery, or stalled and unfinished projects.

On 28 August 2026, the Ministry of Housing and Urban-Rural Development, the Ministry of Natural Resources, and the Financial Regulatory Administration jointly issued a notice making completed-home sales the priority option for new housing, as [China News Service](https://www.chinanews.com.cn/cj/2026/08-28/10686211.shtml) reported. The change is significant: the presale model has operated for more than three decades and has been a defining feature of how Chinese homes are bought and sold.

Crucially, the reform is a dual-track transition rather than an outright abolition of presale. For land newly transferred — and for transferred land that does not yet hold a construction-engineering planning permit — completed-home sales becomes the priority. For projects already holding the permit, completed-home sales is merely encouraged. Existing presale projects obtained before the notice's effective date continue under prior rules — a "new projects, new rules; old projects, old rules" approach.

For those projects that still use presale, the bar has been raised sharply. Presale is now permitted only after the main structure is topped out, and buyer funds are released to developers only after completion acceptance.

## Explaining the Rationale

Officials and researchers have framed the change as a way to shift delivery risk away from buyers and toward developers. Pu Zhan, deputy director of the MOHURD Policy Research Center, said that under completed-home sales, buyers can inspect a property's area, layout, and quality on site — and learn about the surrounding community and facilities — before committing, which "fundamentally prevents delivery risk."

Wu Jing, director of the Tsinghua University Real Estate Research Center, argued that the tangibility of buying a finished home helps convert hesitant would-be buyers into actual ones, "helping consolidate the market's stabilization."

Li Yujia, chief researcher at the Guangdong Housing Policy Research Center, noted that with presale thresholds raised and funds monitored more strictly, "the gap between presale and completed-sale narrows," and more projects will shift toward completed-sale under policy support.

## Interest Subsidies and a Lower Threshold

The second pillar is a direct subsidy on mortgages. On 29 September 2026, the Ministry of Finance, the People's Bank of China, and the Financial Regulatory Administration jointly issued a notice establishing a residential home-purchase loan interest subsidy, effective 1 October 2026, with a provisional one-year implementation period.

Under the terms, first-home purchases of commodity housing with floor area of up to 120 square meters and a total price of up to 1.5 million yuan are eligible. The subsidy equals an annualized 1 percentage point on loan principal, capped at 1 million yuan of principal per household, for a maximum of five years. The policy targets so-called "new citizens," recent graduates, and wage-earning working families. According to analyses of the notice, it is the first time China's central government has subsidized commercial personal housing loans.

Li Yujia estimated that with the 1-point subsidy, buyers' actual average mortgage rate would fall to about 2.06 percent.

The subsidy was complemented by a monetary move: on the same day, the PBOC reportedly cut the one-year rate on Pledged Supplementary Lending by 25 basis points to 1.5 percent. Additional supporting measures announced in August included support for reasonable developer financing, an extension of the maximum term of personal housing loans from 30 years to 40 years, and the creation of a lead-bank system for development loans.

## A Market Beginning to Stir

The policies arrived as China's property market showed tentative signs of life. During the National Day "Golden Week" holiday from 1 to 7 October 2026, key-city transactions rose year-on-year. New-home sales across 25 key cities averaged 33.8 percent higher per day than a year earlier, while existing-home daily transactions across 11 key cities rose 42.8 percent, according to research institute data.

In Shanghai, existing-home volume rose 44 percent and new-home volume 64 percent year-on-year, with existing-home daily transactions up 30 percent. In Shenzhen, existing-home signings climbed about 63 percent over the seven days. Beijing saw new-home daily online signings jump 176 percent in volume and 157 percent in area year-on-year.

Analysts urged caution. Cao Jingjing, general manager of the index research department at the China Index Academy, characterized the rebound as "structural" and warned that online-signing data can be distorted by holiday filing rhythms and base effects. The post-holiday "Silver October" performance is seen as a key window for the fourth quarter.

The subsidy appeared to have primarily activated "returning" buyers — those who had already viewed homes and were hesitating — rather than brand-new customers, with a clearer effect on lower-priced projects that fit the 1.5 million yuan eligibility cap.

## Analysis and Implications

The package reflects a broader strategic pivot toward what officials call a "new model" for real estate development. Yan Yuejin, deputy director of the Shanghai E-House Real Estate Research Institute, described the series of policies as all revolving around building that new model, with "unprecedented" policy intensity.

On the supply side, Pu Zhan noted that in the short term, completed-home sales and higher presale conditions delay the release of new supply — a positive development during the current de-stocking phase — while better protecting buyers' rights and improving demand-side expectations.

Some commentary has also linked the housing measures to China's demographic and fertility agenda, noting that the March 2026 Government Work Report reportedly voiced support for housing security for newly married and first-child families and for improving the housing needs of multi-child families. That framing, however, comes largely from self-media commentary and should be treated as interpretive context rather than official policy text.

## What to Watch Next

Whether the reforms deliver lasting stabilization will depend on execution. The dual-track structure means the full shift to completed-home sales will unfold gradually, project by project, as new land is transferred and old presale projects are completed. The one-year provisional window on the interest subsidy also creates a natural test period for gauging whether the demand boost is durable or merely a pull-forward of purchases.

For now, Beijing has signaled clearly that it is willing to deploy both regulatory reform and fiscal incentives to coax buyers back — and to move the risks of a half-built home off their shoulders.
