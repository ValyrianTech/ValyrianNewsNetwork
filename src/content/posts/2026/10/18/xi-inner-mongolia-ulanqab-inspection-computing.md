# Xi Touts Patience and Green Tech on Inner Mongolia Tour

Xi Jinping inspected Ulanqab, in the Inner Mongolia Autonomous Region, on October 16, calling on the country to pair lofty goals with patience and steadiness as he spotlighted wetland restoration and the fast-growing computing industry, [Xinhua reported](https://www.news.cn/politics/leaders/20261017/7266a3a7012d49db94f7b4861825713c/c.html). The visit, detailed in a lengthy Xinhua feature published October 17 under the headline "With high aspirations and more patience and composure," marked the General Secretary's fourth inspection of Inner Mongolia since 2012.

## Protecting the 'original ecology'

Xi's first stop was Huangqi Hai, an inland lake in Chahar Right Front Banner that nearly dried up in the 1970s and 1980s as drought and low rainfall took their toll. After years of comprehensive treatment, the lake's water surface has recovered to more than 85 square kilometers as of late September, and native fish populations were observed returning in May 2025.

"Green development is an inherent requirement of high-quality development," Xi said, urging officials to coordinate high-quality development with high-level environmental protection. "The key to wetland protection is preserving the original ecology; we must respect nature, follow nature and protect nature." Addressing the region's broader desertification battle, he warned that controlling sand was a heavy task: "Persist and strive for 'green advancing, sand receding.'" He also pointed to overgrazing, urging local leaders to resolve the problem as they develop livestock farming.

Inner Mongolia has become a showcase for the national "Three-North" shelterbelt program. According to official figures cited in the coverage, the region has completed 130 million mu of ecological construction since 2023 — equivalent to 85 percent of the work completed across the first five phases of the decades-old project.

## A bet on 'green computing'

Xi then visited the Chahar High-tech Development Zone, home to Ulanqab's green computing cluster, one of China's eight national computing hubs and ten data center clusters. Positioned as a computing base serving the Beijing-Tianjin-Hebei region, the city has drawn more than 84 signed data-center projects with total investment exceeding 500 billion yuan, according to [Guangming Daily](https://news.gmw.cn/2026-01/26/content_38557281.htm). Its average annual temperature of 4.3°C enables natural cooling, with power usage effectiveness below 1.2, and network latency to Beijing of roughly 4.2 milliseconds.

"China is already running neck-and-neck or leading in some fields, and the computing power industry is one of them," Xi said. But he paired that optimism with a caution against disorderly expansion. "What worries me most is disorderly development — rushing in together, then scattering together," he said, warning against blind competition and "involution" regardless of local conditions.

Speaking to technology workers, Xi invoked the Long March: "Have lofty goals, but more patience and composure. Like the Red Army's Long March, we are on a new Long March — you are a contingent for high-level sci-tech self-reliance." He called for extending the industrial chain and deepening integration of the digital and real economies, turning computing advantages into development advantages.

## A strategic signal

The inspection took place as 2026 marks the opening year of China's 15th Five-Year Plan, ahead of 2027's 80th anniversary of Inner Mongolia's establishment. Xi was accompanied by Inner Mongolia Party Secretary Wang Weizhong and regional government chairman Bao Gang, while Vice Premier He Lifeng and officials from central and state departments also joined the tour, according to the [official inspection readout](https://www.news.cn/politics/leaders/20261016/2e0fa7c03c33497bafb504fc030839f7/c.html).

Analysts read the trip as a careful balancing act. By praising the computing boom while warning against "involution," Xi appeared to signal that Beijing wants disciplined, sustainable growth in its AI infrastructure buildout rather than a race to build data centers at any cost. The emphasis on "correct views of political achievements" — avoiding vanity projects — echoed a recurring theme in his recent messaging on high-quality development.

## What's next

The dual focus on ecology and computing reflects Inner Mongolia's assigned role under national strategy: building "two barriers" of ecological and security protection, "two bases" for energy and agriculture, and an opening-up bridgehead to the north. How local officials translate Xi's call for patience into concrete policy — and whether it tempers the sector's exuberance — will be closely watched as the 15th Five-Year Plan gets underway.
