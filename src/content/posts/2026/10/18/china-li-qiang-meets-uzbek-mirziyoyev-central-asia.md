# China's Li Qiang Meets Uzbek Leader Amid Central Asia Push

Chinese Premier Li Qiang met Uzbek President Shavkat Mirziyoyev in Tashkent on 17 October, opening a two-day official visit aimed at translating high-level political agreements into concrete trade, energy and infrastructure deals, according to [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/politics/leaders/20261018/8f4b6ccea4e74ead972963a0fc0899bb/c.html).

The meeting, held during Li's broader Central Asia tour, was framed by both sides as a follow-through on the August 2026 summit between Chinese President Xi Jinping and Mirziyoyev on the sidelines of the SCO summit in Bishkek. After Uzbekistan, Li is scheduled to travel to Tajikistan for the SCO Council of Heads of Government meeting.

## A 'Golden Age' of Ties

Mirziyoyev described current Uzbekistan–China relations as having entered a "golden age," congratulating Beijing on the 105th anniversary of the founding of the Communist Party of China and reaffirming Tashkent's firm adherence to the One-China principle, Xinhua reported. He also pledged continued joint efforts against what both governments term the "three evil forces" of terrorism, separatism and extremism, and voiced support for Xi's four global initiatives.

Li, in turn, said China is ready to work with Uzbekistan to expand trade scale, optimize its structure and strengthen cooperation in energy, minerals, finance and connectivity. He called for high-quality implementation of the China–Kyrgyzstan–Uzbekistan (CKU) railway, and for deeper scientific and technological collaboration in artificial intelligence, the green economy and aerospace. The Chinese delegation included State Councilor and Secretary-General Wu Zhenglong.

## Deep Investment Footprint

According to the [official website of the President of Uzbekistan](https://president.uz/ru/lists/view/9736), the portfolio of joint "solid projects" has reached approximately US$60 billion, with more than 6,000 joint ventures now operating in Uzbekistan. Bilateral trade is showing steady growth, the number of Chinese tourists is rising, and the two sides continue an active exchange of experience on poverty reduction.

Priority cooperation areas named in the readout span "green" energy, chemicals, geology, automotive manufacturing, metallurgy, agriculture and infrastructure modernization, alongside peaceful nuclear energy, digital technology, AI, machine tools and robotics. Construction of the strategic CKU railway is proceeding at an accelerated pace, with its launch expected to give a powerful boost to regional trade and transport connectivity.

Both sides also discussed expanding tourism, cultural and educational exchanges, including plans to increase flight frequency between cities in the two countries, upcoming Days of Uzbek Culture in China and the mutual opening of cultural centers. Coordination within the SCO and the China–Central Asia ("C+C5") format was also on the agenda.

## Trading Up

The visit builds on the August meeting in Bishkek, where the two leaders set a target of raising bilateral trade to US$30 billion in the coming years, up from a current level approaching US$18 billion, as [Podrobno.uz](https://podrobno.uz/cat/politic/uzbekistan-i-kitay-natselilis-na-tovarooborot-v-30-milliardov-dollarov/) reported. That earlier summit also covered investment, industrial cooperation, energy, transport, high technology, education and humanitarian exchanges.

Notably, both the US$30 billion trade goal and the US$60 billion project portfolio are aspirational or pipeline figures rather than realized totals — a distinction worth keeping in view as the relationship deepens.

## What's Next

Li's stop in Uzbekistan is one leg of a wider Central Asian itinerary that continues in Tajikistan, where the SCO's prime-ministerial council will convene. The tour underscores Beijing's sustained focus on a region central to its Belt and Road Initiative, energy security and counter-terrorism priorities — with Uzbekistan, Central Asia's most populous country and a key transit hub, at the heart of that effort.

The two countries are also preparing to mark the 35th anniversary of diplomatic relations in 2027, with the third China–Central Asia summit slated to be held in China that year. No agreement texts or memoranda of understanding were publicly released alongside the readouts, leaving the precise scope of any signed deals unclear for now.
