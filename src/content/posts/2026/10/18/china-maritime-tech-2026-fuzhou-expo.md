# China Showcases Maritime Tech at 2026 Fuzhou Expo

More than 7,000 marine equipment products and innovations went on display at the 2026 World Maritime Equipment Conference, held October 16-19 in Fuzhou, Fujian Province, alongside the fourth Fujian International Marine Equipment Expo. According to [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/tech/20261017/3ec5b1ab3d924b7aa86e356e167a590e/c.html), the exhibits ranged from underwater robots and deep-sea aquaculture platforms to smart ship cockpits, spanning from the surface to the underwater and from nearshore waters to the far-reaching deep sea.

The event, an annual fixture in China's marine technology calendar, is framed by state media as a showcase for the country's new momentum and vitality in ocean science and engineering, and as a window into how automation, electrification, and deep-sea reach are reshaping the maritime sector.

## Context: A Platform for a Marine Power Strategy

The World Maritime Equipment Conference is jointly organized by the Fujian provincial government, the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology, and the Ministry of Transport, while the accompanying China Marine Equipment Expo is hosted by the state-owned China State Shipbuilding Corporation. The event aligns with China's broader marine power strategy and Fujian's ambitions to become a marine strong province.

Prior editions demonstrate the expo's rapid growth. As [Fujian Daily](https://www.fjdaily.com/app/content/2025-10/13/content_3621375.html) reported, the 2025 edition drew roughly 100,000 square meters of total exhibition space, including 80,000 square meters indoors across 11 themed zones at the Fuzhou Strait International Conference and Exhibition Center, plus about 20,000 square meters of outdoor displays. The same report noted a dedicated China State Shipbuilding pavilion with nearly 50 secondary units and more than 300 exhibits, including the country's first domestically built large cruise ship and a 270,000-cubic-meter LNG carrier.

## Intelligence Reshapes the Waterline

A recurring theme at this year's gathering was the digitization of vessels and equipment. In the expo's high-tech and electric ship zone, battery giant CATL presented its Yunlan integrated smart cockpit, which consolidates the dense array of knobs and gauges on a traditional bridge into a single screen displaying radar, heading and speed, position, water depth, and power status.

Xia Dezhou, head of R&D for CATL's electric ship digital-intelligence platform, said vessels equipped with the cockpit can achieve autonomous driving, automatic obstacle avoidance, route and speed planning, and voice-command control of navigation lights and mode switching. Xinhua reported that CATL's batteries have already been installed on more than 1,000 electric and hybrid vessels, spanning cargo ships, sightseeing boats, research vessels, workboats, and offshore engineering ships.

The push toward automation extends below the waterline. At the Shanghai Jiao Tong University booth, doctoral student Zhang Runtao presented Kaituo No. 2, a deep-sea heavy-duty mining vehicle prototype independently developed by the university. The vehicle is designed to extract polymetallic crusts, polymetallic nodules, and deep-sea sulfides, the mineral deposits that have made deep-sea mining one of the most closely watched frontiers in ocean resource development.

## Green Power and Deep-Sea Food

Offshore wind formed another major pillar of the exhibition. At the Fujian Offshore Wind Power Equipment special zone, China Three Gorges Corporation displayed a model of a 20-megawatt-class offshore wind turbine. Zhang Youbin, deputy general manager of the company's Fujian branch, said the unit was grid-connected in the waters off southern Fujian in February 2026. The figure underscores the rapid upward scaling of turbine capacity; the previous year's expo, per Fujian Daily, featured a world-first 16-megawatt unit alongside a 26-megawatt design.

The expo also highlighted efforts to move aquaculture farther offshore. Chen Ming, brand manager of Fujian Qiandong Offshore Granary Technology, described the company's Qiandong series of deep-sea aquaculture platforms, deployed in water roughly 20 meters deep and equipped with self-rotating, self-lifting cages designed to withstand wind, waves, and red tides. The aim, Chen said, is to help farmers stop relying on the heavens, a reference to the sector's traditional dependence on weather and natural conditions.

## Analysis: Three Converging Trends

The Fuzhou exhibits point to three converging currents in China's maritime industry. First is electrification: CATL's move from electric vehicles into marine batteries, already aboard more than a thousand vessels, signals that the battery supply chain is extending to the water. Second is autonomy, visible in both the smart cockpit's obstacle avoidance and the self-adjusting behavior of aquaculture cages. Third is deep-sea reach, embodied by the Kaituo mining vehicle and the growing scale of offshore wind.

A common thread is the framing of these advances as independently developed, an emphasis on domestic innovation that fits the broader push for technological self-reliance. Yet the picture presented at the conference is largely that of a single state-affiliated source: this year's coverage comes from Xinhua, and independent confirmation of the 2026 edition's exact scale beyond the 7,000-plus products figure was not available.

## What's Next

The 2026 conference closes on October 19, and the technologies on display are likely to be judged by how quickly they move from the exhibition floor to commercial deployment. Key questions to watch include whether autonomous ship systems advance toward broader commercial certification, how quickly deep-sea mining vehicles progress from prototypes to operational hardware, and whether China's offshore wind sector can sustain its rapid jump in turbine capacity. For now, Fuzhou offered a snapshot of a maritime industry being rebuilt around batteries, algorithms, and machines built to work ever deeper beneath the waves.
