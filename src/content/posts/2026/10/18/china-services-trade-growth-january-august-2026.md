# China Services Trade Grows 7.9% in First Eight Months 2026

China's services trade maintained steady expansion in the first eight months of 2026, with total imports and exports rising 7.9% year-on-year to 50,701.6 billion yuan, according to data released by the Ministry of Commerce and [reported by CCTV](https://news.cctv.com/2026/10/17/ARTIlmttkaE9fNenqYPwtAtr261017.shtml). Services exports grew far faster than imports, signaling improving competitiveness in higher-value segments of the economy.

## Exports Outpace Imports

Services exports climbed 14.8% year-on-year over the January-August period, nearly four times the 3.8% growth recorded in imports. The widening gap between exports and imports underscores a structural shift in China's trade profile, as the world's second-largest economy diversifies beyond goods manufacturing toward services and consumption.

The Ministry of Commerce's figures, carried by CCTV's *Xinwen Lianbo* news broadcast, identified travel, transportation, and knowledge-intensive services as the standout performers within the export basket.

## Travel and Transportation Lead Traditional Growth

Transportation services exports surged 28.2% year-on-year — the strongest gain among traditional services categories — reflecting buoyant logistics and shipping activity tied to goods trade flows. Travel services exports rose 15.3%, a sign of continued recovery and expansion in inbound tourism and related consumption.

Both sectors are closely watched as barometers of cross-border mobility and supply-chain activity, and their sustained momentum through the summer months suggests resilient demand despite softening global trade conditions.

## Knowledge-Intensive Services Now a Majority of Exports

Knowledge-intensive services exports grew 10.9% year-on-year and now account for 53.7% of China's total services exports. Within this category, personal, cultural, and recreational services expanded at the fastest rate, jumping 66%, while financial services rose 21.6%.

The figures point to China's growing role in higher-value, talent- and capital-intensive global services trade. The leap in cultural and recreational exports in particular signals rising international demand for Chinese content and experiences, while financial services growth hints at deeper integration of Chinese institutions into global markets.

## A Consistent Trend Through 2026

The January-August reading is consistent with the trajectory documented earlier in the year. In the first half of 2026, total services trade reached 38,872.6 billion yuan, up 8.0% year-on-year, with knowledge-intensive services accounting for roughly 53.5% of exports. The cumulative rate for January-July stood at 8.2% on a total of 45,781.6 billion yuan, indicating slightly moderating monthly momentum but still solid overall growth.

The stable share of knowledge-intensive services across successive releases suggests the shift toward higher-value exports is not a one-off fluctuation but an entrenched trend.

## Analysis: Rebalancing in Action

The data reinforces the structural evolution of China's trade profile. Services — especially knowledge-intensive and travel-related categories — are increasingly functioning as key export engines, complementing the country's long-dominant goods trade.

Strong services export growth also carries implications beyond trade balances: it reflects deeper integration into global value chains at the higher end, where returns are typically less exposed to commodity cycles and labor-cost competition. The 66% surge in cultural and recreational services, though from a smaller base, and the 21.6% rise in financial services illustrate where Beijing's policy emphasis on high-quality development is beginning to show measurable results.

## What to Watch

The slight deceleration from January-July's 8.2% to January-August's 7.9% merits monitoring in the coming months for signs of a sustained slowdown. A fuller monthly breakdown by category, which the ministry typically publishes with more granular data, would help clarify whether the moderation stems from travel, transportation, or other segments.

For now, however, the headline numbers point to an economy steadily rebalancing toward services — and to a services sector that is increasingly competitive on the world stage.
