# China's MIIT Forms Industrial Software Standards Committee

China's Ministry of Industry and Information Technology (MIIT) has established an Industrial Software Standardization Technical Committee, a specialized body tasked with developing and revising industry standards for the software that underpins modern manufacturing, according to [Xinhua News](http://www.news.cn/tech/20261023/5b8898cc4b1a48b7aad86606c1100846/c.html). The founding meeting was held in Hangzhou, Zhejiang Province, on October 21, with Vice Minister Xiong Jijun attending and delivering a speech, [China News](https://www.chinanews.com.cn/gn/2026/10-22/10718896.shtml) reported.

The move signals Beijing's intent to accelerate the maturation of a strategic software segment that has long been a bottleneck in the country's push for technological self-sufficiency.

## Context: Why Standards Matter

Industrial software — spanning design tools such as CAD/CAE/CAM, electronic design automation (EDA), manufacturing execution systems, and industrial control systems — sits at the intersection of software and heavy industry. It is high-value, technically demanding, and deeply embedded in production processes, making interoperability and reliability critical concerns.

Standardization has become a key lever for China's broader industrial policy. By setting common technical benchmarks, regulators aim to reduce fragmentation, break down proprietary silos, and foster a domestic ecosystem capable of competing with established foreign vendors. The committee's establishment aligns with national priorities on "new industrialization" and the high-quality development goals emphasized during the 15th Five-Year Plan period, which covers 2026 to 2030.

## Key Developments

According to the official release distributed through MIIT's "工信微报" WeChat account and reported by China News, the committee will be responsible for the revision and development of industry standards across five core areas: key technologies, data interoperability, products and services, integrated applications, and ecosystem building. Its secretariat is hosted at the Fifth Electronics Research Institute of MIIT, a Guangzhou-based body focused on electronic component reliability and testing.

The ministry framed the committee's creation as an important measure to "fully leverage the foundational, strategic, and leading role of standards" and to "accelerate the landing, transformation, and promotion of scientific and technological innovation achievements," according to the official statement. The stated goal is to guide and promote the high-quality development of the industrial software industry.

Following the founding meeting, the committee convened its first plenary session of all members. That session reviewed and approved the committee charter, the secretariat's working rules, and the procedures for standards development, while also setting priorities for the next stage of work.

## Analysis: Breaking Down Silos

The emphasis on data interoperability and ecosystem building is telling. China's industrial software market has historically been fragmented, with different vendors' products struggling to communicate and many high-end tools dominated by foreign suppliers. Standards that mandate interoperability could lower barriers for domestic firms and make it easier for factories to mix and match software from multiple providers.

The creation of a dedicated technical committee also reflects a maturing approach to governance. Rather than issuing directives from the top, MIIT is institutionalizing the process through a standing body that brings together ministry officials, committee members, and industry experts. This structure mirrors the network of national standardization technical committees China has assembled across other strategic sectors, from information technology to commercial aerospace.

For foreign vendors, the committee bears watching. Standards can serve as a pathway to integration, but they can also function as tools of market shaping, favoring domestic players who help write the rules. How the committee balances openness with the push for self-sufficiency will determine its impact on a global industrial software market worth tens of billions of dollars.

## What's Next

With its charter approved and work priorities set, the committee now moves into the substantive phase of drafting and revising standards. The first standards are likely to focus on the interoperability and key-technology areas flagged as priorities.

Observers will be watching for the pace of standard releases, the degree of participation by foreign and private firms, and whether the new benchmarks translate into measurable gains in the competitiveness of China's domestic industrial software industry. For now, the establishment of the committee marks a clear signal that Beijing views standards as a strategic instrument in its broader campaign to modernize and self-reinforce its industrial base.
