# China Outlines Commerce Priorities Under 15th Five-Year Plan

China's Ministry of Commerce laid out its priorities for the 15th Five-Year Plan (2026-2030) at a State Council Information Office briefing on October 22, emphasizing expanded consumption, a diversifying trade network, and two-way investment, according to [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/fortune/20261022/7b5f82108f0a4c5c8415c22ca133fbdc/c.html). Vice Minister of Commerce Jiang Chenghua led the session, part of the office's "Starting and Getting Off the Ground" thematic series devoted to the new planning period.

## Consumption as the Main Engine

Officials framed domestic consumption as the central driver of growth. Since the 18th Party Congress in 2012, final consumption expenditure has contributed an average of 55 percent to China's economic growth annually, while total retail sales of consumer goods have grown 7.1 percent per year on average.

"During the 15th Five-Year Plan period, MOFCOM will work harder on optimizing supply, innovating scenarios, and improving facilities, to continuously unleash the consumption potential of urban and rural residents," Jiang said.

Yang Mu, director-general of the Department of Market Operations and Consumption Promotion, detailed measures including expanding commodity consumption, full-chain expansion of automobile consumption, a work plan to accelerate smart products in consumption, and deeper cultivation of international consumption center cities. The ministry also plans to accelerate efforts to shore up county-level consumption.

A notable bright spot is the "China Haul" trend of inbound shoppers. China now has more than 18,000 tax-refund stores nationwide, a fourfold increase over end-2024, and departure tax-refund sales rose roughly 60 percent year-on-year in January-August 2026, with the number of people processing refunds tripling. Yang said the ministry will continue strengthening the "Buy in China" brand.

## Building a Strong Trading Nation

Yang Tao, director-general of the Department of Foreign Trade, said the ministry will advance goods, services, and digital trade as its "three pillars." For goods trade, he outlined four "cultivations" of entities, brands, markets, and new drivers, spanning cross-border e-commerce, overseas warehouses, green trade, and smart foreign-trade products.

On the trade network, Jiang said a globally oriented free trade architecture has taken initial shape. China has signed 26 free trade agreements with 33 countries and regions; FTA partners account for 45 percent of China's total foreign trade and 70 percent of attracted foreign investment. In the first three quarters of 2026, trade with FTA partners grew 30 percent year-on-year, contributing more than 70 percent of the foreign trade increment.

Next steps include pushing for early entry into force of the China-ASEAN FTA 3.0 and the China-Peru FTA upgrade, accelerating negotiations with South Korea, New Zealand, and Central Asian countries, and advancing RCEP expansion and upgrading.

## Two-Way Investment

On inbound investment, Wu Di of the Foreign Investment Administration said actual utilized foreign direct investment fell 4.3 percent year-on-year in January-September 2026, but the decline narrowed by 6.1 percentage points from a year earlier. High-tech industry investment attraction grew 33.8 percent, accounting for 41.6 percent of the total. China now hosts more than 560,000 existing foreign-invested enterprises.

"Next, we will fully implement national treatment for foreign-invested enterprises, treating domestic and foreign firms equally in government procurement, bidding, standards-setting, and other areas, step up intellectual property protection, and foster a fair-competition environment," Wu said. The ministry reported 84 new key foreign-invested projects and 6 landmark projects this year, with total investment of nearly US$20 billion.

On outbound investment, Zhao Chunfeng, director-general of the Department of Outward Investment and Economic Cooperation, said China's outbound investment stock reached nearly US$3.4 trillion by end-2025, ranking in the global top three for nine consecutive years. In the first three quarters of 2026, outbound investment in green, low-carbon, and digital industries grew more than 20 percent year-on-year. The ministry has established more than 90 bilateral investment cooperation working groups, guided by the principle that "wherever enterprises go, investment cooperation mechanisms follow."

## Analysis and Outlook

The briefing signals a deliberate strategy on several fronts. The heavy emphasis on inbound consumption, through tax-refund expansion and the "Buy in China" push, suggests Beijing is courting foreign shoppers as a services-export substitute while stimulating domestic demand amid uncertain external conditions. The FTA drive, anchoring 45 percent of trade and 70 percent of FDI, reflects an effort to diversify away from reliance on any single market and lock in regional trade architecture.

The investment figures tell a more nuanced story: headline FDI remains in contraction, but the narrowing decline and a 33.8 percent surge in high-tech inflows point to a structural upgrade toward higher-value, service-sector investment. Meanwhile, the outbound stock and the focus on protecting overseas assets and personnel underscore China's expanding global economic footprint.

What remains to be seen is how consumption stimulus will be funded and calibrated, whether inbound-consumption momentum can offset softer domestic sentiment, and whether FTA upgrades can deliver before external trade frictions bite.
