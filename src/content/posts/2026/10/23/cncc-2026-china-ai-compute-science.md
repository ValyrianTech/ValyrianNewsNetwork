# China's CNCC 2026 Spotlights Compute and AI-for-Science Push

The 23rd China Computer Conference (CNCC) opened on October 22 at the Western China International Expo City in Chengdu, drawing more than 11,000 registered participants and marking the first time in the event's more than 20-year history that it has been held in Sichuan province. Under the theme "Data-driven, Intelligent Computing for the Future," the four-day gathering—running October 21–24—has become a barometer for where China's AI sector is heading, with computing infrastructure and AI's entry into scientific laboratories dominating the agenda.

## A Window on China's Computing Trajectory

Organized by the China Computer Federation (CCF), CNCC is widely described as the country's largest, highest-specification and most influential annual academic and industry gathering in computing. According to [China News Service](https://www.chinanews.com.cn/gn/2026/10-22/10719105.shtml), this year's program includes 17 invited keynote reports, three plenary forums, 167 special-topic forums and more than 40 side events, organized around ten thematic tracks—including AI foundation models, intelligent agents, and information computing and network architecture. The content is structured into a three-tier "foundation, frontier and future" system.

A parallel CCF Computer Exhibition covers roughly 15,000 square meters with more than 200 booths across four zones, from industry enterprises to an "AI black-tech market," and is open to the public free of charge. In his opening address, conference chair Feng Dengguo—a CCF Fellow and academician of the Chinese Academy of Sciences—said data as a new factor of production is being woven into every stage of economic and social development, while intelligent computing is reshaping the paradigms of scientific research and industrial development.

## Compute as the Foundation

A recurring theme on the show floor was that AI infrastructure is shifting from optimizing individual components toward systemic engineering coordination spanning compute, communication, storage, chips and software scheduling.

"As model parameters grow and agents accelerate deployment, computing power systems are moving toward intensification and scale; innovation is needed around an integrated, tightly coupled system spanning computing, storage, interconnect, power and cooling," said Li Bin, senior vice president of Sugon, [according to Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/tech/20261022/15a3938d60194d06a14e9252e4efc56d/c.html). He noted that Sugon's "8000 (Dengfeng)" fully domestic 100,000-card AI supercluster, launched in July 2026, has already supported more than 300 key scientific engineering simulations and 10,000-card-scale model pre-training.

Sun Jianpeng, an AI computing design expert at Hygon, said large model parameters are climbing from the hundred-billion level toward trillions and even tens of trillions—a scale at which, he argued, a single card or machine can no longer support large-scale training and inference.

## AI Moves Into the Laboratory

"Scientific intelligence" (AI for science) emerged as one of the conference's most discussed topics, with speakers focused on the data bottlenecks that stand between AI and the lab bench.

"For AI to enter the lab, the first hurdle is data," said Zhu Xiangdong, a professor at Sichuan University and head of a pilot base at the National Engineering Research Center for Biomaterials. He noted that materials science data has long suffered from "scattered resources and a lack of coordinated standardization." Sichuan has begun standardizing data collection across literature, computation, experimentation, production and service for fields including biomedical materials and functional composites.

During the conference, Sichuan launched the "Tianfu Huiyan" integrated platform for AI-enabled sci-tech innovation. Centered on a 1.0 science foundation model with roughly 35 billion activated parameters, the platform is backed by domestic computing power, scientific data and dry/wet intelligent laboratories, and aims to extend to application scenarios in new materials, drug creation and clean energy—linking prediction, experiment, validation and feedback to move research from trial-and-error toward intelligent iteration.

## Industrial Applications Take Shape

Beyond the research frontier, speakers pointed to concrete deployments. Fan Qiwu, chief technology officer of Sichuan Natural Resources Digital Technology Co., described a mining large model whose exploration phase is already in use at more than ten units, with extraction trials underway at the Hongge South Mine and a Sichuan Gold subsidiary. On rock and mineral identification, he said, AI trained on 50,000 high-quality annotated samples processes 1,000 to 10,000 slides a day at 93% accuracy—up from 85% for human specialists—with expected annual economic value in the tens of millions of yuan.

## Sichuan's Regional Bet on AI

The conference also served as a platform for Sichuan's ambitions. The province treats "AI+" as its top innovation project. Xinhua reported that Sichuan's AI industry revenue reached 191.97 billion yuan in 2025, up 22.9% year-on-year, with 2,806 key AI enterprises including 2,380 high-tech firms and 93 specialized "little giant" companies. In the first three quarters of 2026, revenue reached 152.1 billion yuan, up 21% year-on-year. A provincial AI industry development report was released at the event.

## What to Watch

CNCC 2026 underscores a Chinese AI sector increasingly focused on the unglamorous but decisive layers of the stack: compute density, data standardization and the translation of models into industrial workflows. The coming year will test whether the tightly coupled "super-node plus super-cluster" architectures championed in Chengdu can deliver on their efficiency promises, and whether science foundation models like Tianfu Huiyan move from pilots into routine research use.
