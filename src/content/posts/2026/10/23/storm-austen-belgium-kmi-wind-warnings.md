# Storm Austen Hits Belgium as KMI Warns of Powerful Gusts

Storm Austen, the first autumn storm of the 2026 season, swept across Belgium on Friday, prompting the Royal Meteorological Institute (KMI) to issue a code orange wind warning for West Flanders, East Flanders and Hainaut. The eastern breakwater in Ostend has been closed, the port of Antwerp-Bruges has halted ship departures, and neighbouring France is grappling with widespread power outages, [VRT NWS reported](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/liveblog/kmi-verwacht-krachtige-windstoten-161000-gezinnen-zonder-stroo~1792663198457/).

## What the Warnings Mean

The KMI's code orange warning for wind took effect at 12:00 on Friday and runs until 01:00 on Saturday. A code yellow for wind covers the rest of the country, while a nationwide code yellow for rain applies from 05:00 Friday until 05:00 Saturday. Forecasters expect gusts reaching up to 90 km/h in the centre of the country and up to 100 km/h at the coast, with locally stronger bursts still possible, VRT NWS reported, citing the KMI.

VRT weather presenter Jacotte Brokken said conditions would build through the day. "Today we get a preview, but the fiercest rain and wind is for tomorrow," she said, noting that gusts inland could reach 90 km/h and up to 100 km/h at the coast. By Friday afternoon, she observed that the storm was beginning to weaken compared with what had been felt in England and France.

## Coastal Closures and Transport Disruption

In Ostend, the eastern breakwater (Oostelijke Strekdam) has been closed, and although the western breakwater remains open, police are advising walkers to stay away. Sportpark De Schorre, the staketsel pier and the Oktoberfoor have also been shut.

At the port of Antwerp-Bruges, no ships have departed since 11:00, and vessels cannot enter the harbour. Ships already anchored on the North Sea must wait offshore. "The port authority wants to avoid vessels having to moor in stormy conditions around 18:00 tonight," said Tom Cuypers of Port Antwerp-Bruges.

Rail operator Infrabel and SNCB/NMBS are limiting train speeds in the coastal zone from 15:00 on the Bruges-coast and De Panne-Lichtervelde lines, allowing drivers to react more quickly to obstacles. Flemish grid operator Fluvius has said public street lighting will stay fully on overnight to help emergency services respond.

## Parks and Public Spaces Shut

Brussels closed its parks, nature reserves and the Sonian Forest from 12:00, an hour earlier than initially planned. Flanders and Wallonia announced widespread closures of provincial domains, forests and nature areas, warning that entering wooded zones is dangerous while gusts can bring down branches and trees. In Grobbendonk, the scheduled opening of the Terra Nova natural cemetery was postponed to 12 November. "In stormy weather you're best off out of the woods," said Mayor Marianne Verhaert. "Because code yellow has been declared, we're taking no risks."

The Brussels fire service urged residents to secure loose objects. "Bring loose items on balconies, terraces and in gardens inside or fasten them firmly," spokesman Walter Derieuw said, also advising people to close windows and doors and to park vehicles away from trees and scaffolding.

## Regional Impact: France and the UK

The storm made landfall overnight in France and England, bringing coastal gusts of up to 140 km/h. By Friday afternoon, French network operator Enedis reported roughly 288,600 households without power: 148,500 in Brittany, 88,800 in Normandy, 49,600 in the Pays de la Loire and 7,800 in Hauts-de-France. Enedis said it had 2,000 staff on standby to restore supply "as soon as weather conditions allow."

In the United Kingdom, around 10,000 households lost power, including 9,215 in Cornwall linked to 21 electricity incidents. Ferries between France and England were suspended, no trains ran in Normandy, and schools and roads closed across southern England.

## Why the Storm Is Called Austen

Belgium, Portugal, Spain, France and Luxembourg have run a joint storm-naming system since 2018. For the 2026-2027 winter season they chose the names Adam, Bruna and Charles, while the UK, Ireland and the Netherlands use a separate list including Austen, Boelo and Chloe. Names follow the country where the first code orange is issued, which this time was the UK, giving Belgium "Storm Austen" rather than its intended "Storm Adam."

## What to Watch

The 1722 number has been activated by the FPS Interior for non-life-threatening storm or water damage, while 112 remains the line for emergencies, [VRT NWS reported](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/10/21/nummer-1722-geactiveerd-voor-risico-op-storm-of-wateroverlast/). The KMI expects unsettled, windy weather to persist until Tuesday, with the strongest gusts easing after Friday evening. Authorities will reassess park and forest closures on Saturday, and rail services in the coastal zone may see knock-on delays as speed restrictions remain in place until the end of the day's service.
