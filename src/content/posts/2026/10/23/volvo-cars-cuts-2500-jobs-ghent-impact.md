# Volvo Cars to Cut 2,500 Jobs Globally; Ghent Impact Unclear

Swedish automaker Volvo Cars announced on 23 October that it will eliminate 2,500 jobs worldwide as part of a cost-reduction drive, with the bulk of the cuts falling in Sweden. According to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/10/23/volvo-schrapt-2500-jobs-zweden/), roughly 2,000 of the positions will disappear in Sweden, while the company has not yet specified where the remaining 500 will be cut. That leaves the fate of Volvo's plant in Ghent, Belgium, uncertain for now.

The company framed the reductions not as outright dismissals but as "the termination of consultant contracts, natural attrition, and targeted departure arrangements," according to a press release cited by the Belgian public broadcaster.

## Why Volvo Is Cutting

Volvo pointed to a "further deteriorating external climate" as the reason for the savings, citing competition with China and the United States among the pressures. In total, the company aims to reduce costs by 450 million euros.

"We continue to operate in a highly challenging environment with strong external factors: deglobalisation, hypercompetition and climate change," CEO Håkan Samuelsson said. "We are convinced that we have the right long-term strategy to tackle these challenges."

The announcement is not the first round of reductions for Volvo. In May 2025, the automaker announced a restructuring that eliminated approximately 3,000 roles, mainly among office staff. That operation ultimately had only a limited impact on employment in Belgium.

## Uncertainty in Ghent

While Volvo confirmed the 2,000 Swedish cuts, it has left open where the other 500 positions will be removed. "We are going to look internally at what these savings could mean for our site," Barbara Blomme of Volvo Car Gent said.

Outside its home country, Volvo Cars operates factories in Ghent, the United States and China, with another under construction in Slovakia. The Ghent facility, which employs more than 6,300 people, has faced questions about its future for some time. The company is reportedly considering producing vehicles for other automakers in the future.

In July of this year, the Swedish manufacturer had pledged its commitment to the future of the Ghent plant after reaching an agreement in principle with the federal and Flemish governments on support measures worth up to 119 million euros. VRT NWS [reported](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/07/15/steunpakket-van-119-miljoen-euro-vlaamse-en-federale-overheid-s/) at the time that the deal was framed domestically as a rescue of the factory in exchange for Flemish public money.

## Broader Pressure on European Automakers

The Volvo announcement reflects widening strain across Europe's automotive sector. Manufacturers are navigating slowing demand for electric vehicles, intense price competition from Chinese producers, and high US import tariffs that are squeezing profit margins.

For Belgium, the stakes are significant. Volvo Car Gent is among the country's most important automotive employers, accounting for a substantial share of Belgian car exports and supporting thousands of jobs across the Ghent region. The plant builds the fully electric EX30, one of Volvo's best-selling EV models, tying its fortunes closely to the segment now under pressure.

Volvo Cars has been owned by China's Zhejiang Geely Holding Group since 2010 and employs around 43,000 people globally, with operations spanning Sweden, Belgium and the United States.

## What to Watch

Several questions remain unanswered. Volvo has not identified which departments, functions or countries will absorb the 2,500 cuts, and it is unclear whether the Ghent plant will be affected and, if so, how many of its jobs. The timing of formal consultations with Belgian trade unions also has yet to be announced.

With EV demand and tariff pressures showing little sign of easing, the central question for Ghent's workforce is whether this round of savings will prove as limited in Belgium as the last one — or whether the plant's recent public funding deal will be tested by a tougher market ahead.
