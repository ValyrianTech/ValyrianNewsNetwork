# Students Block ULB Solbosch Campus in Brussels

Students at the Universite Libre de Bruxelles (ULB) partially blockaded the Solbosch campus in Ixelles, Brussels, from 6:00 AM on Thursday 22 October 2026, at the call of the collective "Gen-Z contre-attaque" (Gen-Z Counter-Attack). The action forms part of a wider European student mobilization and primarily targets a sharp rise in the university registration fee (the minerval) and the reform of the "decret Paysage," the [RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/contestation-dans-l-enseignement-des-etudiants-bloquent-le-campus-solbosch-de-l-ulb-le-collectif-gen-z-contre-attaque-derriere-l-initiative-11804119) reported.

According to the broadcaster, access to the campus remained possible for students, but certain buildings were blocked, making the action a partial blockade rather than a total shutdown. The atmosphere was described as "chaleureuse" (warm), with coffee distributed and a fire lit to keep participants warm, and arriving students were invited to join the movement.

## Context: A Fee That Jumped 43 Percent

The blockade is the latest flashpoint in a dispute over the cost of higher education. The minerval rose this academic year from 835 euros to 1,194 euros (+43%) for the full-paying tier. Citing data from the Federation Wallonie-Bruxelles, RTBF [reported](https://www.rtbf.be/article/prix-de-l-abonnement-letec-minerval-et-precarite-etudiante-que-disent-les-chiffres-des-revendications-des-eleves-11796931) that this affects 58% of higher-education students who pay the full price.

The fee is tiered by household income: scholarship (boursier) students pay 0 euros, modest households 374 euros, intermediate households 835 euros, and those with no particular condition 1,194 euros. Minister Elisabeth Degryse (Les Engages) estimated roughly a year ago that 22% pay nothing, 10% pay 384 euros, 10% pay 835 euros and 58% pay the full 1,194 euros. The precise proportions cannot yet be confirmed, as registrations and scholarship applications remain open until 31 October. Amounts are indexed annually, capped at a 3% increase per year, and non-EU students may face additional specific registration fees of 4,175 euros.

## Grievances Beyond the Minerval

The collective's demands extend well past tuition. According to RTBF, "Gen-Z contre-attaque" also denounces the reform of the decret Paysage, which governs success and financing rules in higher education; the underfunding of education; rising public transport costs on LeTec bus fares; and what it describes as repression of protests and police interventions against demonstrating youth. The group brings together students, student associations (cercles etudiants), researchers, teachers and ULB staff, and had asked the university in vain to cancel classes on Thursday.

On the picket line, one action invited students to throw darts at targets bearing the faces of Education Minister Valerie Glatigny, MR president Georges-Louis Bouchez and Higher Education Minister Elisabeth Degryse, with explanations of the contested measures displayed alongside.

The decret Paysage has been a recurring source of anger. The so-called Glatigny reform was voted in December 2021, and the reformed rules entered into force at the start of the 2025-2026 academic year, generating frustration among students who found themselves non-financables. RTBF [examined](https://www.rtbf.be/article/decret-paysage-etudiants-non-financables-et-colere-etudiante-face-a-la-ministre-degryse-5-questions-pour-tout-comprendre-11602560) the resulting anger directed at Minister Degryse.

## A Broader Wave of Mobilization

The Solbosch blockade follows months of escalating student action across Belgium. Students mobilized in Brussels against the minerval hike on 16 September, and early October brought a wave of school and student protests in Liege, Mons and Brussels against the minerval, LeTec fares and budget cuts, culminating in a national demonstration. On 17 October, contestation in Brussels and Liege turned turbulent, with burning bins and an overturned car. Police surveillance was reported in Liege, with roughly 200 arrests since 1 October.

ULB has sought to soften the blow for the most vulnerable. In July 2026, the university [announced](https://www.lesoir.be/759356/article/2026-07-14/lulb-renforce-son-aide-pour-amortir-la-hausse-du-minerval) reinforced financial aid to cushion the minerval increase.

## Analysis: Competing Narratives on Cost

The dispute turns on how the fee increase is framed. For the protesters, the jump from 835 to 1,194 euros is a barrier that threatens access to education. One higher-education student speaking at the University of Mons told RTBF that the minerval "goes from 835 euros at university to 1200 euros" and, for hautes ecoles, "from 200 or 400 euros to 1200 euros," adding: "This massively blocks access to education."

Economists offer a different lens. Jean Hindriks of the Economics School of Louvain argued that the change is "nothing other than an indexation of a minerval that was frozen for more than 10 years," contending that "at some point, it has to follow the cost of living." He added that Belgian costs "are not exorbitant," with studies subsidized at roughly 90% and access without selection or entrance tests for most tracks.

The tension between affordability and sustainability of a heavily subsidized system is unlikely to be resolved by a single blockade. But the mobilization shows no sign of fading, and it is increasingly linking campus grievances to broader questions of youth precarity and public spending.

## What to Watch

Key questions remain. Whether ULB and other universities adjust aid further, whether the Federation Wallonie-Bruxelles revisits the decret Paysage, and whether the movement spreads to other campuses will determine the trajectory of the dispute. With registrations and scholarship applications open until 31 October, the true scale of the fee's impact will become clearer in the coming weeks. For now, the Solbosch blockade stands as the latest expression of a European student movement testing how far institutions will go to keep education within reach.
