# Xi Marks 90th Long March Anniversary With Rally, Key Speech

President Xi Jinping on Thursday led China's top leadership in commemorating the 90th anniversary of the Chinese Workers' and Peasants' Red Army's Long March victory, delivering a keynote address that cast the 1930s military retreat as the foundation of the Party's legitimacy and called on the nation to undertake a "new long march" toward national rejuvenation.

The grand rally was held in the Great Hall of the People in Beijing on the morning of October 22, [Xinhua reported](https://www.news.cn/politics/leaders/20261022/ea585c6f63b74a99a4bf8cba035eb796/c.html), drawing roughly 3,000 representatives from across society, the military, and the country's democratic parties for an event steeped in revolutionary symbolism.

## A Full Leadership Turnout

The rally was attended by the entirety of the Politburo Standing Committee — Premier Li Qiang, Zhao Leji, Wang Huning, Cai Qi, Ding Xuexiang, and Li Xi — alongside Vice President Han Zheng. Zhao Leji, who chairs the National People's Congress Standing Committee, presided over the ceremony, which opened with the national anthem and closed with the "Internationale."

A moment of silence was observed for revolutionary martyrs before Xi took the podium. The hall was decorated for the occasion: the Party emblem hung above the stage beneath the numerals "1936–2026," flanked by ten red flags, while a balcony banner urged attendees to "carry forward the great Long March spirit, walk the new long march well, and strive to advance the building of a strong nation and the great cause of national rejuvenation."

## The Speech: History as Mandate

Xi framed the Long March — a year-long retreat from October 1934 to October 1936 in which the Red Army crossed more than ten provinces, marched some 25,000 li, forded nearly a hundred rivers, and fought over 600 engagements — as a turning point whose lessons remain binding today.

"Only by remembering history can we open up the future," Xi said, according to the [full authorized text of his speech](https://www.news.cn/politics/leaders/20261022/8771efbecd384ffba61dc44720e065d1/c.html) released by Xinhua.

The President attributed the Long March's success to what he called the "path to victory," built on four pillars: correct Party leadership — pointing specifically to the January 1935 Zunyi Conference, where Mao Zedong's leadership was consolidated — firm revolutionary will, independence and the principle of seeking truth from facts, and the support of the broad masses.

"History eloquently proves that correct Party leadership and the establishment of Comrade Mao Zedong's core position were of decisive significance to the Long March victory," Xi said. The emphasis on a decisive "core" leader is widely read as a mirror of contemporary messaging around Xi's own position at the Party's center.

He defined the "great Long March spirit" as placing the fundamental interests of the people and the nation above all, maintaining firm revolutionary ideals, fearing no hardship or sacrifice, and relying on the people — a spirit he said "embodies the power of loyalty, truth, justice, and unity."

## The 'Five Musts' of the New Long March

The heart of the address was a policy framework Xi presented as the "Five Musts" for the road ahead:

1. **Uphold the Party's ideals, convictions, and purpose**, guided by Xi Jinping Thought on Socialism with Chinese Characteristics for a New Era.
2. **Promote high-quality development** and meet the people's growing needs for a better life, advancing common prosperity and balancing economic quality with reasonable growth.
3. **Carry out the great struggle with many new historical features**, better coordinating development and security to guard against major tests and risks.
4. **Accelerate building the People's Army into a world-class military**, upholding absolute Party leadership over the armed forces and achieving the PLA's centenary goal on schedule.
5. **Uphold Party leadership and strengthen Party building**, advancing full and rigorous self-governance to ensure the Party remains the "strong leadership core" of socialism with Chinese characteristics.

The fourth point places particular weight on military modernization, tying the anniversary to the People's Liberation Army's centenary goal set for 2027 — a near-term benchmark that has anchored defense planning under Xi's tenure.

"The new journey is an expedition full of glory and dreams — the Party's and the people's new long march," Xi said.

## Official Echoes and Broader Campaign

The speech was immediately reinforced across state media. [People's Daily](http://politics.people.com.cn/n1/2026/1023/c1024-40819874.html) ran front-page coverage and a front-page editorial titled "Joining Hands to Walk the New Long March Well," alongside its signature "Ren Zhongping" analysis piece. Zhao, in his role as master of ceremonies, praised the address as "highly political, ideological, and instructive," formally distilling the "Five Musts" for the assembled leadership.

The anniversary also coincided with an extensive cultural campaign. State broadcasters released a feature film on the Red Army's Four Crossings of the Chishui River, documentary retrospectives, and social-media explainers, while regional commemorations were held in provinces such as Guizhou and Zhejiang.

## Analysis: Continuity and Contemporary Priorities

The rally follows a well-established pattern in which major revolutionary anniversaries serve as occasions for ideological reinforcement. By anchoring his "Five Musts" in the Long March narrative, Xi links the Party's founding-era survival story to present-day policy goals: high-quality growth, the coordination of development and security, military modernization, and internal Party discipline.

The emphasis on the Zunyi Conference and on correct leadership carries a clear contemporary subtext. In the Xi era, the "new long march" has become a recurring rhetorical frame connecting revolutionary history to the "Two Centenary Goals," Chinese-style modernization, and common prosperity.

It is worth noting that coverage of the event comes almost exclusively from official outlets — Xinhua, People's Daily, and CCTV — and presents a unified message without independent or dissenting voices. The framing of the Long March spirit and the Five Musts should be understood as official political communication rather than neutral historical reporting.

## What's Next

The immediate test of the address's themes will be their translation into policy. The military modernization commitment points toward the 2027 PLA centenary, while the economic and security pillars of the "Five Musts" will shape priorities as Beijing pursues its 14th Five-Year Plan and sets the stage for the next phase of national development.

For now, the anniversary serves as a reminder of how centrally the Long March — and the leadership narrative built around it — remains embedded in the Party's self-understanding, nine decades after the Red Army's forces converged.
