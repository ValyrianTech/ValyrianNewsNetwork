# China Women's Football Coach Milicic Departs After Poor Run

The Chinese Football Association (CFA) announced on 23 October that Ante Milicic is no longer head coach of the China women's national football team, with his entire foreign coaching staff departing alongside him. The official statement, [reported by Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/sports/20261023/310b4cf0c63c47b48f7a8c6f731643e7/c.html), attributed the decision to Milicic's "personal family circumstances" following what it described as friendly consultation between the two sides.

The departure comes just weeks after China finished fourth at the 2026 Aichi-Nagoya Asian Games — a result that ended the team's run of Asian Games medals and intensified pressure on the Australian coach.

## What the Association Said

The CFA statement was carefully worded, thanking Milicic and his team for their "hard work" during their tenure and wishing them well in future endeavours.

"After friendly consultation between the two sides, and considering Mr. Milicic's actual personal family circumstances, the association has decided that Milicic will no longer serve as head coach of the Chinese women's national football team," the statement read, according to Xinhua. "The foreign coaching team departs simultaneously."

The association added that it would "study and determine the next-phase coaching arrangement" in line with the team's preparation tasks, and would "steadily advance new-cycle team building and Olympic qualifying preparation."

## A Tenure Marked by Setbacks

Milicic, an Australian former international who holds an AFC Pro coaching licence, was appointed in May 2024 after review by an expert panel, tasked with preparing the team for the 2026 Women's Asian Cup and the 2027 Women's World Cup.

His tenure ultimately unravelled through a series of disappointing results. In November 2025, China suffered a 0-8 defeat to England at Wembley — a joint-record heaviest loss matching the 0-8 reverse to Germany at the 2004 Athens Olympics. The result triggered widespread domestic criticism, with prominent football commentators publicly questioning the team's progress since the coaching change.

Milicic survived that crisis, reportedly retained in part because a substantial contract buyout deterred dismissal. But the reprieve proved temporary. At the March 2026 Women's Asian Cup in Australia, China topped its group before losing 1-2 to the host nation in the semifinal, failing to defend the title it had won in 2022.

## The Asian Games Collapse

The decisive blow came at the Aichi-Nagoya Asian Games. On 29 September 2026, China lost 0-2 to North Korea in a rain-soaked semifinal, managing only a single shot on target, [according to Xinhua's match report](https://www.xinhuanet.com/20260929/8661d26df4d0420b802cd54567a78ff9/c.html). Days later, in the bronze-medal match on 2 October, the team fell 0-1 to South Korea, [as reported by Observer](https://www.guancha.cn/GuoJi·ZhanLue/2026_10_02_903017.shtml), conceding from a set piece in the fourth minute.

The fourth-place finish marked China's first Asian Games without a medal in three editions, a stark symbol of the team's decline from its regional dominance of the 1990s and 2000s.

## Why the "Family Circumstances" Framing Draws Scrutiny

The official explanation cites personal family reasons, but the timing — arriving mere weeks after the Asian Games disappointment — has led many domestic observers to read the exit as results-driven. Chinese media editorials had openly called for a coaching change following the tournament, with at least one commentary arguing the team had become "weak against the weak and surrendering against the strong."

Unlike the earlier March 2026 episode, when a reported buyout effectively blocked dismissal, this exit is presented as a consensual parting — a formulation that avoids a large payout and allows a face-saving rationale for both sides.

## What Comes Next

The CFA has tied the coaching decision explicitly to Olympic qualifying preparation. The 2027 Women's World Cup in Brazil and the 2028 Los Angeles Olympic qualifiers — reportedly beginning around October 2027 — now loom as decisive tests for a program in transition.

With the senior team's 2026 season effectively over, the coming weeks offer an opportune window for a leadership reset. Succession remains unresolved: the CFA said only that it would "study and determine" the next arrangement. Names floated in Chinese media over the past year include Chang Weiwei, coach of Wuhan Chegu Jiangda; former national team boss Shui Qingxia, who reportedly declined an earlier approach; and Chen Wanting.

For a women's program that once set the standard in Asia, the appointment will carry weight far beyond the touchline. The question now is whether a new coach can arrest the slide before the World Cup and Olympic cycles demand answers.
