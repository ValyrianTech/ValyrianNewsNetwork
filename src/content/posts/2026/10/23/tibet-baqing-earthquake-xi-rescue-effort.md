# Tibet Quake Kills 16 as Xi Orders Full Rescue Effort

A magnitude-7.0 earthquake struck Baqing County in Nagqu City, in China's Tibet Autonomous Region, at roughly 10:33 p.m. Beijing time on Thursday, killing at least 16 people and injuring dozens more, according to [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/20261023/c61bb6628dd744328b5e539fd30c98e3/c.html). The quake, centered at a depth of 10 kilometers, prompted top-level emergency instructions from President Xi Jinping and Premier Li Qiang as rescue teams raced through the night to reach high-altitude communities in cold weather.

## Context

Baqing sits on the Tibetan Plateau at more than 4,000 meters above sea level, and the late-October timing means rescue operations are unfolding in freezing conditions — a combination that has repeatedly complicated disaster response in the region. The earthquake struck at 32.40°N, 93.79°E, according to the China Earthquake Networks Center.

## Key Developments

By 2:25 a.m. local time Friday, the Nagqu Emergency Management Bureau reported 16 dead and 32 injured — 11 seriously and 21 with minor injuries — with 339 households' homes collapsed, the bureau said. Later updates from Chinese media put the injury toll higher, at 65, though officials cautioned that casualty figures were still being verified.

Xi, in "important instructions" published by [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/politics/leaders/20261023/c3ceb86e65d94375b24d64eeef281e45/c.html), ordered that search and rescue be carried out "with full force" and that treatment of the injured be prioritized to "minimize casualties to the greatest extent." He warned that the disaster zone's high altitude and cold weather raised the risk of secondary disasters, calling for close monitoring of seismic activity and weather conditions and for rescue work to be conducted "scientifically."

Li Qiang issued follow-up instructions directing authorities to verify the scale of the disaster quickly, organize rescue and medical treatment, and relocate affected residents. He also ordered the Ministry of Emergency Management and the China Earthquake Administration to step up monitoring and forecasting, noting the area's vulnerability to aftershocks.

In response, the Ministry of Emergency Management dispatched a working group to the disaster zone. Tibet's regional government has mobilized rescue forces and safely relocated 12,489 residents, while medical experts, relief supplies including tents and bedding, and crews from State Grid and China Railway converged on the affected area. Hu Changsheng, the Tibet Autonomous Region Party Secretary, traveled overnight to the disaster zone and visited the injured at Suo County People's Hospital.

Eyewitness accounts described violent shaking. Residents told local media the quake felt "like several hundred yaks running at once," with buildings creaking and some homes developing cracks. Rockfalls damaged highway guardrails, and one resident's car was dented by a falling rock. A supermarket owner spent the night on a hill after being warned not to stay indoors, as police maintained order in the streets.

## Analysis

Xi's "important instructions" are a standard, high-visibility signal in China's political system that the top leadership is personally engaged in a crisis — and the speed and prominence of the response, published within hours on state media's front pages, reflects a familiar playbook for disasters in ethnic minority border regions. The January 2025 magnitude-6.8 earthquake in Dingri County, Shigatse, which killed more than 100 people, offers a close precedent for how Beijing frames leadership attention as a demonstration of Party care for affected communities.

The Baqing quake also tests China's tiered emergency response system. A national-level earthquake response was reportedly activated, with central disaster-relief funds and supplies pre-positioned and dispatched. The key variables in the coming days will be the final casualty count, the ability to move relief supplies along mountain roads in freezing conditions, and whether aftershocks cause further damage to already-weakened structures.

## What's Next

Rescue operations are continuing, with search teams working through the night to reach remote settlements. The death toll may rise as disaster conditions are further verified. The coming 72 hours — typically the critical window in earthquake search-and-rescue — will determine whether further survivors are found, even as cold weather and the terrain remain formidable obstacles.
