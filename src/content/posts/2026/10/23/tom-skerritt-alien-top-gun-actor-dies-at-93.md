# Tom Skerritt, 'Alien' and 'Top Gun' Actor, Dies at 93

Tom Skerritt, the veteran character actor whose granite-jawed authority made him a fixture on screen for seven decades as dads, cops and commanding officers, died Wednesday of natural causes. He was 93.

Skerritt's family confirmed the death in a statement, according to [The Hollywood Reporter](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-news/tom-skerritt-dead-picket-fences-alien-mash-1236738429/), which reported that he died in Seattle. A representative also confirmed the death to [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/10/22/981035400/tom-skerritt-dead).

"We are heartbroken by his loss," his family said in the statement. "While the world knew Tom through his remarkable acting career, we knew him for his humor, curiosity, wisdom, generosity, and the love he brought into our lives."

## A rugged on-screen presence

As NPR put it, there was no avoiding the word rugged with Skerritt: the mustache, the jaw, the crinkly eyes and the cool authoritarian bearing he brought to his most memorable parts.

The Detroit native who described himself as a "blue-collar kid" served in the U.S. Air Force before attending UCLA, where he turned to theater. He spent much of the 1960s working steadily in television, including *Gunsmoke*, *Combat*, *The Virginian*, *My Three Sons* and *The Alfred Hitchcock Hour*.

His breakout came in 1970, at age 37, as Captain Duke Forrest in Robert Altman's anti-war comedy *M*A*S*H*. He was one of the few characters from the film who did not carry over to the long-running television series.

## Signature roles

Skerritt's best-known parts spanned film and television:

- **Captain Dallas** in Ridley Scott's *Alien* (1979), the ill-fated captain of the Nostromo
- **Viper** in *Top Gun* (1986), the commanding officer to Tom Cruise's Maverick
- **Sheriff Jimmy Brock** on CBS's *Picket Fences*, a role that won him a 1993 Emmy Award for Outstanding Lead Actor in a Drama Series

He also played dads in *Ice Castles*, *Steel Magnolias* and *A River Runs Through It*, a small-town sheriff in *The Dead Zone* and a cop in *Harold and Maude*. His wider filmography included *Singles*, *SpaceCamp*, *Tears of the Sun*, *Contact*, *Poison Ivy*, *Poltergeist III* and *Ted*, in which he played himself. On television he appeared in a six-episode arc on *Cheers* and later in *Madam Secretary*, *The Good Wife*, *Brothers & Sisters* and *White Collar*.

He continued working into his nineties; his final film role was in the 2025 Western *Broke*, [Rolling Stone](https://www.rollingstone.com/tv-movies/tv-movie-news/tom-skerritt-alien-picket-fences-actor-dead-obituary-1235646827/) reported.

## Tributes and a life beyond acting

Lauren Holly, who co-starred with Skerritt on *Picket Fences*, paid tribute on social media Thursday, describing him as the leader of the production.

"I adored Tom Skerritt," she wrote, according to Rolling Stone. "His example made those years such a joy and we all loved and respected him so much. He cared deeply for his family, both the one at home and the one at work."

Off screen, Skerritt was civically engaged, particularly around veterans' causes. He co-founded The Red Badge Project, a program offering storytelling courses to veterans to help them share their experiences, and promoted independent film and theater in the Pacific Northwest, where he lived.

## A durable archetype

Skerritt's longevity, from 1960s television through a 2025 film role, made him one of the most recognizable character actors of his generation, even if his name was less famous than his face. He specialized in figures of quiet authority: men whose stillness reads as competence and whose mustache seems to arrive before they do.

The obituaries published Thursday were consistent on the essentials: Skerritt died of natural causes at 93, and the news was confirmed by both his family and a representative. Tributes and remembrances have flowed from co-stars and fans across the entertainment industry.

He is survived by his wife, Julie Tokashiki, his children and grandchildren.
