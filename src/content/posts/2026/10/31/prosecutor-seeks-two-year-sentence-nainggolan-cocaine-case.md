# Belgian Prosecutor Seeks 2-Year Sentence for Nainggolan in Cocaine Case

The Brussels prosecutor's office has requested a two-year prison sentence for former Belgium international footballer Radja Nainggolan, who is suspected of helping launder EUR 105,500 on behalf of a criminal organization, [RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/trafic-de-cocaine-le-parquet-requiert-deux-ans-de-prison-contre-le-footballeur-radja-nainggolan-11808148) reported. The request, made Friday before the Brussels criminal court, came as part of a larger trial into an alleged international drug-trafficking ring in which prosecutors are seeking sentences ranging from 12 months to 14 years for the other defendants.

## A Case Built on Money Flows, Not Trafficking

The distinction matters. Nainggolan, 38, has never been suspected of involvement in drug trafficking itself. According to RTBF, he is implicated because of the money trail: he received more than EUR 100,000 over several months from Nasr-Eddine Sekkaki, the brother of convicted criminal Ashraf Sekkaki, and is charged with participation in a criminal organization in connection with money laundering.

The prosecution's case stems from a wider investigation into the alleged importation of cocaine from South America into Europe through the port of Antwerp. In late January 2025, the Federal Judicial Police in Brussels carried out 30 searches, mainly in Antwerp province and Brussels but also in the Brussels periphery, and arrested 18 people.

Nainggolan was [arrested](https://www.rtbf.be/article/radja-nainggolan-arrete-dans-le-cadre-d-une-enquete-pour-trafic-de-drogue-11495399) in late January 2025 and charged, then released under conditions. In March 2026, the Brussels council chamber dropped the case against three people and committed 16 others, including Nainggolan, to the criminal court.

## A Changing Account

The footballer's explanation for the money has shifted over the course of the investigation. He initially said he had lent the funds to Sekkaki "par amitie," meaning out of friendship. He later changed his account, admitting he had received the money from Nasr-Eddine Sekkaki because his bank accounts had been blocked following a divorce.

That reversal has been central to the prosecution's argument that the payments were not a personal loan but part of a laundering arrangement. Nainggolan's legal position remains that he was not knowingly part of any criminal enterprise, and the court has yet to rule on guilt.

## A Career in Freefall

The case has unfolded against the backdrop of a steep decline in Nainggolan's fortunes. Once a mainstay of Belgium's "Golden Generation" of Red Devils and a fan favorite at Inter Milan, he has seen his club career fade in recent years. In September 2026, his club, Patro Eisden, [suspended](https://www.rtbf.be/article/radja-nainggolan-suspendu-par-son-propre-club-il-peut-fumer-et-sortir-mais-on-a-fixe-certaines-limites-11788691) him, a move that underscored how far the midfielder's standing has fallen amid the legal proceedings.

## The Bigger Picture: Antwerp as a Cocaine Gateway

The trial is one strand of a much larger challenge for Belgian authorities. Antwerp has become a principal entry point for cocaine shipped from South America, and Belgian customs seizures have hit record levels: 55 tonnes were intercepted at the port in 2025 alone, [RTBF reported](https://www.rtbf.be/article/55-tonnes-de-cocaine-saisies-par-la-douane-a-anvers-en-2025-11665355). That volume has fueled a parallel money-laundering economy in which prosecutors say professional footballers and other high-profile figures can become unwitting, or complicit, conduits for criminal cash.

## What's Next

The Brussels court will now weigh the prosecution's sentencing request. For Nainggolan, a two-year sentence would be a symbolic and personal reckoning; for Belgian authorities, the case is a test of whether the justice system can meaningfully prosecute the financial scaffolding of the cocaine trade, not just the trafficking itself. A verdict and sentencing decision are expected in the coming weeks.
