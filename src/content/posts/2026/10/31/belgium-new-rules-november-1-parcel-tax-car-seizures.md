# Belgium's New Rules Nov 1: Parcel Tax, Car Seizures

Belgium ushers in a package of consumer, justice, fiscal and energy changes on Sunday, 1 November, from a new 2-euro European handling fee on parcels arriving from outside the EU to the mandatory confiscation of cars belonging to drivers who ignore a driving ban. The measures, [compiled by VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/10/28/nieuw-op-1-november-2026/) and attributed to news agency Belga, span seven distinct areas of daily life and business.

## A 2-euro fee stacked on the parcel tax

The headline change for online shoppers is a new European handling fee of 2 euros on parcels imported from outside the EU. Crucially, it is charged **per product category, not per parcel**, and it applies to all shipments — including those worth more than 150 euros. It sits on top of the 3-euro import tax (the so-called "pakjestaks") that has applied since 1 July to orders under 150 euros.

According to [VRT NWS's explainer](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/10/29/vanaf-1-november-nieuwe-pakjestaks-wat-betekent-dat-voor-jou/), the distinction is technical: the 3-euro levy is a genuine tax, while the 2-euro fee is a "retributie" — a charge designed to compensate customs authorities for handling costs. As customs lawyer Philippe Heeren told VRT, the difference makes little practical difference to consumers.

"This measure comes on top of the 3-euro tax. It applies to all parcels, not just shipments under 150 euros," said Francis Adyns, spokesman for the FOD Financiën (Federal Public Service Finance). "What's more, the measure is of a permanent nature." The 3-euro levy, by contrast, is set to run until 1 July 2028.

The urgency is clear. The EU has struggled for years with a flood of cheap parcels, largely from China. In 2024, 4.6 billion small parcels entered the EU; in 2025 that rose to 5.8 billion. The July tax appears to have had an effect: [customs data](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/10/28/pakjestaks-resultaten-minder-goedkope-pakjes-meer-inkomsten/) show the levy has raised 364 million euros since July — about 28 million euros a week — and declarations of cheap parcels from countries such as China fell 65 percent over the past three months compared with the same period last year.

The fee only applies when a product enters the EU from outside. Goods already sitting in a European warehouse — such as Amazon stock held in France or Germany — are exempt, a detail that could reshape how platforms route their inventory.

## Car confiscated for ignoring a driving ban

On justice, police courts will from November be **obligated** to order the confiscation of a vehicle when a driver is caught violating a driving ban. Only in exceptional, extensively motivated cases may a judge deviate. The car becomes state property and is typically sold at public auction, with proceeds going to the treasury.

The law was introduced on the initiative of N-VA Member of Parliament Wouter Raskin. "These are people whom the police court has denied the right to drive, but who ignore that ban," he [told VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/10/20/wie-rijverbod-negeert-is-voortaan-auto-kwijt/). "You simply take away the toy from that kind of profile when they refuse to change their behaviour."

The measure has a notable gap: confiscation is only possible when the vehicle is owned by the person who violated the ban. Raskin said he is working quietly on a similar proposal to also catch drivers using company or leasing cars.

## Payroll withholding adjusted — net wages may rise or fall

The most far-reaching measure for workers is a recalculation of the payroll withholding tax (bedrijfsvoorheffing), the monthly advance on personal income tax withheld by employers. It is a flagship of the "Arizona" coalition government, rolled out step by step, and November marks the first visible tranche.

The tax-free allowance rises for all workers, worth an average of 8.47 euros net per month according to HR provider Liantis. But the marital quotient — a benefit for couples where one partner has little or no income — is being phased down, with its maximum falling from 13,790 to 11,780 euros in 2026, hitting the higher-earning partner. For parents, a first child brings roughly 5 euros extra net per month, but from a second child the effect turns negative: about 1 euro less for two children, and up to 18 euros less for five. Pensioners and those in the SWT early-exit system see no change from November.

## Midwives may charge a co-payment

From November, midwives may charge a personal share (remgeld) for certain reimbursed services: up to 2.50 euros for postnatal care in the first five days after birth, and up to 4.25 euros for prenatal care and postnatal care from the sixth day. People with increased reimbursement pay less, and no co-payment may be charged for injections, deliveries or care during labour. Charging is optional, not mandatory. The RIZIV says the measure raises midwives' fees for pre- and postnatal care by 7.13 to 9.13 percent and is budget-neutral, with the funds reinvested in the sector.

## Energy discount for big industry

Energy-intensive companies get a long-requested discount on their electricity bill. About 50 large power consumers — among them BASF and Nyrstar — will pay 50 to 70 percent less in network tariffs from November and through 2027, at a cost of 130 to 160 million euros across the two years. To shield households and small firms, the government is lowering excise duties on electricity and drawing on grid operator Elia's tariff balances.

"From 1 November this norm finally becomes reality," Energy Minister Mathieu Bihet (MR) said. "Our industry is under pressure. This measure must strengthen its competitiveness, support investment and help guide the electrification of society." The new tariffs still required final approval from energy regulator CREG.

## Wallonia eases burial rules; electric vans get more leeway

In Wallonia, it becomes possible to be buried in a biodegradable body bag — in full ground only, not in a crypt — or with the ashes of a cremated pet, bringing the region in line with Flanders. Separately, the maximum permitted mass of electric and hydrogen delivery vans rises to 4.25 tonnes, so that their heavier batteries do not eat into cargo capacity and so they are not caught by 3.5-tonne traffic restrictions. A standard category B licence still suffices to drive them.

## What to watch

Taken together, the measures show a government using fiscal and regulatory levers across several fronts at once — squeezing cheap imports, hardening road-safety enforcement, reshaping take-home pay and easing industrial energy costs. The open questions are whether platforms absorb or pass on the new parcel fee, whether Raskin's follow-up law closes the company-car loophole, and whether the energy discount delivers the investment the minister promises. For households, the practical message is simple: check your November payslip, and expect a little more at checkout on the next parcel from abroad.
