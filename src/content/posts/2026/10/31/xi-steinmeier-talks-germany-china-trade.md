# Xi and Steinmeier Talk Trade as Germany Presses Beijing

Chinese President Xi Jinping held formal talks with German Federal President Frank-Walter Steinmeier at the Great Hall of the People in Beijing on the afternoon of 30 October, capping a six-day state visit that both sides used to project partnership even as Berlin pressed for fairer terms of trade, according to [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/politics/leaders/20261030/e4538b78cb15469cbadeb768812118ee/c.html).

The meeting came at a delicate moment for the two export giants. Xi framed the relationship as stable and open, while Steinmeier used the visit to push Beijing to reduce the large trade surplus it runs with Germany — a gap that has become a central irritant in Europe's largest economy.

## A Visit Eight Years in the Making

Steinmeier's trip, which ran from 26 to 31 October, was the first state visit by a German head of state to China in almost eight years, a gap that reflected how far relations had cooled. The visit also fell just ahead of the 55th anniversary of China-Germany diplomatic ties, established in 1972, a milestone Xi referenced as a point of pride.

Xi noted that China and Germany are the world's second- and third-largest economies and called for what he described as a "new paradigm of equal, mutually beneficial and strategically stable cooperation." Invoking a classical phrase, he said the "value of governance lies in consistency," presenting China's Germany policy as "consistent, stable and predictable."

## Two Readings of One Meeting

The official Chinese readout and German coverage of the talks tell subtly different stories, a divergence that underscores the sensitivity of the moment.

In the account carried by China's Ministry of Foreign Affairs, Xi welcomed German companies to "deeply cultivate" the Chinese market and share the benefits of its "super-large market," while asking Berlin to provide a "fair, transparent and non-discriminatory" environment for Chinese firms. He proposed deeper cooperation in artificial intelligence, green and low-carbon technology, services trade and the digital economy to "enhance future competitiveness."

Steinmeier, for his part, affirmed that "Germany firmly pursues the one-China policy" and voiced confidence in the relationship's prospects, calling for deeper cooperation in trade, science, AI and environmental protection. The two sides, he said, could resolve their differences through dialogue for "mutual benefit and win-win."

But German-language reporting foregrounded a blunter message. According to [Deutschlandfunk](https://www.deutschlandfunk.de/bundespraesident-steinmeier-draengt-bei-treffen-mit-xi-auf-abbau-des-handelsueberschusses-100.html), Steinmeier pressed Xi to reduce Germany's high trade deficit with China, arguing that the "framework conditions must be reviewed again and again" so that economic ties bring "sustainable benefit to both sides."

Germany runs a substantial trade deficit with China, and Brussels has moved to curb imports of Chinese plug-in hybrid vehicles while Beijing has restricted exports of rare earths — moves that have alarmed German industry.

## A Broader Balancing Act

Steinmeier's visit unfolded alongside a separate Indo-Pacific trip by Chancellor Friedrich Merz, who was traveling through Japan, Singapore and South Korea with a large business delegation. Analysts quoted by [Deutsche Welle](https://www.dw.com/de/steinmeiers-staatsbesuch-in-china-balanceakt-in-heiklen-zeiten/a-79759624) described the twin trips as "a very well-coordinated message" that Germany remains committed to partnership with China while pursuing a policy of "de-risking" — a difficult balancing act.

The German president's own evolution mirrored that shift. Steinmeier was once a prominent advocate of "change through trade," the doctrine that economic engagement would gradually liberalize China. On this visit, he conveyed the tougher European stance, delivering a candid speech at Peking University in which he criticized China's course and urged it to shoulder more responsibility as a world power.

Strategic undercurrents were never far from the surface. Steinmeier said the "international order is under pressure, and the world expects China to play a greater role," urging joint efforts to defend the UN Charter, support free trade and WTO rules, and "oppose protectionism and oppose trade wars" — a formulation that also applied pressure on Russia's war in Ukraine and, by extension, on Beijing's alignment with Moscow.

## What to Watch

Xi used the occasion to brief Steinmeier on the ruling Communist Party's recent Fifth Plenum, an unusual overlap that placed a foreign head of state inside a week of major Party business — a sign, analysts say, of Beijing's wish to present a stable and self-assured image to a key European partner.

The relationship now hinges on whether words translate into movement on the trade imbalance and on export controls that have unsettled German manufacturers. For Berlin, the test is whether its dual strategy of dialogue and de-risking can hold as economic friction with China deepens. For Beijing, the visit offered a chance to cast itself as an open partner at a time when Europe's patience is wearing thin.

What neither side resolved is the underlying question: whether two of the world's great exporting economies can rebalance their relationship without a sharper turn toward protectionism.
