# Firework Bomb Blows Door Off Home; Car Burned in Limburg

A firework bomb exploded in front of a home in Mechelen-aan-de-Maas, a district of the Belgian municipality of Maasmechelen, blowing the front door off the house, Belgian public broadcaster [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/10/31/maasmechelen-vuurwerkbom-mechelen-aan-de-maas/) reported. No one was injured. Hours later, a car burned out in a nearby residential district, and police are investigating whether the two incidents are connected.

## The Explosion

Residents were startled by a loud bang at about 9:30 p.m. Friday in the Neerveldestraat, local police reported. The device turned out to be a firework bomb, a heavy commercial firework repurposed as an improvised explosive. On the house that was targeted, the front door was blown outward.

One resident, visibly shaken, described the moment in the VRT NWS report. "I was so scared," the resident said. "We were in the living room when the explosion happened. They set something off at our front door, which sent the whole door flying, damaged our inner wall and also shifted the steps of the staircase. The car was damaged too."

The local police cordoned off the area to carry out examinations, and the Maasmechelen fire station of the Oost-Limburg emergency services zone closed the damaged door with wooden boards. Both the judicial forensic laboratory and the army bomb disposal unit DOVO attended the scene, according to the Limburg prosecutor's office.

"The police have launched an investigation into the circumstances of the explosion and into the exact material used to cause it," said Anna Degraen, a spokesperson for the Limburg prosecutor. "Who is behind the bomb is under investigation."

## Car Fire in Nearby District

Shortly before 8 a.m. Saturday, the neighbourhood was rattled again, this time by a burning vehicle in the Vrijhei social housing district, a few streets from the Neerveldestraat. The fire was first reported by the newspaper Het Belang van Limburg and confirmed by police. A Fiat 500 was completely destroyed.

Police zone Lanaken-Maasmechelen carried out initial examinations. "All lines of inquiry are being investigated, including arson," a police statement said. "We currently have no indication that there is a link between the two events, but that is being investigated as well. The laboratory is still due on site."

The prosecutor's office, for its part, already sees no connection between the two incidents. "Presumably this was an accidental fire, but all lines of inquiry must be examined," Degraen said.

## Firework Bombs and Their Appeal to Criminals

Firework bombs, known in Dutch as "vuurwerkbommen," have become a recurring tool in Belgian feuds, intimidation, and drug-related attacks. Because commercial fireworks are relatively easy to obtain, a heavy firework can be readily adapted into a weapon that generates a large blast with little preparation. The practice has fuelled recurring debate about restricting fireworks sales, a discussion that tends to resurface around the fireworks season at New Year.

In this case, investigators have disclosed little about motive or suspects, and it is not clear whom the device targeted beyond the home itself. The presence of the forensic laboratory and the bomb disposal unit points to a serious investigation, and the prosecutor's confirmation that the question of who is behind the bomb remains open suggests the case is still at an early stage.

## What's Next

Investigators are working on two tracks: the material and method behind the firework bomb, and the cause of the car fire. The key question for residents is whether the two are connected. Police say they have found no evidence of a link, and the prosecutor already leans against one, but neither has ruled it out while forensic work continues.

Until those results come in, the neighbourhood is left with the unsettling reality that, for now, no one has publicly been identified or arrested over the explosion, and the firework bomb that shattered a quiet Friday evening may not be resolved quickly.
