# Court Overturns Inner Mongolia Village Land Rights Ruling

A court in Chifeng, Inner Mongolia, has revoked a government land-rights confirmation that had awarded the vast majority of a 1,095.1-hectare tract to one of two feuding villages, pushing a dispute spanning more than six decades back to the starting line of the administrative process.

According to [The Paper](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_34375104), the Chifeng Intermediate People's Court issued a first-instance judgment in recent days striking down both the Balin Left Banner government's land-rights handling decision and the Chifeng city government's administrative reconsideration decision that had upheld it. The court ordered the Balin Left Banner government to issue a new confirmation decision within the statutory period.

## A Sixty-Year Dispute

The contested land, known as "Dabeichagou," lies at the boundary of Henghezi Village and Wulan Daba Village in Fuhe Town, Balin Left Banner. The two are adjacent administrative villages under the same township government.

Speaking with The Paper in a [prior in-depth report](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_31930499), Ba Fu, 73, a former secretary of Henghezi's Xiaduan old brigade, described the dispute as generational. "From the previous generation's dispute to now — I'm 73 this year, already the second generation. Now we're waiting for the problem to be solved."

Ba Fu traced the origin to around 1961, when, he said, Wulan Daba's pasture was tight and Henghezi lent Dabeichagou for grazing. Wang Sen, a former Wulan Daba village secretary, disputed that account, telling The Paper: "In my impression, Dabeichagou has always belonged to Wulan Daba Village and has long been substantially used by it."

## The Procedural Path

The case moved into formal legal channels only recently. In June 2024, both villages applied to the banner government for rights confirmation. In September 2024, the first confirmation awarded 1,092.54 hectares to Wulan Daba and just 2.57 hectares to Henghezi.

Henghezi challenged the result, and in January 2025 the Chifeng city government revoked it, finding the facts unclear and the decision clearly improper. A second confirmation in September 2025 was essentially unchanged, awarding 1,094.596 hectares to Wulan Daba and 0.504 hectares to Henghezi. On April 10, 2026, the city government upheld that decision on reconsideration. On May 6, 2026, Henghezi's Xiaduan villagers' group filed suit, and the Chifeng Intermediate Court accepted the case.

## Why the Court Ruled as It Did

The central factual question — who has actually possessed and used the land over the long term — is precisely the finding the court overturned.

Per the Second National Land Survey cited in the judgment, the bulk of the disputed tract is grassland: about 788 hectares of other grassland, 3.8 hectares of natural pasture and 0.5 hectares of planted pasture, alongside about 196 hectares of forested land, 92 hectares of shrubland and 14 hectares of dry farmland.

The court reasoned that because most of the land is grassland rather than the forest land covered by forestry-rights certificates held by Wulan Daba villagers, a finding of actual possession and use should account for land type and actual management. The banner government, the court wrote, had found that most of the disputed land was actually possessed and used by Wulan Daba "based on forestry-rights registration processed under forest-land contracts" — a conclusion it deemed "evidence-insufficient."

The court also faulted the banner government for failing to examine whether evidence submitted by the plaintiff was relevant or probative. That evidence included a Balin Left Banner administrative boundary map, a cultural-relic safety-responsibility public notice board, an August 5, 1983 boundary-line agreement between two former communes' brigades, and two collective land-ownership certificates that had already been revoked for illegal boundary-demarcation procedures.

## Questions Over Ownership Records

The Paper's earlier reporting detailed irregularities in the documentary record. Henghezi said it never received or knew of a collective land-ownership certificate purporting to date from 2012 that placed the disputed land under Wulan Daba. A handwriting and fingerprint appraisal later found that, of four boundary-point designators, only one signature was genuine; the others were not their own. In May 2025, the banner government revoked the registration of the contested plot in Wulan Daba's collective ownership certificate.

Qiao Dongdong, a lawyer at Beijing Huacheng Law Firm representing Henghezi's Xiaduan group, argued that because collective land-ownership certificates already existed from 2012, the banner government's re-confirmation was procedurally unlawful.

Henghezi has also pointed to a 2007 Balin Left Banner map, compiled by the banner government and archived by the Civil Affairs Bureau, that places all of Dabeichagou within Henghezi's administrative jurisdiction, as well as historical maps from 1935, 1958, 1987 and 2020 consistently showing the tract within Henghezi's village boundary.

## What's Next

The judgment returns the matter to the Balin Left Banner government, which must now re-issue a confirmation within the statutory period — a process that, given the record, will require the government to weigh competing historical maps, forestry registrations, boundary agreements and land-type data anew.

Zuo Xianglong, a deputy banner-head of Balin Left Banner, told The Paper the dispute has lasted many years and is complex, suggesting a face-to-face discussion rather than a phone response. For the villagers, the ruling offers procedural relief but not resolution. Whether a re-confirmation can produce a durable answer — or simply extend a contest that has already outlasted two generations — remains the open question.
