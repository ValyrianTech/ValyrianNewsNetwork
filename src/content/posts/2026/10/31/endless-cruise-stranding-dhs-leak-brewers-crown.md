# A Cursed Cruise, a Data Leak and a Brewers Crown

It was a week that swung between triumph and disaster. A residential "endless cruise" marketed as a retirement at sea collapsed off Cambodia, stranding more than 200 passengers, while a sprawling data leak exposed the personal details of tens of thousands of U.S. Homeland Security employees — and, on a lighter note, the Milwaukee Brewers finally won their first World Series. The roundup, drawn from [NBC News' weekly news quiz](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/news-quiz/super-bowl-halftime-show-news-quiz-rcna606368), captures a week's worth of headlines, from the heartbreaking to the celebratory.

## A Dream Voyage That Ran Aground

The week's most dramatic story was the unraveling of the MV Odyssey, a 642-foot former Fred Olsen liner marketed by Florida-based Villa Vie Residences as a four-year, never-ending cruise. A "skip forward" package built around a U.S. presidential term cost $319,998 per couple, plus monthly fees. As [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/news/ng-interactive/2026/oct/31/mv-odyssey-cruise-passengers-nightmare-stranded-cambodia) reported, the dream ended abruptly in late October when more than 200 residents — most of them American — were given roughly seven hours' notice to disembark in Sihanoukville, Cambodia.

The company blamed soaring fuel costs tied to the oil-price shock from the U.S.–Iran conflict, saying monthly fuel costs per cabin in parts of Asia had risen from about $1,800 to as much as $8,000. "Nobody that's tried residential cruising has succeeded and we have gone further than anyone else," Villa Vie founder Mikael Petterson said, adding that the operation would have been "absolutely fine" were it not for the war.

For passengers, the collapse was both financial and emotional. "People were shell-shocked, numb, crying... People were angry. They were hysterical. They were sad," said Kit Cassingham, who had spent two years aboard with her husband Randy. Warning signs had mounted for months — deteriorating food, fewer shore excursions, and a stop in Singapore where residents were told the company lacked funds and some contributed their own money to keep the ship moving.

The Odyssey's fate echoes earlier failures in the niche. A 2023 three-year cruise by Miray Cruises was cancelled weeks before departure when its economics failed to add up. Industry analyst Prof. Andrew Coggins noted residential cruising remains a tiny niche in an otherwise booming industry that carried 37.2 million passengers globally in 2025. The Cassinghams struck a philosophical tone despite their losses: "Live your dreams. Go for the gusto. Live life while you can."

## A Doxxing Spree at Homeland Security

On Oct. 28, the transparency nonprofit Distributed Denial of Secrets published data on more than 41,000 Department of Homeland Security employees and contractors — an "unprecedented" leak attributed to an entity calling itself "the Department of Peace." According to [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/oct/28/homeland-security-data-leak), the trove included names, phone numbers, email addresses, job titles, and details on special operations, surveillance and undercover work.

The group said it acted in opposition to immigration enforcement, declaring that "DHS employees have our information, so it's only fair that we have theirs." DDoSecrets co-founder Emma Best said the data was harvested from DHS's Okta sign-on service, though Okta denied any breach of its systems. Reuters could not fully verify the trove but matched personal details of several DHS employees to credit bureau records and confirmed some phone numbers directly.

It was the third major U.S. government data leak in a month, following an FBI data theft claimed by ShinyHunters and a Defense Department breach exposing data on millions of military personnel. DHS did not immediately comment.

## Brewers End a 57-Year Wait

Against that backdrop, baseball delivered a genuine feel-good moment. The Milwaukee Brewers won the 2026 World Series — the 122nd edition — defeating the Tampa Bay Rays 4–1 in Game 5 on Wednesday, capturing the franchise's first championship in its 57-year history and ending one of the longest title droughts in Major League Baseball. Standout contributors included Jackson Chourio, Christian Yelich and rookie sensation Jacob Misiorowski.

## Also This Week

Elsewhere, Jay-Z was announced as the headliner for the Apple Music Super Bowl LXI Halftime Show at SoFi Stadium on Feb. 14, 2027 — the eighth halftime show produced by his Roc Nation. And DoorDash agreed to acquire grocery-delivery company Instacart in a deal valued at roughly $17 billion, its largest acquisition to date, expected to close in late 2027 or early 2028 pending regulatory approval.

## What to Watch

Villa Vie has said it is evaluating "restructuring and recapitalisation" over the next 60 days, leaving passengers — and stranded crew, for whom residents passed a collection jar — waiting to learn what, if anything, they will recover. The DHS leak, meanwhile, raises fresh questions about the security of federal systems after a month of high-profile breaches. And for Milwaukee, the question is simpler: whether a long-awaited title is the start of a new era or a one-off celebration.
