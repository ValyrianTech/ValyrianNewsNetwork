# Oregon Couple Says ICE Officer Slashed Their Tire

A married couple in Hillsboro, Oregon, says a masked Immigration and Customs Enforcement officer chased them down and stabbed a knife into their rear tire twice after they filmed agents conducting public enforcement activity. The encounter, captured on the couple's own video and released by the American Civil Liberties Union on October 29, has prompted an administrative complaint with the Department of Homeland Security, according to [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/us-news/video-shows-ice-officer-running-puncturing-couples-tires-said-was-terr-rcna606629).

The couple, identified only as A.P. and M.P. in the complaint, say the October 7 incident left them terrified and reflects a broader pattern of federal agents retaliating against people who document their operations in public.

## How the Encounter Unfolded

According to the ACLU's account, the couple was driving on the morning of October 7 when they spotted an ICE SUV and began following it, part of what they describe as routine monitoring of immigration enforcement in their community. The SUV pulled into a supermarket parking lot where two other ICE vehicles were parked.

The couple lowered their windows, shouted "This is ICE! This is ICE!", blew whistles, and honked their horn. One agent grabbed a shopping cart and approached the car; the couple say they heard him say something to the effect of, "Let's block them in with the cart."

As the couple drove away, several agents gave chase on foot. Three ICE vehicles pursued their car through the streets, driving aggressively and at times nearly colliding with them, according to the complaint, without initially activating lights or sirens. The chase continued through several streets and an empty parking lot before the ICE vehicles turned on their emergency lights and sirens. The couple continued driving for about a minute before stopping at a traffic light.

It was then, the video shows, that a masked agent stepped out of his vehicle, walked to the couple's car, and punctured a rear tire with a knife before returning to his car. When the couple's car crept forward into a turn lane, the same agent got out again and stabbed the tire a second time. All three ICE vehicles then switched off their lights and sirens and drove away, without arresting or speaking to the couple.

[ACLU of Oregon](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DARTK_rr91Y) published footage of the episode.

## 'What Happened to Us Is Not Unique'

The couple framed their decision to come forward as an act of accountability. "What happened to us is not unique, and that is what is so terrifying," they said in a statement released through the ACLU. "We observe and record public ICE activity in order to shine a light on their abusive behavior and to create a record of what our government is up to."

They added that the agents are "terrorizing us and anyone else who dares to exercise their First Amendment right to hold our government accountable."

Scarlet Kim, a senior staff attorney with the ACLU's Speech, Privacy, and Technology Project, said the right to record police in public is a cornerstone of democratic oversight. "If we cannot see and document what our own government is doing on our streets, we cannot hold it accountable for running roughshod over our rights," she said.

The complaint, filed with the DHS Office of Inspector General and two other DHS oversight offices, asks that the agents be identified, investigated, and disciplined, and calls for a wider inquiry into what the ACLU describes as a pattern of retaliation against legal observers. The [administrative complaint](https://www.aclu.org/documents/10-29-26-administrative-complaint-to-dhs) itself notes that people around the country have reported being tailed, boxed in, and threatened after filming ICE agents at work.

## DHS Says Incident Under Investigation

DHS's Office of Public Affairs said in a statement that the matter is "currently under investigation," while defending the agency's conduct more broadly.

"ICE law enforcement officers are trained to use the minimum amount of force necessary to resolve dangerous situations," a spokesperson said, adding that agents are "held to the highest standards of professionalism, integrity, and ethical conduct." The department declined further comment.

The episode fits into a documented pattern cited in the complaint. In October 2025, ICE agents in the Portland area broke the windows of a U.S. citizen's car as she filmed them, dragged her out, and held her for roughly seven hours before releasing her without charges. The ACLU of Tennessee, meanwhile, filed a class-action lawsuit in May 2026 accusing a Memphis federal task force of harassing legal observers. And every federal appeals court to weigh the issue — nine so far — has upheld the right to record law enforcement activity in public, according to the complaint.

## What to Watch

Whether the inspector general's review produces any findings or discipline remains an open question, and ICE has not publicly identified the agents involved. The case also arrives amid heightened tension over immigration enforcement in the Portland area, where the ACLU of Oregon has separately won an emergency order barring DHS officers from retaliating against protesters outside a Portland ICE facility.

For now, the video has become the centerpiece of the couple's complaint — and a test of how federal oversight offices handle allegations that agents used force against people whose only offense, they say, was watching.
