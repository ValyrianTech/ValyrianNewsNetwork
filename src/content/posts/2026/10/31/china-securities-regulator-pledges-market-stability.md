# China Securities Regulator Pledges Market Stability

China's top securities regulator pledged to "make every effort to stabilize the market and expectations" and to "continuously enhance the resilience and confidence of the capital market," according to a readout of a Party Committee meeting published by state media outlet [The Paper](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_34369285). The meeting, chaired on October 30 by China Securities Regulatory Commission (CSRC) Chairman Wu Qing, came one day after the close of the 20th Communist Party of China (CPC) Central Committee's Fifth Plenum.

While the headline messaging emphasized market stability, the substance of the meeting was primarily a Party-discipline study session tied to the Plenum's central focus on full and rigorous Party governance.

## Context: A Plenum Centered on Party Discipline

The Fifth Plenum, held in Beijing from October 26 to 29, adopted a "Decision on Persistently Advancing Full and Rigorous Party Governance," according to the official communiqué published by [gov.cn and Xinhua](https://www.gov.cn/yaowen/liebiao/202610/content_7084479.htm). The document, rather than an economic five-year plan, was the gathering's principal output, making the CSRC's governance-heavy agenda a direct follow-through on the Plenum's priorities.

The communiqué also set out economic guidance, urging officials to "seek progress while maintaining stability," fully utilize macro policy, tap domestic demand, and "achieve a good start for the 15th Five-Year Plan" in 2026.

## Key Developments

The CSRC meeting laid out five governance priorities, each framed around stricter discipline (从严). Among them, it called for tightening oversight of the "key few" — leadership teams — and for a deepening of anti-corruption work across the commission.

Most notably, the readout stated that the CSRC must "deeply reflect on the lessons of the Yi Huiman, Wang Huimin, Wang Jianjun, and Fang Xinghai cases" and "use cases to promote reform and governance." Yi Huiman, the immediately prior CSRC chairman, was expelled from the Party and dismissed from public office, the Plenum communiqué confirmed, listing his serious disciplinary and legal violations among the cases formally endorsed by the Central Committee.

The market-stabilization instruction appeared in the meeting's operational closing paragraph, where the CSRC also called for "deepening comprehensive investment and financing reform" and "improving the full-chain regulatory system."

## Analysis: A Signal of Continuity, Not a Rescue

The meeting's dual messaging — anti-corruption discipline alongside market stability — reflects a broader effort to align regulatory loyalty with market-policy implementation. By naming its own former chairman among the case lessons, the CSRC underscored that discipline within financial regulators is now central to how capital-market priorities are enforced.

The timing is significant. With 2026 designated the opening year of the 15th Five-Year Plan and the 21st CPC National Congress scheduled for the second half of 2027, both market and economic stability carry heightened political importance. The CSRC's stability-first posture also builds on earlier 2026 signals, including Wu Qing's "stability first" messaging at the commission's annual work conference in January.

Still, the readout announced no new concrete stimulus, liquidity, or trading measures. Analysts should treat the meeting as a reiteration of established priorities rather than a standalone market-rescue package.

## What's Next

The key question is whether the renewed emphasis on "stabilizing expectations" will translate into tangible policy action in the coming weeks — such as fresh investment-financing reforms or regulatory adjustments — or remain primarily a governance-driven signal. Investors and observers will watch how the CSRC operationalizes its "full-chain regulatory" agenda as the 15th Five-Year Plan period begins.
