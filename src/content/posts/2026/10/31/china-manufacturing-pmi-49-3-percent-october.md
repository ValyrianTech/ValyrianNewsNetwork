# China Manufacturing PMI Falls to 49.3% in October

China's official manufacturing Purchasing Managers' Index (PMI) fell to 49.3% in October 2026, down 0.8 percentage points from September's 50.1% and back below the 50-point mark that separates expansion from contraction, according to data released jointly by the [National Bureau of Statistics](https://finance.sina.com.cn/stock/roll/2026-10-31/doc-inixaray1747098.shtml) (NBS) Service Industry Survey Center and the China Federation of Logistics & Purchasing (CFLP) on October 31. The reading signals that the world's second-largest economy began the fourth quarter with manufacturing activity losing momentum.

A PMI reading above 50% indicates month-on-month expansion; below 50% reflects contraction. The October figure marks a reversal from September, when the index had edged back into expansion territory.

## Seasonal Pressures Drive the Pullback

Officials and analysts attributed the decline primarily to seasonal factors rather than structural deterioration. According to [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/fortune/20261031/2653616255d249e3807be987c25b8e7b/c.html), the drop was influenced by "some demand being released early before the National Day holiday and the complex and volatile international environment."

The October 1–7 "Golden Week" holiday had drawn forward consumer demand into late September, while a post-holiday consumption lull followed. Overseas orders that had been bunched into the prior month also pulled back in October, and basic raw-material industries entered their customary off-season.

"The consumption peak from the National Day holiday eased after the holiday, traders' demand for related products slowed, and basic raw-material industries entered a transition to the off-season in October," said Wen Tao, an analyst at the China Logistics Information Center, in the [CFLP analysis](https://www.chinawuliu.com.cn/lhhzq/202610/31/669400.shtml). "But new-momentum market demand continued to expand."

## Sub-Indices Show Broad Cooling

The headline index was softened by weakness across most of its five components. The production index fell to 49.8%, down 1.9 percentage points, indicating slower production activity. The new orders index dropped 1.1 points to 49.4%, reflecting cooler market demand. The raw-material inventory index edged up 0.2 points to 48.4%, while the employment index held flat at 48.4%. The supplier delivery time index, a counter-cyclical component, rose slightly to 50.2%.

The new export orders index fell to 48.7%, down 1.3 points from September, highlighting softer external demand as year-end holiday orders were front-loaded into the previous month.

By enterprise size, the divergence was pronounced. Large enterprises remained in expansion at 50.2% (down 0.4 points), while medium enterprises fell to 48.4% (down 1.3 points) and small enterprises slipped to 47.5% (down 1.4 points).

## New-Momentum Sectors Provide a Cushion

Despite the headline contraction, high-tech and equipment manufacturing continued to expand, underscoring Beijing's emphasis on what it calls "new quality productive forces" as a growth stabilizer. High-tech manufacturing PMI stood at 51.3%, and equipment manufacturing registered 50.6% — both above the 50-point threshold.

The new orders index for equipment manufacturing rose 1 percentage point to above 51%, driven partly by physical work volumes linked to the "six networks" infrastructure initiative, according to the CFLP analysis. Production indices for both equipment and high-tech manufacturing have remained in expansion for 27 consecutive months.

Sector performance varied sharply. Railway, shipbuilding and aerospace equipment, and computer, communications and electronics sectors saw both production and new orders at 53.0% or above. By contrast, textiles, non-metallic mineral products, and non-ferrous metal smelting and rolling posted both indices below the critical threshold, reflecting weakness on both the supply and demand sides.

## Prices Ease but Remain Elevated

Inflation pressures in the manufacturing pipeline moderated. The main raw-material purchase price index fell 4.0 points to 56.8%, and the factory-gate (ex-factory) price index dropped 2.3 points to 51.7%, according to NBS Chief Statistician Huo Lihui. Both remain in expansion territory, meaning prices are still rising, but at a slower pace — influenced by falling prices for some non-ferrous metals and other commodities.

Still, as [CCTV](https://news.cctv.com/2026/10/31/ARTI0kPH6WKPMaLhaexceg8J261031.shtml) noted, both the purchase and ex-factory price indices remain more than 4 percentage points above year-earlier levels, keeping industrial prices at a relatively high level. Sectors tied to rising international crude prices — oil and coal processing, and chemical fiber and rubber and plastics — kept both price indices above 60.0%.

## Broader Activity Also Cools

The weakness extended beyond manufacturing. The non-manufacturing business activity index fell 0.5 points to 49.7%, dipping below the threshold. Construction activity dropped sharply, with its business activity index down 2.3 points to 48.0% and new orders plunging 5.7 points to 40.0%. The services business activity index held at the 50.0% break-even line.

The composite PMI output index, which blends manufacturing production and non-manufacturing business activity, fell 1.0 point to 49.7%, indicating overall business activity slowed.

## What to Watch

The mixed picture leaves China's policy outlook finely balanced. The October decline appears largely seasonal — officials repeatedly described it as a "phased change" — and the CFLP projects that manufacturing has a stable foundation entering November.

Wen Tao cited three supports for the coming month: concentrated issuance of special-purpose local government bonds that could accelerate physical work volumes and lift demand for equipment and steel; e-commerce promotional campaigns that may spur a consumption peak; and the rapid expansion of artificial intelligence, which continues to drive demand for related products.

Nonetheless, as temperatures fall and some industries enter seasonal downturns, the room for a broad manufacturing rebound may be limited. The business activity expectations index for production stood at 53.4% in October, down 0.4 points but above the threshold for a ninth straight month — a sign that firms remain cautiously optimistic. Whether that optimism translates into a return above 50% in November will be the key test of whether October was merely a seasonal dip or the start of a deeper slowdown.
