# China to Send Three Astronauts to the Moon; Two to Land

China's first crewed lunar exploration mission is planned to carry a crew of three astronauts, two of whom will descend to the lunar surface, a senior space official disclosed on Saturday. The mission will rely on a "two launches, one moon landing" architecture and aim to put Chinese astronauts on the Moon before 2030, [The Paper](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_34375545) reported, citing a dispatch from the Global Times.

The plan was detailed by Wang Xiang, deputy chief designer of China's Manned Space Engineering program and chief designer of the lunar exploration project, at the 7th Manned Space Academic Conference held at Beihang University in Beijing.

## A Lunar Orbit Rendezvous Approach

According to The Paper, the mission will use a lunar orbit rendezvous and docking flight mode, a technique that splits the payload across two separate launches rather than lifting everything aboard a single rocket.

Under the plan, a first Long March 10 heavy-lift rocket will send the Lanyue lunar lander toward the Moon, placing it in lunar orbit. A second Long March 10 will then launch the Mengzhou crewed spacecraft, which will rendezvous and dock with the lander. Two of the three astronauts will transfer into the lander, separate from the spacecraft, and descend to the surface to conduct scientific exploration.

After completing surface operations, the astronauts will ascend from the Moon in the lander and perform a second rendezvous and docking with the Mengzhou spacecraft in lunar orbit. All three crew members will then return to Earth aboard the spacecraft. Wang Xiang said that development and construction work across the program's lunar-phase tasks is progressing steadily and on plan.

## Why Two Rockets

The architecture mirrors the lunar orbit rendezvous concept used by NASA's Apollo program, which conserves mass compared with direct-ascent or Earth-orbit rendezvous approaches. China does not yet field a single super-heavy rocket of Saturn V scale, making the division of payload across two Long March 10 launches a pragmatic engineering choice.

The program draws on a hardware suite that includes the Long March 10 rocket, the Mengzhou crewed spacecraft, the Lanyue lander, the Wangyu lunar surface suit, and the Tansuo crewed lunar rover. In March 2025, the China Manned Space Engineering Office said these elements were in prototype development. A key milestone followed in February 2026, when the Long March 10 rocket system completed a successful low-altitude demonstration flight test at the Wenchang Space Launch Site, described as an important phased breakthrough.

China's uncrewed lunar efforts have laid groundwork for crewed operations: the Chang'e series has completed multiple lunar orbital missions and sample-return flights, building experience in lunar navigation, landing, and surface operations.

## A Race Back to the Moon

The disclosure comes amid intensifying competition in human lunar exploration. The United States is pursuing its Artemis program; Artemis II, a crewed lunar flyby rather than a landing, launched in April 2026, with the first US crewed landing planned for no earlier than 2028. The precise US schedule has shifted repeatedly, while Chinese officials have consistently reiterated the pre-2030 target.

For now, major questions remain. China has not announced the identities of the astronauts selected for the mission, disclosing only the three-person composition and the plan for two to land. The exact date of the mission within the 2030 window is also unspecified.

## What to Watch

The coming years will test whether China's phased development — from prototype hardware to integrated flight tests — can hold to schedule. A successful low-altitude demonstration is a long way from a crewed lunar landing, and the two-launch rendezvous architecture introduces additional coordination challenges in lunar orbit. Still, the public articulation of a concrete crew plan marks a notable step: China is no longer describing its lunar ambitions only in general terms, but in the specific terms of who will go and how they will get there.
