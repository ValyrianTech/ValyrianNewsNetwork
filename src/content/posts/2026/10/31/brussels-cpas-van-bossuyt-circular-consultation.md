# Brussels CPAS Slam Lack of Consultation on Van Bossuyt Rules

Brussels public social welfare centres (CPAS) have sharply criticised the way a new federal circular tightening the conditions for social assistance was drafted, saying the organisations that must apply it were neither informed nor consulted. The circular, issued by Federal Minister of Social Integration Anneleen Van Bossuyt (N-VA), sets out concrete criteria for judging whether a person receiving the integration income is sufficiently "willing to work" — and warns that those judged insufficiently willing could see their support refused or withdrawn.

Sébastien Lepoivre, president of the Federation of Brussels CPAS, said he discovered the circular "with astonishment" ([RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/les-cpas-bruxellois-denoncent-une-absence-de-concertation-pour-la-circulaire-van-bossuyt-11808545)). He stressed that the federations of the Centres publics d'action sociale had been neither informed nor consulted on the measures.

## What the Circular Says

The minister framed the initiative in blunt terms. "We have been too lax for too long," Van Bossuyt said, arguing that "our social protection is there for those who need it. But rights imply duties. We cannot accept that people hide behind excuses not to work" ([RTL Info](https://www.rtl.be/actu/belgique/societe/nous-avons-ete-laxistes-trop-longtemps-larizona-durcit-le-ton-envers-les-cpas-et/2026-10-30/article/805015)).

According to RTL Info, the circular spells out several criteria that CPAS should weigh when assessing willingness to work. Cultural or religious reasons cannot justify refusing a job — for example, the view that women should not work. Refusing, without valid reason, an available childcare solution, an adapted working schedule, a training course or professional guidance can count as insufficient willingness. Learning the country's language is a key criterion, and recipients must show steps to improve their language skills. Health problems do not automatically mean a person cannot work; CPAS are asked to consider whether part-time or adapted work remains possible. Student beneficiaries must also remain willing to work and may be expected to be available on weekends and during holidays. CPAS, however, retain discretion to assess each case individually and take the final decision.

The "willingness to work" requirement is long established as a condition for the integration income — Belgium's social assistance safety net of last resort — but the minister intends to reassert and sharpen the framework governing how CPAS apply it.

## A Cross-Community Consensus of Criticism

The reaction has been unusually uniform across Belgium's language communities. In Brussels, Lepoivre said CPAS "continue to unambiguously share the objective of intensively supporting beneficiaries towards training and employment, where possible. But we can nevertheless remain concerned about certain orientations, which risk failing to recognise the social realities experienced by many people," he said.

He also accused the minister of stigmatising people already "highly exposed to discrimination" and the sworn social workers who support them, arguing that a modern integration policy should combat structural hiring discrimination — based on origin, supposed nationality, religion, gender or age — "with as much vigour as it encourages access to employment."

In Wallonia, Alain Vaessen, president of the Walloon CPAS federation within the Union of Cities and Municipalities of Wallonia (UVCW), voiced the same grievance. "This circular is a surprise for us," he said. "We regret not having been consulted. All the more so because it is a circular that has a lot of impact on the social frontline" ([RTL Info](https://www.rtl.be/actu/belgique/politique/les-cpas-surpris-par-la-circulaire-de-la-ministre-van-bossuyt-nous-regrettons-de/2026-10-30/article/805051)). He pointed to a clear "hardening of tone" and noted a timing oddity: the government is still working to reform the law on the individualised social integration plan (PIIS), which covers several points relating to beneficiaries' work. "So here we have a circular that in a way precedes a law whose revision is not yet complete," he said. The Walloon federation plans a first impact study, to be discussed at its steering committee on 4 November.

In Flanders, Nathalie Debast of the Association of Flemish Cities and Municipalities (VVSG) rejected the premise outright. "OCMWs know what they are doing. They have long been working on activating people," she said, adding that calling them "too lax" is "a bridge too far" ([VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/10/30/ocmw-s-verbaasd-over-omzendbrief-van-bossuyt-over-leefloners-i/)). "OCMWs are not a hammock, but a springboard. So give them more staff and resources to activate more people."

## The Broader Context

The circular lands amid significant strain on Belgium's social protection system. Roughly 100,000 people lost their unemployment benefits this year as a result of time-limiting, and 53% of them applied for an integration income from their CPAS, according to VRT NWS. At the same time, Flemish Minister of Work Zuhal Demir (N-VA) scrapped Flemish employment premiums, which Debast said "hits hard" because those premiums were intended to activate people.

Debast also singled out a specific provision as "dangerous": the instruction that CPAS take into account any suspensions in another system, such as health insurance, when assessing a client. "OCMWs exist precisely to give people a new chance and therefore assess the current situation of a client," she said. "OCMWs need a motivating minister."

The episode fits a broader pattern. It follows an earlier Van Bossuyt circular, issued in December 2025, allowing aggression against CPAS staff to lead to suspension or refusal of the integration income. Both reflect the federal "Arizona" coalition's push to toughen conditionality in social protection.

## What to Watch

The immediate test will be implementation. The minister insists CPAS retain discretion to judge cases individually, which may soften the practical effect — but the federations' concern is less about any single criterion than about the broader signal and the process behind it.

The Walloon federation's impact study, due before its steering committee on 4 November, will offer the first structured assessment of how the circular is likely to play out on the ground. And with the PIIS reform still unfinished, the circular-versus-law sequencing that Vaessen flagged may yet become a live political question. For now, the rare spectacle of French- and Dutch-speaking CPAS federations criticising a federal N-VA minister in near-identical terms suggests the government has a consultation problem on its hands as much as a policy one.
