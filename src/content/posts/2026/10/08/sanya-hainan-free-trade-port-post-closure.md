# Sanya Tests Hainan Free Trade Port's Post-Closure Model

Sanya expects to receive 996,700 tourist visits over China's combined National Day and Mid-Autumn Festival holiday, a 14 percent year-on-year increase, with total tourism revenue projected to reach 3.168 billion yuan, up 16 percent, according to [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/20261008/e404150e5efa4e67aa0a5ae828cb6678/c.html). The holiday was the first since the Hainan Free Trade Port launched full-island independent customs operations on December 18, 2025, making Sanya a test case for how the island's landmark policy shift translates into real-world economic activity.

## Why This Holiday Mattered

When Hainan began full independent customs operations last December, it marked a milestone in China's push for higher-level institutional opening-up. The customs closure created a system built around zero tariffs and low tax rates, alongside freer flows of trade, investment, cross-border capital, people, transport, and data — a framework designed to make Hainan a gateway for the wider economy.

The first national holiday after closure arrived with particular symbolic weight. Because the National Day and Mid-Autumn holidays fell together, travel cycles lengthened and long-stay residential tourism became the mainstream mode of travel. As Xinhua framed it, the convergence of Mid-Autumn family reunions, National Day atmosphere, and free-trade-port opportunity made Sanya's holiday market an important window into the industry's direction.

## A Governance Upgrade, Not Just a Holiday Push

The most notable shift, according to the report, was in how the city managed the surge. Rather than the concentrated, campaign-style measures of past holidays, Sanya folded visitor services into its everyday operating system. Seventy-four government agency and village and community parking lots opened free of charge to residents and tourists, targeting parking shortfalls near popular scenic spots, commercial districts, and rural tourism sites.

At Sanya Phoenix International Airport and Sanya Station, warm-service activities ran from September 30 to October 4. The city's Easy Travel luggage delivery program, with 232 outlets, served 5,100 person-times during the holiday, addressing a pain point for long-stay visitors carrying heavy bags.

Market oversight was stepped up in parallel. Multiple departments conducted joint inspections of seafood restaurants, marine recreation, wedding photography, and transport. Despite the higher visitor numbers, tourism-related complaints fell 17.71 percent year-on-year, with no major cases of price gouging or predatory business practices reported.

## Culture as the New Competitive Edge

Sanya is best known for its coastline, but the holiday data pointed to a broadening of demand. Visitor flows to the Luobidong National Archaeological Site Park climbed steadily, and family groups made up a significantly larger share. The park ran immersive experiences — historical lectures, rock-art rubbing, and site study tours — that draw on the area's deep timeline from prehistoric settlement through modern free-trade-port development.

Professor Zhang Shuoren of Hainan Tropical Ocean University said the shift reflects a broader evolution in Chinese vacation consumption. Coastal scenery long formed Sanya's core competitiveness, but as mass tourism upgrades, visitors are no longer satisfied with simple scenery check-ins and increasingly value the content quality and cultural substance of a trip, he said. Under the long-stay model, marine resources alone struggle to fill a stay of more than a dozen days or to build repeat-visit appeal.

## Inbound Travel Becomes a Structural Story

Perhaps the clearest signal of the free trade port's effect showed up in international arrivals. A new pattern emerged in which Russian-speaking and Central Asian visitors enter through Sanya, roam across multiple cities, and exit through Sanya again — a long-loop itinerary that replaces the older single-city, short-stay model.

The numbers behind it are substantial. Nationally, China received 22.914 million inbound foreign tourists in the first half of 2026, up 20.4 percent, with visa-free entry accounting for nearly 80 percent of the total. Sanya received 945,900 inbound tourists from January to August 2026, a 47.47 percent jump — well above the national average. To smooth the way, more than 50 key merchants in the city have updated multilingual signage, cross-border payment, and foreign-facing services under the so-called Passport Friendly plan.

Huang Xing, director of the Sanya Tourism, Culture, Radio, Television and Sports Bureau, said the holiday's strong inbound market was not merely a matter of visitor numbers but part of a shift from policy advantages toward scenario, reputation, and brand advantages.

## What to Watch Next

Vice Mayor Fan Weizheng said the city would build on the holiday's experience to push its cultural tourism industry from traffic growth toward quality growth and from resource-driven toward content-driven development, while deepening market regulation against price fraud and forced consumption.

Sanya's holiday performance fits a pattern visible since closure. In its first 100 days, Hainan recorded 16.96 billion yuan in first-line zero-tariff imports, 15.6 billion yuan in offshore duty-free shopping, and over 80 billion yuan in foreign trade, according to a [100-day review](https://xinwen.bjd.com.cn/content/s69c6a40ae4b0687a28932868.html) published by Beijing Daily. The province also added 85,000 new business entities, including 70,100 new enterprises, up nearly 40 percent.

That momentum carried into 2026's holiday seasons. Sanya's flagship Atlantis resort, which has served more than 38 million domestic and international visitors since opening in 2018, used the first Spring Festival after closure to test new marine-themed programming, positioning the free trade port's visa and investment reforms as a bridge to global tourism markets, as [China Daily](https://hain.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202602/14/WS69904f01a310942cc499fd36.html) reported.

The open question for Sanya is whether the culturally driven, internationally oriented model can be sustained once holiday demand fades. The city's own leadership frames the transition as permanent rather than seasonal — a shift from a regional hotspot to what Fan described as an internationalized, high-quality resort city.
