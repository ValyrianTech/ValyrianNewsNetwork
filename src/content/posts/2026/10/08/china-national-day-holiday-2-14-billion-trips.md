# China Logs 2.14 Billion Holiday Trips in National Day Surge

China recorded an estimated 2.144 billion cross-region passenger trips during the seven-day National Day holiday, averaging roughly 306 million trips a day and marking a 0.7% increase over the same period last year, according to Ministry of Transport data [reported by People's Daily](http://finance.people.com.cn/n1/2026/1008/c1004-40810266.html). The figures underscore the scale of domestic mobility during one of the country's busiest travel periods and offer a closely watched gauge of consumer activity.

The holiday ran from October 1 to 7. The transport system operated "generally stable and orderly" throughout, the ministry said.

## A Near-Plateau of Mobility

The modest 0.7% year-on-year gain places 2026's holiday travel at a high but largely stable level, following years of post-pandemic recovery. Highway travel remained the dominant mode by a wide margin: an estimated 1.9642 billion trips were made by road, or about 280.6 million a day, the People's Daily report said.

Within that total, commercial highway passenger volume reached 286.76 million trips, up 6.8% year-on-year. Self-driving accounted for nearly 80% of highway travel, and new energy vehicle traffic rose 35% compared with the previous year's holiday.

Rail was the standout performer in percentage terms. China Railway Group reported 152.36 million railway passenger trips over the seven days, [according to Yicai](https://www.yicai.com/news/103385322.html), while the 13.1% year-on-year increase in railway volume was documented in the People's Daily report. Railway volume exceeded 20 million passengers a day for three consecutive days, the ministry data showed.

Waterway passenger volume reached 10.99 million trips, up 7.8% — also carried in the People's Daily report — while civil aviation carried 16.95 million passengers, a daily average of about 2.42 million, roughly flat year-on-year.

## New Rail Lines, Smoother Roads

Officials attributed the strong demand in part to a continuously improving transport network. Four new high-speed rail lines opened just before the holiday, including the Xiongshang section of the Beijing-Hong Kong line and the Harbin-Yichun line, linking parts of North and Northeast China and boosting travel along their routes.

Infrastructure and management measures also eased some of the holiday's traditional pain points. The number of severe highway congestion segments — those of 10 kilometers or longer — fell 70% year-on-year, [according to the Beijing Youth Daily report](https://m.163.com/dy/article/L8MADJOS0514R9KQ.html). The car rental market was described as "very active," with daily rental rates up 25%.

## Consumption as a Barometer

Chinese authorities routinely frame cross-region passenger flow as an indicator of domestic consumption and economic vitality, and official commentary on the 2026 data followed that pattern.

"Cross-region movement of more than 300 million person-trips per day effectively unleashed residents' consumption potential, promoted the growth of both offline and online consumer services, and strengthened consumption momentum while expanding growth space," said Gao Bo, deputy director of the Ministry of Transport's transport services department.

Local governments amplified the holiday push with subsidies. Guangzhou, for instance, invested more than 600 million yuan in national and provincial subsidies covering auto trade-ins, home appliances, and digital goods during the Mid-Autumn-to-National-Day period, [according to the Guangzhou Daily](https://news.dayoo.com/guangzhou/202610/07/139995_55010363.htm).

## Sector Divergence and a Definitional Debate

The data reveal a notable divergence between rail and air travel. Rail grew at double-digit rates while civil aviation stayed flat, a contrast that analysts may read as a sign of shifting domestic travel preferences and the pull of an expanded high-speed rail network.

Public discussion also focused on methodology. Some readers questioned how "cross-region" movement is defined — whether it counts travel between provinces, cities, districts, or even neighborhoods — highlighting ongoing debate over how the headline metric is compiled and what it ultimately measures.

## What to Watch

With the 2026 National Day holiday now concluded, attention turns to whether the travel volumes translate into measurable holiday retail and tourism spending, and whether the consumption-boosting policies that accompanied the break have a lasting effect. The performance of the newly opened high-speed rail corridors will also be worth tracking as they settle into regular service.

For now, the numbers point to a Chinese public that remains highly mobile — a pattern officials are eager to present as evidence of resilience in domestic demand.
