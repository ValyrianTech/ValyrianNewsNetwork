# China Autumn Grain Harvest Tops 40 Percent Nationwide

China's autumn grain harvest has passed the 40 percent mark nationwide, the Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Affairs (MARA) reported on October 8, as the season's most important phase for the country's annual food output advances across all major growing regions. According to [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/politics/20261008/5a0e8005e4184e7f991ec658e7f31f8e/c.html), the latest agricultural situation dispatch showed the harvest had crossed 40 percent, with the Huang-Huai-Hai plain — the country's largest grain granary — past the halfway point.

The ministry's regional breakdown showed wide variation reflecting China's geography and climate: the southwest led with more than 70 percent harvested, while the mid-lower Yangtze region and the Huang-Huai-Hai plain each passed 50 percent. The northwest stood at nearly 50 percent, South China at more than 20 percent, and the northeast — which lags due to its later growing cycle — reached about 25 percent.

## A Steady, Fast-Paced Season

The October 8 milestone caps a steady progression over the first week of the month. A dispatch reported by [Jiemian News](https://www.jiemian.com/article/15159503.html) put the national figure at over 30 percent as of October 1, when the southwest was already near 70 percent and the mid-lower Yangtze at 45 percent. By October 6, the harvest had reached 35 percent, according to [Huashang Online](https://news.voc.com.cn/news/202610/33925784.html), with the southwest near 70 percent, the mid-lower Yangtze near 50 percent, and both the Huang-Huai-Hai plain and the northwest above 40 percent.

By October 8, both the Huang-Huai-Hai region and the mid-lower Yangtze had crossed the 50 percent mark, and the northeast had jumped from roughly 10 percent to 25 percent — a sharp acceleration in the country's key corn and soybean belt.

## Why Autumn Grain Matters

Autumn grain is the decisive phase of China's agricultural year. Vice Minister of Agriculture and Rural Affairs Zhang Xingwang said in July that autumn grain "is the bulk of the year's grain production, accounting for roughly three-quarters of annual output," as reported by [Economic Information Daily](https://www.jjckb.cn/20260724/eeab9567d45a43c09279d736eb410d8d/c.html).

The 2026 season follows a record summer grain harvest of 3,014.9 hundred-million *jin* (301.49 billion *jin*, or roughly 150.75 million tonnes) — the first time output has exceeded 3,000 hundred-million *jin* (300 billion *jin*). That result, achieved despite large-scale late sowing and multiple rounds of rainfall during the earlier harvest and described by the ministry as a strong opening to the year's grain production, has raised expectations for a full-year bumper harvest if the autumn season holds.

## Favorable Weather and Government Push

Unlike the rain-battered 2025 autumn harvest — when persistent September and October rains caused flooding, moldy corn, and delayed harvesting in the Huang-Huai-Hai region — early October 2026 weather has been reported as predominantly clear and favorable across northern China, conducive to the harvest.

A state-backed push has also underpinned the season. In July, MARA launched a "100-day campaign to strengthen field management, combat disasters, and secure the autumn grain harvest," deploying agronomists to key regions, promoting integrated yield-boosting techniques, and coordinating closely with meteorological, water-resource, and emergency-management departments. Authorities also designated 1,000 counties and 20 cities for large-scale per-unit yield improvement, and expanded pest and disease control for threats including rice planthoppers, rice leaf-rollers, and corn southern rust, according to the same Economic Information Daily report.

## What to Watch

The concentrated autumn harvest period is expected to run through late October. The ministry has urged regional authorities to step up late-season field management, coordinate machinery supply and demand in advance, and seize favorable weather to advance the harvest.

With autumn grain representing roughly three-quarters of national output, the pace over the coming weeks will determine whether China can consolidate the momentum from its record summer harvest and secure a full-year bumper crop. Key variables include any late-October rainfall in the Huang-Huai-Hai plain that could slow the remaining work, and the subsequent progress of winter wheat and rapeseed planting for the next season.
