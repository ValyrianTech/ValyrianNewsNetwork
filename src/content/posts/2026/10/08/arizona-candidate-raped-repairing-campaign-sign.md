# Arizona Congressional Candidate Says She Was Raped While Repairing Campaign Sign

An Arizona congressional candidate says she was knocked unconscious and raped in August while repairing a campaign sign in the middle of the night, an account that has renewed attention on the safety of candidates during the 2026 midterm campaign season.

Bernadette Greene-Placentia, the Democratic nominee for Arizona's 8th Congressional District, told [Phoenix New Times](https://www.phoenixnewtimes.com/news/congressional-candidate-is-raped-while-repairing-her-campaign-signs-40705248/) that an attacker struck her over the head in a Sun City parking lot near 99th Avenue and Bell Road just after 2 a.m. on Aug. 14. She said she blacked out and woke to find a man raping her. The Maricopa County Sheriff's Office confirmed an active investigation and said a report was filed on Aug. 31.

## What authorities say

The Maricopa County Sheriff's Office confirmed the report and said its investigation is ongoing. A spokesperson declined to comment further, saying the office cannot release the paperwork until the investigation is completed. No arrests or suspects have been publicly identified.

According to [ABC News](https://abcnews.com/Politics/arizona-congressional-candidate-raped-repair-campaign-sign/story?id=137079796), Greene-Placentia told the outlet she decided to speak publicly because she had been talking about fighting for Arizona residents on the campaign trail "and I didn't feel like I could keep saying that if I weren't also fighting for women that are victims of sexual assault."

In a statement to ABC News, a campaign spokesperson said the attack occurred around 2 a.m. and that Greene-Placentia "made the difficult decision to speak publicly about the assault because she wants other survivors to know they are not alone."

## An account marked by gaps in memory

Greene-Placentia — a 52-year-old long-haul trucker and small-business owner — described a fragmentary recollection of the attack. She recalled one attacker mumbling an insult she mentally completed as "Democratic bitch," though she said she is unsure of the exact words. A second man approached and nudged her leg apart, she said; she kicked at him before both men left her face down in the lot.

Her silver Jeep Grand Cherokee was still running with its hatch open, she told the New Times. She said she does not remember pulling up her pants, driving home, or collapsing into bed, and that she believes she may have suffered her third concussion but did not seek medical attention. According to the police report cited by the outlet, she bled vaginally for three days after the assault and her body was covered in bruises.

She initially did not want to report the attack, telling ABC15 she was "embarrassed," before eventually contacting the sheriff's office more than two weeks later.

"It's hard. It really makes me think of why other women don't come forward," Greene-Placentia told [ABC15 Arizona](https://www.abc15.com/news/region-phoenix-metro/central-phoenix/arizona-cd-8-candidate-bernadette-greene-placentia-says-she-was-sexually-assaulted-while-repairing-campaign-sign) in a sit-down interview. "I believe very strongly in women's bodily autonomy, and I was angry that these men used their physical size to take that autonomy from me. Speaking up was me taking it back."

## Reaction from her opponent

Greene-Placentia is challenging Republican incumbent Rep. Abraham Hamadeh in a district that covers a swath of the northwest Phoenix Valley, including Peoria, Surprise, and Sun City. Hamadeh, elected in 2025, is a U.S. Army intelligence officer, attorney, and former prosecutor.

Hamadeh said in a statement that he had read "the disturbing story" and called on authorities to investigate thoroughly.

"No American should become a victim of a crime in their own community," he said.

## Safety and political violence in focus

Greene-Placentia described her nighttime sign-repair routine as a practical response to Arizona's heat and traffic, often undertaken with her husband or a volunteer. She said she does not know whether she was targeted because of her campaign. "Maybe I was recognized, but I think it was probably a crime of opportunity," she told ABC15. "I just don't have a way of knowing."

The reported assault has drawn attention to safety concerns facing candidates during a contentious campaign season. State Rep. Stephanie Simacek, a friend of Greene-Placentia, told [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/oct/07/bernadette-greene-placentia-house-candidate-allegedly-raped) that the news was "a big wake-up call," adding that candidates often assume such violence "is not going to happen to us." Greene-Placentia's campaign has said it cannot afford additional security but is taking extra precautions.

## What's next

The sheriff's investigation remains open, and it is unclear whether surveillance footage from the area exists — something Hamadeh specifically urged authorities to pursue. The general election is scheduled for Nov. 3.

Greene-Placentia has said she does not want the attack to define her campaign. "I need to let women know that it's okay to talk about it," she said. "We're asking people to stand up and donate or volunteer. We're asking those folks to be uncomfortable; then I have to be willing to be uncomfortable too."
