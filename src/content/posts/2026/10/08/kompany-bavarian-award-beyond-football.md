# Kompany to Receive Bavarian Award Beyond Football

Vincent Kompany, the Belgian former national team captain and current head coach of FC Bayern Munich, will receive the Personal Prize of the Bavarian Minister-President on Saturday 10 October in Munich. Bavaria's Minister-President Markus Söder is honouring Kompany not only for his sporting success with Bayern but also for his outspoken stance against racism, [VRT NWS reported](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/10/08/vincent-kompany-krijgt-prestigieuze-onderscheiding-in-beieren/).

The award forms part of the Bayerischer Sportpreis 2026, presented at a gala at the BMW Welt in Munich. It recognises figures who make the positive impact of sport on society visible — a criterion that extends well beyond results on the pitch.

## A Prize That Goes Beyond the Sideline

According to the [Bavarian State Chancellery](https://www.bayern.de/ministerpraesident-dr-markus-soeder-verleiht-bayerischen-sportpreis-an-vincent-kompany/), the personal prize is a distinct honour within the annual ceremony, which has been awarded in various categories since 2002.

Söder described the 40-year-old as "a great coach for a great club — and a committed advocate for humanity." In the official announcement, the Minister-President credited Kompany with a clear playing philosophy, leadership strength and charisma both on and off the pitch, noting that the Belgian took charge of a world-class club at just 38.

"Under his leadership, FC Bayern Munich shines with attractive, attacking football," Söder said. He summed up the club's transformation with a pointed wordplay: "The former 'FC Hollywood' has become a successful 'FC Feelgood.'"

## Sporting Record and a Widely Noted Speech

The honour rests on two pillars. On the pitch, [BR24](https://www.br.de/nachrichten/sport/fussball-bundesliga-fc-bayern-muenchen-vincent-kompany-erhaelt-bayerischen-sportpreis-2026-von-soeder,VXBH9Uf) notes that Kompany led Bayern to two German championships and a cup win since arriving in 2024. In the most recent season, the club set a Bundesliga record with 122 league goals, alongside a record goal difference of +86.

Off the pitch, the award is explicitly linked to a nearly twelve-minute statement against racism that Kompany delivered in February 2026. The speech followed racist abuse directed at Real Madrid striker Vinícius Júnior during a Champions League match against Benfica Lisbon, and the reaction of then-Benfica coach José Mourinho, [Sport1 reported](https://www.sport1.de/news/fussball/bundesliga/2026/10/fuer-anti-rassismus-rede-soeder-ehrt-kompany).

Speaking as someone personally affected by racism, Kompany made a plea for racism to have no place in sport or society. "He has always made clear that racism must never have a place in sport or in our society," Söder said. "His clear plea for human dignity has impressed many people far beyond the world of sport."

## Why It Matters

The recognition carries weight in Bavaria's political and cultural landscape. The award is conferred by the Minister-President of the Free State of Bavaria, and Söder's remarks frame Kompany as embodying "Bavarian" values — openness, performance and internationality. "Vincent Kompany embodies this Bavarian self-image. He is a great ambassador of Bavaria in the world," Söder said.

For Belgian audiences, the honour reflects how a figure long associated with the national team and Manchester City has built a formidable reputation in Germany that reaches beyond football. As BR24 observed, Kompany brought something that had recently been rare in Munich: calm. Since his appointment, his position was never called into question.

Kompany joins an illustrious list of previous recipients of the personal prize, including Franz Beckenbauer, Karl-Heinz Rummenigge, Oliver Kahn, Ottmar Hitzfeld, Sepp Maier and Thomas Müller.

His playing career began at RSC Anderlecht before moves to Hamburger SV and Manchester City, where he won four English titles as club captain. He earned 89 caps for Belgium and finished third at the 2018 World Cup.

## What's Next

The gala takes place on Saturday, 10 October, at 19:00 at the BMW Welt in Munich, with Markus Othmer as moderator. The recorded ceremony will be broadcast on BR Fernsehen on Sunday, 11 October from 22:10. Other 2026 winners include referee Deniz Aytekin, skier Emma Aicher and footballer Nathaniel Brown.

Kompany's award cements a striking turnaround: a coach credited with restoring stability to a club once synonymous with turbulence, now recognised as much for his convictions as for his trophies.
