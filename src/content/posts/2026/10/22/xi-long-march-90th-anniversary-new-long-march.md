# Xi Marks 90 Years Since Long March, Charts 'New Long March'

President Xi Jinping led a high-profile rally at the Great Hall of the People in Beijing on Thursday morning to commemorate the 90th anniversary of the victory of the Chinese Workers' and Peasants' Red Army's Long March, delivering a keynote speech that cast China's modernization drive as a "new Long March" and warned of "perilous storms" ahead, [Xinhua reported](https://www.news.cn/politics/leaders/20261022/ea585c6f63b74a99a4bf8cba035eb796/c.html).

The event drew the full top leadership—Standing Committee members Li Qiang, Zhao Leji, Wang Huning, Cai Qi, Ding Xuexiang, and Li Xi, along with Vice President Han Zheng—and roughly 3,000 attendees, including Party, state, and military officials. Zhao Leji, chairman of the National People's Congress Standing Committee, presided over the rally.

## A Founding Epic, Revisited

Xi recounted the arc of the Long March from October 1934 to October 1936, when the Red Army's First, Second, and Fourth Front Armies and the 25th Army crossed more than ten provinces over some 25,000 li, breaking through blockades by "over a million enemy troops" and completing a strategic shift northward to resist Japanese aggression.

The speech repeatedly returned to the Zunyi Conference of January 1935, where Mao Zedong's leadership position was effectively established. According to the [full text of the speech](https://www.news.cn/politics/leaders/20261022/8771efbecd384ffba61dc44720e065d1/c.html), Xi declared that "history eloquently proves that correct Party leadership and the establishment of Comrade Mao Zedong's core position were decisive for the victory"—a framing that links the Party's founding authority to its present leadership.

Xi attributed the victory to four factors: correct Party leadership, firm revolutionary faith, independence and a fact-based approach, and the support of the masses. "Only by remembering history can we create the future," he said.

## The 'New Long March'

Central to the speech was the framing of the present era as a continuation of the revolutionary journey. "The new journey is an expedition full of glory and dreams; it is the new Long March of the Party and the people," Xi said, describing the drive to realize the second centenary goal and advance Chinese-style modernization.

To carry forward what official discourse calls the "Long March spirit," Xi set out what Zhao summarized as "five musts": upholding the Party's ideals and purpose; pursuing high-quality development and meeting the people's growing needs; courageously waging a "great struggle with new historical characteristics"; accelerating the building of a world-class military; and strengthening Party leadership and discipline.

## Security and Military Signaling

The speech carried a pointed security message. Xi spoke of a "complex international and domestic situation" and called on the Party and people to be prepared to "withstand major tests of high winds and rough seas, even perilous storms," tying development closely to national security.

He also reaffirmed absolute Party leadership over the military and the goal of building a "world-class military," tying the commemoration to the People's Liberation Army's centenary objectives—a recurring priority in the current leadership's agenda.

## A Dense Anniversary Year

The rally is the centerpiece of a broader commemorative campaign. In September, a themed exhibition, "The Great Expedition—Commemorating the 90th Anniversary of the Victory of the Chinese Workers' and Peasants' Red Army's Long March," opened at the Military Museum of the Chinese People's Revolution, with Standing Committee member Cai Qi delivering remarks, according to [12371.cn](https://www.12371.cn/2026/09/22/ARTI1790085588466695.shtml).

CCTV's coverage of Thursday's rally corroborated the attendee list and the event's basic facts, [noting](https://news.qq.com/rain/a/20261022A0433Q00) that Xi "attended the rally and delivered an important speech." The 2026 calendar also marks the 105th anniversary of the Party's founding, and the Long March theme has been woven into patriotic "red tourism" and symbolic infrastructure projects.

## Analysis: History as Legitimacy

The speech follows a familiar pattern in official discourse: mobilizing revolutionary history to reinforce present-day authority and discipline. By binding current leadership to the "correct leadership" narrative anchored at Zunyi and Mao's ascendancy, the address draws a parallel between Mao's "core position" and Xi's own status—a linkage that carries clear political weight.

Framing modernization as a "new Long March" also serves a mobilizing purpose. The language of resilience and sacrifice is well suited to a period of economic headwinds and geopolitical friction, justifying continued discipline and unity. The emphasis on "great struggle," security, and a world-class military signals that the leadership views external and internal challenges as tightly intertwined.

As is typical for domestic leadership events of this kind, coverage is tightly coordinated across state media, producing a highly controlled information environment. No independent reporting on the rally was available, and no conflicting accounts were identified.

## What's Next

The commemorations are expected to continue through the anniversary year, with the Long March spirit likely to remain a fixture in Party education and patriotic campaigns. The key question for observers is how forcefully the "new Long March" framing is translated into policy—particularly in the overlap between development goals and the security-first posture Xi outlined. For now, the message is one of continuity: the Party presents the road ahead as an extension of the road already traveled.
