# China Sets Up Industrial Software Standards Committee

China's Ministry of Industry and Information Technology (MIIT) has established a technical committee for industrial software standardization, with its inaugural meeting held recently in Hangzhou, according to [Xinhua News Agency](https://www.news.cn/politics/20261022/4c77a4c3c92f4ea882087f7407719e6a/c.html). The committee is intended to advance standards development for industrial software and support the growth and quality of China's domestic industrial software sector.

## Why Standards Matter

Industrial software refers to the tools used in industrial design, manufacturing, simulation and control -- ranging from computer-aided design and engineering platforms to product lifecycle management and manufacturing execution systems. These are widely regarded as a strategic weak point in China's industrial chain, which has historically depended heavily on foreign vendors for core design and simulation software.

Beijing has repeatedly identified domestic industrial software as a priority in its Five-Year Plans and its push for a new model of industrialization. The creation of a dedicated standardization committee signals an effort to systematize technical requirements around a domestic software ecosystem, an early institutional move in the current 15th Five-Year Plan period that began this year.

## Scope of Work

According to Xinhua's report, the committee is mainly responsible for drafting and revising industry standards in five areas: key technologies, data interoperability, product services, integrated applications and ecosystem construction. Its secretariat is hosted at the Fifth Electronics Research Institute of MIIT (CEPREI), a long-established electronics reliability and testing research institute under the ministry.

Following the inaugural meeting, the committee held its first plenary session of all members, at which it reviewed and approved its charter, the secretariat's working rules and the procedures for drafting and revising standards. The committee also set out priorities for the next phase of work.

## An Industry on the Rise

The move comes as China's software sector continues to expand. Xinhua, citing official data, reported that in the first eight months of 2026 China's software business revenue reached 10,282 billion yuan, up 8.8% year on year. Revenue from basic software products rose 9.8% over the same period, while industrial software product revenue grew 8.3%.

The reported growth in industrial software -- while solid -- trails the broader software market and the faster-growing basic software segment, underscoring the scale of the gap Beijing is seeking to close.

## Analysis: Soft Infrastructure as Industrial Policy

Standardization committees are the standard institutional vehicle in China for developing national, industry and group standards. They typically bring together representatives from government, research institutes, universities and enterprises, and are chartered to draft standards, coordinate technical requirements and align with international bodies.

Hosting the secretariat at a ministry research institute such as CEPREI matters. Standards are "soft infrastructure": an MIIT-backed committee can accelerate interoperability requirements, shape procurement specifications that favor domestic products, and coordinate the translation of innovation into market-ready applications. The committee's explicit mandate to speed the application of scientific and technological achievements points in exactly that direction.

## What's Next

With its charter and procedures now approved, the committee is positioned to begin issuing standards work in its five priority areas. How quickly those standards translate into procurement rules and interoperability requirements -- and how far they tilt the playing field toward domestic vendors -- will be the key signal to watch.

For now, the announcement is a procedural first step. But in China's policy system, procedural steps are often where industrial strategy begins.
