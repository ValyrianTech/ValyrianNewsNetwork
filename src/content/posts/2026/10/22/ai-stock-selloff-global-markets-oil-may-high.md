# AI Stock Selloff Drags Global Markets; Oil Hits May High

A sharp selloff in artificial-intelligence shares dragged stock markets lower around the world on Thursday, while oil climbed to its highest price since May as uncertainty over the war with Iran kept energy markets on edge, according to [AP News](https://apnews.com/article/stocks-markets-rates-earnings-oil-33767691a4a0730b96d6f0c5100ea9b2).

The S&P 500 fell 1% and the tech-heavy Nasdaq composite dropped 2% as of noon Eastern time, while the Dow Jones Industrial Average slipped just a modest 14 points, or less than 0.1%. The split underscored how concentrated the pain was in technology and AI names rather than the broad market.

## A Global Retreat From AI

The pullback began in Asia, where markets are heavily weighted toward technology and chip stocks. South Korea's Kospi tumbled 3.5%, Hong Kong's Hang Seng dropped 3%, and Japan's Nikkei 225 sank 2.7%.

AI-related shares have grown shaky on worries that the industry's long boom may be threatened by how expensive it has become to borrow money to build data centers and buy more AI chips. Investors also fret that prices simply ran too high during a yearslong frenzy, even as companies keep reporting growth.

Lam Research, which makes equipment used in semiconductor production, illustrated the dynamic. It reported summer profit and revenue slightly above analysts' expectations, yet its stock tumbled 10%. The company's forecast for this quarter carried a midpoint slightly below consensus, and the stock had already surged 52.4% for the year—nearly quadruple the S&P 500's gain—leaving plenty of room for disappointment.

Other big names also fell after quarterly reports that contained mixed signals. Meta Platforms lost 5.4% after revenue beat expectations but profit fell short, and the company said it is creating a new class of stock to fund AI investments and other expenses. Tesla slipped 0.8% on stronger profit but weaker-than-forecast revenue. Nvidia, the largest company on Wall Street by market value, dropped 3.7% and was the heaviest single weight on the S&P 500. CoreWeave, which borrowed heavily to buy Nvidia chips it rents out over the cloud, fell 7.8% on debt worries.

## Oil Climbs on Iran War Fears

Adding to the pressure, a barrel of Brent crude rose 2.2% to $111.19 after touching a five-month high. The benchmark sits at the upper end of the $97-to-$112 range it has traded in through October, buffeted by uncertainty over when the war with Iran will let the global energy industry return to normal.

That uncertainty sharpened Thursday, as [AP News reported](https://apnews.com/article/iran-united-states-war-hormuz-trump-israel-2abebce2fca2c085e0e0ee717e42cf28) that seven oil tankers were attacked in the Strait of Hormuz, a critical chokepoint for world oil shipments. Producers have found ways to keep crude flowing through a U.S.-protected lane, but at mounting cost in time and money.

## Bonds and Europe Add to the Strain

In Europe, France's CAC 40 fell 1.4%, one of the continent's sharpest declines. It holds fewer tech stocks than Asian or U.S. indexes but was hurt by another jump in yields on French government bonds, driven by worries over the government's large debt load and strained budget amid violent protests across the country.

Debt concerns are weighing on nearly every developed economy, including the United States, and have pushed Treasury yields to their highest levels in decades alongside stubborn inflation. Yet U.S. yields fell sharply Thursday—the 10-year Treasury yield dropped to 5.00% from 5.13% late Wednesday and from more than 5.30% earlier in October—a signal that investors were buying government bonds as a safe haven while AI stocks tumbled.

## A Resilient Undercurrent

Beneath the tech rout, the broader picture was sturdier than the headline indexes suggested. The majority of stocks within the S&P 500 actually rose on Thursday, only to be drowned out by the drops in influential tech names. A report also showed fewer U.S. workers applied for unemployment benefits last week, suggesting layoffs remain low.

Several companies topped profit expectations and helped limit the market's losses. Procter & Gamble rose 6.2% after standing by its full-year forecasts, with CEO Shailesh Jejurikar citing expected continued "geopolitical and economic challenges." Lockheed Martin and CSX also beat estimates.

## What to Watch

The day's trading captured two simultaneous headwinds: an AI valuation correction on the equity side and war-driven oil inflation on the cost side, all while borrowing costs remain historically elevated. Whether the AI selloff deepens into a broader correction—and whether oil breaks above its October range if Iran tensions escalate—will shape markets in the weeks ahead, with both the Israeli election on Oct. 27 and the U.S. midterms on Nov. 3 looming over the outlook.
