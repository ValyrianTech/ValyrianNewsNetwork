# Voters Want Congress Focused on Costs and Iran, Not Trump

WASHINGTON — Regardless of how the Nov. 3 midterm elections turn out, voters from both major parties want the next Congress to concentrate on making life more affordable and ending the war in Iran, according to a new poll from The Associated Press-NORC Center for Public Affairs Research. Far fewer want lawmakers to define their work around President Donald Trump — either by investigating him or by advancing his agenda.

About 8 in 10 registered voters, including large majorities of both Republicans and Democrats, say lowering the cost of living should be one of the highest priorities for the incoming Congress, the [AP-NORC survey](https://apnews.com/article/trump-midterms-congress-elections-iran-costs-inflation-441515a34e4b28c812f694754ac0582a) found. Roughly 7 in 10 want Congress to prioritize ending the war in Iran.

By contrast, about 4 in 10 voters say investigating the Trump administration should be a high priority, and only about 2 in 10 want Congress to prioritize supporting the president's agenda. The results suggest an electorate more focused on pocketbook pressures and foreign policy than on the political fights that have dominated the campaign season.

## A Pocketbook Election

The cost of living is the single most unifying concern across party lines, overshadowing even Trump-specific questions. Roughly half of voters say reducing the budget deficit and finding common ground between the two parties should be high priorities. About 4 in 10 want passing new laws regulating artificial intelligence to rank as a top priority, and about 3 in 10 prioritize ending military aid to Israel.

For many voters, the calculus is personal. Justin Campbell, a 31-year-old Democrat from Brookhaven, Mississippi, said he wants the government to focus on helping people like him rather than the wealthy.

"I'm not a millionaire and probably won't ever be," he said. "I prefer policy that helps me instead of someone I'll never be."

Arthur Brown, a 71-year-old Democrat in Magee, Mississippi, wants the next Congress to work on strengthening the economy and lowering prices. He feels less urgency about holding Trump accountable, partly because only two years remain in the president's term.

"They should just focus on, you know, trying to tackle these problems," he said. "Eventually down the road, justice will come."

## Iran War Ranks Nearly as High as Costs

Ending the war in Iran ranks almost as high as affordability, a notable signal that foreign policy is a top-tier concern heading into a midterm election that has largely centered on domestic economics. The conflict is stretching into its eighth month, despite Trump's earlier promises it would last only "four or five weeks," according to [an earlier AP-NORC poll](https://apnews.com/article/2026-midterms-trump-approval-economy-change-election-e12dd9b19013c75a3d4ede01c1b9d0c3).

Economic discontent runs deep. That companion survey found about 8 in 10 registered voters disapprove of how Trump is handling the cost of living, and about three-quarters disapprove of his performance on the economy. Even among Republicans, only 44% approve of his handling of the cost of living.

## Investigating Trump Is a Base Issue, Not a Broad One

Though most registered Democrats want Congress to investigate the administration, that priority trails the larger goals of lowering costs and ending the Iran conflict. About 6 in 10 registered Democrats call investigations into the administration "one of the highest priorities."

The party's activist wing is more aggressive. Among Democratic voters who identify as supporters of democratic socialism, about three-quarters want Congress to investigate the administration, compared with slightly less than half of Democrats who do not identify that way. Democratic socialists are also more likely to want Congress to end military aid to Israel and less likely to prioritize finding common ground with Republicans.

House Democrats have begun planning an ambitious 100-day agenda should they win control in November. House Democratic leader Hakeem Jeffries of New York has said the party's top priorities include making life more affordable and conducting vast oversight of the administration — a pairing that reflects the tension between the base's appetite for confrontation and the broader electorate's preference for substance.

## Republicans' Tepid Enthusiasm for Trump's Agenda

For Republican voters, bolstering Trump's goals also falls behind lowering the cost of living, ending the war in Iran and reducing the budget deficit. In a strikingly tepid show of support for a sitting president of their own party, only about 4 in 10 Republican voters say the next Congress should make supporting the administration's agenda one of its highest priorities.

The split within the GOP is sharp. About 6 in 10 MAGA Republicans want Congress to prioritize supporting the administration's agenda, but only about 2 in 10 Republicans who are not MAGA supporters say the same. Richard Longo, a 74-year-old Republican in Lake Forest, California, wants to see the president's agenda pursued but is especially worried about balancing the budget.

"You're not going to balance the budget in one year, but you have to have a plan," Longo said.

Lisa Ostlund, a 46-year-old MAGA supporter from Wayland, Massachusetts, wants the next Congress to prioritize Trump's agenda. She does not back everything he does, but credits his policies with helping her find housing after years of homelessness.

"You got to care about your American people," she said. "He definitely doesn't say it right, but you do have to put them first."

## What's Next

With roughly two weeks until Election Day, the poll points to an electorate driven by affordability and war fatigue rather than by allegiance to any single political figure. How Democratic leaders reconcile their oversight ambitions with voters' preference for cost-focused governing — and whether Republicans' subdued support for Trump's agenda translates into lower turnout — are among the open questions that will shape the next Congress.

The AP-NORC poll of 1,119 adults, including 925 registered voters, was conducted Oct. 8-12 using a sample drawn from NORC's probability-based AmeriSpeak Panel. The margin of sampling error is plus or minus 3.9 percentage points for adults overall and 4.2 percentage points for registered voters.
