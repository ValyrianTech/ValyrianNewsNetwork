# China-Europe Freight Trains Top 140,000 Trips

China's flagship rail freight service to Europe has surpassed 140,000 cumulative trips, with total cargo value shipped exceeding US$560 billion, according to [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/politics/20261022/b8c69cf100d44f8192fb486d12de7b50/c.html), the official state news agency. The milestone was marked on 22 October when train No. X8059 departed Puhe Station in the northeastern city of Shenyang at about 11:30 a.m. local time, bound for Moscow.

The service's network now spans 96 scheduled lines within China — up from 93 earlier this year — connecting 132 domestic cities. Abroad, it reaches 237 cities across 26 European countries and more than 100 cities in 12 Asian countries, a footprint the railway authority describes as covering "basically the entire Asia-Europe landmass."

## A Network Built Over Fifteen Years

The China-Europe freight train network was launched in 2011, when the first container service linked the southwestern megacity of Chongqing with Duisburg, Germany. It has since become a centerpiece of the Belt and Road Initiative — Beijing's multibillion-dollar infrastructure and trade connectivity program — positioning rail as a land-based alternative and complement to sea freight.

Within China, the service is organized around three corridors — west, central, and east — that feed the network of 120-kilometer-per-hour scheduled routes. Internationally, a parallel "north, central, and south" corridor structure has taken shape, giving shippers multiple options across the Eurasian landmass.

## The Pace Is Accelerating

The 140,000-trip mark is the latest in a series of milestones that have arrived ever faster. According to a [China Business Journal](https://cj.sina.com.cn/articles/view/1650111241/625ab30902001gckq) report, the network crossed 90,000 cumulative trips in May 2024 and 100,000 that November — entering what officials call a "half-year per 10,000 trips" phase. It then took about six months and 25 days to reach 110,000 trips and roughly five and a half months to reach 120,000. The jump to 130,000, recorded on 9 May 2026 at Zhengzhou's Putian Station on a service to Hamburg, took five months and 11 days.

China Railway officials note that the time required to add each 10,000 trips has compressed from "several years" to about half a year over the course of more than a decade, and now approaches a brisk pace of roughly five months per 10,000 trips.

The southern corridor — routed through Central Asia and the Caspian region rather than the traditional northern route through Russia — has been a notable growth area. It operated 746 trains between January and September 2026, up 125% year-on-year, Xinhua reported. That percentage reflects a smaller base, but the expansion reflects a deliberate strategy of corridor diversification.

Volume records have followed. In May 2026, the service ran a monthly record of 1,965 trains carrying 197,000 twenty-foot equivalent units (TEU), the highest monthly volume in its history, according to the China Railway Logistics Center.

## The Cargo Has Changed

Beyond raw volume, the composition of goods moving along the rails has shifted toward higher-value freight. According to China Business Journal, new energy vehicles, lithium batteries, solar modules, machinery, and electronics now account for roughly 60% of main cargo — a change that lifts the value carried per container and reinforces rail's appeal for time-sensitive, high-worth shipments.

Rail's core advantage lies between the two dominant modes: faster than ocean shipping and cheaper than air freight. That positioning has become more attractive as e-commerce volumes grow and as supply chains seek alternatives to maritime chokepoints and congested ports.

The railway authority is also working to lift border capacity. Preliminary work is underway on double-tracked standard-gauge projects at the China-Kazakhstan Alashankou–Dostyk crossing and the China-Russia Manzhouli–Zabaikalsk crossing — bottlenecks where gauge differences between Chinese and post-Soviet rail networks force container transfers or bogie changes.

## What the Milestone Means

For Beijing, the 140,000-trip figure is both an economic achievement and a geopolitical talking point. State media frame the network as a "win-win" for Eurasian connectivity, and it serves broader goals: reducing dependence on maritime routes such as the Strait of Malacca, deepening ties with Central Asia and Europe, and building long-term logistics leverage.

Yet the framing is promotional, and the headline figures obscure important nuances. Cumulative totals mask the fact that monthly volumes, while at records, reflect steady rather than explosive growth. The 125% jump in the southern corridor is measured against a modest base. And the network operates amid real headwinds: Western sanctions and EU-China trade tensions, the war in Ukraine's disruption of the northern route, and periodic border and logistics bottlenecks that make corridor diversification a strategic priority rather than a purely commercial choice.

## What to Watch

The next milestone — 150,000 cumulative trips — will arrive in roughly five months if the current pace holds, and officials have signaled that further compression is the goal. Two developments bear watching: whether the double-tracking of the Alashankou–Dostyk and Manzhouli–Zabaikalsk crossings moves from planning to construction, easing some of the network's tightest chokepoints; and whether the southern corridor's rapid growth continues as Europe and China navigate an increasingly contested trade relationship.

For now, the trains keep rolling — faster to the next 10,000 than ever before.
