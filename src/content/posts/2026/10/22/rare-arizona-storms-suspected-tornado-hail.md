# Rare Arizona Storms Spawn Suspected Tornado, Hail

A powerful and highly unusual October storm system lashed Arizona from Wednesday into Thursday, spawning a suspected tornado in a small mountain community, toppling trees across the region and pelting parts of the state with hail the size of golf balls, according to the [Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/extreme-weather-tornado-hail-arizona-2fe815b7570812ad0f2cb4278379e185).

The possible tornado struck Heber-Overgaard in Navajo County, about 140 miles northeast of Phoenix, late Wednesday evening. The county sheriff's office reported significant damage and minor injuries in the community of roughly 3,000 residents, though the National Weather Service had not yet confirmed that a tornado actually touched down as of Thursday.

## A Rare October Outbreak

The storm system prompted tornado watches across multiple areas—the first such watches in Arizona since October 2010. The National Weather Service issued multiple warnings as severe thunderstorms moved through the state from Tuesday night into Wednesday, urging residents to seek shelter and stay away from windows as 2- to 3-inch hail struck the region.

The hail pummeled roofs, shattered windows and dented cars, with some reports describing baseball-sized stones in north Phoenix's Metrocenter area. The [weather service](https://apnews.com/article/extreme-weather-hail-arizona-cca9a495def940aaa1852ec728dc3241) had warned residents of the damaging hail early in the week.

"This is very unusual for this area at this time of the year," said Ryan Bernhart, a meteorologist with the National Weather Service in Flagstaff. Warm, moist air moving north fuels storms coming off the California coast, and changing winds can make them spin—creating the conditions for tornadoes, Bernhart explained.

The system also brought powerful wind gusts. Strong winds toppled trees in Flagstaff and Mesa, with some landing on walls and carports. A few dozen ponderosa pines over 30 feet tall fell in the Coconino National Forest northwest of Flagstaff.

"These are not small trees that are down," Bernhart said. "These are pretty strong trees that had been knocked down."

## Community Damage and Emergency Response

In Heber-Overgaard, the suspected tornado hit shortly after 7 p.m. Wednesday, according to the Navajo County Sheriff's Office. The storm downed trees onto homes, knocked over utility poles and left residents without power. The Overgaard Baptist Church took damage while members were wrapping up a prayer meeting, losing trees, signs, fences and a few windows. No members were hurt.

"We took some damage, lost a lot of trees, signs, fences and a few windows," the church posted on social media. "We had a great prayer meeting in the women's restroom."

The Navopache Electric Cooperative, the nonprofit utility serving the area, reported "areas of catastrophic damage" to its electrical infrastructure and offered no estimate on when power would be restored. State Rep. Walt Blackman, whose legislative district covers parts of Navajo County, urged Gov. Katie Hobbs to declare a state of emergency.

"We have families without power, significant property damage, and at least one reported injury," Blackman said in a statement.

Authorities asked residents to avoid State Route 260 between Mogollon and the Overgaard Airpark, as well as areas north of State Route 377 toward Tonto.

## A Vulnerable Landscape

The event underscored the particular vulnerability of Arizona's high country to severe weather. Heber-Overgaard sits atop the Mogollon Rim at an elevation of about 6,600 feet—terrain not typically associated with tornado activity. And unlike the Midwest, where basements offer shelter, homes in the region generally lack them.

"I was kind of scrambling because we just don't have a basement like in the Midwest to go into," Flagstaff resident Karin Mougin told local reporters. "It was the biggest hail I've ever seen up here."

The storms were part of a broader pattern of turbulent weather across the Southwest. The remnants of Hurricane Polo have been bringing additional rain to a region already saturated and vulnerable to flooding, and forecasters warned that the greatest threat to the Phoenix metro area remained heavy rainfall and flash flooding.

Flood watches and warnings remained in effect across numerous counties—including Coconino, Apache, Navajo, Gila, Graham, Greenlee, Cochise, Maricopa, Pinal and Mohave—into Thursday evening.

## What's Next

Several questions remain as authorities assess the damage. Foremost is whether the National Weather Service will confirm an actual tornado touchdown in Heber-Overgaard through its storm survey, and if so, what rating it will assign. Such surveys typically take days to complete.

Also pending are the full accounting of injuries, the restoration of power to the rural cooperative's customers, and whether Gov. Hobbs will grant the requested state of emergency for Navajo County. Meanwhile, residents across the affected regions are left to tally property and vehicle damage—and to wonder at a storm season that has delivered the state's first October tornado watches in 16 years.
