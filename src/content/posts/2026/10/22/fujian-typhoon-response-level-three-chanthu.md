# Fujian Raises Typhoon Response to Level III for Chanthu

Fujian province upgraded its typhoon emergency response to Level III at noon on 22 October, as Super Typhoon Chanthu churned northwest toward the Taiwan Strait, [state broadcaster Xinhua reported](https://www.news.cn/20261022/a8ccd8d2298447c18ffae3bd70589cae/c.html). The decision by the Fujian Provincial Flood Control and Drought Relief Headquarters followed a consultation and assessment of the storm's track, escalating precautionary measures to protect residents and infrastructure ahead of its approach.

## The Storm: Position and Intensity

As of 11:00 local time on 22 October, the centre of Chanthu — the 30th named storm of the 2026 Pacific typhoon season — was located about 660 kilometres southeast of Taipei, with maximum sustained winds near the centre reaching Force 16, roughly 51 to 56 metres per second, Xinhua reported. The storm was moving northwest at about 20 kilometres per hour, gradually closing on Taiwan's east coast while weakening in intensity.

Forecasters expected Chanthu to turn northeast in Zhejiang's eastern offshore waters on the night of 24 October, skirting Taiwan's northeast coast before heading toward southwestern Japan. Earlier in the morning, China's Central Meteorological Observatory had placed the storm about 750 kilometres southeast of Taipei and continued a blue-level typhoon warning at 06:00 — a distance consistent with the storm's northwestward movement over the following hours.

## Warning and Directives

Wu Qishu, chief forecaster at the Fujian Provincial Meteorological Bureau, warned that the wind and rain impacts of Chanthu "should not be underestimated," and urged the public to closely monitor meteorological warnings and prepare for disaster prevention and reduction.

Fujian's flood control headquarters instructed all localities and departments to track the typhoon's movement and its wind, rain and wave impacts, adjust response levels promptly, deploy preventive measures carefully, resolutely organise evacuations and sheltering, and carry out response work in full.

## A Regional Response

The escalation was part of a wider regional mobilisation. In neighbouring Zhejiang, the provincial flood control headquarters raised its offshore typhoon response to Level IV at 08:00 the same day, according to [the emergency management authorities via Guancha](https://www.guancha.cn/ZhengZhi/2026_10_22_905329.shtml). Seas in the south-central East China Sea were forecast to strengthen to Force 12 to 14, coastal waters were expected to see Force 9 to 11 gusts, and eastern Zhejiang was forecast to receive heavy-to-torrential rain from 23 to 25 October.

The same forecasts warned that the Taiwan Strait, the waters east of Taiwan and the Bashi Channel would see strong gales, with parts of the Taiwan Strait and adjacent seas reaching Force 9 to 11 and gusts of Force 12 to 14. Taiwan's north and east were expected to receive heavy rain, with torrential downpours of 250 to 300 millimetres possible in parts of the northeast.

## Understanding China's Response Levels

China uses a four-tier emergency response system for typhoons, with Level I the most severe and Level IV the lowest. Moving from Level IV to III signals escalating concern: stronger winds and rain are expected, coordination across agencies is heightened, and evacuations, the suspension of maritime and fishing activity, and the pre-positioning of relief resources may follow.

Fujian, a coastal province directly across the Taiwan Strait from Taiwan, frequently raises its response as typhoons approach, given its dense coastal population, fishing industry and exposure to storm surge. The province had been at Level IV before the noon upgrade.

## What to Watch

The storm's trajectory carries overlapping wind, rain and maritime threats across Taiwan, Fujian and Zhejiang, explaining the near-simultaneous response upgrades across the region. A favourable factor for Fujian is Chanthu's expected weakening as it nears Taiwan, though officials stress that wind, rain and wave impacts remain significant.

Forecasters have not ruled out the storm grazing Taiwan's northeast coast. Key questions in the coming days include whether Fujian further escalates to Level II or I, and how much rain falls across eastern Fujian and Zhejiang between 23 and 25 October as Chanthu recurves toward Japan.
