# Go Church Declared Unwelcome Across Flemish Cities

Several Flemish cities and institutions are terminating rental contracts and cooperation agreements with the youth church Go Church, after a joint investigation by VRT NWS Pano and De Morgen documented manipulation, financial exploitation, isolation and sexual abuse of mostly vulnerable young followers, some of them minors. Leuven, Mechelen, Sint-Niklaas and Turnhout have each moved to distance themselves from the organisation, [VRT NWS reported](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/10/22/go-church-fup/) on Thursday.

The wave of decisions followed the publication on 21 October of a wide-ranging [investigation](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/10/21/vrt-nws-pano-onderzoekt-go-church/) based on more than 30 testimonies from current and former followers as well as undercover reporting by Pano and De Morgen journalists.

## Background: a global network, an informal local presence

Go Church is a youth church of African origin and part of the Lighthouse Group of Churches, an international network that the organisation says comprises 7,649 churches worldwide, led by Ghanaian bishop Dag Heward-Mills. In Belgium the movement previously operated as Pleasant Surprise Church International and Mustard Seed Chapel International. It says roughly 450 people attend its services each week across locations that include Antwerp, Ghent, Leuven, Mechelen, Sint-Niklaas and Turnhout.

Crucially, Go Church is not registered as a legal entity in Belgium and therefore does not file external annual accounts. That informal status leaves few direct levers for federal oversight, shifting the immediate response to municipalities, universities and private landlords — actors that control the rooms where the church meets.

## The allegations

According to the investigation, former followers described a gradual tightening of control over their studies, relationships, free time and finances. They reported social pressure to give money during the so-called "offertijd" (offering time), pressure to abandon psychological therapy as "devilish," and expectations that marriages be approved by church leaders evaluated on members' "spirituality and obedience."

Religious sociologist Wim Vandewiele of KU Leuven said the testimonies meet several of the 13 government criteria used to identify harmful sectarian organisations, including mental manipulation, financial pressure, sexual violence, severing ties with family and friends, and preventing members from leaving. "Wherever there is power and authority, there is a chance of abuse," he said. "Here you see that very vulnerable young people are being used to achieve certain goals: financial gain, sexual pleasure and so on."

In a separate account published the same day, a 25-year-old woman identified only as Anna alleged she was [raped weekly for about a year](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/10/21/go-church-pano-seksueel-misbruik/) by her pastor, a man identified by the pseudonym Kojo. She said the pastor also financially exploited young women, opening savings accounts in his name and controlling their access to their own money. A former church leader, Morel Harding Kartel, said five girls told him directly they had been abused by the same pastor.

Go Church said Kojo was immediately and permanently removed after admitting the allegations, and that the women were offered guidance and encouraged to report to police. The church said it did not file a report itself because the women asked it not to, and it contests that any of the women were minors at the time.

## Cities and institutions respond

In Mechelen, where Go Church met in the city's Wijhuis Zuid-Mechelen under a private name, mayor Bart Somers said the contract was immediately severed. He said the church responded "very assertively" through a lawyer in what he described as an attempt at intimidation. "We have alerted our police and prevention services and are looking for the victims, the young people involved," Somers said. "This is a completely unacceptable situation."

In Leuven, Go Church hired rooms at KU Leuven's Maria Theresiacollege and was recognised as a student association by LOKO, the Leuven student umbrella, which paid the roughly €50 per-meeting rental cost. The university suspended the church's use of its rooms with immediate effect. "Signals about humiliation, intimidation or improper pressure are taken very seriously by KU Leuven," the university said. Vice-rector for student policy Tom Van Puyenbroeck said the organisation would be heard before a final decision.

In Turnhout, where the church was active at the youth centre Wollewei, mayor Hannes Anaf said the city was "very shocked" and had contacted the Information and Advice Centre on Harmful Sectarian Organisations (IACSSO). "It goes without saying that we will take the necessary action and deny them access to the buildings," he said.

In Sint-Niklaas, operators of the party hall where Go Church met said they would end the rental contract. At the Kavka open house in Antwerp, where Bible studies were held, rentals stopped immediately after the revelations. "Those practices run counter to everything Kavka stands for: an open and inclusive place where young people can be themselves," the venue said, adding that it is tightening its rental procedure.

## An emerging pattern

A recurring theme is the church securing venues through private bookings or under disguises. In Duffel, mayor Sofie Joosen said young members had presented themselves to the leisure service as a cultural association under a different name; the municipality removed the organisation from its halls in the spring, citing the neutrality requirement for public buildings. That neutrality principle now underpins many of the latest decisions.

The case also raises questions for LOKO about how Go Church obtained student-association recognition and whether vetting failed. Staff and students at KU Leuven with concerns can turn to the confidential advisers network, while Kavka has offered support to affected youth.

## What's next

No police complaint has yet been confirmed, and the church removed the pastor but did not report him itself. Whether prosecutors open an investigation — and whether the underage allegations can be substantiated — remains unresolved. Go Church has "strongly rejected" the characterisation of itself as a sect, saying its Christian convictions "must never be used to justify coercion, intimidation or abuse of spiritual authority."

The broader test is whether a patchwork of local rental decisions amounts to a coherent national response. The case is likely to sharpen calls for tighter oversight of informal religious groups in Belgium, including mandatory registration and publicly available safeguarding policies — all against the backdrop of constitutionally protected religious freedom.
