# Brussels Loses Euro Athletics 2030 Bid to Helsinki

Brussels will not host the 2030 European Athletics Championships. Meeting at its headquarters in Lausanne on Thursday, the Council of European Athletics chose Helsinki over the Belgian capital, ending a bid that had been built around a powerful national symbol. Belgian athletics officials described the outcome as a "surprise" and a "huge blow," according to [RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/bruxelles-non-retenu-pour-l-euro-d-athletisme-2030-une-surprise-et-coup-dur-enorme-11804281).

The decision was announced after a final round of presentations on Thursday morning. Helsinki will host the outdoor championships for a record fourth time, following 1971, 1994 and 2012. The Finnish capital also staged the World Championships in 1983 and 2005.

## A Symbolic Bid Defeated

The Brussels candidacy had been framed around a rare confluence of anniversaries: 2030 marks both the bicentenary of Belgium and the centenary of the King Baudouin Stadium, the national venue that would have been the centerpiece of the event. Brussels has hosted the European outdoor championships only once, in 1950, at what was then the Heysel stadium. Returning the event 80 years later was presented as a compelling narrative.

Six cities initially expressed interest, but only three submitted formal bids: Brussels, Helsinki and Zurich. When [La DH](https://www.dhnet.be/sports/omnisports/athletisme/2026/10/22/championnats-deurope-dathletisme-2030-helsinki-preferee-a-bruxelles-IE4HUY4OVVDEXPPZTTU6GSYGFQ/) reported the outcome, it noted that Zurich withdrew in June 2026 to target 2034 instead, leaving a two-way race in Lausanne.

The bid was carried by the City of Brussels, Belgian Athletics and the sports-event company Golazo, with the support of both the Brussels Region and federal authorities. A six-person delegation defended the dossier in Lausanne: Brussels Mayor Philippe Close, Belgian Athletics co-presidents Patrick Van Waes and Jessica Mayon, Mémorial Van Damme director Kim Gevaert, and Golazo representatives Bob Verbeeck and Ben Geyskens.

## 'We Received No Explanation'

Van Waes, who leads Atletiek Vlaanderen and co-presides over Belgian Athletics, said the presentation had gone well and that the delegation expected a favorable verdict.

"We had prepared it meticulously and the presentation went very well," he told the Belga news agency, as quoted by [BX1](https://bx1.be/categories/sport/championnats-deurope-dathletisme-helsinki-preferee-a-bruxelles-pour-organiser-leuro-en-2030/). "Our budget raised no doubt, nor did our capacity to organize such an event. We felt we had very good chances. Some members of the European Council had even confirmed this to us beforehand."

The announcement for Helsinki "surprised us," he acknowledged. "We are all deeply disappointed. Our dossier was solid. The City of Brussels, the athletics federations and Golazo made many efforts for this candidacy. 2030 was also a beautiful symbolic date, with the bicentenary of Belgium and the centenary of the King Baudouin Stadium. We received no explanation for the reasons behind this failure."

Asked whether Belgium would pursue another major athletics championship in the near future, Van Waes said stakeholders would meet to consider the question.

## Why Helsinki Won

European Athletics offered little public detail beyond its announcement. In a post on [X](https://x.com/EuroAthletics/status/2113201021610860789), the federation wrote: "HELSINKI 2030! Helsinki will host the European Athletics Championships for a record fourth time in 2030, with Europe's best athletes set to return to the iconic Olympic Stadium!"

Belgian coverage pointed to a set of practical factors. [La DH's analysis](https://www.dhnet.be/sports/omnisports/athletisme/2026/10/22/euro-dathletisme-2030-pourquoi-helsinki-a-battu-bruxelles-MUYDZM2SMNDQDO24VR4LXSHFYE/) summarized the verdict as "infrastructure, enthusiasm, tradition" — concluding that the European association "chose Finnish security at the expense of Belgian symbolism." Helsinki's Olympic Stadium, an iconic 1938 venue, reopened in 2020 after extensive renovation and sits within a compact, already-proven event footprint. Brussels, by contrast, still needed to modernize the King Baudouin Stadium. Finland's deep athletics tradition and the city's record as a returning host also weighed in its favor.

The Mémorial Van Damme, Brussels' annual Diamond League meeting, is regularly cited as proof of Belgian organizational know-how, but it is a one-day event rather than a multi-day championship.

## A Bicentenary Without Its Flagship Sport

The loss stings because 2030 is meant to be a sporting showcase for Belgium. The country is still due to host the UCI Road World Championships in cycling, the start of the Tour de France Femmes in Brussels, and the cyclo-cross World Championships in Namur. But athletics — Belgium's No. 1 Olympic sport — will be absent from the festivities.

## What's Next

European Athletics has not publicly detailed how it scored the rival bids, and the lack of explanation may fuel calls for greater transparency in future host selections. The question now is whether Belgium will re-enter the race for a major athletics championship and whether the King Baudouin Stadium's planned modernization will proceed on the same timeline without the 2030 deadline as a driver. Helsinki has yet to announce the full dates and program for its 2030 edition.

For now, the Belgian delegation leaves Lausanne with a symbol lost and no official reason why.
