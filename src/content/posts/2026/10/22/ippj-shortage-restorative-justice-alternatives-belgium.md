# IPPJ Shortage: More Places Won't Fix It, Report Argues

Confronted with chronic saturation of Belgium's youth detention institutions, a director of one of the restorative-justice services meant to offer an alternative is arguing that building more places is the wrong answer. In an interview on [RTBF's Matin Première](https://www.rtbf.be/article/la-creation-de-nouvelles-places-en-ippj-n-est-pas-une-solution-il-faut-mieux-utiliser-les-outils-a-disposition-11804136), Valériane Meyan said the priority should be to make better use of the tools already available rather than expanding capacity at the Institutions publiques de protection de la jeunesse (IPPJ).

The debate has taken on fresh urgency after the violent protests of 9 October 2026 in Brussels and Liège, when minors suspected of serious offences had to be released for lack of a detention place — even in cases where a youth judge had ruled that placement was necessary.

## A System Under Strain

Belgium's French-speaking community (Fédération Wallonie-Bruxelles) is struggling to accommodate minors ordered into its youth detention institutions. The IPPJs currently have a capacity of 245 places, while the number of young people awaiting a closed-regime place is disputed: [RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/manifestation-du-9-octobre-16-mineurs-soupconnes-de-faits-graves-remis-en-liberte-faute-de-places-en-ippj-11798376) reported that 283 young people were on the waiting list, while [La DH](https://www.dhnet.be/actu/belgique/2026/10/22/ippj-privee-bracelet-electronique-et-renforcement-des-places-existantes-des-nouvelles-mesures-pour-encadrer-les-delinquants-I7AG6XBLWRF6HJBNSPQVRDW4GI/) cited 201 currently awaiting a place. Minister Valérie Lescrenier has put the figure at around 300.

The fallout from 9 October made the consequences concrete. As RTBF reported, 16 minors suspected of serious acts were released for lack of IPPJ places, despite prosecutors having requested their placement and, in several cases, a judge having concluded that detention was warranted. Three were eventually placed after the fact. The alternatives did not absorb the overflow either: some 44 young people were also awaiting alternative care, according to the Brussels prosecutor's office.

## The Restorative Justice Alternative

Meyan directs a Service d'Action Restauratrice et Éducative (SARE), a restorative-justice and educational scheme that can be ordered by a prosecutor or youth judge. These services offer restorative dialogue between offender and victim, community service, awareness modules and positive projects.

"We are indeed an alternative to the IPPJ, but above all we are the first thing youth magistrates should think of in the hierarchy of measures," Meyan said. She described restorative conferences in which a young offender and their victim sit around a table to open a dialogue and find solutions. "It is sometimes more responsibility-building for a young person to have to explain themselves before their victim than to end up in an IPPJ," she added.

Meyan stressed that each situation is different and that an IPPJ placement can be essential for some young people. But she argued that when a young person is not placed, "that does not mean nothing is happening." "To protect society, above all you must educate," she said. "If we want these young people to become responsible adults tomorrow, work must be done alongside."

## The 'Build It and They Will Fill It' Argument

At the heart of the debate is a contested criminological claim. Drawing a parallel with Belgium's saturated adult prison system, Meyan argued that adding capacity risks fuelling demand rather than relieving it. "Exactly as in the adult system, the more places you create, the more these places will fill up. So what must be done is to better use the tools available," she said.

She added that if the hierarchy of measures were properly respected and each young person were placed in the right setting, "that would relieve the IPPJs, that is certain." The point is not that detention is never needed, she said, but that the system should not think "uniquely in terms of IPPJ" — the two approaches are complementary.

## A Contrasting Response From Government

Minister Lescrenier has acknowledged the strain — "the system is under tension" — while defending a capacity-expansion track. The Fédération Wallonie-Bruxelles plans to add more than 50 places by 2028, raising capacity from 245 to 301. A pilot project for [electronic monitoring bracelets for minors](https://www.rtbf.be/article/faute-de-places-en-ippj-les-bracelets-electroniques-pour-mineurs-sur-la-table-de-la-fwb-confirme-la-ministre-lescrenier-11803216), currently reserved for adults, is also under study.

The minister has also sought to relativise the headline waiting-list figures, telling [RTL Info](https://www.rtl.be/actu/belgique/politique/300-jeunes-delinquants-en-liberte-faute-de-places-en-ippj-selon-la-ministre-il/2026-10-21/article/804053) that "there are not 300 young people without a response," noting that two-thirds are already subject to a care measure. And as La DH reported, the government plans to create a specialised residential service of 15 places in 2028 — a privately run, small-scale educational and therapeutic facility backed by a staff budget of about 1.2 million euros.

The political pressure runs the other way as well. The Brussels prosecutor's office has publicly called on decision-makers to "restore the credibility of juvenile justice," and Judge Denis Goeman denounced the releases as a "contempt for the rule of law," according to [RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/liberation-de-mineurs-faute-de-manque-de-place-en-ippj-un-mepris-pour-l-etat-de-droit-selon-le-juge-denis-goeman-11798758). Young offenders themselves have described the gap as producing a sense of impunity.

## What to Watch

The disagreement is less about whether the IPPJs are overcrowded than about what to do about it. One camp points to capacity and public-security credibility; the other to a hierarchy of measures meant to funnel young people toward the least restrictive response first. Whether the planned expansion, the electronic-bracelet pilot and the new SRS actually relieve the waiting lists — or fill up as critics predict — will depend on how often magistrates turn to restorative and alternative measures, and on whether those services are given the resources to take on more cases.
