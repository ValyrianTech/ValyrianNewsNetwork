# EA Shuts Down BioWare, Cancels Next Mass Effect

Electronic Arts is shutting down BioWare, the influential Canadian role-playing game studio behind *Mass Effect*, *Dragon Age* and *Baldur's Gate*, and has canceled the next *Mass Effect* game, the fifth mainline entry in a series that began in 2007. The closure, announced by BioWare on social media on Wednesday and reported the same day by [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-21/electronic-arts-to-cut-400-jobs-shut-down-bioware), brings an end to one of the most storied developers in video game history and comes roughly two months after EA completed its $55 billion sale to a consortium led by Saudi Arabia's sovereign wealth fund.

The [Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/bioware-ea-electronic-arts-shutdown-mass-effect-6b6e5c930fe56dd2cd18985d89f6ef24) reported that EA is cutting around 400 jobs across the company, roughly 3% of its workforce, including about 50 roles at BioWare. The studio employed fewer than 100 people at the time of the closure.

## A Studio That Defined a Genre

Founded in Edmonton, Canada, in 1995 by doctors Ray Muzyka, Greg Zeschuk and Augustine Yip, BioWare built its reputation on immersive, choice-driven role-playing adventures. Its 1998 release *Baldur's Gate* helped revive the computer RPG, followed by *Neverwinter Nights*, *Star Wars: Knights of the Old Republic* and *Jade Empire*. EA acquired the studio in 2007, the same year the first *Mass Effect* launched.

What followed was arguably BioWare's commercial and creative peak: the *Mass Effect* trilogy and the *Dragon Age* series, culminating in 2014's *Dragon Age: Inquisition*, the franchise's best-selling entry. The studio's narrative-driven approach, built around dialogue choices, relationships and player agency, influenced a generation of developers. As [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/gaming-industry/rip-bioware-ea-has-shut-down-the-legendary-rpg-developer/) noted, CD Projekt's leadership credited BioWare with helping bring *The Witcher* to life, and paid tribute to the studio following news of the closure.

## A Decade of Detours

The path to closure was shaped by a series of missteps. After the success of *Inquisition*, BioWare pivoted toward live-service multiplayer with 2019's *Anthem*, a move away from its single-player strengths that failed to compete with established online hits like *Fortnite* and *Overwatch*. The game never found a lasting audience, and its servers were shut down for good earlier this year. Meanwhile, 2017's *Mass Effect: Andromeda* and 2024's *Dragon Age: The Veilguard* received mixed reviews and disappointed many longtime fans.

A new *Mass Effect* had been in early development. According to [IGN](https://www.ign.com/articles/bioware-shutting-down-next-mass-effect-canceled), the studio had spent years teasing the project's setting and story, confirming it would be set hundreds of years after *Mass Effect 3* and return the action to the original Milky Way galaxy. Fans will now never learn how that story unfolds.

## The Buyout Looms Large

The layoffs and closure arrive barely two months after EA finalized its $55 billion take-private deal with Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund, Silver Lake Partners and Affinity Partners, the firm run by Jared Kushner, son-in-law of President Donald Trump. The buyout left EA carrying roughly $18 billion in debt, adding pressure for cost-cutting. Some employees and outside observers had warned the studio's future looked precarious well before Wednesday. Anonymous BioWare staff told PC Gamer last year it felt like "a matter of time" before layoffs arrived, while a former *Dragon Age* lead characterized EA as "a hedge fund with a videogame hobby."

In a [message to staff](https://www.ea.com/news/building-eas-next-chapter) published on EA's website, President and Chief Studios Officer Cam Weber and President and COO David Tinson framed the decision as part of a broader strategic reset around three priorities: deepening its core franchises, expanding its reach, and transforming its business.

"As we focus on these larger opportunities, we've had a small team at BioWare working on early-development for Mass Effect. We've made the decision not to move forward with this development, which means we're closing the studio and our Edmonton workspace," the memo read. "BioWare is a legendary studio that helped define the role-playing genre, and we do not take this decision lightly."

"Saying no to some things we're passionate about allows us to focus our teams and resources so we give our biggest opportunities everything they need to succeed," the executives added.

## What Survives — and What Hangs in the Balance

EA's remaining portfolio leans heavily on sports and shooters. The company said it will continue investing in the EA Sports FC franchise, future *Battlefield* titles, and *Apex Legends*, and confirmed that Respawn and DICE are collaborating on a future project widely reported to be *Star Wars: Battlefront 3*. Action-adventure titles *Marvel's Iron Man* and the next *Star Wars Jedi* game remain in development, though EA said it will pursue "fewer simultaneous, large-scale projects" in that genre. On the lifestyle side, development on *The Sims* "Project Lotus" has been paused while "Project Evergreen" continues, and the *Skate* studio has been downsized.

BioWare's own farewell was more elegiac. "What generations of BioWare built together is something to celebrate and remember," the studio said in its social media statement. "These games became so much more because you played them, debated your choices, fell in love with characters, and made these worlds your own." Mike Gamble, the studio's executive producer on *Mass Effect* and a 17-year veteran, told fans: "Your support has meant everything to me."

The closure leaves at least six franchises facing an uncertain future, including *Mass Effect* and *Dragon Age*. Whether EA licenses or sells those intellectual properties, or retires them, remains an open question. A live-action *Mass Effect* series is still in development at Amazon MGM Studios, though its relationship to the games' narrative is now less clear.

## What to Watch

For the industry, BioWare's closure is a signal about where major publishers see their best returns. The retreat from single-player, narrative-driven RPGs toward live-service, sports and licensed titles has been underway for years, and EA's restructuring accelerates it. The coming months will reveal whether the company's debt-driven cost discipline extends beyond BioWare, and whether one of gaming's most beloved catalogs finds a second life elsewhere.

For now, the studio's final word to its players doubles as its epitaph. The worlds BioWare made, it noted, became so much more because people played them.
