# Beijing Deputy Mayor Xia Linmao Placed Under CCDI Review

Xia Linmao, a member of the Beijing Municipal Party Committee Standing Committee and a deputy mayor of the capital, has been placed under disciplinary review and supervisory investigation for suspected "serious violations of discipline and law," China's top disciplinary authority announced on 22 October 2026. The one-sentence dispatch from the Central Commission for Discipline Inspection and the National Supervisory Commission (CCDI/NSC), carried by [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/legal/20261022/519b32774e1d4b60a6561eaae577f282/c.html), gave no further detail on the nature of the allegations.

The announcement makes Xia one of the most senior municipal officials in the country to be placed under a *central* rather than local review, a distinction that underscores the political weight of the case and the continued intensity of the leadership's anti-corruption campaign at the highest levels of urban governance.

## Who is Xia Linmao?

Xia was born in 1970 in Shaanxi Province and is a native of Jianhu in Jiangsu Province, from where his family derives; he entered the workforce in July 1993 and built his career almost entirely within Beijing's municipal system. He served as deputy party secretary, deputy district head and then district head of Shijingshan, before taking charge of Miyun District as party secretary and, from December 2018, serving as party secretary of the central Dongcheng District.

As [Oriental Daily News](https://orientaldaily.on.cc/cnt/china_world/20210212/mobile/odn-20210212-0212_00178_017.html) reported at the time, Xia was elevated to the Beijing Municipal Party Committee Standing Committee in February 2021, putting him among the roughly nine to thirteen officials who form the capital's top decision-making body. He was appointed deputy mayor in January 2023 and subsequently served as Executive Vice Mayor, a role that placed him at the centre of Beijing's fiscal, investment and economic agenda.

In that capacity he publicly fronted several flagship policies, including the 2024 programme to renovate 20 "urban villages" while upholding the principle that "houses are for living in, not for speculation," and the capital's services-sector opening-up agenda. As recently as August 2026 he was briefing the press on Beijing's foreign investment performance and the services-sector opening-up 3.0 plan — a reminder that the review of a sitting official can begin while that official is still publicly active.

## What the case signals

The disclosure follows a familiar template. China's anti-corruption apparatus runs two parallel processes: disciplinary review for party members and supervisory investigation under the National Supervision Law. The "serious violations of discipline and law" formulation, used for senior officials suspected of corruption, typically precedes formal expulsion from the Party, referral to prosecutors and criminal charges. No evidence, amounts or associates have been disclosed.

The significance lies in the pattern. The announcement was made by the central CCDI/NSC rather than a municipal body — a sign of the seniority of the target and of Beijing's determination to police its own upper ranks. The case arrives within the broader enforcement drive reaffirmed at the 20th CCDI Fifth Plenary Session in January 2026, which prioritised deeper rectification in finance, state-owned enterprises, energy, education, development zones and bidding and tendering.

Beijing's administrative weight magnifies the sensitivity. A deputy mayor who previously ran two major districts and then oversaw the capital's economic portfolio wields substantial influence over land, investment and procurement decisions — areas historically vulnerable to corruption. Hong Kong's [Ta Kung Pao](https://www.tkww.hk/a/202610/22/AP6ad9d1bae4b03b525f94f51c.html) reported the case within hours, reflecting the unusually high profile of the target.

## Timing and speculation

The disclosure came during the period of the 20th Central Committee's Fifth Plenary Session in October 2026, a recurring moment when senior personnel and disciplinary actions are often publicised to signal resolve.

Overseas commentators have speculated about factional or elite-political dimensions to the case. Those claims remain unverified, and no official source has linked Xia's review to any political network. The official framing presents the matter purely as a disciplinary and legal one.

## What to watch

The immediate question is whether Xia will be expelled from the Party and referred for prosecution — the standard next step for officials facing charges at this level, and one that would clarify the seriousness of the case. Equally unclear is which part of his lengthy career — his district tenures or his executive economic role — is at the centre of the investigation.

For investors and local officials, the case is a reminder that the anti-corruption campaign continues to reach the most economically consequential offices in the capital. For Beijing's investment and policy messaging, the timing is awkward: a deputy mayor who was promoting the city's openness to foreign capital only two months ago is now the subject of a central disciplinary review.
