# China, Russia Sign Sweeping Pact at 31st PMs Meeting

China and Russia have released the full text of a detailed ten-section joint communiqué signed by Premier Li Qiang and Prime Minister Mikhail Mishustin at the 31st China-Russia Regular Prime Ministers Meeting in St. Petersburg, a document that moves well beyond symbolic statements to lay out sector-by-sector commitments across trade, technology, finance, energy and global governance. The communiqué was jointly signed by the two premiers and published in full by [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/politics/leaders/20261022/943fcc651e00412a9ad6ff103137b7ae/c.html) on October 22, a day after the meeting concluded.

Li visited Russia from October 20 to 21 at Mishustin's invitation. During the visit he met President Vladimir Putin and co-chaired the prime ministers' meeting with Mishustin on the morning of October 21 local time, according to the [Ministry of Foreign Affairs of the PRC](https://www.fmprc.gov.cn/zyxw/202610/t20261021_12044564.shtml). State Councilor Wu Zhenglong also participated. After the talks the two premiers signed the communiqué and witnessed the conclusion of cooperation documents in trade, aerospace, science and technology, and education.

## Anniversary diplomacy

The meeting was framed around two milestones: 30 years since the two countries declared a strategic cooperative partnership, and 25 years since the signing of the Treaty of Good-Neighborliness, Friendship and Cooperation. The communiqué notes that in May 2026 the two presidents agreed to extend the treaty, reaffirming its principles as the political foundation of the relationship.

Addressing the meeting, Li said the partnership "is entering a new phase of greater achievement and faster development," and that China is ready to work with Russia "in a spirit of mutual respect and mutual benefit to deepen cooperation in all fields." Mishustin said the relationship "is at its historical highest level and has set a model for state-to-state relations."

The communiqué is explicit in characterizing the partnership as "not an alliance, not confrontation, not drawn along ideological lines, not targeting third countries, and not affected by the external environment" — a formulation Beijing and Moscow have repeated to distinguish their alignment from a formal military bloc.

## A roadmap, not a statement

The document is divided into ten numbered sections. Its political-strategic opening reaffirms the One-China principle, with Russia recognizing Taiwan as an inalienable part of Chinese territory and opposing any form of "Taiwan independence." China, in turn, supports Russia in safeguarding its security, stability, national unity, sovereignty and territorial integrity, and opposes external interference in Russia's internal affairs.

On trade, the two sides committed to expanding the scale of bilateral trade, optimizing its commodity structure, and cultivating new growth points in cross-border e-commerce, the digital economy and green, low-carbon development. The section also covers service trade — including video games — consumer protection, intellectual property, standardization and agricultural and food trade, and endorses holding the 11th China-Russia Expo in Russia in 2027.

Technology emerges as a major pillar. The communiqué commits both governments to cooperation on artificial intelligence, including a high-level joint academic conference in China in 2027 and the operation of a joint AI expert committee. It also touches on international information security, satellite internet, open-source software cooperation, space — including the International Lunar Research Station and joint planetary and deep-space missions — and interoperability between China's BeiDou and Russia's GLONASS navigation systems.

## Finance and energy at the core

The finance section is notable for its language on sovereign assets. The communiqué asserts that confiscation, freezing, or any other use of a country's sovereign assets without its consent "seriously violates the basic principle of sovereign equality of states and undermines the stability of the global financial order." It also endorses a high share of local-currency settlement in bilateral trade, investment and credit, cooperation between financial institutions, and the possibility of qualified Russian companies issuing panda bonds in China.

Energy remains central to the relationship. Mishustin emphasized during the meeting that Russia is the largest supplier of oil and natural gas to China, as [RT China](https://rtchina.cn/popular/701-2026-10-21-132698/) reported. The communiqué commits the two sides to deepening oil, gas, coal and electricity cooperation, maintaining the security of cross-border energy corridors, and expanding cooperation in renewables, hydrogen, storage, and carbon capture, utilization and storage. It also calls for high-quality construction and operation of Tianwan nuclear power plant units 7 and 8 and Xudapu units 3 and 4, and for gradually advancing cooperation on thermonuclear fusion, fast reactors and closed nuclear fuel cycles.

## A coordinated global agenda

The final substantive section sets out shared positions on global governance. It pairs China's proposals — a community with a shared future for mankind and the Global Development, Security, Civilization and Governance Initiatives — with Russia's Greater Eurasian Partnership and Eurasian security architecture. Both sides reject unilateral coercive measures imposed without UN Security Council authorization and describe extraterritorial sanctions as unlawful.

The communiqué also coordinates positions across multilateral forums. It marks the 25th anniversary of the Shanghai Cooperation Organization, supports Pakistan's chairmanship and the holding of the 2027 summit, and notes Russia's backing for China's 2027 BRICS chairmanship. It references the G20 leaders' summit in Miami on December 14-15, 2026, and backs China's hosting of APEC 2026 while acknowledging China's support for Russia's bid to host APEC in 2035.

## Documents and next steps

The tenth section lists the documents concluded within the meeting framework, including the joint communiqué itself, minutes of the investment, energy and humanitarian cooperation committees, a China-Russia Science, Technology and Innovation Cooperation Roadmap for 2026-2030, a standardization cooperation action plan, a memorandum on Russian scientific instruments aboard China's Tianwen-3 Mars mission orbiter, and agreements to establish a China-Russia Advanced Technology University and a China-Russia Mineral Resources College.

The communiqué confirms that the 32nd Regular Prime Ministers Meeting will be held in 2027, with the specific timing, place and format to be determined through diplomatic channels.

## What to watch

Analysts will be watching whether the directional commitments translate into binding quantitative targets — the communiqué is largely programmatic rather than numeric, and the full details of several annexed documents, including the standardization action plan and the Tianwen-3 memorandum, are not yet public. The meeting also comes amid intensifying Western sanctions on Russia and deepening U.S.-China strategic competition; the document's repeated rejection of extraterritorial sanctions and its language on sovereign assets are likely to draw scrutiny from Western capitals. For now, the breadth of the communiqué signals that Beijing and Moscow intend to keep institutionalizing their partnership — expanding it from declaratory alignment into operational cooperation across a widening set of sectors.
