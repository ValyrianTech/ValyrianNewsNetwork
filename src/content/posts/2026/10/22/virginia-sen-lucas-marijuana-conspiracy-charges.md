# Virginia Sen. Lucas Charged in Marijuana Conspiracy Case

Virginia state Sen. L. Louise Lucas, one of the state's most powerful Democrats, was charged Thursday in connection with what federal authorities alleged was her role leading a wide-ranging criminal enterprise that acquired and distributed large quantities of marijuana and laundered the proceeds, according to [AP News](https://apnews.com/article/l-louise-lucas-virginia-corruption-investigation-767ed83f970ba6dec48048dcff2a28d0). Lucas, 82, surrendered and was expected to make an initial appearance in federal court in Norfolk on Thursday afternoon.

An eight-count federal indictment, returned by a grand jury on October 14 and unsealed Thursday, charges Lucas and six co-defendants — including two of her daughters and a grandson — in the operation prosecutors dubbed "the Lucas Enterprise." According to the [indictment](https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.vaed.608205/gov.uscourts.vaed.608205.3.0_1.pdf), Lucas was "the leader of the enterprise," who "approved and directed criminal activities" over a scheme estimated at $4.5 million across roughly five years.

## Context: A powerful lawmaker at the center of the storm

Lucas has served in the Virginia Senate since 1992 and is president pro tempore, overseeing the chamber in the lieutenant governor's absence. More consequentially, she chairs the Senate Finance and Appropriations Committee, through which all legislation with any budget impact must flow — a post she has used to stop and ultimately kill legislation she opposes.

The charges arrive less than two weeks before the 2026 midterm elections, and immediately raised questions about political motivation. U.S. Attorney General Todd Blanche rejected any suggestion politics played a role, noting the investigation began well before the second Trump administration started in January 2025.

"We don't do political paybacks," Blanche told reporters at a news conference alongside FBI Director Kash Patel. A person familiar with the May search told the AP at the time that the probe was opened during Democratic President Joe Biden's administration.

## The allegations

Patel said in a statement that the charges allege "Lucas and a close-knit group of family members and associates orchestrated a multi-year scheme to enrich their own criminal marijuana enterprise, 'the Lucas Enterprise' — using Lucas' position as Chairwoman of the Senate Finance and Appropriations Committee to insulate the enterprise from criminal scrutiny and influencing legislation to financially benefit themselves."

The indictment also alleges that Lucas worked with another defendant to use her political position to obtain a recreational marijuana license for him, with the understanding that proceeds from sales under the license would be shared with her. Investigators have said the scheme was designed to secure "generational wealth" for its members.

The charges follow the FBI's search in May of Lucas's hometown office and a cannabis shop in which she has a stake. At the time of that search, marijuana was recovered at The Cannabis Outlet in Portsmouth, according to the indictment — a raid Lucas, a vocal critic of the Trump administration, characterized as an attempt to intimidate her.

Since 2021, marijuana has been legal to possess in Virginia, and many shops across the state have sold cannabis-derived products. But full recreational sales are not yet underway; [legislation enacted earlier this year](https://apnews.com/article/marijuana-cannabis-legal-sales-virginia-weed-shops-7da81e3fe8d50f7ea37262a04015e4dc) will allow them beginning in July 2027 — meaning the alleged conduct occurred largely in a gray-market period during which Lucas was a forceful advocate of legalization.

## Lucas vows to fight

A statement attributed to Lucas posted on her social media account did not directly address the substance of the charges but said Thursday's actions amounted to the federal government wielding its "full weight" against a great-grandmother.

"I grew up under Jim Crow. I've seen what happens when people in power try to silence those who stand up to them," Lucas said. "I will not be intimidated. I will not be bullied. And I will not back down."

"This is the circus we always expected it to be," said Brian Woolfolk, chief counsel for the L. Louise Lucas Legal Defense Fund.

The brash lawmaker has drawn comparisons to Trump for her sometimes-profane, unapologetic style. She has amassed a six-figure following on X and national attention, using the platform to advocate for her priorities and to bash critics, including former GOP Gov. Glenn Youngkin. Earlier this year, she helped lead Democrats' ultimately unsuccessful plan to redraw the state's congressional districts into a map intended to elect 10 Democrats and only one Republican.

## Democrats react cautiously

At the time of the May search, many Democrats defended Lucas, expressing skepticism about the basis for it given the Justice Department's efforts to establish criminal conduct by perceived Trump opponents. The initial reaction Thursday was more muted.

Scott Surovell, the Democratic leader of the state Senate, said in a statement that the indictment contained "serious allegations" but emphasized that "Senator Lucas has the right to defend herself, and she has my full support as she does so." Other legislative leaders and Democratic Gov. Abigail Spanberger's office did not immediately respond to requests for comment.

Lucas is not on the ballot this year. But her daughter Lisa Lucas-Burke is running for mayor of Portsmouth, a bid now shadowed by the family's legal peril.

## Analysis: What to watch

The core contested issue is political motivation. Blanche points to the probe's Biden-era origins and the grand-jury process; critics note the pre-midterm timing and the Justice Department's pattern of pursuing Trump's perceived foes. The racketeering and honest-services-fraud charges carry significant penalties, and the honest-services count hinges on whether Lucas traded legislative influence for personal benefit.

Lucas's committee chairmanship and Senate sway make this a seismic event in Virginia politics. How Democratic leadership and Gov. Spanberger respond — and whether Lucas faces calls to step aside from her leadership posts — will be closely watched in the days ahead. Court records in the case had been sealed for months, and several legal and disclosure details may yet evolve as the case moves toward trial.
