# Hong Kong Invites APEC Infrastructure Projects to Finance

Hong Kong Financial Secretary Paul Chan Mo-po has invited APEC economies to bring their infrastructure projects to the city for financing, pitching Hong Kong as a financing gateway for the region's vast infrastructure needs. Speaking to [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/gangao/20261022/1eff3865494b417dbdd3149a22aa0712/c.html) after the close of the 33rd APEC Finance Ministers' Meeting, Chan said Hong Kong could offer financing solutions — including project listings — for the Asia-Pacific's enormous investment demand.

"We welcome infrastructure projects from APEC economies to come to Hong Kong for financing, achieving win-win cooperation," Chan said.

## First Time Hosting the Finance Track

The meeting, held in Hong Kong on 20–21 October, marked the first time the city has hosted the APEC finance ministers' track. It was hosted by China's Ministry of Finance and organized by the Hong Kong SAR government, reflecting China's role as the APEC 2026 host economy. As [HMO](https://www.hmo.gov.cn/2026/10/08/ARTIzF52EPggTURQuhBxaANP261008.shtml) reported ahead of the event, Chief Executive John Lee Ka-chiu thanked the central government for its trust in Hong Kong and pledged the SAR government would fully cooperate to deliver a high-quality conference experience for delegate teams.

The agenda covered global and regional macroeconomic and financial conditions, fiscal policy in support of livelihoods, digital infrastructure financing, inclusive finance tailored to local conditions, and exchanges among young APEC finance and economics talent.

## Inclusive Finance and the CargoX Pitch

One of the meeting's central themes was promoting inclusive finance. Chan presented the Hong Kong Monetary Authority's "CargoX" project as a practical case study — a digital platform that integrates freight and order data to serve as proof of actual business, enabling small and medium-sized enterprises to apply for unsecured loans. The initiative addresses a chronic financing difficulty for SMEs, a challenge shared across developing Asia-Pacific markets.

Chan framed the broader goal in human terms, saying the ultimate aim of development is to improve people's lives.

## Dual-Listing Ambitions

Beyond infrastructure, Chan used the meeting to promote Hong Kong's capital markets as a channel for Asia-Pacific companies to raise funds. He expressed willingness to deepen capital-market regulatory cooperation with Asia-Pacific economies so that companies can pursue listings both in their home markets and in Hong Kong — an overture that drew interest from many delegates, according to Xinhua.

Chan also said delegates called for breaking down intra-regional tariff and trade barriers and focusing on guiding investment and industrial landing. He cited Vietnam and Malaysia, among other Southeast Asian nations, as wanting mainland Chinese enterprises to place industrial-chain operations locally — with Hong Kong able to serve as the "going global" platform for mainland firms thanks to its professional services.

"Through this meeting, everyone has come to recognize more the importance of openness, innovation and cooperation," Chan said.

## A Meeting Overshadowed by Geopolitics

The Hong Kong gathering took place against a delicate US-China backdrop. As [Lianhe Zaobao](https://www.zaobao.com.sg/news/china/story20261009-9810572) reported, citing the South China Morning Post, US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent was likely to skip the Hong Kong meeting to prepare for President Donald Trump's expected attendance at the APEC Leaders' Meeting in Shenzhen on 17–18 November, where Trump is expected to meet President Xi Jinping. That report noted Sino-US trade talks and a trade truce set to expire on 10 January 2027.

## What's Next

The 33rd APEC Economic Leaders' Meeting will be held in Shenzhen in November. Chan said Asia-Pacific political and business figures will then experience firsthand the leading edge of the "Shenzhen-Hong Kong-Guangzhou" innovation cluster in science and technology — and feel the pulse of China's high-quality development. For Hong Kong, the finance ministers' meeting served as a proof of concept: a showcase of its role as an international financial center, and a bid to convert regional infrastructure demand into listings, lending and capital-market activity on its shores.
