# Storm Austen to Lash Belgium with 100 km/h Gusts

Belgium is bracing for the first named storm of the season, with Storm Austen set to strike on Friday afternoon and evening (23 October), bringing wind gusts of up to 100 km/h at the coast and heavy rain across the country. The Royal Meteorological Institute (KMI/IRM) has placed all provinces under a code yellow wind warning from 13:00, escalating to code orange along the coast, as [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/liveblog/vandaag-voorproefje-morgen-echt-stormweer-weervrouw-jacotte-~1792663198457/) reports.

"Today we get a preview, but the fiercest rain and wind is for tomorrow," VRT weather presenter Jacotte Brokken said, as reported by VRT NWS. "Inland, code yellow applies tomorrow with gusts up to 90 km/h. At the coast, code orange applies with gusts up to 100 km/h. Locally it could be even a little more."

## What the Warnings Say

The KMI's wind warning runs from Friday midday to 01:00 on Saturday, and its rain warning from 05:00 Friday to 05:00 Saturday. As [L'Avenir](https://www.lavenir.net/actu/societe/meteo/2026/10/22/tempete-austen-des-rafales-de-vent-jusqua-100-kmh-alerte-orange-a-la-cote-et-jaune-dans-tout-le-pays-ce-vendredi-video-HLNWDW4YGVDYFD7NBL3IUZQODA/) reports, citing the institute directly, "at sea, the wind will reach storm force (9 Beaufort) to severe storm force (10 Beaufort), from the southwest then veering to the west to west-northwest." Gusts inland are forecast at 80 to 90 km/h, and at the coast in excess of 100 km/h.

A parallel rain warning forecasts precipitation of 25 to 50 mm in 24 hours as successive rain zones linked to the storm depression cross the country. KMI meteorologist David Dehenauw has said gusts exceeding 100 km/h are not excluded, [Mancho](https://mancho.be/2026/10/22/kmi-kondigt-code-geel-en-oranje-aan-de-kust-af-wegens-storm-austen-dit-staat-ons-te-wachten/) reports, noting that the KMI's own forecast puts gusts at "80 to 100 km/h, and possibly more at the coast."

The warnings are not cause for alarm everywhere. Farid El Mokaddem, the weather specialist at L'Avenir, cautioned that this is far from a worst-case scenario. "We are a long way from the storm of the century, even if we should remain cautious because of trees that are still laden and sometimes fragile," he said, adding that rainfall would be more moderate than the headline figures suggest — on average 5 to 20 litres per square metre, reaching 30 to 35 in Flanders and the north.

## A Cross-Border Storm

Austen is the first named storm of the 2026/27 season and was named by the UK Met Office in conjunction with Met Éireann and KNMI, the Irish and Dutch meteorological services. As [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/uk-news/2026/oct/20/storm-austen-named-uk-severe-weather-warning) reported, the name honours Austen Nagle, the first director of the Irish service, and the season's alphabetical list runs from Austen through Boelo, Chloe and Dano to Wende. The system is an Atlantic low-pressure area deepening rapidly as it is energised by the jet stream, tracking west to east across north-western Europe.

"While the exact track and position of Storm Austen is still being determined, what's clear is that we have an impactful period of weather on the way," Met Office deputy chief forecaster Tom Crabtree said. Forecasters on both sides of the Channel stress that the storm's precise path and peak intensity remain under review.

## Preventive Shutdowns Across the Country

Authorities have moved quickly to limit exposure. Belgium's FPS Interior activated the 1722 emergency number on Wednesday for storm and flood assistance, with 112 reserved for life-threatening situations. Brussels is closing its parks and green spaces, including the Sonian Forest, from 13:00 Friday; the city's environment agency, Leefmilieu Brussel, warned that climate change is making trees more vulnerable to extreme weather and raising the risk of damage in high winds.

Coastal Oostende has shut its Oktoberfoor fair and closed Sportpark De Schorre, the groynes and the pier, while the Dag van de Jeugdbeweging (Day of Youth Movements) events in Leuven and Tienen have been cancelled outright. "We don't want to take any risk," youth aldermen said. Numerous other cities, including Hasselt, Brugge and Roeselare, have moved youth-day activities indoors.

Rail operator NMBS and infrastructure manager Infrabel will impose temporary speed limits on the Brugge-coast and De Panne-Lichtervelde lines from 15:00 Friday, with possible transfers at Brugge or Lichtervelde. The construction federation Embuild urged firms to secure scaffolding, cranes and loose materials before the wind picks up, warning that timely action limits the risk of accidents and damage.

Across the border, regional rail in France's Hauts-de-France has been suspended until Saturday midday, with crisis cells activated in coastal communes and residents advised to avoid beaches, parks and forests.

## What to Watch

The core advice is familiar: secure loose objects on balconies and in gardens, bring in trampolines and garden furniture, close windows and doors, and park vehicles away from trees and scaffolding. Brussels fire brigade spokesman Walter Derieuw also urged residents to clear gutters and drains where it is safe to do so.

Key uncertainties remain. The KMI could escalate additional inland provinces to orange — as VRT NWS reported, Oost-Vlaanderen, West-Vlaanderen and Henegouwen had by Thursday afternoon been raised to code orange, beyond the coast alone — and the actual peak gusts recorded at the coast on Friday evening will reveal whether forecasts held. With unsettled weather expected to persist until Tuesday, the question is whether park closures and rail restrictions will need to be extended beyond Saturday.

For now, Belgium's message is one of measured preparation: a serious autumn storm, handled with caution rather than alarm.
