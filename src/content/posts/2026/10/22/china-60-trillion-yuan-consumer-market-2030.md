# China Targets 60 Trillion Yuan Consumer Market by 2030

China's consumer market is expanding and upgrading toward a scale of roughly 60 trillion yuan (about $8.4 trillion), according to [a People's Daily analysis](http://finance.people.com.cn/n1/2026/1022/c1004-40819243.html) republished from the Securities Daily on 22 October. The target, set in the 15th Five-Year Plan for Expanding Consumption approved by the State Council in July, marks the first national-level five-year special plan devoted to consumption in the history of the People's Republic.

Reaching 60 trillion yuan by 2030 would require adding roughly 10 trillion yuan over five years, from a 2025 baseline of 50.12 trillion yuan in total retail sales of consumer goods — an implicit annual growth rate of about 4%.

## A Plan Built Around Services and Upgraded Goods

The consumption plan, coordinated by the National Development and Reform Commission and the Ministry of Commerce, deploys 28 key task measures across six areas: improving service consumption, upgrading goods consumption, cultivating new consumption formats and scenarios, enhancing consumer spending capacity, optimizing the consumption environment, and refining the institutional mechanisms that promote consumption.

As [21st Century Business Herald reported](https://m.21jingji.com/article/20260713/herald/179235f36766ee18109e7543c6f85dd2.html) when the plan was released, service consumption sits at its core, spanning seven fields including life services, elderly care, childcare, culture and tourism, health, sports and education. The "one old, one young" demographic — retirees and children — is a priority, while "AI plus consumption" features prominently through digital products such as smart phones, wearables and robots. Housing was classified for the first time under big-ticket durable goods consumption, a symbolic shift that links property-market stability to consumer confidence.

"Achieving this goal will mean consumption's pulling power on growth is further strengthened and the domestic-demand-led development pattern becomes more solid," Fu Yifu, a special researcher at Sushang Bank, told the 21st Century Business Herald.

## The Supermarket Cold Case Tells the Story

Rather than leading with macro statistics, the Securities Daily analysis frames the shift through everyday consumer behavior. In the chilled section, young shoppers now check ingredient lists and shelf lives before price; small-pack prepared meals, low-alcohol fruit wine and sugar-free teas occupy prominent shelf space.

"From comparing price to checking ingredients, from choosing brands to checking traceability — subtle changes in consumer habits reflect the evolution trend of the consumer market," the article noted.

The report uses four industry tracks as proxies for the transformation: dairy (a livelihood necessity), chain tea and dining (high-frequency service), alcohol (traditional brewing) and prepared dishes (food industrialization). Each faces the same three challenges — using standards to protect quality and safety, using efficiency to convert supply-chain capability into cost and delivery advantages, and using professionalism to open new consumption space.

## Standards Tighten and Supply Chains Move to the Forefront

Regulation has been tightening across these sectors. In February, the National Health Commission released a draft national food safety standard for prepared dishes for public comment, specifying that the category excludes staple foods, fresh-cut vegetables, ready-to-eat foods and central-kitchen dishes, while banning preservatives and capping shelf life at 12 months. A guiding opinion on upgrading the brewing industry through 2030 positions alcohol as a "historical classic industry" with a development pattern of billion-yuan parks, hundred-billion-yuan clusters and a trillion-yuan industry. And in July 2025, the Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Affairs issued measures to support dairy producers through a shift toward higher quality and reduced volume.

Supply chains are no longer background costs but core competitiveness. Mixue Bingcheng operates five domestic production bases with combined annual capacity of 1.65 million tons, and in 2025 more than 97% of its revenue came from selling goods and equipment to franchisees. Guming Holdings runs 24 warehouses, with about 98% of its stores able to receive two-day-one-delivery cold-chain distribution in 2025. Luckin Coffee sold 25 million cups on a single day this year during the "first cup of autumn" social media surge, several times its normal volume at some outlets.

"This tests exactly raw-material supply, warehouse delivery frequency, store standardization and replenishment systems," Chen Jingjing, a strategic brand expert at Crystal Brand Consulting, told the Securities Daily. "The advantages of scaled brands with supply-chain and organizational capabilities are further highlighted."

## New Scenarios: Precision Nutrition and Prepared Dishes

Competition is increasingly about matching products to specific life scenarios rather than simply opening more stores. In dairy, Yili is developing personalized formulas for older people, infants and athletes, while Mengniu's Milk Cube project plans three product directions covering milk protein, cheese fat and cheese.

"The core destination of the industry's deep cultivation of precision nutrition is the health needs of all people; the core direction of the dairy upgrade is shifting from 'let more people drink milk' to 'more nutrition absorbed' and precision nutrition for all ages," Song Liang, head of the expert group at the China State Farm Dairy Alliance, told the Securities Daily.

Prepared dishes, meanwhile, are extending into festival family banquets, single-person meals, group catering and school meals. Banning preservatives, capping shelf life and requiring full cold-chain temperature control raise compliance costs, but they also return competition to quality and scenario fit. Industry consensus centers on three directions: mandatory disclosure of prepared-dish usage to protect consumers' right to know, nutrition optimization to combine taste with health, and improved regulation.

## What's Next

The plan's ambitions extend to consumer capacity as well. Its measures include gradually raising minimum wages, increasing property income, raising basic pensions and medical insurance subsidies, expanding unemployment and work-injury insurance coverage, and promoting long-term care insurance. It also calls for fully implementing labor law protections on rest and leave, revising paid annual leave regulations and encouraging flexible off-peak holidays — measures that could release time for tourism and leisure spending.

"This shift from policy-driven to institution-driven is a fundamental change in the logic of promoting consumption," Chen Lifen of the State Council Development Research Center's Institute of Market Economy told CCTV News, as cited by Hubei Daily.

The headline 60 trillion yuan target is achievable but far from guaranteed. Restaurant revenue grew 2.8% in the first half of 2026, slowing from 4.2% in the first quarter, and the National Bureau of Statistics' new combined goods-and-services retail indicator rose just 2.8% year on year from January to May. Against that backdrop, the push toward roughly 4% annual growth by 2030 will depend less on grand announcements than on whether the standards, supply chains and scenarios the plan describes can translate into spending that consumers actually make.

The People's Daily analysis concludes that the key to future competition "is not who has broader channels or more stores, but who can turn standards into a quality baseline, efficiency into cost and delivery capability, and professionalism into product innovation and scenario fit."
