# Brussels Reaches Deal to Halve 2027 Deficit

The Brussels regional government reached agreement on its 2027 budget late on Sunday, concluding a difficult two-day conclave and pledging to cut the capital region's deficit to 719 million euros. Minister-President Boris Dillies (MR) presented the deal to the Brussels Parliament on Monday, framing it as a decisive step toward balancing the books by 2029.

"We started from a deficit of more than 1.3 billion euros. Today we have halved that, to 719 million euros," Dillies said in parliament, according to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/en/2026/10/05/brussels-government-reaches-agreement-on-2027-budget/). "Everyone has had to make an effort."

## Context: A Region Under Pressure

The agreement caps a politically fraught stretch for the Brussels-Capital Region, whose 2025 budget ended 1.6 billion euros in the red. The government-Dillies coalition - spanning MR, PS, Les Engages, Groen, Anders, Vooruit and CD&V - was only formed in February 2026, an extraordinary 613 days after the June 2024 regional elections.

More than a quarter of the regional budget flows to mobility and public transport, making the operator MIVB/STIB a central target in the talks. Federal budget cuts have also loomed over the region's finances, adding urgency to the conclave that began at the Iris Tower on 1 October and stretched across the weekend.

## Key Developments: Where the Money Goes

The deal combines deep cuts to the civil service with targeted spending on welfare, security and housing. The administration will shed staff and merge services, with vacancies no longer automatically filled and workers redeployed through internal mobility. According to [BRUZZ](https://www.bruzz.be/actua/politiek/dit-staat-het-brussels-begrotingsakkoord-2026-10-05), the planned hiring freeze is expected to yield up to 70 million euros, while a broader overhaul aims to save 74 million euros a year on personnel and 39 million euros on operating costs.

On taxes, Dillies insisted the burden would not fall across the board on residents. Supplementary personal income tax will fall by one percentage point, and the BeHome premium - which offsets higher property tax for resident homeowners - will double. Child benefits and service vouchers remain untouched, unlike in Flanders.

Mobility proved the hardest fight. The government had agreed during coalition formation to raise MIVB fare revenue, but the standard ticket price will only be indexed, rising from 2.40 to 2.50 euros from February. Favourable 12-euro annual subscriptions for young people and seniors are retained at the PS's request. "We have finally decided not to implement the planned fare increase," Finance Minister Dirk De Smedt (Anders) told VRT. "Revenue was lower than anticipated. We have compensated for this by cutting operating expenditure."

The region also plans a road vignette agreed with Belgium's other regions, expected to raise 30 million euros, alongside a road tax reform that will exempt small city cars and zero-emission vehicles from the second half of 2027 while taxing large, heavy vehicles more heavily.

Social spending was bolstered. More than 1 million euros goes to prevention, including cancer screening and a heatwave plan; 1.8 million euros funds shelter for homeless families through New Samusocial, covering 140 places; and 6.3 million euros targets addiction prevention. Municipalities and local social service centres (OCMWs) receive around 15 million euros extra. "No child should sleep on the streets in Brussels," Dillies said.

On housing, the deal allocates 104 million euros for more than 1,000 homes sold at moderate prices through Citydev.brussels, and introduces interest-free energy and renovation loans from spring 2027, backed by 66 million euros. Security and cleanliness measures include 5 million euros around stations, a dedicated regional drugs commissioner, 10 million euros for fire services and 92 new recruits.

## Analysis: A Coalition Balancing Act

The conclave laid bare tensions inside the coalition. A source told [BRUZZ](https://www.bruzz.be/actua/politiek/clash-tussen-van-den-brandt-en-de-smedt-zet-begrotingsconclaaf-onder-druk-2026-10-04) that talks "exploded over the MIVB," pitting De Smedt against Mobility Minister Elke Van den Brandt (Groen). At issue was whether commercial revenue beyond the operator's ticket income would flow to the budget or stay with the operator.

That dispute reflects a structural problem: the MIVB's coverage ratio - the share of operating costs it self-finances - sits just under 30 percent, and liberals want it closer to 50 percent, where it stood 15 years ago. "De Smedt wants the commercial extras on top of MIVB revenue to flow to the budget, but Van den Brandt wants to hold onto that money," a source said.

For the two smallest Flemish parties in the coalition, Anders and Groen, the Brussels outcome carries outsized political weight, with each seeking a compelling record ahead of future elections. De Smedt's camp claims the deal marks a return to fiscal discipline; the PS framed it as "responsible," with targeted investments protecting the vulnerable.

## What's Next

Even with the agreement in hand, questions remain. Funding for the Brussels Regional Housing Company (BGHM) for 2027 is still unclear, and the region has not yet detailed which MIVB investments will be trimmed. Talks over the budgets for the community commissions covering health and homelessness are still to come, and federal savings could further reshape the picture.

For now, Dillies has delivered the deal his government needed ahead of his "State of the Union" address. The harder work - turning these promises into a balanced budget by 2029 - lies ahead.
