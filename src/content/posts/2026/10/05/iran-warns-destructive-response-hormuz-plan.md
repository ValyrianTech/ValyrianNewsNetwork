# Iran Warns Enemy Action to Meet More Destructive Response

Iran's foreign minister warned Sunday that any renewed military action by the country's adversaries would be met with a "more destructive" response than in the past, pairing the threat with a fresh diplomatic offer to reopen the Strait of Hormuz — a vital global energy chokepoint that has been disrupted for months.

Abbas Araghchi issued the warning in Tehran during a meeting with ambassadors, chargé d'affaires and heads of foreign and international missions, according to [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/world/20261004/0733bb5345c444b3851bbfb6cbe317d0/c.html). He said Iran remains willing to pursue a "just and dignified" solution through diplomacy, but that its willingness to negotiate should not be mistaken for weakness.

## A Dual Message: Diplomacy and Deterrence

In remarks carried by Iran's [West Asia News Agency (WANA)](https://wanaen.com/araghchi-iran-will-respond-more-forcefully-if-enemies-choose-military-path/), Araghchi said that "if the enemies once again choose the path of military confrontation and war, Iran's response will be much stronger and more decisive than in the past."

He framed the position as a deliberate balance: "It became clear to everyone at this meeting that the Islamic Republic of Iran is serious about diplomacy, just as it is serious and determined in defending itself."

The foreign minister also dismissed the prospect of economic pressure altering Tehran's stance. New US sanctions, he said, are "destined to be futile," arguing there is "no military solution or solution based on additional sanctions" — only one "based on negotiations, justice, fairness and diplomatic dignity."

## The Seven-Day Plan for the Strait of Hormuz

At the center of Iran's diplomatic track is a proposal Araghchi first outlined at the United Nations in late September. Under the [seven-day plan](https://wanaen.com/araghchi-outlines-irans-seven-day-plan-to-reopen-the-strait-of-hormuz/), the clock would start once the United States accepts Iran's conditions: implementation would take four to five days, the Strait would reopen on the sixth day, and the United States would enter a final agreement on the seventh.

"If the US is serious enough, this will happen, and the Strait of Hormuz will be re-opened," Araghchi said. He added that Iran conveyed the proposal to Washington through a Qatari mediator and was awaiting a formal response.

According to [PressTV](https://www.presstv.co.uk/Detail/2026/09/29/777233/Araghchi-No-flexibility-Iran-position-nuclear-issue-Strait-Hormuz-reopening), an official US reply was expected to be relayed through intermediaries around Tuesday, October 6. PressTV referred to the initiative as a "seven-point plan," while Xinhua and WANA describe it as a "seven-day plan" — a discrepancy that may reflect different descriptions of the same proposal.

## Why the Strait Matters

Before the war that began on February 28, 2026, the Strait of Hormuz handled roughly 20% of the world's daily crude oil and liquefied natural gas supply and about 125 large commercial vessels a day, according to [Iran International](https://www.iranintl.com/en/liveblog/202610033293). Its disruption has rippled through global energy markets, with Brent crude trading above $100 a barrel.

The economic stakes extend well beyond the region. The G7 has agreed to coordinate the release of 100 million barrels of oil and fuel products through the International Energy Agency over four months, while OPEC+ kept its November output targets unchanged.

## Context: A War and a Fragile Memorandum

The current standoff traces back to a US-Israeli military campaign against Iran that began on February 28, 2026. Iran characterizes the campaign as "illegal and unjust aggression," citing civilian casualties and the assassination of its Leader and senior officials.

The two sides signed the Islamabad Memorandum of Understanding on June 17, 2026, mediated by Pakistan with Qatari support. Under paragraph 5, Iran was to facilitate safe vessel movement. Maritime traffic reportedly recovered to about 60% of pre-war levels within two weeks before Iran alleges the US violated the agreement.

On the nuclear question, Araghchi insisted Tehran has made no concessions, describing allegations that Iran is near acquiring a weapon as "a big lie first concocted by Israel in 2003." US President Donald Trump, meanwhile, has publicly denied offering Iran sanctions relief or the release of frozen funds, writing on Truth Social: "This is untrue. I offered them NOTHING."

## Regional Escalation and European Pressure

The diplomatic maneuvering is unfolding against a backdrop of rising maritime and regional tensions. The UK Maritime Trade Operations agency has reported at least one attack a day in the Strait of Hormuz or Gulf of Aden since October 2. Separately, Yemen's Saudi-backed government has launched a major military campaign against the Iran-backed Houthis.

Araghchi also pressed European capitals to account for their alleged role in the US-Israeli strikes, referencing NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte's acknowledgment that European bases were used for the operations. "Iran is still waiting for European countries to answer why they had played an effective role in the war," he said.

He further rejected US assertions that the Iranian delegation had been expelled from the United States, saying the delegation traveled on a prearranged schedule and describing Washington's conduct as "childish and contemptible."

## What to Watch

The immediate focus is Washington's response to Iran's seven-day proposal, expected within days. Whether the two sides can bridge their competing conditions — and whether the Strait of Hormuz reopens as envisioned — remains highly uncertain given the deep mutual distrust and ongoing maritime attacks. For global energy markets and regional stability, the coming week could prove decisive.
