# Beijing E-Town Rolls Out AI Talent Plan

Beijing's Economic-Technological Development Area (E-Town), also known as Yizhuang, has released an eight-point policy package to cultivate what it calls "AI native talent" — building a full-chain system that spans school interns to world-class scientists, according to [Guangming Daily](http://edu.people.com.cn/n1/2026/1004/c1006-40809182.html), which published the original report; it was later republished on People.com.cn's education channel. The "AI Talent Eight Measures" (AI人才八条) were announced at the 2026 Beijing Yizhuang Talent Development Conference and focus on three dimensions: AI entrepreneurship, youth employment, and ecosystem cultivation.

## A Strategic Bet on an All-Domain AI City

The policy is a targeted component of E-Town's broader bid to become an "All-Domain AI City." In early 2026, the district published a two-year implementation plan, reported by the [Beijing municipal government](https://www.beijing.gov.cn/ywdt/gzdt/202602/t20260203_4487341.html), setting out goals for end-2027: at least 10 flagship smart-scene complexes, 100 vertical AI models deployed, 1,000 core AI companies, and 10,000 super individuals or independent developers.

That ambition rests on an existing base. The district now hosts more than 800 core AI-industry companies with an industry scale exceeding RMB 100 billion (about USD 14 billion), per the region's [official announcement](https://kfqgw.beijing.gov.cn/ywdt/gzdt/202608/t20260819_4828836.html). "Talent has always been the most solid foundation for the development of the economic development zone," said Shi Wei, deputy secretary of the E-Town Working Committee, noting the district's talent pool is near 400,000 with a talent contribution rate of 69.3%.

## Targeting Everyone from Scientists to 'Super Individuals'

The eight measures deliberately break from the traditional "assess once, benefit for life" approach. Instead, they adopt a dynamic mechanism described as "mature one, assess one, support one" — embedding support into each stage of a company's journey from technical validation to industrialization, as [China News Service](https://www.chinanews.com.cn/cj/2026/08-18/10679928.shtml) reported.

For industry elite talent, support reaches up to RMB 5 million (about USD 700,000), alongside access to compute clusters, custom R&D space, and industrial-scenario matching. For so-called super individuals — one-person companies (OPCs) operating with a light organizational footprint — the policy offers up to RMB 500,000 individually and creates a review committee that includes OPC representatives, gauging "real creative ability, closed-loop delivery capability, and development potential."

The package also introduces Beijing's first city-wide AI-specific internship support measure. It targets competition winners, top-university AI students, those with deep large-model R&D experience, and — notably — liberal-arts graduates skilled in prompt engineering. It also backs the cultivation of Forward Deployed Engineers (FDEs) through school-enterprise collaboration, and offers up to RMB 1 million for qualifying AI competitions, hackathons, and developer conferences. [Science and Technology Daily](https://www.stdaily.com/web/gdxw/2026-08/19/content_566168.html) reported the measures as part of a push to let "every kind of AI creativity find room to grow" in Yizhuang.

## The 'Last Mile' Problem Behind the Policy

The emphasis on FDEs reflects a national diagnosis. In August 2026, MIIT issued a notice encouraging AI application-service providers to build FDE teams that "embed at user sites and ensure scenario deployment." The role originates from Palantir's internal "Delta" engineers, paired with deployment strategists, an approach analyzed in depth by [Guancha](https://user.guancha.cn/main/content?id=1746680), which noted that AI's bottleneck has shifted from models to people.

The core challenge is commercialization. A Beijing Time feature on the district's Modu OPC Community — opened in April 2026 with the open-source community WaytoAGI as operator — captured the tension bluntly. "What these OPCs value most is not rent or utilities discounts, but how to get more orders and become profitable," said Zhou Peng, the community's operations lead, in the [Beijing Time report](https://item.btime.com/43g3t6nk5g58i1ot2sk3nogmt1j). The community has seen about 20 companies move in and roughly 300 applications, with a goal of nurturing 10,000 independent developers and 100 super-individual companies within two years.

That gap explains why the eight measures pair cash support with less visible infrastructure: compute vouchers, research space, scenario matching, and a housing scheme that lets new graduates and post-docs settle with a "stability package." The district is also optimizing its "half-hour commute life circle" to make Yizhuang livable for a young, mobile workforce.

## Analysis: Subsidies Alone Won't Close the Loop

E-Town's framework is notable less for its headline numbers than for its structural logic. By shifting from one-time evaluation to continuous support, the district is effectively treating talent policy as venture capital — placing many small bets, tracking growth, and rewarding results. The explicit inclusion of OPC representatives in evaluation committees, and experiments with social-security continuation and entrepreneurship insurance, signal an attempt to de-risk the jump from employment to solo entrepreneurship.

Yet local reporting suggests the decisive test is commercial, not residential. Rent-free offices and compute discounts draw founders in, but orders keep them there. The Modu community's "order-dispatch platform" — which lets companies post tasks that OPCs can claim — is an attempt to manufacture the demand that subsidies cannot. Zhou's Lego-brick metaphor captures the wager: individual firms that can snap together to win contracts larger than any one-person shop could handle alone.

The FDE push carries its own ambition and its own uncertainty. It aligns Beijing with a national effort led by MIIT, and with cities such as Shanghai, which has moved fastest on FDE training. But as the Guancha analysis cautioned, FDE is a gap-filling role — and the gap may narrow if models become capable of deploying themselves. The bet rests on the last mile persisting for years.

## What to Watch

E-Town has positioned itself as a testbed for a distinctly Chinese approach to building an AI talent ecosystem: coordinated policy clusters (the district also released China's first AI4Chip policy and a "token economy" measure in 2026), open collaboration, and a heavy emphasis on youth. The benchmarks set for 2027 — 1,000 core AI companies and 10,000 super individuals — will be the clearest signals of whether the model works.

The harder questions remain open. Can a subsidy-rich district convert enthusiasm into sustainable revenue for one-person firms? Will the FDE role outlast the technology gap it was designed to bridge? And as Beijing, Shanghai, Shenzhen, and others race for the same talent, whether "native talent" cultivation can outpace simple recruitment is the experiment worth watching.
