# China's Holiday Midpoint: 300M Trips a Day Test Economy

At the midpoint of China's 2026 National Day holiday, state media is framing a vast movement of people as evidence of the country's economic resilience. According to [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/politics/20261005/1fd0f2ad09634d3696cd6d3887b2446f/c.html), cross-region person-trips exceeded 300 million for three consecutive days from October 1 to 3, while China Railway set a single-day record of 25.204 million passengers on the holiday's opening day.

The figures anchor an optimistic portrait of a "moving China" published Monday, but the article also acknowledges pockets of friction — from electric-vehicle charging queues to uneven spending in smaller destinations — that complicate the narrative of effortless growth.

## Travel at Record Scale

The Ministry of Transport reported daily cross-region travel of 329 million, 306 million, and 304 million trips across the first three days of the holiday. The opening-day total alone approached the population of the United States, a comparison Chinese outlets have used to convey the sheer scale of the movement.

The travel surge rests on a decade of infrastructure investment. Xinhua reported that as of the end of 2025, China's national comprehensive transport network exceeded 6 million kilometers, with high-speed rail, expressways, airports, and ports forming a network that spans both cities and rural areas. Several new lines opened just before the holiday, including the Xi'an–Kangyang high-speed railway, the Harbin–Yichun line — China's northernmost high-speed railway — and the Xiong'an–Shangqiu section of the Beijing–Hong Kong corridor.

## Consumption Signals and Policy Support

Holiday spending offered the clearest evidence the state media cited for economic momentum. Ministry of Commerce big-data monitoring of 78 key pedestrian streets and business districts showed foot traffic up 3.4% and turnover up 5.3% year-on-year for October 1 to 3. A consumer goods trade-in program drove 19.63 billion yuan (about $2.7 billion) in sales, benefiting 3.483 million person-times, Xinhua reported.

Those policy-driven gains are central to Beijing's strategy of shoring up domestic demand. Yet the broader picture is more mixed. [PwC's China Economic Quarterly](https://www.pwccn.com/en/research-and-insights/china-economic-quarterly-q3-2026.html) notes that China's economy grew 4.7% in the first half of 2026 — within the government's target — but with "uneven" momentum: strong in exports and technology, and weaker in consumption and property.

[KPMG's China Economic Monitor](https://kpmg.com/cn/en/insights/2026/08/china-economic-monitor-q3-2026.html) sharpens that point, reporting that real GDP growth eased to 4.3% in the second quarter, down 0.7 percentage points from the first, and describing a widening "K-shaped divergence" in which emerging industries and higher-tier cities outperform traditional sectors and lower-tier cities.

The holiday data, in other words, captures a real surge in activity — but against a backdrop of structural divergence that a single festive week cannot resolve.

## County Tourism and the Spending Gap

One of the more candid threads in the Xinhua piece concerns county-level tourism. While Meituan data showed homestay bookings in multiple county destinations more than doubling year-on-year in the first three days, some smaller destinations reported "high foot traffic, low spending."

Yi Shaohua, director of the market circulation and consumption research office at the Chinese Academy of Social Sciences, explained the gap in the Xinhua account. "In the past, tourists were satisfied with checking in and taking photos; now consumers value quality, features, and value-for-money," Yi said. "Some counties relied only on a one-time ticket economy with short industry chains, so naturally it's hard to retain consumption."

The trend is not confined to official narratives. [China Youth Daily](https://m.cyol.com/gb/news/articles/2026-10/01/content_mOya7VcVLZ.html) — citing a China Tourism Association report — noted that county-level off-site consumption grew 14.1% year-on-year in the first half of 2026, as travelers increasingly bypass marquee attractions for smaller towns.

## Charging Anxiety Meets Green Transition

The holiday also stress-tested China's electric-vehicle ecosystem. The Ministry of Transport expects highway EV travel during the holiday to reach 1.8 times normal levels. Vice Minister Li Yang said about 11,000 high-power charging facilities were added at service areas between January and August alone, bringing the cumulative total to roughly 89,000.

Even so, Xinhua acknowledged that charging queues formed at some service areas, with drivers waiting hours to recharge. Operators responded with mobile emergency chargers and guidance to charge to 80% and shift off-peak — practical fixes for a problem that analysts say will require sustained investment in grid and technology.

## What to Watch

The 2026 holiday doubles as a symbolic marker: it is the first year of the 15th Five-Year Plan and the 90th anniversary of the Long March victory, themes that lent a patriotic tint to red-tourism itineraries. Dai Bin, president of the China Tourism Academy, said the pull toward revolutionary landmarks reflects "contemporary people's emotional identification with national history and revolutionary tradition."

For economists, the more telling question is whether holiday momentum carries into the fourth quarter. KPMG notes that the July Politburo meeting reaffirmed expanding domestic demand as the government's top H2 priority, with the 15th Five-Year Plan for Expanding Consumption setting a target of roughly 60 trillion yuan in total retail sales of consumer goods by 2030.

China's holiday snapshot shows a country that can still move hundreds of millions of people with remarkable efficiency — and one that is betting that the spending they generate can help steady a growth model in transition.
