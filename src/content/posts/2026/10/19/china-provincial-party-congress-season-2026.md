# China Enters Provincial Party Congress Season

China has formally entered its provincial party congress season, after the Communist Party of China's Anhui Provincial 12th Congress opened in Hefei on the morning of 19 October 2026, according to [The Paper](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_34278931). The Anhui gathering marks the start of a nationwide wave of once-every-five-years meetings that will reshape provincial leadership bodies and set policy priorities for the years ahead.

## Context

Provincial party congresses are a fixed feature of China's political calendar. Held once every five years, each congress reviews the work of the outgoing provincial committee and its discipline inspection commission, then elects new provincial party committees and discipline inspection commissions. Those bodies in turn select provincial standing committees and secretaries, determining who will lead each province through the next five-year cycle.

This year's round of congresses carries added weight because it precedes national-level political cycles. Provincial gatherings typically establish local leadership slates and align provincial priorities with central direction ahead of national party congresses. The previous national congress was held in 2022, with the next expected around 2027.

## Key developments

The Paper reported that Anhui's congress opened on 19 October, citing the province's official "Anhui News Live" social media account. A preparatory meeting was held the previous afternoon, 18 October, at the Anhui Grand Theater, where delegates approved a 15-member credentials review committee, an 88-member presidium, and Shan Xiangqian as secretary-general of the congress.

The Anhui congress agenda includes hearing and reviewing the report of the 11th Anhui Provincial Committee and the work report of the 11th Anhui Provincial Discipline Inspection Commission, followed by the election of the 12th Anhui Provincial Committee and the 12th Anhui Provincial Discipline Inspection Commission.

Neighboring provinces are following closely. On 18 October, the 12th plenary session of the 11th Henan Provincial Committee, meeting in Zhengzhou, decided that the Henan Provincial 12th Congress will convene in Zhengzhou on 20 October. On the same day, the 13th plenary session of the 12th Shanxi Provincial Committee, meeting in Taiyuan, set the Shanxi Provincial 13th Congress for 20 to 23 October in Taiyuan.

## Analysis

The rapid sequencing of Anhui, Henan, and Shanxi reflects the coordinated scheduling typical of provincial congress cycles, in which provinces announce dates in close succession after their outgoing committees conclude final plenary sessions. Each congress serves a dual function: electing a new leadership team and formally reviewing the record of the outgoing one, a structure that combines personnel renewal with institutional accountability.

The emphasis on discipline inspection commission elections also fits a broader pattern. In recent years, Chinese official sources have stressed party discipline and the supervision of senior officials, and the congress structure embeds that oversight function directly into provincial leadership transitions. The outcome of these elections will signal how provincial anti-corruption and supervision priorities are configured for the coming five-year term.

Beyond personnel, the congresses are expected to align provincial agendas with national planning priorities. The current period corresponds to the 15th Five-Year Plan era covering 2026 to 2030, and provincial party work in this cycle has been framed around implementing decisions from the fourth plenum of the 20th Central Committee.

## What's next

The Henan and Shanxi congresses convene on 20 October, with Shanxi's running through 23 October. Further provinces are expected to announce and hold their own new-term congresses over the remainder of the second half of 2026. The key things to watch will be the composition of new provincial standing committees, the identity of provincial party secretaries, and how each province frames its economic and social priorities for the next five years.
