# China's Yuan Hits Strongest Level Since July 2022

The Chinese yuan strengthened to its highest level against the US dollar since early July 2022 on Monday, extending a multi-week rally that analysts attribute primarily to China's large trade surplus and a surge in exporter foreign-exchange settlement. The onshore spot rate opened at 6.6853, surpassed the prior trading day's high of 6.6849, and touched an intraday high of 6.6831, according to [The Paper](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_34278955), the Shanghai-based outlet that first reported the figures.

The offshore yuan, which more closely reflects international investor sentiment, rose to an intraday high of 6.6825, breaking the previous day's high of 6.6830. The People's Bank of China set the daily central parity rate at 6.7208, an appreciation of 12 basis points from the prior fixing.

## Context: A Year of Steady Appreciation

The move is the latest milestone in a broad strengthening that has run through 2026. Year to date, the central parity rate has appreciated 4.38 percent, the onshore spot rate 4.35 percent, and the offshore rate more than 4.1 percent, The Paper reported. The rally has repeatedly set multi-year highs in October.

On October 9, the onshore spot rate closed at 6.6921, up 93 pips on the day — the highest close since June 29, 2022, a more than four-year high, as [Eastmoney](https://finance.eastmoney.com/a/202610093891507474.html) reported, citing The Paper. A week later, on October 16, the onshore rate broke through the 6.69 level intraday to a high of 6.6888, its strongest since early July 2022, with the central parity at 6.7220. Monday's session extended that trend.

## What's Driving the Rally

The conventional playbook says a widening interest-rate gap between China and the United States should pressure the yuan weaker: higher US rates tend to pull capital toward dollar assets. That relationship has broken down in this cycle.

Since September, rising Middle East tensions pushed up oil prices, and the Federal Reserve kept releasing hawkish signals, lifting both the dollar index and US Treasury yields. The China-US interest-rate spread widened further — yet the yuan kept rising. According to a research note from CITIC Securities, as quoted by The Paper, the principal engine is trade.

"The main driver of this round of yuan appreciation is the trade surplus and FX settlement," the brokerage wrote, according to the report. CITIC explained that after market expectations for the yuan shifted from depreciation to appreciation at the end of 2025, export enterprises stepped up their conversion of foreign-currency earnings into yuan. That shift weakened the historical link between the exchange rate and the China-US rate spread, leaving the trade surplus to dominate.

The scale of that surplus is considerable. China ran a record trade surplus in 2025, reportedly surpassing US$1 trillion on an annual basis for the first time, a structural surplus that underpins persistent demand for the yuan and fuels exporter settlement activity.

CITIC also addressed a common worry: that a decline in AI-related exports could undermine the yuan next year. "Trade surplus growth this year has all come from non-AI goods," the brokerage noted, adding that "as long as global manufacturing sentiment does not change markedly, the logic of the trade surplus supporting the yuan will not change."

## The Central Bank's Stance

The appreciation has unfolded against a clear policy backdrop. On October 8, the People's Bank of China issued a statement reaffirming its exchange-rate framework and explicitly ruling out any strategy of deliberate weakening.

"China implements a managed floating exchange rate regime based on market supply and demand, adjusted with reference to a basket of currencies, and insists on letting the market play a decisive role in exchange rate formation," the central bank said, as reported by Eastmoney. "China has no need and no intention to gain trade competitive advantage through currency depreciation, and never engages in competitive devaluation."

The statement matters because it signals that Beijing is comfortable with a stronger yuan and does not view appreciation as a threat to exports requiring intervention. Under China's managed float, the central bank sets a daily central parity rate around which the onshore spot rate may trade within a band of roughly plus or minus 2 percent.

## Analysis: A Structural Shift

The significance of this rally lies less in the daily numbers than in what it reveals about how the yuan is now priced. For years, the China-US rate differential was the dominant variable for currency watchers. The current cycle suggests that trade flows — and the behavior of exporters sitting on dollar earnings — have become the more powerful force.

That has implications on several fronts. A stronger yuan modestly reduces the cost of imports and can support domestic purchasing power, a helpful tailwind for an economy seeking steady growth. It also complicates the picture for exporters, though the PBOC's stance suggests policymakers are not treating a firm currency as a problem to be solved. And it complicates the calculus for global investors, who must weigh a widening rate gap against a currency that keeps climbing anyway.

The durability of the trend likely hinges on two factors: whether China's trade surplus remains robust, and whether global manufacturing sentiment holds up. CITIC's view is that both remain supportive. A sharp slowdown in global demand, or a sudden reversal in exporter settlement behavior, would be the key risks to watch.

## What's Next

For now, the momentum is intact. Monday's session marked the strongest yuan level in more than four years, and the drivers identified by analysts — a large trade surplus, heavy exporter settlement, and a central bank comfortable with appreciation — show no signs of fading. Traders will be watching the daily central parity fixings and the pace of settlement flows for clues about whether the rally has further to run, while keeping an eye on US rate moves and global manufacturing data for any shift in the underlying balance of forces.
