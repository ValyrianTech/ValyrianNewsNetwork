# Council of State Clears Way for Palais du Midi Works

Belgium's Council of State has rejected three requests to suspend the partial dismantling of the historic Palais du Midi in central Brussels, removing a key legal obstacle to the long-stalled Metro 3 project. The rulings, issued on 27 October and made public this week, allow the Brussels public transport operator STIB to resume preparation of works tied to the new North-South metro line, [RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/bruxelles-le-conseil-d-etat-rejette-trois-demandes-de-suspension-du-demantelement-du-palais-du-midi-11807699) reported.

Two of the recourses targeted the urban planning permit granted on 16 June 2026, while a third contested the Brussels regional government's decision not to open a heritage classification procedure for the building. The requests had been filed in August by Inter-Environnement Bruxelles (IEB), the Atelier de recherche et d'action urbaines (ARAU) and a private individual, with the environmental movement BRAL intervening in the proceedings.

## A High Legal Bar

In its rulings, the Council of State found that the arguments put forward by the associations were "not serious" — the threshold required in interim (référé) proceedings. The private individual's request was dismissed for lack of direct, personal and current interest. Crucially, the substantive annulment proceedings remain separate from these interim decisions, meaning the underlying challenge to the permit continues on a different track.

STIB welcomed the outcome. "It is in the interest of all parties concerned — users of the building and local residents, but also all users of the pre-metro — that the works can be completed," the operator said, as quoted by [La Libre Belgique](https://www.lalibre.be/regions/bruxelles/2026/10/29/metro-3-les-opposants-au-demantelement-du-palais-du-midi-essuient-un-revers-devant-le-conseil-detat-CIIWSI7OFVEKDMJHWBDMN2RMC4/).

## A Building in the Path of Progress

The Palais du Midi is a large, late-19th-century multi-use building housing shops, social housing, sports facilities and community services near the Midi/Anneessens area. It sits directly on the planned route of the Metro 3 tunnel, which would link the future Toots Thielemans station to Anneessens on the North-South axis. The project requires dismantling the building's interior, reconstructing it, and building the tunnel beneath it.

The dispute has festered for years. Opponents argued that the works would cause irreversible harm to architectural and social heritage and that no alternative — such as keeping trams at surface level — had been properly considered. The associations had publicly mobilised again in September calling for a rapid suspension.

## Heritage Question Left Politically Open

The legal setback does not settle the heritage debate. In March 2026, the Royal Commission for Monuments and Sites (CRMS) issued a partially favourable opinion on classifying part of the building, finding that it "contributes significantly to the urban landscape of the central boulevards and Avenue de Stalingrad." The commission argued for listing the full facades and roofs of the north wing, along with parts of the interior, [RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/bruxelles-avis-favorable-au-classement-partiel-du-palais-du-midi-a-bruxelles-11697175) reported at the time. The regional government ultimately declined to open a full classification procedure — the decision that formed the basis of the third, now-rejected, recourse.

When the urban planning permit was issued in June, ARAU director Marion Alecian voiced the opponents' frustration. "The Palais du Midi will not be 'dismantled' but demolished," she said, arguing that 40,000 square metres of collective facilities would be "reduced to nothing" while the Metro 3 project itself remained uncertain, [according to RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/palais-du-midi-le-permis-d-urbanisme-delivre-par-la-region-bruxelloise-11742545). The permit included conditions intended to protect heritage elements such as facades, joinery and ironwork.

## What the Ruling Does — and Doesn't — Decide

The Council of State's decision is procedural rather than a verdict on the project's merits. By finding the associations' arguments insufficiently serious for urgent suspension, it lowers the immediate barrier to preparatory works but leaves the annulment case — and the political question of heritage protection — alive. The groups retain the option of pressing the heritage classification angle through political channels.

## What's Next

The start date for the actual dismantling remains unknown. The timetable must still be coordinated with stakeholders, notably the City of Brussels, which owns the building. Reconfiguration work on Avenue de Stalingrad is already under way, signalling that the wider urban transformation of the district continues regardless.

After years of technical, legal and financial setbacks, the ruling gives STIB and the regional government their clearest opening yet to advance on the Palais du Midi. But with the annulment proceedings pending and the heritage question unresolved, the saga is unlikely to end here.
