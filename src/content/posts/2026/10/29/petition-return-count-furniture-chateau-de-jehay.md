# Petition Seeks Return of Furniture to Chateau de Jehay

A local resident has launched a petition demanding that the historic furniture and art collection of Count Guy van den Steen be returned to the interior of the Chateau de Jehay in Amay, as the Province of Liege prepares to reopen the castle after more than a decade of restoration work. Marie-Pierre Thonet, who lives in Jehay, argues that the 1978 sale agreement obliges the Province to preserve the castle's character as a museum displaying the count's possessions, as [RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/chateau-de-jehay-une-petition-pour-le-retour-du-mobilier-du-comte-van-den-steen-11807203) reported on 29 October.

The dispute pits local attachment to the exceptional Walloon heritage site against the Province's plan to repurpose the building for events and temporary exhibitions rather than as a permanent museum.

## A collection described as inseparable from the castle

Thonet says the collection assembled by the count, who sold the castle to the Province in 1978 and died in 1999, is "very heterogeneous," comprising paintings, period furniture, silverware from the Prince-Bishops of Liege, crystals, Saxony porcelain and antique tapestries notably from the Aubusson manufactory.

"The Province invested heavily in restoring the building and that was necessary, but right now it is an empty shell without a soul," she told [L'Avenir](https://www.lavenir.net/regions/huy-waremme/amay/2026/09/24/elle-refuse-que-le-chateau-de-jehay-devienne-une-coquille-vide-sans-ame-et-demande-le-retour-de-son-mobilier-CHB2CWWAVVHJLLTHS7HKLOAY2A/) in late September, when she first announced plans to launch the petition. "The castle's movable heritage is inseparable from it; it must be reintegrated."

The petition, published on MyPetition.org on 25 September, is addressed to the Provincial College and the members of the Provincial Council of Liege. It calls for the castle to keep its identity by restoring the furniture and collections of the past, "without turning it into a reception and temporary exhibition space." As of late October, the [online petition](https://www.mypetition.org/petition/culture/rendez-une-a-une-identite-au/287417) was displaying 163 signatures, though RTBF reported Thonet had gathered 500 — a discrepancy that may reflect paper signatures or a lag in the online count.

## The 1978 sale deed at the centre of the dispute

The legal crux of the campaign is the agreement under which Count Guy van den Steen sold the castle, its domain and part of his art collection to the Province of Liege en viager — as a life annuity — on 20 July 1978. The van den Steen family had owned Jehay since 1720.

According to Thonet, the deed provides that after the count's death the Province will maintain the movable and immovable property in its current state and preserve the museum and tourist-site character the estate possessed at the time of sale. The Province became full owner on 1 January 2000, following the count's death in December 1999.

Whether the Province can depart from those conditions remains an open question, which RTBF itself raised in its reporting.

## Province: events, not a museum

Andre Denis, the provincial deputy in charge of infrastructure, said the return of the furniture is not on the agenda. The goal of the renovations, he said, is to host events and temporary exhibitions rather than to make the castle a museum. He added that the count's furniture is being carefully stored at the former barracks in Saive.

The Province halted the castle's interior renovation before the summer of 2026, a decision reported by [L'Avenir](https://www.lavenir.net/regions/huy-waremme/amay/2026/09/01/la-renovation-interieure-du-chateau-de-jehay-mise-a-larret-temporaire-on-ne-va-pas-mettre-un-budget-dans-quelque-chose-quon-pourrait-defaire-RFT7IPGE5FHW7BDCG2ZVNWOGZM/) on 1 September. Denis explained that the pause was needed to define the building's future use before committing to interior layouts that might later have to be dismantled.

"We slow the pace to properly define the right use of the premises, so as not to have to dismantle things we might have done," he said. "We won't put a budget into something we might ultimately have to undo." He insisted the halt was not financially motivated, adding that electrical installations, heating and parquet flooring are close to completion.

The castle has been closed to the public since late 2012 for a major restoration campaign. Its exterior envelope has been completed, and the Province hopes to reopen the site in spring 2027. In 2013 the facades, roofs and Gothic cellars were classified as exceptional heritage of Wallonia.

## What to watch

Thonet intends to raise the matter at the next Amay municipal council meeting. The castle is owned by the Province rather than the commune, however, which limits the council's direct leverage — though local political pressure and public attention can still weigh on provincial decision-makers.

With the interior works paused only for what officials describe as a matter of months, the window for influencing the building's final use is narrow. At stake is a broader question familiar to heritage sites across Europe: whether a historic house can be kept financially sustainable without being stripped of the collection that gives it meaning — or, as the petitioners put it, its soul.
