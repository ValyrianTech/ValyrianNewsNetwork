# SPF Finances: No Data Leak, but Tax Services Stay Offline

Belgium's Federal Public Service for Finance (SPF Finances / FOD Financiën) has moved to reassure the public that no taxpayer data was compromised in the cyberattack that struck its digital environment, even as several of its most critical online services remained inaccessible on Thursday. The agency confirmed that the intrusion, detected on the night of Tuesday 27 to Wednesday 28 October 2026, was halted before attackers could modify systems or reach personal data.

According to [RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/le-spf-finances-rassure-il-n-y-a-pas-eu-de-fuite-de-donnees-mais-les-services-restent-inaccessibles-11807725), the finance department detected several attempts to modify its digital environment. "The attack could not be developed far enough within our environment to make modifications or access data," a spokesperson said, adding that potentially infected elements were isolated and the intrusion was successfully stopped.

## What Happened

The cyberattack was first made public on Wednesday, when the FPS reported that MyMinfin, Tax-on-web and Biztax had been taken offline as a precaution, as [RTBF reported at the time](https://www.rtbf.be/article/les-services-numeriques-du-service-public-federal-finances-touches-par-une-cyberattaque-11807164). The agency described the move as a deliberate measure "to limit risks and carry out the technical interventions necessary to guarantee the security of our systems."

The official incident page, [updated by the FOD Financiën](https://fin.belgium.be/fr/news/incident-cybersecurite-spf-finances-28102026) on 29 October at 15:30, states plainly: "The hacking was stopped with success" and "we now know that no data has leaked." A large number of other applications within the department's portfolio remain available with full or partial functionality.

## Why the Services Are Still Down

Paradoxically, the successful containment is precisely why the platforms remain offline. Restoring them is not a simple matter of switching systems back on. According to the agency, the various services "must be restored in an isolated environment," a process that requires time and careful precautions "to exclude any new risk." No fixed date has been given for full restoration — a detail that matters greatly to taxpayers and tax professionals in the middle of the filing season.

## The Tax Deadline Moves to 16 November

Recognising the disruption's practical consequences, SPF Finances has extended the deadline for filing tax returns involving special or specific income. Originally due 31 October, the deadline is now pushed to 16 November 2026, as confirmed by both RTBF and the official FOD statement. The department acknowledged the pressure the outage places on filers, stating that data protection remains a priority while it works to restore services as quickly as possible.

## An Investigation Underway

The Federal Police and the Centre for Cybersecurity Belgium (CCB) are both involved in the investigation to identify the perpetrators, [Euronews reported](https://www.euronews.com/2026/10/28/belgiums-finance-and-tax-agency-hit-by-cyberattack). At this stage, the identity and motive of the attackers remain unknown, as does the precise nature of the attempted modifications. It is not clear whether the incident was a targeted exploit attempt, an act of hacktivism, or a precursor to a ransomware operation.

## Analysis: Reassurance With Caveats

The official message — that no data has leaked — is consistent across the department's own channels and multiple media outlets, and it reflects a forensic assessment that the attack was caught early. Yet the reassurance rests on the agency's own investigation; nothing has been independently verified, and the phrase "nothing indicates" a breach leaves room for revision should a deeper review uncover exfiltration.

Beyond the immediate inconvenience, the incident lands at a sensitive moment for the credibility of Belgium's core tax platforms. The finance department's e-services have suffered repeated outages, and the professional body ITAA — representing some 13,500 accountants and tax advisers — formally served notice on the Belgian State in mid-2026 over structural failures. A cyberattack is a different problem from chronic unreliability, but to a public already frustrated by recurring downtime, the distinction may matter less than the disruption itself. Extending a deadline is a pragmatic fix; as critics have argued, it addresses the symptom rather than the underlying fragility.

## What to Watch

Three questions will shape how this story develops. First, whether the CCB attributes the attack and whether the no-leak reassurance survives a full forensic review. Second, whether other government systems show signs of linked activity, in a year that has already seen cybercriminals breach sensitive state data. Third, whether the incident prompts meaningful investment in the resilience of Belgium's tax infrastructure — or simply another deadline extension. For now, taxpayers can file by 16 November, but only once the platforms come back online.
