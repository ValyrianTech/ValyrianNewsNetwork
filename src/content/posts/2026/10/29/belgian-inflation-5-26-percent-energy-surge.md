# Belgian Inflation Hits 5.26% as Energy Prices Surge

Belgian inflation jumped to 5.26 percent in October, the highest reading in more than three years, as a sustained surge in energy prices weighed on household budgets. According to [Statbel](https://statbel.fgov.be/nl/nieuws/de-inflatie-bedraagt-526), the federal statistics office, the consumer price index rose from 4.69 percent in September and 3.97 percent in August, capping four straight months of acceleration.

The increase is overwhelmingly an energy story. Energy prices now stand 33.22 percent higher than a year earlier and alone account for 2.92 percentage points of the total figure. Natural gas has become more than half again as expensive — up 52.5 percent year on year — while electricity rose 23.7 percent and heating oil 30.8 percent.

## A Split Inflation Picture

Beneath the headline number, the drivers are narrowly concentrated. Excluding energy products, inflation actually eased to 2.60 percent from 2.83 percent, and core inflation — stripping out energy and unprocessed food — fell to 2.80 percent from 3.00 percent. Services inflation cooled to 4.73 percent from 5.25 percent, and food prices were 0.18 percent lower than a year earlier.

That distinction matters for interpreting the headline. As [KMO.partners](https://www.kmo.partners/2026/10/29/inflatie-oktober-2026-5-26-procent-energie-spilindex/) put it, anyone still budgeting energy, prices or wages on summer figures is calculating too low. The cost-of-living squeeze is being felt chiefly through heating bills, fuel and electricity rather than a broad overheating of the economy.

Motorfuels illustrate the point: diesel is 45.1 percent more expensive than a year ago, petrol 28.8 percent and LPG 44.8 percent. Belgian rent, higher-education tuition (+20.7 percent) and airfares added further upward pressure.

One of the more unusual line items is a 48.7 percent jump in external memory storage — hard drives, USB sticks and SD cards — which Statbel attributed to a global supply shortage driven by demand for artificial-intelligence computing.

## Why It Rose, and Why It Could Have Been Worse

The surge traces back to persistent tensions in the Middle East, which have kept European gas and oil supply under pressure for months. [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/10/29/energieprijzen-doen-inflatie-naar-5-26-procent-stijgen/) reported that inflation first spiked above 4 percent in April after US and Israeli attacks on Iran, dipped briefly in June, and has climbed steadily since July. The last time inflation was this high was April 2023 — also driven by soaring energy costs.

Yet the October figure came in below expectations. The [Federal Planning Bureau](https://www.plan.be/nl/data/indexcijfer-der-consumptieprijzen) had forecast 5.47 percent for the month, meaning the outcome was slightly less severe than official projections anticipated.

## The Pivot Index: Not Yet Triggered

For Belgian households and employers, the most consequential question is whether the reading triggers automatic wage and benefit increases. That depends on the smoothed health index, which reached 101.72 points in October — still 0.57 points below the pivot index (spilindex) of 102.29.

Because the threshold was not crossed, October's figure does not itself unlock new indexation. When the pivot index is exceeded, social benefits rise by 2 percent one month later and public-sector wages two months later. The last crossing came in June, which led to indexation in September. The Planning Bureau expects the threshold to be breached again in November, with the next set of forecasts due on 3 November.

The stakes extend to the federal budget. Indexed expenditures rise automatically when the pivot is crossed, adding pressure to the budget negotiations of the De Wever government.

## No Relief From the ECB

The European Central Bank offered no counterweight. On the same day the inflation data was published, the ECB [kept its three key interest rates unchanged](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/pr/date/2026/html/ecb.mp261029~9a493adf17.en.html), holding the deposit rate at 2.50 percent and the refinancing rate at 2.65 percent. The Governing Council cited the continued rise in energy prices as an upside risk to inflation and a downside risk to growth, and noted that financing conditions had tightened since its previous meeting.

## What to Watch

Attention now shifts to two dates. From 1 November, the excise duty on household electricity falls from €46 to €42.20 per megawatt-hour under the country's energy-norm law — a modest factor pushing in the opposite direction, though its index impact has not been quantified. Then on 3 November the Planning Bureau publishes fresh forecasts, which will indicate whether the November reading is likely to breach the pivot threshold as expected.

For now, the picture is one of an economy hit by an external energy shock rather than domestic overheating. How long that shock persists — and whether it spreads into wages and services — will determine how far the 5.26 percent headline travels.
