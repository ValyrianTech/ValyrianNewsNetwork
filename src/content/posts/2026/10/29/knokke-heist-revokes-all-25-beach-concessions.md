# Knokke-Heist Revokes All 25 Beach Concessions

The seaside municipality of Knokke-Heist has withdrawn the allocation of all 25 beach concessions — 24 beach bars and one children's park — after Belgium's highest administrative court suspended the original award. Candidates learned of the decision by email on the morning of 29 October, less than five months before operators are due to rebuild their seasonal establishments for the new season.

The move follows a ruling by the Council of State (Raad van State) that [suspended](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/10/23/strandbars-geschorst-knokke-heist/) the August allocation in three of the four coastal zones, siding with a group of disadvantaged operators who had challenged how a jury awarded the concessions.

## Background: a contested summer award

On 28 August 2026, Knokke-Heist completed the allocation of its beach concessions across four zones — Heist, Duinbergen, Knokke and Het Zoute — following a jury process. Permits were awarded from west to east, from Heist's Zonnestrand and Marea through to Het Zoute's Famouz and Zoutestrand 19, and included the children's park 'Kids Beach'.

The outcome drew immediate controversy. Six beach-bar operators who lost their places disputed the procedure, and a seventh candidate — who had never operated a beach bar in Knokke-Heist — soon joined them. Their appeals led to the Council of State's suspension ruling, reported on 23 October, which found that the municipality had not investigated three matters carefully enough.

According to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/10/29/knokke-heist-schrapt-vergunning-van-alle-25-strandbars/), the court questioned first whether certain candidates could actually afford the exceptionally high concession fees. Bidders were required to submit a financial plan demonstrating the realism and feasibility of the fee they offered, and the Council found the municipality had not examined those figures with sufficient care.

Second, the court found that many proposed beach-bar concepts appeared not to comply with applicable urban-planning (stedenbouwkundige) regulations, and that the municipality had failed to assess whether the deviations were limited or essential — or to factor them into other evaluation criteria such as the financial plan. Third, the Council raised doubts about the duration of the concessions: nine years, renewable once, for a maximum of 18 years. The municipality, the court said, had not demonstrated good reasons for awarding permits over such a long period.

## From three zones to all four

The Council's suspension covered only three zones — Duinbergen, Knokke and Het Zoute — leaving the beach bars of Heist, the westernmost zone, nominally secure. Four appellants received favourable rulings: Lekkerbek Beach, Blue Buddha, Albertstrand and Yssis Beach.

But after studying the court's remarks, the Knokke-Heist college of mayor and aldermen concluded that the allocation could not be retained in its current form. In its email to candidates, the municipality stated that the decision applied to "all beach bars and children's parks, in the 4 zones Heist, Duinbergen, Knokke and Zoute" — extending the revocation even to the zone the court had not individually suspended.

## Operators left without certainty

The practical consequences are severe for every concession holder, winners and losers alike. With the beach bars due to be rebuilt roughly five months from now, no operator in Knokke-Heist has any certainty over their permit.

One beach operator described the situation bluntly to VRT NWS: "A pure disgrace. The municipality communicates nothing to us. We are simply air. For more than a year we have been under gigantic stress, but we get no information."

How the municipality will re-award the concessions is not yet clear. The administration was initially unreachable for comment.

## Analysis: a transparency problem, not just a procedural one

The dispute strikes at the heart of how one of Belgium's most affluent coastal resorts manages its prized beach frontage. Knokke-Heist — home to the prestigious Knokke and Het Zoute districts — commands exceptionally high concession values, and the Council's findings point to a deeper problem than a single paperwork error.

By questioning whether bidders could realistically pay the fees they offered, the court effectively flagged the risk that the award process may have rewarded ambition over capability — a concern that matters not only for the integrity of the tender but for the stability of the beach season itself. The urban-planning findings raise parallel questions about whether winning proposals were assessed on a like-for-like basis.

The inclusion of Heist in the withdrawal also opens a new front: operators in a zone the court did not suspend now lose their permits too, and may yet challenge a decision that goes further than the ruling required.

## What's next

The immediate question is timing. Knokke-Heist has not said how or when it will re-run the allocation, nor whether the process will change — for instance through stricter affordability checks, revised urban-planning criteria or a shorter concession term. The municipality has said it will study the consequences of the ruling before taking a substantive position.

Until a new award is finalised, Belgium's best-known beach bar strip faces an unusual winter: an entire coastline of concessions has been wiped clean, and the clock is already ticking toward spring.
