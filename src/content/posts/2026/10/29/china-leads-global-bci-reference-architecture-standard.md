# China Leads New Global BCI Reference Architecture Standard

The International Organization for Standardization and the International Electrotechnical Commission (ISO/IEC) have released a new international standard for brain-computer interface (BCI) reference architecture that was led by Chinese experts, according to [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/tech/20261029/4f321fd778db4963a04e7673853999f5/c.html). The standard, titled "Information Technology — Brain-Computer Interface — Reference Architecture," was developed with experts from Italy, South Korea, the UK, India, the US, Japan, Sweden, Finland, Australia and Germany.

The release is being framed in Chinese state media as another milestone in the country's effort to lead international standardization in an emerging field that spans medical rehabilitation, consumer health, industrial production and traffic safety.

## What the Standard Does

According to [IT之家](https://www.ithome.com/1/014/875.htm), citing CCTV News, the standard is organized around four core questions — what BCI is, how it is used, what functions it has, and how it is realized — and defines a complete technical framework covering concept, application, function and implementation.

The goal is to unify how industry stakeholders worldwide understand the composition of BCI systems, their functional boundaries and their typical implementation methods. By resolving inconsistencies across different technical routes and application scenarios, the standard is intended to provide a unified technical reference for BCI product research and development, system design, and downstream data, interface, testing and application standards.

As BCI moves from laboratory research toward real-world deployment, a common baseline architecture is seen as increasingly important for enabling technical coordination and orderly industrial development.

## A Sequence of Standards

The reference architecture standard is described as "another important achievement" in China's BCI international standardization work, implying a prior milestone. That earlier step was ISO/IEC 8663:2025, the "Information technology — Brain-computer interfaces — Vocabulary" standard, which Chinese coverage has described as the first international standard in the BCI field led by China.

The new release also follows domestic momentum. In September 2026, China's National Medical Products Administration approved the country's third BCI medical device standard — described as the world's first BCI medical device product standard using artificial intelligence to process EEG data, effective September 1, 2027. Separately, China's Ministry of Commerce and eight other departments issued opinions in October 2026 aimed at accelerating the commercialization of frontier technologies, including BCI and embodied AI.

## Analysis: Standards as Strategic Leverage

The significance of the reference architecture extends beyond technical coordination. In emerging technologies, whoever shapes the foundational standards often shapes interoperability, safety requirements and market access rules — a form of influence that can outlast individual products or companies.

China's BCI standardization push fits a broader pattern in which Beijing is positioning itself to set global technical standards in frontier fields ranging from artificial intelligence to telecommunications and now neurotechnology. The reference architecture's multinational co-development — spanning ten countries across Europe, Asia and North America — suggests China is working through the formal ISO/IEC process rather than pursuing a purely domestic track, which may lend the standard broader international legitimacy.

At the same time, the framing that the standard was "led by China" comes primarily from Chinese state media, and this research pass did not locate independent foreign confirmation of how much credit China should receive relative to its co-developers. The exact ISO/IEC number for the reference architecture standard was also not specified in the Chinese coverage, which cited only the title; the number is documented only for the earlier vocabulary standard.

## What's Next

The immediate focus will be on how industry and regulators adopt the reference architecture and whether it becomes a genuine global baseline or primarily a reference point for Chinese firms and standard-setters. The forthcoming September 2027 implementation of China's AI-based EEG medical device standard will offer an early test of how the country's domestic and international BCI standards tracks interact.

For now, the release marks a concrete step in the race to define the technical foundations of neurotechnology — a contest whose rules are being written well before the products that will depend on them reach the market.
