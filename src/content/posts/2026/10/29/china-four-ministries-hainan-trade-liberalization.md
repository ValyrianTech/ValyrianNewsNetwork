# China's Four Ministries Deepen Hainan Trade Opening

Four Chinese government departments have jointly issued a new notice to further raise the level of trade liberalization and facilitation at the Hainan Free Trade Port, unveiling 16 targeted measures across goods trade, services trade and emerging trade formats. The notice, carried by state broadcaster CCTV and reproduced by [The Paper](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_34358386) on 29 October, arrives about ten months after the island launched full island-wide customs closure operations — the step that turned the free trade port from a policy blueprint into a functioning separate customs territory.

The document, numbered 商自贸发〔2026〕187号, was issued by the Ministry of Commerce, the National Development and Reform Commission, the Ministry of Ecology and Environment and the General Administration of Customs.

## A narrower, more surgical follow-up

The new notice is deliberately modest in scope. It sets out 16 measures under three headings — optimizing goods trade, facilitating services trade, and innovating new trade formats — compared with the 28 measures issued by some 20 departments in the 2021 predecessor notice. That shift in shape is telling: rather than erecting a new framework, Beijing appears to be fine-tuning specific sectors now that Hainan's customs regime is operational.

The preamble frames the intent plainly: to "build the Hainan Free Trade Port to high standards" and make it "an important gateway leading China's opening-up in the new era."

## What the measures actually do

In goods trade, the notice brings international-standard inspection to aviation kerosene held in bonded warehouses for re-export, and allows bonded and export-supervision warehouses for gasoline, kerosene, diesel, fuel oil and naphtha in the Yangpu Economic Development Zone to be integrated under a flexible new monitoring model. A pilot will permit the import of faulty key auto parts for research, development and testing.

Energy and commodities feature prominently. Once liquefied natural gas futures list, the notice supports LNG futures delivery warehouses in Hainan and permits bonded delivery to be settled using energy metering — a technical but significant step toward deeper commodity-market integration. Foreign firms will also be allowed to use Hainan's LNG terminal bonded facilities, and new-type barter trade is to be developed on a prudent, orderly basis, with pricing in RMB encouraged.

On services, the notice lets foreign physicians holding lawful overseas practice rights work clinically in Hainan under licences of up to three years, renewable on expiry. Authority to approve foreign-co-hosted economic and technical exhibitions is delegated to the Hainan Provincial Department of Commerce, and recognition of overseas professional qualifications is to expand, alongside more convenient work permits and residence policies for foreign nationals.

For new trade formats, the notice accelerates offshore trade through cross-departmental data sharing and permits bonded remanufactured products from customs special supervision zones to enter domestic pilot free trade zones, with duties and taxes paid and the goods clearly labelled as remanufactured. It also allows Hainan to self-assess and expand the "two-ends-outside" bonded maintenance catalogue, and calls for deeper economic and trade cooperation with Hong Kong, exploring rule alignment and closer coordination with the Greater Bay Area.

## Analysis: opening-up with a safety catch

The most notable element may be the last. Explicit language on aligning rules and mechanisms with Hong Kong signals ambitions that extend beyond tariffs and customs into financial and professional-services integration — a test of how far mainland experimentation can borrow from the territory's international standards.

Yet the notice is carefully hedged. Its closing paragraph urges departments to "balance development and security" and to "firmly hold the safety bottom line," assigning Hainan primary responsibility for implementation and for building a risk-control system. The pattern is consistent with Beijing's broader approach to opening-up: liberalize in measurable, contained steps, with oversight built in from the start.

## What to watch

Implementation will be the real test. Several measures are contingent — LNG futures delivery depends on the contracts listing first, and the barter-trade and remanufacturing provisions rely on detailed rules still to come. How quickly Hainan's provincial authorities convert these 16 measures into operational procedures, and whether the Hong Kong alignment produces concrete mechanisms, will determine whether this notice is remembered as a meaningful refinement or a modest administrative footnote.

For now, the signal is clear: China's flagship free-trade experiment is moving into a phase of post-closure consolidation, and Hainan remains the laboratory where the next round of opening-up policies is being tested.
