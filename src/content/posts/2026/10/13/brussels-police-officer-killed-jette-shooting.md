# Brussels Police Officer Killed in Jette Shooting

A 41-year-old police officer was shot and killed overnight during an intervention in Jette, in the Brussels-Capital Region, after his unit moved to intercept a stolen vehicle. The Brussels public prosecutor's office has scheduled a press conference at 14:30 on Tuesday to provide further details on the circumstances of the incident.

The officer, a member of the Anti-Robbery Brigade (Anti-Overvalbrigade, AOB) of the Brussels-Capital/Elsene police zone, was struck by gunfire and died in hospital, according to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/10/13/agent-overleden-bij-interventie-in-brussel/).

## How the Incident Unfolded

According to VRT NWS justice correspondent Chris Verhaeghe, the events began with a carjacking in Tubize (Tubeke) in Walloon Brabant on the evening of 12 October. An ANPR camera — an automatic number-plate recognition system widely used by Belgian police — subsequently detected the stolen vehicle, prompting the dispatch of the Anti-Robbery Brigade.

Upon the officers' arrival in Jette, the suspects reportedly exited the vehicle immediately and opened fire. The officer was struck almost at once and stood no chance, according to the initial account. He was taken to hospital with a gunshot wound and died there.

A second, apparently unrelated fatal shooting occurred the same night in Elsene, where one man was killed. Whether the two incidents are connected remained unclear, though VRT's initial information suggested they were not.

## A Region in Mourning

By Tuesday morning, flags were flying at half-mast at police stations across Belgium. The Brussels-Capital/Elsene police zone placed a mourning band on social media, writing that it learned of the death "with deep sorrow and great dismay," and extending condolences to the officer's family and colleagues.

Police Zone Rivierenland (Mechelen, Willebroek, Bornem and Puurs-Sint-Amands) also lowered its flags. "The news of the colleague's death in Brussels hits every employee hard," said Dirk Van de Sande. "Every police employee is aware of the danger... It can happen to anyone. Every intervention can involve danger." Several West Flemish police zones, including Bruges, Ostend, VLAS and Arro Ieper, followed suit.

According to the VSOA police union, the officer is the 36th police officer to die on duty in 25 years. His name will be added to the "wall of memory," inaugurated in 2019 at the Polis Center, the largest police building in Brussels.

## Officials Express Condolences

Prime Minister Bart De Wever (N-VA) offered his "deepest condolences" to the officer's family, loved ones and the entire police zone, praising the "men and women of our police services who carry out their duty every day with the greatest courage and sometimes at risk of their own lives."

Justice Minister Annelies Verlinden (CD&V) said her thoughts were with the family, friends and colleagues of the deceased inspector, adding that "the devastation and sadness are great." Interior Minister Bernard Quintin said he was "deeply affected" and pledged that "the perpetrator or perpetrators will face the full severity of the law."

Brussels Mayor Philippe Close (PS) said he went to the scene immediately, together with police chief Michel Goovaerts, to join King's Prosecutor Julien Moinil, who was already there with investigation teams. "Today one of them has given his life," Close said. "Let us never forget that these officials watch over our safety every day."

## Unions Renew Calls for Tougher Action

The death has intensified long-standing demands from police unions for a firmer response to violence against officers. Vincent Houssin, vice-chair of the VSOA Politie union, described the incident bluntly as "outright murder."

"This always hits very hard," Houssin said. "We regularly sound the alarm, with the message that violence is escalating and becoming ever more brazen. But we get little hearing from the political authorities."

He criticized what he described as unfulfilled promises of stricter punishment, referencing recent riots in which, he said, even the prosecutor's office complained that juvenile perpetrators had to be released because the justice system could not keep up. "This was outright murder," he repeated.

Joery Dehaes of the NSPV union said the unions have long fought for better follow-up of violence against police, with some improvement, but that "the situation is not yet optimal."

## What's Next

The Brussels prosecutor's office is leading the investigation, with a federal police forensic team deployed to the scene. A second car, bearing Polish license plates, was towed from the location, though its relevance to the shooting was unclear.

The officer's identity has not been released. As the investigation continues, the 14:30 press conference is expected to clarify the precise circumstances of both overnight shootings — and, crucially, whether they are in any way linked. For a police force and a city already grieving, the answers cannot come soon enough.
