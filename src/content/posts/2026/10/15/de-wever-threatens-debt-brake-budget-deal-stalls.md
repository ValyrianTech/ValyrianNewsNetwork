# De Wever Threatens Debt Brake as Budget Deal Stalls

Belgian Prime Minister Bart De Wever (N-VA) has warned he will push a so-called "debt brake" through parliament if his five-party coalition fails to agree on a full multi-year budget by the end of the year. According to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/liveblog/premier-de-wever-wil-schuldenrem-als-begrotingsakkoord-niet-ti~1790599460224/), the premier now aims for a first package of measures worth 3 to 4 billion euros for 2027, and wants that stopgap agreement in place — ideally by the weekend of 17-18 October.

The debt brake, or *schuldenrem*, is a mechanism that would allow the government to freeze public spending in an emergency, acting as a financial "parachute" or shock absorber if the coalition were to enter political crisis or fall. It could be activated via parliament at the end of the year if a full budget running to 2029 is not reached. De Wever's party insists the mechanism "does not in any way replace the negotiations on the multi-year budget."

## A Coalition Pulled in Two Directions

The standoff marks the most serious test yet for the so-called "Arizona coalition," an ideologically diverse five-party government of N-VA, Vooruit and CD&V on the Flemish side and MR and Les Engagés on the francophone side. Led by De Wever since February 2025, the coalition has repeatedly missed budget deadlines — Belgium entered 2026 under "provisional twelfths," a stopgap that lets the government spend one-twelfth of the previous year's budget each month.

At the heart of the deadlock is a proposed VAT (btw) reform, which would shift taxation from labour to consumption by raising the standard rate from 21 to 22 percent. N-VA argues experts have long called for such a shift. The French-speaking liberal MR, led by Georges-Louis Bouchez, has blocked the measure.

On Wednesday, De Wever dropped the VAT reform from his immediate proposal, focusing instead on a 2027 budget. As [HLN](https://www.hln.be/binnenland/de-wever-wil-snel-akkoord-over-schuldenrem-van-3-a-4-miljard-euro-voor-als-begroting-niet-op-tijd-af-geraakt~a9cb0fe2/) reported, the VAT overhaul is only needed over the longer term, so it has been set aside for now.

## MR Seen as Winning the Round

Political analyst Ivan De Vadder of VRT NWS said the shift leaves the impression that one of the five coalition partners has won the argument outright. "You get the idea that MR has won the debate and that the other parties, with the prime minister at their head, have had to come off worst," he wrote in a [VRT NWS analysis](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/10/15/nieuw-begrotingsvoorstel-zonder-btw-hervorming-mr-gewonnen-de-o/). "MR has pocketed everything it wanted."

De Vadder warned that the concession may change the internal dynamics of the coalition. "Red lines pay off. And that could well change the dynamic among the coalition parties," he said, predicting that Vooruit would now resist index jumps while CD&V pushes for tax relief and Les Engagés defends health-care spending.

Bouchez defended his position on X, arguing that "the VAT in this budget is used to close the deficit, not to lower labour costs... raising taxes is not a reform." N-VA party chair Valerie Van Peel countered that holding back the reform would make the challenge harder. "Every day we wait, the challenge grows," she said.

## Why the Markets Are Watching

The urgency reflects mounting pressure on Belgium's public finances. The country's long-term interest rate climbed above 3.8 percent in August 2026, its highest level since 2012, as [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/08/19/stijgende-rente-gevolgen-begroting/) reported. Belgium was placed in the EU's excessive deficit procedure in July, and the National Bank has warned the debt trajectory is unsustainable, projecting it above 109 percent of GDP this year.

Economists caution that delay carries its own cost. "If politicians don't intervene themselves, financial markets will eventually decide for them," said Willem Sas, professor of public economics at UHasselt and KU Leuven. Selien De Schryder of Ghent University warned of a "debt snowball" that builds when interest rates outpace economic growth: "The longer you wait, the greater the costs ultimately become."

De Wever has explicitly rejected a budget based on provisional twelfths, calling it an embarrassment and a bad signal to financial markets. His government's original target was to find 10 billion euros by 2029.

## What to Watch

The premier is attending a European summit in Brussels on Thursday and Friday, after which budget talks are expected to intensify. Opposition parties have accused De Wever of avoiding parliamentary scrutiny after he skipped his customary State of the Union address on Tuesday.

If the coalition reaches a 2027 deal in the coming days, De Wever will still need to return to the harder multi-year negotiations — with the VAT question firmly back on the table. Political journalist Bart Verhulst has noted that a full budget must reach parliament by late October or early November to be passed in time. Whether the debt brake ultimately serves as a genuine safeguard or as a pressure tactic to force a wider deal remains the central question hanging over the talks.
