# Court Blocks White House Ban on CNN, MS NOW and Politico

A federal judge has temporarily blocked the Trump administration from enforcing a White House press ban against CNN, MS NOW and Politico, ruling that the three outlets are likely to win their lawsuit alleging the ban violated their constitutional rights. U.S. District Court Judge Timothy J. Kelly issued a preliminary injunction late Tuesday, Oct. 13, replacing an earlier temporary restraining order and keeping the outlets' press credentials in place while the case proceeds.

The Justice Department notified the court on Wednesday, Oct. 14, that it would appeal the ruling, setting up a potentially lengthy legal battle over press access to the White House.

## What the Ruling Says

According to [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/10/14/nx-s1-6001398/white-house-media-ban), Kelly found that President Trump's decision to bar more than 70 journalists from the three outlets violated their free speech rights "in two separate ways as well as their due process rights." The judge found the outlets were likely to succeed on a Fifth Amendment due process claim and on two First Amendment claims — retaliation and viewpoint discrimination.

The ruling is notable because the earlier temporary restraining order had rested primarily on due process grounds. As [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/14/trump-white-house-msnow-cnn-politico-media-ban.html) reported, Kelly "said for the first time in this case, the outlets were likely to succeed on two First Amendment claims in their civil suit."

Kelly leaned heavily on Trump's own public statements. "President Trump's own comments suggest that he intended the revocation of Plaintiffs' hard passes to alter their reporting on him, bolstering the case that his action here is sufficient to deter a reporter of ordinary firmness from reporting in the same way she had before," the judge wrote.

He added: "It is not hard to conclude that President Trump likely banned Plaintiffs from the White House because he believes that their reporting is generally inaccurate and biased against him." That, Kelly wrote, amounts to a form of unlawful retaliation.

## The Ban and Its Aftermath

The dispute began on Sept. 18, when Trump announced on Truth Social that he was banning the three outlets, writing that they "shouldn't be able to constantly write or report FICTION and LIES when they're covering the President of the United States." Trump also said that day he had become "sick" of "cumulative stories" by the outlets.

The ban took effect the following day, Sept. 19, when journalists from the three organizations were denied access to White House grounds and had their hard passes confiscated by Secret Service officers. NPR reported that "more than 70" journalists were affected, while CNBC reported the ban "reportedly led to 78 journalists having their so-called hard passes" revoked — an unresolved discrepancy in the record.

The outlets sued on Sept. 21, arguing the ban was "a more direct assault on the First Amendment," and Kelly issued a temporary restraining order on Sept. 24 that allowed journalists to return to the White House. The TRO expired on Oct. 13, the same day Kelly issued the preliminary injunction.

## National Security Claim Rejected

Kelly rejected the Justice Department's argument that national security justified the ban. "There is no evidence in the record that President Trump made the decision to revoke Plaintiffs' hard passes on national security grounds from the get-go, nor at any time has he directly stated that national security was the reason for his decision," Kelly wrote, calling the argument "merely a post-hoc justification for the decision" after the outlets sued.

As [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/oct/14/white-house-media-ban-lifted-cnn-msnow-politico) reported, the White House gave no advance notice that the outlets risked losing their credentials, providing notification only after access was already cut off — a process that Kelly found violated binding court precedent.

The judge nonetheless acknowledged that the government has legitimate interests. "No one should interpret the Court's decision to suggest that President Trump's concerns about accuracy and fairness in the media's reporting are unfounded, or that the government is powerless when its true aim is to protect national security when classified information is unlawfully disclosed," he wrote.

## Reaction and Limitations

Theodore J. Boutrous Jr., the First Amendment attorney representing the three outlets, welcomed the decision. "When the President issued his 'ban on the free press,' he blatantly and explicitly violated due process and the First Amendment," Boutrous said. "The harm this ban did to our clients, the First Amendment, and the public as a whole is profound."

The White House signaled it would fight on. "The Fake News has no place demanding a spot in the President's Oval Office," a White House official told [Fox News](https://www.foxnews.com/media/white-house-confirms-appeal-trump-appointed-judge-extends-ruling-restoring-cnn-msnbc-politico-access). "The lies they smear are a disservice to the American public and tear at the very fabric of our Democracy. They're completely capable of reporting from directly outside. We intend to appeal this misguided decision."

The preliminary injunction is not a final ruling on the merits of the case. It also does not compel the White House to restore access to smaller spaces such as Air Force One or the Oval Office, where the full press corps cannot fit — a limitation that has prevented CNN from serving as the video pool's designated representative. The judge required the media plaintiffs to post a bond of just $1.

## Broader Battle Looming

The ruling arrives amid what NPR described as the administration's multi-front campaign against the press. That campaign has included a review of broadcast licenses for ABC's local stations, efforts to force outlets out of the Pentagon, investigations of all major networks except Fox, and pressure on congressional Republicans to cut federal funding for public media. On Oct. 11, the Justice Department said it was investigating five major TV networks over antitrust concerns related to their coordinated response after CNN was banned.

The dispute also unfolds just weeks before the Nov. 3 midterm elections. Kelly, a Trump appointee, previously ruled against the administration in the 2018 press-pass dispute involving then-CNN correspondent Jim Acosta — a precedent that shapes the current case.

With the Justice Department's appeal now in motion, the central question — whether a president may revoke press credentials over the content of coverage — is likely to move to a higher court. For now, the three outlets retain their access, and the press corps is watching closely for what comes next.
