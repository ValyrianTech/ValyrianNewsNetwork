# Amazon Debuts Always-Listening Bee AI Devices

Amazon unveiled two new artificial intelligence devices on Thursday that listen to everyday conversations, transcribe them, and turn them into summaries, reminders, and to-dos—a launch that expands the company's push into ambient AI hardware while renewing questions about always-on listening in consumer products.

The devices come from Bee, the San Francisco startup Amazon [acquired](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/07/22/amazon-ai-bee-wearable.html) in July 2025 for an undisclosed sum. The first, the **Bee Frontier**, is a silicone wristband that can also be clipped to clothing and retails for $74.99. The second, the **Bee Eclipse**, is a credit card-sized device that magnetically attaches to the back of a phone and is priced at $99.99, according to [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/amazon-personal-ai-wearable-devices-bee/). The Frontier is available now; the Eclipse is listed as "coming soon."

## How the devices work

Equipped with dual microphones and advanced noise filtering, Bee devices are designed to capture conversations and convert them into useful artifacts. According to [Amazon](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/devices/amazon-personal-ai-bee-frontier-bee-eclipse), the system "takes note of the details you might otherwise forget and turns them into useful conversation summaries, to-dos, reminders, and insights."

Unlike Amazon's Echo smart speakers, which activate only when they hear a wake word, Bee devices are always recording when switched on. As [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/15/amazon-bee-frontier-eclipse-ai-devices.html) reported, that always-on behavior is what enables features such as reviewing "memories from the past few days" and tracking recurring "trends or themes" to generate personalized goals and actions.

Users can connect Bee to Gmail, Google Calendar, Microsoft Outlook, Apple HealthKit, and photos, allowing the AI to draft follow-up emails or create calendar invites based on a conversation. New integrations with Amazon's Alexa+ assistant and Amazon.com enable hands-free access to summaries and personalized shopping recommendations.

The Frontier offers up to seven days of battery life, IP67 water resistance, USB-C charging, and comes in Lavender, Sage, and Onyx. The Eclipse, in Silver, Midnight, Honey, and Blush, promises more than 21 days of battery life.

## A subscription-driven business model

Bee is free to use for conversation summaries, recaps, and 14 days of "memory." More advanced capabilities—unlimited recent summaries, Alexa+ and Amazon.com integrations, third-party app connections, suggested to-dos, daily memories, a personal chat assistant, and a desktop app—require **Bee Pro**, which Amazon prices at $11.99 per month. All new customers receive 30 days of Bee Pro free. CNBC cited a $12 monthly figure.

The Frontier's $74.99 price marks roughly a 50% increase over Bee's original $49.99 always-listening wristband, reflecting both the upgraded hardware and Amazon's efforts to build a recurring-revenue subscription around it.

## Privacy at the center

Amazon has sought to preempt privacy concerns, saying the devices are built on "Bee Private Compute," a secure, isolated processing environment. In a [company explainer](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/devices/bee-private-compute-data-protection), Amazon states that audio is processed into insights in real time and deleted afterward, and that "no one, including Amazon, can access your data" unless a user chooses to share it. Transcripts are encrypted with keys the device creates and controls, and a single press turns the devices on or off with an LED indicator light.

Amazon also says users "are prompted to gain permission from call participants each time a recording is started" and can configure the device to shut off automatically after a period of silence, such as five or 15 minutes.

The Verge framed the launch pointedly, [headlining](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1011475/amazon-bee-frontier-eclipse-price-specs) that "Amazon's always-listening Bee wearable can now help you buy stuff," underscoring how the device's insights now feed directly into the company's commerce engine.

## An increasingly crowded—and scrutinized—field

Bee lands in a fast-growing market of AI wearables and note-taking gadgets, including Meta's Ray-Ban smart glasses and its palm-sized Muse Charm pendant, Plaud's card-shaped recorder, and Friend's AI necklace. The category has drawn a hostile reception from some consumers and advocacy groups: Meta's glasses have generated widespread privacy and civil-rights concerns from users, lawmakers, and advocates, while Friend's pendant drew public backlash at launch.

One unresolved issue is consent. Two-party and all-party consent laws vary by U.S. state, complicating the recording of bystanders who never agreed to be captured—a question that always-listening devices like Bee raise by design. Amazon's approach places the onus on users to seek permission, but the devices record ambient conversation by default when switched on.

The launch also reflects Amazon's broader AI hardware ambitions. The company has rolled out Nova models, Trainium chips, a shopping chatbot, and the Bedrock marketplace, and has rebuilt Alexa with generative AI. It previously experimented with wearables through its Halo fitness band, which it sunset in 2023.

## What to watch

Whether consumers embrace—or resist—a device that listens by default will shape the next phase of the ambient AI market. Privacy advocates have yet to mount device-specific campaigns against the Bee Frontier and Eclipse, but the category's track record suggests scrutiny is likely to follow. For Amazon, the bet is that convenience and continuity—remembering what users would otherwise forget—will outweigh the discomfort of an always-on microphone. The coming holiday season, when the Eclipse reaches shelves, will offer an early verdict.
