# China Extends US Trade Barrier Probes to December

China's Ministry of Commerce has extended the deadline for two trade barrier investigations into US practices and measures to December 27, 2026, prolonging a legal case that sits at the center of the world's most consequential economic rivalry. The ministry announced the extension on September 30, citing the complexity of the case.

The two investigations, launched on March 27, 2026, target what Beijing describes as US actions that disrupt global production and supply chains and US actions that obstruct green product trade. Under the filing rules, each probe was due to conclude within six months; the extension pushes both to the nine-month maximum allowed under Article 32 of the Foreign Trade Barrier Investigation Rules.

## Origin of the Investigations

According to [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/fortune/20260930/38a66e83e37d4068b1c63312c0d37107/c.html), MOFCOM issued Announcements No. 17 and No. 18 of 2026 on March 27, opening the two probes under the Foreign Trade Law of the PRC. The ministry framed the moves as a "reciprocal response" to two US Section 301 investigations that had been launched two weeks earlier.

The US Trade Representative opened the first investigation on March 12, 2026, targeting China and 15 other economies over alleged "overcapacity." A day later, it opened a second probe against China and 59 other economies over an alleged failure to effectively prohibit imports of forced-labor products. At the time, MOFCOM said it was "strongly dissatisfied and firmly opposed" to the US actions and pledged to "resolutely defend" China's legitimate rights and interests.

In its March filings, Beijing enumerated the US measures it intended to scrutinize. The supply-chain investigation covers US restrictions on Chinese products entering the US market, limits on high-tech exports to China, and curbs on two-way investment in key sectors. The green-product investigation covers US restrictions on green product exports, the slowing of new energy project deployment, and limits on related technology cooperation. China argues some of these measures may violate World Trade Organization rules and bilateral agreements.

## A Case Built on Complexity

In announcing the extension, MOFCOM said that "given the complexity of the case," per Article 32 of the rules, it had decided to extend both investigations to December 27. The [March announcement](https://www.news.cn/world/20260327/72a8fa2b858c4a97bccb78eb826ccc41/c.html) had already flagged that the probes could be extended by no more than three months in special circumstances — a window the ministry has now fully used.

The extension lands at a delicate moment. Just days earlier, Chinese President Xi Jinping made an official visit to Washington for a meeting with US President Donald Trump, after which the two sides announced ten outcomes of their trade talks and agreed to extend the so-called Busan trade truce from November 10, 2026 to January 10, 2027. As [Lianhe Zaobao](https://www.zaobao.com.sg/news/china/story20260930-9762563) reported, the deadline extension comes even as the two economies ease tensions, underscoring that détente and mutual pressure are running in parallel.

## What's Next

The investigations give Beijing a legal instrument it can invoke if it chooses to retaliate. MOFCOM has repeatedly said it will "take corresponding measures based on the investigation" — language that leaves open the possibility of new duties, restrictions, or other countermeasures against US goods and firms.

The December 27 deadline is the last stop built into the current rules, meaning China must either conclude the probes or find another legal avenue if it wants to keep them alive. Whether Beijing uses the findings to escalate — or lets the clock run out as a gesture of goodwill while the truce holds — will be a key signal of how stable the latest US-China thaw really is.

For now, both sides appear to be holding positions rather than resolving them: the probes remain open, the truce remains temporary, and the structural disagreements over industrial policy, technology, and green trade remain untouched.
