# Hegseth Unveils Drone Command, Cuts 20% of Generals

Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth announced Wednesday that the Pentagon will create a new four-star command devoted to drones and autonomous systems and cut the military's general and flag officer corps by 20%, part of a sweeping restructuring he framed as necessary to refocus the armed forces on combat readiness. The directives, delivered during his second annual "State of the Force" address at Marine Corps Base Quantico in Virginia, take effect immediately, according to the [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/us-news/hegseth-announces-new-drone-command-cuts-20-generals-rcna600883) report on the speech.

## A New Autonomous Warfare Command

The centerpiece of the announcement was the proposed Autonomous Warfare Command, or AUTOWARCOM, which Hegseth said would become a four-star combatant command by October 1, 2027. According to [Breaking Defense](https://breakingdefense.com/2026/09/hegseth-announces-plans-for-new-4-star-drone-command-further-general-officer-cuts/), the new command would carry "service-like authorities built to scale autonomous and robotic capabilities across the joint force," which Hegseth described as "the fastest peacetime shift in modern military history."

As an interim step, Hegseth said the Pentagon's existing autonomy portfolio manager would be rebranded as "Project Agincourt," to be led by Defense Innovation Unit Director Owen West and Navy SEAL Senior Chief Max Strasiser. The effort aims to fuse operators with entrepreneurs and move "decisions and dollars" closer to frontline units.

The drone push builds on earlier programs, including the Drone Dominance initiative to buy aerial weapons and Joint Interagency Task Force 401. Hegseth pointed to Ukraine's use of drones as a model, saying in the [Federal News Network](https://federalnewsnetwork.com/defense-main/2026/09/hegseth-looks-to-remake-the-military-from-generals-to-drones/) account of the address that "Ukraine's lesson is not that one type of drone wins wars, but rather the need for a purpose-built system that maximizes the ability to adapt."

The announcement comes as the U.S. remains locked in a seven-month war with Iran over control of the Strait of Hormuz, where cheap Iranian drones have killed and wounded American troops while U.S. forces expend hundreds of expensive missile interceptors in defense. Creating a combatant command with acquisition authorities is almost certain to require Congressional approval, as was the case previously for Special Operations and Cyber commands. A Senate version of the fiscal 2027 defense authorization bill calls for a similar "Robotic and Autonomous Systems Combatant Command," though no authorization bill has yet passed.

## Deep Cuts to the Officer Corps

Hegseth said he had directed the services to double the 10% reduction he ordered last spring for flag and general officer positions, producing a total 20% decrease to be completed by January 1, 2027. Federal News Network [reported](https://federalnewsnetwork.com/defense-main/2026/09/pentagon-to-cut-20-of-general-and-admiral-positions-by-jan-1/) that the reduction would remove roughly 170 active-duty generals and admirals from their posts, leaving around 680 in charge of key organizations worldwide. Some billets will be demoted to be filled by colonels and Navy captains, while others will be eliminated altogether.

"The media calls that a purge," Hegseth said. "I call it accountability and long overdue, and frankly, the bare minimum."

More than 80 generals and admirals have already been fired or denied promotions since Hegseth took office in January 2025, including Chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff Gen. CQ Brown, Army Chief of Staff Gen. Randy George, and Chief of Naval Operations Adm. Lisa Franchetti.

The move drew sharp bipartisan scrutiny. Sen. Mark Kelly, D-Ariz., a former Navy combat pilot, said Hegseth is "purging a generation of great leaders of the military, and it's going to take a generation to recover from it," according to NBC News. Sen. Mike Rounds, R-S.D., a member of the Senate Armed Services Committee, said, "If it's simply a matter of eliminating a very heavy top, there may be some validity to it, but I want to see it first."

Some within the military's senior ranks worry that downsizing will discourage the most capable officers from seeking top roles. "People will get out early based on the lack of opportunity," a retired four-star told Federal News Network. "There will be less competition for promotion to 1-star, reducing the overall quality" of the general officer corps.

## Six Initiatives and a Broader Restructuring

Beyond the drone command and officer cuts, Hegseth unveiled several other initiatives, as detailed by [Military.com](https://www.military.com/hegseth-quantico-dod-secretary-covers-ai-religion-war-fighting-gender-and-the-press):

- **Project Meridian**, a 120-day study of the future of warfare overseen by Pentagon Chief Technology Officer Emil Michael and co-led by Elon Musk, Palmer Luckey, and Newt Gingrich. A report is due January 28, 2027.
- **FORTRESS America**, a plan for major installations to generate their own power independent of the civilian grid, reshore supply chains, and harden bases against strikes and cyber threats.
- **A new domestic military base**, the first built on American soil in four decades, housing up to 25,000 troops. Governors have been invited to compete to host it.
- **America's Corps of Cadets**, a new commissioning program based on classical education, expanding pipelines at five universities.
- **An Office of Religious Affairs** reporting directly to the secretary, aimed at empowering chaplains.

## The Press, Faith, and Standards

The address leaned heavily on culture-war themes that have defined Hegseth's tenure. He framed the military as "no longer the woke department or the weak department," adding, "The ideological clowns are out. The patriotic cowboys are in, with testosterone testing on top."

The Office of Religious Affairs drew the sharpest criticism. According to Military.com, Rachel Laser, president of Americans United for Separation of Church and State, said the plan elevates Christianity "to an even higher role in shaping U.S. military policy" and advances a "Christian Nationalist agenda." Hegseth, who included biblical references in the speech, quoted Psalm 33:12: "Blessed is the nation in which God is the Lord."

## Analysis: Ambition Meets Washington Reality

Hegseth's vision is expansive, but much of it depends on forces outside his direct control. AUTOWARCOM cannot exist without Congressional authorization, and lawmakers are divided on the Iran war and the drawdown of munitions stockpiles. Experts say U.S. contractors will need at least three years to replenish key weapons systems used heavily in the conflict.

The officer cuts, meanwhile, raise questions about institutional experience at a moment when the military is conducting major operations around the globe. As Sen. Jack Reed, D-R.I., the top Democrat on the Senate Armed Services Committee, put it, the cuts risk "disrupt[ing] command when continuity matters most." Reed called on Hegseth to justify the reductions to lawmakers in writing before proceeding.

Hegseth, for his part, cast the changes as a restoration. "The warrior ethos has been revived, our military is being rebuilt, and American deterrence is back," he said. "The state of our force today is stronger than it's ever been."

## What's Next

Several timelines now frame the coming months: the officer reduction is due to be complete by January 1, 2027, the Project Meridian report by January 28, and AUTOWARCOM's planned stand-up by October 1, 2027 — assuming Congress authorizes it. Whether lawmakers acquiesce, resist, or reshape the plan will determine how much of Hegseth's blueprint becomes reality, and how quickly.
