# Belgium Dismantles Drug Gang Smuggling Via Brussels Airport

Belgian authorities have dismantled an international criminal organization that smuggled cocaine and cannabis through Brussels Airport for years, arresting 21 suspects in a coordinated operation spanning Belgium, France and the Netherlands, [VRT NWS reported](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/10/01/criminele-organisatie-opgerold-die-jarenlang-drugs-smokkelde-via/).

The operation on Wednesday, carried out with support from Europol and Eurojust, involved 27 house searches — 21 in Belgium and six in France — resulting in 19 arrests in Belgium and two in France. Two additional suspects had been arrested earlier in the Netherlands, [HLN detailed](https://www.hln.be/binnenland/internationale-operatie-legt-drugsbende-bloot-die-jarenlang-smokkelde-via-brussels-airport-21-verdachten-opgepakt~a799d963/).

## How the Network Operated

The investigation began in February 2025, when Halle-Vilvoorde prosecutors opened a case after sports bags containing roughly 30 kilograms of cocaine were found abandoned at Brussels Airport, arriving on a flight from Cancún, Mexico. The discovery led investigators to a man who had booked a ticket but never boarded a flight — using it purely to gain access to the baggage hall. He was among those arrested last week.

The organization's method was unusually elaborate. According to federal police, the network had a member travel on each flight carrying narcotics from Africa or the Americas into Brussels Airport. Couriers then left the airport without collecting the drug-laden suitcases, avoiding the risk of passing through customs controls with the drugs.

The suitcases were later retrieved by "extractors" — recruited mainly through social media — who bought cheap last-minute tickets solely to access the baggage hall. They moved the luggage to agreed locations near the airport, where it was handed to other members of the network.

"Further investigation revealed a criminal organization that consistently had a member travel on the flight on which the narcotics were transported," the federal police said, as quoted by HLN.

## A Tactical Shift After Interceptions

The operation changed after several extractions were intercepted. In April 2025, customs caught a suspect leaving the baggage hall with a suitcase from Canada containing about 30 kilograms of cocaine. He was placed under arrest by the investigating judge. A few weeks later, another suspect was intercepted with two suitcases holding roughly 55 kilograms of cocaine — and investigators later linked the earlier suspect to the same smuggling run.

After these setbacks, the organization adapted. It recruited airport employees to remove drugs from luggage before the suitcases reached the baggage belt. The empty suitcase was then placed on the belt, and the passenger left the airport with a now-empty bag — while the narcotics exited through a route outside the controlled baggage circuit entirely.

"In this way, the criminal organization could bypass the checks of the security services," the federal police stated.

Investigators also found that several members used false or fraudulently obtained identity documents, with some suspects traveling under multiple identities — significantly complicating identification efforts. Customs analyses proved crucial, linking different members of the organization and mapping multiple smuggling operations.

## International Links and Seizures

As the investigation progressed, clear connections emerged with France and the Netherlands. Several members of the organization traveled frequently between the three countries, prompting close cooperation with French and Dutch authorities while Europol and Eurojust coordinated the investigation internationally. Foreign members of the network were identified as a result.

During the action day, authorities seized more than 57,000 euros in cash, cryptocurrency wallets, eight vehicles, two handguns and one alarm pistol, along with valuable goods.

Belgian justice authorities are now seeking the extradition of suspects arrested in France and the Netherlands. Suspects arrested in Belgium are being brought before the investigating judge, who will decide on their continued detention, according to [7sur7](https://www.7sur7.be/belgique/un-trafic-de-drogue-qui-durait-depuis-des-annees-via-brussels-airport-demantele-21-suspects-arretes~a799d963/).

## A Growing Problem at Zaventem

The dismantled network is part of a broader, escalating trend. According to figures from the Halle-Vilvoorde prosecutor's office, the number of drug couriers brought before the investigating judge at Zaventem rose to 123 in 2025, up from 99 the previous year and just 28 in 2023 — a nearly 25 percent year-on-year increase, [CrimeNieuws reported](https://crime-nieuws.nl/explosieve-stijging-drugssmokkel-via-belgische-luchthaven-zaventem-ruim-25-ton-onderschept/).

Most cases involved imports. Thailand was the leading departure point, with 60 suspects, followed by Curaçao, Mexico and the United States. The courier profile has shifted markedly: where Dutch nationals once dominated, 2025 saw mostly British nationals, followed by Dutch, Belgian, French and American suspects — 28 nationalities in total, with many in their twenties.

The type of drugs seized has also changed. Cannabis accounted for the largest share, found on 71 couriers, while cocaine — long the most intercepted drug at Zaventem — was found on 30 people. Ketamine appeared on 12 passengers. In total, authorities seized more than 2,500 kilograms of drugs from passengers in 2025, nearly double the previous year's volume.

On the freight side, customs intercepted more than six tons of cannabis — a dramatic increase. Analysts suggest the partial legalization of cannabis in countries such as Thailand, the United States and Canada may have made it easier for criminal networks to export large quantities.

## What to Watch

The investigation remains ongoing. Federal police say the objective is to identify and prosecute everyone involved in the international network and to confiscate their criminal assets. Multiple large-scale investigations into the organized networks behind airport smuggling continue, with dozens of arrests and searches already carried out across 11 airport-focused files.

For Belgium, the case underscores a structural challenge: Brussels Airport, like the port of Antwerp, has become a significant entry point for illicit narcotics. Officials stress that cooperation between customs, federal police and the airport operator is intended to hinder further smuggling — but with volumes climbing year after year, the pressure on Zaventem's controls is unlikely to ease.
