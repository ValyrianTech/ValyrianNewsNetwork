# New COVID Variant in Belgium: Experts Urge Vigilance

Belgian health experts are calling for vigilance as a new COVID-19 variant circulates in the country, with authorities relying on national wastewater surveillance to track the virus's spread. The warning reflects a broader European picture in which the XFG variant has become dominant across the EU and EEA, even as no strain currently meets the criteria for a "variant of concern."

## What Is Actually Circulating

According to the [European Centre for Disease Prevention and Control (ECDC)](https://www.ecdc.europa.eu/en/covid-19/variants-concern), there are currently no SARS-CoV-2 variants meeting the variant-of-concern (VOC) criteria as of its 25 September 2026 update. The dominant strain in the EU/EEA is XFG, which the agency classifies as a "variant under monitoring" but lists its transmission in the region as "dominant."

XFG carries a set of spike mutations including S31P, K182R, K444R, N487D and T572I. Crucially, the ECDC records "no evidence" that XFG is more transmissible, better at evading immunity, or more severe than earlier lineages — meaning it spreads widely without being shown to be more dangerous.

A second lineage, NB.1.8.1, is also under monitoring and circulating at community level. The ECDC has described it as present at low proportions but "expected to rise in coming weeks" in a prior epidemiological update. The only variant of interest (VOI) remains BA.2.86, a long-circulating Omicron lineage treated as a stable baseline.

## Belgium's Early-Warning System

The country's variant picture is built largely on wastewater surveillance run by [Sciensano](https://www.sciensano.be/en/projects/national-wastewater-based-epidemiological-surveillance), Belgium's national public health institute. The programme launched for SARS-CoV-2 in September 2020 and was expanded in January 2024 to include influenza and RSV. By sampling at wastewater treatment plant intakes nationwide, it covers roughly 39% of the Belgian population — a statistically meaningful early-warning indicator now that mass clinical testing has wound down.

Because it detects rising viral circulation before clinical cases are reported, wastewater monitoring has become Belgium's primary tool for spotting variants as they emerge.

## Experts Urge Calm Alongside Vigilance

When the NB.1.8.1 variant was previously flagged in Belgium — also detected in popular holiday destinations France, Italy, Spain and the UK — virologist Steven Van Gucht offered a measured assessment. Speaking to [Redactie24](https://www.redactie24.be/coronavirus/nieuwe-coronavariant-belgie-en-populaire-vakantielanden-steven-van-gucht-geeft-uitleg-182142), he said the variant did "not yet appear to occur very often" in the country.

Van Gucht noted a geographic nuance that has recurred throughout Belgium's COVID monitoring: "Brussels shows more virus than the rest of the country, but there is no question of a significant increase or a big wave." He also sought to reassure travellers, saying that "in holiday countries the variant remains limited in absolute numbers."

His comments capture the tone of Belgium's post-pandemic approach: authorities monitor and advise rather than impose restrictions, framing variant news around reassurance balanced with continued surveillance.

## Analysis: Vigilance, Not Alarm

The "vigilance" framing is consistent with how COVID-19 is now managed across Europe. The emergence of a dominant but not demonstrably more severe strain is a familiar pattern of SARS-CoV-2 evolution, in which a more transmissible lineage outcompetes its predecessors without necessarily raising the health threat.

The concentration of the virus in Brussels — a dense, highly international city and travel hub — is a recurring theme in Belgian monitoring and a reminder that urban density and mobility shape local transmission. For public health officials, the value of the current system lies less in any single variant alert than in the steady stream of data that wastewater surveillance provides.

## What to Watch

The key indicators in the coming weeks will be whether NB.1.8.1 rises in proportion as the ECDC has anticipated, and whether XFG's dominance translates into any measurable change in severe disease. For now, the official message remains one of watchfulness rather than worry: the virus is circulating, the monitoring is active, and no variant has crossed the threshold into genuine concern.
