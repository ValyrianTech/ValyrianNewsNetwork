# Heroism in Bend: How Bystanders Stopped a School Shooter

A passing truck driver, a local father and a quick-thinking teacher all acted within moments to confront a gunman at Silver Rail Elementary School in Bend, Oregon, authorities said Friday — interventions credited with preventing an even worse tragedy after a Thursday attack that killed three children and a teacher.

According to [OPB](https://www.opb.org/article/2026/10/16/bend-grade-school-shooting-details-unfold/), the first 911 call came in at 12:59 p.m. from the driver of a pickup truck who reported someone was shooting at them. Less than five minutes later, police were on the scene and the suspected shooter was already dead.

## Remembering the Victims

Bend Police Chief Mike Krantz identified 5-year-old kindergartener Rhoam Summer and 25-year-old teacher Megan Lambert among the dead. He did not release the names of the two boys, ages 8 and 11, who were killed, at the request of their families. A 7-year-old child and a 54-year-old teacher were seriously injured; the child is in serious but stable condition at OHSU in Portland, and the teacher — identified by a family spokesperson as Becky Drury — is expected to survive.

Lambert's family, in a statement issued through Bend police, said she was a 2019 graduate of Bend's Mountain View High School and a 2023 graduate of Boise State University. "Her goal was to be an educator and teach in the school district she loved," the family said. "She loved Silver Rail. This was her dream job."

Brad Jolly, Rhoam's grandfather, told OPB the boy was a bright, playful "lovely little kid." "He had a scooter he would run up and down the hallway of our house," Jolly said. "We talked about many things. Read books together. He always gave me a big hug when he left."

## A Rapid Sequence of Intervention

Krantz's account describes a chain of ordinary people responding in rapid succession. At least two drivers saw the 16-year-old suspect, identified by police as Griffin J. John II, approach the school with a gun, authorities said. The teen threw his weapon over a fence and climbed it to reach campus. One driver in a pickup truck verbally confronted him; the teen shot at the truck, striking it at least three times, before that driver drove down the street to call 911.

The man in the second vehicle — a father of two Silver Rail students, identified in press accounts as Kaylub Giamanco — followed the armed teen into the school's parking lot, grabbed his legally secured .40-caliber pistol and followed him inside, Krantz said. Giamanco's mother, Kenda Freeman, confirmed his role to reporters.

Inside, the gunman shot out the windows of locked doors, entered a classroom immediately to the left of the entry and fired as many as 15 rounds. A teacher in that room threw her cell phone at the suspect, then tackled him and fought to take the firearm. She was not injured.

"She almost certainly helped prevent more bloodshed," Krantz said at an emotional press conference. "The teacher who was willing to stand, fight for her children: incredible. And I think we all recognize that that is true heroism."

Investigators recovered a Maverick .88 pump-action 12-gauge shotgun and 44 rounds of ammunition with or near the teen. His specific motives remain unclear, though Krantz said he was previously a student at the school.

## A Community in Mourning

As [OPB reported](https://www.opb.org/article/2026/10/15/bend-reels-after-school-shooting/), hundreds of people gathered at Drake Park's amphitheater hours after the shooting, holding candles and filling the air with hymns and prayers. Dozens more gathered outside Silver Rail on Friday night on the other side of yellow crime scene tape, bringing balloons, candles, crayons and stuffed animals.

For some residents, the violence echoed earlier tragedy. Inge Fryklund, 80, recalled a 2022 shooting at a Bend Safeway. "Like every school since Columbine, somehow you keep going, and then there's going to be another one," she said. "We're just the most recent."

Freeman said her son, Giamanco, is "not seeking recognition" but instead wishes to express gratitude to first responders, school personnel and the community for the outpouring of support.

## Analysis and What's Next

The Bend shooting is likely to sharpen two overlapping debates: how former students can access secured school campuses, and the contested role of armed civilian bystanders in active-shooter scenarios. The teacher's decision to physically confront the gunman has been widely framed as heroism, while Giamanco's actions are already being invoked by advocates on both sides of the concealed-carry debate — some citing him as proof that armed citizens can stop attacks, others cautioning that such outcomes are rare and carry serious risk of mistaken identity.

What remains unresolved is why a former student targeted the school, and how a locked campus was breached so quickly by shooting out door windows. For a city of about 100,000 still absorbing the 2022 Safeway attack, the answers will shape not only school security policy but the community's sense of itself as a safe place. In the days ahead, investigators will continue piecing together the timeline, while Bend begins the longer work of grieving — and, as one young boy wrote on a memorial banner outside the school, of hoping "this never happens again."
