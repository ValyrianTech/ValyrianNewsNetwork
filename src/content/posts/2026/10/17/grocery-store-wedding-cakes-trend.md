# Why More Couples Say 'I Do' to Grocery Store Cakes

Faced with an average wedding bill of roughly $36,000, a growing number of U.S. couples are finding an unlikely place to cut costs: the supermarket bakery. According to [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/10/17/nx-s1-5976921/grocery-store-wedding-cakes), chains such as Publix and Costco are quietly becoming go-to vendors for wedding cakes, offering the ceremonial centerpiece at a fraction of the price charged by specialty bakers. The shift reflects broader consumer anxiety over inflation and tariffs, and a willingness to save on the smaller line items so couples can splurge where it counts.

## The Price of Tradition

The savings can be stark. A specialty baker's wedding cake can exceed $1,000, while a Costco half sheet cake runs about $30, or roughly 70 cents per slice, according to NPR's reporting. A Publix three-tier white-frosted cake, meanwhile, costs about $1.50 less per slice than the national average of $5.50, per [Zola](https://www.zola.com/expert-advice/whats-the-average-cost-of-a-wedding). For a 94-serving Publix chocolate cake with fondant, the Birmingham-area price guide lists $388.

That math explains the appeal for couples like Anna Holleran, who married this year in California's Bay Area. "We love Costco, first of all," she told NPR. "Their cakes are so good. How can you go wrong?"

For many, the cake is one of the few places where trimming feels low-stakes. Zola pegs cake and desserts at an average of about $917, one of the smallest line items in a wedding budget dominated by venues (about $8,573) and catering (about $6,927). "When it comes to a wedding, every couple always has to sit down and say, Where are we going to save, and where are we going to splurge? And cakes just don't seem to rise to the splurge occasion for a lot of couples," Emily Forrest, head of communications at Zola, told NPR.

## An Economic Undercurrent

The trend is a window into how inflation is reshaping celebrations. NPR notes that President Trump's tariff policies have raised prices on imported flowers, bridal gowns and cocoa used in chocolate and desserts. [CBS News, citing Bank of America Institute data](https://finance.yahoo.com/economy/articles/couples-spending-8-5-more-202758825.html), reported that wedding spending among customers was up 8.5% year-over-year in May, even as overall inflation pressure mounted.

Zola's 2026 data captures the mood: 78% of couples worry the economy and tariffs will push their final bill higher, and 84% believe their wedding would cost more today than the same event two years ago. The trade-offs are real; Zola reports that 59% of couples are delaying buying a home to pay for weddings, and 52% are pausing other milestones.

Grocery chains are responding. According to NPR, Publix stores around Birmingham, Alabama, now sell about 50% more wedding cake business than before 2020. "There is definitely big savings when it comes to it," Jeff Brancato, a Publix retail improvement specialist, told NPR. The chain also offers couples a four-flavor tasting flight, including red velvet, carrot, chocolate and vanilla, before they order.

## Keeping the Moment

Grocery store cakes are not without limits. They typically offer fewer flavor options and less customized decoration than specialty bakers, and Costco does not formally market its half sheets as wedding cakes. But for couples, the trade-off preserves what matters most: the ritual of cutting the cake together.

Lily Alana Stewart, who married last year in central Alabama, ordered her cake from Publix and decorated it with baby's breath. "You can't skip the cake! Everybody looks forward to a cake at a wedding," she told NPR. When co-workers learned the source, she said: "They said, Wait, wait, wait. Your wedding cake was from Publix? And they were still mind blown."

Holleran framed the choice in simpler terms. "The real point is that you're sharing a dessert for the first time with somebody that you love," she told NPR. "It's kind of symbolic of all the sweet moments that you're supposed to share in a marriage."

## What to Watch

The grocery cake trend is likely to persist as long as inflation and tariff pressures weigh on household budgets. Zola's Samantha Kobrin told NPR that economic anxiety is widespread, but couples are "just being more intentional about their trade-offs so they can focus on what really matters." Whether supermarkets expand further into wedding services, or specialty bakers respond with more accessible price points, will determine how permanent the shift becomes. For now, the cake aisle is doing a brisk business in happily-ever-after.
