# China Q3 High-Frequency Data Points to Steadier Growth

China's economy showed firmer momentum across consumption, trade and investment in the third quarter of 2026, according to high-frequency indicators released by a government-affiliated think tank — data that officials present as evidence the world's second-largest economy is stabilizing with progress and improving in quality.

The figures, compiled by the Big Data Development Department of the State Information Center under the National Development and Reform Commission (NDRC), were published by [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/fortune/20261011/4ed20b0b019d4d5c83dad5547bf99338/c.html) on 11 October. They cover the July–September period and September specifically.

## Consumption Accelerates

Offline consumption payment amounts rose 2.6% year-on-year in the third quarter, 0.7 percentage points faster than in the second quarter, Xinhua reported. September offline payments grew 3.1% year-on-year, up 0.5 percentage points from August — the third consecutive month of acceleration.

A companion report from [People's Daily](https://www3.xinhuanet.com/fortune/20261011/1e66e6a3d2e04f29899627aae5779ceb/c.html) added that commodity consumption grew 4.5%. Data from Shouqianba, a payment indicator tracking small and mid-sized merchants, showed September payment amounts up 5.7% year-on-year, 1.3 percentage points faster than the previous month.

"Since the start of the third quarter, China's various consumption-promotion policies have continued to expand and intensify, residents' willingness to consume has steadily recovered, and market consumer demand has been continuously released," said Wei Ying, deputy director of the Big Data Development Department at the State Information Center, according to Xinhua.

## Trade and Investment Firm

Foreign trade indicators were notably strong. Load tonnage for cargo vessel departures at major ports grew 43.1% year-on-year in the third quarter, while arrivals rose 0.3% — both improvements over the second quarter. In September alone, departures grew 53.2% and arrivals 4.5%, Xinhua reported.

Investment showed a similar upward tilt. New special-purpose bond issuance in September grew 50.2% year-on-year, 43.6 percentage points faster than in August. Bidding for projects tied to computing power, data and network new infrastructure climbed 34.2% in the third quarter and 50.4% in September alone, according to [Sina Finance/Caiwen](https://finance.sina.com.cn/jjxw/2026-10-11/doc-iniuvhum1857374.shtml).

The sharpest growth came in frontier sectors: capital investment in artificial intelligence and humanoid robotics rose 184.1% year-on-year in the third quarter, and 198.7% in September. Sina Finance also reported that the national construction-machinery operating rate — the so-called excavator index — rose 1.71 percentage points month-on-month in September, with 28 provinces posting gains, nine more than in August.

"In the third quarter, especially in September, various policies to stabilize investment were implemented as a coordinated combination... investment in frontier fields such as artificial intelligence maintained strong momentum," Wei said.

## An Official Series, With a Caveat

The release is part of a recurring Xinhua series that presents positive high-frequency indicators, typically compiled by the same NDRC-affiliated agency. High-frequency data — payment volumes, port tonnage, bond issuance, project bids and machinery operating rates — are alternative indicators designed to track activity with less lag than official GDP figures. They are not National Bureau of Statistics (NBS) data, and the figures could not be independently cross-verified against official third-quarter GDP statistics, which had not yet been published at the time of the report.

That distinction matters. The data originates from a government agency and is presented with an explicitly optimistic framing, and analysts have offered a more mixed picture.

KPMG's [China Economic Monitor: Q3 2026](https://kpmg.com/cn/zh/insights/2026/08/china-economic-monitor-q3-2026.html) describes an economy marked by K-shaped divergence — external demand stronger than domestic, new industries stronger than traditional ones, services more resilient than industry, and property prices stabilizing in high-tier cities while continuing to fall in lower-tier ones. It notes that AI-led new momentum is growing quickly but is not yet large enough to fully offset the contraction of traditional growth drivers.

Reported first-half GDP growth was about 4.7%, with second-quarter growth at roughly 4.3%. The report casts the coordination of existing stock policies and new incremental measures as the driver of the third quarter's improvement.

## What to Watch

The high-frequency release is widely read as an effort to project economic resilience and optimism ahead of the NBS's full third-quarter GDP figures. The key question is whether the momentum seen in consumption, exports and frontier-sector investment translates into headline growth strong enough to keep China within its annual target range — and whether the AI-driven new economy can grow fast enough to bridge the gap left by cooling traditional sectors.

For now, the official read is one of a finely balanced recovery. The independent view is that the balance is tilting unevenly across regions, industries and income groups — a divergence that full GDP numbers may soon make harder to smooth over.
