# China Mortgage Subsidy Speeds Up to Ease Homebuying

China's first-ever central-government mortgage interest subsidy took effect nationwide on October 1, and major state banks have moved quickly to deliver it — offering eligible first-time buyers a 1-percentage-point discount on their home loans and helping lift property transactions during the country's peak autumn sales season.

The policy, announced on September 29 by the Ministry of Finance, the People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the National Financial Regulatory Administration, applies a 1-percentage-point annualized subsidy to qualifying first-home commercial mortgages, with support lasting up to five years on loan principal of as much as 1 million yuan ($140,000). According to [People's Daily](http://finance.people.com.cn/n1/2026/1011/c1004-40812298.html), the measure is part of a broader effort to reduce the burden on homebuying families and support so-called "rigid demand" among young, first-time and returning migrant buyers.

## How the Subsidy Works

The program carries three strict eligibility conditions, all of which must be met: the buyer must use a newly issued commercial individual housing loan to purchase a first home (refinancing existing loans does not qualify); the property's floor area must not exceed 120 square meters; and the purchase price must be no more than 1.5 million yuan (about $210,000).

According to [Caixin](https://finance.caixin.com/2026-09-29/102489686.html), the 1-point subsidy equates to roughly one-third of the current interest rate on first-home commercial mortgages. On a long-term 1 million yuan loan, the policy can cut total interest payments by nearly 50,000 yuan over five years. The program is tentatively set to run for one year, and funding is split between the central government (90%) and provincial and local authorities (10%).

A key design feature is "免申即享" — no-application, auto-enjoy processing. Borrowers do not need to file a separate subsidy request. Banks assess eligibility at the point of loan processing and automatically deduct the subsidy from each repayment installment once the loan is approved. The six largest state banks — ICBC, Agricultural Bank of China, Bank of China, China Construction Bank, Bank of Communications and Postal Savings Bank of China — published implementation announcements the day after the policy was released and set up service booths at property sales sites, housing expos and agency branches.

## Early Signs of Impact

The timing placed the subsidy squarely in the middle of the "Golden September, Silver October" sales season and the week-long National Day holiday. Market data released on October 9 pointed to a meaningful pickup. Citing China Index Academy figures, [CLS reported](http://news.cnfol.com/guoneicaijing/20261009/32390965.shtml) that new-home transactions across 25 key cities reached 551,000 square meters during the October 1–7 holiday, a daily average increase of 33.8% from the same period a year earlier, while second-hand transactions across 11 key cities rose 42.8% year-on-year on a daily basis.

First-tier cities led the gains. In Shanghai, second-hand home transactions monitored by Lianjia rose 44% year-on-year and new-home volumes climbed 64%; in Shenzhen, real-time second-hand signings tracked by Beike increased 63%. Beijing saw daily new-home registrations jump 176% year-on-year, according to China Index Academy data.

Banks issued subsidized loans from day one. China Construction Bank's Sichuan branches issued 2.1 million yuan in qualifying loans to four buyers on the first day, while ICBC's Chengdu branch issued eight loans worth 4.228 million yuan. During the holiday week, China Construction Bank's Guangdong branches processed more than 500 million yuan in subsidized loans, according to People's Daily.

The policy's effect was felt most directly by individual families. A buyer surnamed Chen in Guangzhou, who purchased a 92-square-meter two-bedroom flat with an 800,000 yuan loan from Agricultural Bank of China, said the subsidy would save her about 40,000 yuan in interest. "After working in Guangzhou for so many years, I finally have my own small home, and with the good subsidy policy, the burden on my shoulders is much lighter," she said.

## A Broader Policy Push

The subsidy complements an earlier credit reform. On August 28, the PBOC and the financial regulator issued new rules that extended the maximum individual mortgage term from 30 to 40 years and raised the debt-to-income ratio ceiling from 55% to 60%, as [Caixin detailed](https://finance.caixin.com/2026-08-28/102479458.html). The August rules also shifted loan disbursement for presold homes to after project completion, aiming to reduce buyers' concerns about stalled construction.

The central subsidy also builds on a wave of local experiments. Since September 2025, 13 Chinese cities have rolled out their own housing-loan subsidy policies, beginning in second- and third-tier cities and gradually expanding toward first-tier and strong second-tier markets, [Caixin noted](https://www.caixin.com/2026-08-29/102479511.html).

## Analysis: A Bump, Not Necessarily a Turning Point

Analysts are cautious about reading too much into the holiday surge. The subsidy appears to have primarily activated "revisit" customers — buyers who had already viewed homes and were hesitating — rather than entirely new demand, and its impact has been strongest on lower-priced projects that fit the 1.5 million yuan and 120-square-meter caps.

According to [Caixin Global](https://www.caixinglobal.com/2026-09-29/beijing-steps-in-with-mortgage-subsidies-to-revive-housing-demand-102489811.html), the measure is a first for low-income groups from the central government and signals Beijing's reliance on coordinated fiscal and monetary tools to release pent-up housing demand. Yet the price ceiling largely excludes tier-one cities, where homes routinely exceed 1.5 million yuan, limiting the policy's reach in the most expensive markets.

Cao Jingjing, general manager of index research at China Index Academy, said the post-holiday "Silver October" performance will be an important window for judging the fourth-quarter property market. Xiao Xiaoping, dean of the Shenzhen Beike Research Institute, credited the dual rollout of the August and September policies with activating holiday demand, noting that many buyers chose to complete consultations, viewings and even signings during the break.

One property-industry observer cautioned that the National Day period is itself a peak marketing window and that registration data can be distorted by holiday timing and base effects. Short-term double-digit gains, the observer said, do not necessarily signal a full market reversal — a reminder that the subsidy may be steadying demand rather than reversing the sector's long downturn.

## What to Watch

With the subsidy tentatively set to last only one year and the central government funding the bulk of its cost, the key questions are whether the boost outlasts the holiday season and how much genuinely new demand it draws in. Much will depend on the "Silver October" figures and on follow-through from broader housing-sales reforms. For now, the policy has done what it set out to do: put cash in buyers' pockets and pull some hesitant families off the sidelines.
