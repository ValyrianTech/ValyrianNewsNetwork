# Katie Bailey Tapped as White House Legislative Director

President Donald Trump has named Katie Bailey to lead the White House Office of Legislative Affairs, elevating a seasoned Capitol Hill aide to the administration's top liaison post with Congress less than a month before the November midterm elections.

Trump offered Bailey the position on Friday, October 9, and she accepted, according to [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/katie-bailey-new-head-of-white-house-office-of-legislative-affairs-sources/). The president confirmed the move in a Truth Social post on Saturday night, writing that Bailey "will be fantastic, and work tirelessly with Congress to pass Bills that help, MAKE AMERICA GREAT AGAIN."

Bailey replaces James Braid, who stepped down after leading the office. Braid's final day in the role is Tuesday, October 13.

## Context

The Office of Legislative Affairs serves as the administration's chief channel to Capitol Hill, tasked with shepherding the president's agenda through Congress. Vice President JD Vance had described the director's job as "one of the toughest jobs in the entire Administration," praising Braid for steering the administration's biggest legislative wins — including a sweeping tax cut, immigration and border-wall funding, a landmark crypto bill, and what he called the fastest Cabinet confirmation process in decades.

Bailey inherits the post at a delicate moment. Both chambers of Congress are held by slim Republican majorities, and [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/trump-administration/trump-announces-katie-bailey-lead-white-house-outreach-congress-rcna602727) noted that the balance of power is at stake in the midterms. If Democrats capture one or both chambers, advancing the president's legislative priorities would become considerably harder.

## Key Developments

Bailey is not new to the White House or to Congress. She currently serves as both special assistant to the president and deputy director of legislative affairs, a role in which she liaised with the Senate and, per Trump, helped secure the confirmations of Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin and Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton.

Her LinkedIn profile indicates she served as special assistant to the president from September 2025 to June 2026 before moving into the deputy director post, and that she held numerous legislative roles on Capitol Hill — most recently as legislative director for Sen. Mike Braun, R-Ind., during his six-year Senate term. That tenure ended in January 2025, when Braun was elected governor of Indiana.

The appointment is the second high-profile White House personnel announcement in as many days. It follows Trump's naming of Katie Zacharia as his new White House press secretary, a pairing that suggests a broader staffing refresh ahead of the midterms.

Trump praised the outgoing director, saying Braid "has done a fabulous job, and is now going into the private sector, where he will be very successful." Braid had announced his exit in late August, saying he would leave in September, and described himself as "profoundly grateful" to Trump and Vance. Before joining the administration, Braid served as Vance's top policy aide and deputy chief of staff while Vance was a senator.

## Analysis

Bailey's promotion is, in one sense, a continuity move: she already knows the office, the players, and the president's priorities, which could smooth a transition at a time when the White House has little margin for a learning curve. Her background as a Senate legislative director for a Republican senator also signals an administration betting that relationships and procedural fluency on the Hill will matter more than ever.

But the timing carries its own risks. Personnel changes this close to an election can be read as either steadying or disruptive, and the incoming director's effectiveness may ultimately hinge on an outcome she cannot influence: whether Republicans keep their majorities. A Democratic-controlled chamber would transform her role from advancing an agenda to defending against one.

The back-to-back announcements — a legislative director and a press secretary within 48 hours — also hint that the White House is consolidating its communications and legislative operations for the final stretch of the campaign season, positioning itself for whichever political landscape emerges in November.

## What's Next

Bailey formally takes over when Braid departs on Tuesday. Between now and the midterms, her immediate task will be maintaining the administration's congressional relationships during a period when many lawmakers are focused on their own reelection fights. The larger test — and the one most likely to define her tenure — will come after voters render their verdict on control of Congress.
