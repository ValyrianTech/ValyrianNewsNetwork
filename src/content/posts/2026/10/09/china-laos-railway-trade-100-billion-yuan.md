# China-Laos Railway Trade Value Tops 100 Billion Yuan

Cumulative import and export cargo value on the China-Laos Railway has surpassed 100 billion yuan, Kunming Customs announced, marking a milestone for the flagship Belt and Road Initiative project linking Kunming in southwest China with the Lao capital, Vientiane. As of 7 October 2026, the railway had operated more than 25,000 international freight trains and carried over 20.77 million tonnes of goods worth more than 100.4 billion yuan (approximately USD 14.9 billion) since it opened in December 2021, according to [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/fortune/20261008/d767443a640d467cb0c6cf57dffb4f4d/c.html).

The milestone underscores the railway's transformation from a regional transport corridor into what Chinese customs officials describe as an international logistics artery, deepening economic ties between China and Southeast Asia.

## From Landlocked to Land-Linked

The 1,035-kilometer electrified railway was conceived as a flagship project under China's Belt and Road Initiative, designed to turn Laos from a landlocked country into a "land-linked" hub at the center of Southeast Asian trade. Kunming Customs reported that import and export cargo volumes on the route have grown at an average annual rate exceeding 24% since operations began.

The railway's reach now extends to 19 countries and regions, including Laos and Thailand, and its commodity categories have expanded to more than 3,900 types, serving over 7,000 enterprises across China, the customs authority said.

Regional media have tracked the corridor's rapid expansion. According to [The Laotian Times](https://laotiantimes.com/2026/08/19/laos-china-railway-trade-surges-as-cross-border-freight-hits-new-highs/), imports and exports along the route reached 17.17 billion yuan (USD 2.53 billion) in the first half of 2026, up 33.8% year-on-year. The railway has carried more than 90 million tonnes of freight since opening, including more than 21 million tonnes across the China-Laos border. At its busiest, it now handles up to 23 cross-border freight trains a day.

## A Shifting Trade Mix

The composition of goods moving along the railway reflects a complementary trade relationship. In the first eight months of 2026, Southeast Asian tropical fruits — durians, mangosteens and longans — imported via the railway reached 5.26 billion yuan, up 12.3% year-on-year, per [People's Daily](http://en.people.cn/n3/2026/1009/c90000-20506407.html). In the same period, exports of mechanical and electrical products reached 7.42 billion yuan, a surge of 58.4% year-on-year, as the share of high-value, high-technology goods such as smartphones, industrial robots and new energy vehicles continues to rise.

The regular operation of specialized cold-chain trains has been central to the fruit trade, cutting delivery times for fresh produce. Ren Zhengwei, deputy general manager of the Kunming branch of China Railway Container Transport Corp., credited the cold-chain service with enhancing the timely delivery and freshness of imported fruits such as fresh Thai durians.

Kunming Customs said it has implemented a special initiative to facilitate cross-border trade, deploying large container inspection systems and 5G smart devices at the Mohan railway port to improve clearance efficiency and openness.

## Lower Costs, Wider Connections

The economic effects extend beyond customs figures. According to [CGTN](https://news.cgtn.com/news/2026-06-01/China-Laos-Railway-How-trade-travel-boom-along-the-golden-corridor-1NCOjmmQIs8/p.html), citing a World Bank report, the route has cut shipping costs between China and Laos by 40% to 50% and reduced domestic logistics expenses within Laos by 20% to 40%. Transit times between Kunming and Vientiane have fallen from three to seven days to just one or two.

The railway is also becoming part of a broader regional network. In June 2026, Boten Railway Station in Laos began its first major expansion since opening, and Lao railway authorities signed an agreement to join an international rail framework linking Vientiane to the Baltic Sea through China, Vietnam, Central Asia and Russia. Goods carried on the line now reach 31 Chinese provinces.

The 2026 milestone carries added symbolic weight: it falls in the year marking the 65th anniversary of China-Laos diplomatic relations.

## What to Watch

With passenger services also expanding — four international trains now run daily between Kunming and Vientiane — the corridor's dual role in freight and travel appears set to grow. The key questions ahead concern whether the railway can sustain its double-digit cargo growth as it approaches its fifth year of operation, and how competing regional rail and port projects will shape Southeast Asia's evolving logistics map. For now, the 100-billion-yuan threshold stands as the clearest evidence yet that the Kunming-Vientiane line has moved from promise to functioning trade artery.
