# Belgian King Begins Landmark State Visit to Kazakhstan

King Philippe and Queen Mathilde of Belgium departed Sunday for a three-day state visit to Kazakhstan, the first-ever Belgian state visit to Central Asia and, according to Kazakh officials, the first state visit by any European monarch to the country. The trip, running through 14 October, is aimed at deepening economic, academic and diplomatic cooperation between Brussels and Astana as Europe sharpens its focus on a region long overshadowed by Russia and China.

The royal couple will meet President Kassym-Jomart Tokayev in Astana before travelling to Almaty, the country's largest city, [RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/visite-d-etat-au-kazakhstan-le-couple-royal-se-rend-au-kazakhstan-pour-sa-premiere-visite-d-etat-en-asie-centrale-11797394) reported. The visit programme centres on bilateral cooperation in the economic, university and cultural domains.

## A Deliberately Weighted Delegation

The scale of the Belgian delegation signals the visit's importance. It comprises 110 people, including roughly 25 chief executives, seven university rectors and four ministers, according to [La Libre](https://www.lalibre.be/belgique/societe/2026/10/04/une-visite-detat-inedite-attend-le-roi-philippe-et-la-reine-mathilde-il-y-a-beaucoup-de-sens-a-consolider-nos-liens-V3REYFCDVJFL5NLCA6NEWNEYMM/). Foreign Minister Maxime Prevot stayed behind for budget negotiations, with Mobility and Climate Minister Jean-Luc Crucke taking his place. Flemish minister Cieltje Van Achter, Brussels Minister-President Boris Dillies and Walloon Minister-President Adrien Dolimont are also on the trip.

The visit also carries a personal dimension. King Philippe travelled to Kazakhstan three times as crown prince — in 2002 and 2009 for the launches of Belgian astronaut Frank De Winne's ISS missions from Baikonur, and in 2010 to lead an economic mission — [RTL Info](https://www.rtl.be/actu/belgique/famille-royale/une-premiere-historique-le-couple-royal-va-senvoler-pour-une-visite-detat/2026-10-09/article/802740) noted.

## The Middle Corridor at the Centre

A central theme of the visit is the Trans-Caspian International Transport Route, known as the Middle Corridor, a trade artery linking China and Asia to Europe while bypassing Russia. Its strategic value has surged since Russia's 2022 invasion of Ukraine.

According to [Kazakhstan Today](https://www.kt.kz/rus/authors_column/korol_belgii_posetit_kazahstan_pochemu_etot_vizit_1378001598.html), freight volumes on the route have grown fivefold in six years, from 0.8 million to 4.1 million tonnes annually, with a target of 10 million tonnes. The corridor runs via the Caspian Sea, the South Caucasus, Azerbaijan, Georgia and Turkey. For Belgium and its ports, the route offers a major logistics alternative to Asia; for Kazakhstan, it reinforces its role as a hub between China, Turkey, the Middle East and Europe.

The visit programme also includes the fourth edition of the Kazakhstan-Belgium Business Council, created in 2023, whose agenda focuses on logistics and agriculture. According to the [Benelux Chamber of Commerce in Kazakhstan](https://benelux.kz/news/korol-belgii-posetit-kazahstan-s-istoricheskim-gosvizitom), Belgium ranks among Kazakhstan's top ten investors, having invested roughly $15 billion over the past two decades, with bilateral trade exceeding $580 million last year. Tokayev has said that more than 110 companies with Belgian capital operate in the country.

"Belgium has the expertise. Kazakhstan has the ambition and a strategic position. Bringing these assets together is obvious," Prevot said, as quoted by Kazakhstan Today.

## Diversification, Not Endorsement

Belgian officials frame the visit as part of a broader strategy of diversifying partnerships, following recent state visits to South Africa, Vietnam and Chile. One organiser told Kazakhstan Today that Central Asia "is not a region we are used to travelling to, yet it is a part of the world of strategic importance and offering real opportunities for Belgium."

That framing matters because Kazakhstan presents a delicate balance for European governments. It is the EU's largest supplier of crude oil after the United States and Norway, but its exports still flow mainly through Russia, which is why the Middle Corridor has become a shared priority. The country of about 20.6 million people shares the world's longest continuous land border with Russia — 7,591 kilometres — and also borders China.

Kazakhstan's ambassador to Belgium, Roman Vassilenko, described the war in Ukraine starkly: "For us the war in Ukraine is not a war happening somewhere on the Moon. It is happening right next to us." He also stressed that Astana pursues a "multi-vector" foreign policy: "We have no adversaries."

Belgian officials have been careful to avoid the impression of blanket endorsement. A cabinet staffer told Kazakhstan Today that "the state visit should not be taken as a sign of unconditional approval," pointing out that the King has also made a state visit to China. Human-rights and corruption questions, including the historical "Kazakhgate" scandal, remain part of the backdrop. Vassilenko insisted that "today we have a new president, a new Constitution and a new Kazakhstan. So all that is history."

## A New Constitution and a Wider European Push

That new Constitution, adopted in March 2026 and in force since 1 July, created a single-chamber parliament — abolishing the Senate — and a new People's Council. The ambassador says the president now holds "somewhat less power" than before, though independent observers contend that Tokayev has in fact accrued greater authority.

The visit sits within a wider European re-engagement with Central Asia, including an EU-Central Asia summit in Samarkand in April 2025 and a €12 billion Global Gateway investment plan for the region. Belgium opened an embassy in Tashkent, Uzbekistan, in September 2026, preparing a second diplomatic foothold in the region; Prevot has said he plans a 2027 tour of all Central Asian states plus Mongolia.

Belgium recognised Kazakhstan's independence on 31 December 1991, formalised diplomatic ties in 1992 and long maintained its only Central Asian embassy in Astana.

The programme also includes a visit to the Alem.AI International Centre for Artificial Intelligence, linking the trip to Belgium's own domestic debate on AI — a subject Queen Mathilde addressed at a roundtable on 6 October. Identified sectors for cooperation include transport and logistics, critical minerals, petrochemicals, agriculture, digital transformation and finance.

## What to Watch

The visit comes just two weeks before Kazakhstan's Republic Day on 25 October and is being treated in Astana as a milestone. Yet its real test will be whether symbolic diplomacy translates into concrete projects — particularly along the Middle Corridor, where infrastructure bottlenecks and coordination across multiple countries remain formidable. For Belgium, the gamble is that early engagement in a strategically vital region will yield dividends as Europe seeks reliable partners beyond Russia and reduces its dependence on China. Belgium is not alone in that race, but the state visit suggests it intends to move early.
