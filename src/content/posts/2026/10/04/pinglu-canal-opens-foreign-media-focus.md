# Pinglu Canal Opens, Drawing Global Attention

Foreign media across Asia, the Middle East, Africa, and Europe are spotlighting the full navigation of the Pinglu Canal (平陆运河), a landmark waterway in southern China's Guangxi region that officially opened to shipping on September 16, 2026, after four years of construction. The 134.2-kilometer canal, built at a cost of roughly 72.7 billion yuan (about US$10.83 billion), connects southwest China's inland river network to the Beibu Gulf and is being widely described as a strategic gateway for trade with Southeast Asia.

According to [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/20261004/27f34d66938f47279c577648f9f12528/c.html), the state news agency that compiled the foreign coverage, outlets in Vietnam, Qatar, Saudi Arabia, Malaysia, Rwanda, Kenya, Canada, and Spain have all examined the canal's implications for regional logistics, trade, and China's engineering capacity.

## A Canal That Connects River to Sea

The Pinglu Canal is the first waterway built under national-level coordination since the founding of the People's Republic of China that links an inland river system directly to the sea — a milestone described in Chinese as "通江达海," or "connecting river to sea." It is a backbone project of the New International Land-Sea Trade Corridor, the trade route that the country has been developing to move goods between western China and global markets.

The canal runs from the Pingtang River mouth in the Xijin reservoir area of Hengzhou, Nanning, passes through Lingshan County in Qinzhou, and follows the Qin River south to the Beibu Gulf. Built to China's highest inland navigation standard, it can accommodate 5,000-tonne vessels.

Construction began on August 28, 2022. The canal was fully filled with water on June 3, 2026, entering a period of water-based debugging, and reached full navigation on September 16, 2026 — ending what officials describe as the historical situation in which southwest China's rivers and seas were "not connected."

## Regional Trade Implications

Much of the foreign commentary focuses on what the canal means for trade between China and the Association of Southeast Asian Nations (ASEAN). [Xinhua's ASEAN-focused analysis](https://www.news.cn/world/20260917/a8568f9b43b4467da4d2e18ce51709f3/c.html) noted that Vietnam's Da Nang Newspaper described the canal as an important part of the New International Land-Sea Trade Corridor, one that would strengthen economic ties between China and ASEAN through goods, investment, and cross-border cooperation — offering ASEAN exporters a high-volume, low-cost logistics option.

Qatar's Al Jazeera observed that the canal arrives as China–ASEAN trade grows rapidly, linking inland shipping with the Western Land-Sea Corridor and potentially supporting trade and industrial-chain cooperation. Saudi Arabia's Al-Eqtisadiah wrote that the canal would not only boost connectivity between China's inland regions and overseas markets but also further drive China–ASEAN economic and trade exchanges.

Malaysia's Malay Mail argued that the canal's economic impact would far exceed transport itself, with the bigger opportunity being for ASEAN agricultural products, commodities, manufactured goods, and increasingly high-tech intermediate goods to reach China's western market. It urged regional governments to focus on customs coordination, port connectivity, multimodal transport, trade document digitization, and industrial cooperation.

Kenya's KeNews said the canal is expected to change the logistics landscape of southwest China and offer a reference for other countries exploring river-sea intermodal transport.

## The Numbers Behind the Project

Chinese officials have projected substantial economic returns. Zhang Zhiwen, deputy secretary-general of the Guangxi Zhuang Autonomous Region Government, said cargo from southwest China sailing to the sea via the canal shortens the inland voyage by more than 560 kilometers compared with traditional routes, cuts comprehensive logistics costs by 18% to 30%, and saves society over 5 billion yuan in transport costs annually.

To accommodate the new traffic, the Beibu Gulf Port Group upgraded 13 berths in the Qinzhou port area, 11 of which have been fully accepted, adding about 20 million tonnes of river-sea intermodal capacity, according to the [China-Global South Project](https://chinaglobalsouth.com/2026/09/21/china-pinglu-canal-guangxi-asean-trade-route/), which has tracked the canal's development.

The broader trade context is significant. China–ASEAN trade in the first half of 2026 reached 4.34 trillion yuan, up 18.2% year-on-year, according to China Customs figures cited by Al Jazeera.

## Beyond Pure Economics

Not all commentary frames the canal in purely commercial terms. Jean-Paul Rodrigue, a professor of maritime management at Texas A&M University at Galveston, told Canada's RCI that direct investment return is not the canal's only goal. "Once the new corridor is created, all sorts of knock-on effects follow, such as new investment, new markets, and new trade relationships," he said.

Rwanda's "Africa-China Review" called the canal an important milestone in strengthening connectivity between China's inland economy and global markets, saying its construction complexity demonstrates China's maturing engineering technology. A companion piece described it as "yet another infrastructure miracle."

Spain's El País noted that China is advancing multiple waterway projects in Jiangxi, Henan, Hubei, and Hunan provinces to reduce freight costs and connect inland regions to international markets — suggesting the Pinglu Canal is part of a broader national push.

Foreign investment is already responding. Thomas, the Nanning project director for France's SNF chemical group, said the Pinglu Canal was a key factor in the company's decision to invest and build a plant in Nanning, and that the canal would help the company develop more production lines and set up an R&D center.

## China's Engineering Ambitions and Questions Ahead

The canal has been presented as a showcase of Chinese engineering. To reduce ecological impact, the project incorporated animal crossing bridges, bird ecological corridors, and fish migration channels. The navigation channel uses permeable ballast-type block revetments, and monitoring systems based on BeiDou satellite navigation, cloud computing, and artificial intelligence enable full-segment visual control and advance warning of navigation risks, according to [Chinese-language reference material](https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/平陆运河).

The project also fits into a wider regional pattern. Cambodia's Chinese-backed Funan Techo Canal, valued at about US$1.7 billion, is a parallel undertaking, and Cambodia's ambassador has expressed hope that the two canals will complement each other.

A note of skepticism exists as well. Some overseas commentary has argued that cutting through the watershed could cause serious ecological disruption — a counterpoint worth acknowledging even as the canal's supporters emphasize its environmental mitigation measures.

## What to Watch

With the canal now operational, attention turns to whether the projected logistics savings materialize and whether the economic belt that Guangxi hopes to build around the canal attracts the industrial clusters officials envision. Hubs such as the Nanning Liujing Industrial Park have already drawn major investors including BYD, Sun Paper, and France's SNF.

For ASEAN economies, the key question is whether coordinated customs procedures, port links, and digital trade documentation can keep pace with the new physical infrastructure. For China, the canal is both an economic tool and a demonstration of its capacity to execute large-scale engineering — one that foreign observers, from Doha to Nairobi, are now watching closely.
