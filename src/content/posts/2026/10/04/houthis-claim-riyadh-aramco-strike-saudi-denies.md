# Houthis Claim Riyadh Aramco Strike; Saudi Denies

Yemen's Houthi (Ansar Allah) armed group said on Saturday, October 3, 2026, that it struck a Saudi Aramco oil facility in Riyadh with ballistic missiles and drones, claiming "precise and direct" hits that set the site ablaze. Saudi authorities and Aramco did not confirm the attack, and the Saudi-led coalition later dismissed the Houthi claim as "misleading" — leaving the central factual dispute unresolved even as witnesses reported flames near the facility.

## The Claim and the Denial

The Houthi military spokesman, Yahya Saree, announced the operation in a televised statement, saying the group carried out a "qualitative military operation with a number of ballistic missiles and drones targeting Aramco in Riyadh, the capital of the Saudi enemy," according to [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/world/20261004/c5664d395ba8458f8f062c33f9a451f4/c.html). Saree said the strike was "precise and direct and led to fires breaking out at the targeted sites," describing it as a response to recent Saudi strikes on Sanaa and other Houthi-controlled areas.

Saree added that Yemen had been hit by 60 airstrikes and missile strikes in the preceding 24 hours, bringing the cumulative total since the current escalation began to 1,410 — a figure that [Anadolu Agency](https://www.aa.com.tr/ar/%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AF%D9%88%D9%84-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B9%D8%B1%D8%A8%D9%8A%D8%A9/%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AD%D9%88%D8%AB%D9%8A-%D9%8A%D8%B9%D9%84%D9%86-%D9%82%D8%B5%D9%81-%D8%A3%D8%B1%D8%A7%D9%85%D9%83%D9%88-%D8%A8%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B1%D9%8A%D8%A7%D8%B6-%D9%88%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AA%D8%AD%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%81-%D9%8A%D9%86%D9%81%D9%8A-%D9%85%D8%B2%D8%A7%D8%B9%D9%85-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%A7%D8%B3%D8%AA%D9%87%D8%AF%D8%A7%D9%81/4077292) reported as 1,420. The group vowed to continue "blockade for blockade, and escalation for escalation" until Saudi Arabia halts its attacks and lifts restrictions on ports and airports in Houthi-held territory.

The Saudi-led coalition's spokesman, Brig. Gen. Turki al-Maliki, rejected the claim, saying the Houthi assertions of targeting Riyadh on Saturday were "misleading" and that the group was trying to "divert attention from the scale of their losses and the growing popular anger" at its conduct. The denial was carried by Saudi media including Asharq Al-Awsat and cited by Anadolu.

## Fire Seen Near Aramco Site

Independent witnesses offered partial corroboration that something had occurred. An AFP journalist reported flames and black smoke rising from an Aramco facility south of Riyadh, with fire trucks, ambulances and police seen near the site, according to [Al-Araby TV](https://www.alaraby.com/news/%D8%A8%D9%85%D8%B3%D9%8A%D8%B1%D8%A7%D8%AA-%D9%88%D8%B5%D9%88%D8%A7%D8%B1%D9%8A%D8%AE-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AD%D9%88%D8%AB%D9%8A%D9%88%D9%86-%D9%8A%D8%B9%D9%84%D9%86%D9%88%D9%86-%D8%A7%D8%B3%D8%AA%D9%87%D8%AF%D8%A7%D9%81-%D9%85%D9%86%D8%B4%D8%A3%D8%A9-%D9%84%D8%A3%D8%B1%D8%A7%D9%85%D9%83%D9%88-%D9%81%D9%8A-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B1%D9%8A%D8%A7%D8%B6). [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/10/4/houthis-claim-strike-on-saudi-energy-facility-as-yemen-fighting-intensifies) also reported that a large plume of smoke and fire was seen rising near an Aramco facility in Riyadh. Neither Saudi authorities nor Aramco commented on the fire's cause.

It was the second Houthi attack on an Aramco facility in Riyadh within two weeks. Two weeks earlier, the group targeted an Aramco fuel tank near King Khalid International Airport — its first attack on the Saudi capital since 2022, Al-Araby TV reported.

## A Widening War

The strike claim comes amid a rapid military escalation on multiple fronts. Early on Sunday, the Saudi-led coalition said its forces carried out 97 "precise targeting operations" against Houthi reinforcements on the Tor al-Baha front and the Taiz axis, using warplanes, drones, artillery, missiles and snipers. Colonel Majed Abdullah al-Nazili, spokesman for Yemen's internationally recognized government forces, said the attacks targeted "at least 260 Houthi fighters and destroyed or disabled 44 military vehicles," according to Al Jazeera.

On Friday, the coalition said it intercepted and destroyed two ballistic missiles and four drones in Khamis Mushait and Jazan in southern Saudi Arabia. Earlier in the week, the coalition said the Houthis attacked a power station in the holy city of Medina. Late last month, the Houthis said they struck Aramco facilities in Yanbu, a Red Sea port; Saudi Arabia said it intercepted six ballistic missiles fired toward Taif and Yanbu with no reported damage to Aramco sites.

## Why It Matters

The dispute carries significance beyond the immediate exchange of fire. Houthi control of the Bab al-Mandeb strait — a vital route for global oil shipments — directly threatens Red Sea shipping and global energy logistics, and the group declared a maritime "blockade" on Saudi Arabia in July.

According to reporting by Reuters cited by [DW](https://www.dw.com/ar/%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%AD%D9%88%D8%AB%D9%8A%D9%88%D9%86-%D9%8A%D8%B9%D9%84%D9%86%D9%88%D9%86-%D8%A7%D8%B3%D8%AA%D9%87%D8%AF%D8%A7%D9%81-%D9%85%D9%86%D8%B4%D8%A3%D8%A9-%D9%84%D8%A3%D8%B1%D8%A7%D9%85%D9%83%D9%88-%D9%81%D9%8A-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B1%D9%8A%D8%A7%D8%B6-%D9%88%D8%B3%D8%B7-%D8%AA%D8%B5%D8%A7%D8%B9%D8%AF-%D8%A7%D9%84%D9%85%D9%88%D8%A7%D8%AC%D9%87%D8%A9-%D9%85%D8%B9-%D8%A7%D9%84%D8%B3%D8%B9%D9%88%D8%AF%D9%8A%D8%A9/live-79496943), regional and Western officials say Saudi Arabia is planning a major offensive against the Iran-aligned Houthis. Options under consideration include a limited operation around Bab al-Mandeb to secure the Red Sea corridor, or a wider multi-front attack across Al-Bayda, Marib, Taiz and Al-Jawf. Saudi-supervised Yemeni forces would lead ground operations backed by Saudi airstrikes, with more than 100,000 Yemeni troops potentially mobilized. The United States is already providing intelligence support, and some regional and European states are supplying defensive military assistance.

The war itself has deep roots: Yemen has been at war since the Houthis seized Sanaa in 2014, and since March 2015 a Saudi-led coalition has backed the internationally recognized government. Fighting intensified after a fragile 2022 truce collapsed in July 2026, displacing hundreds of thousands of people as the Houthis captured the port city of Mocha and strategic islands in the Bab al-Mandeb strait.

## What's Next

The immediate question — whether the Riyadh strike was as successful as the Houthis claim — may never be fully verified, since neither side has an incentive to settle it publicly. What is clear is that the conflict is broadening: from the Red Sea shipping lanes to the Saudi capital itself, and increasingly toward the prospect of a large-scale Saudi ground offensive. Whether that offensive materializes in the coming weeks will likely determine whether the current escalation remains a war of attrition or tips into a new phase of the conflict.
