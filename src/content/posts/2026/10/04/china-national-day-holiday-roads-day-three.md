# China's Holiday Roads Stay Smooth on Day Three

China's national road network remained "overall smooth and orderly" on the third day of the National Day holiday, the Ministry of Public Security's Traffic Management Bureau said, as millions of travelers continued their journeys across the country. Traffic volume declined from its early-holiday peak but stayed at a high level of operation, according to [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/politics/20261003/5b126b162fd14ae590d1bf0f511e603c/c.html), which reported the bureau's update from Beijing.

According to Xinhua, traffic flow "continued to decline but remained at high-level operation" through October 3. As of 7:00 p.m. local time that day, the agency described conditions nationwide as stable and well-managed. No major incidents or casualty figures were reported, and the official framing was a routine status update rather than a breaking-news event.

## Context: A Record-Heavy Travel Season

The 2026 National Day holiday runs from October 1 to 7, with toll-free passage for passenger vehicles of seven seats or fewer in effect from the first day at midnight until midnight on October 7. This year the holiday stands apart from the Mid-Autumn Festival, which fell separately on September 25 to 27 and did not carry the same highway toll exemption—unlike years when the two periods overlap and concentrate travel into a single surge.

The Ministry of Transport had forecast an average of about 62.8 million vehicle-trips per day on China's expressways during the holiday, according to [IT之家 (ITHome)](https://m.ithome.com/html/1009031.htm). October 1 was expected to be the single busiest day, with daily flows of 62 million to 64 million vehicle-trips holding through October 6 before return traffic disperses.

## Key Developments: Controlled Traffic and New Energy Pressure

During the holiday period, traffic management departments at all levels adjusted patrol and deployment plans to match holiday travel patterns, reinforcing guidance and controls around scenic areas and key road sections while strictly enforcing traffic violations, Xinhua reported.

The bureau also issued safety advisories to drivers. Motorists were urged to monitor weather, plan travel times and routes reasonably, and—in rain or fog—to "slow down, use taillights, control distance" (减速、亮尾、控距). The agency warned against drunk driving during holiday gatherings, stressed seat belts for all occupants, and reminded motorcyclists and e-bike riders to wear helmets. On highways, it cautioned against speeding and fatigued driving, and advised that after an accident or breakdown drivers should immediately "move the vehicle to the side, evacuate people, and call police" (车靠边、人撤离、即报警).

A growing share of the holiday traffic is electric. New energy vehicles accounted for roughly 27 percent of total daily flow—about 16.8 million vehicle-trips—and NEV travel ran at about 1.8 times the normal daily volume, ITHome [reported](https://m.ithome.com/html/1009227.htm). Charging demand followed a distinct pattern: "dispersed on the outbound trip, concentrated on the return," with peak demand expected on October 5 from 11:00 a.m. to 9:00 p.m. and October 6 from 10:00 a.m. to 10:00 p.m.

Provinces including Zhejiang, Jiangsu, Anhui, and Shandong anticipated record highway charging volumes, with the Changshen, Shenhai, and Hukun expressways the busiest for NEV charging. State Grid pre-deployed 435 emergency charging guns at service areas prone to queuing, and the Ministry of Transport established a list of "especially busy charging service areas" to focus mobile and high-power fast-charging equipment where it was needed most.

## Analysis: A Daily Data Point in a Bigger Story

Daily holiday traffic updates from China's public security and transport authorities are a familiar genre—brief, official, and reassuring. Their real value lies not in any single day's bulletin but in the aggregate picture they sketch: a motorized, increasingly electrified traveling public moving in vast numbers across a dense highway network.

The 2026 numbers suggest the sheer scale of that movement is the story. With average daily expressway flows near 63 million vehicle-trips and NEVs at more than a quarter of the total, the holiday is a stress test not just of road capacity but of the charging infrastructure that a rapidly electrifying fleet depends on. The "dispersed outbound, concentrated return" charging curve also reveals a structural challenge: outbound travel spreads demand across days and routes, but the return trip compresses it into a narrower window on a smaller set of corridors.

Officials have responded with forecasting and pre-positioning—busy-service-area lists, emergency charging capacity, and real-time tools like the "e-Lu Changtong" WeChat mini-program for locating charging points and calling for roadside assistance. Whether such measures keep pace with NEV adoption in future holidays will be a key measure of China's green-transport transition.

## What's Next

Attention now turns to the return-leg peak. October 5 and 6 are the core days for return travel, and authorities expect highway charging to surge during the announced peak windows, with pressure concentrated on major inter-provincial corridors and city-entry roads. October 7, the final day, could see brief concentrated exits of small passenger cars near the midnight toll-free cutoff.

For travelers, the message is unchanged: watch the weather, plan routes, and drive carefully. For policymakers, the holiday offers another data-rich rehearsal for managing a massive and increasingly electric mobility system.
