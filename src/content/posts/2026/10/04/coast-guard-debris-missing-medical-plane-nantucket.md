# Coast Guard Finds Debris from Missing Plane Off Nantucket

The U.S. Coast Guard has located an extensive debris field off the coast of Nantucket, Massachusetts, from a Canadian-operated air ambulance carrying six people that vanished early Saturday while flying from Bermuda to Boston, according to [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/10/03/g-s1-146338/plane-missing-coast-guard-search-massachusetts). The search for the six people on board - two Bermudian nationals and four Canadians - continued through the weekend, with rescuers still hunting for the jet's flight data recorder.

![U.S. Coast Guard crews search by air and on the water for a missing air ambulance off the coast of Nantucket, Massachusetts.](https://bbc.bm/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/coast-guard-1.webp)

## What Happened

The Gulfstream G100, tail number C-GRJP, departed L.F. Wade International Airport in Bermuda at 12:41 a.m. ET Saturday, bound for Boston Logan International Airport, the [Bermuda Broadcasting Company](https://bbc.bm/no-people-recovered-yet-from-lost-air-ambulance) reported. The aircraft was registered to Latitude Air Ambulance, a Hamilton, Ontario-based operator. Its four crew members - two pilots and two medical staff - were all Canadian, and the two passengers were a Bermudian patient and one family member.

The FAA issued a search-and-rescue alert at roughly 1:20 a.m. and notified the Coast Guard at about 2 a.m. that contact had been lost. Latitude Air Ambulance's operations manager, Clarence Togeretz, said the company lost contact with the aircraft around 1:30 a.m. as it approached Boston and alerted authorities after the plane failed to arrive.

## Final Communications

Air traffic control audio indicates the pilot declared an emergency before contact was lost. According to [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/us-news/coast-guard-searching-nantucket-missing-jet-bermuda-boston-rcna601265), controllers asked the aircraft whether it was "coming in blind," then asked a nearby Ethiopian Airlines jet to attempt contact - with no response. A fire rescue dispatcher warned emergency crews to prepare for the plane's arrival, describing it as 15 minutes away, suffering from electrical issues and having lost radio and radar, audio reviewed by [The Boston Globe](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2026/10/03/metro/debris-found-medical-plane-boston/) showed.

Flight tracking data showed the jet dropped from 24,000 feet to about 11,000 feet in roughly two minutes before briefly leveling off and disappearing. A source with knowledge of the investigation told [ABC News](https://abcnews.com/US/medical-transport-airplane-missing-off-massachusetts-coast-coast/story?id=136969608) that officials believe the plane descended about 7,500 feet before hitting the water.

## The Search Operation

The debris field was found roughly 20 miles off Nantucket and confirmed to be associated with the missing Gulfstream, the Coast Guard said. Speaking at a Sunday press conference, Capt. John McTamney said the field sits in water about 80 to 90 feet deep, and Rear Adm. Matthew W. Lake described it as "extensive," spanning some 30 nautical miles wide.

By Sunday's briefing, the Coast Guard reported having searched roughly 2,840 square miles over about 53 hours, using fixed-wing aircraft, helicopters, boats and cutters, working alongside the New York National Guard's Air Rescue Wing and Massachusetts authorities, according to [CP24](https://www.cp24.com/local/toronto/2026/10/04/large-and-demanding-search-continues-after-debris-found-from-missing-ontario-air-ambulance/). The Massachusetts Environmental Police were expected to survey the area with sonar equipment.

"Our search remains ongoing," Lake said. "Our priority is to locate the six personnel on that aircraft. My thoughts and those of every Coast Guard crew member are with the families on that aircraft."

No people had been recovered as of Sunday, and the jet's black box had not yet been located - in part, officials said, because the debris field is so spread out.

## A Cross-Border Flight

The disappearance links three jurisdictions: a Canadian operator and crew, Bermudian passengers and a search conducted in U.S. waters. Officials from each responded.

Canada's Minister of Transport, Steven MacKinnon, said he was aware of a Canadian-registered air ambulance that went missing and that four Canadians were among those on board. "My thoughts are with everyone on board, as well as their families, loved ones and colleagues during this incredibly difficult time," he said, adding that Ottawa was monitoring the situation and working with relevant authorities. Bermuda Premier David Burt said his "thoughts are with everyone on board and their families during this deeply worrying time," and that the island was "praying that all six people will be found safe," as [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/10/3/air-ambulance-missing-on-flight-from-bermuda-to-boston) reported.

Togeretz, of Latitude Air Ambulance, said the company was "praying for a safe outcome." The firm, which has operated since 2009 and advertises a "flawless safety record," said this was the first time it had lost contact with one of its aircraft. Ontario's air ambulance service, Ornge, expressed solidarity with its colleagues, and the Massachusetts Port Authority, which operates Boston Logan, said its thoughts were with those on board and their loved ones.

## Analysis: Why This Case Stands Out

Air ambulances occupy a distinctive niche in aviation: they fly urgent, often long-distance missions with patients whose survival depends on timely transport, frequently over water and at hours when margins for error are thinnest. That this aircraft departed Bermuda in the middle of the night with a critically ill patient aboard underscores how little slack exists in such operations.

The clues gathered so far - a declared emergency, a pilot reported to be "coming in blind," lost radio and radar, and a rapid, thousands-of-feet descent - point to a serious in-flight failure rather than a gradual deterioration. But investigators have been careful not to speculate. The Coast Guard has said it is "just interested in finding the people" and has declined to detail what materials have been recovered, though it confirmed items have been preserved for a future investigation by aviation officials.

The breadth of the debris field and the water depth could complicate recovery of both the victims and the flight data recorder. The Massachusetts Environmental Police's sonar survey may prove critical to mapping the site before divers or remotely operated vehicles can work it.

## What's Next

The Coast Guard is expected to provide further updates as the search and investigation develop. The immediate priorities are locating the six people on board and recovering the black box, which would help explain why the jet went down. Until then, the cause remains undetermined, and officials from Canada, Bermuda and the United States will continue to coordinate across borders on both the rescue and the eventual inquiry.
