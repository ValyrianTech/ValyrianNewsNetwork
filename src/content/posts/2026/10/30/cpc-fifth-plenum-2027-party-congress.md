# CPC Sets 2027 Congress, Codifies Xi Party-Building Thought

The Communist Party of China closed the Fifth Plenary Session of its 20th Central Committee on 29 October, adopting a sweeping decision on Party self-governance and announcing that the 21st National Party Congress will convene in Beijing in the second half of 2027, according to the [official communique](https://www.news.cn/politics/leaders/20261029/3dc769be3bff40aeafcbf957eb4e47bb/c.html) released by Xinhua. The gathering, expected to shape the leadership line-up for the next five years, will take place at what the Party describes as a critical juncture on the road to the Second Centenary Goal.

## What the Plenum Decided

The four-day session, held 26–29 October in Beijing, drew 162 full Central Committee members and 117 alternates, with Central Discipline Inspection Commission members and selected grassroots delegates attending as non-voting participants. General Secretary Xi Jinping delivered the Politburo's work report and an explanation of the draft decision.

The centerpiece was adoption of the "Decision on Persisting in Comprehensively and Strictly Governing the Party" — a programmatic document that formally names **Xi Jinping Thought on Party Building** as the "Party-building chapter" of Xi Jinping Thought on Socialism with Chinese Characteristics for a New Era. The communique characterizes it as a major achievement in advancing the Party's theoretical innovation.

In a separate [announcement](https://www.news.cn/politics/20261029/83773200cd8646a28d563e4bec9e072b/c.html) confirming the congress timing, Xinhua stated the plenum decided the 21st National Congress will be held in Beijing in the second half of 2027 — a decision the Party frames as "a major event in the political life of the Party and the state."

## Personnel Reshuffle

The plenum produced several consequential personnel moves. It co-opted **Zhang Shengmin**, a vice-chairman of the Central Military Commission, as a full member of the Politburo — a rare mid-term addition to the Party's top decision-making body. It also promoted ten alternate members to full Central Committee status, including Lu Dongliang, Bao Gang, Xing Shanping, Qu Yingpu, Lü Jun, Zhu Tianshu, Zhu Guoxian, Liu Jun, Liu Jie and Liu Qiang.

The session also confirmed a large slate of disciplinary actions. It approved CCDI reports on 18 civilian officials and CMC reports on 16 military personnel, ratifying earlier Politburo decisions to expel high-profile figures including former Central Military Commission vice-chairman Zhang Youxia, former Xinjiang party secretary Ma Xingrui, and former International Department head Liu Jianchao. Former financial regulator Yi Huiman and ex-industry chief Jin Zhuanglong were among the others expelled. Li Yunze received the lesser sanction of removal from Party posts rather than expulsion.

## A Defining Emphasis on Discipline

Unusually for a Fifth Plenum at this stage of a Central Committee's term — sessions that often focus on economic planning — this one centered squarely on Party self-governance and ideological consolidation. The communique acknowledges that previous years saw "weakened, diluted and marginalized Party leadership" and problems "shocking to the eye," stating that the Party has "eliminated serious hidden dangers existing within the Party, the state and the military" through sustained anti-corruption work.

That framing signals the leadership's priorities ahead of the 2027 congress. By codifying a distinct body of thought on Party building, elevating discipline messaging, and reshaping the Central Committee through expulsions and promotions, the leadership is consolidating ideological and organizational control before the venue where the next leadership reshuffle will occur.

## Economic Message and What's Next

Alongside the governance agenda, the communique urged officials to complete this year's economic targets and secure a "good start" to the 15th Five-Year Plan period, which began in 2026. It emphasized macro policy support, domestic demand, reform and opening, and risk prevention, and called on newly appointed leadership teams to adopt a "correct view of political achievements."

The immediate question for observers is how far the reshaped Central Committee will go toward personnel renewal before 2027. The scale of elite turnover — and the Party's own admission of serious internal dangers — underscores continued focus on discipline and control at the highest levels. With the congress date now fixed, attention turns to the preparation campaign and the cadre decisions that will shape China's leadership heading into the next five-year cycle.
