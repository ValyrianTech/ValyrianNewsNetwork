# 15 Chinese Provinces Outpace National Growth in Q3

Fifteen of China's 31 provincial-level regions posted GDP growth above the 4.6% national rate in the first three quarters of 2026, according to an analysis of regional economic reports published by [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/20261030/efb1aaca153e42ecb5270e35e917d94b/c.html). Tibet led the country at 6.2%, while Shandong and Zhejiang tied for second at 5.6%, as major economic provinces continued to act as a stabilizing "ballast" for the national economy.

The reports, compiled from provincial statistics releases issued over the past week, offer a snapshot of a cautiously recovering economy in the opening year of China's 15th Five-Year Plan. They also reveal widening structural gaps beneath the headline numbers: growth increasingly stems from high-tech manufacturing and new industries, even as fixed-asset investment and the property sector remain under heavy pressure.

## Major Provinces Lead the Pack

By total economic output, the top ten provinces all exceeded 4 trillion yuan. Guangdong ranked first at 11.109 trillion yuan, followed closely by Jiangsu at 10.863 trillion yuan. Shandong, Zhejiang, Sichuan, Henan, Hubei, Fujian, Shanghai and Anhui rounded out the top ten.

The fifteen provinces that beat the national rate were Tibet, Shandong, Zhejiang, Shanghai, Anhui, Jiangsu, Beijing, Qinghai, Henan, Gansu, Ningxia, Sichuan, Inner Mongolia, Tianjin and Hubei.

"Local economies have generally continued their recovery trend, with regional development showing steady progress and narrowing divergence, while major economic provinces have performed relatively steadily," said Pang Ming, a member of the China Chief Economist Forum. He noted that growth improvement now relies less on traditional factor inputs and more on industrial structure optimization and the cultivation of new growth points.

Fu Yifu, a special researcher at Suning Bank, said the mid-to-high growth of major provinces, along with rebounds in Shandong, Zhejiang and Shanghai, "shows that policy effects and industrial upgrading are taking effect." Pang added that major provinces, with their large scale, complete industrial chains and active market entities, "often serve as a 'ballast' and bellwether" for the wider economy.

## New Drivers Power the Recovery

High-tech manufacturing emerged as the clearest differentiator between fast- and slow-growing regions. Anhui's high-tech manufacturing value-added jumped 50.3%, while Hubei's rose 45.5% and contributed 102.4% of the growth in its industrial value-added, driven by optoelectronics and high-end equipment. Beijing posted 22.1% growth in high-tech manufacturing and a 16.2% rise in strategic emerging industries, with service robot output up 170% and industrial robot output up 47.6%.

Investment followed the same pattern. Ningxia recorded a 120% increase in high-tech industry investment and a 230% rise in computing-power investment, while Tianjin and Sichuan posted gains of 41.8% and 25.3% respectively. "Relying on advantageous industries to accelerate transformation, the effect of high-tech industry investment is gradually emerging, becoming an important pillar of the province's investment," said Yang Zhigang, deputy director and spokesperson of the Sichuan Provincial Statistics Bureau.

At the national level, new drivers — high-end manufacturing, the digital economy and modern services — contributed more than 40% of growth in the first three quarters, according to [Yicai](https://www.yicai.com/news/103398866.html). The services sector grew 5.2%, faster than the overall economy, and contributed 66.3% of growth, making it the main engine. NBS spokesperson Mao Shengyong highlighted the "AI+" initiative as a new growth engine, with AI accelerating from technological innovation into industrial application.

## Investment and Property Remain a Drag

Despite the upbeat growth figures, the underlying investment picture is fragile. Fixed-asset investment fell 7.2% year-on-year, manufacturing investment slipped 2.1%, infrastructure investment declined 4.2%, and real estate development investment plunged 20.6%, worsening by 0.7 percentage points from the January-August period.

Domestic demand contributed more than 70% of growth, with final consumption expenditure contributing close to 50%, yet analysts warn that weak household income growth and uneven consumption recovery remain unresolved. Mao acknowledged the tension directly: the international environment is "complex and changeable, the problem of insufficient domestic demand remains prominent, and the foundation for steady improvement still needs to be consolidated."

Fu cautioned that while the dual high growth of high-tech output and investment reflects accelerated cultivation of new quality productive forces, policymakers must guard against "emphasizing investment over application and duplicate construction."

## What to Watch

With the full-year growth target within reach, attention now turns to fourth-quarter policy support. Analysts expect continued fiscal measures, including new policy financial instruments and additional local government bonds to bolster infrastructure investment.

Pang argued that the next stage's policy focus should remain on expanding domestic demand and stabilizing market expectations — boosting consumption, stabilizing corporate expectations and strengthening private investment willingness. Local governments, he said, should cultivate new growth drivers based on their own industrial bases rather than "simply pursuing speed targets."

The Q3 data suggest China's regional economies are diverging along a clear line: provinces that moved early into high-tech manufacturing and strategic industries are pulling ahead, while those still reliant on traditional investment and property face mounting pressure. Whether that divergence narrows — or widens — will depend on how effectively the coming stimulus reaches consumers and private investors.
