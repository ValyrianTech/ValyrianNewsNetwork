# Cop on Trial in Death Ruled Suicide as FBI Alleges Murder

BOSTON — When Sandra Birchmore was found dead in her Canton, Massachusetts apartment in February 2021, local investigators quickly concluded she had taken her own life. Five years later, a former Stoughton police detective is standing trial in federal court, accused of strangling the 23-year-old and staging the scene to look like a suicide — a case that now effectively puts the original police investigation itself on trial.

Matthew Farwell, 40, is charged with killing a witness or victim and with causing the death of Birchmore's unborn child, according to [AP News](https://apnews.com/article/police-officer-massachusetts-killing-sandra-birchmore-76069b1c984c3add28879970bcdf1764). He has pleaded not guilty and faces a potential life sentence if convicted. Prosecutors wrapped up their case on Wednesday after calling 38 witnesses over 12 days; the defense is now presenting its own witnesses, with the jury potentially beginning deliberations as early as next week.

## A death ruled suicide

On Feb. 1, 2021, Farwell entered Birchmore's apartment building at 9:14 p.m., according to security camera footage cited at trial. A digital forensics expert testified that Birchmore's phone stopped moving at 9:40 p.m., three minutes before cameras recorded Farwell leaving. Her body was found three days later, seated beneath a doorknob with a duffel bag strap tied tightly around her neck and affixed to the door handle.

The state medical examiner, relying partly on information from police, initially ruled the death a suicide. Prosecutors say that conclusion was shaped by investigators who never wavered from their judgment — and who reassured others that no foul play was involved.

"Not only did they not investigate, they very quickly told others, including the medical examiner, that this was definitively a suicide and there was no foul play," Assistant U.S. Attorney Torey Cummings told jurors.

The prosecution's characterization of the original probe is damning. Farwell was interviewed for just 15 minutes in a parking lot, investigators took no notes, did not examine him for injuries, and did not sample his DNA. Police looked at his phone only after he had already deleted his texts with Birchmore. The lead investigator — a longtime family friend of the Farwells — told Farwell's twin brother that he wanted the case closed, saying he needed the investigation like he needed a "bullet in the head."

## A decade of alleged grooming

Birchmore met Farwell when she was about 12 years old and a participant in the Stoughton Police Department's Explorer program, a law-enforcement mentorship program for young people interested in policing. Farwell, then about 24, soon friended her on Facebook and later offered to tutor her.

According to [The Marshall Project](https://www.themarshallproject.org/2024/08/30/police-explorer-sexual-abuse-massachusetts-birchmore), which has extensively investigated abuse in police Explorer programs nationally, prosecutors allege Farwell groomed and controlled Birchmore for roughly a decade. The two exchanged some 40,000 texts over the years, some of which were read aloud at trial. In one 2020 exchange, Birchmore recalled the day they first had sex — April 10, 2013, when she was 15 and he was 27. "Best day of my life," she wrote. "Me too," Farwell replied.

Prosecutors allege Farwell tracked her phone location, punished her with violent sex acts, and met her for sex while on duty more than two dozen times. In one 2020 message, he wrote: "I'll grab your throat and say you won't say a word to anyone or you will be sorry."

The relationship became more volatile when Birchmore became pregnant. She believed Farwell was the father — prosecutors say the two had made a deal that he would impregnate her in exchange for her silence — and she pressed him to sign a birth certificate. When she texted him the news, Farwell responded that she was "truly the worst person on the face of the earth." In January 2021, a woman called Stoughton police to report the relationship, and prosecutors allege Farwell, convinced he had lost control of her, began plotting to silence her permanently.

"It was no longer a matter of whether his crimes would be exposed; it was a matter of when," Cummings said. "He had lost control of Sandra. He had a real problem. He needed a way to silence her permanently."

A paternity test later revealed Farwell was not the father of Birchmore's unborn child. The biological father was Army recruiter Jason Pickard, who testified that he had a sexual relationship with Birchmore starting in 2019.

## Forensic evidence points to homicide

The case has hinged on a reversal by the state medical examiner and on expert testimony that the physical evidence contradicts a suicide.

Dr. Maria Del Mar Capo Martinez, the medical examiner who originally ruled the death a suicide, amended her ruling in 2026, deeming the manner of death "undetermined" and the cause "asphyxia." She testified that had she known homicide was a possibility, she might have approached the examination differently.

Dr. William Smock, an emergency medicine physician who trains law enforcement on staged crime scenes, testified that Birchmore was strangled and struggled before dying. As [MassLive](https://www.masslive.com/news/2026/10/expert-tells-farwell-jury-sandra-birchmores-injuries-point-to-homicide-not-suicide.html) reported, Smock cited a fracture of the hyoid bone — which he said does not occur from incomplete hangings in people under 30 — along with hemorrhages below the ligature, scratches consistent with fingernails clawing at the strap, scratches around the nose consistent with suffocation, and a chest injury matching the strap's clasp. That last finding, he said, indicates she was killed before being tied to the doorknob.

"This is an altered crime scene and she was killed before the ligature was tied to the doorknob," Smock testified. "When you take all of those injuries, you can see Ms. Birchmore was the victim of a homicidal act."

DNA evidence offered further support for the prosecution. Elizabeth Duval, a State Police DNA supervisor, testified that Farwell's DNA was linked to a portion of the duffel bag strap tied to the doorknob, as [MassLive](https://www.masslive.com/news/2026/10/matthew-farwells-dna-linked-to-duffel-bag-strap-found-around-sandra-birchmores-neck.html) reported. She said it was 6.2 trillion times more likely the DNA came from Farwell, Birchmore and two unknown people than that Farwell did not contribute at all. His DNA was also found in semen stains on Birchmore's underwear. The defense, however, has emphasized that Farwell's DNA was not found under Birchmore's fingernails.

## The defense: a troubled woman, not a murder

Farwell's attorneys have acknowledged their client had an affair but insist he is no killer. Attorney Kimberly Stevens told jurors Birchmore was a troubled woman who began taking psychiatric medication at age 6, had attempted suicide as a teenager, and was desperate after becoming pregnant. Birchmore had less than $3 in the bank and was alone for the holidays, Stevens said. Farwell, she argued, went to the apartment to break up with her, not to kill her.

"He has made regrettable mistakes. He is human," Stevens said. "Matthew did not kill Sandra Birchmore."

The defense has called a psychiatry professor and Birchmore's ex-boyfriend to reinforce the suicide theory, and cross-examined Smock about his lack of formal forensic pathology credentials. Prosecutors have countered with friends, a therapist and an OB-GYN who say Birchmore was thriving and excited about her pregnancy.

## What the case says about the system

How Birchmore's death was investigated has drawn as much scrutiny as the killing itself. The FBI began investigating in 2023, partly at the urging of former Stoughton Deputy Chief Brian Holmes, who had led an internal affairs probe and concluded Farwell had a sexual relationship with Birchmore. Farwell resigned before those findings were released and was decertified as an officer in March 2024.

Birchmore was allegedly sexually involved with at least three Stoughton officers associated with the Explorer program — Matthew Farwell, his twin brother William, and program director Robert Devine — underscoring what critics describe as systemic, not merely individual, failure. William Farwell testified under a grant of immunity; he and Devine have denied wrongdoing.

Former acting U.S. Attorney Joshua Levy, announcing the 2024 indictment, said federal authorities allege Birchmore survived "years of grooming, statutory rape and then sexual violence, all at the hands of Matthew Farwell" — and that when he could no longer control her, he silenced her permanently. He stressed that the FBI's investigation is ongoing, raising the possibility of further charges.

## What's next

As the jury prepares to weigh the competing narratives, the pivotal question is how it will balance Birchmore's documented mental health struggles against the increasingly detailed forensic case for homicide. Deliberations are expected to begin as early as the week of Nov. 2, 2026.

Beyond the verdict, the case continues to raise unanswered questions about accountability. There is no public indication yet of disciplinary action against the investigators who handled the 2021 probe, and the Birchmore family's wrongful death lawsuit remains paused pending the criminal case. Activists and the Marshall Project's reporting have also fueled broader scrutiny of police Explorer programs and the youth-safety oversight meant to protect the teenagers who join them — questions that will linger long after this trial ends.
