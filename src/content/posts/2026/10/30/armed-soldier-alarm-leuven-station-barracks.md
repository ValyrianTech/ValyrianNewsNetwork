# Armed Soldier Sparks Alarm at Leuven Station By Getting Lost

A uniformed professional soldier carrying a weapon on his back caused a scare at Leuven railway station during Friday morning rush hour, prompting several worried commuters to call the police. The alarm turned out to be unnecessary: the serviceman was simply lost on his way to the military barracks in Heverlee and had stopped to ask for directions.

According to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/10/30/gewapende-militair-vindt-weg-niet-naar-kazerne-en-zorgt-voor-onr/), the soldier was in full kit, with a large backpack and a weapon slung across his back, and was wandering alone through the station at one of its busiest moments of the day.

The sight of an armed serviceman in a crowded civilian transport hub was enough to raise concern. Leuven station, a major hub in Flemish Brabant, is traditionally packed during the morning commute, and multiple travellers phoned the police after spotting the man on his own.

## Police Find the Soldier Asking for Directions

A police patrol was dispatched to the scene and located the soldier inside the Lijnwinkel, the De Lijn public transport ticket shop at the station, where he was trying to find the way to his destination.

"We sent a patrol to the scene and found the soldier in the Lijnwinkel," Marc Vranckx of the Leuven police told VRT NWS. "It turned out to be a French-speaking soldier on exercise, from Eupen. He was asking there for the way to the Heverlee barracks. Our officers went and dropped the lost soldier off at the barracks gate."

The soldier, who has not been named, is a French-speaking professional from Eupen, the eastern Belgian city that serves as the capital of the country's German-speaking Community. He was travelling in full equipment as part of an exercise, which explains why he was armed and carrying a large pack in public.

## A Benign Incident, but a Sensitive One

No injuries were reported, no arrests were made, and there was no suggestion of any hostile intent. The episode was resolved within minutes once officers established who the man was and where he needed to go.

Yet the reaction it drew speaks to a heightened public vigilance in Belgium when it comes to armed military personnel in civilian spaces. In 2021, the high-profile case of Jürgen Conings — an armed soldier who went missing with weapons taken from a barracks and made threats against public figures — left a lasting impression. Many Belgians today are quicker to report an armed serviceman acting unusually, even when the explanation turns out to be entirely mundane.

The incident also unfolded against a backdrop of increased visibility for the military. Defence has featured prominently in Belgian public debate in recent months, and the sight of uniformed soldiers in transit is not unusual as exercises and deployments increase.

## What to Watch

The Leuven police response was quick and proportionate, escorting the soldier to his destination rather than escalating the situation. No official statement from the Belgian Defence department was immediately available, and it remains unclear why the soldier was navigating the route alone and unaccompanied.

The episode may invite questions about how soldiers on exercise are transported or guided to their barracks — and whether clearer arrangements could prevent a repeat of a scare that, in this case, ended with nothing more serious than a wrong turn.
