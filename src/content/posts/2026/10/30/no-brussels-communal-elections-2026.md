# No, Brussels Is Not Holding Communal Elections in 2026

A report circulating this week claimed that political parties in Brussels are fine-tuning their candidate lists ahead of a "2026 communal election." The premise is false: Belgium is not holding municipal elections this year. The country's last communal and provincial vote took place on **13 October 2024**, and its next is scheduled for **October 2030**.

## What the calendar actually says

The confusion likely stems from the phrase *"municipales 2026,"* which refers to France's municipal elections, not a Belgian ballot. Belgian municipal councils serve **six-year terms**, and the country's electoral schedule places the next communal and provincial elections in 2030, according to the overview of [elections in Belgium](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89lections_en_Belgique).

The most recent local elections were held on Sunday, 13 October 2024, when voters across the country — including in Brussels' **19 communes** — elected communal and provincial councillors, as documented in the record of the [2024 Belgian communal and provincial elections](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89lections_communales_et_provinciales_belges_de_2024). Those ballots followed the federal, regional and European elections of 9 June 2024 during what was widely described as a "super electoral year."

## A source that does not exist

The item cited a single article on the Belgian daily *Le Soir* as its basis. That link does not resolve: it returns a "page not found" error, and its address does not match the newspaper's actual article format, which embeds a numeric identifier. No verifiable reporting underpins the claim that Brussels parties are preparing for a 2026 vote.

## Why the distinction matters

Election timing is not a technicality. Candidate lists, campaign spending rules, coalition arithmetic and voter-registration deadlines all hang on the calendar. Publishing a phantom election date risks confusing voters and distorting coverage of genuine local politics.

Brussels is, in reality, in an active political period — but not because of an election. The region installed a new government in February 2026 under Minister-President Boris Dilliès following a record-length coalition formation, and it has since faced budget and policy tests. Those are the real stories worth following.

## What to watch

The next Belgian communal elections are due in **October 2030**. Until then, the meaningful Brussels political developments will unfold inside the region's governing coalition and its 19 communes — not on a 2026 campaign trail that does not exist.
