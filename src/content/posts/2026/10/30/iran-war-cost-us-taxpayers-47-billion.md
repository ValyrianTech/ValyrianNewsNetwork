# Iran War Cost to US Taxpayers Hits $47.1 Billion

The cost of the war with Iran to American taxpayers has climbed to an estimated $47.1 billion, according to a new Pentagon estimate provided to Congress and obtained by [The Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/iran-war-costs-trump-congress-81d781a54149709d1ca6754d2aab78bc). The figure, current as of October 16, represents a $3.5 billion increase since early September, when the tab stood at about $43.6 billion, and it is emerging as a potent political liability for President Donald Trump and Republicans just days before the November 3 midterm elections.

The estimate comes from U.S. Central Command, which oversees operations in the Middle East, and offers the most up-to-date accounting of the conflict since it was launched in late February by the United States and Israel.

## Where the Money Goes

According to the Centcom breakdown, the single largest expense is replacing expended munitions, at roughly $29 billion — nearly 60 percent of the total. The advanced weapons most in demand, and whose stockpiles have been most heavily drawn down, are defensive missile interceptors such as those launched by the Army's Patriot and THAAD systems, with a single Patriot interceptor costing millions of dollars.

An additional $11.9 billion is tied to operational costs, including support for service members, flight operations and cyber activities, while about $6 billion covers equipment losses. By service branch, the Air Force accounts for roughly $7.6 billion, the Army more than $2 billion, and the Navy about $1.45 billion — a notable drop from the $2.2 billion attributed to the Navy in early September. When asked to explain the decrease, both the Navy and Centcom referred questions to the Pentagon, which did not immediately respond, the AP reported.

[CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/iran-war-cost-centcom-47-billion/) independently obtained the same estimate, reporting that the figures were provided to lawmakers and that roughly $6.1 billion was earmarked for lost equipment.

## A Rising Bill, and a Transparency Question

The headline figure understates the war's true fiscal exposure. The estimate does not include damage to U.S. military bases across the Middle East, which Iran has struck repeatedly with ballistic missiles and drones. A Pentagon inspector general report released last month found that Iranian attacks have damaged and destroyed hundreds of buildings and other structures at U.S. bases in Kuwait, Bahrain, Qatar, the United Arab Emirates, Saudi Arabia, Iraq, Oman and Jordan.

The total is also well above earlier projections. Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth told lawmakers in July that the war had cost about $37.5 billion — meaning the latest estimate is more than 25 percent higher than the figure presented just three months ago. And the Congressional Budget Office has estimated the conflict could run $2 billion to $3 billion per month for as long as it continues, driven in part by the need to replenish interceptor missiles used to defend against Iranian attacks.

The Pentagon's official casualty tally lists 21 service members killed and 865 wounded, including troops who died in medical events and other situations not directly tied to combat. Of those killed, 18 died from Iranian strikes, combat aircraft crashes or the disposal of Iranian drones.

## The Political Cost

With the war entering its ninth month, the financial toll has become a central issue on Capitol Hill and on the campaign trail. Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer of New York laid the blame squarely on the president. "Trump and his ego led us into this war with no plan, no authority, and no exit strategy," Schumer said in a statement. "Senate Democrats will continue to do everything in our power to end this disaster of a war and stop the costs, chaos, and corruption seeping from the White House."

Democrats have repeatedly tried to end the conflict through war-powers legislation, but those efforts have failed in the Republican-controlled Congress. A recent AP-NORC poll found that most voters want lawmakers to prioritize ending the conflict, and its economic ripple effects on energy prices could hurt Republicans at the ballot box.

The expense is colliding with other major fiscal demands. The Trump administration is pushing for passage of a historic $1.5 trillion military budget proposal and is asking lawmakers to approve a separate $95 billion package that would help fund the Iran war and other priorities. Democrats, including Sens. Mark Kelly and Elissa Slotkin, have pressed Hegseth for a more detailed accounting of the war's costs.

## What to Watch

Trump has said new strikes on Iran remain an option after the midterms and is sending a third aircraft carrier and thousands of additional troops to the region — despite initially promising Americans a campaign that would last only a matter of weeks. "We may do another little Epic Fury, or they'll agree," he told supporters at a Florida rally last week.

That open-ended posture suggests the $47.1 billion is a floor, not a ceiling. Voters heading to the polls on November 3 will weigh a conflict that has already cost more than eight months and tens of billions of dollars — and whose final price remains, by the Pentagon's own admission, unknown.
