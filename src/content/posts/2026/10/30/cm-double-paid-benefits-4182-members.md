# CM Double-Paid Benefits to 4,182 Members by Mistake

Belgium's largest health insurance fund, CM, accidentally paid the sickness benefit of more than 4,100 members twice, the fund confirmed, and it is now reclaiming the overpaid money. Although the error originated entirely on the fund's side, the affected members are legally required to return the excess amount, [VRT NWS reported](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/10/30/meer-dan-1-400-cm-leden-kregen-uitkering-per-vergissing-dubbel/).

## What Happened

On 28 October, a technical fault in CM's software caused the benefit for members on work incapacity to be paid out twice in a single batch.

"On 28 October, 4,182 CM members who are unable to work received their benefit twice by mistake. That is approximately 2.5 percent of all benefits CM paid out that day," the CM Gezondheidsfonds said in a response to VRT NWS.

The fund said the cause was a technical error in its software, "which has since been resolved." Affected members were informed immediately and asked to transfer the overpaid amount back.

"The members concerned received a message asking them to transfer back the amount. That is legally mandatory," CM said. The fund has apologised for the inconvenience caused.

> Editor's note: Early versions of the story and its web address referenced "more than 1,400" members. CM's own confirmed figure, and the one used throughout this article, is 4,182.

## How the Story Surfaced

The incident came to light through a reader. A CM member contacted VRT NWS after noticing her benefit had appeared twice on her bank account. When she contacted the fund, she was told there had been a technical problem. The broadcaster's report was built on that tip.

## Why Recipients Must Repay

The obligation to repay does not depend on who was at fault. Under Belgian law, money paid by mistake without a legal basis is an "undue payment" (*onverschuldigde betaling*). The new Belgian Civil Code provides that anyone who receives an undue payment must return it, as [legal reference elfri.be](https://www.elfri.be/artikel/onverschuldigde-betaling) sets out, citing Articles 5.133 and 5.134 of the code.

In social security, overpaid benefits are additionally recoverable by law — which is why CM describes repayment as "legally mandatory." The fact that the error was the fund's does not transfer ownership of the overpaid money to the recipient.

## The System Behind the Payment

Sickness and incapacity benefits in Belgium are paid by the health insurance fund to which a person belongs, as the federal [RIZIV](https://www.riziv.fgov.be/nl/faq/burgers/arbeidsongeschiktheid-vaststelling-uitkering-toegelaten-activiteiten) explains. CM is the country's largest such fund, handling an enormous daily volume of payments. That 4,182 erroneous payments represented only about 2.5 percent of a single day's disbursements underscores just how many benefits the fund processes.

## Analysis: A Fairness Question

The case highlights an uncomfortable asymmetry in administrative law. A citizen who did nothing wrong can be legally compelled to return a windfall created entirely by a technical glitch. For beneficiaries living on tight budgets — some of whom may already have spent the money — that obligation can create real hardship.

Several questions remain unanswered. CM has not disclosed the total sum overpaid, nor whether members are being offered instalment plans or other flexibility. It is also unclear how quickly and proactively all members were notified: the story surfaced only because one member noticed the duplicate payment herself and raised it with the fund. And there has been no public indication of whether RIZIV or another supervisor will review the software failure or the safeguards meant to prevent a repeat.

## What's Next

The error itself has been fixed, but its aftermath is not. What to watch: whether CM offers repayment relief for members who cannot pay immediately, whether the supervisory authorities open an inquiry, and whether similar mass overpayment incidents have occurred at other Belgian health funds. For now, the affected members face a simple but unwelcome message: the money was never theirs, even though the mistake was not theirs either.
