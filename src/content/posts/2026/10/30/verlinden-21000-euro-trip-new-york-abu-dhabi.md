# Verlinden's €21,000 Trip Rerouted via New York to Abu Dhabi

Belgian Justice Minister Annelies Verlinden (CD&V) spent about €21,000 on business travel that took her from Brussels via New York to Abu Dhabi in late September, with the bill falling partly on her own department's budget after the Finance Inspectorate had issued a negative opinion, according to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/10/30/verlinden-dienstreis/). The revelation, first reported by Le Soir, lands as the minister is already under intense pressure over the failed i-Police police digitisation project.

At issue is not only the cost but the accounting route: a ministerial trip financed from the operational budget of the FOD/SPF Justice — the ministry she oversees — at a time when the federal government has ordered savings on exactly this kind of spending.

## What the trip involved

According to VRT NWS, the minister travelled to Abu Dhabi for the United Nations Congress on Crime Prevention and Criminal Justice, held from 26 September to 1 October. She did not fly from Brussels. She was in New York for the high-level week of the UN General Assembly on 23 and 24 September, then flew directly from New York to the Emirates on the afternoon of 24 September.

The figure of €21,000 covers all travel and hotel costs for both travellers — the minister and one cabinet staffer — across New York and Abu Dhabi. Verlinden flew business class; her staffer flew economy. French-language outlets frame the costs differently: [RTL Info](https://www.rtl.be/actu/belgique/justice/un-voyage-25000-euros-alors-que-le-budget-lui-avait-dabord-ete-refuse-annelies/2026-10-30/article/805011) reports that the New York–Abu Dhabi business-class leg alone cost €11,500 and puts the total stay at close to €25,000, while [Le Soir](https://www.lesoir.be/779878/article/2026-10-30/le-voyage-en-business-hors-budget-dannelies-verlinden) similarly cites €11,500 for that single flight.

## The budget problem

Verlinden's cabinet did not have the money. As part of the December 2025 savings agreed by the federal 'Arizona' coalition, spending on cabinet foreign travel was cut. For Verlinden, that meant a reduction of €49,000, leaving her cabinet with an annual travel budget of €17,000.

The cabinet was short roughly €15,000 to cover the trips, according to VRT NWS, and asked the FOD Justice to take the amount on. To do so, the ministry sought to raise its 'foreign missions' budget — by €12,000, per [La Libre/Belga](https://www.lalibre.be/belgique/politique-belge/2026/10/30/annelies-verlinden-vole-en-classe-business-a-11500-euros-entre-new-york-et-abu-dhabi-linspection-des-finances-avait-emis-un-avis-negatif-G3RBEE6RJ5ANPGNZGISJZOZ7MU/).

The Finance Inspectorate issued a negative opinion the next day, arguing that the overspend ran contrary to the December 2025 government decision to cut foreign travel spending. Verlinden appealed to Budget Minister Vincent Van Peteghem, a fellow CD&V member, who approved the move — on condition that the extra cost be "fully compensated" from within the FOD Justice's own budget.

Van Peteghem was careful to note that he was not ruling on "the usefulness, added value or cost of this business trip itself." The FOD Justice says it met the requirement. "The FOD Justice has realised the imposed saving of €49,000 on foreign business trips for 2026," spokesperson Sharon Beavis told VRT NWS, adding that the effort was spread across departments based on their past underspending.

According to VRT NWS, it is "very exceptional" for the Budget Minister to have to rule on a foreign-travel budget overspend — no other minister is said to have made such a request this legislature.

## Why the minister says it was necessary

The cabinet mounted a detailed defence, framing the trip as a core part of Belgium's fight against organised crime and stressing the value of cooperation with the UAE. "The fight against international organised crime is not waged only from Brussels," the cabinet said in a communiqué. It also argued that combining the New York and Abu Dhabi missions avoided additional intercontinental flights and extra nights, and that business class was justified by a tight schedule and a roughly twelve-hour overnight flight.

On the hotels, the cabinet said the first two nights in Abu Dhabi were paid by the conference organiser, as for other delegations, while a third night in Dubai followed an embassy recommendation for planned consultations with Dubai police. It pointed to a concrete return: of 45 major criminals sought internationally, 26 have been extradited to Belgium by the UAE, 22 of them since the current legislature began.

## The political backdrop

The travel story arrives with Verlinden already on the defensive. On 27 October it emerged that her Dutch-language spokesperson and a spokesperson for the Brussels prosecutor had been placed under investigation for breach of professional secrecy, in a case tied to the i-Police affair, [VRT NWS reported](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/10/27/woordvoerder-verlinden-in-verdenking-gesteld-na-lekken-in-zaak-r/). Verlinden, who signed the €299 million i-Police contract as interior minister in 2021, has denied any wrongdoing and says she has "nothing to hide."

Opposition MPs seized on the travel financing. "It is really unusual for a minister's business trip to be financed with the administration's funds," said Arthur Orlians of Anders. "The Finance Inspectorate rightly gave a negative advice, but Budget Minister Van Peteghem overruled it." PVDA's Sofie Merckx used the moment to renew calls for a public register of ministerial travel expenses, arguing that MPs should not have to extract answers through written questions.

## What to watch

The Chamber of Representatives is expected to hear Verlinden on i-Police at the earliest in the week beginning 2 November, and the travel question is likely to surface there. Several open points remain: how and from which budget line the €12,000–15,000 compensation was actually booked; why VRT's €21,000 total differs from the roughly €25,000 reported in the French-language press; and whether any other minister has used the same budget route this legislature. For a minister already fighting to defend a €140 million digital failure, questions about how she books a plane ticket carry outsized political weight.
