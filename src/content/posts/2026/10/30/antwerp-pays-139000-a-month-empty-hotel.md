# Antwerp Pays €139,000 a Month for an Empty Hotel

The city of Antwerp will continue paying roughly €139,000 a month — €1,666,818 a year — for a former hotel in Borgerhout that has stood empty since the last Ukrainian refugees left at the end of April 2026. The lease now runs until 2 May 2027, and opposition party Groen says the city is squandering public money while thousands of residents face housing insecurity. The [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/10/30/stad-antwerpen-betaalt-nog-half-jaar-139-000-euro-per-maand-voor/) first reported the renewed scrutiny on 30 October.

The building is the former City Inn Hotel on Kalverveld, near the Borgerhout motorway exit, with 90 studios and 180 beds. Antwerp rented it from 3 May 2023 for three years to shelter people fleeing Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine.

## A missed deadline — or a deliberate choice

The lease was due to end in May 2026, but it had to be cancelled at least three months in advance — by early February 2026. The city did not cancel in time, so the agreement was [silently renewed for a year](https://www.groenantwerpen.be/stad_betaalt_1_67_miljoen_euro_huur_voor_leegstaand_hotel_kalverveld) to 2 May 2027, according to the written reply of alderman Koen Kennis (N-VA) to a Groen question.

"The city is throwing money out the window renting an empty building. This is a huge waste of taxpayers' money and opportunities," said Groen city council member Joke Laukens.

She pointed to the city's acute housing needs: young people searching for a place to live, families on social housing waiting lists, migrant workers in unlicensed housing, and more than 3,400 people who are homeless or roofless. "Yet ninety studios, for which the city pays no less than 1.67 million euros a year, remain unused."

## Kennis defends the decision

Alderman Kennis concedes the facts but rejects the framing of negligence. He says that when the cancellation deadline arrived in early February 2026, dozens of Ukrainian displaced people — including families — were still living in the building, with no guarantee they would all find alternative housing by May.

"Cancelling meant that we might have had to put those people on the street from one day to the next in May, possibly via a legal procedure. We did not want that," he said. "Silently renewing the contract was therefore not an oversight, but a deliberate choice not to abandon people to their fate."

The last resident left at the end of April 2026. Since then, the city's collective shelter team has been preparing the building, together with the owner, to be returned: broken or missing furniture is being replaced and defects repaired. Kennis says the contract has now been cancelled and will not run beyond 2 May 2027.

## The dispute over alternative use

Groen has called on the city government to put the former hotel to use for people living in precarious conditions during the cold winter months. "There are enough social partners in our city to quickly give this a temporary purpose. Shelter for six months during the winter could make a world of difference for many," Laukens said.

Kennis counters that simply opening the building to other groups is not legally straightforward. "The suggestion that the city could simply open the building to other target groups is incorrect. The contract only allows that with the prior consent of the landlord," he said.

The college of aldermen itself indicated in January 2026 that space might become available for other target groups, such as foreign labourers, but no concrete plan followed. In a committee meeting on 24 September, alderman Van Baren reportedly said it was "not opportune" to house people there now, because the contract will be cancelled next year.

## Background and wider context

Kalverveld and an emergency village (nooddorp) on Linkeroever formed Antwerp's collective shelter infrastructure for Ukrainians with temporary protection. The [Linkeroever site, which housed over 700 people, was already empty by mid-2025](https://www.gva.be/regio/antwerpen/regio-antwerpen/antwerpen/antwerpen-stopt-begin-2026-met-laatste-opvanginitiatief-voor-oekraense-vluchtelingen/100391814.html) and was being dismantled by year-end. As of late October 2025, [Antwerp counted more than 10,000 Ukrainian refugees with temporary protection](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2025/10/27/antwerpen-opvang-oekraiense-vluchtelingen/) on its territory, the vast majority living on the private housing market.

## What's next

The story lands amid broader budget tensions in Antwerp. Local reporting has noted that the college has been vague about a budget agreement, with clarity on the impact of savings only expected at the end of December — sharpening the political sensitivity of spending roughly €1.67 million on an empty building.

For now, the Kalverveld hotel will remain vacant for the winter, and the lease will expire on 2 May 2027. The political argument, however, is likely to outlast the contract: N-VA will defend the renewal as a humane choice not to evict vulnerable families, while Groen will use it as a symbol of missed opportunities in a city under housing pressure. Whether the contract really prevented any interim use — and why no transition plan was prepared in time — remain open questions the two sides answer very differently.
