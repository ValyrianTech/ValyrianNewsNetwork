# OCMW's Push Back on Van Bossuyt Welfare Circular

Flemish public social welfare centres (OCMW's) have reacted with astonishment to a new circular from Federal Minister of Social Integration Anneleen Van Bossuyt (N-VA) urging them to take a tougher line on living-wage (leefloon) recipients who are unwilling to work. The umbrella body representing Flemish cities and municipalities, the VVSG, called the guidance "a bridge too far", according to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/10/30/ocmw-s-verbaasd-over-omzendbrief-van-bossuyt-over-leefloners-i/).

At the heart of the dispute is the minister's claim that welfare centres have been too lenient. "We have been too lax for too long," Van Bossuyt said, arguing that anyone receiving a leefloon must be willing to work. The circular instructs OCMW's to be stricter toward recipients who are not being activated and to weigh suspensions in other systems, such as the health insurance scheme, when assessing a client's situation.

## A Sector That Says It Knows What It Is Doing

The VVSG's Nathalie Debast rejected the premise outright. "OCMW's have long been activating people and know very well what they are doing," she said. "That the OCMW's would act too laxly is a bridge too far."

Debast stressed that the people in question are not a group that can be moved into work overnight. "This is a clientele you cannot activate from one day to the next. Often these are people who are, for example, poorly housed or have health problems," she said. She framed the centres not as a passive safety net but as a stepping stone: "OCMW's are not a hammock but a springboard. Give them more people and resources to activate more people."

The VVSG also singled out one passage as "dangerous": the instruction to account for suspensions in other systems, such as health insurance, when assessing a recipient. Debast argued this conflicts with the OCMW's core purpose. "OCMW's exist precisely to give people a new chance and therefore assess the current situation of a client," she said.

## A Surge of New Claimants

The circular lands at a difficult moment for local welfare services. As part of a federal reform that limits unemployment benefits in time, roughly 100,000 people lost their benefit this year, and 53 percent of them applied for a leefloon at their local OCMW, [VRT NWS reported](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/08/06/ruim-helft-van-werklozen-die-uitkering-verloor-vroeg-leefloon-a/) in August. Earlier waves produced similar pressures: after benefits were stopped for the third group of unemployed people at the end of March, about four in ten had applied for a leefloon by late May, rising to 44 percent in more urbanised municipalities, according to the [VVSG](https://www.vvsg.be/nieuwsoverzicht/4-op-10-werklozen-uit-derde-golf-vragen-leefloon-aan).

The strain is compounded by decisions at the Flemish level. Work Minister Zuhal Demir (N-VA) scrapped the Flemish employment premiums, which Debast said "cuts deep" because they were designed precisely to activate people. Separately, the Flemish government ended funding for guidance within temporary work experience (article 60 §7), a move the [VVSG warned](https://www.vvsg.be/nieuwsoverzicht/vlaamse-regering-schrapt-financiering-begeleiding-tijdelijke-werkervaring) would remove the compensation that lets welfare centres guide people into jobs. Those cuts sit within a broader Flemish budget agreement that maps out roughly 2 billion euros in savings for 2027, [according to VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/09/29/wat-staat-in-begrotingsakkoord-vlaamse-regering-inhoud-maatregelen/).

## Analysis: A Governance Mismatch

The confrontation exposes a mismatch between two levels of government. The call for stricter activation comes from a federal minister, while the funding that makes activation possible is being trimmed by the Flemish government. Both Van Bossuyt and Demir belong to N-VA, whose welfare-to-work agenda is a hallmark of the federal "Arizona" coalition, yet the timing — amid a surge of new claimants and simultaneous budget cuts — has sharpened the conflict with local authorities.

There is also a legal question mark. The instruction to factor in cross-system suspensions is contested by the sector, echoing earlier scrutiny of a December 2025 circular that allowed leefloon to be withdrawn from recipients who are aggressive toward OCMW staff. How strictly OCMW's apply the new guidance will depend heavily on their discretion and their already-stretched capacity.

The outcome may matter less in the statute books than on the ground, where welfare centres must process a growing caseload within tight deadlines. "OCMW's need a motivating minister," Debast said — a line that captures the sector's frustration as much as its demands.

## What to Watch

Several questions remain open. Will OCMW's apply the circular fully, or selectively, given capacity and legal concerns? Will the cross-system passage survive legal challenge? And how will the next wave of benefit terminations affect services that already report struggling to keep pace? For now, the standoff between federal tough-love guidance and Flemish funding cuts looks set to escalate politically.
