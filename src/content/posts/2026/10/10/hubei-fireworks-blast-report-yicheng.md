# Hubei Fireworks Blast Report Faults Customer, Lax Oversight

Chinese authorities have publicly released an investigation report into a fireworks explosion in Yicheng, Hubei Province, that killed 12 people — seven adults and five minors — concluding that an illegal customer test-firing triggered the disaster but that systemic failures in safety regulation and official oversight were equally to blame.

The report into the "2·18" accident at the Qianhe Department Store was published on October 9 by the Hubei Provincial People's Government's accident investigation team, according to [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/local/20261009/ce35cb55e2354fb19db14c88c61ddef3/c.html). The blast, which also caused direct economic losses of 12.79 million yuan (about US$1.8 million), occurred on February 18, 2026, at a general merchandise store licensed to retail fireworks in Jinpu Village, Zhengji Town.

## What Happened

According to the investigation, the immediate cause was a customer illegally test-igniting an oversized "Zhentianlei" double-banger firecracker in front of the store. The second report of the device ignited large quantities of fireworks displayed outside the shop, setting off a chain of explosions that spread to fireworks inside the premises.

[China News Service](https://www.chinanews.com/sh/2026/10-09/10709692.shtml), citing the report, confirmed that people who had been outside fled into the store to escape the flames. The front entrance was then blocked by the burning fireworks, while the rear roller shutter door was closed, leaving them trapped. The resulting death toll of 12, which included five minors, made the accident one of the deadliest fireworks incidents in China in recent years.

## The People Held Responsible

The report names three individuals as directly responsible, [Lianhe Zaobao](https://www.zaobao.com.sg/news/china/story20261010-9813861) reported.

Customer Xie Weilong, 26, who illegally test-fired the firecracker, has been arrested on suspicion of negligently endangering public safety by dangerous means. Lin Chenfei, 26, the store's actual operator, was also held directly responsible, but because he died in the accident, the report recommends exempting him from criminal liability. Yang Bo, 48, a co-operator of the store, was found to have engaged long-term in illegal outdoor displays, over-quota storage of fireworks, and the sale of over-specification products; the report recommends administrative penalties and the referral of suspected criminal conduct to public security organs.

## Systemic Failures

The investigation identified a chain of regulatory breakdowns that allowed the tragedy to happen. According to Xinhua, the store operated illegally with no functioning safety responsibility, administrative licensing approvals were granted without proper vetting, and over-specification, over-powder products flowed onto the market because of quality oversight gaps.

The report also found that local Party committees, governments, and relevant departments failed to perform their duties. A dedicated accountability review team was established by the Hubei Provincial Commission for Discipline Inspection and Supervisory Commission to hold public officials in Xiangyang City, Yicheng City, and Zhengji Town to account, covering departments of administrative approval, emergency management, market supervision, public security, and firefighting.

Written self-criticisms were ordered at three levels of government: the Xiangyang Municipal Party Committee and Government to the provincial authorities, the Yicheng Municipal Party Committee and Government to Xiangyang, and the Zhengji Town Party Committee and Government to Yicheng.

## A National Crackdown

The Yicheng accident unfolded against the backdrop of an intensified national safety campaign. On February 19, 2026, the State Council Work Safety Committee listed the accident for supervised handling and formed an on-site supervision team with the Ministry of Emergency Management, the Ministry of Public Security, and the National Fire and Rescue Administration, [21jingji](https://www.21jingji.com/article/20260219/herald/5ae3109ad8f04393c40f20b490decbb6.html) reported.

That same day, the committee office and the Ministry of Emergency Management convened an emergency video meeting that issued eight hard measures — known as the "five prohibitions, three musts" — to tighten fireworks safety nationwide, [Guangming Daily](https://m.gmw.cn/2026-02/19/content_1304348793.htm) reported. The prohibitions ban operating fireworks stores beneath residences, over-quota storage, placing products outside the store, igniting or testing fireworks within 100 meters of a retail outlet, and fireworks use in prohibited zones or crowded places. The measures require dedicated on-site supervision, strict control of the number of people entering a store, and the posting of warning signage.

Citing both the Yicheng accident and a separate incident in Donghai, Jiangsu Province, the meeting ordered "one pole to the bottom" unannounced inspections and said compliance would be included in the annual central work-safety assessment.

## Analysis and Implications

The Yicheng case illustrates a recurring pattern in Chinese work-safety disasters: a single reckless act — in this case, a customer's illegal test-firing — combines with deeper institutional weaknesses to produce catastrophic results. The investigation's focus on licensing, product quality, and official duty reflects Beijing's longstanding effort to treat major accidents not merely as individual negligence but as failures of a regulatory system.

The accident also highlights the particular dangers of the Lunar New Year period, when demand for fireworks peaks. It occurred on the second day of the holiday, a time when retail outlets are often crowded and enforcement is stretched.

## What's Next

The disclosure of the report clears the way for the recommended administrative and criminal proceedings against those involved. The referral of official-duty problems to the provincial discipline and supervisory commission suggests further disciplinary actions against public officials may follow.

For residents of Yicheng and the families of the 12 victims, the report offers a measure of official accountability. Whether the national crackdown it has prompted will prevent a similar tragedy — particularly as demand for fireworks rises again in the next festival season — remains the open question.
