# Hong Kong Passes First Five-Year Plan Motion

Hong Kong's Legislative Council on October 9 passed two motions supporting the city's first-ever five-year development plan and the Chief Executive's 2026 Policy Address, concluding a three-day debate that lawmakers and officials framed as a milestone in the special administrative region's integration with national strategy.

According to [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/gangao/20261009/73fafb405cc949bdbd5e5ef71567b1a0/c.html), the debate spanned nearly 27 hours over three consecutive days, with 88 lawmakers offering views on the blueprint. Chief Executive John Lee Ka-chiu thanked the body and pledged continued cooperation with the legislature.

## An Unprecedented Planning Document

The vote marks the first time since Hong Kong's 1997 return to China that the city has adopted a medium- to long-term strategic development plan. The document, formally titled "The First Five-Year Plan for Economic and Social Development of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region (2026-2030)," was released on September 16, 2026, jointly with the 2026 Policy Address.

According to the [official plan portal](https://www.hk5yplan.gov.hk/2026-2030/en/index.html), the plan is a "forward-looking, strategic and directional guiding document" designed to "proactively align with national development strategies, and better integrate into and serve the overall national development." Its stated objectives include achieving new breakthroughs in economic development, expanding global competitiveness, accelerating Northern Metropolis development, enhancing social well-being, and deepening integration with the national agenda.

Lee described the two documents as "of the same lineage," characterizing the five-year plan as the guiding framework and the Policy Address as the annual action plan for implementing it, according to [news.gov.hk](https://www.news.gov.hk/eng/2026/10/20261009/20261009_193419_428.html).

## What the Motions Cover

The government motion on the Five-Year Plan was moved by Chief Secretary Eric Chan Kwok-ki. It commits the administration to several priorities: consolidating Hong Kong's status as an international financial, shipping, and trade center and aviation hub; building an international innovation and technology center and high-end talent hub; advancing the "four centers, one hub" construction; accelerating Northern Metropolis development with a "University Town" as a core engine; and deepening Greater Bay Area cooperation and Belt & Road participation.

A second, separate motion of thanks endorsed the 2026 Policy Address, themed "A Strategic Vision for a Bright New Era, Driving Reform and Boosting Development, Unleashing Opportunities and Enhancing Livelihood." Both are non-binding motions, as [RTHK](https://news.rthk.hk/rthk/en/component/k2/1873348-20261009.htm) reported.

LegCo House Committee chairman Ronick Chan Chun-ying noted the scale of the debate, telling the chamber that 88 lawmakers had contributed views across nearly 27 hours. In his closing remarks, Chief Secretary Chan praised what he described as constructive executive-legislative interaction.

"The process of formulating Hong Kong's five-year plan, as well as this in-depth debate in the Legislative Council, precisely demonstrates the positive executive-legislative interaction, working together for Hong Kong's development," Chan said. "You are our solid partners."

## From Short-Term Governance to Long-Term Strategy

The plan reflects a deliberate shift in Hong Kong's governance model. In a March 2026 interview with China News Service, Lee explained that all 15 policy bureaux established preparatory groups to draft the plan based on the national 14th Five-Year Plan, positioning the city to respond quickly to the 15th Five-Year Plan's opening year in 2026.

"There are three points I think are very important," Lee said in that [interview](https://news.jschina.com.cn/gn/202603/t20260306_s69aaadabe4b02ad3932e27a1.shtml). "First, develop the economy. Second, improve people's livelihood. Third, consolidate and enhance competitiveness."

The city has also established a governance framework that officials describe as "five-year plan leadership, annual policy implementation, budget support, and annual reporting," tying planning cycles to fiscal and accountability mechanisms.

Several of the plan's targets are concrete. According to Sing Tao Daily, as cited in compiled research, the plan aims to raise Northern Metropolis "spade-ready land" output from 120 hectares during 2021/22-2025/26 to 900 hectares across 2026/27-2030/31. It also targets gross domestic expenditure on research and development reaching 3% of GDP after 2030.

## Analysis: A Test of Executive-Legislative Mechanics

The debate's significance extends beyond its procedural outcome. Since the overhaul of Hong Kong's electoral system under the "patriots administering Hong Kong" principle, the government has emphasized what it calls "positive interaction" between the executive and the legislature — a model in which the LegCo is expected to offer constructive feedback rather than serve as an opposition check.

The five-year plan gives that model a concrete test. Unlike annual Policy Addresses, a five-year blueprint creates measurable benchmarks against which the administration can be assessed. The government's decision to route all 22 member questions to written replies suggests some limits on the depth of scrutiny during this debate.

At the same time, the plan embeds Hong Kong firmly within national planning cycles. By aligning the city's strategy with the national 15th Five-Year Plan in its opening year, the administration is formalizing a relationship in which Hong Kong's development priorities are set in dialogue with Beijing's broader economic and technological goals.

This carries both opportunity and risk. Alignment could accelerate investment in innovation, talent attraction, and infrastructure such as the Northern Metropolis — areas where Hong Kong has long identified capacity gaps. But it also means the city's economic direction is increasingly shaped by national priorities, leaving less room for independent deviation should local conditions diverge from the broader plan.

## What to Watch

The near-term focus will be implementation. The government has said it will "carefully analyse and study" lawmakers' suggestions to ensure policy outcomes are delivered. Northern Metropolis land output, R&D spending relative to GDP, and progress on talent and innovation hubs will be the clearest early indicators of whether the plan translates into measurable change.

Lee's term is also approaching its end, with this reportedly his last Policy Address of the current term — raising questions about continuity of the plan across leadership transitions. And with national security remaining an explicit pillar, the administration has signaled it will continue expanding related educational and institutional efforts alongside the development agenda.

For now, the passage of the motions confirms that Hong Kong's governance has entered a planning-led phase — one that will be judged not on the ambition of its blueprint, but on the delivery of the targets embedded within it.
