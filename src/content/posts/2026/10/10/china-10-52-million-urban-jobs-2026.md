# China Adds 10.52M Urban Jobs, Hitting 87.7% of 2026 Goal

China created 10.52 million new urban jobs in the first three quarters of 2026, completing 87.7% of its full-year target, the State Council Information Office announced on October 10. The average urban surveyed unemployment rate for the first eight months of the year stood at 5.2%, which officials described as evidence of a "generally stable" employment situation, according to [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/20261010/9904bb66dd234a5487c6f6e1fab74642/c.html).

## Context

The figures were released at a press conference in the "Starting the 15th Five-Year Plan" themed series, marking the launch of China's 2026–2030 development period. The annual target of 12 million new urban jobs was set in the Government Work Report delivered in March 2026, which also projected GDP growth of 4.5%–5% and an urban surveyed unemployment rate of around 5.5%, as [another financial outlet reported](https://news.10jqka.com.cn/20260305/c675089330.shtml).

At 87.7% of the target with a full quarter remaining, the government appears on track to meet its goal, though the pace is marginally softer than a year ago. China created 10.57 million new urban jobs in the first three quarters of 2025 and 12.67 million for the full year, according to earlier official releases.

## Key Developments

The employment data lands squarely in the middle of the 15th Five-Year Plan's opening year, a period in which Beijing has signaled that "high-quality and full employment" will be a central objective. The plan's outline is the first to dedicate a dedicated employment section, and the State Council recently issued an "Employment Priority Strategy" plan for the 2026–2030 period.

Minister of Human Resources and Social Security Wang Xiaoping has framed the challenge explicitly as a matter of matching supply to demand. Writing in June, she argued that China must "improve the human resource supply-demand matching mechanism" to build an "employment-friendly development model," noting that population changes and new technologies such as artificial intelligence are making mismatches between job seekers' skills and available positions more pronounced, as [21st Century Business Herald reported](https://www.21jingji.com/article/20260615/herald/888fe75932a56b5a4cec5208f329a107.html). She emphasized that AI both creates and disrupts jobs, and that technical training must keep pace so workers can adapt to "job adjustment and career transition."

That theme has become a recurring policy focus. In January 2026, the Ministry of Human Resources and Social Security said it would issue a document specifically addressing AI's impact on employment, part of a broader "stabilize jobs, expand capacity, improve quality" campaign, according to [Southern Metropolis Daily](https://m.mp.oeeee.com/a/BAAFRD0000202601271515388.html). The ministry has also released 93 new occupations across six batches, concentrated in AI, electronic information, and new services.

Vice Minister Yu Jiadong struck a confident tone on the outlook, telling state media that authorities had "made comprehensive analyses and sufficient forecasts of this year's employment situation" and prepared for uncertainty. "Our employment policy toolbox is ample," he said, expressing "confidence and conviction" that overall employment stability can be maintained, [People's Daily reported](https://www.peopleapp.com/column/30048983302-500006241910).

## Analysis

The headline number masks a more complicated picture. Officials consistently acknowledge both "total volume pressure" and "structural contradictions" — a formulation that captures the paradox of firms struggling to recruit skilled workers while many job seekers struggle to find suitable work. Youth and migrant-worker employment remain priority concerns, and the unemployment rate has hovered near the government's comfort ceiling of 5.5% for much of the year.

The year-on-year comparison offers a subtle signal: at 10.52 million, the 2026 first-three-quarters figure trails 2025's 10.57 million, even as policy support has intensified. The difference is small, but it suggests that stabilizing employment at scale is an ongoing effort rather than a settled achievement.

AI's role cuts both ways. A World Economic Forum forecast cited in Chinese reporting projects roughly 92 million jobs displaced globally between 2025 and 2030 alongside 170 million new roles — a net gain of about 78 million, but one requiring significant retraining. Beijing's response has been to pair job-creation measures with an emphasis on "investment in people," skills training initiatives such as "Skills Illuminate the Future," and financial support for enterprises that maintain headcount.

## What's Next

The immediate question is whether the remaining quarter delivers enough new jobs to clear the 12 million target. Meeting it would require roughly 1.5 million jobs per month — a pace consistent with recent performance.

Longer term, the 15th Five-Year Plan sets more ambitious markers: 25 million re-employed urban unemployed persons and 6.5 million jobs for workers from difficult-to-employ groups, with the unemployment rate kept below 5.5%. Whether those structural targets can be met will depend less on headline job counts than on resolving the skills mismatches that officials themselves now describe as the defining challenge of China's labor market.
