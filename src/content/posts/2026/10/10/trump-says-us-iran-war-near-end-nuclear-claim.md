# Trump Says US-Iran War Near End After Nuclear Victory Claim

President Donald Trump said Saturday that the end of the US-Iran war is near, declaring he had stopped Tehran from obtaining a nuclear weapon as the conflict appeared to enter a winding-down phase. The remarks, delivered through a Truth Social post and White House comments, came amid a broader day of geopolitical developments connecting the war to global energy markets, including a US-Russia diesel arrangement and renewed Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia.

Trump said he was "about to settle, or win" the war, according to [Fox News](https://www.foxnews.com/live-news/us-iran-war-saudi-arabia-trump-putin-oil-energy-prices-10-10-26). In the same post, he claimed, "I stopped Iran from having a Nuclear Weapon, and blowing up the entire Middle East and, ultimately, far beyond!"

The declaration marks the sharpest public signal yet that Washington believes the nearly eight-month conflict may be concluding, though the specific nature of the claimed nuclear achievement remained undefined and could not be independently verified by international monitors.

## A Victory Claim Without Verification

Trump's assertion that he "stopped" Iran from acquiring a nuclear weapon has not been confirmed by the International Atomic Energy Agency or other independent bodies. It remains unclear whether the claim refers to a halted weaponization program, the destruction of specific facilities, or a broader strategic outcome.

Speaking at the White House on Friday during a Columbus Day celebration, Trump offered a vivid description of the stakes as he framed them. "If they had a nuclear weapon, they would have used it and they would have used it fast," he said. "They would have totally blown up Israel, number one, but they would have blown up the Middle East, and then they would have headed for our country."

Yet Western intelligence assessments complicate that narrative. Bloomberg reported that Iran retained ballistic missile and drone production capabilities despite eight months of US-Israeli strikes, suggesting core military capacity remains intact. Iranian leaders have projected defiance rather than defeat. President Masoud Pezeshkian said, "Our enemies attacked us, but we stood firm. They blocked our way, but we found a way through."

The contrast underscores a central uncertainty: Trump's framing of the war as won is contested by the very intelligence and rhetoric emerging from the region.

## A War on Two Political Clocks

The timing of Trump's remarks is inseparable from two upcoming elections. The US midterms on November 3 and Israel's Knesset elections on October 27 have shaped military decision-making for weeks.

The US president has pledged not to launch new strikes on Iran before the midterms. As [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/oct/08/trump-iran-pause-attack-midterms) reported, Trump wrote that Washington was "having productive discussions with the Islamic Republic of Iran" and would not attack "at any time prior to the Midterm Elections." Public opinion appears to be a driver: a Reuters/Ipsos survey cited by the Guardian found only about a third of Americans approved of the strikes on Iran, while more than four in five expected the conflict to last for an extended period.

According to a Maariv report republished by [The Jerusalem Post](https://www.jpost.com/middle-east/iran-news/article-911224), the United States pressed Israel to carry out a solo strike operation against Iran during discussions over the past two weeks, seeking to shift the political risks of a military operation away from Washington before the midterms. The report said the US has since backed down from that demand, at least for now, leaving Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's position unclear.

[Al-Monitor](https://www.al-monitor.com/originals/2026/10/trump-rules-out-new-iran-attack-us-midterm-elections) noted that Trump's pause followed a surge in oil prices driven by media reports of potential new US strikes before the elections, underscoring how tightly the war, energy costs, and electoral politics have become entwined.

## The Diesel Deal and a Widening Paradox

On October 9, Trump and Russian President Vladimir Putin spoke by phone, after which Trump announced that Russia had agreed to supply diesel fuel to American and global markets. Putin agreed to "immediately supply over 300,000 Tons of Diesel Fuel," with another 500,000 tons in November and 1,000,000 tons thereafter, according to [Fox Business](https://www.foxbusiness.com/politics/trump-says-russia-agreed-deliver-millions-diesel-fuel-highly-successful-discussion).

The Kremlin framed the call more broadly. Spokesman Dmitry Peskov said that "in coordination with the president of Iran, Putin conveyed to Trump the Iranian side's view on a possible resolution of the conflict." White House press secretary Katie Zacharia defended the arrangement as consumer-focused, saying Trump "is putting American interests first" and that "we will have cheaper gas, cheaper groceries with this diesel deal."

The deal drew immediate condemnation from Ukraine. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said Russia "expressed its thanks for the lifting of sanctions on diesel exports by dropping five glide bombs on residential buildings in Zaporizhzhia," killing twelve people including three children. "Every additional billion dollars they can earn from exports will be spent on nothing but sourcing and importing critical components and equipment to manufacture weapons," he warned.

The apparent contradiction is notable: the US had recently signed the Lindsey O. Graham Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act of 2026, yet is now easing energy-market pressure by accepting Russian fuel — a trade-off that prioritizes domestic fuel prices over sanctions cohesion.

## Escalation on Other Fronts

Even as Washington signals a wind-down with Iran, violence has intensified elsewhere. Iran-backed Houthis attacked King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh earlier in the week, killing three Saudi citizens including a pilot. A fresh explosion struck the airport on Saturday, prompting its closure, the cancellation of Kuwait Airways flights, and warnings from Germany and Spain to their citizens, as reported by [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/liveblog/2026/10/10/iran-war-live-kremlin-says-trump-welcomed-russia-effort-in-iran-deal).

In the Strait of Hormuz — a chokepoint through which roughly a fifth of the world's oil and liquefied natural gas once passed — eleven ships were struck in October, and vessel traffic has fallen far below pre-conflict levels. The US military said it had redirected 133 commercial vessels to enforce its blockade of Iranian ports under "Operation Epic Fury."

Baraa Shiban, a Yemen expert at the Royal United Services Institute, cautioned against reading the region as calming. "The fighting is spreading on multiple fronts, but it is a situation that will keep escalating," he told Al Jazeera. "There's no way this is kind of dying down. There is no mediation happening this time."

## What to Watch

Trump's declaration of near-victory rests on a nuclear claim that remains unverified, against a backdrop of continued Iranian capability and regional escalation. The diesel deal has eased fuel-price pressure but widened a rift with Ukraine and exposed the limits of the US sanctions posture toward Russia.

Three developments will test whether the war is truly ending: the outcome of Israel's October 27 elections and the US midterms on November 3, which could unlock or constrain further military action; whether the Russian diesel arrangement is actually implemented; and any independent IAEA reporting on the status of Iran's nuclear program. Until then, the gap between Trump's declared victory and conditions on the ground remains wide.
