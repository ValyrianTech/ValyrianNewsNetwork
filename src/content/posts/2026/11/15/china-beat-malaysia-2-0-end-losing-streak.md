# China Beat Malaysia 2-0 to End Three-Game Losing Streak

China's men's national football team defeated Malaysia 2-0 in an international friendly on 14 November, ending a three-game losing streak that had piled pressure on head coach Shao Jiayi ahead of a tougher test against Bahrain. First-half goals from Wang Yudong and Zhang Yuning inside the opening 20 minutes settled the match at the Zhaoqing New Area Sports Center in Guangdong, according to [The Paper](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_34480933).

## A Fast Start Breaks the Drought

The match was part of the "Zhaoqing International Football Invitational" and kicked off at 20:00 local time. China began with urgency: in the second minute, Zhang Yuning went down in the box and the referee awarded a penalty, only to overturn the decision after a VAR review found he had fouled first.

China did not have to wait long. In the 10th minute, Wang Shangyuan slipped a pass to Lin Liangming, whose cross from the right found Wang Yudong for a header that made it 1-0 — the team's first goal in four matches, ending a goal drought that domestic media put at 307 minutes. Nine minutes later, Wang Shangyuan launched a long ball over the Malaysian back line and Zhang Yuning ran in behind, chipping the advancing goalkeeper with the outside of his foot to double the lead.

The goals gave China a cushion that proved decisive, even as the match grew increasingly fractious. Malaysian challenges escalated through the first half, and in the 44th minute a confrontation broke out on the touchline after Chen Jun Ren kicked the ball at a grounded Yang Xi; players from both benches briefly clashed, and a yellow card was issued.

## A Nervy Second Half

China's command did not last. After the interval, Shao made sweeping changes, and the team's control slipped. Malaysia pressed higher and, in the 77th minute, appeared to score through Fergus Tierney after a Xu Bin back-pass error — only for the goal to be ruled out for offside following a VAR review.

Goalkeeper Li Hao, the 22-year-old who has emerged as first choice, preserved the clean sheet with a remarkable double save in the 86th minute. Malaysia's frustration spilled over in stoppage time when Tierney received a second yellow card after a tangle with Yang Xi and was sent off, leaving Malaysia with ten men.

[Sohu](https://www.sohu.com/a/1101620738_122387568) noted that despite the win, China was outplayed on several measures, holding only 43 percent possession and conceding five corners to one — a reminder that the scoreline flattered the hosts in the second half.

## A Win That Stops the Rot

The result mattered most for what it ended. China had entered the match after three friendly defeats in the previous international window — 0-3 to New Zealand, 0-5 to Palestine and 0-1 to Tajikistan — a run in which the team conceded nine goals without scoring. The losing slide had intensified scrutiny on Shao Jiayi, whose position dominated domestic discussion.

Zhang Yuning's goal was his 10th in his 50th cap, while Wang Yudong's opener ended a personal national-team goal drought stretching back to July 2025. The win also extended China's unbeaten run against Malaysia to 46 years, dating to a 1-3 defeat in Kuala Lumpur in March 1980.

The victory earned China 3.69 ranking points, lifting the team to 95th in the FIFA world ranking, past Bahrain and Guatemala. Malaysia entered the match ranked 133rd.

## Shao Jiayi Urges Caution

At the post-match press conference, Shao congratulated his players but was quick to temper expectations. "First I want to congratulate my players — in terms of both the process and the result, as coach I am very satisfied," he said, crediting them for summoning energy at the end of a demanding 30-round domestic league season. Weighing the result, however, he added that while "this victory is indeed very important for us," the team had "no grounds to be overly optimistic," as [Sohu](https://www.sohu.com/a/1101769092_122313676) reported.

He pointed to Malaysia's depleted squad as a caution. "You were missing nearly 20 players and still showed this kind of strength — that is Asian football at this stage," he said, warning that no opponent could be taken lightly. He was equally measured about Li Hao, saying the goalkeeper had earned his place through performance but remained "still very far from my requirements."

## What's Next

China faces a far sterner examination on 17 November, when it meets Bahrain in the same stadium, just three days after the Malaysia match. Bahrain, a compact and physically robust West Asian side, opened the invitational with a 3-1 win over Yemen and sat one place above China before the Malaysia result.

Shao acknowledged the quick turnaround would be difficult. "We must actively recover — it's very difficult, but as professional players they must get used to it, must adapt," he said, adding that further squad rotation is likely.

For now, the 2-0 result is best read as relief rather than revival. It ends the losing streak and restores some morale, but China's second-half retreat — and Shao's own caution — suggests the team's deeper problems in midfield control and defensive solidity remain unresolved. Whether the win becomes a turning point or a brief pause depends on what happens against Bahrain.
