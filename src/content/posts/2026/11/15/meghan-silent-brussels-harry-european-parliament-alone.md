# Meghan Silent in Brussels as Harry Addresses EU Alone

Meghan, Duchess of Sussex, withdrew from a planned joint address to the European Parliament in Brussels on Tuesday, leaving Prince Harry to speak alone as the couple's first joint working visit to Europe since their move back to England was overshadowed by the arrest of her father. According to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/10/harry-en-meghan-europees-parlement/), the duchess felt "not strong enough" to speak publicly following the arrest of Thomas Markle on suspicion of possessing images of child abuse.

## A Planned Joint Speech Becomes a Solo Address

The couple had been invited to Brussels by the European Parliament's Intergroup on Children's Rights. The original plan called for a 15-minute joint speech on the importance of digital safety for children—a cause the Sussexes have long championed, having previously worked with the United Nations and the World Health Organization on mental health. This time, the focus was on protecting young people from the dangers of the internet in an era of social media and artificial intelligence.

Meghan had been expected to introduce her husband. Instead, she pulled out at the last moment, days after her 82-year-old father was arrested.

## The Allegations Against Thomas Markle

Thomas Markle was arrested in Florida on 6 November and appeared in a California court the following day, arriving in a wheelchair. As [VRT NWS reported](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/07/thomas-markle-onder-elektronisch-toezicht/), a judge placed him under electronic monitoring at a Florida care home, restricted his travel to two districts, and ordered a psychiatric evaluation given his age and frail health. He can only be questioned once there is clarity on his mental state.

The material at the center of the case—dozens of slides, film negatives and books—was reportedly found in two Los Angeles storage units he had rented since 2008. Two photographs are said to show him with a nude minor, reportedly dating back 30 to 40 years. The FBI investigation is ongoing, and Philippine police reportedly found hundreds of images during a search of an apartment in Cebu. If convicted, Markle faces up to 20 years in prison.

Kash Patel, director of the FBI, said: "Mr. Markle would have targeted innocent children and tried to steal their innocence." Markle has told the Daily Mail that he is not a pedophile.

## A Family Fractured

Meghan definitively broke off contact with her father in 2025, though she had written him at least two letters that later leaked to the press. Her mother, Doria Ragland—married to Thomas Markle from 1979 to 1987—is reportedly cooperating with the FBI investigation. In 2019, Ragland reportedly alerted police by letter about her ex-husband's "disturbing" trips to Asia, where he visited young girls.

Meghan previously responded in writing, saying she hoped justice would be done and that "the protection of innocent children remains paramount," calling the alleged abuse "disgusting." In late October, she disclosed that she had deliberately kept her children away from her father since learning of the allegations in 2019.

## A Deliberate Silence

Meghan still accompanied Harry to Brussels but chose silence, avoiding a heavy press presence. Journalists were pre-briefed that the couple would not answer questions. Her silence is presumed to be intended to avoid jeopardizing the legal proceedings involving her father.

After his address, Harry opened an exhibition and visited an art installation commemorating children who died as a result of online bullying. Meghan accompanied him to both.

## What to Watch

Royalty expert Pascale Mertens told VRT NWS that "the timing is particularly bad" for the couple's carefully managed public return to Europe. The Brussels appearance underscores the difficulty of separating the Sussexes' advocacy work from a deepening family scandal that continues to unfold in US and Philippine investigations.

For now, the duchess has chosen to let her husband carry the public message, protecting both her family's legal position and her own composure as the case against her father moves forward.
