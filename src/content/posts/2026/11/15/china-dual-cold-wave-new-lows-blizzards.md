# Dual Cold Wave to Push China's Temperatures to New Lows

Two successive cold air masses will sweep across China from November 15 to 18, driving temperatures down 6-10C across most of central and eastern China, with localized drops exceeding 12C, before resetting this year's second-half lows, according to a [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/local/20261115/fc91794a06734ff5b54aa8765b76c764/c.html) republication of the China Meteorological Administration's forecast.

The chill will be paired with a large-scale rain and snow event: blizzard conditions are forecast in parts of northern China, while the south will see mostly rain. Authorities warn of impacts on travel and daily life.

## Two waves, two phases

The first cold surge, affecting November 15-16, is expected to focus on areas north of the Yangtze River. The second and stronger mass, arriving November 17-18, will spread across most of central and eastern China. The northern chill is expected to bottom out around November 17, with the south following on November 18.

After the cooling, temperatures will broadly refresh this year's second-half lows. The maximum-temperature 0C line will push south to northern North China, and the 10C line will reach the central Huang-Huai region. Among major cities, Hohhot's high will drop to 0C on November 17, the first time this half-year, while Nanjing and Shanghai will fall to 10C or below the same day.

Winds of force 4-6, with gusts of 7-8, will buffet most of central and eastern China, improving atmospheric diffusion conditions but adding to the biting feel of the cold.

## Snow in the north, rain in the south

The precipitation footprint mirrors the north-south split. Eastern Northwest China, central Inner Mongolia, and western and northern North China are forecast to see moderate-to-heavy snow or sleet, with localized blizzards. From Huang-Huai southward, light-to-moderate rain is expected, with heavy rain in parts of Jiangnan and South China.

The heaviest snowfall window falls on November 17-18, when eastern Jilin and eastern Heilongjiang may see heavy-to-blizzard snow, with localized severe blizzards. On November 15, heavy snow and localized blizzards are forecast for central-western Inner Mongolia and northwestern Hebei.

According to the forecast, the public should promptly check local weather warnings, take anti-slip measures when traveling, and guard against the adverse traffic effects of wet, icy roads.

## A record El Nino in the background

The timing of this cold wave is consistent with the seasonal outlook issued by the [Science and Technology Daily](https://www.stdaily.com/web/gdxw/2026-11/03/content_607491.html) at a CMA press conference on November 3. National Climate Center Deputy Director Gao Rong said a super El Nino event formed in September 2026 and by October had become the strongest on record, expected to peak in late autumn or early winter.

For China's 2026/2027 winter, the outlook calls for overall above-normal temperatures nationally, but with somewhat more frequent cold waves and large temperature swings, a "cold early winter, warm late winter" pattern with sharp swings in December and January. The risk of strong cooling and snow in the north, and periodic low-temperature rain, snow, and icing in the south, is described as prominent.

As the [Economic Daily](http://www.ce.cn/xwzx/gnsz/gdxw/202611/t20261103_3282222.shtml) reported, the CMA forecast four cold air processes for November, close to the historical average of 4.47, with a "strong early, weak late" intensity profile. That early-season strength is now playing out, following an earlier cold episode from November 5-9 that already pushed North China into winter and South China into autumn.

## What to watch

For a country as large and climatically varied as China, a dual cold wave of this scale tests infrastructure and daily routines alike. The CMA has advised that transport, energy, and power departments prepare contingency plans for low-temperature rain and snow, and that facility agriculture and livestock operations take early cold-weather and wind-resistance precautions.

The immediate focus is on the northern blizzard belt, where heavy snow and icy roads could disrupt travel through the November 17-18 peak. Beyond that, the broader question is how this sharp early-winter cold fits the El Nino-driven "cold early, warm late" pattern, and whether the swings forecast for December and January prove as pronounced as the models suggest.

For now, residents across the region are being urged to watch local alerts and prepare for a week that will feel markedly more like deep winter than mid-November.
