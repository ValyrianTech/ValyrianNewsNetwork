# Wari Railway Sets Coal Freight Record, Pressuring Daqin

China's Wari Railway moved more than 100 million tons of freight in 2026 faster than in any prior year, crossing the threshold 35 days earlier than it did in 2025 and setting a new record for the shortest time to reach the milestone in a single year, according to [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/fortune/20261114/4f3f2644a0b646eb8c7fa24377c19000/c.html).

The line, a backbone of the country's "West-to-East Coal Transport" strategy, reached 100.2629 million tons by November 13. It is the fourth consecutive year since 2022 that the corridor has surpassed 100 million tons, underscoring its rising weight in Chinese energy logistics — and its growing challenge to the older Daqin Railway.

## A key heavy-haul corridor

The Wari Railway runs 1,269 kilometers from Watang Town in Xing County, Lüliang City, Shanxi Province, east to the port city of Rizhao in Shandong, cutting through Shanxi, Henan, and Shandong. It was China's first heavy-haul railway built in a single phase to the 30-ton axle-load standard, and it links with seven major trunk lines — including the Jingguang, Jingjiu, and Jinghu corridors — as well as the Beijing-Hangzhou Grand Canal and the Yellow Sea waterway, forming a key heavy-haul energy channel to the sea.

The milestone figure came from Jinyulu Railway Corridor Co., Ltd., the operating company cited by Xinhua. China Railway Taiyuan Group, which coordinates operations on the line, has adopted a "one enterprise, one policy" approach — optimizing train formation, operating schedules, capacity allocation, and yard organization to compress wagon loading and transit dwell times. It is also developing coke and steel cargo categories and strengthening rail-water intermodal chains to diversify beyond coal.

## Operational gains accumulate

The record pace did not come out of nowhere. In April 2026, [China News Service](https://news.qq.com/rain/a/20260416A06K5V00) reported that the railway's 10,000-ton heavy-haul trains had cumulatively operated more than 30,000 runs, with cumulative freight since opening surpassing 700 million tons. Daily train counts had grown from roughly 10 at the start of operations to more than 140, and traction tonnage from 5,000 tons to 12,000 tons.

The operating environment is demanding. On the Zhengzhou Group's section through the Taihang Mountains, the line includes a continuous downhill run of more than 100 kilometers, with the steepest grade producing a vertical height difference between the head and tail of a 10,000-ton train equivalent to roughly an 11-story building. "We organized drivers of the 10,000-ton heavy-haul trains to conduct in-depth analysis and assessment of key sections, continuously optimizing train operating methods to ensure the safe operation of the 10,000-ton trains," said Zhang Feixiong, party branch secretary of the Changzi South operating workshop at the Xinxiang locomotive depot, in comments carried by China News Service.

## A competitive reshuffle among coal corridors

The record matters beyond the line itself. Analysis by [21st Century Business Herald](https://www.21jingji.com/article/20260121/herald/c66892b3d9c5f7192fb624c3c8948c8f.html) frames Wari's ascent as a direct competitive challenge to the Daqin Railway, long the dominant "West-to-East Coal Transport" artery. The report noted that Wari's 2025 volume first broke through 100 million tons, that its November 2025 single-month volume reached 13.4 million tons (up 28.3% year-on-year), and that its containerized coal business surged 424% year-on-year in 2025.

That pressure arrives alongside competition from the Haoji Railway, the country's longest dedicated coal line, which diverted an estimated 30 million tons — about 8% — from Daqin in 2024, a share expected to rise above 10% in 2025. Daqin's own volumes have slipped: its core Daqin line moved 390 million tons in 2025, down 0.54%, extending a negative trend, with capacity utilization falling from a peak above 95% to around 75%. The cumulative effect has been severe for Daqin's finances — net profit attributable to shareholders fell 27.66% in the first three quarters of 2025 even as revenue edged up 3.34%.

## Energy transition casts a longer shadow

The longer-term backdrop is the reshaping of China's power mix. In 2025, the country's new-energy installed capacity reached 1.82 billion kilowatts, surpassing 50% of total capacity at 51.3% to become the largest power source, while thermal power generation growth slowed to about 1.2%. Ultra-high-voltage transmission is also substituting for physical coal movement: according to the 21st Century Business Herald analysis, a single 1,000-kilovolt UHV direct-current line can deliver 66 billion kilowatt-hours a year, equivalent to the generating value of about 30 million tons of standard coal.

For coal rail corridors, that structural shift means eventual demand pressure, even as near-term volumes remain large. The early record on the Wari line may reflect faster early-year loading, a larger absolute annual total, or mix effects — Xinhua did not break down the coal versus non-coal shares of the 100 million tons, nor offer a full-year 2026 forecast, leaving those questions open.

## What to watch

Two dynamics will shape the Wari Railway's trajectory. In the near term, the competition among coal corridors — Wari, Haoji, and Daqin — will determine how freight is allocated as the lines expand non-coal cargo and intermodal services to defend their volumes. Over the longer term, the pace of China's energy transition and the build-out of UHV transmission will set the ceiling on how much coal needs to move by rail at all.

For now, the record stands as a marker of both efficiency gains and a still-hungry eastern seaboard. The coming winter heating season and the full-year 2026 tally will show whether the early milestone translates into record annual throughput — or simply a faster start to a plateau.
