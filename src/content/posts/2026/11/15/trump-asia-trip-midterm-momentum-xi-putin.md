# Trump Heads to Asia to Shift Momentum After Midterms

WASHINGTON — President Donald Trump departed for Asia this week hoping to make attention-grabbing moves on the world stage after his party [suffered deep losses](https://apnews.com/article/midterm-election-house-senate-democrats-republicans-trump-f126e12e05b579a327b98481c0ae2226) in the midterm elections, a rebuke that has sharply diminished his standing at home.

The Republican president is using the international trip to try to change the political narrative. He is poised to make a splash with a [historic three-way meeting](https://apnews.com/article/trump-xi-putin-summit-china-5fe92253ef38100bae913834a9b74d72) with Presidents Xi Jinping of China and Vladimir Putin of Russia to discuss their nuclear stockpiles — an unprecedented gathering of the heads of the world's biggest nuclear powers. Trump has long boasted about leveraging his relationships with the leaders of two of America's biggest global adversaries, though he has so far failed to achieve major breakthroughs with them.

## An Itinerary Built for Optics

Trump first heads to the Philippines to meet Tuesday with Southeast Asian leaders who look to the United States for help countering Beijing's aggression in the region. He will then travel to China on Wednesday for the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit in Shenzhen, where he will hold his third face-to-face meeting this year with Xi and also see Putin. Plans for the trilateral meeting were confirmed by the White House on Friday and the Kremlin on Saturday.

The White House has layered on additional agenda items meant to project momentum. Trump is expected to sign a trade deal with Thailand in Manila along with a host of commercial deals, and to participate in talks with the leaders of the Quad — the U.S., Australia, India and Japan — the first such meeting since Trump returned to the White House in 2025. He will also be the guest of honor at a leaders' dinner.

Yet analysts caution that symbolism may outweigh substance. Expectations are low that the summit will yield major breakthroughs, and China's far smaller nuclear arsenal gives Beijing little incentive to agree to restraints that would bind it more tightly than Washington or Moscow.

## A Diminished Hand at Home

The backdrop for the trip is a midterm rout that has left Trump politically weakened as he prepares to govern alongside a Congress controlled by the opposition. Voters delivered a strong rebuke to Trump and Republicans, driven by deep frustrations over high prices, the Iran war and concerns over government corruption. Democrats won control of both the House and the Senate, flipping states that had voted Republican for decades.

That loss carries direct consequences for Trump's foreign policy. With Democrats taking control of Congress in January, his ability to wield two of his signature tools — threatening import taxes and authorizing military action — will be constrained. He leaves for Asia as a president whose leverage abroad is shadowed by his diminished mandate at home.

"I think he, like other presidents, has figured out that in a moment where things are really hard domestically: Get on an airplane and go international," said Kurt Campbell, a former deputy secretary of state in the Biden administration and chairman of The Asia Group consultancy.

Democrats in Congress signaled they will be watching closely. "We're going to have a very close eye on it," said Rep. Gregory Meeks of New York, the top Democrat on the House Foreign Affairs Committee. Meeks also criticized what he described as inconsistent diplomacy. "It seems as though when Xi or Putin comes to America, he rolls out the red carpet for them — but other American allies come, he has not rolled out the red carpet for them."

## A Pattern Presidents Know Well

Trump's trip follows a familiar playbook. In 2014, Democratic President Barack Obama flew to Beijing days after his party lost the Senate to negotiate carbon emission targets with Xi. Republican President George W. Bush went to APEC in Vietnam after the GOP lost Congress in 2006, with the Iraq War hanging over his travels. Campbell described the move as a pattern presidents follow.

A year ago, Trump used a similar Asia trip to agree to a trade truce with China and to secure investment pledges from Japan and South Korea — establishing a baseline for what a momentum-shifting success looks like. Whether foreign-policy theatrics around a three-way nuclear summit can offset domestic political losses is an open question; historical precedents suggest such trips rarely reverse domestic political trajectories.

## Distractions Waiting at Home

The trip also unfolds amid domestic controversies that could undercut the momentum narrative. Trump left as airlines struggled after a nationwide ground stop on Friday led to thousands of flight cancellations, with the administration pointing fingers at predecessors, a contractor and Congress.

Separately, Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang's [stunning resignation](https://apnews.com/article/nvidia-jensen-china-ai-chips-1b7431af84c9d78773c277672a9486f3) after reports of extramarital affairs, including with a suspected Chinese spy, has sent ripples through the technology industry and could complicate the AI rivalry that has become central to U.S.-China relations. Beijing denied involvement, and administration officials said Trump does not plan to confront Xi on the issue.

Elon Musk, the head of SpaceX and Tesla and the world's wealthiest man, will accompany Trump on the trip. Musk spent at least $445 million trying to help Republicans keep Congress, and Democrats have signaled they want to investigate his ties to the administration.

## What to Watch

For all the pageantry, the trip's measurable outcomes will be limited by both Trump's reduced leverage and the structural realities of the talks. A substantive three-way nuclear agreement is a long shot. The trade deals and Quad meeting offer more achievable, if incremental, wins.

The larger question is whether a week of international summitry can shift a domestic narrative that voters have already rendered judgment on. With Democrats preparing congressional oversight and Trump's two most potent foreign-policy tools constrained by a hostile Congress, the trip may project strength more than it delivers it.
