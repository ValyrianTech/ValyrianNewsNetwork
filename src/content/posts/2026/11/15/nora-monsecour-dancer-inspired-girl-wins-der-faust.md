# Nora Monsecour: Dancer Who Inspired 'Girl' Wins Der Faust

Flemish dancer and choreographer Nora Monsecour, whose life inspired Lukas Dhont's 2018 film *Girl*, has won Germany's prestigious Der Faust theatre prize for her first solo choreography, *Façade*. The 30-year-old received the award in the "Dance Performer" category at a ceremony at the Hessisches Staatstheater Wiesbaden on 14 November 2026, a recognition that marks the most significant international honour of her career to date, as [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/15/wie-is-nora-monsecour-danseres-inspiratie-film-girl/) reported.

"I am completely overwhelmed," Monsecour said after the win, according to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/14/danseres-nora-monsecour-wint-belangrijke-duitse-theaterprijs/). "I didn't see it coming, but I'm really overjoyed. It's a beautiful crowning achievement on my work. The circle is complete now."

The award caps a remarkable year for a performer who has moved steadily between the stage, the screen and television, and whose personal history has helped reshape how mainstream audiences in Belgium and beyond understand trans lives.

## A life that became a landmark film

Monsecour was born in a boy's body and has always identified as a woman. "I have never had doubts," she said in a 2023 documentary, per VRT NWS. "I identify as a woman; I always have." Raised near Ghent, she joined the Royal Ballet School in Antwerp at the age of eight or nine and began her transition process at Ghent University Hospital shortly afterwards, starting puberty blockers at 11.

She has spoken candidly about the pace of that process. "If I had followed my own tempo, I would have completed my full transition at 11," she said. When the Royal Ballet School split students along gender lines in secondary school and would not let her dance with the girls, she moved to the MUDA Atheneum in Evergem, where she could. In 2015 she left Belgium to train at the Northern School of Contemporary Dance in Leeds.

Her story reached a global audience through *Girl*, Dhont's portrait of a 15-year-old transgender ballerina. Monsecour initially declined the director's request. "I had no interest in sharing my story," she recalled. Dhont returned a year later, and she agreed. The film premiered at Film Fest Gent in 2018, won prizes at Cannes for lead actor Victor Polster and for Dhont, and became Belgium's submission for the Academy Awards.

She has described the film as both necessary and imperfect. "Back then, in 2018, that film was really needed," she told [De Zondag](https://www.dezondag.be/lifestyle/entertainment/ik-ben-echt-een-carrierebeestje-nora-monsecour-over-so-you-think-you-can-dance-gent-en-haar-toekomst-in-danswereld/). "The subject was not discussed as openly as it is today." Even so, she has been careful about the role of figurehead: "I would rather stand among people than be an example."

## From ensemble member to prize-winning choreographer

Professionally, Monsecour built her career largely in Germany. After an internship in Mainz she joined the company tanzmainz at the Staatstheater Mainz from the 2018/19 season, touring internationally. In 2023 she stepped back from the permanent ensemble to pursue a freelance career, now working half-time with the German company while taking on television and choreography projects.

The prize-winning *Façade* is her debut as a choreographer. Premiered on 12 December 2025 and performed by Monsecour alone, the solo piece explores identity and authenticity — the question, in her words, of "how authentic am I?" As [SWR Kultur](https://www.swr.de/kultur/buehne/facade-nora-monsecour-choreografie-tanzmainz-staatstheater-mainz-100.html) described it, the work is staged within a set bounded by long white curtains, a kind of private room, in which she plays with exaggerated femininity and masculine gesture, moving as though on a catwalk.

The Der Faust jury praised exactly this quality. "She makes her body a projection screen for other people's ideas, fearlessly lays bare the search for meaning, and captivates with her engaging dance presence," the citation read. The jury noted that in *Façade* she "plays with an exaggerated femininity and presents herself as though on a catwalk, making her body a projection screen for the ideas of others."

Monsecour has stressed that the piece reaches beyond her own biography. "I think it's not only a theme for trans men or trans women, but for everyone," she told SWR. "Everyone has a façade. The piece is inspired by my trans experience... but in a way, the piece is very universal."

Der Faust, awarded since 2006 by the Deutscher Bühnenverein, is among the most important theatre prizes in the German-speaking world. The 2026 edition was the 21st, held at the Wiesbaden state theatre, with nominations announced in June.

"It was a really beautiful gala where all the great people from the German theatre world were present," Monsecour said. "A lovely evening with many creative people who now also know my name."

## A widening public role

Beyond the stage, Monsecour has become a familiar face on Flemish television. She joined the jury of VTM's *Dancing with the Stars* in 2025 and became a jury member on VTM's *So You Think You Can Dance* in 2026, alongside Dan Karaty and Kenzo Alvares. She also returned to work with Dhont, choreographing his 2026 anti-war film *Coward*.

Her connection to *Girl* remains a defining thread. "The film is still a big part of my life," she told SWR. "People still talk about it... I am very proud of the film." Monsecour, who published a memoir, *De weg naar mezelf* ("The Road to Myself"), in 2019, has consistently positioned herself as a working artist first and a symbol second.

At 30, she frames the Der Faust win as a beginning rather than a summation. "Now that I'm 30, I want to pursue new dreams and ambitions," she told De Morgen in October. "I want to tell young people things they can use, because this generation is so talented."

She is also clear-eyed about the span of a dancer's career. "A modern dancer like me can continue until their forties, maybe forty-five," she told De Zondag. "So I still have some margin."

## What to watch

For Belgian audiences, the Der Faust is a reminder that one of the country's most distinctive cultural exports has matured into an artist recognised on her own terms — not merely as the subject of a celebrated film. For the international dance world, Monsecour's win signals a continued appetite for work that blurs autobiography and abstraction.

The immediate question is what comes next: more choreography, further television work, or new collaborations on screen. What seems certain is that Monsecour intends to keep moving. "I'm really a career animal," she said. "My full attention goes to the work and the challenges I still want to take on."
