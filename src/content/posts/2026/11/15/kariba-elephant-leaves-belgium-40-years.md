# Kariba the Elephant Leaves Belgium After 40 Years

Kariba, an African elephant held in captivity for roughly four decades, has left Belgium for a sanctuary in Portugal in what animal-welfare organisation GAIA is calling a "historic rescue." The elephant, Belgium's last remaining African elephant, was transferred from Pakawi Park in Olmen to Pangea Elephant Sanctuary in the Alentejo region, arriving in the early hours of 11 November after a journey of some 2,200 kilometres.

The move was coordinated and financed by [GAIA](https://www.lesoir.be/782822/article/2026-11-15/un-sauvetage-historique-apres-40-ans-de-captivite-une-elephante-quitte-la), the Belgian animal-rights group, with the cooperation of Pakawi Park. It marks the end of a long chapter for an animal whose life has been shaped by captivity since she was a calf.

![Kariba the African elephant at Pangea sanctuary](https://www.pangeatrust.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Kariba-gallery-2-400x516.jpg)

## From the wild to a Belgian zoo

Kariba was captured from the wild as a calf in Zimbabwe in the 1980s and was sent from Zimbabwe to a zoo in Germany in 1984, according to [Pangea](https://www.pangeatrust.org/press-release-our-second-elephant-arrival/), the sanctuary that now houses her. She spent decades in European zoos before moving to Pakawi Park in 2012, where she was paired with Jenny, another African elephant retired from circus life. When Jenny died in 2022, Kariba was left living alone — a situation that welfare experts considered unsuitable for a highly social species.

"Elephants are social animals that need their own kind," GAIA president Michel Vandenbosch told [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/09/olifant-kariba-pakawi-park-olmen-portugal-pangea-gaia-michel-van/). "That Kariba lived here alone was therefore not ideal." Tommy Pasteels, director of Pakawi Park, said the park could no longer see her isolated. "It's a relief she won't spend another winter here living all alone," he said in a Pangea press release. "Elephants need to be with other elephants."

## A journey more than a year in the making

The transfer required more than a year of preparation, including progressive training and veterinary monitoring. The original plan had been set for May 2026, but the move was delayed by a foot abscess and then two further infections — one of which cost Kariba part of an ear. She finally departed in a transport crate on a truck, with 37 hours of road travel spread across several days and rest stops along the way.

She arrived at Pangea at 1am on Wednesday 11 November, hours before Storm Charles hit Portugal and triggered a nationwide state of alert. "Watching her step off the truck in the middle of the night, and then hearing Julie rumble to her the next day, are two moments we will never forget," said Kate Moore, managing director of Pangea.

## A new companion and a new home

At Pangea, Kariba is being introduced gradually to Julie, Portugal's last circus elephant, who arrived at the sanctuary in July 2026 after nearly 40 years performing in a Portuguese circus. The two had already been communicating from a distance, and Pangea describes Julie as fascinated by the new arrival.

The sanctuary, Europe's first large-scale elephant reserve, spans 402 hectares — roughly 1,000 acres — across the municipalities of Vila Viçosa and Alandroal. It is closed to visitors for welfare reasons and is supported by founding members including the Born Free Foundation, Fondation Brigitte Bardot, Olsen Animal Trust and World Animal Protection.

## A template for retirement, or a rebuke to zoos?

GAIA is framing the transfer as a precedent. "Zoos must take inspiration from this historic rescue and have the courage to offer wild animals a retirement, rather than keeping them captive until their death," Vandenbosch said. He argued that zoos routinely invoke conservation to justify captivity, "even though nearly 80% of the species they hold are not threatened with extinction." He called on Boudewijn Seapark in Bruges to do the same for its dolphins.

The case is also a flashpoint in a broader European debate over whether zoos should keep large, wide-ranging, social animals. Pangea said it is already in talks with other zoos about rehoming elephants living alone across Europe. "We hope Julie and Kariba will be the first of many," Moore said.

It is worth noting that Kariba's departure is unrelated to the October 2026 decision by Flemish Animal Welfare Minister Ben Weyts to order the closure of 16 animal enclosures at Pakawi Park — a decision later suspended by the Council of State. VRT reported that the transfer predates that decision and was planned from mid-2025.

## What's next

Kariba's departure means Belgium no longer holds any African elephants. How quickly she integrates with Julie, whether other Belgian facilities follow GAIA's call, and whether Pakawi Park's suspended enclosure closures are re-imposed are all open questions. For now, the immediate outcome is simpler: an elephant that spent four decades in captivity has, at last, company and space to roam.
