# Berkshire Q3 Net Profit Falls 28% as Buybacks Surge

Berkshire Hathaway's third-quarter net profit dropped 28% to $22.11 billion from $30.80 billion a year earlier, the conglomerate reported, as weaker investment gains overshadowed modest revenue growth. Under new chief executive Greg Abel, the Omaha-based company sharply accelerated share buybacks, remained a net seller of stocks, and pushed its cash reserves to a fresh record.

Total revenue rose 11.24% year-over-year to $105.65 billion, according to [The Paper](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_34429766), which cited the regulatory filing. Yet the headline profit decline was driven largely by the investment portfolio rather than by operations: investment gains fell roughly 46% to $9.41 billion from $17.31 billion a year earlier.

## A Warning on Headline Earnings

Berkshire reiterated in the filing its long-standing caution that quarterly investment swings make net income a poor gauge of performance. Under U.S. accounting rules, unrealized gains and losses on equity holdings flow through net income, rendering the headline figure volatile.

"The amount of investment gains/losses in any given quarter is usually meaningless... the resulting net earnings figure can be extremely misleading to investors who have little or no knowledge of accounting rules," the company said, as noted by The Paper.

Operating earnings — which exclude portfolio fluctuations and are the metric Warren Buffett historically favored — totaled $12.71 billion, down about 6% from $13.49 billion, [Reuters reported](https://www.reuters.com/business/finance/berkshire-hathaway-accelerates-stock-buybacks-operating-profit-falls-2026-11-07/). [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/07/berkshire-hathaway-q3-2026-earnings-buybacks.html) put the decline at 5.8%, attributing the drop to a pullback in insurance results.

## Insurance Drags, Rail and Retail Grow

Results across Berkshire's businesses diverged. Insurance underwriting profit tumbled 43% to $1.35 billion from $2.37 billion, while insurance investment income fell 14% to $2.73 billion — together the biggest drag on operating earnings.

Other segments posted gains. The BNSF railroad's after-tax profit rose about 19% to $1.73 billion, manufacturing, service and retail operating profit climbed roughly 12% to $4.04 billion, and Berkshire Hathaway Energy profit increased about 8% to $1.61 billion, according to The Paper.

## Buybacks More Than Double

Berkshire repurchased about $9.6 billion of its own stock in the quarter — more than double the roughly $4.5 billion bought back in the second quarter, and a single-quarter record. Year-to-date buybacks now total about $14.4 billion.

The acceleration signals that high-quality acquisition targets meeting Berkshire's standards remain scarce, and that the company may view its own shares as the most attractive investment available, The Paper noted.

At the same time, Berkshire was a net seller of about $426 million in equities during the quarter, a reversal from the first half of 2026, when it was a net buyer of roughly $116.25 billion in stocks. Its five largest holdings — Alphabet, American Express, Apple, Bank of America and Coca-Cola — remained unchanged, together representing about 65% of the equity portfolio's fair value.

The Alphabet position has proven a drag: Berkshire's AI-linked investment in the Google parent, built as part of an $80 billion equity raise in June 2026, has coincided with Alphabet shares falling roughly 25% since mid-year, weighing on third-quarter investment gains.

## Cash Hits a Record

Cash and equivalents climbed 3% to $377.4 billion as of September 30, up from $365.5 billion at the end of the second quarter, CNBC reported. The network also noted a nuance: excluding rail and utility cash along with T-bill payables, cash actually slipped 1.9% quarter-over-quarter to $352.3 billion. The Paper reported the same headline figure, putting cash at $377.4 billion versus $365.51 billion at the prior quarter-end.

The figure differs from a separate calculation published by Reuters, which reported that Berkshire ended September with $356.9 billion of cash and equivalents, down from $380.2 billion six months earlier. The two totals reflect differing definitions of what counts toward the cash pile rather than a contradiction in the underlying results.

Even so, the record stockpile underscores a defensive posture as markets grow choppier.

## Defensive Appeal

The caution appears to be resonating with investors. Over the past month, Berkshire's Class A shares have risen more than 12% while the S&P 500 fell 5.5%; year-to-date, Berkshire has gained 13% versus 7.8% for the broad index, CNBC reported. During the reporting week alone, Berkshire rose about 5% as the S&P 500 dropped 2.68% amid a shakeout in AI-related trades.

Analysts read the outperformance as a sign that defensive capital is seeking a haven and that anxiety over the leadership transition — Buffett's departure from the CEO role — is fading as Abel settles in.

## What to Watch

The report crystallizes Berkshire's stance under Abel: rich in cash, disciplined on acquisitions, and willing to return capital to shareholders through buybacks while trimming equities. The key questions ahead are whether insurance results recover, whether the company finally deploys its $377 billion war chest into a major acquisition, and how the Alphabet bet performs as the AI trade swings. For now, Berkshire looks less like an aggressive buyer and more like a fortress — one that is increasingly content to invest in itself.
