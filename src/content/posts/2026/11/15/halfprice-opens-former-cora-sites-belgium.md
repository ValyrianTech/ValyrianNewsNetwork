# HalfPrice Opens on Former Cora Sites in Belgium

Polish off-price retailer HalfPrice opened its first Belgian store in Châtelineau, near Charleroi, on Friday 13 November 2026, taking up space in one of the former Cora hypermarket galleries that were abandoned earlier this year. The opening drew large crowds and marks the start of a six-store Belgian roll-out that will repopulate some of the country's most painfully vacated retail sites.

## A retail revival after a painful closure

The Belgian launch is the most visible sign yet that the seven former Cora sites, whose closure eliminated 1,779 jobs in early 2026, are being brought back to life. Cora, a French hypermarket chain, announced in April 2025 that it would shut all seven of its Belgian hypermarkets at the start of 2026, and the adjacent shopping galleries were put up for sale.

As [RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/short/belgique-lenseigne-halfprice-simplante-sur-les-anciens-sites-de-cora-7156) reported, HalfPrice is now setting up shop on those former Cora sites, repurposing the vacated locations. The redevelopment is led by Belgian real-estate group Mitiska Reim, which acquired the Cora galleries and specialises in proximity retail parks anchored by a large supermarket and surrounded by national and international brands.

The stakes were high. Belgian media described the Cora shutdown, particularly in Wallonia where several stores were among the largest private employers in their towns, as one of the country's worst social catastrophes in fifteen years. By mid-September 2026, only around 30% of the laid-off Châtelineau workers had found new work.

## The roll-out

Following the Châtelineau opening, four more HalfPrice stores are scheduled to open before the end of November 2026: Anderlecht (19 November), Messancy (20 November), Rocourt (26 November) and Hornu (27 November), with a sixth and final store in La Louvière slated for November 2027. The five 2026 stores together represent more than 15,000 m² of retail space, with individual stores of roughly 3,000 m².

According to [RetailDetail](https://www.retaildetail.be/fr/news/mode/la-chaine-polonaise-de-magasins-dusine-halfprice-simplante-dans-six-centres-shopping-cora/), HalfPrice is a Polish off-price chain with more than 220 stores across 15 European countries. It sells international brands in fashion, shoes, accessories, home decoration, sport and leisure at heavily reduced prices, with a constantly changing assortment. It belongs to the Polish Modivo Platform group, which also owns footwear retailer CCC, online shoe brand eobuwie, ShockPrice, SK Store, Boardriders, the Warsaw Sneaker Store and Beverly Hills Polo Club. HalfPrice reportedly accounts for roughly 30% of the group's revenue.

## What it means for the sites

Mitiska Reim says 95% of the former Cora galleries are once again occupied and that around 2,500 jobs have been created or secured across the redeployed sites. A single HalfPrice store is estimated to create 30 to 40 jobs.

Bram Thomas, CEO of Mitiska Reim, framed the arrival as a strategic win. "Its concept, its very broad product offering and its off-price positioning — still under-represented in Belgium — will bring new dynamism to the sites and strengthen their attractiveness well beyond their immediate catchment area," he said in the RetailDetail announcement.

The openings are being read locally as a symbol of the galleries "coming back to life," a turnaround after the painful Cora shutdown. Cora's own chief executive, Olivier Haller, had been blunt about the format's decline, telling RTBF that the company had explored every avenue and that "no one wants to invest in this commercial format that scares people anymore."

## Analysis: a model shift, and a partial recovery

HalfPrice's arrival signals a broader shift in Belgian retail: the retreat of the large single-operator hypermarket in favour of off-price and multi-brand discount formats, a segment still relatively under-developed in Belgium. Mitiska Reim's proximity-retail-park recipe, proven at Mechelen where Albert Heijn and Action moved in quickly, is being used to repopulate spaces that a single big-box operator could no longer sustain.

Retail analyst Pierre-Alexandre Billiet of Gondola credited Mitiska Reim with going "to sites that are unique and exceptional" and with combining different players so effectively that "everybody trusts them on the quality of the sites."

The job figures, however, warrant caution. Mitiska Reim's claim of 2,500 jobs created contrasts with the loss of 1,779 Cora jobs and the low re-employment rate among former staff. The new jobs are not necessarily equivalent to the old ones in employer, hours or pay, and the figures are company-provided and have not been independently audited. The net employment picture across the affected communities is more nuanced than either number alone suggests.

The expansion is also part of a westward push by Central European discount groups into Western European markets, and it speaks directly to Belgian consumers under cost-of-living pressure, even as it raises questions about the long-term sustainability of the off-price model and the pressure it places on full-price retailers.

## What's next

With the Châtelineau store open and four more due before the end of November, attention now turns to whether the revamped galleries can hold their momentum. The final La Louvière store, due in November 2027, will complete the HalfPrice roll-out. The key question is whether the jobs and footfall promised by Mitiska Reim materialise into durable recovery for the towns that lost Cora, and whether Belgium's off-price segment can grow without eroding the full-price retail it sits alongside.
