# Indonesia Opens RMB Clearing Bank to Cut Dollar Reliance

Indonesia officially inaugurated a renminbi (RMB) clearing bank in Jakarta on November 12, a step expected to let companies settle trade with China directly in yuan and reduce their dependence on the US dollar. Bank of China (BOC) will serve as the designated clearing bank, with roughly 100 representatives from Chinese and Indonesian government agencies, enterprises and financial institutions attending the ceremony at the central bank, [Xinhua reported via People's Daily](http://world.people.com.cn/n1/2026/1113/c1002-40832949.html).

## Context: A Build-Up to De-Dollarization

The clearing house is the culmination of years of deepening financial cooperation. The plan was first set in motion at a June 11 meeting in Shanghai between Bank Indonesia (BI) and the People's Bank of China (PBOC), when the two central banks unveiled a deal on establishing a clearing bank for the Chinese currency, [Jakarta Globe reported](https://jakartaglobe.id/business/indonesia-to-establish-renminbi-clearing-bank-to-deepen-financial-ties-with-china). The arrangement adds to a broader push by Jakarta to settle more cross-border commerce in partner currencies rather than through the greenback.

As [Jakarta Globe](https://jakartaglobe.id/business/indonesias-nondollar-transactions-more-than-double-to-526-billion) noted, Indonesia's local currency transactions with seven partner countries reached $52.6 billion in the first nine months of 2026, more than double the $25.7 billion recorded throughout all of 2025. That leap reflects the central bank's drive to strengthen the rupiah, which has come under heavy strain this year and briefly weakened beyond Rp 18,000 per dollar.

## How the Clearing Bank Works

The infrastructure is already in place. Twelve local and foreign banks — including Bank of China, Industrial and Commercial Bank of China, China Construction Bank, Bank Mandiri and Bank Negara Indonesia — act as authorized cross-currency market makers and participants in the clearing bank, according to Xinhua. The mechanism is designed to ensure RMB liquidity is available inside Indonesia and to encourage direct rupiah-renminbi transactions.

The demand appears substantial. As of July, domestic renminbi demand stood at around $38.9 billion, already close to BI's internal estimate of $40 billion a year, [Jakarta Globe](https://jakartaglobe.id/business/indonesia-to-have-renminbi-clearing-bank-by-q4-2026) reported. Ahead of the launch, Indonesia's trade with mainland China had already risen about 19% year-on-year to $84 billion in the first half of 2026.

## Officials Frame Move as a Cushion Against External Shocks

Speaking at the opening, BI Governor Destry Damayanti said the mechanism would let firms sidestep the dollar entirely. "With the launch of the RMB clearing bank and the improvement of the local currency transaction mechanism, enterprises can directly settle trade with China in renminbi, which helps reduce dependence on third-party currencies, maintain exchange rate stability and enhance regional economic resilience against external shocks," she said.

Airlangga Hartarto, Indonesia's Coordinating Minister for Economic Affairs, linked the change to Jakarta's trade exposure. "China is one of Indonesia's largest non-oil-and-gas import sources; expanding settlement in currencies other than the US dollar will significantly ease the external pressure Indonesia faces," he said.

Zhang Hui, President of Bank of China, cast the clearing house as part of a longer-term development strategy, tying it to the "Golden Indonesia 2045" vision and pledging financial support for mineral downstream processing, energy infrastructure and the green transition. Zhou Kan, Chargé d'affaires of the Chinese Embassy in Indonesia, noted that China has been Indonesia's largest trading partner for 13 consecutive years and a top-three investment source for 10, framing the clearing bank as the latest in a series of steps that included 2021's local currency settlement mechanism and Indonesia's panda bond issuance this year.

## Analysis: A Node in a Wider Yuan Network

The clearing bank does more than cut transaction costs. It deepens Indonesia's integration into China's RMB infrastructure across Southeast Asia, a region central to Beijing's ambitions for its currency. A second clearing bank from an Indonesian state-owned "Himbara" bank is already in the administrative pipeline, BI has said — a signal that Jakarta intends to broaden the channel rather than rely on a single institution.

That momentum is often read through the lens of US-China financial competition, given the clearing system's links to China's Cross-border Interbank Payment System (CIPS). Yet Jakarta's approach remains deliberately diversified: it has signed local currency arrangements with Malaysia, Thailand, Japan, South Korea, the United Arab Emirates and Singapore as well, positioning the RMB clearing bank as one option among many rather than a wholesale pivot away from the dollar.

## What's Next

The immediate test is take-up. The theoretical demand for RMB settlement is large, but how much of the $84 billion half-year trade flow actually shifts into yuan will determine the venture's real impact. The key indicators to watch are the volume of direct rupiah-renminbi transactions, the pace at which the second clearing bank is named, and whether the new channel meaningfully eases pressure on a rupiah that remains Asia's worst-performing currency this year.
