# China Flies First Manned 10-Ton Electric Commercial Aircraft

China's state-owned planemaker COMAC has completed the first manned test flight of its 10-ton-class electric commercial aircraft, the CE-25A, a step toward large-scale electric propulsion that the company is framing as a major milestone in the country's push into electric aviation. According to [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/photo/20261114/7e7ed43e9e45489f93aad567ef97db3b/c.html), the aircraft conducted the "intelligent" manned flight on November 14 in Binzhou, Shandong Province, with its status normal and all indicators meeting expectations.

State broadcaster [CCTV News](https://news.cctv.com/2026/11/14/ARTI0T3HPsiH5Mt03BTdli7r261114.shtml) reported that the aircraft carried two pilots and one flight test engineer. The test sortie lasted 57 minutes 46 seconds in total, including 16 minutes 38 seconds of airborne flight covering 60.4 kilometers. It marked the second major milestone for the program, following the CE-25A's first unmanned autonomous flight on February 2, 2026.

## What the CE-25A Is

The CE-25A is COMAC's first electric commercial aircraft, designed primarily for inter-city commercial travel. It carries 19 passengers, has a maximum takeoff weight of 10 tons, a cruise speed of 300 km/h, a cruise altitude of 2,400 meters and a range of 400 kilometers. The aircraft's name encodes its design logic: "C" stands for China and COMAC, "E" for electric, "25" for compliance with China's CCAR-25 transport-category airworthiness regulations — the same safety standard applied to large commercial jets — and "A" for the first product in the line.

The aircraft is powered by a wing-mounted distributed electric ducted-fan propulsion system. As [IT之家](https://www.ithome.com/1/027/075.htm) explained, the design places multiple smaller electric ducted fans across the wings rather than relying on a few large engines, with five fans on each wing for a total of ten, blended into the wing structure. This arrangement exploits the divisibility of electric motor power to improve fault tolerance and aerodynamic efficiency, allowing the aircraft to maintain controlled flight even if some propulsion units fail.

The CE-25A was developed by COMAC in partnership with battery giant CATL and Shanghai Jiao Tong University. In July 2023, the three parties established the joint venture COMAC Times (商飞时代) with CATL and COMAC each holding 47%, focused on aviation electric propulsion research. Public information cites a condensed-matter battery with an energy density of 500 Wh/kg; its significance, IT之家 noted, lies less in the headline figure than in meeting aviation-grade thermal runaway protection and cycle-life requirements — a markedly different validation standard from automotive-grade batteries.

## Why This Flight Matters

The core significance lies in the combination of "manned" and "intelligent" flight. All 19 previous validations were unmanned autonomous flights. The manned intelligent flight means the pilot sits in the cockpit while the aircraft can still complete missions via autonomous systems, with the pilot retaining in-loop monitoring and intervention capability. From an engineering-validation standpoint, this is closer to the real "pilot plus intelligent system" human-machine collaboration model of future commercial operations than a pure unmanned first flight.

Before this manned flight, the CE-25A completed ground taxiing tests in late October 2026, moving the program from scale models and drawings into full-aircraft verification. The program sits within China's broader national strategy emphasizing "new quality productive forces" and the development of a green, low-altitude economy — a policy push that has channeled investment into electric aircraft and related infrastructure.

## The Certification Gap

Despite the milestone, a substantial gap remains between this test flight and actual passenger service. China's Civil Aviation Administration (CAAC) has accepted the type certificate application for the CE-25A, formally starting the airworthiness certification process. But acceptance only means the review has begun: type certification and entry into commercial passenger service still require extensive compliance verification testing and regulatory review.

The CE-25A is best understood as a technology demonstrator and validation aircraft rather than a production airliner. Its commercial viability is also constrained by its performance envelope — a 300 km/h cruise speed and roughly 400 km range position it in a regional, short-haul niche rather than competing with conventional jetliners on trunk routes.

## What to Watch

The next milestones to watch are the pace of compliance testing under CAAC review, the full battery system's safety certification status, and any announced timeline for type certification. Distributed electric propulsion is a pathway pursued globally for electric and hybrid aviation, and China's achievement of a manned flight at the 10-ton scale is a notable step in that race. Whether it translates into certified, commercially flown aircraft — and how quickly — will determine whether this milestone marks the beginning of a new category of regional air transport or remains an impressive engineering demonstration.

What is clear is that the November 14 flight moves the CE-25A past the unmanned-validation phase and into territory that more closely resembles real operations. For China's electric aviation ambitions, that is progress measured not in altitude or speed, but in the credibility of the next round of tests.
