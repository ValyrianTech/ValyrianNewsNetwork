# 12 Leaders to Attend APEC CEO Summit in Shenzhen

Twelve APEC economy leaders will attend the 2026 APEC CEO Summit in Shenzhen on November 17–18, Chinese organizers announced Sunday, as Beijing frames its host year around the message that openness is the "lifeline" of Asia-Pacific cooperation. The business summit runs just ahead of the 33rd APEC Economic Leaders' Meeting on November 18–19, where a first-ever trilateral between the presidents of the United States, China and Russia is set to dominate the agenda.

## Who is attending

At a press conference on 2026 APEC business activities held in Shenzhen, Li Qingshuang, vice chairman of the China Council for the Promotion of International Trade, said President Xi Jinping and the leaders of Australia, Canada, Chile, Hong Kong (China), Indonesia, South Korea, Malaysia, Mexico, Russia, Thailand and Vietnam will attend the summit and deliver speeches, remarks or join thematic discussions, according to [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/world/20261115/5667a85ff3214962a5bbc18ba3b9fe13/c.html).

The event will be held at the Qianhai International Convention Center and is billed as an important component of APEC Leaders' Meeting Week. More than 100 Fortune Global 500 companies are expected to send representatives, with the United States accounting for the largest foreign corporate presence at nearly 20 percent of participating firms.

## The 'openness' framing

Chinese state media has leaned heavily into an openness narrative in the run-up to the meetings. A Xinhua editorial published Sunday, titled "Openness is the lifeline of Asia-Pacific cooperation," quoted Xi across a decade of APEC appearances to argue that economic integration is the region's antidote to rising protectionism and geopolitical friction.

> "The Pacific Ocean is vast because it has no natural barriers; we should not set artificial barriers for it," Xi said at an APEC CEO Summit in 2013, a line the editorial revived.

The framing serves a dual purpose for Beijing. It burnishes Shenzhen — the southern tech hub that symbolizes China's reform-and-opening era — as the natural stage for Asia-Pacific commerce, while projecting China as the anchor of regional economic integration at a moment of considerable global turbulence.

## The geopolitical backdrop

The headline business gathering sits atop a far more consequential diplomatic event. The White House has confirmed that President Donald Trump, Xi and Russian President Vladimir Putin will hold their first three-way meeting on the sidelines of APEC in Shenzhen on November 18–19, as [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/14/trump-xi-putin-trilateral-meeting-at-apec-what-to-expect) reported.

That distinction matters: Trump does not appear on the CEO Summit speaker list for November 17–18, but will attend the Leaders' Meeting that follows. Talks are expected to focus on nuclear proliferation, with the New START treaty having lapsed in February, alongside the US-Israeli war on Iran — now past 100 days with the Strait of Hormuz still closed — and the fragile US-China trade truce.

Expectations for concrete outcomes are low. A senior Trump administration official told Reuters the nuclear discussion would start a conversation but that "we do not expect to get any kind of agreement." China has said joining a trilateral nuclear disarmament process is "neither fair, reasonable nor realistic" at this stage, given it had no part in framing the original treaty.

## Business sees opportunity

For the corporate world, enthusiasm for the Shenzhen gathering is running high. Alex Parle, executive vice president of the US National Center for APEC, told Chinese financial outlet Southern Finance that US companies' interest in the summit is "unprecedentedly high," citing their existing footprint in the Greater Bay Area. He described Shenzhen as intuitively demonstrating China's decades-long transformation and re-integration into the global economy.

Parle flagged digital-infrastructure investment and cross-border anti-fraud cooperation as priority areas for the business community — practical, if modest, deliverables that may stand a better chance of materializing than the grander diplomatic ambitions on display.

## What to watch

The APEC "China year" — Beijing's third time hosting, after Shanghai in 2001 and Beijing in 2014 — has staged roughly 300 events across the country. The Shenzhen finale will test whether that organizational muscle translates into tangible economic signals.

Key questions remain: whether the Trump-Xi-Putin trilateral yields anything beyond imagery, whether the strong US corporate turnout produces deals or stays symbolic, and whether the openness rhetoric survives contact with the protectionist pressures still shaping global trade. For now, Beijing's message is clear — and the world's attention, as much as the region's, will be fixed on Shenzhen this week.
