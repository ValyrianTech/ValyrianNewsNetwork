# Hollywood's Johnny Depp Comeback Test Begins

Nearly eight years after domestic abuse allegations and a pair of bruising defamation trials pushed him to the margins of the movie business, Johnny Depp is returning to major studio filmmaking. His vehicle is *Ebenezer*, Paramount Pictures' gothic reimagining of Charles Dickens's *A Christmas Carol*, which premiered in New York on Nov. 1 and opens in the United States and the United Kingdom on Nov. 13.

The release is being watched far beyond the holiday box office. As [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/pop-culture/pop-culture-news/johnny-depp-ebenezer-hollywood-uncanceling-return-to-big-screen-rcna606019) reported, the film sits at the center of a broader industry reassessment of celebrity "cancellation" — the notion that Hollywood is now prepared to "uncancel certain stars — if audiences are willing to." For Depp, a once-bankable A-lister sidelined by scandal, that conditional is the entire gamble.

## A Carefully Managed Return

*Ebenezer*, directed by indie-horror favorite Ti West and written by Nathaniel Halpern, casts Depp as Scrooge opposite Rupert Grint, Andrea Riseborough, Daisy Ridley, Ian McKellen and Sam Claflin. It carries a modest budget of roughly $38 million — a fraction of the mega-budget tentpoles that once defined Depp's career. That lower bar is central to the strategy: even a middling opening can be counted a success, particularly with holiday legs and overseas appeal.

Paramount's marketing has been deliberately unconventional. Rather than submit Depp to traditional press junkets where he might be pressed on the abuse litigation, the studio has deployed him in character — appearing as Scrooge in a snow-covered installation at San Diego Comic-Con in July and staging a mischievous Christmas-market stunt in Manhattan's Bryant Park on Nov. 2. The trailer's tagline, "The name you know, the story you don't," and Depp's in-character line — "It's good to be back" — frame the film as a performance showcase rather than a personal vindication.

That distinction matters, according to crisis strategists. Amber Melville-Brown, a media lawyer at Withers, told [IndieWire](https://www.indiewire.com/features/general/johnny-depp-comeback-paramount-ebenezer-promotion-controversy-1235225408/) that a campaign built around vindication "invites an argument about who, precisely, has been vindicated and for what — starting the debate over again," whereas "a campaign built around the performance sparks a different conversation."

## The Numbers Behind the Bet

The early signals have been encouraging for Paramount. A test screening in Paramus, New Jersey, in August scored in the 80s — above the industry norm — with Depp's character scored at 86 and women over 35, a demographic presumed to be resistant, rating it most favorably. Depp's Q Score, a measure of public familiarity and appeal, reportedly rose to 27 from 24 in 2023, and a research study earlier in 2026 ranked him among the most-cited actors who would motivate audiences to see a film in a theater.

Box-office tracking is more measured. As [/Film](https://www.slashfilm.com/2305949/johnny-depp-movie-ebenezer-a-christmas-carol-box-office-preview/) reported in early November, projections put the domestic opening in the $17 million to $24 million range, with earlier estimates as high as $35 million. Against a $38 million budget, the math is forgiving but not guaranteed. The review embargo lifts on Nov. 12, mere hours before screenings begin — a timing widely read as a sign of tempered studio confidence in the critical reception.

## The Wounds That Preceded the Return

The comeback is inseparable from the events that interrupted it. In 2016, Depp's then-wife Amber Heard filed for divorce alleging domestic abuse. In 2020, Depp lost a U.K. libel case against The Sun, with a judge ruling that the "wife beater" characterization was "substantially true," and he was asked to resign from the *Fantastic Beasts* franchise. The 2022 U.S. defamation trial, however, ended largely in his favor, with a jury siding with him on multiple claims. That split outcome — a U.S. court vindicating him even as a U.K. court stood by its findings — is precisely why the "uncanceling" remains contested.

Critics have not softened. Writing for [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/film/2026/jul/24/johnny-depp-is-playing-scrooge-i-am-not-here-for-his-big-hollywood-comeback) when the trailer debuted, columnist Stuart Heritage described the rebranding of a man "found by a UK civil court to have repeatedly punched his wife" as "the master of misfits" as "a piece of extraordinary public relations."

It is also worth noting that Depp's commercial momentum had already begun to erode before the scandal, amid reports of erratic on-set behavior and costly flops. His exile from studio filmmaking was driven by both reputational and business factors — a nuance often flattened in the "cancellation" framing.

## A Template — or a Cautionary Tale

The industry will be watching closely, and not only because of Depp. *Ebenezer* is one of the first films greenlit after Paramount's 2025 acquisition by Skydance, giving its performance symbolic weight for the new leadership. Depp also has a second return vehicle — the Lionsgate revenge thriller *Day Drinker*, directed by Marc Webb and co-starring Penélope Cruz, due in March 2027 — meaning the box office verdict may shape how both studios proceed.

Beyond that lies the question of *Pirates of the Caribbean*. At Disney's D23 Expo, producer Jerry Bruckheimer floated hopes of bringing Depp back, though reports suggest a supporting role alongside a Margot Robbie-led story. Depp has said he would be open to returning, telling Entertainment Tonight the only involvement he truly wants is "just to send them off on a better note."

## What to Watch

The next few weeks will produce the answers that months of strategy could not. If *Ebenezer* lands within tracking and holds through the holidays, it will hand Hollywood a template for rehabilitating sidelined stars: subdued budgets, performance-first marketing and a quiet pivot away from scandal. If it stumbles, it may validate the caution that has kept Depp at arm's length for the better part of a decade.

For now, the industry's stance is being set reactively. Paramount made the early bet; rivals are watching the returns. Whether Johnny Depp has truly been forgiven — by audiences, if not by critics — is a question the box office is about to answer.
