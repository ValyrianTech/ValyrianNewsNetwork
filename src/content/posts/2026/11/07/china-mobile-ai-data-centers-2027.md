# China Mobile Bets Bigger on AI Data Centers

China Mobile Ltd., the country's largest wireless carrier, will invest more than 100 billion yuan ($15 billion) in computing infrastructure in 2027 — roughly a fifth of its planned 500 billion yuan procurement budget — as it races to build gigawatt-scale data centers for surging artificial intelligence demand, [Caixin Global reported](https://www.caixinglobal.com/2026-11-06/china-mobile-bets-bigger-on-data-centers-as-ai-demand-grows-102501919.html).

Chairman Chen Zhongyue announced the plans on Friday at the 2026 Mobile Network Conference in Guangzhou, saying computing power had become the single biggest source of the company's incremental investment this year. "Computing power became the biggest source of the company's incremental investment this year," Chen said.

## From Telecom Carrier to AI Infrastructure Provider

The headline figure is only part of a broader strategic shift. China Mobile is repositioning itself from a conventional telecom operator into a provider of AI computing power and cloud services — a pivot that mirrors moves by China Telecom and China Unicom, but at a far larger scale.

Chen said the company began construction in 2026 on three gigawatt-scale intelligent computing parks and expects to deliver about 1.5 gigawatts of capacity this year. It plans to build two more in 2027, bringing the total to seven gigawatt-scale data parks. "This year we started construction on three gigawatt-scale computing parks, expected to deliver 1.5 GW of capacity; next year we'll build two more, bringing the total to seven gigawatt-scale data parks," he said, according to the [Caixin Chinese original](https://www.caixin.com/2026-11-06/102501797.html).

The company has already built multiple 10,000-card intelligent computing clusters and is now laying out several ultra-large-scale clusters. Capacity is expected to double in each of the next two years, Chen said.

All new facilities will use standardized modular designs with high-density liquid cooling — a technical choice that signals a shift toward the dense GPU racks typical of AI training workloads. The approach is already paying off in construction speed: in Zhongwei, Ningxia, China Mobile built a single-site computing hub of 170,000 square meters in three months and delivered capacity within five months.

## Demand Validates the Build-Out

The investment is being driven by a measurable surge in traffic. Chen said mobile internet traffic grew 18% in 2026 — double last year's rate — while AI-generated content traffic grew fivefold. He described three structural changes AI is bringing to telecom: rising overall traffic, a surge in uplink demand, and the need for cloud and network to jointly solve latency problems, [Caixin reported](https://www.caixin.com/2026-11-06/102501701.html).

The financial returns are already visible in the data center business. In 2025, data center revenue rose 8.7%, AI-focused data centers gained 35.4%, and intelligent computing services surged 279%, according to Caixin's digest.

## A Sector-Wide Reallocation

China Mobile's move follows a period of capex discipline. In March 2026, the company reported full-year 2025 revenue of 1.05 trillion yuan, up just 0.9% year-on-year — a sharp slowdown from 3.1% in 2024 and 7.7% in 2023 — while net profit slipped 0.9% to 137.1 billion yuan, [Caixin reported](https://www.caixin.com/2026-03-26/102427775.html). The company also disclosed that its 2026 capital expenditure would fall 9.5%, even as it introduced revenue targets for computing and intelligent services for the first time.

The company is also pushing to commercialize computing power, setting up a group-level "Token Office" in June 2026 to advance that effort, [Caixin reported](https://www.caixin.com/2026-06-30/102459424.html).

## Risks and Questions

The scale of the ambition is striking, but so are the risks. Caixin has documented a global tech sell-off testing the economics of the AI boom and reported regulatory warnings that the AI investment boom is crowding out traditional industries, [Caixin Global reported](https://www.caixinglobal.com/2026-11-03/china-regulator-warns-ai-investment-boom-crowds-out-traditional-industries-102500480.html).

Monetization remains the open question. Rapid traffic and AI-content growth validate the build-out, but rented computing capacity can depreciate quickly as chip generations turn over. Readers commenting on the Caixin article itself voiced skepticism, with one writing that "infrastructure providers' rented computing power will quickly become obsolete."

## What's Next

China Mobile's seven gigawatt-scale parks would make it one of the world's largest AI data-center builders by power capacity, comparable in ambition to global hyperscalers. Executives said capital spending on computing would be matched by rising revenue from computing services. Investors will be watching whether China Telecom and China Unicom match the pace — intensifying a domestic AI data-center race — and whether utilization rates can justify the outlay.
