# Xi Votes in Beijing District People's Congress Elections

Chinese President and Communist Party General Secretary Xi Jinping cast his ballot at a polling station in central Beijing on November 6, as the capital held elections for district- and township-level people's congress deputies, [Xinhua News reported](https://www.news.cn/politics/leaders/20261106/d5594b53afa94e50a108281009fc1477/c.html). Xi voted at the Huairientang polling station in the Zhongnanhai electoral district of Xicheng District, an event framed around his call to "practice whole-process people's democracy" and to "ensure the election of representatives satisfactory to the people."

The vote marked Xi's participation in a routine, five-yearly re-election cycle covering Beijing's districts and townships — a civic ritual that carries significant symbolic weight in China's political calendar.

## The Ballot and the Message

According to Xinhua, Xi arrived at the Huairientang polling station at around 3:30 p.m., listened to explanations of the election process, handed his voter certificate to staff for verification, received a single ballot, filled it out at a writing desk, and walked to the ballot box to cast his vote.

The hall, the wire service noted, was decorated with a red flag and a ballot box bearing the national emblem, with the scene described as orderly and solemn.

In remarks released with the report, Xi emphasized the institutional significance of the occasion. "Electing people's congress deputies is the foundation for upholding, improving, and running the people's congress system well, and a major event in the country's political life," he said, calling for strengthened oversight over the entire election process to ensure it "proceeds in the right direction and with clean conduct."

## Other Leaders Follow Suit

Xi was not alone in voting. According to Xinhua, Premier Li Qiang and Vice Premier Ding Xuexiang cast their ballots at the Ziguangge polling station in Xicheng District's Wenjin Street electoral district. Zhao Leji, chairman of the National People's Congress Standing Committee, voted at the Great Hall of the People station in the Zhongnanhai electoral district; Wang Huning and Cai Qi, the Beijing Party Secretary, voted at the Huairientang station in the same district. Li Xi, who heads the Central Commission for Discipline Inspection, voted at its station in Dongcheng District's Taijichang electoral district.

Vice President Han Zheng and former leader Hu Jintao voted by proxy at the Huairientang station, the report said. Other Party, state and military leaders, along with retired officials, voted in their respective districts or by proxy.

## The Scale of the Exercise

Beijing set up 13,054 polling stations across the capital on the day, according to Xinhua. Through differential, or competitive, elections, voters were to choose 4,898 new district people's congress deputies and 11,137 township-level deputies.

Under China's electoral system, deputies to district, county and township people's congresses are elected directly by voters, while higher-level congresses — at the municipal, provincial and national levels — are chosen indirectly by the congresses beneath them. This round covers Beijing's districts and townships.

## Analysis: Ritual, Signaling and the Limits of Comparison

Coverage of the president casting his ballot is a recurring feature of each local congress cycle, and its news value lies less in electoral uncertainty than in the leader's participation and the messaging attached to it. The framing around "whole-process people's democracy" — a signature concept promoted under Xi that describes a system of elections, consultation, decision-making, management and supervision meant to broaden participation beyond periodic voting — reflects the official emphasis on the people's congress system as a foundational institution.

The reported figures — 4,898 district deputies and 11,137 township deputies — closely mirror those from Beijing's previous cycle, suggesting administrative continuity rather than any expansion in the capital's local congresses.

It is worth noting that independent assessment of the elections' competitiveness should be treated as interpretive rather than factual. In China's single-party framework, candidate lists are vetted by Party structures before voting, and foreign observers and critics frequently describe the process as non-competitive in the multi-party sense — a characterization distinct from the official framing that the report advances. All substantive details of the vote derive from Xinhua's account, which secondary outlets republished, corroborating rather than independently verifying the narrative.

## What's Next

The immediate next step in Beijing's cycle is the finalization of the list of elected deputies, followed by the formation of the Beijing municipal people's congress — which, in turn, participates in selecting deputies to the National People's Congress. Xinhua's report did not detail those subsequent stages.

For now, the November 6 vote served its dual function: a working day of local democracy across 13,054 polling stations, and a carefully staged affirmation, by the country's top leadership, of the institutional framework that underpins it.
