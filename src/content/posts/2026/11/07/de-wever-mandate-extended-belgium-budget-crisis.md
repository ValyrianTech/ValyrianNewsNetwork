# De Wever's Mandate Extended as Belgium Budget Crisis Drags On

King Philippe has refused Prime Minister Bart De Wever's resignation, granting the N-VA leader a 10-day extension to salvage a multi-annual federal budget, according to [RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/bart-de-wever-prolonge-la-majorite-veut-y-croire-l-opposition-reclame-un-changement-de-cap-11811785). The reprieve, announced on Friday, November 6, 2026, hands De Wever until November 16 to report back to the palace, as the five-party "Arizona" coalition insists it wants to "get back to work" while the opposition demands a change of course.

## A Third Resignation, A Temporary Reprieve

The decision marks the third time De Wever has tendered his resignation to the King, reflecting two and a half years of political instability. The Prime Minister offered to step down on Monday, November 2, after the coalition failed to agree on a budget during an overnight inner-cabinet session. Rather than accept the resignation outright, the King held it in suspense and consulted the five majority party presidents before formally refusing it.

The goal now is a budget through 2029 requiring roughly €10 billion in savings, as [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/06/de-wever-begroting-koning-ontslag/) reported. De Wever has made clear he will only remain Prime Minister with a multi-year plan. "Otherwise it makes no sense," he told Parliament, warning that he wants to lead only a government willing to make "difficult choices" and end "the bad management of the past."

## The Emergency Budget Problem

Because no full 2026 budget has been passed, Belgium is operating under "provisional twelfths" — an emergency mechanism allowing the state to spend one-twelfth of the previous year's expenditure each month to pay pensions and civil-servant salaries. De Wever has called the situation an "absolute failure," noting his government requires an emergency budget for the second consecutive year.

Political scientist Bart Maddens argued the November 16 date is needed "to keep the pressure on," because "due to the provisional twelfths, the natural deadline disappears." The system is typically activated for three months, removing the built-in incentive to reach a deal.

## Coalition Wants Momentum, Opposition Wants Change

On the majority side, MR president Georges-Louis Bouchez said everyone had "been able to position themselves on the fact that it was indispensable to succeed," calling early elections "completely irresponsible given Belgium's budget situation." He stressed that with €348 billion in public spending, finding €10 billion is "not very reasonable" to avoid.

"I am in the starting blocks to restart the negotiation meetings as soon as the Prime Minister summons us," said Les Engagés deputy PM Maxime Prévot, insisting the country's economic and social situation "requires a fair and credible budget for the future as soon as possible."

To break the deadlock, De Wever introduced a "+1" negotiation formula: from Monday he will meet all vice-premiers, each permitted to bring one additional person, allowing party presidents to join the table if they wish.

The opposition was scathing. On X, PS president Paul Magnette framed the 10 days as a final chance for the government to "change course entirely," describing the coalition as "a bunch of kids in a playground arguing and forced to go to the teacher to settle their conflict" — giving "a disastrous image of politics." He estimated the absence of a timely budget had already cost the country roughly €1 billion. PTB's Sofie Merckx said De Wever must "listen to workers, young people and the social movement," while DéFI's Sophie Rohonyi said it took the King to "tell off" the Arizona party presidents so they would do their job.

## What's Next

The same flashpoints remain unresolved: further healthcare savings, the extension of the securities tax, a possible "index jump" limiting wage and benefit indexation, and tax benefits for top footballers. With no natural deadline left, the November 16 report will determine whether De Wever secures a deal, tenders a fourth resignation, or Belgium drifts further into emergency budgeting — and whether voters will eventually be asked to break the impasse.
