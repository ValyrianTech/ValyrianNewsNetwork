# China Braces for Snow, Rain and Sharp Cold as Winter Begins

A powerful cold air mass is sweeping across central and eastern China, bringing heavy snow to the north, torrential rain to the south and sharp temperature drops to much of the country, according to a forecast issued by the National Meteorological Center for November 7 to 9. The advisory coincided with *Lidong* (立冬), the traditional solar term marking the start of winter, and was accompanied by a national "Cold and Flu Index Map" warning that large areas face an "extremely high" risk of catching colds, [Sina News reported](https://news.sina.com.cn/c/2026-11-07/doc-inixwthe8291330.shtml), citing CCTV.

## A Collision of Cold and Warm Air

Meteorologists describe the event as a textbook clash of air masses. As cold air pushes south from the weekend, warm, moist air strengthens and surges north, and the two collide violently to produce widespread rain and snow from North China down to South China.

A second branch of cold air travels a more convoluted path: it crosses Northeast China, passes over the Yellow Sea and then loops back into North China. Picking up moisture over the ocean before being lifted by the terrain of northern North China, this pattern is described as the most typical setup for winter snowfall in the region.

According to the [CCTV forecast](https://content-static.cctvnews.cctv.com/snow-book/index.html?item_id=18297171539996357740&channelId=1119), temperatures across eastern Northwest China, central-western Inner Mongolia, North China, the Huang-Huai region and most areas to the south will fall by 4°C to 6°C, with some places dropping more than 8°C.

## Heavy Snow in the North, Torrential Rain in the South

The peak of the snowfall is expected from Saturday night into Sunday morning, with widespread heavy to blizzard-level snow across northern Shanxi, northern Hebei, northern Beijing and southwestern Liaoning, and localized severe blizzards. By Sunday morning, snow depth could reach 5 to 10 centimeters, and more than 15 centimeters in isolated areas.

Forecasters warned that because moisture is abundant and temperatures are not especially low, the snow will be predominantly wet and heavy. Facility agriculture and temporary structures have been urged to clear snow promptly to prevent collapse.

In the south, the convergence zone stretching from Guizhou to the middle and lower reaches of the Yangtze River will see its heaviest rainfall over the same window, with heavy to torrential rain that, in some places, will be unusually intense for this time of year.

## Cold Pushes Southward

From this weekend into early next week, many places are expected to set new lows for the second half of 2026. The 0°C line will push south to southern North China, while the 10°C line will press down to northern South China.

The cold spell will also accelerate the seasonal transition. Normally at *Lidong*, winter's leading edge reaches northern Huang-Huai and autumn arrives along the South China coast. This year, however, the shift has been notably slow. After this event, more of North China is expected to enter meteorological winter, and many parts of South China will experience genuine autumn cool for the first time this season.

## Mapping the Cold Risk

The Cold and Flu Index Map, produced by China Weather Network, classifies regions by their risk of weather-related illness. According to the forecast carried by Sina News, on November 7 most of Inner Mongolia, Qinghai, Heilongjiang and Liaoning were forecast to see maximum temperatures below freezing, while Beijing, Tianjin, Shanxi and southern Shaanxi face pronounced wind-chill effects. All of these areas were designated "extremely high cold risk" zones, with authorities urging strengthened cold-weather protection.

China Weather Network routinely issues such indices, linking meteorological forecasts to public-health messaging and highlighting vulnerable groups such as the elderly, children, pregnant women and people with cardiovascular or cerebrovascular conditions.

## What to Watch

The immediate concern is the combination of heavy wet snow and strong winds, which raises the risk of structural collapses and transport disruption across northern China, alongside flooding and travel hazards in the south. Beyond the weekend, attention turns to how far the cold penetrates and whether the delayed seasonal transition brings an unusually abrupt onset of winter across the country.

For now, the message from forecasters is simple: the season has turned, and much of China should prepare accordingly.
