# LeTec Strike to Halt Half of Wallonia's Bus Lines

Private bus drivers working for Wallonia's public transport operator LeTec are set to strike on Monday, in action the operator warns could disrupt around half of the region's regular bus lines and halt school transport entirely. The movement, led by the Christian trade union CSC Services publics, threatens to leave commuters and parents scrambling for alternatives across much of French-speaking Belgium.

According to [RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/une-greve-des-chauffeurs-prives-de-letec-pourrait-paralyser-lundi-la-moitie-des-lignes-classiques-et-la-totalite-du-ramassage-scolaire-11811718), close to half of LeTec's transport offer is at risk on Monday. Because school transport is entirely operated by these private drivers, the entire school bus service could be interrupted. More than 57 private depots are expected to be affected by the action.

## Who is striking, and why

The dispute exposes a structural fault line in Wallonia's transport network: roughly 49 per cent of LeTec drivers are employed by private subcontractors rather than the operator itself. Those drivers run part of the regular lines as well as the school transport services, giving their walkout outsized impact.

The CSC is advancing four principal pay-and-conditions demands. It wants the meal-voucher value raised to eight euros; reimbursement of home-to-work travel while maintaining the free-travel benefit; parking and standing time paid at 100 per cent of the hourly wage; and a bonus for drivers of articulated, or bendy, vehicles.

Beyond pay, the union is pressing for a so-called "social clause." As [La Libre Belgique](https://www.lalibre.be/dernieres-depeches/2026/10/22/les-chauffeurs-prives-travaillant-pour-letec-en-greve-le-9-novembre-et-le-4-decembre-EFGCVMCKSZEDBOSXC6WWI4HAM4/) reported, the CSC wants the Walloon government to write this clause into its own contract specifications and impose it on every operator that wins LeTec contracts. The aim is to stop private firms from underbidding competitors by offering lower wage costs—a dynamic the union says is creating a two-tier workforce.

The demands are addressed to Walloon Mobility Minister François Desquesnes of Les Engagés and to the OTW, the Opérateur de Transport de Wallonie, which oversees LeTec.

## A warning from the operator

LeTec has acknowledged the scale of the potential disruption. In the Liège-Verviers zone alone, 92 of 209 lines are operated wholly or partly by private transporters, and the operator has cautioned that depot blockades could sharply curtail service.

"The movement is taking hold. It's the beginning of a change," said Karl Di Stefano, permanent secretary of CSC Services publics Transports.

LeTec is advising passengers to check letec.be or its app as close to their departure time as possible, since the precise impact will depend on participation levels the operator says it cannot predict in advance.

## A second front in December

The action does not end on Monday. A second day of protest is planned for Friday 4 December, when drivers will again strike and the union will hold a demonstration outside the Walloon Parliament in Namur. The CSC has made clear it will escalate if its demands go unmet.

"If these two actions aren't enough, we'll carry out more until an agreement is reached," the union warned.

## A network under repeated strain

The private-drivers' action is the latest flashpoint in a turbulent year for LeTec. The network has faced repeated strikes in 2026—including a week-long walkout in late January over Walloon government savings measures, a two-day stoppage in late August over the scrapping of a €100 annual eco-voucher, and protests that saw union activists gather outside Minister Desquesnes' home in February.

Those earlier disputes were rooted in a savings trajectory of roughly €20 million by 2029 set out in the 2024–2028 public service contract governing LeTec. The current action is distinct: it is led specifically on behalf of private-sector drivers and centres on pay, benefits and the social clause rather than the broader anti-austerity fight.

## What to watch

The immediate test is Monday's participation rate—and whether depot blockades materialise as announced. Beyond that, the central question is whether the Walloon government and the OTW will open negotiations on the social clause, which would mark a significant shift in how the region contracts out its public transport. For now, commuters and parents across Wallonia are being urged to plan ahead and verify their routes before travelling.
