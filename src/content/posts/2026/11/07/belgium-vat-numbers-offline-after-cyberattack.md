# Cyberattack Leaves Belgian VAT Numbers Stuck Offline

Thousands of Belgian entrepreneurs and self-employed workers are still waiting to activate their VAT numbers more than a week after a cyberattack struck the digital systems of the country's Federal Public Service for Finance (SPF Finances). According to [RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/les-numeros-tva-ne-peuvent-plus-etre-actives-en-ligne-en-raison-de-la-cyberattaque-11812188), new VAT numbers can no longer be activated online — a service that is indispensable for businesses that must issue invoices and make purchases.

## What happened

The intrusion was detected in the night of Tuesday 27 to Wednesday 28 October 2026, when multiple attempts to modify the digital environment were flagged. As a precaution, the administration isolated several core platforms, including MyMinfin, Tax-on-web and Biztax, [RTBF reported](https://www.rtbf.be/article/les-services-numeriques-du-service-public-federal-finances-touches-par-une-cyberattaque-11807164). Spokesperson Francis Adyns said the intrusion was spotted within a few hours.

Some services have since been restored. MyMinfin and Tax-on-web are accessible again, [according to RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/les-sites-internet-du-spf-finances-myminfin-tax-on-web-et-biztax-sont-a-nouveau-accessibles-11808686). But other digital tools remain unavailable, most notably the online activation, modification and cessation of VAT numbers.

## A manual workaround, and a growing backlog

To keep businesses moving, the SPF Finances has set up an emergency procedure: requests can be sent by email and are processed through a manual activation. The catch is that the workaround is far slower than the usual online route.

The backlog is substantial. Business service counters at Liantis and Xerius alone report thousands of pending dossiers. The SPF Finances itself estimates that about 5,500 files are still awaiting an activation, modification or cessation of a VAT number, RTBF noted.

The stakes are direct for new businesses. In Belgium, a VAT number is the enterprise number prefixed with "BE", and online activation is the standard fast track for a company to become operational. A prolonged outage blocks new firms from invoicing and purchasing — effectively stalling their launch.

## No data breach, authorities say

The SPF Finances has moved to reassure the public that the attack did not compromise data. "The attack could not be sufficiently developed within our environment to make modifications or access data," the administration stated, as reported by [RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/le-spf-finances-rassure-il-n-y-a-pas-eu-de-fuite-de-donnees-mais-les-services-restent-inaccessibles-11807725).

Investigators are still working to identify the perpetrators. Belgium's federal police and the Centre for Cybersecurity Belgium (CCB) are both involved in the inquiry.

The disruption has also rippled into tax administration. The deadline for filing tax returns with special or specific income, initially set for 31 October, was extended to 16 November 2026 to accommodate affected taxpayers.

## Restoring trust, carefully

The SPF has stressed that the recovery cannot be rushed. Infected elements were isolated, and services must be brought back online in an isolated environment — a process that requires time to rule out any new risk. "The various services must be restored in an isolated environment. This takes time, with the necessary precautions to exclude any new risk," the administration said.

## A wider warning

The incident is the latest in a series of cyber troubles for Belgian institutions, and it underscores a systemic vulnerability at the heart of the country's public digital infrastructure. RTBF framed the episode as "the latest sign of the vulnerability of public digital services to cyber threats."

It also lands amid heightened warnings from Belgium's own cyber defenders. Around the same period, the head of the country's Cyber Force cautioned that "a single flaw can be enough" for a successful attack — a reminder that even well-defended systems depend on flawless execution across every layer.

## What to watch

Several questions remain open. The SPF has not given a firm date for re-enabling online VAT activation, and it is unclear how long it will take to clear the roughly 5,500-file backlog through manual processing — delays that could persist for weeks. No attribution for the attack has been made public, and the motivation — whether ransomware, hacktivism or a state actor — remains unconfirmed.

For now, Belgian entrepreneurs face a slow, paper-and-email path to becoming operational, and a broader question lingers: how quickly can the country's tax systems be made both accessible and secure.
