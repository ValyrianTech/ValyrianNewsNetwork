# Tesla to Wind Down Cybertruck Production by Mid-2027

Tesla plans to wind down production of its Cybertruck at its Texas gigafactory around the middle of next year, CEO Elon Musk said on Friday, November 6, 2026, marking a retreat from one of the company's most high-profile and controversial vehicle launches after years of persistently weak sales.

Musk announced the decision in a post on X, saying the move would free up manufacturing capacity to "make room for increased Cybercab production" — the two-seat robotaxi that has become central to Tesla's autonomy ambitions, according to [Reuters, via NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/tesla-wind-cybertruck-production-years-poor-sales-markets-rcna607817).

## A Launch That Never Met Its Promise

The Cybertruck was unveiled in 2019 and deliveries only began in late 2023, delayed by design finalization, stainless-steel body panel production challenges and the pandemic. At the reveal, Musk promised a sub-$40,000 price tag for the cheapest version and up to 500 miles of range for the top-end model. Neither materialized.

The sales figures tell the story. Tesla sold just 20,237 Cybertrucks in the U.S. in 2025, down 48% from 38,965 in 2024, figures from Kelley Blue Book cited by Reuters show. Sales fell a further 38% through September 2026, according to a [Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/11/06/cybertruck-s-days-are-numbered-here-s-what-tesla-has-planned/) analysis.

Before launch, Musk predicted annual sales of 250,000 units, and Giga Austin was built with capacity for roughly that figure. The company was selling about one-tenth that amount. Cox Automotive's Kelley Blue Book estimated total Cybertruck sales at only around 71,000 units since the vehicle went on sale.

Musk had once boasted that demand was "so far off the hook, you can't even see the hook," and Tesla claimed over 1 million preorders — a conversion to actual sales that never materialized. In his announcement, Musk still called the Cybertruck the "best vehicle Tesla has ever made" and described the wind-down as the "end of an era," as [BBC News](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cq1j33w7d232o) reported.

## Design and Market Limits

The vehicle's sharp-edged stainless-steel design proved a liability in key markets. The Cybertruck's angular profile made European regulatory approval impossible due to pedestrian impact rules, limiting it to "just a handful of markets," the BBC noted. The vehicle has also faced multiple recalls and quality issues.

Andy Palmer, former CEO of Aston Martin, said the decision was overdue. "It doesn't make sense to keep it going when it's not meeting its targets. Every rational auto guy would say the same," he said, adding that it "will no doubt only be a rounding error on Tesla's balance sheet, but the statistics speak for themselves." On the European market, Palmer was blunt: "Anywhere where you have crash regulation that takes into account pedestrian impact, that Cybertruck won't go, can't go."

The Cybertruck's struggles are part of a broader slump in electric pickups in the U.S., where Ford's electric pickup and others have also underperformed. "All of them lost the competition because electric pick ups aren't popular in the US," said automotive analyst Felipe Munoz.

## A Pivot Away From Cars

The wind-down reflects Musk's push to reposition Tesla from an automaker into an AI, robotics and autonomy company. The Cybercab — which has no steering wheel, pedals or mirrors — is central to a planned global robotaxi network. Tesla began offering paid rides in a small fleet of Cybercabs in Austin in September 2026, though it still must answer the U.S. auto safety agency's questions on whether it has properly self-certified the vehicle to comply with federal motor vehicle safety standards.

Wes Morrill, the Cybertruck's lead engineer, framed the truck's legacy in those terms on X: "Cybertruck was an incredible technology development platform and lives on through Cybercab."

The Cybertruck joins the Model S sedan and Model X SUV in being discontinued, with that Fremont factory space repurposed for Optimus robot lines. The pattern raises questions about the future of Tesla's remaining consumer EVs if the company's central bet is on technology rather than cars.

## What's Next

It remains unclear whether Cybertruck production will be moved to another facility or phased out entirely. The Austin plant is the only known Tesla facility equipped to manufacture the model's specialized components.

After the Cybertruck exits, Rivian's R1T and GM's Chevrolet Silverado and GMC Sierra EV will be the main EV pickup options, with Ford set to release its Fathom compact EV pickup and startup Slate — backed by Jeff Bezos — planning a roughly $25,000 bare-bones pickup.

For Tesla, the coming months will test whether the Cybercab can deliver the volume and regulatory clearance that the Cybertruck never achieved — and whether investors accept a carmaker that increasingly looks like a robotics and autonomy company.
