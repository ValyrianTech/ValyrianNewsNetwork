# Four Injured in Fog-Linked A19 Chain Collision in Zonnebeke

Four people were injured late on Friday night when three cars were involved in a chain collision on the A19 motorway near Zonnebeke in West Flanders, with sudden fog suspected of having sharply reduced visibility, [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/07/zonnebeke-kettingbotsing-gewonden-a19/) reported. None of the injuries were life-threatening.

## What Happened

The crash occurred at around 23:45 on Friday 6 November, on the A19 heading toward Ieper (Ypres), a few hundred metres before the Beselare exit in the municipality of Zonnebeke. The impact left one vehicle in the central median, while the other two came to a stop on the carriageway.

One driver was seriously trapped in the wreckage and had to be freed by firefighters, who removed the roof of the car to reach him safely. [HLN](https://www.hln.be/zonnebeke/vier-gewonden-bij-zware-kettingbotsing-op-a19-in-beselare-brandweer-verwijdert-dak-om-bestuurder-te-bevrijden~aa1a37c6/) quoted an officer at the scene explaining the decision: "We had to completely remove the roof of a car to safely free a driver who was trapped." The drivers of the other two cars and one passenger also had to be helped out of their vehicles, though they were not trapped.

All four injured people were taken to hospital by ambulance, accompanied by a medical emergency team (MUG). Police, several ambulances and the fire brigade attended the scene.

## Fog as a Suspected Factor

Investigators say sudden fog may have played a role in the pile-up. The condition is consistently described as a *possible* contributing factor rather than a confirmed cause, and the precise sequence of events remains under investigation.

The stretch of the A19 around Zonnebeke has a troubled history with fog-related crashes. In December 2013, a massive chain collision on the same motorway involved around 140 vehicles and prompted a large-scale emergency response, an event [commemorated a decade later](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2023/12/03/kettingnbotsing-herdacht-van-tien-jaar-geleden-op-de-a19-in-zonn/) by local authorities and survivors. Friday's incident, though far smaller, fits a recurring pattern of fast-changing autumn weather producing dangerous conditions on this route.

## Why Driver-Assistance Tech May Not Help

The collision also highlights a broader safety challenge. Modern driver-assistance systems rely on cameras and sensors that can perform poorly in dense fog, and drivers frequently misjudge following distance and speed when visibility drops abruptly. A [VRT NWS analysis](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/10/05/sensoren-auto-mist-kettingbotsing-gatrijden/) from October 2026 warned that such chain collisions remain difficult to prevent even with these technologies, echoing a similar pile-up in Kontich earlier that month.

## Disruption and Clean-Up

The A19 in the direction of Ieper was closed for about an hour to allow emergency response, towing and road cleaning. Traffic was diverted via the Wervik exit, and vehicles were later able to pass slowly along the hard shoulder, creating a long queue toward Ieper. Three cars were heavily damaged and debris was spread across the roadway before the scene was cleared. The first drivers were allowed past via the hard shoulder shortly after midnight, and the motorway was soon fully reopened.

## What to Watch

As fog season sets in across the Westhoek, the incident is a reminder of how quickly visibility can deteriorate on motorways. Investigators have not confirmed fog as the cause, and no further details on the victims have been released. Motorists are advised to reduce speed and increase following distance whenever conditions change rapidly.
