# After Maredsous Blast, Christmas Markets Tighten Gas Rules

A gas cylinder exploded in a chalet at the Maredsous Christmas market on Sunday, injuring five people and destroying two wooden stalls, and the accident is now forcing organisers and emergency services across Wallonia to rethink how gas is handled at the region's holiday markets. According to [RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/incendie-a-maredsous-en-pleins-preparatifs-les-marches-de-noel-redoublent-de-prudence-11816708), the fire broke out shortly before noon at what had been billed as the first Christmas market of Wallonia, which had opened only two days earlier, on Friday 13 November.

Three of the injured were initially reported in serious condition, two of them in "absolute emergency." On Monday, the Namur prosecutor's office gave an updated toll of three seriously injured, with the life of one victim still in danger.

## What Went Wrong

According to [RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/incendie-au-marche-de-noel-de-maredsous-trois-blesses-graves-et-deux-legers-11815837), the two seriously injured include a person airlifted to a hospital in Liège and another taken to the military hospital at Neder-Over-Heembeek. Two other people suffered lighter injuries. The mayor of Anhée, Pierre Rondiat, confirmed the market was evacuated and closed that Sunday in consultation with the organiser.

On Monday, the prosecutor's office confirmed the cause: a leak caused by a faulty or improper connection of a gas cylinder. Patrice Liétard of the Dinaphi emergency zone, quoted by [RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/explosion-au-marche-de-noel-de-maredsous-un-mauvais-branchement-de-bonbonne-de-gaz-a-l-origine-de-l-accident-11816175), explained that propane and butane are heavier than air and likely pooled at ground level before igniting and triggering the explosion. An expert has been appointed, and an investigation has been opened.

## The Ban Nobody Followed

Central to the inquiry is a troubling question: the gas cylinders were supposed to be forbidden in the market's chalets. Mayor Rondiat said a safety meeting was reportedly held on 3 September with organisers, firefighters and police, and that a formal record allegedly noted the ban, with the exception of the abbey's permanent installations. The presence of two or three bottles in the chalet that burned is now the focus of the investigation.

Whether the cylinders were inside or outside the chalet remains to be determined, and whether the organiser or operator will bear liability is unresolved. The case has also exposed a regulatory gap: prior inspection of market installations by firefighters is not, in principle, mandatory, leaving it to each mayor to decide whether such a check is organised.

## A Hero in the Flames

Amid the destruction, a story of courage has emerged. According to [La Libre](https://www.lalibre.be/belgique/societe/2026/11/16/il-a-mis-sa-vie-en-jeu-pour-sortir-ines-de-ce-brasier-le-frere-dune-des-victimes-de-lincendie-sur-le-marche-de-noel-de-maredsous-temoigne-FYX5ULFDVJBFVMZ54AFMH4UXSQ/), one of the seriously injured victims, Inès, was trapped inside the burning chalet. The person accompanying her, Costel, escaped but went back into the flames to pull her out.

"Costel a fait acte de courage, de bravoure et de sacrifice," her brother wrote in a social media testimony, describing how Costel risked his life to bring Inès out of the blaze. The two survivors were given first aid before being flown to specialist burn centres. Inès has burns covering half her body, required a skin graft and was placed in an artificial coma; her prognosis remains life-threatening, though her brother said her vital signs were stable.

## New Precautions for a Fragile Season

With the Huy Christmas market — "Plaisirs de Noël" — due to run from 20 November to 3 January, organisers and firefighters are now issuing detailed guidance. Colonel Stéphane Bouquette, commander of the Huy firefighters, told RTBF that the emphasis will be on how sites are laid out and, above all, on gas. Cylinders are to be secured in cages, hoses limited to a maximum length and checked for manufacturing dates and validity, and connections and stove proximity to walls verified.

"L'idée, c'est de montrer patte blanche à tout moment," Bouquette said, noting that the mayor's responsibility for preparing an event is transferred to the operator, who must keep installations in order at all times.

The accident has also sharpened scrutiny of a moment when public-space explosions are especially sensitive in Belgium; a separate, unrelated "criminal explosion" near Brussels was drawing headlines in the same news cycle.

## What to Watch

For now, Maredsous is closed as organisers mourn and investigators work. The market season, however, is only beginning, and the coming weeks will show whether the lessons of Sunday translate into a region-wide shift away from gas — toward electric heating and cooking — or merely tighter checklists for the cylinders that remain. For Inès and the other seriously injured, the immediate concern is medical. For thousands of market-goers, the first test comes in Huy, where six weeks of holiday events open later this week.
