# Bomb Attack Damages Apartment Building in Steenhuffel

A powerful bomb attack struck an apartment building in the centre of Steenhuffel, part of the municipality of Londerzeel in Flemish Brabant, in the pre-dawn hours of Tuesday, causing extensive material damage but leaving no one injured. Eight residents were evacuated as a precaution and a security perimeter that had cordoned off the village centre was later lifted as investigators took over the scene.

According to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/17/felle-explosie-in-dorpscentrum-steenhuffel/), the explosion occurred around 5 a.m. and was confirmed as a deliberate bombing by Londerzeel mayor Nadia Sminate (N-VA). "It was indeed a heavy explosion at around 5 am this morning... It is indeed a bomb attack," Sminate said. "Eight people were evacuated from the apartment building, and luckily no one was injured. The explosion was so violent that even interior doors flew off. This is very intense."

## What Happened

The blast blew out a garage door, the front door and several interior doors, shattered windows and damaged masonry. A fire broke out near the garage door shortly after the explosion. [De Morgen](https://www.demorgen.be/nieuws/explosief-gegooid-naar-appartementsgebouw-in-steenhuffel-8-bewoners-geevacueerd-geen-gewonden~bbd88d61/) reported that an explosive was thrown at the building, citing the mayor's account that the device was "fairly heavy, which caused considerable damage to the building."

Commissioner Franky Maes of the KLM police zone (Kapelle-op-den-Bos, Londerzeel, Meise) confirmed that a perimeter was established and that the army's DOVO bomb disposal unit, the forensic laboratory and the Halle-Vilvoorde public prosecutor's office were dispatched to the scene. The case has since been transferred to the Federal Judicial Police (FGP) of Halle-Vilvoorde, which is leading the investigation.

## A Fleeing Suspect

An anonymous neighbour told VRT NWS that he and his wife were woken by a loud bang at 5 a.m. "I looked through the window and saw a suspected perpetrator, who had been hanging around the neighbourhood, flee. He was wearing a helmet and was dressed in black." No arrest has been announced and authorities have not released an official suspect description. [RTL Info](https://www.rtl.be/actu/regions/flandre/vitres-brisees-quartier-boucle-cette-commune-flamande-proche-de-bruxelles/2026-11-17/article/806837) confirmed the criminal origin of the blast and the transfer of the file to the federal police.

The attack occurred a stone's throw from the De Huffeltjes kindergarten. Director Evelyne Dehertogh said all children had arrived as normal and that the municipality was keeping the school informed. "For now there is no great concern among the children, their parents and staff," she said. Police escorted pupils past the cordoned area during the morning.

## Echoes of Kortenberg

The Steenhuffel bombing drew immediate comparisons to a series of three explosions earlier in November in the Brouwerijstraat in Kortenberg, also in Flemish Brabant. In that case, a family home was targeted twice, with a third device detonating near a bakery further down the street. [VRT NWS reported](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/13/kortenberg-opnieuw-explosie-brouwerijstraat-bom/) that investigators believe the family was specifically targeted and that one blast may have hit the wrong house. Whether the Steenhuffel attack is linked "must still be determined by the investigation," VRT noted.

## Why It Matters

The attack fits a broader pattern of explosive violence in Belgium that has increasingly spread from major cities into smaller, quiet communities in Flemish Brabant, Antwerp and the Brussels periphery. Such bombings are frequently linked to narcotics trafficking—a phenomenon locally dubbed "drugsgeweld" or "narco-terror"—in which improvised devices are placed at front doors to intimidate rivals, debtors or their families. No motive has been confirmed in the Steenhuffel case.

Sminate acknowledged the unease among residents of the targeted street. "I understand that," she said, speaking both as mayor and as a mother, adding that police had assured her the situation was safe. She described the strike on a rural municipality as "very confronting," and pointed to changing demographics in recent years, saying "it is a losing battle"—a politically loaded remark likely to draw scrutiny.

## What's Next

Investigators now face several open questions: whether a specific resident was targeted and why, whether the attack connects to the Kortenberg series, and what forensic analysis of the device will reveal about its type and delivery. No suspects have been identified or arrested. For Londerzeel, the immediate priority is reassurance—restoring a sense of safety in a village more accustomed to quiet mornings than bomb squads.
