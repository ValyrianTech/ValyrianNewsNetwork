# Meghan Silent in Brussels as Harry Speaks Alone

Meghan, Duchess of Sussex, withdrew at the last minute from a planned joint address to the European Parliament in Brussels on Tuesday, leaving her husband Prince Harry to deliver the keynote alone, days after her father was charged in the United States with possessing child sexual abuse material. The Duchess accompanied Harry to the event but made no public comment, answering no questions as the couple — both dressed in black — walked past a large press pack.

The visit was the couple's first joint working trip to Europe since they moved back to live in Britain, and it had been carefully framed around children's digital safety. Instead, it was overshadowed by the personal scandal engulfing Meghan's 82-year-old father, Thomas Markle Sr.

## A keynote delivered solo

Harry and Meghan were invited by the European Parliament's Child Rights Intergroup. The centrepiece was the opening of the "Lost Screen Memorial," an art installation honouring children and young people whose deaths have been linked to online harm, featuring smartphone-shaped light-boxes displaying the lock-screen photographs and dates of death of 50 young people, as [The Brussels Times](https://www.brusselstimes.com/2401191/prince-harry-urges-eu-to-put-children-before-social-media-profit) reported.

The couple had been due to give a 15-to-20-minute joint address on children's digital safety, with Meghan slated to introduce her husband. A spokesperson confirmed the change: "The duchess is travelling with the duke to Brussels and will accompany him to the event but will not speak."

A source told The Telegraph, relayed by [LBC](https://www.lbc.co.uk/article/meghan-not-strong-enough-speech-fathers-arrest-5Hjdm9w_2/), that Meghan was "just not strong enough, emotionally, to be doing any public speaking right now" after her father's arrest.

In his address, Harry did not reference his father-in-law. He instead pressed the case for regulation, tying the memorial to the EU's efforts to protect minors online, including the EU Kids Act. "How did we reach a point where we can build technology capable of extraordinary things, yet still struggle to make it safe for a child?" he said, according to [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/meghan-wife-prince-harry-will-no-longer-speak-brussels-event-2026-11-10/).

"I'm not here as an expert or a politician. I'm here as a father, my wife, as a mother and together, as a couple who stand alongside parents like Sandra, Megan and Sewell — in New York, Los Angeles, Geneva and now Brussels," he added.

He argued that safety must be built into platforms from the outset rather than addressed after harm occurs. "This whole mission has to be about safety by design. By the time things are on a phone, on an app, in a kid's hands, it is already too late," he said. "The majority of these companies don't let their children on social media because they know the dangers."

## The case against Thomas Markle

Thomas Markle Sr. was arrested by FBI agents on 6 November near Tampa, Florida, and charged with possession of child sexual abuse material. On 7 November, a court placed him under home detention with an electronic ankle tag in a Florida care home and ordered a psychiatric examination amid concerns about possible dementia and his ability to understand proceedings. If convicted, he faces up to 20 years in prison.

A parallel investigation is under way in the Philippines, where police reportedly found a large quantity of child abuse images during a search of his flat in Cebu. Markle has denied the accusations, calling them "sick lies."

Meghan has been estranged from her father since the run-up to her 2018 wedding. She says she was informed of allegations against him via a letter from the UK Metropolitan Police in 2019 and kept her children — Prince Archie, 7, and Princess Lilibet, 5 — away from him to protect them. In an earlier written statement, she called the alleged abuse "disgusting" and said she "fervently hoped justice would be done and that the protection of innocent children would remain paramount."

[VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/10/harry-en-meghan-europees-parlement/) reported that the Met has admitted it did not share the 2019 letter's details with other law enforcement agencies, and that the UK police watchdog, the Independent Office for Police Conduct, is investigating the force's conduct.

## Analysis: message discipline amid a personal storm

The Brussels trip demonstrates a deliberate communications strategy. Journalists were briefed in advance that the couple would not answer questions; questions were permitted from the audience at a later panel but not from the media; and Harry's speech stayed tightly on the policy message. That discipline served two purposes: it kept the spotlight on the children's-safety cause the couple has long championed, and it avoided any remark that could complicate an active US criminal investigation and the parallel UK inquiry.

The result was a double-edged outcome familiar to the Sussexes. The cause gained visibility from a European Parliament platform, but the spotlight inevitably drifted from the memorial's message to the family scandal.

## What's next

Several threads will determine how the story develops. The psychiatric assessment of Markle Sr. will shape whether he can be tried or interviewed. Investigators in the US and the Philippines continue their work, and the IOPC's findings on the Met's handling of the 2019 letter will carry institutional weight in Britain. On the policy front, the question is whether the EU Kids Act advances — and whether the Sussexes continue this advocacy circuit, which has already travelled from New York to Geneva and now Brussels.

For now, the lasting image is of a couple walking into the European Parliament in black, one of them silent, the other making the case on behalf of parents whose children never came home.
