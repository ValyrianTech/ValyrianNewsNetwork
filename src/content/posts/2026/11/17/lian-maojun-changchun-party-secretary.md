# Lian Maojun Named Changchun Party Secretary

Lian Maojun, an alternate member of the Chinese Communist Party's 20th Central Committee, has been appointed party secretary of Changchun, the capital of northeastern China's Jilin province, according to [Caixin](https://china.caixin.com/2026-11-17/102505599.html). The transfer, announced on November 16, 2026, moves a sitting member of the party's elite from a senior post in Tianjin back to the Northeast region where his career began.

The appointment was confirmed at a meeting of Changchun leadership cadres attended by Jilin Party Secretary Huang Qiang, who delivered a speech. Li Mingxiang, head of the Jilin provincial Organization Department, read out the Central Committee's decision. The Central Committee approved Lian's appointment as a Jilin Provincial Party Committee Standing Committee member and Changchun Party Secretary, while removing Zhang Enhui from the Changchun post.

## A Return to the Northeast

For Lian, the move represents a homecoming of sorts. As Caixin reported, Lian served for many years in Liaoning's provincial capital of Shenyang before being promoted and transferred to Tianjin in 2019, where he has governed the Binhai New Area for six and a half years. His career began in 1993 at China Northern Airlines after graduating from Liaoning Commercial College, and he subsequently held a series of roles across Shenyang's development zones and districts, including Yuhong District chief in 2011, Shenyang municipal government secretary-general in 2013, and a seat on the Shenyang Party Standing Committee in 2016.

In November 2019, Lian was promoted to deputy mayor of Tianjin at vice-ministerial rank. In April 2020, he became a Tianjin Party Standing Committee member, Binhai New Area Party Secretary, and director of the Tianjin Free Trade Zone management committee. Born in November 1970 in Chaoyang, Liaoning, Lian holds a master's degree in management and a doctorate in economics, and is a senior economist and senior political engineer.

His predecessor, Zhang Enhui, departed the Changchun post as he approached age 60 — a milestone that reflects the informal age norms governing the rotation of leading cadres at this level. Caixin noted that the Changchun top post was adjusted after a gap of three and a half years, meaning Zhang had served roughly that tenure.

## The Revitalization Mandate

At the November 16 meeting, Huang framed the adjustment in the standard language of party personnel management. According to Caixin, Huang said the change was decided by the Party Central Committee with Xi Jinping at its core, proceeding from the overall situation of Jilin's economic and social development and aimed at strengthening the provincial and municipal leadership teams after comprehensive consideration and careful study.

Lian, in turn, pledged to resolutely implement the Northeast Full Revitalization strategy deployment, accelerate the building of the Changchun modern metropolitan area, and promote high-quality development.

The Northeast Full Revitalization is a longstanding national strategy to revive China's industrial heartland across Heilongjiang, Jilin, and Liaoning — a region that was once the backbone of the country's heavy industry but has struggled with slower growth and economic restructuring. Changchun itself is a major automotive hub, home to the FAW Group. Lian's stated mission ties his new role directly to that revitalization agenda and to the integration of the Changchun metropolitan area.

His experience in Binhai carries potential relevance for that task. As one of China's major national-level new areas and free-trade pilot zones, Binhai has given Lian six and a half years of exposure to export- and manufacturing-oriented development policy — experience that may be intended to inform Jilin's efforts at industrial upgrading. Whether the appointment signals a specific policy emphasis on autos, advanced manufacturing, or metropolitan-area integration remains an open question, as Caixin's full analysis is behind a paywall.

## What to Watch

The appointment is one of several personnel rotations across China's provincial and municipal leadership in 2026, part of a broader pattern of cadre reshuffles that Caixin tracks on its personnel-watch beat. Lian's elevation to lead a sub-provincial-level capital city, while retaining his standing as an alternate Central Committee member, places him in a significant regional post at a time when the Northeast's economic fortunes remain a priority for Beijing.

Two things bear watching. First, Lian's departure leaves his post as Tianjin Binhai New Area Party Secretary vacant — a successor will signal where Tianjin's own development priorities lie. Second, the coming months will show whether Lian translates his Binhai experience into concrete moves on Changchun's industrial base and metropolitan planning, or whether the appointment is primarily a leadership consolidation. For a region long defined by its industrial past, the answer will matter well beyond Jilin's borders.
