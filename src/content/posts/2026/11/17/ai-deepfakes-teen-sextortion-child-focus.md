# AI Deepfakes: A New Weapon in Teen Sextortion

Artificial intelligence has become the latest tool in sextortion schemes targeting very young teenagers, Belgian child-protection organisation Child Focus warned ahead of the European Day for the Protection of Children against Sexual Exploitation on 18 November. In an investigation published by [RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/l-intelligence-artificielle-nouvelle-arme-de-sextorsion-pour-les-predateurs-aupres-de-tres-jeunes-ados-11815234), the organisation reported that predators are using AI-generated video to impersonate peers, gaining victims' trust before blackmailing them — and that financial-sextortion reports have more than doubled in a year.

## A Trust Signal That No Longer Holds

Sextortion — a contraction of "sexual" and "extortion" — is blackmail based on an intimate image, typically sent by a teenager who believes they are talking to another teenager. The blackmail may aim at obtaining more intimate images or at extracting money, the latter known as financial sextortion.

What is new is how easily criminals can now fake the identity of the person behind the screen. AI video-generation tools have become affordable enough — RTBF tested one available for a subscription of around 60 euros a month — to swap a real person's face onto another body while preserving gestures, facial expressions and lip movement. RTBF described the results as extremely realistic, meaning that even a live video call is no longer a reliable sign that the person on the other end is who they claim to be.

That is exactly the scenario described by "Eline", a listener on Child Focus's 116 000 helpline. "We had a case where a young person asked the person he was chatting with to call by video," she told RTBF. "He saw a young girl, and that gave him more confidence in the relationship. He sent her intimate photos. They were then used for sextortion."

## A Veritable Explosion in Cases

Child Focus is launching its annual awareness campaign around the problem this year because the numbers have surged. Spokesperson Selyna Ayuso Ferrandiz told RTBF that in general "perpetrators increasingly use AI to impersonate someone else", and that the organisation is seeing a huge increase in sextortion cases, especially financial sextortion.

"This year we are already at 550 financial-sextortion cases," she said. "Last year at the same period we were at 218. We are truly seeing an explosion in reports."

Annual figures confirm the trend. According to the Child Focus annual report for 2025, covered by [DH/Les Sports+](https://www.dhnet.be/actu/belgique/2026/05/19/grooming-sextorsion-sexting-sans-consentement-le-nombre-de-victimes-dexploitation-sexuelle-en-ligne-a-explose-en-2025-OZ2JKPAVIJGKTKENEIR7JWIJOU/), the number of victims of non-consensual sexting, grooming and sextortion doubled in three years to 837, up from 401 in 2023 — a rise attributed notably to the arrival of AI. Sextortion alone grew 143%, with 433 new victims.

## Boys Aged 14 and 15 Are the Main Targets

Perhaps the most striking finding is who is being targeted. Boys aged 14 to 15 are now the principal victims, and they made up 92% of financial-sextortion victims, whose average age was 15. For the first time, boys are the largest group of online sexual-exploitation victims assisted by Child Focus.

Child Focus offered possible explanations without assigning blame to victims. "There are different possible explanations. We haven't done studies, but we think boys are more apt to take risks, to be more impulsive, and perhaps they are looking a little more for validation from girls," the organisation told RTBF. Helpline staff, it said, tell callers first of all that "it is not your fault".

The refusal to blame victims matters because shame is part of the predator's toolkit. According to anti-scam volunteer Cedric Glineur, who runs the site sextorsions.com as a member of the association "Arnaque-moi si tu peux", criminals use loaded keywords to frighten their targets — "pervert", "nude", anything that can provoke shame or fear.

## When AI Complicates the Investigation

There is an important nuance in the debate over AI's role. Glineur, who helps victims have extorted images taken down, believes AI is not the main factor in most cases. Scammers often steal photos from real accounts or from platforms such as OnlyFans. Of AI, he told RTBF: "They don't need it."

But when AI is used, it makes investigators' work harder. The commissioner in charge of the federal police's Child Abuse cell told RTBF that identifying a suspect from an image is more difficult "since the physical appearance has been altered using artificial intelligence". Police also report more paedocriminal content online.

The advice for victims is consistent and urgent: do not pay. "You pay once, you think you will be rid of it, but no," Glineur warned. "If you have paid, why would [the scammer] let it go? He will ask for more." Child Focus advises taking screenshots, cutting contact, telling a trusted adult, calling 116 000, and filing a complaint with police.

## What to Watch For

Child Focus is pressing for strict enforcement of the EU Digital Services Act, which obliges major platforms such as Meta, TikTok and Google to protect children, and it supports a permanent legislative framework — the "Child Sexual Abuse" regulation — that is still under negotiation. The broader international picture reinforces the urgency: US reporting bodies have logged tens of thousands of financially motivated sextortion cases annually, with growing numbers involving minors.

For now, the barrier to a convincing identity fraud has collapsed, and the consequences are landing hardest on children barely into their teens. As one content creator quoted by RTBF argued, it has never been so easy to use the face of a teen or a child for the benefit of criminals — a warning that even a live video call can now be part of the deception.
