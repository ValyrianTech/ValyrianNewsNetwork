# Bitcoin Slips Below $70,000 as Strong Dollar Bites

Bitcoin has fallen below the $70,000 mark (roughly €63,200) for the first time since August, while Ethereum — the second-largest cryptocurrency — dropped under $2,000 (about €1,800). The slide, reported by [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/13/waarde-van-bitcoin-duikt-onder-kaap-van-70-000-dollar/), reflects a broader flight from risk assets as a strengthening US dollar and elevated interest rates make non-yielding investments less attractive.

## Why investors are retreating

The immediate trigger is economic uncertainty. Investors are seeking less risky investments, and the crypto exchanges are showing it, the broadcaster noted, as money rotates toward assets perceived as safer. The move is not confined to digital currencies: it is one visible symptom of a wider risk-off mood that has gripped global markets through November.

A week before the crypto decline, stock markets worldwide suffered heavy losses after the Democratic victory in the US midterm elections. Tech stocks were hit hardest, on fears that the sector could face stricter regulation. According to [VRT NWS's coverage of the selloff](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/06/wat-moeten-beleggers-doen-na-beurscrash/), the technology-heavy Nasdaq closed 5.52% lower and South Korea's Kospi index, home to tech giants Samsung and SK Hynix, lost 5.3%. European markets were largely spared because they carry far fewer technology companies.

That political shock was compounded by a corporate one. Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang stepped down amid revelations about extramarital affairs, and the AI giant's stock shed roughly $1.9 trillion (about €1.7 trillion) in value over a month — a decline that rippled through the many indices and funds in which it is heavily weighted, as [VRT NWS detailed](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/12/nvidia-beurs-impact-etf-s-trackers-pensioenfondsen/).

## The dollar–bitcoin seesaw

The deeper, structural driver is monetary policy, and it explains why crypto in particular is feeling the pinch. US inflation keeps climbing, driven largely by high energy prices, and Tuesday's consumer price index came in higher than expected. That raises the odds the Federal Reserve will lift interest rates further to cool prices.

Higher rates make it attractive to hold dollars — for instance in US Treasury bonds, widely regarded as a safe haven. That surge in demand lifts the value of the US currency. The dollar index, which measures the greenback against six major currencies, has risen above 103 points, its highest level since April 2025.

In the current environment, the dollar and bitcoin move in opposite directions. Because bitcoin generates no yield, it loses appeal when risk-free government debt pays more. The result is a familiar chain: rising inflation leads to higher Fed rates, higher Treasury yields strengthen the dollar, and that strength drains demand for speculative, non-yielding assets such as cryptocurrencies.

## A lesson in correlation

The episode underscores how tightly crypto has become woven into the broader financial system. Bitcoin was once pitched as an uncorrelated asset — a hedge against both inflation and the banking system. This month's slide tells a different story: when volatility spikes and investors need liquidity or safety, digital tokens are sold alongside other risk assets rather than held as protection.

That is also why the crypto drop should be read as a signal rather than an isolated event. The same forces pushing bitcoin lower — a firmer dollar, climbing yields and nerves over tech valuations — sit behind the equity selloff, making the two markets symptoms of a single shift in the global appetite for risk.

There is also a question of how far the tech-driven anxiety can spread. Analysts cited by VRT NWS played down fears that the Nvidia crisis was a black swan capable of setting off a general market collapse. Erik Joly, chief economist at ABN AMRO MeesPierson, put the probability of such a chain reaction as small, while Tom Simonts of KBC argued the problems were person-specific rather than tied to the company itself. Joly nonetheless counselled caution, invoking the investor maxim never to catch a falling knife.

## What to watch next

For crypto holders, the near-term path hinges on two variables: the next US inflation readings and the Fed's willingness to keep tightening. Should price pressures ease and rate expectations soften, the dollar's pull could weaken and give bitcoin room to recover. Should inflation stay hot, the pressure on risk assets — crypto included — is likely to persist.

The broader lesson for investors is about diversification and patience. As market analyst Stefan Willems put it during the equity selloff, small investors should avoid panic: money intended for the long term should not be derailed by daily swings. In a month when a strong dollar, political upheaval and a leadership scandal have all conspired against risk takers, that counsel may prove as relevant to crypto as to stocks.
