# Fire Brigade: Check Christmas Lights Every Year

A fire caused by faulty Christmas lighting has left a home in Tessenderlo-Ham temporarily uninhabitable, and firefighters are using the incident to urge the public to inspect their festive lights annually. It is the second Christmas tree fire in a single week for the South-West Limburg emergency response zone.

According to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/17/woning-in-tessenderlo-onbewoonbaar-door-brand-aan-kerstboom/), the blaze broke out around 6:30 a.m. on 17 November in a house on the Schotermolenstraat. When firefighters arrived, a large plume of smoke hung inside the home. The residents were able to get out in time.

## What Happened

"When we entered the building, we saw that the Christmas tree had already burned out completely and was still smouldering a little," fire brigade commander Bert Ceyssens of the Hulpverleningszone Zuid-West Limburg told VRT NWS.

The damage was driven less by flame than by smoke. "A lot of black smoke was released and it spread through the entire home, because a number of doors stood open," Ceyssens said. "As a result, all walls, floors and ceilings were coloured black by a layer of dirty, greasy smoke."

Two residents — a mother and her son — were taken to hospital as a precaution for smoke inhalation. A dog that was in the house was quickly brought to safety. Because of the extent of the damage, the home is temporarily uninhabitable.

## A Pattern, Not an Accident

What makes the Tessenderlo-Ham fire notable is its timing. The previous week, an almost identical fire struck a home in Heusden-Zolder, also within the same emergency zone. Ceyssens was on the scene for both.

"She had only just set up her Christmas tree. She had placed both the tree and all the lighting together on a small table. That is where the fire started, and that home was temporarily uninhabitable," he said of the Heusden-Zolder case.

The Hulpverleningszone Zuid-West Limburg, the emergency response zone that responded to both fires, covers Tessenderlo, Heusden-Zolder, Beringen, Hasselt and several other Limburg municipalities — making the two-in-one-week sequence a local cluster the service felt compelled to address publicly.

## The Cause: The Electrics, Not the Tree

Firefighters stressed that the evergreen itself is not the ignition source. "The Christmas tree itself will never start burning on its own. The cause lies with an external source, namely electricity," Ceyssens said. "If those lights are broken, a short circuit can occur and that can cause fire. That can happen with both a real tree and an artificial tree."

The greatest risk sits in the transformer that regulates the alternating voltage. "The type of lighting plays no role; it has to do with the transformer," Ceyssens explained. "That is where the greatest heat development occurs, and that transformer is usually right next to the tree."

That detail matters: the fire risk does not depend on whether the tree is natural or artificial, or whether the bulbs are traditional or LED. It turns on the condition of the wiring and the transformer.

## The Safety Message

With more and more people putting up their Christmas tree earlier each year, the fire service wants the public to treat festive lighting as equipment that needs an annual inspection.

"We use Christmas lighting for at most two months a year. It is then enormously important to check the material anew every year," Ceyssens said. "Look carefully to see if nothing is broken, such as bare cables. If that is the case, throw it away."

He also advised against leaving the lights on around the clock. "That will ensure that the transformer is not hot all the time. That can provide cooling and thus also reduce the risk of fire." And if a fire does break out, he urged residents to keep interior doors closed, limiting smoke damage to the other rooms.

Belgian safety guidance corroborates the fire service's advice. [Gezondheid.be](https://www.gezondheid.be/artikel/veiligheid/tips-voor-veilige-kerstverlichting-12754) recommends inspecting cables and bulbs before use, replacing broken bulbs immediately because defective ones can overheat, and checking for CE marking and Dutch-language warnings. It notes that much of the Christmas lighting sold online has been found to be unsafe, and advises fully unrolling extension cords to prevent overheating.

## What to Watch

Mid-November is early in the festive season, but both fires show the danger is live well before the December peak. The open question is whether the cluster prompts a formal investigation or a broader seasonal prevention campaign by the emergency zone — and whether Belgian authorities will step up structured public messaging as more households string up their lights earlier each year.

For now, the fire service's message is simple and annual: check the lights before they go on the tree, and replace anything that looks worn.
