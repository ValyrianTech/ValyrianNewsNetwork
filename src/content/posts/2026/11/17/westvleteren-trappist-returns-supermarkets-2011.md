# Westvleteren Trappist Returns to Supermarkets Since 2011

Belgian supermarket chain Colruyt will begin selling Trappist Westvleteren beer on Wednesday 18 November, the first time the celebrated abbey brew has appeared on retail shelves since 2011. The beer will be offered not on its own but as part of a limited-edition gift box of six Trappist beers, priced at €19.99 and restricted to just 10,000 boxes across 204 stores.

The boxes, announced on Tuesday, contain Westmalle Tripel, Orval, Chimay Bleue, Rochefort 8, La Trappe Blond and Trappist Westvleteren Blond. It is a deliberately small-scale gesture: according to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/17/trappistenbier-west-vleteren-opnieuw-in-supermarkt/), the Colruyt sale represents only 0.4 percent of the Sint-Sixtus Abbey's total production.

## A rare appearance

Westvleteren is brewed by the monks of Sint-Sixtus Abbey in West Flanders and is normally sold exclusively through direct, on-site sales at the monastery itself. Its flagship Westvleteren XII has repeatedly been named the best beer in the world, a reputation that has fuelled chronic global demand and made any wider availability a notable event.

The only previous supermarket sale came in 2011, when Colruyt offered 93,000 "bouwsteenbox" packages of Westvleteren XII — each containing six bottles and two tasting glasses — to help finance the construction of the abbey's new monastery. Those boxes sold out in less than 48 hours, with long queues forming outside stores on launch day.

## Six abbeys, one box

This time, the collaboration has widened from a single brewery to six Trappist producers. In a [statement](https://press.colruytgroup.com/colruyt-verkoopt-10000-exclusieve-trappistenkistjes-met-onder-meer-trappist-westvleteren), the retailer framed the box as a celebration of Belgian brewing heritage. "Belgian beers are part of our cultural heritage," said Dieter De Brackeleer, beer buyer at Colruyt. "As a Belgian retailer we therefore offer our customers an extensive range of Belgian beers: no less than 95% of the beers on our shelves come from our own country, from both large and small producers."

For the abbey, the rationale is promotional rather than commercial. Alain Monteyne, general director of Sint-Sixtus, said the end-of-year campaign was intended to raise the profile of the Trappist tradition. "With the 10,000 Trappist boxes that will be sold, beer lovers across the country can get to know our Blond and five other Trappists," he said. "At the same time it remains an exclusive product, because only 0.4 percent of our total production is sold through Colruyt. We remain committed to direct sales at our abbey."

## Analysis: scarcity as strategy

The abbey's insistence on protecting its direct-sale model is central to what makes Westvleteren Westvleteren. The monks deliberately brew in modest volumes relative to commercial breweries, and the difficulty of obtaining the beer — historically by reservation and telephone at the monastery — is inseparable from its mystique. A mass-market rollout would risk diluting exactly the scarcity that drives its international reputation.

Seen in that light, the Colruyt box is a carefully calibrated compromise: enough presence to make a cultural statement and support a retail partner, but not enough to disturb a business model built on restraint. As [The Brussels Times](https://www.brusselstimes.com/2410074/colruyt-to-sell-rare-westvleteren-beer-as-part-of-trappist-gift-set) noted, this is only the second time Westvleteren beer has been sold in supermarkets in the abbey's modern history.

For Colruyt, the appeal is equally strategic. The promotion reinforces its positioning around Belgian, local producers and offers a premium, experience-driven product heading into the holiday season — a useful differentiator in a crowded grocery market. Bringing six Trappist breweries together also broadens the story beyond a single cult beer.

## What's next

The comparison with 2011 is unavoidable — and instructive. With only 10,000 boxes on offer this time — roughly a ninth of the 2011 volume — against even higher global awareness of the beer, demand is all but certain to outstrip supply. Long queues and rapid sellouts seem likely. Colruyt has not confirmed whether purchases will be capped per customer or require prior reservation.

Whatever the outcome on Wednesday, the sale is unlikely to signal a permanent return of Westvleteren to supermarket shelves. The abbey has been explicit that its future lies in the monastery shop, not the aisle — and that, for the monks, appears to be precisely the point.
