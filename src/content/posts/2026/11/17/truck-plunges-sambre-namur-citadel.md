# Truck Plunges Into Sambre at Foot of Namur Citadel

A heavy-goods truck left the road and plunged into the Sambre river at the foot of the Citadel of Namur on Tuesday morning, prompting a rescue and pollution-control operation. The driver, though injured, managed to free himself from the cab and get out of the water unaided, according to [RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/un-camion-est-tombe-dans-la-sambre-au-pied-de-la-citadelle-de-namur-le-chauffeur-a-pu-sortir-de-l-eau-11812576).

The accident occurred shortly before 10:00 a.m. local time on rue du Bord de l'Eau, the riverside road that runs directly along the Sambre beneath one of Belgium's largest and most visited fortresses.

## Two Others Cut Free From Cars

RTBF reported that the truck driver lost control of his vehicle at the foot of the Citadel before it entered the river. Two cars were also involved in the accident but did not go into the water. Firefighters had to extricate two people from those vehicles, cutting them free from the wreckage.

The severity of the driver's injuries was not immediately specified, and authorities have not yet stated what caused the loss of control.

## Fuel Spill Contained

A pollution-control boom was being installed across the river to recover diesel leaking from the truck's tank and limit contamination of the Sambre, a waterway sensitive for local ecology as it flows through Namur before joining the Meuse.

The presence of the boom signals a genuine risk of environmental damage, though the volume of fuel lost and whether any contamination has already occurred remain unknown.

## Questions Remain

The incident raises several open questions. It is not yet clear whether an investigation has been opened, nor whether speed, a mechanical failure, or a medical event contributed to the crash. The riverside curve beneath the Citadel leaves little margin for error, and vehicles that leave the roadway there are at risk of ending up in the water.

The scene is being treated as a local traffic and safety incident rather than a national event, and at the time of reporting the accident appeared to have been covered only by RTBF — typical for a fast-breaking, small-scale accident that has not yet been widely picked up.

Emergency services remained on site through the late morning, managing both the recovery operation and the fuel containment. Further details on the driver's condition, the occupants of the other vehicles, and the environmental impact of the spill are expected to emerge as the investigation unfolds.
