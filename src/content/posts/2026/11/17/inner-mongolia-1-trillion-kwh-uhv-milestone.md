# Inner Mongolia Hits 1 Trillion kWh UHV Power Milestone

Inner Mongolia has become the first province-level power-sending base in China to transmit more than 1 trillion kilowatt-hours (kWh) of electricity through ultra-high-voltage (UHV) lines, state media reported, a threshold that underscores the scale of Beijing's bet on long-distance, high-capacity transmission to shift energy from resource-rich regions to demand centers.

According to [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/fortune/20261116/80f3c63520b24429a1ea42ec91580693/c.html), cumulative UHV outbound transmission from the northern region reached 10,005 billion kWh (roughly 1.0005 trillion kWh) as of midnight on November 16, 2026, officially launching a single provincial sending base into what the agency called the "trillion-level era."

## What the Numbers Mean

The figure is difficult to visualize at human scale. Xinhua's companion [feature](https://www.news.cn/20261116/f812ebc2f9c94cd9a0800cf40d297888/c.html) likens 1 trillion kWh to roughly seven years of Beijing's total electricity consumption, or the full annual output of more than 10 Three Gorges hydropower stations running at capacity for a full year. Those analogies are the agency's own and are intended as illustration rather than precise accounting.

The trajectory behind the milestone is steep. Inner Mongolia's annual outbound transmission climbed from about 7.4 billion kWh in 2017 to 188.6 billion kWh in 2025 — a roughly 25-fold increase in eight years. The cumulative tally crossed 900 billion kWh in early May 2026 before passing the trillion mark seven months later, a pace that reflects sustained build-out rather than a one-off surge.

## A Backbone of Eight Channels

The milestone rests on physical infrastructure. State Grid Inner Mongolia East Electric Power Co. (Guowang Mengdong) has completed and put into operation eight UHV transmission channels — described as "five AC and three DC" — with total cross-provincial outbound capacity of 48.7 million kilowatts. That operation scale ranks among the largest in China, and the operator says the eight corridors carry roughly one-eighth of the country's entire cross-regional UHV transmission capacity.

Liu Jun, director of the Energy Strategy and Planning Research Institute at the State Grid Energy Research Institute, placed the achievement in national context. State Grid has now built and commissioned 44 UHV AC and DC projects, with cross-regional and cross-provincial transmission capacity exceeding 380 million kilowatts, he said. Inner Mongolia's eight major channels, he added, "support roughly a 'one-eighth' share" of that national capability.

## From Coal to Green

The milestone is also a marker of China's energy transition. Liu Haibo, deputy director of the development planning department at Guowang Mengdong, said clean energy accounts for more than 20% of the 1 trillion kWh transmitted from Inner Mongolia, while the under-construction Mengxi–Jingjinji channel is designed for a new-energy share exceeding 50%.

That nuance matters. Nationally, clean energy now makes up nearly 60% of the power carried on cross-regional UHV direct-current channels, so Inner Mongolia's cumulative total — shaped by its historically deep thermal-power base — remains more carbon-intensive than the national channel average. As of October 2026, the Mengdong grid and the Inner Mongolia UHV collection grid had a connected installed capacity of 110 million kilowatts, with renewables making up more than half and now standing as the largest single power source on the sending end.

The shift is framed by state media as a move from "transporting coal" to "transporting green." UHV, Xinhua wrote, is "the aorta" of China's West-to-East Power Transmission strategy, and bundling "wind, solar, thermal and storage" ensures that both green power gets through and that supply volumes remain stable.

## The Mengxi–Jingjinji Project

The next phase is already under construction. The Mengxi–Jingjinji ±800 kV UHV direct-current project — Inner Mongolia's first outbound channel tied to a "desert-gobi-wasteland" renewable mega-base — will deliver power from a 12-million-kilowatt base in the Kubuqi Desert to the North China load center. Approved in June 2025 and launched on December 30, 2025, it is the first UHV DC project to land in Hebei province and applies domestically developed UHV core equipment, using China's "double-800" hybrid DC technology for the first time.

## Why It Matters

China built the world's first commercial UHV AC project in 2009 and is now the only country mastering the full set of UHV technologies. The network forms the backbone of a national unified power market, connecting coal- and renewable-rich western and northern regions with demand centers in the east and south — a model other large economies have studied but not replicated at this scale.

Inner Mongolia is a natural sending node. It combines a thick thermal-power base with abundant wind and solar resources, and — straddling China's "Three Norths" region — sits within the economic transmission range of UHV links reaching North, East and Central China. That combination of volume and proximity underpins its outsized role.

## What to Watch

The "15th Five-Year Plan" targets set the direction. State Grid aims to commission 15 UHV direct-current projects, raise cross-provincial transmission capacity by 35%, and more than double flexible inter-regional power mutual-aid capacity — reflecting continued heavy investment in grid infrastructure.

For Inner Mongolia, the trillion-kWh benchmark is less a finish line than a staging post. The open question is how quickly the region's mix tilts toward the green side of the ledger, and whether newer channels deliver on their promise of majority renewable supply. For China, the milestone is a measure of how far its long-distance power strategy has come — and how much more it intends to build.
