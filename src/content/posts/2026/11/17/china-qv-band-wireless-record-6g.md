# China Sets Q/V Band Wireless Record, Eyes 6G Lead

Researchers in China have completed a verification of high-code-rate wireless communication in the Q/V frequency band, achieving two-way transmission rates of up to 27 Gbps and setting what the team describes as a new domestic record. The result, announced on November 17, is positioned as core technical groundwork for China's next-generation low-orbit, high-throughput satellite internet and its ambitions in the global race toward 6G.

## The Numbers Behind the Breakthrough

The experiment was led by the Aerospace Information Research Institute of the Chinese Academy of Sciences (AIR-CAS) and conducted at the Lijiang Station of the China Remote Sensing Satellite Ground Station. According to [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/tech/20261117/d6323540dccf4e2a976c48a762795956/c.html), a single channel using 512QAM modulation reached a maximum two-way transmission rate of 6.75 Gbps — 2.25 times China's existing same-band single-channel rate.

Combining dual-carrier and polarization multiplexing, the team pushed the two-way rate to 27 Gbps, a new high for the Q/V band in China. The researchers characterized the key metrics as "domestically leading and internationally advanced," and said they are now targeting two-way capability exceeding 50 Gbps.

## Why the Q/V Band Matters

The Q/V band occupies the millimeter-wave region and offers roughly 10 GHz of usable bandwidth — abundant spectrum well suited to high-speed satellite communications. But its millimeter-wave signals are sensitive to RF nonlinearity and phase noise, making high-quality, high-code-rate transmission technically demanding. Improving spectral efficiency and satellite-to-ground throughput has become a central research focus.

The team reported overcoming several hurdles, including a lightweight Q/V antenna, intelligent signal compensation, and phase-noise compensation. Tests followed a staged path — wired closed-loop testing, far-field wireless docking, and full-link performance assessment — relying on domestically developed antennas, power amplifiers, channel, and baseband equipment. Notably, the experiment marked China's first systematic test of 64QAM, 128QAM, 256QAM, and 512QAM modulation schemes for boosting Q/V two-way rates.

China News Service [reported](https://www.chinanews.com.cn/gn/2026/11-17/10733281.shtml) that the work was carried out jointly with Beijing Rongwei Technology, the 13th Research Institute of China Electronics Technology Group Corporation (CETC), and Nanjing Diange Communication Technology. The ground station is described as a national major science-and-technology infrastructure funded by the National Development and Reform Commission and China's first remote-sensing station network capable of receiving high-order modulated/demodulated signals.

## Analysis and Caveats

The achievement is significant on several fronts. It extends China's demonstrated expertise in high-order modulation — already established in the X and Ka bands — into a higher, more demanding frequency range directly relevant to very-high-throughput satellite systems. That positions the technology for the government-backed low-orbit constellations China has been accelerating, alongside its 6G research efforts.

Some framing deserves careful reading. The "internationally advanced" label is the researchers' own characterization rather than an independently benchmarked result; the 2.25-fold and 27 Gbps figures are comparisons against China's existing same-band performance, not against foreign systems. The experiment was also a ground-based platform simulating a satellite's on-orbit state rather than an actual in-orbit demonstration. No independent foreign wire-service coverage or peer-reviewed publication was located at the time of reporting.

## What's Next

The joint team said it will continue raising the technical indicators, aiming for two-way transmission beyond 50 Gbps. If achieved, the group argues, the technology would provide core support for designing and engineering China's next-generation low-orbit high-throughput satellite internet — and help the country stake out a commanding position in the global 6G competition. Whether the leap from simulated ground tests to deployed constellations follows on schedule is the question to watch.
