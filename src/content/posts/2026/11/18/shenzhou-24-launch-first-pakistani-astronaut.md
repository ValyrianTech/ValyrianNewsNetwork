# Shenzhou-24 Set to Launch with First Pakistani Astronaut

China is entering the final stretch of preparations for its Shenzhou-24 crewed spaceflight, after the spacecraft and its rocket were moved to the launch area at the Jiuquan Satellite Launch Center. The mission, expected to lift off in the coming days, will carry the first foreign astronaut ever to visit China's Tiangong space station, [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/tech/20261117/6051f1517f204adab2fece714a27cf01/c.html) reported.

The Shenzhou-24 spacecraft and its Long March 2F Y24 carrier rocket were transported to the pad on November 17, according to the China Manned Space Engineering Office. The agency said launch-site facilities and equipment are in good condition, with pre-launch functional checks and joint tests to follow as planned.

## A Milestone for China-Pakistan Cooperation

The headline element of the mission is the participation of a Pakistani astronaut, marking the first-ever joint mission of Chinese and foreign astronauts in the history of China's manned space program. "The Pakistani astronaut will be the first foreign astronaut to step into China's space station," Foreign Ministry spokesperson Guo Jiakun said, according to [Global Times](https://www.globaltimes.cn/page/202611/1373944.shtml).

Guo described the mission as "yet another key outcome of bilateral cooperation" as the two countries mark the 75th anniversary of diplomatic ties, and he framed it within Beijing's broader vision of building "a community with a shared future in the domain of outer space."

The arrangement traces back to February 2025, when the China Manned Space Agency and Pakistan's Space and Upper Atmosphere Research Commission signed a cooperation agreement on astronaut selection and training in Islamabad. After a three-stage selection and evaluation process, two Pakistani reserve astronauts were chosen; one will fly the mission while the other serves as backup, [Global Times reported](https://www.globaltimes.cn/page/202502/1329270.shtml) at the time.

The two candidates, Muhammad Zeeshan Ali and Khurram Daud, were congratulated by Pakistani Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif in April. Sharif called the mission "a matter of pride for the entire nation" and said he hoped it would "add new dimensions to the brotherly relationship between Pakistan and China."

## Mission Profile

According to [Wikipedia's mission summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhou_24), Shenzhou-24 is planned for launch on November 25, 2026, a date cited by external trackers and not yet confirmed officially. The flight will be the 18th crewed Chinese spaceflight and the 24th overall of the Shenzhou program, following Shenzhou-23, which launched in May 2026.

The spacecraft will carry three taikonauts and is expected to dock at the forward port of the Tianhe core module. The mission is planned to last about 180 days, with landing in Inner Mongolia set for May 2027. In keeping with Chinese convention, the crew — beyond the Pakistani payload specialist — is not expected to be announced until about a day before launch.

The Pakistani astronaut will make a short stay in orbit and is expected to return aboard the Shenzhou-23 spacecraft, rather than completing the full six-month expedition.

## A Mature and Maturing Program

The mission underscores the steady cadence China has built at Tiangong, which has been continuously inhabited since 2022 and hosts rotating crews on roughly half-year expeditions. It also highlights the program's operational resilience. In November 2025, after Shenzhou-20 was suspected of being struck by orbital debris, China launched Shenzhou-22 as an emergency response — a demonstration of its ability to maintain a continuous crewed presence and conduct rapid rescue operations.

Analysts have framed the Pakistani astronaut's participation as a high-visibility expression of the China-Pakistan "all-weather strategic cooperative partnership" and of Beijing's push to offer an alternative model of international space cooperation. As Wang Yanan, chief editor of Aerospace Knowledge magazine, told Global Times, allowing foreign astronauts to take part in the risky ascent and descent phases suggests that China's spacecraft and station are "sufficiently safe, comfortable, and reliable."

For Pakistan, the cooperation offers a route to human spaceflight without a domestic human-rated launch capability — what some coverage has called a "Chinese shortcut" to space.

## What to Watch

Shenzhou-24 sits alongside the rollout of China's next-generation Mengzhou crewed spacecraft and the planned Xuntian space telescope, marking a transition period for the country's crewed program. The immediate focus will be the official launch confirmation and the crew announcement in the days ahead. For now, the rocket is on the pad, and the mission that will bring the first foreign national aboard Tiangong is counting down.
