# China's Jewellery Sales Tumble as Consumers Tighten Purses

China's gold jewellery market is in the grip of a sharp downturn, with sales tumbling as cautious households pull back on discretionary purchases. Gold jewellery consumption fell 33.86 per cent year on year to 178.612 tonnes in the first nine months of 2026, according to [data from the China Gold Association](https://www.jwview.com/jingwei/html/11-04/695501.shtml) published on 4 November — a decline that lands as Beijing struggles to reignite household spending.

The collapse in adornment gold stands in stark contrast to a surge in investment demand. Sales of gold bars and coins climbed 26.26 per cent over the same period to 444.576 tonnes, a divergence the association described as a "significant structural divergence" driven by high gold prices and a new tax policy on the metal.

The figures underline how a market once viewed as a reliable bright spot for luxury demand has become one of the clearest casualties of China's consumption chill.

## A Consumer Pullback Written in Gold

The human face of the slowdown is captured by the [South China Morning Post](https://www.scmp.com/economy/china-economy/article/3374688/caution-grips-consumer-spending-chinas-jewellery-sales-tumble), which reported on 18 November that women like 46-year-old Tang Fang have stopped buying rings and bracelets altogether. Tang, a self-employed saleswoman with three sons, said she loves "bright, shiny things" but is now prioritising her children's education as business orders slow.

"I really wanted to, because I love bright, shiny things," she told the newspaper. "But I'm under a lot of financial pressure now. My two youngest sons are in primary and middle school, and I need to spend more on their education."

The hesitation is part of a wider pattern. October retail sales — a key gauge of consumer spending — rose just 0.4 per cent year on year, according to [National Bureau of Statistics data reported by the SCMP](https://www.scmp.com/economy/economic-indicators/article/3374531/chinas-economic-activity-falters-october-amid-sluggish-retail-sales). That missed a forecast of a 1.07 per cent gain and marked a slowdown from 1.1 per cent in September, while fixed-asset investment contracted further.

## The Gold Paradox

Counterintuitively, record-high and then sharply volatile gold prices have hurt rather than helped the jewellery trade. Gold surged to an all-time high of US$5,598.75 an ounce at the start of 2026 before a drawdown of nearly 30 per cent, settling at US$4,176.30 an ounce at the end of September. The [World Gold Council data cited by Kitco](https://www.kitco.com/news/article/2026-11-17/indias-gold-demand-boosted-lower-prices-october-jewelry-etfs-and-imports) put year-to-date gold prices down about 11 per cent in dollar terms by mid-November.

Those elevated and whipsawing prices have pushed Chinese buyers away from adornment and toward pure investment. As the China Gold Association noted, investment products are shifting from distribution to direct sales, while jewellery lines are being pushed toward higher value-added, fixed-price pieces.

Two structural forces are compounding the pain. Marriage registrations, a cornerstone of wedding-linked jewellery demand, have fallen for four consecutive years. And cheaper lab-grown diamonds are eating into the natural-diamond segment — a shift captured by a Reuters photograph of a customer trying on lab-grown pieces at a showroom in Henan, a major production hub, in September.

## Brands Feel the Squeeze

The downturn is now hitting the balance sheets of China's jewellery giants, and nowhere more visibly than at Lao Feng Xiang, a 178-year-old flagship brand. The company reported that its first-half revenue halved roughly 40 per cent to 19.987 billion yuan, a drop of about 13.4 billion yuan year on year, according to [a report by Sina Finance](https://t.cj.sina.com.cn/articles/view/2286037382/8842298601901j1x6).

The reason lies in its business model. Lao Feng Xiang operates a heavily franchise- and wholesale-dependent network in which franchise stores account for about 96 per cent of its outlets, leaving it exposed when retailers grow reluctant to restock. In the first half of 2026, the brand closed more than 540 franchise stores, over 60 per cent of them low-efficiency outlets in third- and fourth-tier cities.

Its margins are thin even in good times. The company's net margin sits at roughly 3.5 per cent, meaning it keeps just three-and-a-half yuan of profit on every 100 yuan of revenue — far below rivals such as Laopu Gold, whose gross margin is around 40 per cent.

The pain is industry-wide. Chow Tai Fook closed nearly 1,000 stores in its 2026 fiscal year, while Chow Tai Seng's revenue shrank by more than 20 per cent in the first half. The divergence between winners and losers is sharp: Laopu Gold's revenue grew more than 60 per cent over the same period, while Chow Sang Sang posted a 17 per cent gain.

## What's Next

The jewellery slump is a telling bellwether for the broader Chinese economy. When consumers as devoted as Tang hesitate over a gold bracelet, it signals a deeper caution that stimulus measures have so far struggled to dislodge.

Brands are responding by retrenching and repositioning. Lao Feng Xiang has pivoted toward premiumisation, launching "ancient-method" gold and celebrity-endorsed collections, while scaling back a planned foray into high-end luxury through a stake in Maybach Luxury Asia-Pacific. Others are leaning on livestreaming, e-commerce and expansion into Southeast Asia to offset a shrinking domestic market.

Whether that strategy can revive volumes depends largely on two variables: a sustained rebound in household confidence and a calmer gold price. For now, the shine has plainly come off China's jewellery counters — and the industry is bracing for a lean season ahead.
