# Storms Batter Belgium, Halting Brussels-Mons Rail Traffic

Heavy rain and strong wind gusts swept across Belgium on Tuesday and Wednesday, causing material damage from Brussels to Luxembourg province and disrupting rail and road travel. The headline impact came on the Brussels-Mons line, where a tree fell onto the tracks near Hal shortly before 07:30 on Wednesday, halting traffic until Infrabel teams cleared the obstruction around 09:30, [RTBF reported](https://www.rtbf.be/article/intemperies-en-belgique-rafales-de-vent-et-fortes-averses-causent-des-degats-le-trafic-des-trains-entre-bruxelles-mons-interrompu-11817190).

No injuries or fatalities were reported in the available coverage.

## A national yellow warning

The Royal Meteorological Institute (IRM/KMI) issued a yellow precipitation warning covering virtually the entire country except West Flanders, initially running to Wednesday midday before being extended to 15:00. Gusts reached about 60 km/h, occasionally near 65 km/h, from the south-west, according to [RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/la-belgique-en-code-jaune-pour-les-precipitations-jusqu-a-mercredi-midi-11816956).

IRM projected 24-hour rainfall totals exceeding 25 litres per square metre in several provinces, locally up to 40 l/m², with forecasts for the following days ranging from 35 to 70 mm—and locally 70 to 120 mm on the relief south of the Sambre-et-Meuse furrow. "Several zones of rain or showers will follow one another over Belgium until Friday inclusive," the institute said.

## Rail disruption—and a separate criminal closure

The Brussels-Mons line was cut after a tree came down between Braine-le-Comte and Hal. Infrabel said teams dispatched to the site finished their intervention around 09:30 and restored traffic, though residual delays remained possible. SNCB advised passengers they could travel between Mons and Brussels-Midi via Ath.

The weather was not the only cause of rail chaos. On the Ottignies-Charleroi-Central line, traffic was halted from about 07:00 between Charleroi-Central and Fleurus due to cable theft—a criminal act unrelated to the storms. No resumption forecast was immediately available, and SNCB planned replacement buses, advising passengers to route via Namur.

In Brussels, STIB tram lines 7, 10 and 35 were disrupted from Tuesday afternoon after an uprooted tree fell on overhead cables. The interruption was later limited to segments—between Heembeek and Docks Bruxsel for lines 7 and 10—with full resumption expected during Wednesday morning, [STIB told RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/intemperies-l-interruption-des-trams-7-10-et-35-a-bruxelles-durera-probablement-jusqu-a-mercredi-matin-11817018).

## Emergency services stretched across the regions

The Brussels fire service logged about 30 interventions since 17:00 on Tuesday—24 for flooding, five for fallen branches or uprooted trees, and one for an object blown by the wind. The federal Interior Ministry activated the 1722 non-emergency number nationwide to keep the 112 emergency line clear.

In Liège province, the fire zones of Liège and Huy recorded roughly 70 interventions between Tuesday evening and Wednesday morning, responding to flooded cellars and roads and fallen trees. In Namur province, the NAGE and Val de Sambre rescue zones handled about 40 and 30 interventions respectively, with Fosses-la-Ville and Jemeppe-sur-Sambre hardest hit. Storms briefly cut power in Bossière and Corroy-le-Château in the Gembloux commune.

On the roads, multiple accidents were reported in Wallonia, including on the E411 at Villance and the E42 at Couthuin. A fallen power cable closed the N4 at Martelange toward Luxembourg before being reopened, and an obstruction was reported on the E40 at Oreye. The Walloon traffic centre PEREX urged reduced speed and greater following distance because of aquaplaning risk, and barred trucks over 7.5 tonnes from overtaking on motorways.

## Watercourses under watch

Heavy rain put Walloon rivers under surveillance, with the Semois and Chiers catchments receiving the most precipitation—more than 40 l/m² recorded at Arlon by Tuesday morning. Conditions were initially favourable because soils were dry and river levels low, reducing but not eliminating flood risk.

The SPW Hydrométrie service used the episode to launch a revamped monitoring portal at hydrometrie.wallonie.be, adding a new vigilance level for hard-to-predict phenomena such as small-stream overflows and surface runoff flooding, [RTBF reported](https://www.rtbf.be/article/pluies-abondantes-les-cours-d-eau-sous-surveillance-la-wallonie-optimise-sa-communication-11816692).

## What's next

IRM forecast continued unsettled weather, with further rain bands crossing the country into Thursday and another significant disturbance on Friday, followed by a clearer, colder weekend with possible morning frosts on Sunday. The episode underscores the vulnerability of Belgian rail infrastructure to wind-thrown trees—and the separate, chronic problem of cable theft that can compound a weather disruption at the worst possible moment.
