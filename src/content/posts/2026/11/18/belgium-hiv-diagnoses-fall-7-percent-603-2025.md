# Belgium's HIV Diagnoses Fall 7 Percent to 603 in 2025

Belgium recorded 603 new HIV diagnoses in 2025, a 7 percent decline from the 662 registered in 2024, according to the annual surveillance report from Sciensano, the country's national public health institute. The figures, [reported by VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/17/603-mensen-kregen-vorig-jaar-een-hiv-diagnose-in-ons-land-een-l/), mark the first clear decline after several years of stagnation and renewed growth, but health authorities caution that the epidemic remains far from resolved.

The drop is most pronounced among Belgian men who have sex with men (MSM), the group that nonetheless continues to account for the largest share of new infections. Of the 603 diagnoses, 312 (51 percent) were recorded among heterosexual men and women, while 249 (41 percent) were among MSM. The remainder falls into other categories, including injecting drug use and unknown transmission routes.

"The decline in HIV diagnoses among Belgian men who have sex with men is encouraging and could indicate that prevention strategies such as PrEP, the medication that prevents an HIV infection, are reaching more people," said Jessika Deblonde of Sciensano. "However, it is important to emphasize that this remains the largest group within the new HIV diagnoses in Belgium."

## A nuanced picture

According to [Sciensano's surveillance reporting](https://www.sciensano.be/sites/default/files/rapport_hiv_2024_nl_final.pdf), the overall decline reverses a troubling multi-year trend. New diagnoses had risen steadily in the preceding years, prompting concern that prevention efforts were losing ground. The 2025 figures suggest those warnings may be bearing fruit—but only partially.

Within the heterosexual group, 91 of the diagnoses were recorded among women of sub-Saharan African background, a subgroup that Sciensano identifies as a key population within the Belgian epidemic and one marked by a high share of late diagnoses.

"There are the Belgian men who have sex with men as the main group. But there are also heterosexuals of sub-Saharan African origin. They too form an important group within the Belgian HIV epidemic," Deblonde said. "We see in this group a large share of late diagnoses. Some of them already contracted HIV before migrating to Belgium."

Late diagnosis carries consequences both for individual health—delaying access to treatment and raising the risk of long-term complications—and for onward transmission. People on effective treatment whose viral load becomes undetectable cannot transmit the virus, a principle known as U=U (undetectable equals untransmittable).

"A late diagnosis does not necessarily mean there was insufficient access to testing in Belgium," Deblonde added. "But that does not take away that it remains important to offer sufficient testing opportunities after arrival in Belgium, to detect HIV in time and to guide people with a positive diagnosis to the right care as quickly as possible."

## Prevention as a matter of tailoring

Sensoa, the Flemish expertise center for sexual health, welcomed the decline but stressed that each affected community requires a distinct approach. "We see that each target group needs a separate form of prevention. We work differently toward people from African communities than toward Belgian men who have sex with men. And we have developed special actions for men who have sex with men who come from Latin American countries," said Boris Cruyssaert, Sensoa's general spokesperson.

Cruyssaert argued that strengthening prevention also means removing practical barriers to access. "If you want to strengthen prevention, you also need to ensure that a number of prevention methods are more easily available," he said. "Among young people, for example, we notice that the price of condoms prevents them from using them. So a system must be considered to make condoms free for young people."

The [Groot Condoomonderzoek](https://www.sensoa.be/het-groot-condoomonderzoek-condoomgebruik-vlaanderen), a large-scale Sensoa survey on condom use in Flanders, found that 31 percent of teenage respondents had at some point skipped using a condom because it was too expensive.

## Why the milestone matters

The decline is welcome news, but Sciensano is explicit that it does not signal victory. The institute states that the World Health Organization's goal of eliminating HIV and AIDS as a public health problem in Europe by 2030 "does not appear immediately within reach."

Belgium's trajectory illustrates how fragile progress against HIV can be. The pandemic years disrupted testing and drove diagnoses down artificially; subsequent years of rising figures showed how quickly gains can erode when prevention and testing efforts slip. The fact that 2025 marks the first genuine decline after that rebound—rather than a bounce-back—is the number health officials have been waiting for.

Yet the composition of the 603 diagnoses shows where gaps remain. The concentration of late diagnoses among people of sub-Saharan African origin points to the enduring need for post-arrival testing and linkage to care, while the persistence of MSM as the largest single transmission group underscores that PrEP rollout must continue and expand. And with cost still deterring young people from condom use, structural measures—not just messaging—may be required.

## What to watch

Several questions will shape the next annual report. Chief among them: whether the decline among MSM is sustained, or whether it reflects a one-year fluctuation in a population that remains heavily affected. Whether testing uptake improves among sub-Saharan African communities—closing the late-diagnosis gap—will determine how much of Belgium's progress translates into better long-term outcomes.

Policy developments will also matter. Sensoa's call for free condoms for young people and for tailored, community-specific prevention programs awaits a government response. With the WHO's 2030 deadline looming just four years out, Sciensano's cautious framing is a reminder that a single year of improvement is a beginning, not an endpoint.
