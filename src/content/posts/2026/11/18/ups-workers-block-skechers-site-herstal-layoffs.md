# UPS Workers Block Skechers Site in Herstal Over Layoffs

Around 300 UPS employees facing redundancy blockaded the Skechers distribution centre in Herstal, near Liège, on Wednesday morning, escalating a labour dispute over the logistics giant's plan to cut hundreds of jobs. The workers, acting in a united union front, sealed off all five main gates of the site early in the morning, preventing trucks from entering or leaving for several hours.

The action, reported by [RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/sketchers-a-herstal-bloquee-par-300-travailleurs-d-ups-menaces-de-licenciement-11817272), targets one of UPS's largest clients rather than the company's own facilities. Skechers was chosen deliberately, the unions said, to draw a major customer into the dispute and increase pressure on the US courier firm.

## The Dispute

UPS announced in June 2026 its intention to eliminate 525 positions from roughly 800 employees in Belgium, essentially its entire operational service, [VRT NWS reported](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/06/23/ups-wil-tot-525-banen-schrappen/). The cuts would affect drivers, parcel sorters and customs clearance staff, most of them based at the company's Diegem site in Flemish Brabant, with a distribution centre in Lummen, Limburg, also operated.

Unions argue the company intends to hand the work to cheaper subcontractors, a practice they describe as "social dumping." As the united front of CNE, Setca and Synova put it: "Jobs are disappearing, but the work remains."

Negotiations under Belgium's so-called Renault law, which governs collective redundancies, are currently deadlocked. According to Didier Lebbe, permanent secretary of the Christian union CNE, management is seeking to force the closure of the first phase of the procedure.

## Why Skechers

The choice of Skechers was calculated. "It is one of UPS's most important clients. In that sense, they are somewhat complicit in what is happening at UPS," said Peggy De Greef, permanent secretary of the socialist union Setca. The unions want the footwear brand to call UPS and demand accountability, or broker an arrangement.

The blockade follows a spontaneous strike by around 200 UPS staff at Diegem the previous week, where workers demanded that unions be fully heard in the social consultation process.

## Black Friday Threat

The timing of the action, just over a week before Black Friday, is no accident. The unions warned they can no longer guarantee the smooth delivery of goods handled by the company. "Those counting on UPS for their Black Friday deliveries risk never receiving their parcels," the unions said.

They also issued a pointed political demand, calling for a strong and urgent signal from the authorities. "If the political world lets this happen, it risks creating an extremely dangerous precedent" for the entire Belgian parcel delivery and logistics sector, they warned.

## Broader Context

The blockade comes amid mounting social tension in Belgium. UPS first announced 20,000 job cuts worldwide in April 2025, and a strike over staff anxiety about the Diegem site's future had already broken out in June last year. The dispute also coincides with a broader season of labour unrest, as the same week saw union marches against the Walloon government's budget choices.

Targeting a client site rather than UPS's own facilities marks a step up in the unions' campaign. By disrupting the operations of a major customer in peak delivery season, the workers aim to convert a company-level redundancy dispute into a reputational and commercial problem for UPS, and to force third parties to weigh in.

## What's Next

Several questions remain open. It is unclear whether UPS will return to the negotiating table and respect the Renault-law procedure or press ahead with forced redundancies. Skechers has so far issued no public response to the pressure. And it remains to be seen whether federal and regional politicians will answer the unions' call for intervention.

For now, the dispute shows no sign of cooling. With Black Friday approaching and talks deadlocked, the risk of further disruption to Belgium's parcel delivery network appears likely to grow rather than fade.
