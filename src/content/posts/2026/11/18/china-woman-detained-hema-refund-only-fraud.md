# China Woman Detained Over 400 Hema Refund-Only Claims

A 29-year-old woman in the northwestern city of Xi'an has been criminally detained on suspicion of fraud after allegedly filing more than 400 "refund-only" claims on Alibaba's Hema (Freshippo) grocery platform, obtaining goods worth roughly 17,000 yuan (about US$2,400) without ever returning them, according to [The Paper](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_34492459).

The case, announced by the Xi'an Public Security Bureau's Economic Development Branch on November 17, 2026, has drawn fresh attention to vulnerabilities in a consumer-protection feature that lets shoppers claim a refund without shipping the product back.

## How the Scheme Worked

According to the police account, the woman began ordering snack foods and daily necessities on the Hema app in 2023. After each delivery, she selected a specific refund reason — such as "bad taste" or "not fresh" — to secure a refund-only settlement, keeping the merchandise while getting her money back.

Over roughly three years she filed more than 400 such claims, averaging one fewer than every three days, with items ranging from snacks and beverages to household necessities. The cumulative loss to the merchant reached more than 17,000 yuan.

Investigators found she frequently rotated delivery addresses and phone numbers to disguise her activity, splitting orders across different recipient identities. After Hema blocked her from ordering on its own app for repeated violations, she shifted to Hema storefronts on other e-commerce platforms to continue the same scheme, [Sina Finance](https://finance.sina.com.cn/jjxw/2026-11-17/doc-iniyzswc5469272.shtml) reported, citing the Xi'an Economic Development Branch.

## The Break in the Case

In early November 2026, Hema merchant staff reported the anomaly to the Zhangjiabao Police Station. An internal review had flagged a user who ordered almost daily since 2023, with 400-plus refund-only claims filed using highly similar reasons and no goods ever returned. Staff could not reach her by phone.

On November 5, officers went to her residence, where they found Hema snacks and daily-use goods piled throughout the living room and on bedroom nightstands — some half-eaten, some still unopened.

"With the intent of illegal possession, she fabricated refund reasons to defraud the platform of goods; the amount involved has reached the criminal filing standard, and her conduct is suspected of fraud," investigating officer Qi Yinan said, according to The Paper.

Police said she claimed the act gave her a sense of "thrill" that relieved stress built up during years of preparing at home for postgraduate entrance exams. She admitted feeling guilt each time but could not resist repeating it, and said she targeted Hema specifically because she believed its products were of good quality.

## Why It Matters

"Refund-only" was introduced by major Chinese e-commerce platforms as a consumer-protection tool, typically for low-value or perishable items where return shipping is uneconomical. The policy has been widely exploited by so-called *yangmao dang* — "wool-pullers" who fabricate quality complaints to obtain free goods. In 2024, the abuse drew intense merchant backlash, prompting platforms to tighten rules.

By 2026, enforcement has escalated from platform-level penalties to criminal prosecution. The Xi'an case echoes a similar one in Shanghai, where a woman surnamed Wang allegedly applied for refund-only on 399 items in three months, involving about 8,000 yuan, and was placed under criminal coercive measures for suspected fraud, [IT之家](https://www.ithome.com/1/011/094.htm) reported.

Under China's Criminal Law Article 266, fraud involving "relatively large" amounts is prosecutable, with the national threshold generally set between 3,000 and 10,000 yuan depending on the province. The Xi'an case, at more than 17,000 yuan obtained through fabricated reasons and retained goods, was deemed to cross that line.

The distinction from mere opportunistic behavior turns on two elements: intent to illegally possess property, and fabrication of facts used to induce the merchant to part with goods.

## What's Next

Police framed the detention as a warning to the broader community of refund-only exploiters. "'Refund-only' is an after-sales rule merchants set up to enhance the consumer experience; it has clear applicable conditions and is not a back door for 'getting goods for free,'" the Xi'an police said, according to The Paper.

The case remains under investigation, and no sentencing or restitution outcome has been disclosed. Whether platforms such as Hema respond with stronger identity verification or cross-platform blacklists — and how courts will distinguish this conduct from legitimate consumer complaints — are questions the coming months are likely to answer. As The Paper put it, what she "fleeced" over 400-plus orders was not a bargain; it ultimately became a criminal record.
