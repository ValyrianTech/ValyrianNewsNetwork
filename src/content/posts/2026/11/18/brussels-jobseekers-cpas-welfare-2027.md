# 10,000 More Brussels Jobseekers Face CPAS by 2027

More than 10,000 additional Brussels jobseekers risk being pushed onto CPAS social welfare by June 2027, the region's employment minister warned on Wednesday, as the fallout from a federal overhaul of unemployment benefits continues to swell the capital's welfare rolls.

Speaking before the Economics Affairs Committee of the Brussels Parliament, Minister for Economy and Employment Laurent Hublet (Les Engagés) said the number of Brussels residents receiving a Revenu d'Intégration Sociale (RIS) has more than doubled — from 17,030 in January 2025 to 37,564 in October 2026, [RTBF reported](https://www.rtbf.be/article/plus-de-10-000-demandeurs-d-emploi-bruxellois-risquent-de-passer-au-cpas-d-ici-juin-2027-11817294). He estimated that roughly 10,000 more people could join that category by mid-2027.

## A welfare system under strain

The figures sit at the intersection of two pressures: a federal reform that progressively limits the duration of unemployment benefits, and the mounting burden on Brussels' local welfare centres (CPAS) and its public employment service, Actiris. Both are being asked to absorb the influx without additional funding.

Brussels jobseekers passed the 100,000 mark in September 2026, the first time since 2016, Hublet told the committee. Nationally, the picture is equally stark: the national employment office recorded 99,144 end-of-rights cases since the start of the year, of which 26,986 were in Brussels, according to [BruxellesToday](https://www.bruxellestoday.be/economie/chomage-exclu-tourne-vers-cpas-bruxelles1.html).

## What the data show

Actiris has been tracking the first waves of people who lost their benefits. Its monitoring of 15,501 jobseekers who exited the system in the first quarter of 2026 — those with eight or more and twenty or more years of unemployment — found that 47.6% became CPAS/RIS beneficiaries within three months of exclusion. Only 12.6% found any job, and just 7.1% secured a contract of 28 days or more. A further 8.9% moved onto health and disability insurance, while only 2.2% entered training.

The [Actiris monitoring report](https://press.actiris.be/reforme-du-chomage-pres-dun-bruxellois-en-fin-de-droit-sur-deux-se-tourne-vers-un-cpas-apres-exclusion-lors-des-deux-premieres-vagues) describes the profiles as highly vulnerable: 63.6% have a low level of education and 39.1% are aged 50 or older, many having been out of work for more than eight years.

![Actiris press release illustration accompanying the unemployment reform monitoring report](https://cdn.uc.assets.prezly.com/f6e0ca83-e89e-4c8a-a979-d7f3b0bf8414/-/preview/-/format/auto/Actiris-2.jpg)

By the end of the first half of 2026, Actiris counted 27,500 Brussels jobseekers who had lost their unemployment or insertion allowance. The transition is not spread evenly: in proportion, Etterbeek (57.8%), Auderghem (53.6%), Brussels-Ville (52.9%) and Saint-Gilles (51.7%) recorded the highest shares of people joining a CPAS, per [La DH](https://www.dhnet.be/regions/bruxelles/2026/11/18/reforme-du-chomage-pres-de-10000-bruxellois-de-plus-pourraient-basculer-au-cpas-dici-juin-2027-HMIGQN5RQZFL7K6C7SV6BUDAIE/).

## Squaring the circle

Opposition lawmakers seized on the figures to challenge the regional government's handling of the crisis. Fabian Maingain of the French-speaking liberal group (Lib. res) warned of "the squaring of the circle between the lack of additional means and an ever-greater number of jobseekers, due to the economic situation and the unemployment reform."

Bernard Clerfayt (DéFI), Hublet's predecessor as Brussels minister, singled out the low training rate. "This training rate is particularly low; there will need to be monitoring," he said.

The Brussels MR has gone further, with its president David Weytsman and group leader Loubna Azghoud arguing that many of those reaching CPAS are in fact close to the labour market and pressing Hublet to reform Actiris, [L'Echo reported](https://www.lecho.be/economie-politique/belgique/bruxelles/le-mr-bruxellois-presse-le-ministre-hublet-de-reformer-actiris/10684547.html).

Hublet acknowledged the resource constraint. Actiris, he said, must "proceed with a reallocation of internal resources and refine its offer to concentrate interventions towards the jobseekers who most need it" — confirming that no additional budget will be allocated to expand the agency's capacity.

He also offered a more optimistic reading of the road ahead, arguing that those expected to join the welfare rolls by June 2027 are "a priori less distant from the labour market compared with the first waves of unemployment exclusion." That view is not universally shared.

## Federal reform, regional costs

The friction reflects a long-standing tension in Belgian federalism: a policy decided at the national level generates costs borne by regional and municipal authorities. The federal reform, championed by Employment Minister David Clarinval (MR), has been presented by the federal government as a success, with the minister pointing to the share of people who found work around the time of their end-of-rights — using a different methodology and observation window than Actiris.

As [Le Soir reported](https://www.lesoir.be/783430/article/2026-11-18/reforme-du-chomage-10000-demandeurs-demploi-bruxellois-supplementaires-risquent), the projected transfers add fresh urgency to a debate that has been building for months. Actiris and the federation of CPAS have been finalising a partnership framework since June 2026 to coordinate support, but its funding and scope remain unresolved.

## What to watch

For now, Actiris has begun accompanying those losing benefits: 4,647 people have started a support pathway since October 2025, and 52.1% were taken on within the three months before their benefits effectively ended. A separate effort for long-term sick workers has registered 3,542 people, 1,349 of them since January 2026.

Yet the core questions remain open. Will the CPAS–Actiris partnership be concluded — and with what resources? Will the next waves of exclusions prove more employable, as Hublet suggests, or will they deepen the strain on municipal welfare budgets? And how will Brussels and the federal government resolve a funding mismatch that shows no sign of easing before the June 2027 deadline.
