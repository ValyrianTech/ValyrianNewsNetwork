# China Approves Eight Provinces for 6G Innovation Pilots

China's Ministry of Industry and Information Technology (MIIT) has approved eight provincial-level regions to launch 6G innovation development pilot programs under a ministry-province coordination framework, as Beijing accelerates efforts to develop and commercialize next-generation wireless technology.

The eight approved regions, confirmed in an official reply letter dated November 17, 2026, are Beijing, Hebei, Shanghai, Jiangsu, Zhejiang, Anhui, Hubei, and Guangdong. Together they will form what officials describe as a nationwide "one chessboard" (一盘棋) approach to 6G research, industrial development, and application.

## What the Approval Means

According to [Xinhua's Science and Technology Daily](https://www.news.cn/tech/20261118/760e0446616d4bf0a78b90d9f637fb0c/c.html), the regions were selected after a competitive process of self-application, formal review, on-site inspection, and solicitation of expert opinions. Each region submitted a tailored work plan that was then reviewed and refined by industry specialists.

The program traces back to a notice issued in June 2026, which first laid out the ministry-province coordination framework and set a July 31 deadline for provincial governments to submit their plans. The latest MIIT reply letter, numbered 工信厅通信函〔2026〕558号, formally converts that framework into three-year implementation tracks for the eight chosen regions.

The pilot rests on the government's "new-type whole-nation system" (新型举国体制), a governance model that mobilizes local resources under national direction, and is anchored in the country's 15th Five-Year Plan, which treats next-generation information and communications technology as a strategic priority.

## Goals and Focus Areas

As detailed in the [MIIT reply letter](https://www.miit.gov.cn/jgsj/txs/wjfb/art/2026/art_ab6fba9058b848d0a1fa3cd7d3e91e2c.html), the goal by 2029 is to form a batch of independently-innovated 6G technical solutions, cultivate promising new business application scenarios, and produce diverse new terminal products to support eventual commercial rollout.

The regions are expected to emphasize their local industrial strengths, pursuing differentiated and mutually complementary development paths rather than duplicating efforts. Key focus areas include cultivating the 6G application industry, consolidating the foundational industry, and exploring new product roadmaps.

Specific technology priorities span immersive communications, industrial manufacturing, the low-altitude economy, embodied intelligence, and smart ocean applications. The related industrial chain is broad: new terminals, chips and key components, operating systems, and satellite internet. A separate notice from Guangdong province, released to align with the pilot, pointed to 6G integrated communication-sensing-computing test technology and a Greater Bay Area quantum communication backbone.

## Strict Regulatory Guardrails

The approval came with significant compliance conditions, reflecting Beijing's desire to manage a strategically sensitive and geopolitically salient technology.

As [IT之家](https://www.ithome.com/1/028/273.htm) reported, enterprises involved in 6G-related commercial applications must obtain the relevant telecommunications business operating license before exploring any commercialized application scenarios. The reply letter also requires pilots to comply with national radio management rules, securing frequency licenses, radio station licenses, and equipment type-approval certificates where spectrum is used.

Notably, the ministry imposed publicity discipline on the pilots. Reporting on local 6G work organization, research progress, and conference activities must adhere to an "internal-external distinction" (内外有别) principle with strict review to ensure objectivity and accuracy. News touching on national-level or whole-industry 6G development must first seek the opinion of the competent business department.

The MIIT said it will establish a coordinated working mechanism, guide the pilot regions, conduct periodic evaluations, and strengthen support in technical trials, spectrum guarantees, project layout, and ecosystem cultivation. The IMT-2030 (6G) Promotion Group is expected to organize 6G technical trials that will help pilot regions build test environments and share results.

## Analysis: A Race for Standards and Supply Chains

The pilot is a signal of China's ambition to lead global 6G standardization and commercialization, building on its 5G leadership position amid ongoing technology competition with the United States. By designating regional clusters now, Beijing is betting that early coordination between national strategy and local industry can compress the path from research to commercial deployment.

Chinese financial media linked the announcement to investor expectations around the 6G supply chain, with 6G-related stocks seeing gains amid anticipation of demand for equipment, components, and test instruments. The three-year implementation window — running through roughly late 2029 — gives the pilot regions a defined runway, with the possibility of extension if evaluations justify it.

## What to Watch

The immediate unknown is the content of the individual regional work plans, which detail each province's one to three chosen focus directions. These plans were submitted internally and refined per expert feedback, and have not been publicly released. How the eight regions differentiate their roles — and how strictly the licensing and publicity controls are enforced — will shape whether the "one chessboard" framework produces integrated breakthroughs or fragmented local efforts. The first periodic evaluation by the MIIT will be the clearest early signal of progress.
