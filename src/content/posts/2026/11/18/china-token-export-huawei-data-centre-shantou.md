# China's Token Export Push Takes Shape in Shantou

China's ambition to sell artificial intelligence computing capacity abroad as metered "tokens" has moved from strategy document to working hardware, with the activation of a Huawei-powered data centre in the southern coastal city of Shantou, according to the [South China Morning Post](https://www.scmp.com/tech/big-tech/article/3374791/chinas-token-export-ambitions-take-shape-new-huawei-powered-data-centre). The 1 billion yuan (about US$150 million) facility runs on Huawei's home-grown Ascend 950DT chip and is built around a business model that packages computing power and cheap electricity as an exportable digital service rather than shipping semiconductors overseas.

The project is a joint effort between Huawei, state telecoms giant China Telecom and IT-services firm Guangdong Tianyima Information Industry, which operates the cluster. It sits at China Telecom's submarine cable landing station in Shantou, a location chosen for its international connectivity and its proximity to offshore wind power.

## Why tokens, and why Shantou

The "token export" (词元出海) model lets China monetise two advantages the United States cannot easily replicate: abundant, inexpensive electricity and dense computing infrastructure. By selling AI inference and generation capacity measured in tokens to overseas customers, Beijing can earn digital-services revenue without physically exporting restricted chips, a workaround that sits outside the reach of hardware export controls.

Shantou was purpose-built for the test. According to a [China Economic Daily report](http://finance.eastmoney.com/a/202611173932229819.html) carried via Eastmoney, the city's submarine cable station carries roughly 52% of China's international export bandwidth and holds China's only "international internet data dedicated channel" pilot along with a national "data processing on arrival" policy pilot. Its planned offshore wind capacity is about 53% of Guangdong province's total, with around 1.2 GW already grid-connected.

The numbers cited by state media are striking, if not yet independently verified. The Shantou Overseas Chinese Economic and Cultural Cooperation Pilot Zone says it has validated a full-chain closed-loop business model in just five months, with average daily token call volume rising from 100 million to 100 billion. China Mobile, China Telecom and China Unicom's international arms are jointly building a "token export global resource pool" at the port.

## The chip at the centre

The Ascend 950DT is the technical heart of the project. Part of Huawei's next-generation Ascend 950 series, it offers 144GB of memory using Huawei's self-developed HiZQ 2.0 "Zhuque" HBM-type memory, 4TB/s memory bandwidth and 2TB/s interconnect bandwidth. It was slated for commercial release in the fourth quarter of 2026, and its deployment in Shantou marks one of its first large commercial outings.

Lin Shusheng, deputy general manager of Tianyima, framed the milestone as a shift in the nature of the AI race. "Large-model competition is shifting from competition over single-chip performance to competition over the systemic capability of chips, interconnection, storage, scheduling and basic software working together," he said. Landing domestic high-end chips in commercial scenarios, he added, would "effectively solve the chip-shortage problem" and provide an "autonomous and controllable computing base."

## A hedge as Washington tightens the screws

The launch carries geopolitical weight. On 16 November 2026, the US Bureau of Industry and Security issued new rules requiring licences for advanced AI chip exports and re-exports to Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Brazil, and extended controls to overseas data-centre rentals by firms headquartered in China, as [BBC News Chinese](https://www.bbc.com/zhongwen/articles/cmpdwn010nyvo/simp) reported. The move closed a "rent-not-buy" loophole that Chinese firms had used to access American-chip compute in third-country facilities.

Beijing called the rule "economic coercion." Against that backdrop, the Shantou cluster reads as a deliberate hedge — an autonomous, domestic, export-facing compute channel built precisely as the overseas rental route narrows.

The scale ambitions are large. The accompanying "International Zero-Carbon Computing Port" plans a 200,000-card-class domestic intelligent computing resource pool, with a first phase of 400MW of total IT power, alongside a six-network-coordinated green computing park. Broader demand signals are also emerging: [Guancha](https://m.guancha.cn/economy/2026_09_05_830131.shtml) reported in September that DeepSeek would procure roughly 160,000 Ascend 950DT chips for an Inner Mongolia data centre.

## What to watch

For all the momentum, several caveats remain. The Ascend 950DT is only reaching commercial availability now, and broader supply constraints — high-bandwidth memory and advanced packaging in particular — could temper the 200,000-card vision. The blockbuster growth metrics come from state media and await independent verification. And exporting tokens raises data-sovereignty and compliance questions that the facility's physical-isolation "digital bonded zone" design is meant to address.

Cao Anding, a senior official at the Shantou pilot zone, framed the strategy in competitive terms. "Zero-carbon, low-cost computing power has become a core element of global digital services trade competition," he said, describing Shantou's "green power, submarine cable, and institutional channel" advantages as a basis for China's growing AI service exports.

What happens next will be closely watched in Washington and Beijing alike. If the token-export model scales, it could give China a durable services-trade channel that sidesteps chip controls — and give other nations a template for selling compute, not hardware, to the world.
