# Sextortion Reports to Child Focus Surge Past 550 in 2026

Belgium's Child Focus has already received more than 550 reports of sextortion this year, a roughly 152 percent increase on the same period in 2025, with adolescent boys the primary victims and artificial intelligence emerging as a potent new tool for predators. The foundation released the figures on 18 November, the European Day for the Protection of Children against Sexual Exploitation and Abuse, alongside a new awareness campaign aimed at parents.

According to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/17/stijging-sextortion-jongens/), the organisation logged more than 550 cases of sexual extortion by 2026 to date. At the same point last year it had recorded 218 files.

"This year we are already at 550 financial sextortion cases. Last year at the same period we were at 218 files. We are really seeing an explosion in the number of reports," Child Focus spokesperson Selyna Ayuso Ferrandiz told [RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/l-intelligence-artificielle-nouvelle-arme-de-sextorsion-pour-les-predateurs-aupres-de-tres-jeunes-ados-11815234).

## A Crime Built on Trust

Sextortion — a contraction of sexual and extortion — is blackmail founded on an intimate image. Predators typically pose as peers, often through fake profiles, build a rapport with their target, and persuade them to share an intimate picture. Once the image is in hand, the tone flips abruptly to threats: distribute the picture, or send money (financial sextortion) or more images.

What stands out in the Belgian data is who is being targeted. Financial sextortion is the only form of sexual exploitation where Child Focus receives more reports from boys than from girls — 92 percent concern boys. "In the cases that reach us, it is often young boys aged 14 to 15 who get trapped," the organisation told RTBF, citing impulsivity, risk-taking and a search for validation as possible explanations. Child Focus is careful not to blame victims; the first words its helpline staff typically say is that it is not their fault.

## AI as a New Weapon

Predators are increasingly turning to artificial intelligence to lower a victim's guard and to apply pressure. Criminals create hyper-realistic, even moving AI-generated profiles that can speak a victim's own language, according to VRT NWS. Some are convincing enough to survive a live video call.

Eline, a counsellor at Child Focus's 116 000 helpline, described one such case to RTBF: a young person asked the person he was chatting with to switch to video. "He saw a girl and that gave him more confidence in the relationship. He sent intimate photos. They were then used for sextortion."

AI is also being used to fabricate partially or fully faked naked images from real photos of victims, which are then deployed to extract money. A federal police commissioner in charge of the Child Abuse cell told RTBF that AI-altered faces and bodies make it harder to identify suspects, and that officers are seeing more child sexual abuse material online.

## The Limits of the AI Narrative

Not everyone sees AI as the driving force. Cédric Glineur, a Brussels volunteer who runs bots that scan the web to remove extorted images and operates the resource site sextorsions.com, told RTBF that most scammers still steal photos from real accounts or platforms rather than generating them. Of AI, he said simply: "They don't need it."

The emerging consensus is therefore more nuanced: AI is a complicating and escalating factor, not the sole cause of the surge.

## An Underreported Toll

Because shame and fear keep many victims silent, Child Focus believes the true number is significantly higher than the reports suggest. Citing the Apenstaartjaren study, VRT NWS noted that 9 percent of young people have already been a victim of sextortion. Broader research from [Sensoa](https://www.sensoa.be/sexting-bij-jongeren-feiten-en-cijfers) on Flemish pupils illustrates how commonplace intimate image-sharing has become among teenagers — the coercive extreme of which is sextortion.

Glineur warned against paying. "You pay once, you think you'll be rid of it, but no. If you've paid, why would the scammer drop it? They will ask for more."

## A Script for Parents

Child Focus marked the European awareness day by launching "The Script," a short film in which a 15-year-old boy tells his parents he has been a victim of financial sextortion. The parents are played by Belgian actors Tine Embrechts and Boris Van Severen, and the storyline is drawn from real testimonials the foundation received.

"As a parent, it's not always self-evident to discuss certain topics with your child. Criminals have a script — parents deserve one too," said CEO Nel Broothaerts. The [campaign film](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PC3CVvHoEiY) is accompanied by practical guidance.

The organisation's advice is blunt: do not judge, listen, do not pay the extortionist, take screenshots of the conversations, stop all contact immediately, and report the facts to Child Focus and the police. Sextortion is a criminal offence, and victims can file a complaint.

## What to Watch

The figures raise questions that Belgian authorities and their European counterparts will face in the months ahead: whether regulation can keep pace with cheap, accessible AI video tools used to build false identities; how many of the 550 reports will translate into prosecutions given the identification difficulties AI creates; and whether platform accountability — for the TikTok and other mainstream networks where fake profiles flourish — will tighten. For now, Child Focus's central message to teenagers caught in the trap is unchanged: it is not your fault, and help is available.
