# Jeffries Nominated as First Black House Speaker

House Democrats nominated Rep. Hakeem Jeffries of New York on Wednesday to be the next Speaker of the House, putting him on track to become the first Black lawmaker to hold the chamber's top post when the new Congress convenes in January. Jeffries secured the closed-door nomination 251-11, brushing past skeptics within his own party even as a group of progressive newcomers withheld their support.

According to [The Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/hakeem-jeffries-house-leadership-speaker-democrats-260433358f776473f29151992569f81d), which reported the vote from Washington, the nomination was the first order of business for dozens of members-elect whose careers began before they were even sworn into office. A separate roll-call vote of the whole House to formally elect the speaker will take place when the new Congress convenes on Jan. 3, 2027.

## A Historic First, Generations in the Making

The milestone traces back more than 150 years, to Joseph Rainey, the first Black American elected to the House in 1870. As [AP detailed](https://apnews.com/article/hakeem-jeffries-election-house-democrats-017ab70f264d4abe90da0325c1f03350) in its profile of Jeffries' rise, the Brooklyn-born 56-year-old now stands at the precipice of a position no Black lawmaker has ever held. He represents parts of Brooklyn once represented by Shirley Chisholm, the first Black woman elected to Congress.

Jeffries, who has led House Democrats since 2023, ran unopposed for the nomination. He was put forward by five Democrats representing all corners of the caucus, including Rep. James Clyburn of South Carolina, the veteran lawmaker who was himself once the highest-ranking Black Democrat in the House.

"I embrace those conversations," Jeffries told reporters ahead of the vote, describing his outreach to the party's various factions.

## A Party Eager for Change

The nomination caps a dramatic turnaround for Democrats, who won one of the biggest incoming majorities in decades — potentially reaching 260 seats once all votes are counted — after ousting Republicans in a midterm rebuke of President Donald Trump. The victory gives Jeffries room to spare in the January floor vote.

But the 11 votes against him underscored the internal tensions he will have to manage. The holdouts, several backed by the Democratic Socialists of America, said in a joint statement that there is "still work to be done" to secure their priorities.

"Whether on Medicare for All, stopping ICE terror, getting money out of politics, or ending the genocide in Gaza, our constituents sent us to Congress with a mandate on these issues," the members-elect said, referencing the acronym for Immigration and Customs Enforcement. "We have worked to secure commitments from Leader Jeffries in good-faith conversations."

Those signing the statement included Darializa Avila Chevalier of New York, Adam Hamawy of New Jersey, Frederick Haynes of Texas, Melat Kiros of Colorado, Donavan McKinney of Michigan, Chris Rabb of Pennsylvania and Claire Valdez of New York.

## A Leadership Trio of Firsts

Jeffries will lead a team that would also make history. Rep. Katherine Clark of Massachusetts, elected majority leader, would be the first woman to hold the floor-boss job, while Rep. Pete Aguilar of California, elected majority whip, would be the first Latino to serve in that role. Both ran unopposed. Rep. Joe Neguse of Colorado was elected assistant speaker, and Rep. Ted Lieu of California was elected Democratic Caucus chair, set to become the highest-ranking Asian American House member ever.

The trio represents the first new generation of Democratic leaders of the post-Pelosi era. When Nancy Pelosi stepped aside from leadership in 2022 after Democrats lost the House, it set the stage for Jeffries, Clark and Aguilar to rise together as a team.

## What's Next

Jeffries has promised a robust 100-day agenda centered on affordability, accountability and American values, signaling early votes on ending Trump's tariffs, clawing back Republican-led Medicaid cuts and restoring expired Affordable Care Act tax credits, alongside an aggressive oversight agenda targeting the administration.

Not everyone is focused on the history. Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez of New York told reporters at the Capitol that "there is one candidate in this race: Hakeem Jeffries," adding that "everyone is far more concerned what that leadership means, what is it promising."

Rep. Yassamin Ansari of Arizona, a progressive, offered a measure of confidence in the incoming speaker. "His job is to reflect all of us, not just a faction," she said.

For Rep. Shomari Figures of Alabama, who won a stunning victory in a redrawn district, the choice was simple. "It's not even like a complex question," he said. "I'm not going to not stand by him right here on the precipice of history."

The formal election on Jan. 3 is widely expected to confirm what Wednesday's vote set in motion — a new era of Democratic leadership, and a barrier broken at the top of American government.
