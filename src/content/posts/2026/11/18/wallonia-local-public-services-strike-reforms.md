# Wallonia's Local Public Services Strike Over Sweeping Reforms

Public-service workers across Wallonia are staging a one-day strike and mass demonstration in Namur on Wednesday, targeting a cluster of regional reforms they say will dismantle frontline services and undermine job security. The action, called by the joint union front of the socialist CGSP and Christian CSC, affects communes, provinces, CPAS welfare centres, intermunicipal agencies and emergency services.

According to [RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/wallonie-greve-dans-les-communes-provinces-cpas-et-intercommunales-ce-mercredi-11816416), the unions are protesting reforms and budget cuts advanced by the regional government led by Minister-President Adrien Dolimont, whose MR-Les Engagés coalition has pushed an austerity and modernisation agenda through the local public sector. In a tract, the unions wrote: "Local public servants and frontline public services say STOP. This isn't a reform, it's a massacre."

## What's at stake

The grievances are not a single demand but a bundle of overlapping reforms. The unions denounce the end of statutory appointments and the general shift to contractual employment, the merger of communes and CPAS, the method used to phase out the provinces, the reduction or abolition of APE employment aid, cuts to funding for local authorities, and a forthcoming decree creating a single employment regime across all local bodies known as "La Locale."

"We consider it a massacre because the reforms attack every pillar of the local civil service, and could make it ineffective," Olivier Nyssen, secretary-general of the CGSP's administrations sector, told RTBF. "So we say: the cup is full, we don't want any more." Mounia Benslama, who heads the local administrations sector at the CSC, warned that "the viability of administrations risks being undermined, as does the durability of some services, and this could have dramatic consequences on the ground."

## The reforms behind the strike

The strike is the culmination of several reforms spearheaded by two ministers: François Desquesnes (Les Engagés) for local authorities and the provinces, and Pierre-Yves Jeholet (MR) for employment and the APE scheme.

"La Locale," adopted in first reading, would create a common local civil-service framework. According to [21news.be](https://www.21news.be/la-locale-ce-qui-va-changer-pour-les-fonctionnaires-des-communes-et-cpas-wallons/), the employment contract would become the norm for new hires, except for legal grades such as directors-general and financial directors, while currently nominated agents keep their status within a transitional phase-out system. The reform would harmonise HR rules and scrap current administrative and pay statutes. It covers nearly 125,000 local public agents in Wallonia, more than 80% of whom are already contractual. "With 'La Locale', we are laying the foundations for a fairer, more legible local civil service better adapted to today's realities," Desquesnes said.

The provinces reform is equally contentious. As [RTBF reported](https://www.rtbf.be/article/comprendre-les-enjeux-autour-de-la-reforme-des-provinces-11686392), the majority intends the October 2024 provincial elections to be the last in Wallonia, with no provincial ballot in 2030, removing some 250 mandates. Provincial colleges and councils would be replaced by assemblies of mayors, ending public sessions and majority-opposition debate. Political scientist Geoffrey Grandjean of the University of Liège warned of a "democratic weakening," asking before which body such an assembly of mayors would be accountable.

On employment, the APE scheme — worth €1.3 billion a year and financing roughly 52,000 full-time equivalents across some 3,875 employers — will be trimmed by €217 million by 2029. The reform shifts the budget from the public employment service Forem to individual ministers, who will allocate it by objective criteria, phased in progressively from 2027 over a maximum of six years. "This 'seismic' reform is the fruit of a long process... it was necessary to change course," Jeholet told the Walloon Parliament, as [RTBF reported](https://www.rtbf.be/article/la-reforme-des-ape-au-parlement-wallon-sismique-mais-necessaire-selon-le-ministre-jeholet-11817007), insisting the changes "do not aim to reduce employment in the non-profit sector" provided there are two major retargetings.

## Disruption and mobilisation

Wednesday's action is expected to disrupt public counters at communes and CPAS, as well as some crèches, retirement homes and public hospitals, swimming pools and household waste collection. The demonstration begins at 10:00 at Namur's SNCB station, with the procession heading to the Walloon Parliament and dispersal expected before 13:00.

Calogero Morina, regional secretary of CGSP-Admi Centre, told local outlet Antenne Centre that participation "could be three times higher than normally recorded," though no official figure has been confirmed. He added that nearly 80% of workers are not yet fully informed about the impact on their jobs, particularly regarding APE schemes or the indexing of grants.

## Analysis: one battle in a wider autumn

The local-authorities strike is deliberately timed ahead of a national demonstration in Brussels on 23 November, signalling that this is a staging action in a broader season of mobilisation. It follows a 10,000-strong rally in Namur in June against budget cuts and repeated national actions against the federal government.

What makes this dispute distinctive is how the unions have fused four separate reforms into a single narrative of existential threat to local public service. The scale is significant: some 125,000 local agents and roughly 52,000 APE-equivalent jobs are touched, with €217 million in APE cuts by 2029 and the effective elimination of the provincial layer.

Democratic-governance concerns cut across the labour dispute. The provinces reform's replacement of elected councils with assemblies of mayors, and the loss of roughly 250 mandates, has drawn unease from political scientists about accountability — a debate the unions are not leading but that amplifies public disquiet.

Politically, the MR-Les Engagés majority is driving the agenda but needs opposition support to clear the two-thirds threshold required to remove provincial elections. The PS, PTB and Ecolo opposition is using both parliamentary procedure — including a Council of State referral on the APE reform — and street mobilisation, aligning with the unions. PS MP Eliane Tillieux claimed 11,000 jobs are threatened by 2029, a figure the government disputes.

## What's next

The immediate test is turnout on Wednesday and whether the reforms face further parliamentary delay. The APE commission has yet to examine the articles of the bill, and the opposition's request for a Council of State opinion introduces a 30-day delay. Whether the 23 November national action escalates will signal how far the mobilisation spreads. For now, Wallonia's local public servants have made clear they intend to be heard — and the government's reform timetable faces its most direct challenge yet.
