# China, South Korea Complete FTA Upgrade Talks

China and South Korea have substantively completed the second phase of negotiations to upgrade their bilateral free trade agreement, China's Ministry of Commerce announced, capping more than eight years of talks aimed at deepening economic ties between the two neighbours. The announcement, carried by the state-run [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/world/20261119/c6ab333d4f1245e9aefe7ad494272c80/c.html) news agency, was made the same day that Chinese President Xi Jinping met South Korean President Lee Jae-myung on the sidelines of the APEC summit in Shenzhen.

An official from the ministry's Department of International Trade and Economic Affairs said the two sides, guided by the consensus of their heads of state, had reached a "high-level, mutually beneficial outcome" after more than eight years of negotiations.

## What the Upgrade Covers

The phase-two deal targets the areas the original 2015 agreement left for later, upgrading the FTA's chapters on **investment, trade in services, financial services and economic cooperation**, along with related annexes. Crucially, both sides adopted a **negative-list model** for commitments in investment, cross-border trade in services and financial services — a mechanism that opens all sectors by default unless explicitly exempted. According to the ministry, this establishes "transparent, stable and predictable" market-access rules for two-way investment and services.

The agreement also sets out specific implementation arrangements in several areas, including cooperation on **traditional medicine services, cultural cooperation and online/network services**, with detailed communication mechanisms between the two governments.

The ministry framed the deal as one that will "continuously release the momentum of free trade development," create new opportunities for companies in both countries, and reinforce the foundation of bilateral economic and trade cooperation.

## A Diplomatic as Much as an Economic Signal

The timing was deliberate. Hours before the ministry's announcement, Xi met Lee in Shenzhen, where the two leaders placed the trade deal at the centre of a broader effort to stabilise relations.

"China and South Korea have substantively completed the phase-two FTA negotiations, which helps consolidate the win-win situation," Xi said during the meeting, according to the [Chinese government's readout](https://www.gov.cn/yaowen/liebiao/202611/content_7086520.htm). "Both sides should make full use of this institutional arrangement, strengthen macro-policy coordination, and cultivate cooperation ecosystems in emerging tech industries such as AI to share development opportunities."

Lee, for his part, said the momentum of a "comprehensive recovery" in Korea-China relations was steadily consolidating, with bilateral economic and trade cooperation growing rapidly. He said Seoul would work with Beijing to strengthen cooperation in trade, investment and industrial and supply chains.

The exchange points to a relationship that has moved well past the chill of the 2016–17 dispute over the US THAAD missile-defence system, which had soured ties and stalled the upgrade talks for years. That the deal was sealed on the sidelines of APEC — and presented jointly by the two presidents — underlines how much Beijing and Seoul now want to advertise their rapprochement.

## Framing the Deal Against Protectionism

The ministry was explicit in casting the agreement as a statement about the global trading system, describing it as "a concrete measure by both sides to practice multilateralism and free trade" and "a concrete action jointly opposing unilateralism and protectionism and upholding the multilateral trading system."

That language is notable given the wider trade environment. Both China and South Korea have been navigating US tariff pressures and a fractious global trading landscape, and Beijing has increasingly positioned itself as a champion of the rules-based system. Xi echoed the theme in Shenzhen, saying both countries are "beneficiaries of multilateralism and free trade" and calling for efforts to safeguard the stability and smooth flow of global industrial and supply chains.

For Seoul, the deal offers a way to deepen its largest trading relationship while keeping open a diplomatic channel to Beijing — a balancing act as it manages its alliance with Washington.

## Trade Ties Already Deep

The original China-Korea FTA was signed on 1 June 2015 and entered into force on 20 December 2015. It has since gone through 11 rounds of tariff cuts, steadily releasing the benefits of goods-trade liberalisation and helping keep two-way trade at high levels, with the two economies' industrial and supply chains deeply intertwined.

According to Chinese statistics cited by Xinhua, bilateral trade reached **US$331.24 billion in 2025, up 1.2% year on year**. The same report cites January–October 2026 trade at a figure it describes as up 63.7% year on year — a rate that is difficult to reconcile with the prior full-year total and appears to reflect an error or unusual base in the source. Readers should treat the 2026 figure with caution pending confirmation from Chinese or Korean customs data.

## What Happens Next

The deal is not yet signed. The ministry said the two sides will now comprehensively review and confirm the technical details of the final text and complete **translation and legal review**, preparing for the formal signature of the upgraded agreement. No timetable for signature was given.

The upgrade also matters beyond the bilateral ledger. By adopting negative lists and locking in services and investment rules, the two neighbours are building a more institutionalised economic relationship — one that Xi suggested should extend into future industries such as artificial intelligence. Whether that ambition translates into concrete cooperation will be the next test.

For now, the message from both capitals is clear: after years of drift, China and South Korea are betting that deeper economic integration serves both their interests — and that saying so loudly, at an APEC summit and in the language of free trade, is itself part of the point.
