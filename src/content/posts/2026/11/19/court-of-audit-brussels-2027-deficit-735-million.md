# Court of Audit Puts Brussels 2027 Deficit at €735 Million

Belgium's Court of Audit has calculated that the Brussels-Capital Region will run a deficit of €735 million in 2027 — €16 million more than the €719 million projected by the regional government — as deputies gathered on Thursday to debate the draft budget, [Le Soir reported](https://www.lesoir.be/783562/article/2026-11-18/la-cour-des-comptes-chiffre-le-deficit-bruxellois-735-millions-deuros-en-2027). The estimate lands on the eve of the parliamentary debate and casts fresh doubt on the government's stated goal of balancing the books by 2029.

The finding is significant less for the size of the gap than for what it signals: an independent auditor's view that the region's fiscal trajectory, already tight, is fragile and rests on assumptions that remain uncertain. Le Soir noted that "sizeable uncertainties remain at this stage" regarding the figures.

## A budget under scrutiny

The 2027 draft, steered by Minister-President Boris Dillies (MR) and Finance and Budget Minister Dirk De Smedt (Anders), targets a deficit of €719 million — a reduction of €238 million, or roughly half, from 2026. The Court of Audit's near-identical figure for 2027 suggests the government's headline numbers are broadly credible, but it also confirms that the region is far from the balance promised by the end of the decade.

For 2026, the Court of Audit estimates the deficit at €956 million, unchanged after a recent government adjustment. That figure deserves caution: documents from a monitoring committee reported earlier in the year suggested the 2026 shortfall could approach €1.25 billion, a discrepancy that, if confirmed, would undercut the government's "halving" narrative.

## The debt burden behind the numbers

The scale of the challenge is rooted in the region's debt. Brussels carries €14.5 billion in direct debt in 2026, rising to €17.4 billion when the liabilities of regional public bodies are included. Interest payments alone now exceed €550 million a year, according to Dillies — a fixed cost that consumes scarce revenue before a single euro is spent on services.

Dillies has defended the government's record, arguing that the region started from a deficit of more than €1.3 billion and has since halved it. "Fixing a Region takes time," he said. "We have a little less than 1,000 days left. That is little to turn a region around." The government's multi-year path foresees a €416 million deficit in 2028 before reaching balance in 2029.

## Measures and political friction

The 2027 plan combines savings and targeted spending. On administration, the government plans to reorganise departments around four pillars, saving €74 million on personnel costs, €39 million on operating costs and €7 million on Brussels Environment. It intends to sell the Ducale and Regent buildings housing the Minister-President's offices and relocate to the BIP on Place Royale. On mobility, a planned public transport fare increase was abandoned after revenues fell short of forecasts; De Smedt said the shortfall was offset by cutting operating expenses.

On employment, the government is targeting a 70% employment rate by 2030, scaling up its effort from €40 million in 2026 to €55 million in 2027 and €75 million by 2029, while ending the Activa aid scheme, which had an activation rate of only about 10%. On security, the budget allocates €5 million for the Gares plan in 2027 and reinforces the SIAMU emergency service with 92 recruits and a new headquarters. Social-democrat ministers secured additional funding for family reception, addiction prevention, school-dropout prevention, communes and CPAS, and social housing.

Negotiations were tense, particularly between the PS and MR, and the budget has drawn criticism from across the political spectrum. Health insurers, including the Mutualités Libres, gave a positive but cautious welcome, warning that future savings remain unknown and could produce a "boomerang" effect on health financing.

## What to watch

The parliamentary debate will test whether the coalition can hold its line on consolidation while absorbing the Court of Audit's caveats. Two variables loom largest: whether the 2026 baseline is revised upward, and whether the federal government — itself navigating a budget crisis — will provide the support Brussels is seeking. For now, the €735 million figure stands as a sobering reminder that the road to balance in 2029 runs through difficult choices the region has yet to fully confront.
