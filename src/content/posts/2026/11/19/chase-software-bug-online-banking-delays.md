# Chase Software Bug Triggers Online Banking Delays Nationwide

A software bug at JPMorgan Chase rippled through the U.S. financial system on Monday, causing online banking delays that left thousands of customers at Chase and other major banks temporarily unable to complete transactions, according to [AP News](https://apnews.com/article/bank-outage-chase-wells-fargo-truist-50fee2693c22f597fa6ca51a97ef2cda). The bank said the disruption was not a cyberattack and was resolved the same day.

The incident at the nation's largest bank by assets sent a wave of outage reports across the country, with customers at Bank of America, Wells Fargo, U.S. Bank, Capital One, and Truist also reporting trouble — even though those institutions said their own systems were operating normally.

## What Went Wrong

Chase spokesman Joe Evangelisti traced the failure to a faulty software release. "JPMorgan Chase has experienced a temporary issue related to a software bug in an on-premise hardware system — it is not cyber-related," he said, according to [Fox Business](https://www.foxbusiness.com/money/jpmorgan-chase-systems-recovering-following-hourslong-nationwide-technical-outage).

An FBI official confirmed the disruption was traced to a software release rolled out earlier that Monday and said there was no indication of malicious or external cyber activity. Federal regulators were in touch with the affected banks almost immediately and quickly determined the incident was contained.

Evangelisti stressed that the problem was confined to Chase. "This is not a systemic issue — other banks may have been impacted by slowness in transactions only to the extent that they were a party to a transaction with us," he said.

## Why Other Banks Appeared Affected

The apparent breadth of the outage highlighted how deeply interconnected the modern banking system has become. Because Chase sits at the center of the U.S. payment network, its failure made it look as though competitors were also down.

Bank of America said its "systems have been operating normally today," while U.S. Bank confirmed it was "not directly impacted" but noted that clients sending or receiving Zelle payments involving another institution might see delays. PNC and Capital One similarly reported no direct impact, according to [Newsweek](https://www.newsweek.com/are-chase-wells-fargo-down-latest-update-on-bank-outages-12722872). Only Wells Fargo declined to comment, though its customers reported problems.

The Zelle payment network itself stayed fully operational, said its operator Early Warning Services. Delays occurred only for transfers involving a Chase customer. "An issue at the financial institutions may be delaying Zelle payments for some of their customers," a spokesman said.

Customers who tried to bank online Monday were greeted with a message: "Some services aren't available. We're working on them. Please try back later."

## A Week of Tech Failures

The Chase outage capped a striking week of high-profile technology breakdowns across the U.S. economy, none of which officials linked to the others. A major Microsoft outage days earlier disrupted Outlook, Teams, and its Copilot AI assistant. A malfunctioning router overwhelmed the Federal Aviation Administration's aging air traffic control system, grounding all U.S. flights for hours. United Airlines also resolved an internal system outage that delayed departing flights.

That cluster of failures has intensified public anxiety about the fragility of the digital infrastructure underpinning daily American life — and about what the artificial intelligence era means for it, as [AP News reported](https://apnews.com/article/tech-outage-ai-security-patch-0c0f0cf4983a0f81ba88c4c4d2b8383f). AI is helping organizations detect software vulnerabilities and patch them faster, but that urgent patching can itself introduce glitches, while making it easier for attackers to exploit systems.

"Even when the cause is a technical failure rather than a cyberattack, an issue at one point in the system can disrupt transactions elsewhere," Christina Powers, a cybersecurity and banking specialist at West Monroe, told AP in [an explainer](https://apnews.com/article/bank-outage-chase-wells-fargo-truist-zelle-aeb4b0d6b6789c8c96bb2520454a6d1e) on the incident.

## The Phishing Risk That Follows

Officials and security experts warned that the real danger may come after the outage. Scammers routinely exploit confusion during service disruptions by sending fake messages claiming a customer's account was affected.

"The biggest cybersecurity risk for the public right now may actually come after the outage," Sean Connery, chief security officer at Orbis Solutions, told Newsweek. "Criminals watch these. They can send you a text message saying your account was affected, click here to verify your account. Do not click that link."

## What to Watch

Chase said transactions were posting normally by Monday evening and confirmed the problem was fully resolved by Tuesday. Money in customer accounts was never at risk and remained protected by federal deposit insurance up to $250,000.

Still, the episode raises questions about the operational resilience of institutions at the heart of the financial system. The exact number of affected customers was not disclosed, and it remains unclear whether regulators will open a formal review of Chase's notification and resilience practices. For a bank with roughly 94 million U.S. customer relationships, a single software release was enough to briefly unsettle confidence across the entire banking sector.
