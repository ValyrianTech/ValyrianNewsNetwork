# Belgium's Richest and Poorest Municipalities Revealed

Belgium's statistical office has published its annual ranking of the country's municipalities by median income, and the results lay bare a persistent divide. According to [Le Soir](https://www.lesoir.be/783627/article/2026-11-19/voici-les-dix-communes-les-plus-riches-et-les-plus-pauvres-de-belgique), Attert, a rural commune in the province of Luxembourg, tops the list with a median income of 45,790 euros, while Saint-Josse-ten-Noode in Brussels ranks poorest at 21,891 euros for the 2024 reference year. The richest commune earns roughly 2.1 times the median income of the poorest, a gap that underscores the sharpest economic contrasts in the country.

## A Tale of Two Belgiums

The geography of wealth is strikingly consistent. Six of the ten richest municipalities sit in the province of Luxembourg, in Belgium's southern Walloon region, while the other four are in Flemish Brabant. The wealthiest ten are Attert (45,790 €), Messancy (41,682 €), Saint-Léger (38,830 €), Habay (32,213 €), Etalle (38,115 €), Arlon (37,813 €), Kraainem (37,730 €), Tervuren (37,082 €), Oud-Heverlee (36,964 €) and Wezembeek-Oppem (36,649 €).

At the other end of the spectrum, six of the poorest ten municipalities are in the Brussels-Capital Region, with two in Hainaut and two in the province of Liège. The ten poorest are Saint-Josse-ten-Noode (21,891 €), Molenbeek-Saint-Jean (22,220 €), Anderlecht (22,931 €), Koekelberg (23,815 €), Farciennes (24,248 €), Charleroi (24,271 €), Schaerbeek (24,578 €), Dison (24,615 €), the City of Brussels (24,670 €) and Seraing (25,044 €).

Readers should note a quirk in the Le Soir table: Habay's figure (32,213 €) is lower than the entries ranked directly below it, suggesting the printed order may contain a typographical inconsistency. The individual income figures are the reliable data points; the precise mid-table ranking should be treated with caution.

## Measuring the Gap

Median income is the midpoint of a population's earnings — half of residents earn more, half earn less. Statistical agencies prefer it to average income for inequality analysis because it is far less distorted by a handful of very high earners. Statbel derives commune-level figures primarily from tax declaration data, producing a per-capita, after-tax picture of household wealth.

The pattern is far from new. Under the previous release, based on 2022 data, [Statbel](https://statbel.fgov.be/fr/nouvelles/attert-est-la-commune-la-plus-riche-et-saint-josse-ten-noode-la-plus-pauvre-en-2022) reported that Attert was already the richest commune at 42,211 euros and Saint-Josse-ten-Noode the poorest at 19,288 euros. Between the two reference years, Attert's median income rose by roughly 8.5 percent, while Saint-Josse's climbed by about 13.5 percent — meaning the poorest commune's income is growing faster in percentage terms, even as the absolute gap widened from about 22,900 euros to roughly 23,900 euros.

## Why the Divide Persists

The rankings reflect deep structural forces rather than any single year's fluctuation. As [RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/la-commune-la-plus-riche-de-belgique-est-en-wallonie-la-plus-pauvre-a-bruxelles-11465365) noted in its coverage of the 2022 data, the wealthiest communes fall into two distinct categories: affluent rural municipalities in Luxembourg province near the Grand Duchy of Luxembourg, which benefit from cross-border workers and high-value jobs, and leafy residential suburbs of Brussels and Leuven in Flemish Brabant, home to many EU and international professionals.

At the opposite extreme, the poorest municipalities tell a story of two different kinds of hardship. The Brussels communes — Saint-Josse, Molenbeek, Anderlecht, Schaerbeek and Koekelberg — concentrate lower-income and migrant populations within a city where high average wealth coexists with pronounced inequality. Meanwhile, Farciennes, Charleroi and Seraing are former coal and steel heartlands of Wallonia whose deindustrialisation left lasting socio-economic scars; Dison, in the Verviers area, shares that post-industrial profile.

## Poverty Risk Reinforces the Picture

The Statbel data on poverty risk — the share of a commune's population living below the national poverty threshold — reinforces the income ranking. The lowest poverty-risk communes are concentrated in Flanders, while the highest are all in Brussels. The 2022 figures placed Saint-Josse-ten-Noode at a 34 percent poverty risk, followed by Molenbeek-Saint-Jean at 31.5 percent and Anderlecht at 28.2 percent.

## What to Watch

The 2026 publication offers a familiar snapshot: a durable north-south and urban-rural split that Belgium's regional and municipal policies have yet to close. The key questions lie in whether the faster percentage growth in the poorest communes can translate into a narrowing of the absolute gap, and whether Brussels — home to six of the ten poorest municipalities — can address the intra-city inequality that its aggregate wealth tends to mask. For now, the data confirm that in Belgium, your postcode remains one of the strongest predictors of your income.
