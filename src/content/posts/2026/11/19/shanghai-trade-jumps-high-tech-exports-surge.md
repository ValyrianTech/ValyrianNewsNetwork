# Shanghai Trade Jumps 18.7% as High-Tech Exports Surge 38.6%

Shanghai's goods trade totaled 4.41 trillion yuan (about $620 billion) in the first ten months of 2026, up 18.7% year-on-year, according to figures released by Shanghai Customs and reported by [The Paper](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_34509066). Exports rose 23.4% to 2.02 trillion yuan, while imports climbed 15% to 2.39 trillion yuan. Within the headline totals, high-tech product exports surged 38.6% to 677.9 billion yuan, now accounting for 33.5% of the city's total export value.

The data makes Shanghai one of the most dynamic engines of China's foreign trade and reinforces Beijing's "new quality productive forces" policy narrative of industrial upgrading. But a closer look at the composition reveals that a disproportionate share of the growth flows through a single, highly concentrated electronics and AI-hardware supply chain — a boom that may be more cyclical than the headline suggests.

## Steady momentum across the year

The growth has been remarkably stable rather than volatile. In the first half of 2026, Shanghai's foreign trade grew 18.6%; through October the rate held at 18.7%, indicating that the pace neither accelerated nor decelerated materially across the third quarter. October alone was strong: single-month trade rose 26.1% to 464.83 billion yuan, with exports up 33% and imports up 20.8%. Shanghai has now posted double-digit growth in all three indicators — total trade, exports, and imports — for the year to date.

Foreign-invested enterprises remain the largest trading bloc, accounting for 2.29 trillion yuan, or 52% of the city total. Yet private enterprises are growing faster: their trade rose 22.6% to 1.81 trillion yuan, with exports up 34.7%, reflecting a structural shift in who is driving Shanghai's export machine. State-owned enterprises, meanwhile, grew just 1.5%, serving a stabilizing role through imports of 197.19 billion yuan.

## Trading partners tilt toward Asia, away from the US

The geography of Shanghai's trade illustrates China's broader diversification strategy. Trade with the European Union rose 8.7% to 728.02 billion yuan and trade with ASEAN jumped 36.5% to 724.36 billion yuan; together the two account for 33% of the city's total. Trade with the United States grew only 3.3% to 366.59 billion yuan — well below the overall pace — while South Korea surged 73.8% to 310.69 billion yuan, the fastest growth among major markets and a signal of deep electronics and semiconductor supply-chain linkages. Trade with Africa rose 23.2% to 150.81 billion yuan, with imports from the continent up 40.3%.

The US figure is particularly telling. At a time of persistent tariff and trade tensions, Shanghai's exports are increasingly finding homes in Asia and Europe rather than across the Pacific.

## The AI electronics engine

Beneath the high-tech headline sits one dominant driver: automatic data processing equipment and parts (computers and servers) exported 236.58 billion yuan, more than doubling with growth of 130%. Integrated circuit exports rose 23% to 205.34 billion yuan, and ship exports increased 30.1% to 57.07 billion yuan. Labor-intensive products — the traditional base of the export economy — grew just 7% to 176.04 billion yuan.

The import side tells the same story. Integrated circuit imports rose 28.7% to 285.79 billion yuan, and semiconductor manufacturing equipment imports climbed 23% to 83.83 billion yuan. The Paper explicitly attributed the pull to "investment in the AI industry," a direct reflection of the global boom in computing infrastructure and data centers.

Shanghai's special customs supervision zones amplified the trend. The city's ten such zones handled 1.86 trillion yuan in combined trade, up 22% and equal to 42.3% of the city total, while bonded logistics grew 32.5% to 1.32 trillion yuan. This reflects Shanghai's role as an entrepôt and re-export hub, not purely a site of domestic production.

## How much is upgrade, how much is cycle?

The concentration raises a genuine question about durability. Analysts cited in national coverage are increasingly cautious. Peng Bo, a researcher at the Chinese Academy of International Trade and Economic Cooperation under the Ministry of Commerce, cautioned in [an analysis by 10jqka](https://news.10jqka.com.cn/20261118/c681931261.shtml) that once the AI electronics chain is excluded, growth in general trade, traditional manufacturing, and commodity and consumer-goods imports narrows significantly. If a handful of sectors — above all electronics — dominate the increment, order swings in those sectors could amplify volatility across the whole.

There is also a base-effect caveat. Zhang Lin, deputy director of the Far East Credit Rating Research Institute, noted that October's record high-tech manufacturing growth partly reflects a low base, since the comparable month in 2025 grew only 7.2%. A single month's surge, in other words, should not be extrapolated linearly.

In the national context, the "new momentum" contribution of more than 50% to industrial growth is a contribution-to-growth metric, not a share-of-total metric. When overall industrial value-added growth is only 4.8%, a contribution exceeding 50% corresponds to a modest absolute increment. Reading the contribution rate as a share of the total would overstate how much of the economy has already been transformed.

## What to watch

The sustainability of Shanghai's export boom depends on several conditions. On the demand side, whether global appetite for smart and green products holds will determine whether high-tech orders continue. On the supply side, the smart and green upgrading of traditional industries — visible in the 30.1% growth in ship exports — must scale enough to offset any electronics slowdown. And on the policy side, the pace at which green-trade and digital-trade frameworks are implemented will shape the next leg of growth.

For now, the headline is unambiguously strong. Shanghai has sustained double-digit growth across total trade, exports, and imports, and high-tech goods now make up a third of its exports. The open question is whether that growth rests on a durable, broad-based industrial upgrade or on a narrower AI-driven electronics cycle — and the answer will become clearer in the months ahead.
