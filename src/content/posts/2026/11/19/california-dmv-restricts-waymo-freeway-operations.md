# California DMV Restricts Waymo Freeway Operations

The California Department of Motor Vehicles has barred Waymo from operating or testing its driverless robotaxis on the state's freeways, citing what it called an "imminent hazard to public safety." The order took effect November 11 but was widely reported this week, after the agency detailed the incidents that triggered it. In response, Waymo said it has temporarily suspended freeway operations across all of its U.S. markets.

According to [KQED](https://www.kqed.org/news/12110123/hazard-to-public-safety-california-dmv-restricts-waymo-freeway-operations), the restriction followed a string of recent incidents on Bay Area highways that raised fresh concerns about how the Alphabet-owned company's vehicles handle construction zones and emergency vehicles.

## Why the DMV Acted

DMV spokesperson Jonathan Groveman told KQED that the department "determined that Waymo's driverless freeway operations pose an imminent hazard to public safety due to recurring incursions by its vehicles into active construction zones where construction workers, vehicles and equipment are present."

Two events in the week before the order are central to the decision. In one, three Waymo autonomous vehicles entered an active construction closure on U.S. Route 101 near Burlingame. In another, a Waymo vehicle appeared to attempt to bypass a California Highway Patrol traffic break. As [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2319947/waymo-is-pausing-all-freeway-rides-after-california-dmv-says-the-robotaxis-are-imminent-hazard-to-public-safety/) reported, the latter incident involved a driverless Waymo caught trying to pass a Highway Patrol vehicle that was slowing traffic.

Groveman said the incidents came after "prior restrictions and corrective actions implemented following similar incidents on May 18, 2026." That history is well documented: Waymo paused freeway service in May and in June issued a recall of nearly 4,000 robotaxis to stop them from driving into highway construction zones. According to [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/18/waymo-recalls-nearly-4000-robotaxis-to-stop-them-driving-into-highway-construction-zones/), Waymo had identified at least 13 instances of its vehicles driving into highway sections closed for construction. The company resumed freeway driving in July 2026.

That regulators returned to the issue months later suggests the earlier fix did not fully resolve Waymo's handling of construction zones.

## Waymo's Response

Waymo said none of the recent incidents resulted in injuries or collisions, and its robotaxis continue to operate on surface streets across California.

"In alignment with our safety-first principles, we have also temporarily restricted freeway operations across our other operating markets to evaluate our performance," Waymo spokesperson Dana Gallagher said in an emailed statement to KQED. "Safety is at the foundation of everything we do."

The company said it will continue freeway testing with "trained autonomous specialists" — human safety drivers — while the restriction remains in place.

The DMV said the order will stay in force until "Waymo demonstrates to the Department that it has taken effective corrective actions to address the deficiencies that precipitated the restrictions," including sufficient evidence that its vehicles can detect and respond to construction zones and emergency vehicles.

## What It Means in Practice

The restriction is confined to freeways, but its practical effects are significant. The ban effectively prevents customers from hailing a Waymo for trips to San Francisco International Airport, which requires freeway access, and it reshapes routes, ride length and cost for longer journeys.

It also lands at a sensitive moment for the company. Waymo began offering fully autonomous freeway trips in the Bay Area, Phoenix and Los Angeles in November 2025 and has expanded aggressively since, with service in eight states and planned launches in Boston, Seattle, London and Tokyo. By choosing to pause freeway rides nationwide — not just in California — Waymo is signaling a precautionary posture aimed at heading off similar regulatory action in other markets.

The DMV action also arrives as California weighs broader autonomous-vehicle rules. The California Public Utilities Commission, which oversees passenger service, was scheduled to consider new AV regulations on November 19, following months of pressure from lawmakers, labor groups and local officials demanding more local control over deployment.

## What to Watch

The central question is how quickly Waymo can satisfy the DMV that its vehicles handle construction zones and emergency responders reliably — and whether the company's self-imposed national pause prompts other states to open their own reviews of highway operations. For now, California's freeways are closed to driverless Waymo rides, and the company's ambitious expansion plans face their most direct regulatory test to date.
