# Anthropic's Record $81bn IPO Tests AI Boom

Anthropic, the artificial intelligence company behind the Claude chatbot, raised $81.3 billion in the largest initial public offering in history on Wednesday, pricing its shares at $83 each before its Nasdaq debut on Thursday. At that price, the company is valued at roughly $1.5 trillion, making it one of the twelve most valuable companies in the world — an extraordinary figure for a firm that is still losing money. The float has become an immediate referendum on whether the AI investment boom can justify its soaring valuations, and on whether ordinary investors should buy in.

The company sold 980 million Class A shares, up from the 825 million initially planned, after the offering drew demand several times over. Including a separate $26 billion private placement of non-voting shares sold to major technology partners, the total deal reached $107.3 billion — surpassing the $75 billion SpaceX raised in June, according to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/16/anthropic-naar-de-beurs/). The shares began trading Thursday under the ticker "ANT," with co-founder and chief executive Dario Amodei ringing the opening bell at 9:30 a.m. New York time.

## What Anthropic Is

Founded in 2021 by Dario Amodei, his sister Daniela and a group of researchers largely drawn from OpenAI, Anthropic was positioned as an "ethical and safer alternative" to its rival. It now builds frontier AI models, most notably Claude, and competes at the leading edge of the sector. Its swift rise from a privately held start-up — last valued at $965 billion in a May 2026 funding round — to a trillion-dollar public company encapsulates both the promise and the anxiety surrounding artificial intelligence.

Pre-market indications suggested the shares could open 20 to 26 percent above the IPO price, while Nasdaq futures rose nearly 2 percent. The offering was reported to be between seven and ten times oversubscribed, a sign that demand for AI exposure remains intense despite recent market turbulence.

## A Different Kind of Giant

The comparison with SpaceX, which until this week stood as the largest IPO on record, is instructive. SpaceX floated at a valuation of about $1.77 trillion, which rose to $2.1 trillion after its first trading day. But as KBC financial economist Tom Simonts explained to VRT NWS, the underlying businesses are very different.

"The big difference is that SpaceX already has a revenue model thanks to its Starlink satellites," Simonts said. "At AI companies like Anthropic, there are above all a great many investments needed right now, while it remains uncertain what profits will replace them."

Anthropic posted an operating loss of more than $8 billion in 2025, with expenses of about $13 billion against revenue of $4.6 billion. The company has committed to roughly $900 billion in future spending on computing power and infrastructure. Its revenue is nonetheless expected to climb steeply, from about $55 billion this year toward an estimated $685 billion by 2030 — though costs are projected to surge alongside it.

"Anthropic isn't doing badly, but it is a risky investment regardless," Simonts said. "If Anthropic ever becomes the first to develop a superintelligent system that doesn't just answer questions but can independently take actions, then the company has a gigantic advantage. But whether that will actually succeed remains, of course, a big gamble."

## Should Ordinary Investors Buy In?

The debut put the question squarely to retail investors. Up to a fifth of the shares were reserved for them, and in Europe roughly $1.1 billion of stock was sold to UK and continental European buyers. The UK platform Hargreaves Lansdown reported 45,811 clients applying, with an average order of about £6,661, though they were allocated only 33.6 percent of what they sought.

The cautionary case is well established. Simonts noted that many popular companies trade as much as 50 percent below their peak share price a year after listing.

"Especially with large IPOs there is usually a lot of interest at first and those companies become somewhat overvalued," he said. "After 12 months we often see a more realistic level, which for the 10 largest IPOs of 2026 in the US is about 20 percent below the subscription price. The likelihood is high that this will be the case with Anthropic too."

Market observers framed the listing as a litmus test. Garry White, chief investment commentator at Raymond James, said in comments carried by [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/business/live/2026/nov/19/anthropic-float-stock-market-nasdaq-raises-81bn-record-ipo-latest-news-updates) that a strong post-listing rally would signal markets remain convinced a handful of AI leaders can eventually generate profits large enough to justify today's vast spending. Conversely, he said, a weak reception could suggest investors are becoming more selective.

Others were blunter. Kathleen Brooks, research director at XTB, noted that a trillion-dollar-plus valuation is more typical of a mature company with a history of strong fundamentals. "Anthropic could not be more different," she said. "It has generated strong revenue growth, but only in the short term; added to this, it is still unprofitable."

## The Bubble Question

The IPO arrives at a delicate moment for the AI trade. The Nasdaq Composite has fallen more than 10 percent from its early-October record amid uncertainty over data-center construction following the Democrats' midterm wins, and after the departure of Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang shook confidence in the sector.

Professor Richard Whittle of the University of Salford warned that the record listing pushes the boom into riskier hands. "This record listing shows a continuation of AI exuberance that may bring a tipping point closer," he said. "Bubbles don't burst on the day of the largest deal. They burst later, if the positive future becomes suspect."

Ownership also raises questions. Although no single venture investor holds more than 5 percent, strategic partners Amazon (about 15 percent) and Google (about 14 percent) together own nearly 30 percent of the company after the listing — a highly concentrated structure that could draw antitrust scrutiny.

## What to Watch

The first test comes Thursday, when ANT begins trading and the grey-market premium meets real order books. A strong close would validate continued enthusiasm for AI; a fade would signal that investors have grown choosier about which AI bets they will back.

The larger question is whether Anthropic can convert rapid revenue growth into profit before its enormous capital commitments come due — and whether a better or cheaper model from OpenAI or China upends the field. Those are outcomes nobody can predict, but they will determine whether this record listing is remembered as a new chapter for AI or as a peak of the boom.
