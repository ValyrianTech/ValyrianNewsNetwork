# Jining Turns Ancient Canal Into Smart, Green Hub

Jining, a landlocked city in eastern China's Shandong province, has become the first inland river port in northern China to handle more than 100 million tonnes of cargo in a year, according to [Guangming Daily](https://epaper.gmw.cn/gmrb/html/content/202611/13/content_30140.html), which on 13 November profiled the city's push to fuse centuries of Grand Canal heritage with a modern, low-carbon logistics economy.

The milestone — 116 million tonnes of cargo in 2025 — anchors a broader transformation: Jining is repositioning itself as the "Capital of the Canal" for a new era, pairing automated container terminals and a new-energy shipbuilding industry with tourism that celebrates the waterway that once made it rich.

## A Water Ridge Turned Trade Artery

The navigable section of the Beijing-Hangzhou Grand Canal runs 877 kilometres, and 210 of them — nearly a quarter — pass through Jining. Historically, the city sat at the canal's highest point, the so-called "water ridge," where shortages often interrupted north-south grain transport.

That obstacle was solved in 1411, when Ming dynasty engineers built the Nanwang water-diversion hub, channeling the Dawen River into the canal to keep it navigable for more than 500 years. Officials today frame the modern port as a continuation of that hydraulic ingenuity. "After the Yuan dynasty opened the Huitong River, Jining became the throat of north-south grain transport," guide Yang Bingjie told Guangming Daily, explaining how the Ming and Qing canal administration earned the city its "Canal Capital" name.

## From a Dried Wetland to a 100-Million-Tonne Port

The centerpiece of the modern revival is Liangshan Port, which as recently as 2021 was an empty wetland that dried up and blocked navigation. Jining committed to inland shipping in 2014, and the port's position at the junction of the Grand Canal and the heavy-haul Wari Railway turned it into a bridge between coal-producing inland regions and the Yangtze Delta.

"Comprehensive freight costs are one-third of rail and one-seventh of road, saving 50 to 60 yuan per tonne and cutting enterprise logistics costs by more than 30 percent," Liangshan Port general manager Wang Bing told [Economic Daily](http://www.ce.cn/xwzx/gnsz/gdxw/202602/t20260204_2749744.shtml). The port handled more than 40 million tonnes of coal in 2025, accounting for 70 percent of Shandong's coal supply guarantee volume.

Downriver, Longgong Port has become a showcase for automation. Sixteen driverless container trucks guided by 5G and BeiDou navigation move cargo around the terminal, while a digital-twin control room tracks every vessel and container in real time. According to [Guangming Net](https://news.gmw.cn/2026-01/25/content_38556630.htm), the fully automated terminal lifted handling efficiency by 80 percent while cutting staffing by 60 percent, with driverless trucks positioning to within 2 centimetres. Longgong's own throughput passed 335,000 TEU in 2025, and Jining Port overall topped 600,000 TEU.

## A Port That Attracts Industry

Jining's strategy has shifted from a "pass-through economy" to port-anchored industrial clusters. A flagship example is the Lyocell fibre plant run by Sateri (Shandong) Fiber in Yutai County, whose first 150,000-tonne production line began operating in March 2026. Raw material travels from Brazil by sea, transfers to river vessels in Shanghai, sails north up the Grand Canal and reaches the factory via the Baima River branch.

"The whole journey is 'one box to the end,' straight to the plant," Sateri public affairs manager Zhao Huachong told Guangming Daily, noting transport costs run a quarter of rail and a tenth of road. The project, listed by China's commerce ministry as a landmark foreign-invested venture, has drawn more than 20 downstream textile firms.

The model has pulled in 58 Fortune Global 500 companies, the city says, forming "seven 10-billion-yuan port-adjacent parks" that link port, warehouse and factory in a single chain.

## Greening the Fleet

The environmental dimension is equally central. Shandong New Energy Shipbuilding, a venture backed by Shandong Ronghui Group, CIMC, battery maker CATL and Wuhan University of Technology, has scaled up production sharply: from six vessels delivered in 2024 to 82 in 2025, with about 200 expected in 2026. It has signed more than 180 orders and exported new-energy vessels, including to France's CMA CGM.

On 29 December 2025, five pure-electric multi-purpose cargo ships were delivered at Zoucheng's Baima River, forming Shandong's first scaled, organised electric cargo fleet, with 50 more on order. Each vessel carries 2,000 tonnes, has a range of 270 kilometres on a full charge and can swap batteries in 15 minutes through a "boat-battery separation" design.

In late September, a pure-electric vessel was trialled on the roughly 90-kilometre Liangshan-Longgong route using "shore-based driving control, vessel-side monitoring" — semi-autonomous navigation with remote emergency takeover. "Green smart vessels are gradually turning traditional crew from mechanical operators into system administrators, moving from experience-driven to data-driven," New Energy Shipbuilding staff member Feng Jinge said.

## Balancing Heritage and Growth

The transformation is not only industrial. Jining's canal tourism has expanded, with tour boats, heritage walks and a redeveloped ancient town drawing families eager to "walk and study the canal." Along the banks, ecological corridors, riverside parks and cultural venues have replaced the industrial waterfront of earlier decades.

According to [China News Service](https://www.chinanews.com.cn/gn/2026/01-16/10553312.shtml), the city is deliberately promoting a "green, digital, cultural" trio of strengths — from automated ports to cross-border e-commerce and Confucian heritage tourism — as a template for high-quality development in the 15th Five-Year Plan period.

## What's Next

Shandong's provincial planning explicitly backs Jining as a "Northern Inland River Shipping Center." The city aims to lift cargo throughput to 140 million tonnes and container volume to 700,000 TEU by the end of 2026, and to reach 200 million tonnes and 1.5 million TEU by 2030.

Analysts caution that the official narrative is largely celebratory, and challenges remain — protecting water quality for the South-to-North Water Diversion, weaning the local economy off coal, and making electric vessels commercially viable at scale. Yet the direction is clear. As transport expert Zhang Ruhua of Shandong University put it, an inland port exceeding 100 million tonnes signals world-leading capacity that can deeply empower regional sustainable development.

For a city whose fortunes were once made by grain barges, the canal is proving once again to be its most valuable asset — now carrying containers and clean energy rather than just silt and history.
