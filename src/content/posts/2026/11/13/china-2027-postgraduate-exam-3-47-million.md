# China's 2027 Postgraduate Exam Draws 3.47 Million

A total of 3.47 million candidates have registered for China's 2027 national postgraduate entrance examination, the Ministry of Education announced, a slight rebound that halts three consecutive years of steep decline in what had been dubbed the country's "kaoyan fever."

The figure was disclosed at a national video conference on examination security held in mid-November, according to [Xinhua News](https://education.news.cn/20261113/2df722625f8542c5a665129c56dd1ad2/c.html), which cited ministry statistics. The exam itself is scheduled for December 19–20, 2026.

The 2027 total of 3.47 million represents an increase of roughly 40,000, or about 1.2 percent, over the 2026 cycle. It marks the first year-over-year rise since registrations peaked at 4.74 million around the 2023 cycle and then fell sharply — to 4.38 million, 3.88 million and, for 2026, 3.43 million — before edging back up this year.

## A Reversal That Surprised Forecasters

The modest uptick runs counter to expectations among many education analysts, some of whom had projected that the 2027 figure would continue sliding into the 3.2–3.35 million range. Instead, the number stabilized and ticked upward, suggesting the long retreat from postgraduate study may be leveling off.

The decline of recent years was widely interpreted as a cooling of the "kaoyan" phenomenon, driven by growing doubts about the cost-benefit calculus of a master's degree, a shrinking pool of eligible candidates, and the pull of alternatives such as civil service examinations, direct employment and study abroad. Some commentators argued the drop reflected not a loss of appetite but a shift toward a smaller, more determined cohort of candidates who prepare more deliberately.

The 2027 rebound lends weight to that view. Rather than signaling a renewed surge in demand, the stabilization may reflect a market that has found a new equilibrium after several years of rapid adjustment.

## Policy Tailwind: Expanding Postgraduate Enrollment

A key structural factor behind the numbers is official policy. The government has moved to expand postgraduate training as part of a longer-term effort to build a stronger higher-education system and supply talent in strategic fields.

A landmark policy document issued in early 2025 — the Education Powerhouse Construction Plan (2024–2035) — called for the orderly expansion of quality undergraduate enrollment and a larger postgraduate training scale, with a steady rise in the share of doctoral students and vigorous development of professional degree programs. The subsequent 15th Five-Year Plan similarly proposed expanding postgraduate training, with an emphasis on science, engineering, agriculture and medicine.

Officials have framed this as a decade-long strategy. In September 2026, a senior policy research official at the National Development and Reform Commission said the coming period would see the orderly expansion of quality undergraduate and postgraduate enrollment and support for building new research-oriented universities — elevating postgraduate expansion into a sustained national priority.

The growth of graduate programs relative to undergraduate cohorts — a phenomenon sometimes described as "benke-yan" inversion at top-tier universities — has become increasingly visible, and the policy push has reinforced it.

## Competition Remains Intense

Despite the multi-year decline in registrations, admission to postgraduate programs remains highly selective, particularly at elite institutions. Estimates for the 2026 cycle put admissions at roughly 760,000 including exemption-track candidates, with about 480,000 admitted through the unified exam — a unified-exam admission rate of only around 14 percent.

That means declining registration numbers have not necessarily translated into easier entry. For candidates targeting the most competitive universities and programs, the odds remain steep, and the rebound in registrations could intensify pressure at the margins.

## Exam Security in Focus

The registration figure was released at a national video conference devoted to examination security, underscoring the high stakes attached to the exam. Vice Minister of Education Xiong Sihao attended and spoke at the meeting, which was convened by the Ministry of Education together with member units of the national inter-ministerial joint conference on unified education examinations.

According to Xinhua, the meeting emphasized safeguarding the integrity of exam-setting, strengthening venue security and candidate screening, and cracking down on cheating. Departments involved in the deployment included the Cyberspace Administration of China, the Central Secrecy Office and State Secrecy Bureau, the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology, and the Ministry of Public Security.

The coordination reflects the elaborate security apparatus built around the exam, spanning online rumor and cheating control, protection of test papers, prevention of electronic cheating, and action against organized fraud. Officials described the goal as achieving a "safe postgraduate exam" and upholding what they called the bottom line of educational fairness.

## What to Watch

The 2027 figures will be watched closely as a bellwether for China's higher-education trajectory. If the rebound holds, it could signal that postgraduate study is regaining its appeal as a path to opportunity, reinforced by expanded enrollment capacity. If it proves a one-year blip, the broader debate over the value of advanced degrees is likely to continue.

Either way, the December exam will draw millions of candidates into one of the world's largest and most consequential academic competitions — and the numbers behind it will keep informing how China thinks about talent, fairness and the future of its workforce.
