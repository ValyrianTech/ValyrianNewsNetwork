# Microsoft Outage Drags Into Second Day Worldwide

A sprawling Microsoft outage that knocked out Outlook, Teams, the Copilot AI assistant and much of the Microsoft 365 suite stretched into a second day on Friday, disrupting businesses, courts and government services around the world as the company scrambled to restore normal service. Microsoft said it was making headway, reporting "encouraging recovery progress" as some users began to see their email return, according to [AP News](https://apnews.com/article/microsoft-outage-outlook-email-365-status-down-d92547a821930290db2964388016979a).

The disruption began just before 1 p.m. Eastern time on Thursday, November 12, and by Friday had touched everything from a halted jury trial in Britain to police crime reporting, airline customer service and even the AI platforms that increasingly connect to Microsoft's software.

## How the outage unfolded

The incident first surfaced when nearly 130,000 users reported problems accessing Outlook on the outage tracker Downdetector shortly after 1 p.m. EST Thursday. Reports fell from that peak, but many users still could not send or receive emails or load their inboxes, while thousands more reported issues with Teams, Copilot and other Microsoft 365 services.

Microsoft attributed the disruption to a number of servers that failed during automated health checks, with its fallback measures to bring them back online not taking effect as expected. Parts of its infrastructure were "experiencing elevated load," the company said. Microsoft added there was no indication the outage was caused by malicious activity.

Early on, the company acknowledged that its remedies were falling short. "Our actions taken thus far have not provided the anticipated relief," Microsoft wrote as part of a series of 22 posts on X. The Microsoft 365 Status account added that the company was "aggressively pursuing all pathways to reduce impact."

By Friday, Microsoft reported "broader improvements" in North America, "sustained improvements across much of Europe" and "encouraging recovery signals" in the Asia-Pacific region. Email queues were "significantly reduced and approaching normal levels," though the company warned some users could still face "intermittent access issues or delayed email delivery."

## A global ripple effect

The total number of affected users is not known, but the scale of the disruption reflects Microsoft's reach: the company said in January it had more than 450 million paid Microsoft 365 commercial subscriptions.

In Britain, the outage brought a halt to a trial in Manchester, where Judge Maurice Greene sent jurors home because of delays he said illustrated the danger of the court's reliance on technology. British police forces said they were unable to receive crime reports online and urged people to phone in urgent requests, while the National Pharmacy Association warned that people could face delays getting prescriptions because the outage was affecting the email systems of the state-run National Health Service. The British government said it was "working closely" with Microsoft and that "robust contingency plans" were in place.

In Norway, the government said the outage affected several ministries and had received no indication of when the problem would be resolved. Greece's Minister of Digital Governance, Dimitrios Papastergiou, said ministries using Outlook, Teams and Copilot were affected but not severely enough to significantly hamper their work.

In the Asia-Pacific region, Australia's Home Affairs Minister Tony Burke said Microsoft had likened the disruption to the 2024 CrowdStrike outage — a [faulty update](https://apnews.com/article/what-is-crowdstrike-worldwide-outage-94b4fc5ac6eed46ddcd565a5f1e4b916) to Windows computers that grounded flights and disrupted hospitals, banks and retailers — rather than a cyberattack. "They (Microsoft) don't believe they are dealing with a cyberattack," Burke told reporters in Sydney. "They believe that they are dealing in some ways (with something) similar to what we saw with respect to CrowdStrike where there is an update that has gone awry."

In Hong Kong, businessperson Danny Lau said his aluminum firm could not receive updates from its U.S. customers through Outlook and warned that a prolonged outage could "impact the production timeline." Some banks in the financial hub also saw operations disrupted. In India, Air India temporarily rerouted its email services while IndiGo reported its email support was disrupted. Singapore said government emails were down, and the Philippines' Supreme Court said some operations were affected, including email responses. Japan's Deputy Chief Cabinet Secretary, Masanao Ozaki, said the government's IT infrastructure was affected and that officials were securing alternative communication tools such as telephones.

In the United States, industrial technology company Workhorse Group delayed a quarterly earnings call. OpenAI and Anthropic also reported problems connecting with Microsoft 365 tools — a reminder of how deeply AI platforms now depend on other companies' software.

## The dependence problem

The episode drew swift commentary on the risks of concentrating so much of the world's digital life in a single provider.

"With no emails coming or going, many office workers feel like they are having a virtual snow day," said Jeremy Singer, a professor at the University of Glasgow's School of Computing Science. "This problem serves to emphasize the dependence we have built on one company's digital infrastructure, and may lead us to question whether we need more resilience in our IT ecosystem."

Papastergiou, the Greek minister, told private Skai television that the outage underscored how countries should develop technological alternatives for services considered crucial to the smooth operation of government. Germany's digitization ministry, meanwhile, said the federal government does not use Microsoft in the cloud and relies on an email client that remained stable — though spokesperson Florian Druckenthaner said the incident shows "the significance of building up redundancies and alternatives."

The outage lands amid a broader push in Western Europe for "digital sovereignty" — efforts by governments and institutions to [reduce their reliance](https://apnews.com/article/europe-digital-sovereignty-big-tech-9f5388b68a0648514cebc8d92f682060) on a handful of large U.S. tech providers.

## What to watch

Microsoft says recovery is progressing, but several questions remain unanswered: the precise root cause of the server failures, the total number of users and organizations affected, and whether regulators will open inquiries given the outage's cross-border impact. For enterprises, the episode may also renew debate over compensation and the value of building redundant systems that can keep operating when a single cloud provider falters. For now, the two-day disruption stands as one of the clearest recent demonstrations of how a single company's technical trouble can ripple across the globe in a matter of hours.
