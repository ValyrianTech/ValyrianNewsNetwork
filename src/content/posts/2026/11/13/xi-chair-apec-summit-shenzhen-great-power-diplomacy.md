# Xi to Chair APEC Summit as Great-Power Talks Loom

President Xi Jinping will chair the 33rd APEC Economic Leaders' Meeting and deliver a speech when the forum convenes in Shenzhen on November 18–19, China's Foreign Ministry announced on Friday. The summit, held under the theme "Building an Asia-Pacific Community to Prosper Together," will also see Xi host a welcome banquet for visiting leaders and hold a series of bilateral meetings on the sidelines, according to the official [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/politics/leaders/20261113/7b2c435da8314e378d00c2a8153cb6f4/c.html) announcement.

On its face, the statement is routine protocol. But it lands at an unusually charged diplomatic moment — with reports that U.S. President Donald Trump will travel to Shenzhen, and that Washington and Moscow are pressing Beijing to host a first-ever Trump–Putin–Xi trilateral meeting on the margins of the summit.

## A Host Year Built Around Openness, Innovation and Cooperation

China is the 2026 APEC host economy, and Shenzhen — one of the country's first special economic zones and now its leading foreign-trade hub — is the stage. When Beijing unveiled its agenda at the APEC Informal Senior Officials' Meeting in Shenzhen last December, it framed the year around three priorities: openness, innovation and cooperation, as the [APEC Secretariat](https://www.apec.org/press/news-releases/2025/china-unveils-apec-2026-theme-and-priorities-in-shenzhen) reported.

Deputy Foreign Minister Ma Zhaoxu, opening that meeting, warned that "rising protectionism and anti-globalization trends have created many uncertainties and challenges," and asked how the region could "strengthen policy coordination and sustain Asia-Pacific economic growth." Ma cast openness as the region's "lifeblood," noting that average APEC tariffs have fallen from around 17 percent to about 5 percent, and he pointed to 2026 as the 20th anniversary of the proposed Free Trade Area of the Asia-Pacific (FTAAP).

The host city itself is part of the message. Shenzhen's GDP has grown from just 270 million yuan in 1980 to 3.68 trillion yuan in 2024, with total trade of roughly 4.5 trillion yuan last year — figures Beijing is keen to present as proof of the dividends of opening up.

## The Economic Backdrop

APEC's own economists project the region will grow 3.4 percent in 2026, up from 3.2 percent last year, even as inflation is expected to rise from 2.4 percent to 3.2 percent. At a Singapore briefing earlier this month, APEC Secretariat Executive Director Eduardo Pedrosa said cooperation on trade, artificial intelligence and economic resilience had advanced through the year, with AI expected to feature prominently when leaders meet.

"Everything we do is for the prosperity of all our people and future generations," Pedrosa said, according to the [APEC Secretariat](https://www.apec.org/press/news-releases/2026/apec-advances-trade--ai-cooperation-ahead-of-leaders--week). Carlos Kuriyama, director of the APEC Policy Support Unit, added that "the benefits are very much going to those economies that are ready to embrace AI."

## A Summit Shadowed by Great-Power Maneuvering

The economic agenda, however, may be overshadowed by geopolitics. On November 3, Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov confirmed that "work is underway" to arrange a meeting between Trump, Russian President Vladimir Putin and Xi on the sidelines of the Shenzhen summit — a gathering that, if realized, would be the first of its kind.

"Work toward arranging such a meeting is ongoing, and hopefully it will be finalized in the next few days," Peskov said. "Of course, our Chinese friends as hosts serve as the main organizing force here."

As [Breitbart](https://www.breitbart.com/asia/2026/11/03/kremlin-says-arrangements-underway-for-trump-putin-xi-meeting-in-november/) reported, citing the Financial Times, U.S., Chinese and Russian officials have discussed the prospect, with Washington and Moscow said to be keen and attempting to persuade Beijing. China's Foreign Ministry has been more guarded, saying only that it is "in communication with relevant parties." Russia's Deputy Foreign Minister Andrey Rudenko has likewise cautioned that "no final decision has been made."

Because Beijing is the host, it controls the scheduling — and therefore holds the power to either facilitate or quietly let the trilateral idea fade. Analysts have noted the appeal for Xi of convening the American and Russian leaders, a stature-enhancing role. But they also flag Beijing's wariness: a great-power bargain struck over smaller nations' heads carries echoes of Yalta, and China has long resisted Trump's push to fold it into a trilateral nuclear-arms framework.

## What Else Is on the Table

Beyond the leaders' session, the APEC CEO Summit will run on November 17–18 under the theme "Jointly Promote Prosperity, Win the Future," with Xi attending the opening ceremony and delivering a keynote address. Leaders' Week formally begins on November 14 with senior officials' and business representatives' meetings, building toward the leaders' meeting.

Trade facilitation is expected to be a focus, with the APEC Secretariat citing the new APEC Center of Excellence for Paperless Trade and research suggesting that digitalizing trade documents could cut transaction costs by 5.8 percent. Discussions on AI governance — covering data privacy, trust and technical standards — are also expected to carry into the leaders' meeting.

## What to Watch

Three questions will define the summit's significance. First, whether Trump's attendance is formally confirmed and whether a Trump–Xi bilateral takes place — a meeting that could set the tone for the U.S.–China trade truce reportedly extended into early 2027. Second, whether the Trump–Putin–Xi trilateral actually materializes, or dissolves into diplomatic hedging. Third, what concrete deliverables emerge on FTAAP, AI governance and supply-chain resilience.

For Xi, the summit offers a carefully staged opportunity: to present China as the anchor of Asia-Pacific openness and prosperity, and to position Beijing as the indispensable convenor of great-power diplomacy — ambitions that will be tested as the world's leaders arrive in Shenzhen next week.
