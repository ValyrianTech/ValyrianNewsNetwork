# PS Enters Campaign Mode, Opens Door to N-VA

Belgium's French-speaking Socialist Party (PS) has moved into campaign mode and, for the first time in a sustained public way, signalled it would be willing to govern with the Flemish nationalist N-VA after any snap federal elections — provided the results favour the Socialists. The shift, reported by [Le Soir](https://www.lesoir.be/782426/article/2026-11-12/le-ps-se-met-en-mode-campagne-et-ouvre-la-porte-la-n-va-apres-deventuelles), comes as the federal budget crisis around Prime Minister Bart De Wever's "Arizona" coalition pushes the country toward a possible return to the polls.

## A Two-Pronged Strategy

PS president Paul Magnette is running a deliberately two-track approach. First, he is denouncing the government's budgetary messaging as "budgetary hypocrisy." Second, he is preparing his party for an election and conceding that the N-VA will be "unavoidable" on the Flemish side of any future coalition.

Magnette's opening is tightly hedged. He categorically rules out rescuing the current coalition, which he describes as being in a state of "clinical death." As he put it, making a deal with the N-VA to bail out the government now is "no" — noting the PS holds only about sixteen seats in the Chamber of Representatives.

But he accepts a different scenario after a vote. "After the elections, it's another life," Magnette said. "Within the PS, everyone has understood that the N-VA will be unavoidable after the next elections. There are no other solutions on the Flemish side. It's not with great enthusiasm, but we know how to read the numbers."

## Ready to Go to the Polls

The Socialists insist they are election-ready. "In the event of an election, are we ready? Yes, we are always ready," Magnette said, adding that "in three days, everything would be wrapped up. We have 80% of our lists and 80% of our programme."

The party's positioning coincides with signals from elsewhere in the opposition. Raoul Hedebouw, president of the far-left PTB, told RTL that his party is also prepared: "We are not afraid of elections... We are ready, but I hope in any case that an agreement will arrive in the coming weeks."

## The Deficit Blame Game

At the heart of the confrontation is who is responsible for Belgium's deteriorating public finances. Magnette argues the deficit grew by 4% — about €20 billion — under the previous centre-right "Swedish" coalition, and by more than 1% since the Arizona took office. On current projections, he says, it could rise from 2.8% to 5.4% of GDP by 2030, whereas under the previous "Vivaldi" coalition the PS improved the deficit by €14 billion.

He insists the problem is eroding revenue, not runaway spending. The recent pension increase, he argues, represents only 0.07% of GDP, while revenues have fallen by 4% of GDP over ten years — roughly €25 billion. "It is not the Socialists who are digging the deficit," he said.

## A Reversal in Framing

The PS is also turning the N-VA's own logic against it. "If the N-VA is capable of governing neither with the MR — its best friend — nor with the PS, it means it can govern with no one," Magnette said, targeting Francophone liberal leader Georges-Louis Bouchez.

The opening marks a notable shift in tone. Magnette has long argued against a PS–N-VA alliance, writing in 2020 that such a government would be "an addition of opposites that cancel each other out," citing deep divisions over economic model, confederalism and climate policy. The policy gulf remains: on state reform the PS is not seeking confederalism and wants to re-federalise certain powers such as family allowances — the opposite of the N-VA's agenda.

## Pressure on the Arizona

The PS's stance complicates any attempt to broaden or rescue the current five-party coalition of N-VA, MR, Les Engagés, CD&V and Vooruit, which holds a slim majority in the 150-seat Chamber. By framing the choice as either a budget deal inside the Arizona or early elections, Magnette sharpens the pressure on De Wever, who presented his resignation to King Philippe on 2 November; the King has held the decision in suspense and asked the prime minister to keep talking.

De Wever is scheduled to hold further bilateral meetings on Friday before a "kern" — inner cabinet — at 19:00, and hopes to deliver "a positive message" to the King on 16 November. Gaps remain between coalition partners: Vooruit wants €1 billion less in social-security savings, while the MR wants €1 billion less in taxes.

## What to Watch

Whether Belgium actually returns to the polls depends on the 16 November royal report and De Wever's ability to bridge those gaps. The PS, for its part, says it does not "seek" elections — even as it readies its lists. The cost of delay is mounting: three months of provisional twelfths could cost up to €1 billion, reinforcing Magnette's warning that "each time we lose time, we lose hundreds of millions." For now, the Socialists are hedging: pressing for good decisions from the government while quietly preparing for the campaign that could follow.
