# Indonesia Opens RMB Clearing Bank in Jakarta

Indonesia's renminbi (RMB) clearing bank officially opened on 12 November 2026 at a ceremony at Bank Indonesia, the country's central bank, in Jakarta. Bank of China serves as the designated clearing bank, a role it was formally appointed to in June 2026, according to [Xinhua](https://www1.xinhuanet.com/world/20261112/42cf493b07d548b48af7923dcc1da5f2/c.html).

About 100 representatives from Chinese and Indonesian government bodies, enterprises, and financial institutions attended the launch, which marks a significant step in China–Indonesia financial cooperation and in the broader internationalization of the Chinese currency.

## A Decade of Deepening Trade

The clearing arrangement rests on a trade relationship of unusual scale. China has been Indonesia's largest trading partner for 13 consecutive years and one of its top three investment sources for 10 consecutive years, the Chinese Embassy's Chargé d'Affaires, Zhou Kan, noted at the event. That volume has created growing demand for direct settlement in local currencies.

The two sides have built toward this point incrementally. They launched a bilateral local-currency settlement (LCS) mechanism in 2021, reached a bilateral transaction local-currency settlement framework and renewed their currency swap agreement in 2025, and in 2026 achieved cross-border QR code payment interconnection and Indonesia's successful issuance of "panda bonds" — renminbi-denominated debt sold in mainland China. The clearing bank's opening is the latest milestone in that sequence.

## A Market Ready to Trade

The infrastructure is reportedly in place. Twelve local and foreign banks — including Bank of China, ICBC, China Construction Bank, Bank Mandiri, and Bank Negara Indonesia — act as authorized cross-currency market makers and participating banks of the clearing bank, with trading systems prepared ahead of the launch.

A clearing bank provides renminbi clearing and settlement services for cross-border transactions in a given market, reducing the need to route trades through a third currency, typically the US dollar. For Indonesian exporters and importers, that can mean lower transaction costs and faster settlement when dealing with Chinese counterparties.

## 'A Historic Event'

Bank Indonesia Governor Destry Damayanti framed the opening in strategic terms, describing it as "a historic event," particularly as trade between the two countries continues to grow significantly.

"We need an Indonesian RMB foreign-exchange market, which is conducive to diversifying transactions — meaning not only the US dollar, but we can also more widely use the RMB," she said, according to [CCTV News via China.com](http://photo.china.com.cn/2026-11/13/content_118773231.shtml). She added that with the clearing bank's activation and improved local-currency mechanisms, enterprises can settle trade with China directly in RMB, helping reduce dependence on third-party currencies, maintain exchange-rate stability, and strengthen the regional economy's resilience to external shocks.

Airlangga Hartarto, Indonesia's Coordinating Minister for Economic Affairs, said China is one of Indonesia's largest sources of non-oil-and-gas imports, and that expanding settlement in currencies other than the US dollar will significantly ease the external pressure Indonesia faces.

## Bank of China's 'Four Bridges'

Zhang Hui, President of Bank of China, called the new clearing bank "a new important fulcrum in Bank of China's global RMB service network." He said the bank will build "four bridges" — spanning cross-border payments, capital financing, green finance, and financial innovation — to improve cross-border clearing efficiency and enrich the range of RMB financial products.

Around Indonesia's "Golden Indonesia 2045" vision, Zhang said Bank of China will increase financial support for mineral downstream processing, energy infrastructure, advanced manufacturing, the digital economy, and green transformation.

Liu Yue, Deputy General Manager of Bank of China's Jakarta Branch, said the branch will work with local financial institutions to build an RMB ecosystem in Indonesia, "creating more scenarios benefiting enterprises and ordinary people, and jointly promoting bilateral trade and financial exchanges."

## What to Watch

The opening extends Bank of China's RMB clearing network to 19 countries and regions. It also deepens a de-dollarization agenda that Jakarta has pursued steadily since 2021.

The immediate test will be adoption: whether Indonesian businesses shift a meaningful share of China trade into direct RMB settlement, and whether the participating banks can build the products and depth needed to make the market liquid. Broader adoption could also carry trade-balance and currency-management implications for Indonesia, which will bear watching as the arrangement matures.

For now, the clearing bank gives both countries a formal channel that did not exist a week ago — one that supporters say will lower costs and diversify risk, and that marks the clearest sign yet of the renminbi's expanding role in Southeast Asia's largest economy.
