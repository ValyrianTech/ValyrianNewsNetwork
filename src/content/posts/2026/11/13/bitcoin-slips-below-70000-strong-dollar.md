# Bitcoin Slips Below $70,000 as Strong Dollar Bites

Bitcoin fell below $70,000 on Friday for the first time since mid-August, touching a low near $69,000, as a surging US dollar, hotter-than-expected inflation and fresh bets on a Federal Reserve rate hike pulled investors away from riskier assets. Ethereum, the second-largest cryptocurrency, also dropped below $2,000. According to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/13/waarde-van-bitcoin-duikt-onder-kaap-van-70-000-dollar/), the slide reflects economic uncertainty that is steering investors toward safer holdings.

The move extends a bruising stretch for crypto. Bitcoin is now roughly 45% below its record high of $126,198 set on 6 October 2025 and down around 17% over the quarter, with a sixth consecutive weekly loss in sight.

## Why a strong dollar hurts Bitcoin

The core of the story is a simple tug-of-war over where capital goes. US inflation has kept climbing, driven in part by high energy prices, and this week's consumer price index came in higher than economists expected. That has hardened expectations that the Federal Reserve will raise interest rates again at its 8–9 December meeting.

Higher rates make safe assets such as US government bonds more attractive, because they finally pay a meaningful yield. That draws money toward the dollar and away from non-yielding assets like Bitcoin. As VRT NWS put it, high rates make it worthwhile to hold dollars in US Treasuries, and the resulting demand lifts the US currency.

The dollar index, which tracks the greenback against six major currencies, has risen above 103 points — its highest level since April 2025. As the Belgian broadcaster noted, the dollar and Bitcoin are currently moving in opposite directions: a stronger dollar tends to coincide with a weaker Bitcoin.

## A convergence of pressures

The strong dollar is only part of the picture. International crypto coverage points to several forces hitting at once. [CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/11/13/bitcoin-dips-below-usd70-000-as-selloff-gathers-pace) described the drop as a 12-week low, with the dollar rallying on the interest-rate outlook while a wave of forced liquidations amplified the fall.

More than $1.3 billion in crypto futures positions were liquidated in 24 hours, including about $677 million in Bitcoin, according to the research. Breaking the widely watched $70,000 round number triggered the sell-off of leveraged long bets — a mechanical accelerant once a key level gives way.

Flows have also turned negative. US spot Bitcoin ETFs recorded five straight days of net outflows totaling roughly $1.44 billion through 11 November. Those are client redemptions rather than the fund issuers selling their own holdings, but the trend signals a cooling appetite for the asset.

The crypto weakness sits inside a broader risk-off wave in global markets, following last week's US midterm elections and the shock resignation of Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang. The artificial-intelligence giant has shed enormous market value, and because it carries heavy weight in global indices and funds, the shock has rippled through portfolios worldwide, as [VRT NWS reported](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/12/nvidia-beurs-impact-etf-s-trackers-pensioenfondsen/).

## What's next for crypto

Attention now turns to the Fed. Markets have priced in the possibility of a 25–50 basis-point hike in December, and any further upside surprises in inflation data would reinforce the higher-for-longer rate story that has weighed on Bitcoin. [Investing.com](https://www.investing.com/news/cryptocurrency-news/bitcoin-drops-below-70k-as-crypto-appetite-sours-5024070) framed the drop as part of a deep weekly loss as crypto appetite sours.

A second date matters, too. Index provider MSCI will remove Strategy — the largest corporate holder of Bitcoin, with 850,364 coins as of 12 November — from its global indexes after the close on 30 November. The exclusion does not force Strategy to sell, but it may complicate its ability to raise fresh capital to buy more Bitcoin. Some analysts argue the change was widely expected and largely priced in; others warn it dents the institutional-adoption narrative.

For ordinary investors, the takeaway is that Bitcoin's recent fortunes are tied less to its own narrative than to the cost of holding dollars. As long as US yields stay high and the dollar stays firm, the headwind for crypto is likely to persist. The coming weeks — a Fed decision, more inflation prints and the MSCI reshuffle — will determine whether the slide is a pause or the start of a longer crypto winter.
