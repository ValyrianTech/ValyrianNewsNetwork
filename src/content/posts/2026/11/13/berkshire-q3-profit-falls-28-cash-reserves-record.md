# Berkshire Q3 Profit Falls 28% as Cash Reserves Hit Record

Berkshire Hathaway reported a 28 percent decline in third-quarter net profit on Thursday evening, a drop driven almost entirely by weaker investment returns even as the conglomerate's underlying businesses held up and it accelerated share buybacks to a single-quarter record. Net profit attributable to shareholders fell to $22.112 billion from $30.796 billion a year earlier, according to [The Paper](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_34429766), while revenue rose 11.24 percent to $105.647 billion.

The divergence between the headline figure and the company's operating performance illustrates why Warren Buffett's successor, Greg Abel, has urged investors to focus on operating profit rather than the mark-to-market swings that distort net income under U.S. accounting rules.

## Investment Losses Drive the Headline Decline

The profit drop stemmed overwhelmingly from investment results. Berkshire's investment gains fell 45.7 percent to $9.407 billion from $17.311 billion, a decline tied in large part to losses on its sizable new Alphabet position. [Finmarket](https://www.finmarket.ru/news/6745629) reported investment income falling nearly 46 percent to $11.95 billion, a difference from The Paper's figure that likely reflects distinct reporting lines for gains versus total investment income.

Operating profit, the metric Buffett has long championed as the truest gauge of the conglomerate's underlying health, slipped 5.8 percent to $12.705 billion from $13.485 billion, and the company itself cautioned against reading too much into the quarterly net figure.

"The amount of investment gains or losses in any given quarter is usually meaningless, and the resulting net income figures may be extremely misleading to investors with limited understanding of accounting rules," Berkshire said in its report, as cited by The Paper.

## A Portfolio Weighing on Results

As of September 30, five companies — Alphabet, American Express, Apple, Bank of America and Coca-Cola — accounted for 65 percent of the $297.778 billion fair value of Berkshire's equity investments, a concentration unchanged from the end of 2025.

Berkshire first built a position in Google parent Alphabet in the third quarter of 2025, immediately making it a top-ten holding. It deepened that bet in June 2026 by committing $10 billion to Alphabet's $80 billion equity raise, buying $5 billion in Class A shares at $351.81 apiece and $5 billion in Class C shares at $348.20. Alphabet's A and C shares have since fallen nearly 25 percent, turning a forward-looking AI bet into the central drag on third-quarter investment results.

## Segments Diverge as Insurance Weakens

The operating picture was mixed across Berkshire's sprawling businesses. Railroad unit BNSF posted after-tax profit of $1.729 billion, up about 19 percent year over year, while manufacturing, service and retail operating profit rose roughly 12 percent to $4.042 billion. Berkshire Hathaway Energy earned $1.612 billion, up about 8 percent from $1.489 billion.

Insurance underwriting told a different story, with profit falling about 43 percent to $1.352 billion from $2.369 billion, reflecting a harder underwriting environment. Insurance investment income also declined, to $2.734 billion from $3.181 billion.

## Buybacks Accelerate, Cash Pile Swells

Berkshire sharply ramped up repurchases of its own stock, buying back about $9.6 billion in the third quarter — a single-quarter record, according to Wind data cited by The Paper. The pace has climbed steeply through the year, from roughly $235 million in the first quarter to about $4.5 billion in the second, bringing the nine-month total to $14.4 billion.

The buyback surge sends "a clear signal that high-quality acquisition targets meeting the company's standards remain scarce in the current market — and that Berkshire itself may be the most worthwhile asset to invest in right now," The Paper noted.

At the same time, Berkshire turned modestly into a net seller of equities, offloading about $426 million in the quarter after net buying of $116.25 billion in the first half — a reversal that underscores a cautious, capital-disciplined posture. Cash and equivalents climbed to a record $377.4 billion at quarter-end, up from $365.51 billion three months earlier, and total assets grew to $1.304 trillion from $1.222 trillion at the end of 2025.

## A Defensive Haven in a Volatile Market

The cautious stance appears to have been rewarded. Berkshire's Class A shares are up nearly 13 percent year to date, outpacing the S&P 500's gain of under 10 percent, Finmarket reported. In the week of the earnings release, Berkshire rose 5 percent while the S&P 500 fell 2.68 percent amid turbulence in the AI trade — a validation of its cash-rich, low-beta profile as defensive capital sought a haven.

The gains also suggest that market anxiety over Buffett's departure is fading. Abel formally succeeded Buffett as chief executive in January 2026 and presided over his first annual shareholder meeting in May, moving to reassure investors that the conglomerate's discipline would endure.

## What to Watch

With nearly $377 billion in cash and a concentrated portfolio still heavily exposed to the AI-driven valuations of its top holdings, Berkshire faces a familiar dilemma: few attractively priced assets large enough to move the needle. Whether Abel deploys that war chest into a major acquisition — or keeps leaning on buybacks and patience — will be the defining test of the post-Buffett era.
