# John Turturro Loves a Challenge, Even Pickpocketing

John Turturro might be able to lift your wallet — it just depends on how you're dressed. In a wide-ranging interview on NPR's *Fresh Air*, the veteran actor revealed that learning to pick pockets for his latest film was part of a career-long appetite for the unfamiliar, the risky, and the morally complicated.

"I love to begin something because you never know. There's the unknown of, 'I may fail. I may not be able to do that.' And I find that exciting," Turturro told host Terry Gross in the [interview](https://www.npr.org/2026/11/02/nx-s1-6023217/john-turturro-pickpocket) published November 2, 2026.

## A Role With Pockets — and Armor

The occasion is *The Only Living Pickpocket in New York*, in which Turturro stars as Harry Lehman, an aging pickpocket who is also the primary caretaker for his catatonic wife. Harry's life takes a turn when he steals the backpack of a college-age kid who turns out to be the grandson of a crime boss.

To prepare, Turturro trained with Apollo Robbins, a famed sleight-of-hand artist who coached him on the art of misdirection. "He really helped me, gave me exercises, and worked with me," Turturro said, adding that he practiced picking pockets "in the production office and on the set all the time."

He even assessed the practicalities of the trade. "Horizontal pockets that are really tight with tight jeans are hard; back pockets are easier," he said — and even better, "if your purse or your backpack is on your back and your zipper isn't completely zipped up."

The actor said the overcoat he wears in the film helped him get into character. "It's got all the pockets. It's like a piece of armor, sort of like a samurai robe," he said.

The film, written and directed by Noah Segan, premiered at the 2026 Sundance Film Festival in January and had its international premiere out of competition at the 76th Berlin International Film Festival in February. Sony Pictures Classics released it in limited release on October 23, expanding nationwide on October 30, [according to the film's Wikipedia entry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Only_Living_Pickpocket_in_New_York). It holds a 98% Rotten Tomatoes score and a Metacritic score of 81, indicating "universal acclaim." The supporting cast includes Giancarlo Esposito, Steve Buscemi, Tatiana Maslany, Jamie Lee Curtis, and Aida Turturro, John's cousin.

## A Career of Complex Characters

Harry is far from the first morally dubious figure Turturro has played. His credits include *Do the Right Thing*, *The Big Lebowski*, *O Brother, Where Art Thou?*, and, in recent years, the television series *The Night Of*, *The Plot Against America*, and *Severance*.

His approach to those roles, he said, is deliberate and exhaustive. "I really do try to take the elevator down into the sub-basement and mine everything I possibly can because I'm a student," he said.

That instinct has deep roots. Turturro described a childhood shaped by volatility: a father who fought in World War II and carried PTSD from D-Day, and a brother with schizophrenia for whom he later served as legal guardian. "When I was younger and I played all these explosive characters and stuff, I understood the reality of that," he said. "I saw a lot of violence growing up and I was in the middle of it a bunch of times."

His brother, he recalled, once told him: "I've given you a lot of material." Turturro's reply: "You're really right — but at a high cost."

## The Racist Who Sells Pizza

Turturro also reflected on one of his signature roles — Pino, the racist pizzeria owner in Spike Lee's 1989 *Do the Right Thing*. He said he actively sought the part. "I'd rather play the racist guy because that's what it's about, the movie. And he doesn't have a Ku Klux Klan hood on him. He's just the guy who sells pizza," he said. "The thing was to expose it, in a way."

He traced his bond with Lee to their shared upbringing in New York's outer boroughs. Turturro spent his first six years in a predominantly Black neighborhood in Hollis, Queens, before moving to Rosedale. "I think that really bonded me and Spike, because he grew up in an Italian neighborhood," he said.

## Older, and Fuller of Tools

At 69, Turturro said his work has only grown richer with time. "Seventy is the new 50, I guess," he said. "You can't jump over a fence as quickly or run as fast, but you can distill things and understand what stillness is and be really precise because your toolbox is much fuller."

"And when I see *The Only Living Pickpocket*, I see a person who has lived a lot of life that way, and that's me too."

## What to Watch

The *Fresh Air* conversation arrives in the thick of an awards-season push for a film that critics have embraced as a late-career showcase for its star. Whether that translates into Oscar attention will become clearer in the coming months — but for Turturro, the appeal seems less about trophies than about the next unfamiliar challenge. As he put it, the possibility of failure is precisely what makes the work worth doing. For now, watch your back pockets.
