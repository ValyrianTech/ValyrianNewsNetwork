# Connecticut Sen. Chris Murphy Hospitalized With a Stroke

Sen. Chris Murphy, D-Conn., is in the hospital after suffering a stroke over the weekend, his office said Sunday. The 53-year-old lawmaker is awake, alert and "responding well to treatment," and his staff says doctors are optimistic he will make a full recovery.

In a statement, Murphy's chief of staff, Allison Herwitt, said the senator began experiencing numbness in his arm on Saturday afternoon while attending his son's hockey game. He was taken to a local hospital, where doctors determined he had suffered a stroke, then transferred to Johns Hopkins Hospital in Baltimore for further monitoring, according to [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/11/01/nx-s1-6025467/chris-murphy-stroke).

"The doctors are encouraged by his progress, he is awake and alert, and we are optimistic he will make a full recovery," Herwitt said.

## A Stroke at a Charged Political Moment

The episode lands just days before the Nov. 3 midterm elections, as Democrats fight for control of a closely divided Senate. Murphy is not on the ballot, but he had been one of his party's most prolific campaign surrogates, traveling the country on behalf of Democratic House and Senate candidates.

On Friday, he campaigned in Connecticut alongside Gov. Ned Lamont. A planned Sunday appearance in New Hampshire with Senate candidate Chris Pappas did not take place. Earlier in the month he had returned from a multistop Middle East tour, and in September he completed his annual roughly 70-mile "walk across Connecticut."

The news drew an outpouring of support from across the political spectrum. Sen. Richard Blumenthal, D-Conn., said he was confident he would soon be "welcoming him back to the Senate," while Sen. John Fetterman, D-Pa., wrote that his family "is rooting for Chris and his family for a full and speedy recovery," the [AP reported](https://apnews.com/article/chris-murphy-stroke-senate-connecticut-61676279143ddcc6f0de5f058fc6495a). President Donald Trump said he was "really surprised" by the news, and Republican Sens. Chuck Grassley and Pete Ricketts offered prayers.

Lamont, who had campaigned with Murphy two days earlier, called him "no stronger advocate for our democracy" and predicted he would be "walking across the state again very soon."

## A Familiar Senate Health Scare

Murphy's hospitalization is the latest in a string of health crises to hit Congress this year. Sen. Mitch McConnell, R-Ky., missed months of Senate work during a lengthy hospital stay and rehabilitation, and Rep. Tom Kean, R-N.J., was absent from the Capitol for months while being treated for depression.

For Democrats, the episode echoes 2022, when three prominent lawmakers—Sens. Ben Ray Lujan of New Mexico and Chris Van Hollen of Maryland, and then-Senate candidate John Fetterman of Pennsylvania—were all hospitalized for strokes. Fetterman now relies on adaptive technology, including live captioning, in the Senate.

Murphy, first elected to the House in 2006 and to the Senate in 2012, has built a reputation as a champion of aggressive executive-branch oversight and a leading voice on foreign policy, immigration and gun policy. He is widely seen as a possible contender for the 2028 Democratic presidential nomination.

## What to Watch

The immediate questions are clinical. Murphy's office has not disclosed the type or severity of the stroke, the treatment he received, or a recovery timeline—leaving open whether he will require extended rehabilitation or miss Senate votes in the coming weeks.

That matters because control of the chamber may hinge on a razor-thin margin. As [The New York Times](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/11/02/us/politics/senate-midterms-tie-independents-wild-cards.html) noted this week, the Senate could end up with a very narrow majority for either party—or a 50-50 tie—a scenario in which a single senator's absence or allegiance can shape the outcome of close votes.

A full recovery would likely leave Murphy's national ambitions intact. A prolonged one could complicate both his Senate duties and his political future. For now, his staff is projecting confidence, and his colleagues are waiting for him to return.
