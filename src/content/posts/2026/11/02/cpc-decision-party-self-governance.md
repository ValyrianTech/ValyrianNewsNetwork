# China's CPC Unveils Sweeping Party Discipline Decision

The Communist Party of China has published the full text of a landmark internal document setting out how it will sustain "full and rigorous Party self-governance," a program touching on ideology, organization, anti-corruption enforcement and institutional discipline. The decision was adopted on October 29 at the Fifth Plenary Session of the 20th Central Committee, which convened in Beijing from October 26 to 29, and was authorized for release by [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/politics/20261101/46b2b490109642b1a060834fdd1ea42e/c.html) on November 1, alongside General Secretary Xi Jinping's explanatory notes.

## A Party at 100 Million Members Confronts New Tests

The Decision arrives as the Party, founded in 1921 and now counting more than 100 million members, approaches its 105th anniversary and enters the period of the 15th Five-Year Plan. Its framing repeatedly invokes the "four tests" — governance, reform and opening-up, the market economy, and the external environment — and the "four dangers" of mental slackness, insufficient capability, alienation from the masses and corruption.

The document's opening captures the tone: "Full and rigorous Party governance is always on the road," it states, warning that "the longer the Party governs, the longer the period of peace and prosperity, and the more complex the environment, the more it must sharpen its sense of peril and remain vigilant in times of calm."

## How the Decision Is Structured

The Decision is organized in three blocks, a structure explained at an October 30 State Council Information Office press conference by Tang Fangyu, director of the Central Policy Research Office. Xinhua's release of [Xi Jinping's Explanation](https://www.news.cn/politics/20261101/b3a9dee44baf4f719b53067dce636e1d/c.html) of the document confirms that Xi personally headed the drafting group, formed in January 2026, with Wang Huning, Cai Qi and Li Xi serving as deputy heads.

The first block covers the importance, urgency and general requirements of sustained governance, including guiding ideology and seven guiding principles. The second, thematic block spans seven parts: strengthening Party members' character, strictly implementing democratic centralism, forging a high-caliber cadre corps, building an integrated organizational system, improving conduct and tightening discipline, advancing the "three nots" against corruption, and institutionalizing rule-based Party governance. The concluding block addresses the Party and state supervision system and the implementation of political responsibility.

## Anti-Corruption at the Core

Anti-corruption remains central. Zhang Fuhai, a deputy secretary of the Central Commission for Discipline Inspection, told the October 30 briefing that discipline inspection organs have filed a cumulative 8.105 million cases and disciplined 7.922 million people since the 18th Party Congress. In the first half of 2026 alone, 538,000 cases were filed and 412,000 people disciplined, including 74 officials at provincial-ministerial level or above.

The Decision leaves no doubt about its reach, declaring that there will be no "iron-cap princes" or "imperial immunity certificates" — a pointed rejection of any suggestion that senior figures stand above enforcement. It also targets what officials call "new and hidden forms of corruption," including so-called white-glove arrangements, revolving-door hiring, deferred benefits and digital-asset schemes, and calls for big-data supervision platforms to track the exercise of public power.

## Ideology, Emerging Fields and Political Loyalty

Beyond enforcement, the document presses on ideological discipline. It explicitly bans the "seven kinds of misconduct" — including faction-building, nepotism, rumor-spreading and vote-buying — and requires the rooting out of "two-faced people" who profess loyalty while acting otherwise.

Hu Heping, a deputy head of the Central Propaganda Department, described Party character as the first strategic task, while Huang Jianfa of the Central Organization Department pointed to building Party organizations in emerging fields — new economic organizations, new social organizations and new employment groups such as platform and gig workers. The emphasis reflects an effort to extend Party structures into sectors that have grown faster than traditional organization.

## Reaction from Hong Kong and Macau

Officials in the two special administrative regions responded within days. Hong Kong Chief Executive John Lee tied the plenum to the city's first Five-Year Plan and outcome-based governance, while Macau Chief Executive Sam Hou-fai linked it to civil-service reform, administrative streamlining and integrity supervision, according to [China Review News](https://bj.crntt.com/doc/1072/4/9/3/107249345.html).

The two responses differed in emphasis — governance effectiveness in Hong Kong, public-administration reform in Macau — but both framed alignment with the national 15th Five-Year Plan as the common thread.

## What to Watch

Provincial and ministerial officials quoted in [People's Daily](https://paper.people.com.cn/rmrb/pc/content/202610/31/content_30188628.html) uniformly praised the document, with Jiangxi Party Secretary Yin Hong saying that "without full and rigorous Party governance, there would be no good political atmosphere today." Village-level voices offered a ground-level note: Zhong Baijun, a village Party secretary in Jiangsu, said governance "must investigate major cases and major cases of wrongdoing, but also manage grassroots micro-power."

The Decision formalizes what officials describe as Xi Jinping Party-building Thought into a programmatic text. Its test will be implementation — whether the new supervision platforms, loyalty appraisals and expanding organizational reach translate into the discipline the document demands, as the Party enters the 15th Five-Year Plan period and prepares to mark 105 years since its founding.
