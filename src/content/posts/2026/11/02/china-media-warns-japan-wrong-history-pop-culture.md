# China Media Warns of Japan 'Wrong History' in Pop Culture

Chinese state media on November 2 issued a high-profile warning that what it calls Japan's "wrong historical views" have extensively infiltrated popular culture products, urging vigilance against distorted historical narratives spread through anime, manga, games, and television drama. The commentary, carried by [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/world/20261102/911e9d0251a446b6bed5233a64afb1f1/c.html) and originating from the *People's Daily*, marks an escalation in the cultural dimension of already strained China-Japan relations.

## Context

The article is part of a broader wave of Chinese state media criticism of Japan under the government of Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi. It links cultural output to political history, arguing that Japan is systematically downplaying and distorting its wartime aggression against Asian countries. The immediate trigger was the second season of the Japanese TBS network drama *VIVANT*, which Chinese critics allege deliberately smears China and fabricates a false historical narrative.

According to [china.com](https://news.china.com/socialgd/10000169/20261025/49816191.html), the drama depicts Chinese personnel subjecting Japanese intelligence agents to finger-cutting and abdomen-piercing, methods that critics say closely resemble the torture used by the Japanese invading army against Chinese resistance fighters. The same account says the show attributes these acts to modern Chinese people while glorifying Japanese Self-Defense Forces intelligence operatives as righteous responders.

## Key Developments

The *People's Daily* commentary argues that Japan's "wrong historical views" appear both in overt denials of history and in subtler forms of "selective narrative and symbolic entertainment." It points to a range of cultural works. It cites the right-wing manga artist Yoshinori Kobayashi's *On War*, which it says denies the existence of the "comfort women" system, and the 2018 novel *Opening a Second Life in Another World*, whose protagonist is a Japanese soldier who killed thousands in China — a planned anime adaptation was reportedly canceled after voice actors refused to participate.

The article also references the *Kantai Collection* franchise, which anthropomorphizes old Japanese navy warships as girls, and *GATE*, which it says portrays the Self-Defense Forces as a force for justice. It notes that the manga *My Hero Academia* drew protests in China and Korea over a character name, "Shiga Maruta," which it describes as a reference to Unit 731's insulting term for human experiment victims.

More recent incidents cited include a planned Pokémon card-teaching event at a venue associated with the Yasukuni Shrine in early 2026, and a streetwear brand collaboration scheduled for September 18 — the anniversary of the 1931 Mukden Incident. Both were subsequently halted after criticism. The article also notes that Takaichi mentioned *VIVANT* in the Diet and said she intended to watch it, which critics argue amplified the drama's perceived negative impact.

## Analysis

The commentary quotes several Japanese academics and civic figures. Scholar Yoshida Yutaka is cited saying that culture plays a major role in shaping Japanese historical awareness, and that while anime can deepen understanding of real war history, it requires solid factual knowledge and a reflective outlook — otherwise bias easily arises. He warns that when warships, warplanes, and sabers are turned into collectible, trainable entertainment characters, the political context and human catastrophe of war are stripped away.

Meiji University professor Yamada Akira is quoted calling on the Japanese government to face its wartime responsibility. "If Japan cannot face and narrate the history of the harm it inflicted on Asian countries, Japan's claims about peace will find it hard to win the world's trust," he said. Takahari Akiyoshi, of a Kanagawa history education group, warned against feeding children a one-sided historical understanding that could have a long-term impact on the younger generation.

The commentary also highlights Japan's long-running "Cool Japan" soft-power strategy, which promotes anime and games abroad, and notes cooperation between the Self-Defense Forces and military-themed creators — warning that war memory risks becoming further "entertainmentized" and "de-historicized." It argues that Japanese society retains a "victim memory" tendency, focusing on the Tokyo firebombing, the atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki, and the Battle of Okinawa, while lacking full presentation of the Nanjing Massacre, the comfort women system, and Unit 731.

It is worth noting that the article reflects a strongly opinionated perspective aligned with the Chinese government. Several specific allegations — including claims about government spending on AI tools and "algorithm poisoning" of historical content — originate in Chinese media and have not been independently verified.

## What's Next

The article concludes with a call for the international community to resist what it describes as Japan's use of cultural products to spread a distorted historical view. Its publication signals that Beijing intends to keep cultural and historical grievances high on the bilateral agenda. How Tokyo responds — and whether the dispute remains confined to media commentary or escalates into formal diplomatic action — will be a key indicator to watch in the weeks ahead.
