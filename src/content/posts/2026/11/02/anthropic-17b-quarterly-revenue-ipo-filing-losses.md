# Anthropic posts $17B quarterly revenue but losses continue

SAN FRANCISCO — Anthropic's quarterly revenue swelled to $17 billion, but the artificial intelligence giant still posted a net loss for the latest three months, according to [AP News](https://apnews.com/article/anthropic-ipo-revenue-ai-claude-0cbd1a649a4cce904be59af8f6b30179). The figures appeared in an amended IPO filing released Monday, roughly a week after the maker of the Claude chatbot issued its original prospectus ahead of a blockbuster Wall Street debut.

The updated filing offered a rare, detailed look at the finances of one of the leading frontier AI developers — and it laid bare the central tension facing investors. Revenue is growing at a furious pace, but profits remain elusive, and the company has committed to spending an eye-watering sum to keep its models running.

## A $900 billion computing bill

Anthropic disclosed that it has committed to spend approximately $895.4 billion on AI computing and infrastructure such as data centers, [according to the amended S-1 filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130357/000162828026068676/anthropicpbc-sx1ax1.htm) submitted to the Securities and Exchange Commission. That total is almost seven times the roughly $132 billion in cash and short-term investments the company held at the end of September.

About $70 billion of those commitments come due within the next 12 months, and roughly $450 billion are expected to be incurred by the end of 2030. The filing marks the first time Anthropic has put a full dollar figure on its computing obligations.

[Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/article/anthropics-new-ipo-filing-puts-its-computing-bill-near-900-billion-151830444.html) reported that Broadcom tops the list of counterparties with about $161 billion in equipment leases, followed by Alphabet's Google at roughly $121 billion, SpaceX's AI unit at up to $120.5 billion and Amazon at $110 billion. Anthropic also plans an additional $125 billion to $150 billion of Google chips and expects to spend more than $20 billion with AMD — neither of which counts toward the headline total because neither carries a fixed commitment.

## Revenue is soaring; so are the losses

On the top line, the growth is striking. Anthropic's revenue grew 13-fold to $33.2 billion for the first nine months of the year. But it reported a net loss of $104 billion over that period, including a $94 billion charge for an accounting adjustment tied to financing agreements.

On a quarterly basis, the company lost $23.3 billion on revenue of $17 billion. A closer look reveals a more nuanced picture: much of the net loss stems from non-cash fair-value adjustments on convertible instruments rather than day-to-day operations. The company actually swung to a $974 million operating profit in the second quarter — its first ever — before slipping back to a $567 million operating loss in the third.

That reversal partly reflects a capacity crunch. Anthropic books most of its computing 12 to 18 months ahead. When demand outran that locked-in capacity last quarter, it had to buy $5 billion of short-term computing that "currently costs significantly more" than its long-term deals. A $2 billion non-cash charge tied to a donation-matching program added to the loss.

## The wider AI money question

The filing lands amid intense market anxiety about the financing needs of the AI sector. Anthropic, OpenAI and SpaceX's AI division all spend more than they earn, and several tech companies have reportedly pulled IPO plans recently. Anthropic is rushing to public markets to raise cash and diversify its financial base rather than wait until 2027 as OpenAI plans.

"For the hyperscalers, Anthropic is at once a customer, competitor, sales channel and investment," Evercore ISI analyst Mark Mahaney wrote after the first filing, capturing the interlocking web of relationships that now defines the AI economy.

The company has also acknowledged that between 5% and 7% of its first-nine-month revenue came from customers who were breaking usage policies or commercial terms. It stepped up enforcement in August and September and warned the crackdown "may reduce our overall revenue and growth rates in future periods."

## What's next

Anthropic has said it will list on the Nasdaq Global Select Market under the ticker symbol "ANT," with Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley as lead underwriters. The filing gave no indication of how much the company ultimately expects to raise or at what price shares will be offered, though reports have pointed to a listing later this month and a valuation that could approach $1.5 trillion.

The debut will serve as a public stress test of whether frontier-AI economics can sustain trillion-dollar valuations — and its outcome is likely to shape the IPO prospects of rivals across the sector. For now, the numbers tell a simple story with a complicated ending: demand for Anthropic's technology is booming, but turning that boom into sustainable profit remains an open question.
