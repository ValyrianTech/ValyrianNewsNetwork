# Xingtai Party Secretary Yang Meng Under Investigation

Yang Meng, the Party Secretary of Xingtai City in northern China's Hebei Province, has been placed under disciplinary review and supervisory investigation for suspected serious violations of discipline and law, [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/legal/20261102/923046da00d64c38bb7eb4bad9668837/c.html) reported on November 2, 2026, citing the Hebei Provincial Commission for Discipline Inspection and Supervision.

The one-line announcement, republished from the website of the Central Commission for Discipline Inspection and State Supervisory Commission (CCDI/NSC), is the standard formulation Chinese authorities use to signal a case serious enough to likely result in expulsion from the Party and possible criminal referral. The notice did not specify the nature of the alleged violations.

## Why a City Party Secretary Matters

Xingtai is a prefecture-level city of roughly seven million residents, located about 400 kilometers south of Beijing in Hebei's mountainous south. Its Party secretary is the top political authority in the locality, overseeing economic policy, personnel appointments, and public security.

The investigation of a sitting municipal Party secretary is a notable escalation, indicating the case has drawn provincial-level disciplinary attention rather than being handled at the city level. Hebei has seen sustained anti-corruption activity across prefecture- and county-level officialdom in recent years.

## A Rapid Rise Through Provincial Ranks

Yang, born in January 1974 and a 1998 graduate of Hebei Medical University, built his career largely within the province. He served early on in Chengde city, holding posts including district mayor of Shuangluan and county Party secretary of Luanping, before moving to Baoding as vice mayor in 2013. He later worked in the provincial poverty-alleviation and rural revitalization apparatus.

In July 2021, Yang was appointed director of the Hebei Provincial Health Commission, a role of considerable scrutiny during the COVID-19 pandemic, and later also served as its Party secretary. He was named Xingtai Party Secretary in late February 2023, ending a roughly nine-month vacancy left after his predecessor, Qian Sanxiong, [left the post](https://m.bjnews.com.cn/detail/1677389706169718.html) in May 2022 to become vice governor of Anhui Province and head of its public security department.

## Part of a Broader Campaign

The investigation fits a long-running anti-corruption drive that has intensified in scope, touching civilian and military ranks alike. The phrasing "suspected of serious violations of discipline and law" is standard CCDI language and typically precedes a formal finding and disciplinary action.

In 2026 the campaign has generated a stream of high-profile cases, from provincial- and ministerial-level officials to reports of military purges. Each municipal-level case serves as a signal to local officialdom that oversight reaches down to the prefecture level, even as the most senior figures remain the campaign's most visible targets.

For Xingtai, the immediate practical question is who will assume the Party secretary's responsibilities. Provincial authorities have not yet announced a successor or interim arrangement, and the case's specifics—beyond the boilerplate accusation—remain undisclosed, as is customary at this stage in Chinese disciplinary proceedings.

## What to Watch

Attention now turns to whether the Hebei commission issues further details, whether the case extends to other officials in Xingtai or to Yang's earlier posts in Chengde and the provincial health commission, and how quickly a replacement is named. Those signals will indicate the case's scope and the credibility of the campaign's reach into local governance.
