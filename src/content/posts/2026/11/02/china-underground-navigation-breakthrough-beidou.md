# China Unveils Underground Navigation With 0.2m Accuracy

China has achieved a breakthrough in underground navigation, with a BeiDou-based positioning system that locates personnel to better than 0.2 meters where satellite signals cannot reach. The state-backed project, named Xingyuan, passed formal acceptance by the Ministry of Science and Technology, [Xinhua reported](https://www.news.cn/tech/20261102/16035b9293fe414abdaa4ef662cbaa6c/c.html), republishing a report from People's Daily.

## What the system does

The project — formally titled "Underground Large-Space High-Precision Positioning, Navigation and Control Technology" — was led by the National Key Laboratory of Integrated Spatio-Temporal Networks and Equipment Technology at the Network Communication Research Institute of China Electronics Technology Group (CETC).

Rather than relying on satellites that cannot penetrate rock, soil, concrete and metal, the system deploys BeiDou micro-base-stations underground to radiate positioning signals. This allows ordinary smartphones — with no special hardware — to navigate underground and provides seamless positioning above and below ground. Measured performance shows personnel static positioning better than 0.2 meters, while high-speed unmanned vehicles achieve stable sub-meter navigation.

According to Wei Baoguo, the laboratory's director and project lead, satellite navigation signals "cannot penetrate thick rock, concrete and metal structures," and underground spaces present narrow tunnels, structural occlusion and dense metal equipment that create an extreme electromagnetic environment. The team's approach breaks through intelligent network deployment and seamless above-ground/below-ground positioning, he said.

## Engineering firsts behind the accuracy

The team's core innovations address why underground signals are hard to "see" and measure, Wei said. Traditional signal modeling is "like guessing weather by looking at a calendar," he explained — it summarizes historical patterns but cannot keep up when structures, vehicles and people change a signal's propagation.

The researchers instead proposed a digital-twin channel modeling method combining real-time data collection with offline model optimization — analogous to modern weather forecasting, where front-end sensors track change and a back-end forecasting center recalibrates. The core artifact is an "underground wireless environment map" that makes invisible signals observable, predictable and simulatable.

To ensure reliability, the team designed a three-tier "map, network, terminal" elastic positioning standard that Pan Shuguo, the navigation control topic lead, described as "triple insurance" — if one positioning method fails in harsh conditions, others compensate.

## A self-reliant chip

Before the project launched in 2022, China's ultra-wideband (UWB) positioning chips relied on imports, with core technology and standards controlled abroad — a supply-chain and security concern. The team proposed an original HNav-UWB signal standard, shifting from a "communication-first, navigation-secondary" design to "navigation-enhanced, communication-coordinated."

Compared with international common standards, the team claims gains of 28% in coverage range, 6% in ranging accuracy and more than 65% in single-station capacity. Built on that standard is "Tanjie 100," China's first fully self-controllable pulse positioning chip, with ranging accuracy better than 10 centimeters and angle accuracy better than 3 degrees. The team describes its dual-mode customizable UWB as an international first.

## From parking lots to coal mines

Real-world deployments are already underway. In the Xiong'an New Area, the Xingyuan app guides drivers from the surface directly to a specific underground parking space across levels, recommends alternatives if a spot is occupied, and helps pedestrians "find my car." In Inner Mongolia's Menkeqing coal mine in Ordos, miners carry smartphones that show their tunnel, level and height; positioning holds up even in metal-dense workfaces, and miners can hail unmanned shuttle vehicles by phone.

Looking ahead, Wei said parking spaces will become "identifiable, searchable, navigable, dispatchable digital traffic nodes," supporting autonomous parking, underground logistics, robot operations and emergency evacuation. The team aims to expand into urban utility tunnels, subway tunnels, deep mines and underground civil air defense works.

## Caveats and what's next

The performance figures are project and company claims reported by state media and have not been independently verified; the Ministry of Science and Technology acceptance itself was not independently confirmed. Still, the direction is significant: extending BeiDou coverage below ground mirrors China's broader "BeiDou+" push and its drive for technological self-reliance. Wei said the team will continue refining core technology and equipment, aiming to establish a global "Chinese standard" for intelligent underground development.
