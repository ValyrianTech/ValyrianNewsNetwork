# Elia Plans 38km Underground Power Line in Limburg

Belgian high-voltage grid operator Elia is planning a new 38-kilometre underground power connection between Brustem in Sint-Truiden and Herderen in Riemst, in the Haspengouw region of Limburg province. The project, [reported by VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/02/nieuwe-ondergrondse-hoogspanningsverbinding-van-38-kilometer-tus/), will strengthen the regional electricity grid and, in time, replace an aging overhead line.

## What Is Being Built

The new 150,000-volt (150 kV) cable will run from Brustem through Borgloon and Tongeren to Herderen, a route of roughly 38 kilometres. Construction is scheduled to begin in early 2028, with the connection expected to be operational by 2032. Once the new line is in service, the existing overhead connection between Sint-Truiden and Ans can be dismantled. The current 70 kV line is nearing the end of its service life.

Elia says the new cable is needed to accommodate the energy transition. "That is essentially to strengthen the electricity grid in the region," Sarah Maes of Elia told VRT NWS. "Due to the energy transition, more and more electricity is being used, by both households and businesses. And we are adapting our high-voltage grid to that."

## Parallel Upgrades Across the Region

The underground line is only part of a broader overhaul of the Haspengouw grid. The overhead connection between Brustem, Alken and Godsheide in Hasselt currently carries partly 150 kV and partly 70 kV. It will be upgraded to 150 kV along its full length, doubling transport capacity toward Hasselt.

"The line gets an upgrade and becomes a 150,000-volt line over its entire length," Maes said. "That doubles the transport capacity toward Hasselt."

In addition, part of the overhead line between Sint-Truiden and Brustem will be removed, the Sint-Truiden high-voltage station will be dismantled, and the stations at Brustem, Alken, Godsheide, Tongeren and Borgloon will be reinforced.

## Why Underground Here, but Not at Ventilus

Perhaps the most notable element of the project is Elia's choice to bury the cable, a contrast with its approach on the controversial Ventilus project in West Flanders. Ventilus, an 82-kilometre connection that will bring offshore wind power inland, has been partly overhead — a decision that [triggered years of debate and compensation disputes](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/10/16/bedrijven-gecompenseerd-elia-compensatie-40-bedrijven-west-vlaan/). Earlier this month, Elia said roughly 40 West Flemish businesses could receive compensation for disruption caused by that overhead line, at an estimated total cost of 70 to 100 million euros.

According to Elia, the situation in Haspengouw is different for a technical reason. "Because this is a 150,000-volt connection and not a 380,000-volt connection like Ventilus, underground construction is technically possible here," Maes said. Ventilus operates at 380 kV, a far higher voltage at which burying cables was deemed infeasible.

## Analysis: A Template for a Different Kind of Grid

The Sint-Truiden–Riemst project fits a consistent Elia strategy of replacing aging 70 kV overhead lines with new 150 kV underground cables, a pattern seen in comparable projects such as Tienen–Kersbeek–Diest (scheduled for 2026–2029) and Aalst–Zottegem. By burying lower-voltage lines, Elia can reduce visual impact, minimise disruption to agriculture and landscape, and avoid the kind of public backlash that has surrounded Ventilus.

The distinction matters politically as well as technically. Ventilus became a years-long battleground over the visual and health impacts of overhead high-voltage lines, with residents, mayors and business groups pressing for compensation. If the Haspengouw approach succeeds, it may strengthen the case for undergrounding similar lower-voltage connections elsewhere — while leaving the hardest, highest-voltage projects in the overhead-versus-underground debate.

## What to Watch

The preparatory works are set to begin in Brustem, with full construction starting in early 2028 and completion targeted for 2032. Several questions remain: how the route will be permitted across the many land parcels and zoning plans involved, and whether the technical feasibility Elia cites for lower-voltage underground cables will hold up as the regional grid is progressively renewed. For now, Elia frames the project as a practical response to rising demand. The coming years will show whether it also becomes a model for the rest of Belgium's high-voltage network.
