# Global Panda Partner Conference Opens in Chengdu

The 2026 Global Panda Partner Conference opened on November 2 in Chengdu, Sichuan Province, drawing several hundred guests from more than 30 countries and regions under the theme "Panda Promise, World Together." Running through November 4 at the Chengdu Tianfu International Conference Center on the banks of Qinhuang Lake, the gathering is framed around three sub-themes: protecting the panda homeland, building a beautiful world, and sharing open opportunities, according to [Xinhua's Economic Information Daily](https://www.news.cn/fortune/20261102/6df3d62ad5b14005a4ea18730b997b7b/c.html).

## A Growing Circle of Partners

Now in its third edition, the conference has been held annually since 2024 and has become a platform for panda diplomacy and ecological messaging. This year's attendees include international organization representatives, foreign diplomats, local government officials, conservation experts, zoo directors, and green-development enterprises — a deliberately broad coalition that organizers describe as an "ecological promise that transcends national borders."

The event follows a "1+N+X" structure: one main forum, multiple parallel sessions, and a slate of supporting activities. At the main forum, Xinhua's national high-end think tank is releasing a bilingual Chinese-English report on ecological civilization, intended to lay out China's approach to biodiversity protection for an international audience.

## Conservation Milestones in Focus

The conference coincides with the fifth anniversary of the Giant Panda National Park, established in 2021 across Sichuan, Shaanxi, and Gansu provinces, making the park's construction achievements a centerpiece of this year's program. The Sichuan section covers roughly 19,300 square kilometers — nearly 90% of the park's total area — and hosts 1,227 wild pandas, per the Sichuan Forestry and Grassland Administration.

Official figures cited by Xinhua trace a steady recovery: the wild panda population has grown from about 1,100 in the 1980s to roughly 1,900 today, while China has conducted international conservation research with 26 institutions across 20 countries since the 1990s. The IUCN has downgraded the species from "Endangered" to "Vulnerable."

Over the past five years, Sichuan reports it has restored more than 340,000 mu (about 22,700 hectares) of panda habitat, exited 200 mining rights, and shut down 270 of 281 small hydropower stations in the park area. The province also reports the discovery of 14 new plant species and two new amphibian or reptile species, alongside recovering populations of snow leopards and golden snub-nosed monkeys.

## Parallel Sessions and Cultural Exchange

For the first time, parallel meetings are being held in Ya'an, home to the China Conservation and Research Center for the Giant Panda's Ya'an base and Baoxing, the site of the panda's first scientific discovery. Sessions include a dialogue on cooperation for protecting endangered species such as the giant panda, and a forum linking panda and golden monkey conservation with cultural exchange.

Other tracks focus on green development, brand strategy, and cultural tourism. A "Digital-Intelligent Transformation and Green Development Cooperation Conference" addresses low-carbon industry and ecological value conversion, while a Chinese and foreign cultural tourism exchange uses AI-assisted scenario performance to trace a "Su Dongpo" travel narrative linking Leshan, Zigong, and Shijiazhuang. Supporting events include a panda cultural-creative market, an international licensing exhibition, and a photo studio.

A new conference mascot, "Qiuqiu" — a panda in a small red hat and blue overalls — debuts this year in collaboration with the Liangshan "Niuniu Choir." The [agenda](http://www.sc.xinhuanet.com/20261102/82fe125f49184df0a1c820c97bef8ab4/c.html) lays out the full "1+N+X" schedule of forums and side events.

## Analysis: Soft Power Wrapped in Fur

The panda has long served as a vehicle for Chinese cultural diplomacy, from Olympic mascots like Bing Dwen Dwen and Jingjing to the globe-trotting animals themselves. This conference extends that tradition into the ecological arena, positioning conservation achievements as evidence of a broader "ecological civilization" philosophy.

It is worth noting that all primary coverage of the event comes from Xinhua and its affiliated outlets, presenting an official framing that emphasizes conservation gains. The event functions as much as a soft-power showcase as a scientific forum — a dynamic familiar to observers of panda diplomacy, where charismatic wildlife and international goodwill are carefully intertwined.

## What's Next

The main forum convenes on the morning of November 3, when the think tank report is expected to be formally released. With the national park entering its second five-year phase, attention will turn to whether the restoration and species-recovery trends reported this week can be sustained — and whether the conference's international partnerships translate into deeper, verifiable cooperation on endangered-species protection.
