# Trump Asks Justices to Sideline 20,000 California Ballots

WASHINGTON — The Trump administration asked the U.S. Supreme Court on Monday to set aside roughly 20,000 disputed California mail ballots, an emergency appeal filed the day before pivotal midterm elections that thrusts the justices back into the center of a bitter fight over voting and immigration, [the Associated Press reported](https://apnews.com/article/midterm-elections-immigration-voter-fraud-trump-48998968a23a4af2f59eaea3dba4d2f3).

The appeal came hours after a divided federal appeals court declined to grant the request, and after a district judge forcefully rejected the Justice Department's demand. The federal government argues the ballots, cast by people it suspects may not be U.S. citizens, should not be counted until voters' status can be verified. California officials counter that the government's list is highly unreliable, likely includes many U.S. citizens, and cannot realistically be segregated with voting already well underway.

"Once California commingles the challenged ballots with lawful ones, there is no way to get the challenged ballots back," Solicitor General D. John Sauer wrote in the filing.

## How the case reached the high court

The administration sued California election officials in mid-October, accusing them of failing to meet a 48-hour deadline to review a list of 20,000 names delivered on a password-protected thumb drive, [according to AP](https://apnews.com/article/california-justice-department-voting-noncitizens-lawsuit-12c62cf12a7db6480c60975b6dea49b8). The list was sent to Los Angeles County Registrar Dean Logan, though state officials said it contained names from across California — even though Logan has no jurisdiction outside his county.

A district judge, David O. Carter, refused the request, warning that segregating ballots would "wreak havoc" on the election and erode public trust. "If the Executive can discount votes and threaten an ongoing election due to unproven allegations, then our democracy is in danger," Carter wrote.

A 2-1 panel of the 9th U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals then declined to intervene, ruling it was premature for the appeals court to act and that the federal government had moved too late, [AP reported](https://apnews.com/article/california-noncitizen-voting-justice-department-lawsuit-ed14390afb8af8fde1c2d0f6a90a0761). Writing for the majority, Circuit Judge Gabriel Sanchez said setting aside ballots at this stage "would transform California's election rules for an election that is already underway" and "usher in electoral chaos."

Sanchez added that the urgency the government cited was at least partly "the product of the United States's own decisions" to file so close to the election, after votes had begun to be processed. Circuit Judge Patrick Bumatay, appointed by Trump, dissented, arguing that federal law precludes "California from commingling ballots likely cast by aliens with legitimate ballots" and that "stopping that practice ... is the bare minimum."

## A list whose accuracy is sharply contested

At the heart of the fight is a federal list whose reliability California officials and election experts have repeatedly questioned. A review of 200 records by Los Angeles County staffers found the sample included canceled or inactive registrations, individuals with no voting history, and voters who appeared to have been born in the United States.

The numbers the administration has cited have also shifted dramatically. Over the summer, the Department of Homeland Security claimed California had 190,000 noncitizens on its voter rolls but declined to provide its methodology. By October, the Justice Department's list had shrunk to 20,000. "What happened to those 170,000? They just got those wrong," California Attorney General Rob Bonta said.

Secretary of State Shirley Weber noted a parallel episode in Nevada, where the federal government initially flagged nearly 16,000 possible noncitizens but the state determined all were U.S. citizens. She called the recent demands "an emergency of the DOJ's creation" and said the government was "demanding unprecedented measures that threaten thousands of Californians' right to vote, based on unverifiable information or methodology that you still fail to explain or provide."

David Becker, who heads the Center for Election Innovation and Research, called the Justice Department's lawsuit "fairly frivolous" and questioned its timing. "If they were seeking to work in good faith to clean up the voter list that they believed had issues, they would have done this months ago in a much different fashion," he said. "However, it does raise questions as to whether or not the sole purpose of this is to set the stage for false claims about elections."

## Logistical and constitutional stakes

California mails a ballot to all of its roughly 23 million registered voters, and voting has been underway since early October. Hundreds of thousands of ballots have already been processed. State officials say locating and segregating the challenged ballots now would be logistically impossible; Los Angeles's attorney argued that checking even a fraction of the expected mail ballots against the list, at roughly three minutes apiece, would consume more than a decade of staff time.

The dispute also turns on constitutional questions. Judges have weighed whether the federal government can compel state election officials to enforce federal law — a Tenth Amendment "anti-commandeering" concern — and whether the Elections Clause gives state legislatures authority over the manner of federal elections. Bonta further accused the administration of violating a federal law barring changes to voter rolls within 90 days of an election.

Both state and federal law prohibit noncitizens from voting, a practice election experts say is exceedingly rare. The Justice Department has charged about 90 people for election misconduct this year, including 50 for alleged noncitizen voting — against more than 227 million registered voters nationally in 2024. The administration has pursued parallel demands in Washington state and in a case involving Alaska's Senate race, and has sent subpoenas to local California election officials.

## What happens next

The emergency application now goes to Justice Elena Kagan, who by default handles emergency matters arising from California, [The Hill reported](https://thehill.com/regulation/court-battles/6189114-california-mail-ballot-lawsuit-supreme-court/). No response deadline had been announced; Kagan could act on the request alone or refer it to the full Court.

The justices have previously rejected the president's push to restrict mail ballots ahead of the midterms, though the administration has won a series of other emergency appeals in Trump's second term. The underlying case returns to a federal district judge in Southern California, who was set to hear arguments on Election Day itself.

Bonta said he had "strong reason" to believe his office would win again. "This latest move reeks of desperation and is another attempt to undermine our elections and trample on the rule of law," he said. "If the U.S. Supreme Court calls for a response, we will be ready to defend the fundamental right to vote."

With a handful of competitive California congressional races potentially deciding control of the U.S. House, the outcome of the ballot fight — and the speed of any Supreme Court action — could shape not only whose votes are counted, but how millions of Americans view the integrity of the result.
