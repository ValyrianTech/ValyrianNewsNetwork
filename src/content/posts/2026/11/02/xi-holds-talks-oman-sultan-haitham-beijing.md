# Xi Holds Talks With Oman's Sultan Haitham in Beijing

Chinese President Xi Jinping held talks with Sultan Haitham bin Tarik of Oman at the Great Hall of the People in Beijing on November 2, marking the first visit to China by an Omani head of state in the 48 years since diplomatic relations were established between the two countries in 1978. According to [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/politics/leaders/20261102/067a7410c0f9477ba63f6d5d86b6c455/c.html), the meeting was expected to cover bilateral relations, economic cooperation and regional issues of mutual interest, with both sides framing the two-day state visit as a milestone for elevating their strategic partnership.

The visit, made at Xi's invitation, is also the first to China by Sultan Haitham since he ascended the throne in January 2020. Chinese Foreign Ministry spokesperson Guo Jiakun described the occasion as highly significant, noting at an October 30 press briefing that it is "the first visit by an Omani head of state to China since the establishment of diplomatic relations 48 years ago; it is of great significance for promoting the development of bilateral relations."

## A Relationship Anchored in Trade

The talks cap decades of steadily deepening ties. China has been Oman's primary trading partner since 2018, the same year the two countries elevated their relationship to a strategic partnership. Oman, for its part, is also China's fifth-largest supplier of crude oil and its fourth-largest trading partner among Arab countries, as [Anadolu Agency](https://www.aa.com.tr/en/asia-pacific/omans-sultan-to-visit-china/4106742) reported, with bilateral trade reaching $36.73 billion in 2024.

The Sultan arrived in Beijing on the evening of November 1 and was received at Beijing Capital International Airport by Pan Gongsheng, governor of the People's Bank of China, along with senior Chinese officials — a notably finance-focused reception that analysts read as a signal of the visit's economic emphasis. The delegation accompanying the Sultan was unusually heavy with economic, investment, transport and finance officials, according to [ONA](https://omannews.gov.om/topics/en/102/show/131664/), underscoring that investment and commercial agreements were a core focus of the trip.

Earlier on November 2, Xi presided over an official welcome ceremony for the Sultan at the Great Hall of the People, [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/politics/leaders/20261102/ad730617f12246c2a69113be7efbe17e/c.html) reported. Premier Li Qiang was also scheduled to meet the Sultan the same day.

## Economic Diversification at the Core

The composition of the Sultan's delegation pointed to an agenda heavy on investment and industrial cooperation. Dr. Jessica Liu, chairwoman of LY Capital, told ONA that the visit could push economic cooperation "from traditional trade patterns to long-term investment, industrial integration, and innovation and technology-driven development," identifying four priority tracks: energy including petrochemicals, renewables and green hydrogen; infrastructure and logistics built around the ports and special economic zones of Sohar, Duqm and Salalah; the digital economy including artificial intelligence, smart cities and fintech; and diversification into mineral processing, aquaculture, agriculture, healthcare and sustainable tourism.

Ding Long, a professor at the Middle East Studies Institute of Shanghai International Studies University, told the [Global Times](https://www.globaltimes.cn/page/202611/1373414.shtml) that Oman's Vision 2040 — its plan to reduce dependence on oil and gas — aligns closely with Chinese strengths. "China's strengths in renewable energy equipment, infrastructure, digital technology and industrial supply chains offer the external support Oman needs," he said.

Leo Xu, partner and head of the China department at advisory firm 1970, told ONA that Chinese expertise in electric vehicles, batteries, clean-energy storage, aviation and advanced manufacturing could support Oman's technology transfer and industrial localization, while Oman's location positions it as a gateway to Middle East, North African and African markets.

## Strategic Autonomy Amid Regional Tensions

The visit also carries a geopolitical dimension. Sun Degang, director of the Center for Middle Eastern Studies at Fudan University, told the Global Times that Oman seeks stronger Chinese engagement as tensions in the Gulf have escalated sharply, and hopes Beijing will play a greater role in promoting reconciliation and dialogue to help maintain Middle East stability. Ding said that with tensions mounting around the Strait of Hormuz, the visit reflects Oman's efforts to strengthen strategic support and enhance diplomatic autonomy amid great-power rivalry.

The talks come amid a broader wave of Chinese engagement with the Gulf. Since early 2026, Beijing has received a succession of high-level Arab and Gulf visits, including from Saudi Arabia, the United Arab Emirates and Jordan, deepening China-GCC and China-Arab strategic partnerships.

## What to Watch

As of the time of writing, Xinhua's readout of the talks was a brief flash dispatch, and the full details of agreements, memoranda of understanding or a joint statement signed during the visit had not yet been published. For Oman, the visit represents a bid to translate a long-standing trading relationship into investment and technology partnerships underpinning its Vision 2040 diversification drive. For China, it extends a pattern of deepening Gulf ties at a moment of regional uncertainty. The specific deliverables announced in the coming days will indicate how far the two sides have moved from trade toward industrial and strategic integration.

Guo Jiakun framed Beijing's ambitions plainly: "China is willing to work with Oman, taking this visit as an opportunity, to push the two countries' strategic partnership to make greater progress."
