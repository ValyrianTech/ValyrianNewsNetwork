# PBOC Shifts Policy Anchor to Overnight Repo With 541 Billion Yuan Move

China's central bank on Monday conducted 541 billion yuan (about $76 billion) in overnight reverse repo operations at a fixed rate of 1.25%, the first such operation after it formally shifted its main monetary policy rate anchor from the 7-day to the overnight tenor, [Xinhua reported](https://www.news.cn/fortune/20261102/25dc995cacf94be78df410fe0fbaf033/c.html).

The move, effective November 2, marks a structural change in how the People's Bank of China manages short-term liquidity, aligning its primary operating tool with DR001—the interbank overnight repo rate it had already designated as its policy target.

## A Framework Change, Not a Rate Cut

The overnight rate of 1.25% sits 15 basis points below the former 7-day reverse repo rate of 1.4%, but analysts were careful to frame the shift as a technical adjustment rather than outright easing.

"This means the main policy rate has formally switched from the 7-day reverse repo rate to the overnight reverse repo rate," Ming Ming, chief economist at CITIC Securities, told [Caijing Magazine](https://www.163.com/dy/article/LAIHPJJS0519DDOA.html). He noted that DR001 has run in the 1.25% to 1.30% range since October, meaning the new anchor is broadly consistent with the existing funding center.

A bond market participant quoted by Caijing put it more bluntly: a policy rate "anchor switch" does not equal a rate cut. Still, the cheaper overnight option and more flexible 7-day bidding could indirectly pull market rates lower.

## Three Coordinated Adjustments

The PBOC announced three changes on October 30, all effective from November 2:

- Overnight reverse repo tenders will now be conducted every working day at a fixed rate (currently 1.25%) with quantity tender.
- The 7-day reverse repo becomes a fixed-quantity, rate-tender, multi-price auction, conducted mainly during tax periods and month-end liquidity windows—demoted from a daily primary tool to a periodic backstop.
- Other tools previously priced as spreads over the 7-day rate, including temporary repos and the foreign central bank repo facility, are now priced as spreads over the overnight rate, with the spread magnitude unchanged.

Because the adjustment spreads were held constant, the interest rate corridor shifts down by 15 basis points in tandem, Ming explained. The corridor's ceiling is the temporary overnight reverse repo rate and its floor is the temporary overnight forward repo rate.

## Why It Matters

The change deepens the PBOC's long-running pivot from quantity-based toward price-based monetary control—a direction Governor Pan Gongsheng has repeatedly signaled. At the 2026 Financial Street Forum on October 19, Pan said DR001 had become the central bank's target rate and that the PBOC would study switching its main open-market tenor to overnight.

"Defining the target rate as overnight gives the market a clear reference, reduces irrational rate volatility, and helps transmit the policy price signal more effectively," Zhang Xu, chief fixed-income analyst at Everbright Securities, told Xinhua.

## Market Impact Largely Priced In

The downward shift in the funding-rate center had already been substantially reflected in bond markets before Monday's operation. Zhang noted that for the bond market, "the pricing of the overnight handover is largely complete." According to Caijing, the one-year AAA negotiable certificate of deposit yield had fallen to 1.43%, while the 10-year government bond yield eased from 1.68% at end-September to 1.62%.

## What's Next

With the 7-day reverse repo now serving as a liquidity backstop, daily short-end management will hinge on overnight operations—a structural shift in the PBOC's toolkit. Ming expects flexible overnight injections to be the main tool for offsetting year-end volatility from government bond supply and cross-year funding demand, keeping the funding environment stable and accommodative.

The PBOC's party committee reaffirmed on October 30 that it would maintain a "moderately loose" monetary policy and keep liquidity ample. Whether the framework change is followed by a formal policy rate cut remains the key question for markets in the months ahead.
