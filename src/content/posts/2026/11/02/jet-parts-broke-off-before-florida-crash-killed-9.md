# Jet Parts Broke Off Before Florida Crash That Killed 9

Parts of a business jet appear to have broken off before the aircraft crashed in South Florida and erupted into a ball of flames, killing all nine people on board, a federal investigator said Monday, [according to The Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/florida-deadly-plane-crash-fireball-233076d167e2f48eaef7965376dd8ee0). The National Transportation Safety Board’s disclosure points investigators toward a possible in-flight structural failure as a central focus of the probe, rather than a simple ground-impact scenario.

Video recorded by passersby captured the moment on Sunday afternoon when the Bahamas-bound aircraft plunged from the sky and slammed into the ground, exploding in Greenacres, about 7 miles (11 kilometers) southwest of West Palm Beach. Flames flickered around chunks of pavement lining the crater as shocked residents looked on. Thick black smoke engulfed the crash site.

“Debris was everywhere. Debris hit my house. Everything was on fire. The road was on fire,” Anthony Blackmon, who lives near the crash site, told NBC affiliate WTVJ. “I’ve always seen it on the news, never thought it would happen so close to somebody like how it happened today.”

## Investigators Focus on Breakup in Flight

NTSB member Tom Chapman said during a news conference Monday that the aircraft appeared to start breaking up before impact and that he expects parts of the plane will be found far from the main wreckage.

“We do have some indication that the aircraft was breaking up before impact,” Chapman said. “We may find parts of the aircraft some distance away from the actual impact site.”

The finding raises the possibility of an in-flight breakup or the separation of components during the jet’s final descent — a scenario that would scatter debris across a wider area and complicate evidence recovery.

## A Descent Beyond Design Limits

The aircraft, a 2004 Bombardier Learjet 60, had departed Austin-Bergstrom International Airport in Texas en route to North Eleuthera in the Bahamas. Airport spokesperson Jim Reynolds described it as a private aircraft.

The plane was heading east at 35,000 feet (11,000 meters) when the pilot began reporting problems, according to air traffic control recordings captured by LiveATC.net. The pilot said ice was accumulating on the jet and that turbulence was jolting it by as much as 700 feet (213 meters). Rain pounded the region on Sunday.

“We have light, clear ice that is manageable and moderate turbulence,” the pilot told controllers.

The situation rapidly deteriorated. The pilot requested permission to climb another 2,000 feet and then quickly declared an emergency as the jet lost altitude at an alarming rate.

“We’re descending 8,000 — mayday, mayday — we’re descending 10,000 feet per minute,” the pilot radioed. “We are in an overspeed condition descending 20,000 feet per minute,” the pilot added moments later, before transmitting: “We’re going to crash into the ground.”

Video shows the plane banking sharply to the right before crashing and exploding. Florida Governor Ron DeSantis described the scene at the impact site.

“It was a cataclysmic plane crash,” DeSantis told reporters. “It looked like a missile going down and left a big crater on the side of the road.”

## Community Displaced as Scene Is Cleared

Palm Beach County Commissioner Joel G. Flores said at the news conference that the county “doesn’t have a clue” when residents living around the crash site will be able to return home. Around 77 mobile homes were in the area, and residents of those homes have been evacuated to a shelter.

“Our concern right now is clearing the scene so that our residents can get back to normal,” Flores said. “We don’t really have a timeline as to when they are going to be able to comb through all the evidence and do everything they need to do, and that leaves our community looking for answers.”

Palm Beach County Mayor Sara Baxter asked for sympathy for the crash victims. “Please keep them in your thoughts and prayers,” she said.

Authorities have not officially released the names of all those who died. Camila Corteleti told the New York Post and New York Times that her daughter, 20-year-old Nathalia Gadelha Filgueiras, was one of the pilots. She earned her pilot’s license in 2023 while a 17-year-old senior at West Boca Raton Community High School, local news reports said at the time. Her pilot’s license showed she had the rating to fly that model of Learjet but not to be in command of the flight.

## What’s Next

The investigation is in its early stages, and the NTSB has not determined a probable cause. Key questions remain: whether the breakup caused the loss of control or resulted from the extreme overspeed dive, what role icing and turbulence played, and whether airframe or mechanical factors contributed.

For now, the priority is recovering wreckage that may be spread across a wide field, reviewing air traffic control logs and flight data, and examining weather conditions at the time of the crash. As the scene is cleared and evidence is catalogued, investigators will begin assembling the sequence of events that brought the jet down — and the answers a displaced community is waiting for.
