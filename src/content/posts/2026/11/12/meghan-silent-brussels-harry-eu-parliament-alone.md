# Meghan Silent in Brussels as Harry Faces EU Alone

Meghan, Duchess of Sussex, withdrew from a planned speech at the European Parliament in Brussels on Tuesday, leaving her husband Prince Harry to deliver the keynote address alone — a last-minute change of plan that followed the arrest of her estranged father on suspicion of possessing child sexual abuse material. The couple had been billed to give a joint 15-minute address on digital safety for children; instead Meghan, according to [ITV via VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/10/harry-en-meghan-europees-parlement/), felt "not strong enough" to speak publicly in the wake of the scandal.

## Context: A Family Crisis Colliding With a Public Cause

The visit was meant to mark the Sussexes' first joint working trip to continental Europe since their return to England. Invited by the Parliament's Intergroup on Children's Rights, they were to highlight the importance of protecting young people online — a cause the couple has championed for years, working previously with the United Nations and the World Health Organization.

But the event was overshadowed by events in Florida. On 6 November, Thomas Wayne Markle, 82, was arrested and charged with one count of possession of child pornography, according to a [US Department of Justice press release](https://www.justice.gov/usao-cdca/pr/thomas-wayne-markle-arrested-federal-complaint-charging-him-possessing-sexually). Federal agents searching two Los Angeles-area storage units Markle had rented since 2008 seized some 78 items — slides, film negatives and books — including, prosecutors allege, sexually explicit photographs of Markle himself with prepubescent girls. If convicted, he faces a statutory maximum of 20 years in federal prison. A criminal complaint contains only allegations, and all defendants are presumed innocent until proven guilty.

## A Message Disciplined, a Duchess Silent

Organisers pre-briefed journalists that the couple would not answer questions, a signal that they sought to keep the spotlight on the child-safety cause. Meghan appeared only at the start of the day, joining Harry to unveil the "Lost Screen Memorial" — first launched in New York the previous year — which displays photographs of young people whose deaths have been linked to online harms. They were joined by Sandra Spitaels, whose 15-year-old son Glenn took his own life after a nude image of him was shared on social media.

Parliamentary sources told [Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/2026/11/10/prince-harry-and-meghan-back-eu-campaign-against-online-bullying) that Harry attended subsequent meetings with MEPs alone. Meghan accompanied him for the memorial, an exhibition and an art installation commemorating children who died after online bullying — but she did not speak.

Harry, meanwhile, delivered an emotional address accusing social media companies of failing to keep children safe. "These companies have caused so much harm, there has to be accountability," he said, describing the addiction social platforms create as "brainwashing on an extraordinary scale." In remarks to a packed room, he added: "Every time you look at your phone, just remember that there is a child out there that is looking at that phone 20-30 times — more probably — considering taking that life because they don't know how to escape."

He framed the appeal around children's right to feel safe. Yet the singular appearance was itself the story for many observers. Belgian royalty commentator Pascale Mertens told VRT that "the timing is particularly bad."

## Analysis: A Legislative Moment, and an Awkward One

The visit was not merely ceremonial. It landed squarely in the middle of a live legislative debate over the proposed EU Kids Act, which would delay children's access to social media, introduce age-verification checks and restrict addictive design features. Harry praised the bill for shifting the burden of responsibility from parents to platforms. European Commission Vice-President Dubravka Šuica called the event the latest in a long series of EU actions against large platforms to reduce risks for children.

The optics, however, were difficult to manage. A couple campaigning against child exploitation online had to do so while Meghan's own father faced allegations of exactly such conduct — a juxtaposition no communications strategy could smooth over. Meghan's silence appeared calculated: a way to avoid turning the criminal case into a "spectacle," and a recognition that every public word would be read through the lens of the scandal rather than the cause.

## What's Next

Meghan's retreat raises questions about whether it foreshadows a longer withdrawal from public life and what that would mean for the couple's joint advocacy brand. Harry's willingness to carry the public workload alone may become the arrangement of the coming months. Meanwhile, Thomas Markle's case will continue to unfold through the US courts. And the EU Kids Act, the substance beneath the drama, will keep advancing through a Parliament that now has one more vivid illustration of the stakes.
