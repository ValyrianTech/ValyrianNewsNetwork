# Senators Probe Whether RFK Jr. Lied About Samoa Trip

Two Democratic senators are launching an investigation into whether Health Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. lied to Congress about his reasons for a 2019 trip to Samoa, after The Associated Press and The Guardian published documents that refute his testimony. Sens. Ron Wyden of Oregon and Angela Alsobrooks of Maryland sent a letter Wednesday to Children's Health Defense (CHD)—the anti-vaccine nonprofit Kennedy formerly chaired—demanding a broad range of records by December 4.

![Human Services Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr. arrives to speak at a Make Iowa Healthy Again rally, Oct. 28, 2026, in Clive, Iowa. (AP Photo/Charlie Neibergall)](https://newsroom.ap.org/editorial-photos-videos/detail?mediatype=photo&itemid=460dce7481514824824dee069ddba8bc)

## The Contradiction at the Center of the Case

During his January 2025 confirmation hearings, Kennedy repeatedly told senators that his Samoa trip had nothing to do with vaccines. "I went there, nothing to do with vaccines," he told Wyden at the time. "I went there to introduce a medical informatics system that would digitalize records in Samoa." To Sen. Ed Markey he said flatly, "My purpose in going down there had nothing to do with vaccines."

But a letter Kennedy wrote to then-Samoan Prime Minister Tuilaepa Sailele Malielegaoi on January 20, 2019—on CHD letterhead—[told a different story](https://www.documentcloud.org/documents/28581231-letter-from-rfk-jr/). In it, Kennedy proposed that his team "conduct detailed health informatics assessment in Samoa of what happened with your MMR vaccines," using the words "vaccine" or "vaccination" eight times.

The documents were obtained by the AP and The Guardian through an open records lawsuit, with assistance from the Reporters Committee for Freedom of the Press, which compelled the State Department to release emails, many heavily redacted.

## Why Samoa Matters

Kennedy's trip came just months before one of the deadliest measles outbreaks in the Pacific in decades. Samoa's vaccination rate had collapsed after two infants died in July 2018 following MMR inoculations—deaths later attributed to human error by nurses who mixed the vaccine with an expired muscle relaxant rather than sterile water. Anti-vaccine groups, including CHD, amplified fear online, and the government suspended its vaccination program for ten months.

The outbreak that followed infected more than 2.5% of the population and killed 83 people, most of them children under five. Critics, including Samoan officials, have said Kennedy's visit emboldened anti-vaccine activists and worsened the crisis. Kennedy has denied discouraging vaccination, saying he "ended up having conversations with people, some of whom I never intended to meet."

## The Scope of the Demand

The letter requests all documents related to Kennedy's Samoa travel, his 2024 presidential campaign, and his Senate confirmation process. It also seeks all communications between CHD and federal officials since Trump's second term began in January 2025, along with records tied to Kennedy's September 2026 speech to CHD's annual conference.

Wyden, who is expected to chair the Senate Finance Committee—which oversees HHS—when Democrats retake Congress in January 2027, framed the inquiry in stark terms. "We are beginning an investigation into evidence that Secretary of Health and Human Services Robert F Kennedy, Jr unlawfully lied to Congress on multiple occasions," the senators [wrote in the letter](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/nov/12/senate-investigation-rfk-jr-measles-samoa), which was obtained by The Guardian. "In Samoa and in the United States, unvaccinated children have died tragic and preventable deaths during measles outbreaks."

By targeting CHD, Wyden is aiming at the heart of Kennedy's political machine, as The Guardian described it. Since taking office, Kennedy has hired former CHD figures, including Lyn Redwood, a former CHD president who made early outreach on his behalf to Samoa and now works at HHS reportedly on vaccine safety. His closest adviser, Stefanie Spear, ran CHD's newsletter before joining his campaign.

## Legal and Political Stakes

Knowingly giving material false testimony to Congress is a [crime](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/1001) that carries a possible prison sentence, though it is rarely prosecuted—and legal experts consider a Justice Department prosecution unlikely under the Trump administration. The letter is not a subpoena, but watchdogs say ignoring it would be unwise.

"It's not wise to ignore it," said Tim Stretton of the Project on Government Oversight, a nonpartisan watchdog group. "It's really in their interest now to cooperate in a good faith effort." If CHD does not comply, he said, the committee could issue subpoenas to compel the information.

HHS has maintained that the Samoa visit was "unrelated to vaccines" and that "any claim or insinuation based on these letters that Secretary Kennedy lied under oath or to Congress is not only false but outright defamatory." The department has not explained why his testimony contradicted his 2019 letter.

## What's Next

The inquiry is one of a vast array of investigations Democrats plan into the Trump administration after winning midterm control of Congress. On election night, after it became clear Democrats would take the Senate, Wyden posted a single word on social media: "Subpoenas." Alsobrooks has repeatedly called for Kennedy to resign or be fired, and Sen. Bernie Sanders, the incoming chair of the Senate HELP Committee, has said he is open to impeachment.

The immediate test comes December 4, when CHD's document deadline arrives. Whether the group cooperates—or forces a subpoena fight—will signal how aggressively Democrats intend to pursue one of the most contentious chapters of Kennedy's tenure.
