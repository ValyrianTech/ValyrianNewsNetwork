# China, US in Close Talks on Tariff Cuts and Trade

China and the United States are maintaining close communication on implementing a reciprocal tariff reduction framework and on broader trade and investment issues, China's Commerce Ministry said on Thursday, signalling continued engagement even as the arrangement remains short of taking effect. Speaking at the ministry's regular press conference on 12 November, spokesperson He Yadong said the so-called "30 billion vs 30 billion" framework would help stabilise and expand bilateral trade, and that progress on wider consultations would be announced "in due course," according to [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/world/20261112/dbca42412a324e65a4cab293510bef3e/c.html).

The brief but carefully worded statement offers no new concessions and no timetable. Its significance lies less in what it announces than in what it confirms: that the two sides remain in dialogue on an arrangement agreed in principle nearly two months ago but still awaiting the domestic legal procedures required on each side before it can take effect.

## What the framework involves

The "30 billion vs 30 billion" arrangement, agreed during the eighth round of China-US economic and trade consultations held in New York and Washington in late September, would see each side cut tariffs on roughly US$30 billion of the other's imports. About 90% of the covered products on each side would see tariffs reduced to most-favoured-nation levels — effectively removing the additional duties imposed during the trade war for those goods.

On China's side, the cuts are expected to cover about US$30 billion of US imports including agricultural products, personal care products, medical devices and coal. On the US side, the reductions are expected to cover about US$30 billion of Chinese imports including toys, home appliances, baby products, and kitchen and bath products. Chinese tariffs on US coal are to be folded into the framework, supporting Chinese imports of US coal in 2027 and 2028.

Crucially, the cuts are to be implemented "synchronously" only after each side completes its own domestic legal procedures. As of 12 November, that had not happened — which is why He framed the message around "maintaining close communication on implementing this arrangement" and a call to "push it to take effect soon."

## Agriculture as the pressure point

Beijing has effectively linked its commitment to buy more American farm goods to the tariff cut landing. A week before He's remarks, Commerce Ministry spokesperson Huang Ling said China "has always been open to expanding purchases of US agricultural products, but the precondition is that the '30 billion vs 30 billion' reciprocal tariff cut arrangement agreed by the two sides lands," according to a Xinhua report carried by [Eastmoney](https://finance.eastmoney.com/a/202611053923479333.html).

The linkage points to a delicate piece of leverage. Under a May 2026 understanding reached when the two heads of state met in Beijing, China agreed to buy US$17 billion of US agricultural products annually from 2026, on top of prior soybean commitments. US farm groups, facing higher fuel costs, have their own interest in seeing tariffs fall so that large purchases become commercially viable — making them a domestic American constituency with a stake in completion.

The following day, 6 November, a Reuters reporter asked the Foreign Ministry whether the tariff cut was a precondition for China starting the US$17 billion in farm purchases. Spokesperson Guo Jiakun deferred detailed questions to the competent authorities, saying only that "maintaining the stable development of China-US economic and trade relations serves the interests of both countries and both peoples," according to the [Foreign Ministry](https://www.fmprc.gov.cn/fyrbt_673021/jzhsl_673025/202611/t20261106_12054906.shtml).

## A two-track bargaining process

He's simultaneous references to "tariff reduction" and to "trade and investment issues of respective concern" indicate a broad agenda rather than a standalone tariff deal. The September consultations also produced agreement on working rules for a new China-US Trade Council, an Investment Council, an Agriculture Working Group co-led by the Commerce Ministry and the US Trade Representative, and consensus on financial services and an AI dialogue.

Several near-term windows could produce concrete announcements. The first meeting of the Agriculture Working Group is planned before the end of 2026, and a second round of the AI dialogue is expected before the end of November. Separately, the so-called Kuala Lumpur joint arrangement — which paused certain tariffs and non-tariff measures — was extended from 10 November 2026 to 10 January 2027, creating a fresh deadline.

## The signal and the substance

The 12 November message is best read as a deliberately measured confidence signal. China is signalling continued engagement while keeping pressure on Washington to complete its domestic procedures first. The repeated framing that "we hope the US side will work with China" subtly places the onus on the American side to move.

For markets and business communities in both countries — which He said "hold high expectations" for the framework — the absence of a date is a reminder that the gap between an agreement in principle and an agreement in force can be wide. The outstanding questions are concrete: whether and when the domestic legal procedures will be completed; whether China begins the US$17 billion in farm purchases before or only after the cuts; and whether the broader Kuala Lumpur arrangement will be extended again beyond 10 January 2027.

For now, the story is one of continuity rather than breakthrough — a signal that the world's two largest economies remain at the table, even as the substance of their détente waits on paperwork on both sides of the Pacific.
