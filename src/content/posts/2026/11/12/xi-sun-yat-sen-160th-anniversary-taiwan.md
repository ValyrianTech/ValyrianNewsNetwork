# Xi Commemorates Sun Yat-sen's 160th Birthday, Presses Taiwan Reunification

President Xi Jinping marked the 160th anniversary of the birth of Sun Yat-sen on Thursday with a major speech at the Great Hall of the People in Beijing, framing the Chinese Communist Party as the true heir to the revolutionary's legacy and declaring that the "complete reunification" of China with Taiwan "must be realized and will certainly be realized."

The grand commemorative meeting, held at 10 a.m. and attended by roughly 3,000 people, brought together the entire top leadership of the party, state and military. According to [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/politics/leaders/20261112/bb8d52ec046d490780c4a9eebaa0223b/c.html), all six other members of the Politburo Standing Committee—Li Qiang, Zhao Leji, Wang Huning, Cai Qi, Ding Xuexiang and Li Xi—were present, with Wang Huning, chairman of the Chinese People's Political Consultative Conference, presiding over the proceedings.

## A Legacy Claimed by the Party

Sun Yat-sen (1866–1925), who led the 1911 Xinhai Revolution that toppled the Qing dynasty and founded the Republic of China, is regarded as the founding father of modern China and is claimed by both the CCP on the mainland and the Kuomintang (KMT) in Taiwan. Xi described him as "a great national hero, a great patriot and a great pioneer of China's democratic revolution" who "fought all his life to transform China."

Central to the occasion was the Party's long-standing narrative of inheritance. "The Chinese Communists are the most steadfast supporters, the most loyal cooperators, and the most faithful heirs of Mr. Sun Yat-sen's revolutionary cause," Xi said, noting that the grand blueprints Sun sketched in his *Plan for National Reconstruction*—railways, roads, communications, water conservancy, ports and factories—had "not only long become reality, but are richer and grander than he envisioned."

The speech built its argument on six "precious spirits" for the nation to carry forward: patriotism, serving the people, pursuing truth, indomitable struggle, advocating unity and embracing the world. Each theme was mapped onto contemporary Party priorities—from upholding "overall Party leadership" and "whole-process people's democracy" to advancing theoretical innovation and building a "community with a shared future for mankind."

## An Uncompromising Message on Taiwan

The most politically consequential section of the address concerned Taiwan. Xi recalled Sun's insistence that "China is a unified country" and his commitment to national unity, then turned to the present. "Resolving the Taiwan question and realizing the complete reunification of the motherland is the CCP's unswerving historical task, is the common aspiration of all Chinese sons and daughters, and is an inevitable requirement for realizing the great rejuvenation of the Chinese nation," he said.

The full text of the speech, [released in full by Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/politics/leaders/20261112/81585bc90a754b23bc457c9942b3d857/c.html), reaffirmed Beijing's commitment to "peaceful reunification and one country, two systems" and its willingness to hold dialogue and consultation with "people of insight" in Taiwan on the basis of the one-China principle and the 1992 Consensus. But it paired that offer with a sharp warning: "We will absolutely not leave any space for 'Taiwan independence' separatist activities of any form."

Xi also invoked the shared identity of the two sides, calling cross-strait compatriots "one family connected by blood." Yet the closing lines left little ambiguity about Beijing's intent: "The historical trend cannot be stopped. The complete reunification of the motherland must be realized, and it certainly will be realized."

## Timing Steeped in Current Tensions

The commemoration comes amid sustained cross-strait friction. Beijing has accused Taiwan's ruling Democratic Progressive Party of pursuing "de-Sinicization" and of downplaying Sun Yat-sen's historical role—charges Taipei's government rejects. The heavy emphasis on opposing separatism reflects how a historic remembrance has become a vehicle for contemporary positioning.

The event was also carefully choreographed for collective endorsement. The presence of the full Politburo Standing Committee, alongside vice premiers, ministers and military leaders, and the release of the speech verbatim under the designation "authorized release," signaled its high political importance—mirroring the roughly 6,100-character address Xi delivered at the 150th anniversary a decade ago.

Parallel activity across the strait underscored how Sun's legacy remains a contested political resource. As [China News Service](https://www.chinanews.com.cn/gn/2026/11-10/10729840.shtml) reported, the KMT held its own commemorative symposium at its Taipei headquarters two days earlier, where chairwoman Zheng Li-wen and scholars spoke on cross-strait peace and the shared heritage. KMT Vice Chairman Chang Jung-kung used the occasion to stress that the two sides are "not state-to-state relations." Zheng had traveled to the mainland in April, where, according to [Lianhe Zaobao](https://www.zaobao.com.sg/news/china/story20260407-8856403), she was met at the airport by Taiwan Affairs Office head Song Tao and the two sides jointly reaffirmed the 1992 Consensus and opposition to independence.

## What to Watch

Beyond the rhetoric, the anniversary offers little in the way of new policy. The question now is whether Beijing follows the speech with any concrete cross-strait confidence-building measures, or whether it sharpens its pressure campaign against Taipei's government. Formal responses from Taiwan's Mainland Affairs Council and the DPP are likely in the coming days, alongside international reaction—particularly from Washington, given the speech's explicit framing of reunification as inevitable.

For Xi, the occasion served a dual purpose: marshaling the memory of a founding figure to reinforce the Party's legitimacy at home, and projecting determination on Taiwan to audiences at home and abroad. As he put it, the best way to commemorate Sun Yat-sen is to "learn from, inherit and carry forward his precious spirit, and continuously advance the cause of building a strong nation and national rejuvenation."
