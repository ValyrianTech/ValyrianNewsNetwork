# Brooklyn Building Collapse Kills at Least Eight

A three-story residential building in Brooklyn's Flatbush neighborhood collapsed into an adjacent construction pit Wednesday afternoon, killing at least eight people and injuring roughly 17 others as emergency crews worked through a second day of a perilous search complicated by an approaching storm.

The collapse at 1321 Flatbush Avenue, near Bedford Avenue, occurred just after 4 p.m. on Wednesday, November 11, city officials said. The building caved into an excavated lot at 2625 Bedford Avenue, where crews had been digging for a planned new apartment complex. More than 250 firefighters and emergency workers responded, arriving about a minute after the first call, according to [Gothamist/WNYC](https://gothamist.com/news/1-dead-10-injured-after-building-collapse-in-flatbush-brookln-fdny-says).

By Thursday morning, officials confirmed at least eight deaths, a toll that rose steadily from an initial report of one.

## A Toll That Kept Climbing

"Eight New Yorkers have now lost their lives and at least 17 have been injured, including three firefighters," Mayor Zohran Mamdani said Thursday in a statement posted to X. "There are no words for a loss of this magnitude. My heart is with the families and loved ones of those we have lost, those who are injured and everyone grieving today."

Among the injured were at least seven children and three firefighters. Four people were listed in critical condition. Rescue operations continued Thursday under dangerous conditions, with crews using search dogs, drones, cranes and an excavator to comb through the wreckage.

FDNY Commissioner Lillian Bonsignore, speaking as the rescue began, warned that the extent of the toll remained unknown. "We do not know how many people are still in the collapse zone," she said. "We will likely not be finished for some time."

The human cost came into sharp relief through the accounts of witnesses and neighbors. Councilmember Farah Louis, who represents the area, described infants and toddlers being carried out of the rubble on stretchers. She also recounted a husband waiting desperately to learn whether his wife—six months pregnant—had survived. "They did find her, and she is in critical condition," Louis told WNYC. "It's just a horrific scene."

Because students had the day off for the Veterans Day holiday, children were at home when the building came down.

## Exhausted Rescuers and a Looming Storm

As the search stretched into Thursday, the weather became a new adversary. Heavy rain, thunderstorms and gusty winds were forecast for the afternoon, raising fears of further instability in the sheared and hollowed structure. Former FDNY Commissioner Laura Kavanagh told WNYC that "rain and wind can cause further instability," noting that the neighboring building had been partially sheared off by the collapse.

The Red Cross opened a reception center at the Flatbush YMCA on Flatbush Avenue, registering seven households—14 adults and five children—for emergency assistance by 6 a.m. Thursday. Officials urged anyone searching for a missing loved one to call 311 or 212-639-9675.

## Developer Had Prior Enforcement History

The cause of the collapse remains under investigation by the city's Department of Buildings and law enforcement partners, Mamdani said. But questions are mounting about the excavation work next door and the safety precautions that were supposed to guard against exactly this kind of catastrophe.

According to a [Gothamist investigation](https://gothamist.com/news/developer-at-brooklyn-collapse-site-had-record-of-flouting-orders-from-city-agency), the developer of the adjacent lot, Robert Fischman, had a history of flouting city orders. In March 2026, the Department of Buildings issued a stop-work order at the lot during demolition of a prior one-story building for failing to install required safety measures, including netting and worker sheds. A month later, an inspector found demolition work had continued in violation of that order and issued another penalty.

By the time Fischman sent a June letter to neighboring landlords announcing upcoming excavation, the project team had already ignored city commands, Gothamist reported. Structural plans filed by engineer Jeiming Wang called for extensive excavation and bracing—steel, timber and concrete shoring—along with seismic monitors, crack gauges on surrounding buildings and special-inspector oversight. It is unclear whether those precautions were actually in place before the collapse.

The developer had submitted plans in September for a nine-story building that the Buildings Department had not yet approved, though a permit was in place for the excavation work itself. Buildings Commissioner Ahmed Tigani confirmed workers had been digging at the lot before the adjacent structure gave way.

## A Building With Its Own Troubles

The collapsed building, owned by Olufemi Falade, carried 98 open housing code violations, according to city data, including unsafe electrical wiring, an inadequate sprinkler water supply, missing smoke detectors, water outages and a bed bug infestation. [NY1](https://ny1.com/nyc/all-boroughs/news/2026/11/11/people-trapped-in-collapsed-brooklyn-building--fdny-says) reported the property had cycled through the city's tax lien sale process at least five times since 2017.

The disaster unfolded in a rapidly gentrifying section of Flatbush, where the excavation at 2625 Bedford Avenue was one of many similar projects set to rise. Witness Antione Vines told reporters the scene was foreseeable. "Before they build a new building and you're connecting it to another building that's been up since the 1700s, you gotta look at the structure of the building," he said.

For the families of the missing, Thursday brought only agonizing uncertainty. Luis Siguencia waited overnight for word of his son Gabriel, a 22-year-old construction worker who had recently arrived from Ecuador and had worked on the site's foundation for two weeks. "We've been waiting here all night," he said. "We went to four hospitals, and they haven't given us any information. He wanted to get ahead. Because we all come here with the dream of getting ahead."

## What Comes Next

With the rescue still underway, a full accounting of the dead and injured has yet to emerge. As the search concludes, attention is likely to shift to the investigation—specifically, whether required shoring, seismic monitoring and crack-gauge precautions were installed before excavation began, and whether the city's enforcement record on the site was adequate.

The collapse is already being described as among the worst such disasters in recent New York City history. [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/nov/12/brooklyn-building-collapse-nyc) reported that the search would continue as long as crews believed survivors might be found. For now, the neighborhood waits, and the toll remains a number that officials warn could still rise.
