# Jim Jordan Elected House GOP Leader as Party Rebuilds

House Republicans elected Rep. Jim Jordan of Ohio as their new minority leader on Tuesday, choosing a combative conservative firebrand to steer the party through life in the minority after Democrats reclaimed control of Congress in this month's midterm elections. Jordan, 62, defeated current House Majority Leader Steve Scalise of Louisiana in a secret-ballot vote and will succeed Speaker Mike Johnson as the top House Republican when the new Congress convenes in January, according to [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/11/10/nx-s1-6030988/gop-leadership-election-steve-scalise-jim-jordan).

The vote offered rank-and-file Republicans a clear philosophical choice about how to respond to a staggering defeat in which Democrats flipped more than 40 GOP-held House seats. They opted for confrontation over continuity.

## A Party Choosing Its Posture

Jordan, a co-founder of the ultra-conservative House Freedom Caucus and a veteran of both the Oversight and Judiciary committees, pitched an aggressive new direction. In [a letter to colleagues](https://jordan.house.gov/sites/evo-subsites/jordan.house.gov/files/evo-media-document/2026-11-04-jordan-letter-to-members.pdf) announcing his candidacy, he wrote that "for too long, we have operated without a clear plan to survive the hour or the day in front of us. That will not cut it against an emboldened and energetic Democratic party."

"I am battle-tested on the oversight front and I know the Democrats' playbook," Jordan added, declaring that "offense, not defense, wins elections." A former college wrestling coach known for his pugilistic style, he is expected to make the two committees central to the party's response as Democrats prepare a long list of investigations into President Trump and his administration.

Scalise, by contrast, ran as the consensus candidate. The No. 2 House Republican since 2023 and a close Trump ally, he argued in [his own letter to colleagues](https://www.majorityleader.gov/uploadedfiles/scalise_dearcolleague_11.4.2026.pdf) that his approach would be "conservative and consensus-driven," and that his "number one goal as Republican Leader will be to win back the majority."

The Freedom Caucus celebrated Jordan's win, with Chairman Andy Harris of Maryland calling him "exactly the right man for the job to fight back against the radical, crazy agenda of Democrats."

## Succeeding Johnson After a Rout

Jordan's rise follows Johnson's decision to step aside from the leadership role. Johnson served as Speaker after being elevated in 2023, and his tenure [ended in the wake of the midterm rout](https://www.npr.org/2026/11/04/g-s1-151854/2026-midterms-mike-johnson-speaker).

The defeat reshaped the political landscape on Capitol Hill. Democrats won full control of Congress, and Rep. Hakeem Jeffries of New York is expected to become Speaker — the first Black lawmaker to hold the position — when the 120th Congress is sworn in.

The contest was also a rematch of the chaotic 2023 Speaker fight. After Kevin McCarthy was ousted, Jordan and Scalise both sought the top job before Republicans ultimately settled on Johnson. This time, Jordan prevailed — a full-circle moment for a lawmaker who spent years challenging his own party's leadership from the right.

## Two Chambers, Two Messages

The House election stood in sharp contrast to the Senate, where Republicans opted for continuity. Senators re-elected John Thune of South Dakota to a second term as party leader, running unopposed despite criticism from Trump over his refusal to eliminate the filibuster and his handling of the SAVE America Act.

"I'm proud of what this Republican team has accomplished together, and we remain united in our commitment to advancing conservative priorities," Thune said in a statement after the vote.

## The Task Ahead

Jordan inherits a demoralized and divided conference with a dual mandate: unify Republicans and sharpen a message for 2028, while defending the president against the incoming Democratic majority's investigations. As [The Hill](https://thehill.com/the-120/6211341-republicans-elect-jim-jordan-minority-leader/) reported, the Johnson-to-Jordan transition marks a stark contrast in styles — one a bespectacled constitutional lawyer who quotes scripture, the other a combative former wrestling champion known for jousting with Democrats.

The pick drew immediate fire from Democrats, who framed it as evidence Republicans learned little from their defeat. The coming months will test whether Jordan's combative approach energizes a party seeking to win back voters or hardens its image as an uncompromising opposition — a question that will define House Republicans' path back toward the majority.
