# Prosecutors Seek 7 Years Over Baby's Death at Ghent Daycare

The prosecution has demanded a seven-year prison sentence for the main defendant in the trial over the death of a six-month-old girl at the 't Sloeberhuisje daycare centre in Mariakerke, a district of Ghent, according to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/12/parket-eist-7-jaar-cel-in-zaak-rond-sloeberhuisje/). The trial, which opened on Thursday at the Ghent court, reopens one of Flanders' most closely followed childcare tragedies — a case that dates back to February 2022 and has since raised difficult questions about oversight of the sector.

## Who Is Charged

The main defendant is the father of the daycare operator, who also worked at the facility as a carer. The prosecution is seeking seven years against him and is additionally asking that he be placed at the disposal of the sentencing court (strafuitvoeringsrechtbank), a mechanism that allows continued supervision or detention after the prison term ends. For the former operator herself, the prosecution requested a two-year prison sentence. Prosecutors also requested a fine against the Flemish childcare agency Agentschap Opgroeien, with the amount left to the court's discretion.

## What Happened in 2022

The case stems from events in February 2022. As VRT NWS [reported](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2022/09/13/uitbaatster-t-sloeberhuisje-in-mariakerke-en-haar-vader-komen/) at the time, a six-month-old girl was taken to the Ghent university hospital in critical condition after an urgent call from the daycare itself. The child had sustained severe brain trauma under unclear circumstances; the hospital alerted prosecutors, and the girl later died of her injuries on 18 February 2022.

The investigation established that the baby died from "shaken baby syndrome" — injuries so severe that the brain was seriously damaged. The father, who has denied the charge, was charged with intentional assault and battery resulting in death, though without the intent to kill. The operator was charged with involuntary manslaughter and, at the time of the incident, was not present at the crèche. After autopsy results and new evidence emerged, she was eventually arrested in June 2022 for neglect, among other allegations.

Both the operator and her father were initially detained and later released under electronic monitoring following a decision by the Ghent council chamber (raadkamer) that survived a prosecution appeal.

## A History of Warnings

The case has drawn particular scrutiny because of what preceded the tragedy. According to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2022/03/07/klachten-sloeberhuisje/), the childcare organisation Kind en Gezin received 17 unique reports about 't Sloeberhuisje in the weeks before the baby's death — though the agency said most concerned contract disputes and pandemic-related access rules rather than allegations of abuse. "The complaints we received were of a totally different order than what ultimately happened," Nele Wouters of Kind en Gezin said at the time.

Inspection reports since 2015 had also shown that the father, who worked as a carer at the facility, was accused of physical violence against children, while the operator repeatedly denied the allegations. The crèche was shut after the baby's death, and its licence was later revoked by Agentschap Opgroeien. The Flemish government ordered an audit into why earlier signals were not acted upon.

## Why the Case Matters

The trial reflects a broader reckoning over childcare safety in Flanders. The death at 't Sloeberhuisje is not an isolated case: other infant deaths at daycare centres, including at a facility in Avelgem, have sharpened public concern about how crèches are inspected and how complaints from parents are handled.

The prosecution's decision to seek a fine against Agentschap Opgroeien — in addition to sentences for the individuals — signals that the case is being framed not only as a question of individual criminal responsibility but also of institutional oversight. The fact that the case was serious enough at one stage to involve consideration of the hof van assisen, Belgium's court for the most serious crimes, underscores the gravity prosecutors attach to the matter.

For families and campaigners, the years-long proceedings also highlight how slowly such cases move through the justice system, and how difficult it can be for grief-stricken parents to obtain answers.

## What's Next

The court has now heard the prosecution's demands; the defence is expected to respond before judges retire to deliberate. The exact timeline for a verdict is not yet clear, and the requested sentences are not binding on the court, which may impose lighter or heavier penalties.

Whatever the outcome, the case is likely to feed into ongoing debate about childcare regulation in Flanders. The pending audit into oversight failures at 't Sloeberhuisje, and the question of whether the deaths of infants in Belgian daycare should prompt stricter inspection and enforcement, remain open — and central — questions that this trial can inform but not resolve.
