# Berkshire Profit Drops 28% as Buybacks, Cash Hit Records

Berkshire Hathaway reported a 28% year-on-year decline in third-quarter net profit on Saturday, November 7, even as the conglomerate sharply accelerated share repurchases, remained a net seller of stocks and pushed its cash reserves to a fresh all-time high, according to [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/07/berkshire-hathaway-q3-2026-earnings-buybacks.html).

The results, the first full-year reporting cycle under chief executive Greg Abel, who succeeded Warren Buffett earlier in 2026, illustrate a company sitting on an unprecedented pile of capital while its new leader signals a more aggressive stance toward deploying it.

## The Numbers Behind the Decline

Berkshire's net profit attributable to shareholders fell to $22.112 billion from $30.796 billion a year earlier, a drop of roughly 28%. The primary culprit was the investment portfolio: investment gains for the quarter shrank nearly by half, to $9.407 billion. Under U.S. generally accepted accounting principles, unrealized gains and losses on equity holdings flow directly through net income, making the headline profit figure highly volatile from quarter to quarter.

Operating earnings — the measure Buffett long championed as the truer gauge of the underlying businesses — totaled $12.705 billion, down 5.8% from $13.485 billion. The weakness was concentrated in insurance. Underwriting profit tumbled 43% to $1.352 billion, while insurance investment income slipped 14% to $2.734 billion. Those declines were partly offset by gains elsewhere: the railroad, energy and manufacturing divisions all posted year-on-year growth, with manufacturing, services and retail operating profit rising to $4.042 billion from $3.616 billion.

Revenue, by contrast, expanded 11.24% year on year to $105.647 billion, underscoring a business whose top line remains healthy even as insurance margins normalize.

## A Buyback Record

The most striking move of the quarter was on capital allocation. Berkshire repurchased about $9.6 billion of its own stock between July and September — more than double the $4.5 billion bought back in the second quarter, and a steep climb from roughly $2.35 billion in the first quarter. The nine-month total reached about $14.4 billion, and the third-quarter figure stands as a single-quarter record for the company.

The ramp is widely read as a signal. With few large acquisition targets available at attractive prices, management appears to view Berkshire's own shares as among the best uses of its capital. The acceleration also marks a stylistic shift from the Buffett era: analysts have noted that Abel has shown greater willingness to deploy the company's enormous cash hoard, including through buybacks, as [Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/10/26/berkshire-hathaway-is-the-stock-still-a-safe-buy-without-warren-buffett/) examined in its assessment of the post-Buffett company.

At the same time, Berkshire remained a modest net seller of equities, offloading roughly $426 million of stock in the quarter — a marginal figure against its overall portfolio, but a continuation of the cautious posture that has defined its recent trading.

## Cash Pile Reaches a Record

The company's cash and equivalents rose 3% to $377.435 billion as of September 30, up from $365.514 billion at the end of the second quarter, a new high. The figure is somewhat less dramatic on a narrower basis: excluding railroad and utility cash along with Treasury bill payables, cash holdings stood at $352.3 billion, down 1.9% quarter over quarter. Either way, the balance sheet remains one of the largest reservoirs of liquid capital in corporate history.

Berkshire's five largest equity holdings were unchanged in the quarter: Alphabet, American Express, Apple, Bank of America and Coca-Cola. Together they account for roughly 65% of the fair value of the equity portfolio, per [Berkshire's official quarterly report](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/qtrly/3rdqtr26.pdf).

The Alphabet position deserves particular attention. Berkshire first took a stake in the Google parent in the third quarter of 2025, and in June 2026 it made a $10 billion strategic investment in Alphabet's $80 billion equity offering — split evenly between Class A shares at $351.81 and Class C shares at $348.20. Those shares have since fallen nearly 25%, a likely drag on third-quarter investment returns and a reminder that even Berkshire's concentrated bets can sour in the short run.

## Outperforming a Rotating Market

Paradoxically, the earnings decline coincided with a stretch of market outperformance. As investors rotated out of technology and artificial-intelligence shares, defensive names like Berkshire drew fresh demand. Class A shares climbed more than 12% over the past month while the S&P 500 fell 5.5% over the same period; year to date, Berkshire is up 13% against 7.8% for the broad index, according to CNBC. Reuters, in its coverage via the [New York Post](https://nypost.com/2026/11/08/business/berkshire-hathaway-speeds-up-stock-buybacks-though-profit-falls/), similarly framed the quarter around the contrast between faster buybacks and softer profit.

That divergence captures Berkshire's dual identity: a sprawling operating company whose insurance unit is feeling margin pressure, and a fortress-balance-sheet holding vehicle that functions as a safe harbor when markets get choppy.

## What to Watch

For investors, the quarter raises a familiar question in a new guise. Buffett spent years defending Berkshire's growing cash pile as a sign of discipline — a refusal to overpay. Abel's early tenure suggests a different emphasis, one in which that capital is put to work through buybacks when bargains are scarce.

The key tests ahead are whether insurance underwriting stabilizes as pricing cycles turn, whether the Alphabet stake recovers from its post-offering slump, and whether the buyback pace is sustained into the fourth quarter. Each will shape how the market judges the first chapter of the post-Buffett era — and whether Berkshire's next record is another cash high, or a decisive deployment of it.
