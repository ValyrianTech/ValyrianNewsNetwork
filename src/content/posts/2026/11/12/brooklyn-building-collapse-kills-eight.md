# Brooklyn Building Collapse Kills 8, Including 2 Children

Eight people — six adults and two children — were killed when a three-story apartment building in Brooklyn's Flatbush neighborhood collapsed into an adjacent construction pit, New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani said Thursday. At least 17 others were injured, including firefighters, and officials cautioned that others may still be missing as crews shifted from hands-on rescue toward heavy excavation.

The building at 1321 Flatbush Avenue, a mixed-use structure with a commercial unit on the ground floor and four apartments above, gave way just after 4 p.m. Wednesday, per [AP News](https://apnews.com/article/brooklyn-building-collapse-dead-rubble-digging-292b3ece5d9ba6e375413c9f4671cf00). The wreckage crashed into an open excavation at 2625 Bedford Avenue, where construction workers had been digging for a new building. The cause remains under investigation.

## A frantic night of rescue

About 250 firefighters and emergency personnel responded to the scene, using power tools, drones and search dogs to hunt for survivors in the precarious wreckage, according to [CBS News New York](https://www.cbsnews.com/newyork/news/building-collapse-nyc-brooklyn-flatbush-rescue-operation/). Crews worked through the night under floodlights, clambering over piles of twisted wood and brick, punching holes in the ruins to pull out dust-covered survivors.

Firefighters pulled a pregnant woman from the rubble Wednesday night, hoisting her onto a stretcher and rushing her to a waiting ambulance. Other survivors were rescued as the night wore on. One resident, Anderson Velez, told the New York Post that his wife and two young children had been trapped inside; he later learned they were pulled out alive.

As daylight broke Thursday, the operation entered a grimmer phase after search teams had checked every accessible void by hand. Two excavators were brought in to remove large chunks of debris, and the death toll climbed to eight. "Rescue workers have spent the night searching the site of yesterday's building collapse, working under extremely dangerous conditions to find and save anyone who may still be trapped," Mamdani said. "I am heartbroken that eight New Yorkers have now lost their lives."

## Victims include children and construction workers

According to [ABC7 New York](https://abc7ny.com/live-updates/brooklyn-building-collapse-live-updates-1-dead-flatbush/20034152/), the dead include a 9-year-old girl and a 10-year-old boy, whose identities had not yet been released. Four of the victims found Thursday are believed to be construction workers who were on the site when the building fell.

Among those killed was Jose Luis Caguana, 26, a construction worker and immigrant from Ecuador who had been in the United States for about six years, his brother Carlos told WNBC-TV. He had a pregnant wife and a young child. "He was the sole provider for the family," Carlos Caguana said. "Now that he's gone, she is going through an immense amount of pain."

Another worker, 29-year-old Alvaro Miguel Caizaguano Toasa, was hospitalized at Kings County Hospital. Family members told ABC7 that he had worked at the site for eight months and is a father of two, ages 3 and 7. "The construction site was not safe," one relative said. "That's why this tragedy took place."

## A project flagged before the collapse

The collapse occurred as developers pursued an ambitious project next door. City records show the developer sought to build a nine-story building with 64 residential units and a ground-floor grocery store at 2625 Bedford Avenue, though the approved plan was for a three-story structure; the taller design had not been approved, according to [CBS News New York](https://www.cbsnews.com/newyork/news/brooklyn-building-collapse-construction/).

The site had drawn the attention of city inspectors earlier in the year. A stop-work order was issued in March after inspectors concluded the building next door was being demolished without adequate overhead protection, such as netting, to shield neighboring buildings. In April, inspectors found demolition work had resumed in violation of that order, and a violation was issued. Work resumed in May after the problems were corrected.

A demolition contractor denied responsibility for the digging underway Wednesday, blaming the earlier violation on a "missing fire extinguisher" and "housekeeping issues." "We know that there was work going on. There were construction workers actively working when the collapse occurred," FDNY Chief of Department John Esposito said. Buildings Commissioner Ahmed Tigani said crews were performing excavation work at the time.

## Questions mount and investigations open

A full stop-work order has been issued at the construction site while the Department of Buildings investigates the cause. The Brooklyn District Attorney's Office is also examining whether any criminal conduct contributed to the collapse.

The disaster reverberated far beyond the rubble. Flatbush Avenue, a busy Brooklyn thoroughfare, stood at a standstill Thursday morning, and the neighborhood — a diverse area of tightly packed older brick buildings and a mix of Caribbean and Mexican restaurants — reeled. A building that shared a wall with the collapsed structure remained standing, but its wall was sheared off, exposing a bedroom and closets filled with clothing to the street.

The city has established an emergency relief fund to support affected families. Fire Department Commissioner Lillian Bonsignore warned that heavy rain forecast for Thursday afternoon could complicate the work. "It'll turn to mud," she told WNYW-TV, vowing crews would press on regardless.

## A second structural scare in five months

The collapse is the second high-profile structural failure to rattle New York in five months. In July, several buildings in Manhattan were evacuated after support columns buckled inside a skyscraper that was formerly the headquarters of Pfizer — an episode that raised fresh questions about oversight of the city's aging and rapidly developing building stock.

For now, the focus in Flatbush remains on the rubble and the families it has scarred. As excavators dismantle what remains of 1321 Flatbush Avenue, investigators must answer how a fully occupied building could fall into a hole next door — and whether warning signs were missed ahead of a tragedy that claimed the lives of children and workers alike.
