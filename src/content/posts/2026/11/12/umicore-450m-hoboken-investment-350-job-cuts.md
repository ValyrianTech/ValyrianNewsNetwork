# Umicore Bets €450M on Hoboken as It Cuts 350 Jobs

Belgian materials technology and recycling group Umicore confirmed on Thursday that it will invest €450 million in its Hoboken site near Antwerp, even as it announced plans to cut up to 350 jobs worldwide over the next two years—slightly fewer than 100 of them in Belgium. The twin announcement pairs a long-term bet on critical-metals recycling with a cost-cutting drive the company says will save around €60 million a year by the fourth quarter of 2028.

## A Refinery at the Centre of Europe's Raw-Materials Push

The investment will expand the Precious Metals Refining operations at Hoboken, one of Europe's largest recycling facilities. According to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/12/umicore-investeert-450-miljoen-euro-in-hoboken-en-schrapt-tot-35/), the plant already recovers 17 different metals—including gold, silver, platinum, rhodium and palladium—from discarded phones, computers, catalytic converters and batteries, primarily through a smelter.

The expansion adds a second recycling route based on chemicals. That will allow Hoboken to refine and recycle cobalt as an 18th metal for the first time, and to add capacity for recovering antimony and tin more efficiently. "We will be able to recycle antimony and tin more efficiently. And for the first time also cobalt," CEO Bart Sap said.

The €450 million figure had been signalled as early as March 2025 but is now definitively decided. Execution is planned between 2027 and 2030, with commissioning expected in 2030. "This is an important investment for Belgium and the European value chain for critical raw materials," the company said.

## Why Cobalt Matters

Sap framed the investment in explicitly strategic terms, pointing to Europe's dependence on China for the metals that underpin its most sensitive industries. "Critical raw materials come mainly from China. They are essential for sectors such as data and artificial intelligence, the pharmaceutical sector, defense and the energy transition. Through this investment we support the future industries," he said. The company's stated ambition is "to be the safest, most sustainable and most efficient refinery in the world."

The push aligns with a broader EU effort to secure supplies of critical minerals. As [De Tijd](https://www.tijd.be/ondernemen/grondstoffen/achter-de-poorten-van-umicore-in-hoboken-de-wereld-heeft-maar-een-fabriek-als-deze-nodig/10610457.html) described it, the Hoboken plant is a quiet but profitable engine whose 17 precious metals should help Europe gain a firmer grip on critical raw materials.

## Where the Cuts Fall

The restructuring targets indirect and support functions rather than the core industrial operations. According to [HLN](https://www.hln.be/hoboken/umicore-bevestigt-investering-van-450-miljoen-euro-in-hoboken-maar-schrapt-350-banen-wereldwijd~a430bd70/), the affected departments include accounting, human resources, procurement and ICT. Umicore aims to simplify its operating model through automation and the use of artificial intelligence to standardise operations.

Umicore employs around 11,000 people worldwide, of whom about 3,200 are in Belgium, across sites in Hoboken, Olen, Bruges and Brussels. The company said changes "could lead to up to about 350 employees worldwide potentially losing their jobs in the coming two years," while a company spokesperson estimated that a little under 100 jobs would go in Belgium. The precise sites and departments affected have not yet been determined.

Sap cast the two moves as complementary. "To safeguard our performance and growth in the long term, we must both invest in our industrial capabilities and make our organization more effective," he said.

## Two Tracks, One Message

The contrasting scale of the numbers tells the story. A €450 million capital commitment sits alongside roughly €60 million in annual savings—different orders of magnitude serving distinct purposes. The investment secures Hoboken's position in a strategic value chain; the cuts are about making the back office leaner and more competitive.

Notably, the job losses target support functions rather than the production lines the investment is meant to expand, suggesting the Hoboken build-out is not directly imperilled by the restructuring. That distinction matters for Antwerp, where the plant sits close to residential areas and where environmental and safety concerns have drawn sustained attention—including a chemical incident drill with local firefighters in late October and the recent opening of a green buffer zone between the site and nearby homes.

## What's Next

Consultations with trade unions and works council representatives have begun, and the final Belgian headcount impact will emerge from those talks. The investment itself is now locked in, with construction set to run through the end of the decade and the expanded, cobalt-capable refinery due to come online in 2030—the same year Umicore aims to have fully banked its €60 million in annual savings. How smoothly those two timelines converge, and how the union talks resolve, will determine whether the Hoboken announcement is remembered as a forward-looking bet or a more painful trade-off.
