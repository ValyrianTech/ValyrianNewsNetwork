# Democrat Scott Colom Wins Mississippi Senate in Upset

Democrat Scott Colom defeated Republican Sen. Cindy Hyde-Smith in the Mississippi Senate race, [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/mississippi-2026-senate-scott-colom-cindy-hyde-smith/) projected Wednesday, flipping a seat in a deep-red state and delivering Democrats their eighth pickup of the 2026 midterms. Colom, a 43-year-old district attorney, becomes the first Black senator from Mississippi since Reconstruction.

The race was called eight days after Election Day, after a vote count that at one point left only a few dozen ballots separating the two candidates. Colom's victory gives Democrats a Senate majority of at least 55 seats to Republicans' 44 when the new Congress convenes in January, with Alaska's race still uncalled.

## A historic first

Colom is the first Democrat elected to the Senate from Mississippi since 1982, and the first Black person elected to the U.S. Senate from the state since Blanche K. Bruce, who served from 1874 to 1881 during Reconstruction. Because Reconstruction-era senators were chosen by state legislators rather than voters, Colom is also the first Black person to be popularly elected to the Senate from Mississippi.

He thanked supporters and acknowledged the long odds during a victory speech late Wednesday at Tougaloo College in Jackson.

"Throughout this campaign, there were many times where I had to have faith. There were times that I couldn't get donors to answer the phone. They did not believe a moment like this was possible, but I kept the faith," Colom said, according to CBS News. "I did my first town hall in Jackson, Mississippi. ... Less than 10 people showed up, but I kept the faith."

Hyde-Smith, 67, who was appointed to the Senate in 2018 and was the first woman elected to represent Mississippi in Congress, conceded in a video message Wednesday night.

"While this is not the outcome we worked for or hoped for, I respect the decision of Mississippi voters," she said in the [video](https://x.com/cindyhydesmith/status/2120658269945344349/video/1?s=46). "I want to congratulate Scott Colom and wish him well as he takes on the tremendous responsibility of representing our great state."

## How a deep-red state became competitive

The race became surprisingly competitive in a state President Trump carried by 23 percentage points in 2024, [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/2026-election/scott-colom-mississippi-senate-election-win-democrat-hyde-smith-rcna605729) reported. National Democrats announced last month that they were investing in ad spending in Mississippi for the first time, and the Cook Political Report shifted its rating from solidly Republican to leaning Republican.

The headwinds facing Republicans nationally, an unpopular war in Iran and persistent affordability concerns, reached even reliably red states, NBC News reported. Colom campaigned on the cost of living and healthcare, opposing Medicaid cuts and tariffs, and argued that Hyde-Smith had failed to deliver for the state.

The contest also carried an unusual personal dimension. As a senator, Hyde-Smith had blocked Colom's federal judicial nomination during the Biden administration. On election day, before counting concluded, she said: "I blocked Scott Colom from being a federal judge, and you blocked him from being a US senator."

Colom, for his part, told NBC News before the election that Mississippi was not a lost cause but a "betrayed cause" — betrayed, he argued, by Hyde-Smith's record and by a national party that had long stopped competing in the state.

## A national wave

The Mississippi result extends a broad Democratic wave. The party flipped Senate seats in North Carolina, South Carolina, Texas, Ohio, Iowa, Kansas and Maine before adding Mississippi, the largest midterm gain for Democrats since 1986. Exit polling cited by NBC News found that majorities of Mississippi voters disapproved of Trump's job performance, said tariffs had hurt the economy and opposed U.S. military action in Iran.

The win also drew national attention for what it suggested about Black political power in the South. NAACP President Derrick Johnson said the campaign proved what was possible with investment.

"The Scott Colom campaign proves that it is possible to win in the South when there's investment," Johnson said, describing Mississippi not as "deep red" but as "an opportunity state," according to [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/nov/11/scott-colom-mississippi-senate-race).

## What to watch

The victory reshapes Mississippi's Senate representation and adds a new voice to a Democratic caucus that will hold a commanding majority in January. It also raises questions about which seats national Democrats will contest in the future after a cycle in which states once considered out of reach proved winnable.

With Alaska still outstanding, the final Senate balance could shift to 55–45, but Democrats' control of the chamber is now beyond doubt. For Mississippi, the immediate question is how Colom, a first-time statewide officeholder, translates a narrow upset into governing in a state where Republicans still dominate.

Hyde-Smith, meanwhile, closed her concession with a note of continuity. "An election may come to an end, but our commitment to Mississippi never will," she said. "I will always be proud to call Mississippi home."
