# Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang Resigns Over Alleged Spy Affair

Jensen Huang resigned as chief executive, president and board member of Nvidia effective immediately on Thursday, capping a stunning downfall for the leader of the world's most valuable company after revelations of two extramarital affairs—one involving a Chinese businesswoman whom U.S. intelligence reportedly assessed to be a Chinese government asset. The board named Chief Financial Officer Colette Kress as interim CEO and appointed longtime director Harvey C. Jones as its first-ever chair, according to [Nvidia's corporate announcement](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-announces-ceo-transition).

Shares fell as much as 14% at the opening bell before paring losses, at one point wiping more than $320 billion off Nvidia's value on the day and more than $600 billion for the week, [The Guardian reported](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/nov/12/nvidia-ceo-jensen-huang-resigns). The episode has rapidly escalated from a corporate scandal into a matter of U.S. national security, congressional oversight and the intensifying technology competition between Washington and Beijing.

## A Fall From the AI Pinnacle

Huang, 63, co-founded Nvidia in 1993 and built it into the central supplier of the advanced semiconductors that power the global artificial intelligence boom, with a market capitalization that hit $5 trillion at one point last year. Known for his signature black leather jacket, he became the public face of the AI era—a status that made his exit all the more abrupt.

The resignation followed reports that Huang had an affair with Alice Huang (also known as Huang Le), a Chinese citizen and former executive at Singapore-based cloud-computing firm Megaspeed International. The two are not related. According to [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/retail-consumer/nvidia-ceo-jensen-huang-step-down-big-blow-ai-boom-2026-11-12/), U.S. authorities concluded she was a Chinese intelligence asset, and her relationship with Huang raised alarms as high as the White House over concerns it could have been used to influence Nvidia's decisions on making advanced AI chips more available in China.

A second affair, with Nvidia's vice president of healthcare Kimberly Powell—who reported directly to Huang—was reported by The New York Times late Wednesday, adding fresh pressure as the board convened to weigh his future.

## The Board's Investigation

Nvidia's board had already launched an investigation, led by independent directors with outside counsel, into whether Huang had been the "target of a foreign intelligence effort." The company retained the law firm Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz to conduct what it described as a "thorough and independent" probe into the Alice Huang relationship, as [The Guardian detailed](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/nov/11/nvidia-hires-leading-us-law-firm-to-investigate-jensen-huang-relationship).

Nvidia has said it had "no reason to believe" national security or company secrets were compromised. Beijing has dismissed the spying allegations as a smear campaign and denied attempting to ensnare Huang. Through it all, the board's public statement confirming the departure made no mention of the scandal, instead praising Huang's "profound" contributions.

## Kress Takes the Reins

Kress, who has served as Nvidia's CFO since 2013 after nearly 25 years in finance roles at Cisco and Microsoft, will hold both roles while the board searches for a permanent successor from among internal and external candidates. Nvidia acknowledged it had no succession plan in place.

"Nvidia's strength has always come from its people, and together with our leadership team, I am committed to building on the extraordinary foundation Jensen created over the last 33 years," Kress said in the company statement. Jones, the new board chair, said the board has "full confidence" in Kress and expects operations, customer commitments and the product roadmap to "continue without interruption."

## Markets and the AI Bubble Question

The market reaction was severe but not catastrophic. Nvidia shares recovered from their steepest declines as bargain hunters stepped in, though the company remained on course to shed nearly $200 billion in market value, Reuters noted. Rivals AMD and Broadcom each rose about 6% as investors rotated toward competitors.

The turmoil arrives at a delicate moment for AI stocks, which had already wobbled amid soaring cash burn and rising debt at traditionally cash-rich tech giants. Analysts framed the central question as one of execution rather than technology.

"The risk is not that Nvidia suddenly loses its technology roadmap ... The risk is execution. Jensen has been the chief conductor of an extraordinarily complex system," said Michael Feldman, a portfolio manager at ClearBridge Investments. "In a world with real bottlenecks, that coordination is a competitive advantage in itself."

Neil Wilson of Saxo UK told the Guardian that Huang's technical mastery was "basically unique and impossible to replicate," even as he noted Nvidia remains well positioned on upcoming products and orders.

## Political Reverberations

The scandal has spilled quickly into Washington. President Donald Trump, who had initially defended Huang, posted on social media that former Apple CEO Tim Cook should take over: "A Legend replaces a Legend!" House Democratic leader Hakeem Jeffries criticized the intervention as undermining the free market, according to [AP](https://apnews.com/article/nvidia-megaspeed-china-singapore-huang-5403f152a601cf82ce939f1d24fffa0f).

House Speaker Mike Johnson said it was "critically important" to choose the right successor and noted a growing consensus in Congress on limiting semiconductor exports to China. The House Foreign Affairs Committee is still pursuing Huang's testimony, with Chairman Brian Mast threatening a subpoena if terms are not agreed. Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick also faces questions over his handling of a key trade blacklist.

## What to Watch

Nvidia is scheduled to report third-quarter financial results on Nov. 17, its first earnings call without Huang at the helm—an early test of whether the company can steady investor confidence. The Wachtell investigation and a parallel FBI probe may yet produce public findings, while a shareholder lawsuit alleges investors were misled.

Beyond Nvidia, the affair has sharpened scrutiny of how the personal relationships of industry leaders intersect with espionage risk at companies at the heart of the AI race. As analyst Asa Hiken of eMarketer put it, Huang's affair with an alleged Chinese spy "raises much bigger questions about the security risks facing companies at the center of the AI race."

For now, the company Huang built over three decades must prove it can thrive without him—and answer who, ultimately, will lead it into its next chapter.
