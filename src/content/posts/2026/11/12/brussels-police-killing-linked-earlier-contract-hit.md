# Brussels Police Killing Linked to Earlier Contract Hit

The gunmen who fatally shot a Brussels police officer last month are now believed to have killed another man just hours earlier the same evening — a revelation that merges two apparently separate shootings into a single, drug-linked crime series. According to [RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/les-auteurs-du-tir-mortel-sur-le-policier-nordine-el-talbi-auraient-tue-un-autre-homme-plus-tot-dans-la-soiree-11814404), which confirmed the link through its own sources, investigators have concluded that the same perpetrators carried out both killings on 12 October 2026.

## Two deaths, one evening

The first victim, an Albanian national, was gunned down at 17:50 on Monday 12 October in the lobby of an apartment building on Rue Wiertz in Ixelles, in the heart of the European Quarter. He was struck by seven or eight pistol rounds; witnesses saw three or four men flee. The man had no criminal record in Belgium, but multiple sources indicate the killing bore all the hallmarks of a drug-world score-settling — a contract murder.

Hours later, Inspector Nordine El Talbi, 41, was shot dead while, in plain clothes, monitoring a stolen vehicle parked on Avenue Carton de Wiart in Jette. That car had been taken earlier the same evening in a carjacking in Tubize and later abandoned in Jette; drugs and three firearms were found inside it. El Talbi, a member of the anti-aggression brigade with more than 15 years of experience, died from a bullet fired from inside the vehicle.

## A link initially ruled out

At the first press conference following El Talbi's death, Brussels King's Prosecutor Julien Moinil said there appeared to be no connection between the two shootings. Reporting by [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/fr/2026/11/12/les-meurtriers-du-policier-nordine-el-talbi-a-jette-avaient-deja/) indicates investigators have since — reportedly aided by CCTV footage — established that the same perpetrators were responsible for both. The Brussels prosecutor's office declined to comment on the link.

## Six suspects, an international manhunt

The alleged perpetrators fled abroad after the killings. Fewer than a week later, three were arrested in Almería, southern Spain, by the Guardia Civil, apparently heading toward Morocco; a fourth was detained at a Paris railway station. As [VRT NWS reported](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/fr/2026/10/19/six-suspects-sous-mandat-d-arret-pour-le-meurtre-dun-policier-a/) on 19 October, six suspects in total were placed under arrest warrant — two arrested in Belgium, one in France and three in Spain — all of them Belgian nationals from Brussels known to the justice system for drug-trafficking offences.

Five of the suspects were in the stolen car abandoned in Jette. One is a minor; five are adults. It has not been formally established which suspect fired the fatal shot, though RTBF reports that, according to its information, the minor is believed to have fired the round that killed El Talbi. The six are charged with the murder of a person holding public authority and participation in a criminal organisation.

Prosecutor Moinil expressly thanked the Spanish and French police and judicial authorities for their help in the investigation, and confirmed that narcotics and three weapons were recovered from the abandoned vehicle.

## Analysis: violence spills into the European Quarter

The case fits a wider pattern of drug-related score-settling and contract killings in the Brussels region. What sets it apart is where it unfolded: the Ixelles murder took place in daylight at 17:50 on a busy street steps from EU institutions and a densely populated residential neighbourhood — a setting that has heightened public concern about safety in an area usually associated with European governance rather than gangland violence. The link between a contract-style killing and the subsequent fatal shooting of a police officer underscores how drug-gang violence can spill over into public spaces with little warning.

## What's next

The Brussels prosecutor's office has so far refused to officially confirm the link between the two killings, leaving open how the charges against the six suspects may evolve — including whether counts related to the Ixelles contract killing will be added. Questions also remain over the precise role of the minor believed to have fired the fatal shot, and whether the case will prompt political or policy responses on drug-related violence and police safety in Brussels.

The lawyer for the Albanian victim's family has criticised the prosecutor's office for a lack of communication in the case, regretting that the family had to learn of the link through the press — a reminder that behind a widening investigation are two families seeking answers.
