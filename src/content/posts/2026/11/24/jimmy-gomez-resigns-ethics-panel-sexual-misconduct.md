# Rep. Jimmy Gomez Resigns After Ethics Panel Findings

Democratic Rep. Jimmy Gomez of California announced Monday that he will resign from Congress effective Dec. 1, within an hour of the House Ethics Committee releasing a report that found "substantial evidence" he engaged in sexual misconduct by making unwelcome sexual advances and nonconsensual sexual contact toward a House staffer, [the Associated Press reported](https://apnews.com/article/jimmy-gomez-house-of-representatives-sexual-misconduct-4208a3652c33f8709812fed0ba904215).

The five-term congressman, who represented a Los Angeles-area district, was already set to leave at the end of the year after losing his reelection bid this month. His early departure removes a sitting member from the lame-duck Congress and heads off the prospect of a sanction vote by his colleagues.

## Ethics Panel Details Findings

The bipartisan committee concluded that Gomez's conduct caused the staffer "significant and ongoing distress" and contributed to her decision to leave House employment altogether. According to [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/house-ethics-investigation-jimmy-gomez-sexual-misconduct/), the panel said his behavior toward the aide "could constitute criminal misdemeanor sexual abuse."

The report described two encounters. The woman, identified only as "Witness 1," said Gomez made nonconsensual sexual contact with her at a Washington, D.C., bar in February 2024 and later grabbed her and tried to kiss her in his congressional office that summer. She was in her mid-twenties at the time and worked in a House leadership office — not Gomez's own office.

CNN, which [independently spoke with the woman](https://edition.cnn.com/2026/11/23/politics/house-ethics-committee-reports-jimmy-gomez) and reviewed text messages corroborating her account, reported that she described the bar incident as "nonconsensual whatsoever" and said she "was shocked by it." The committee said Gomez "failed in his obligation to behave in a manner that reflects creditably on the House, and his conduct was exacerbated by his failure to appreciate the inherent power imbalance between House Members and House staff."

## Gomez Disputes Report, Acknowledges 'Personal Mistakes'

Gomez rejected the report's conclusions and its process, saying his testimony was "selectively quoted, disparaged, and disregarded." But he also said he takes full responsibility for the "personal mistakes I committed outside of my marriage" and apologized to his family and constituents.

"I have strong disagreements with the Committee's report and its conclusions," Gomez said in his statement. "With this in mind, I see no other alternative than to resign, effective December 1, to allow for my staff to close out important constituent casework and needs."

The committee did not recommend a specific sanction, leaving punishment to the full House. "The House should take whatever action it deems appropriate in light of the Committee's findings," the panel said.

## A Reckoning on Capitol Hill

Gomez is the third House member to resign this year in the face of sexual misconduct allegations, part of an unusually dense cluster of ethics cases involving lawmakers of both parties. Democratic House leaders — Hakeem Jeffries, Katherine Clark and Pete Aguilar — called the findings "serious and deeply troubling" and said it was "appropriate that he is stepping down."

The woman's attorneys, Lisa J. Banks and Sarah E. Nesbitt, said the report "confirms that Representative Gomez sexually harassed and assaulted our client while she was a staffer on Capitol Hill," and urged Congress to act.

"Representative Gomez's conduct did not occur in a vacuum, and immediate steps must be taken by Congress to rein in what the report describes as a 'toxic and predatory culture,'" they said. "Democracy cannot function properly when women must endure harassment and abuse as a condition of public service."

The case has intensified pressure for reform. A bipartisan working group led by the Democratic and Republican Women's Caucuses has been reviewing changes to the way Congress handles harassment complaints, [according to the AP](https://apnews.com/article/congress-sexual-harassment-staff-ethics-complaints-2966245df0c1aaf29d9239a100307e39). The week before the report, the Ethics Committee moved to speed up sexual misconduct probes and to codify public disclosure of its findings.

## What's Next

Gomez's resignation takes effect Dec. 1, and Rep.-elect Angela Gonzales-Torres — the fellow Democrat who defeated him — is expected to be seated when the new Congress convenes in January. Whether the Ethics Committee's finding leads to any criminal referral remains an open question; the panel noted the conduct could constitute misdemeanor sexual abuse but did not refer the matter.

The broader test is institutional. With more lawmakers facing scrutiny than in recent memory, the working group's reform recommendations are expected in the near future — and will determine whether this year's wave of resignations produces durable change or merely a series of individual departures, as [the BBC noted](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/cm2l5kgpw5l5o).
