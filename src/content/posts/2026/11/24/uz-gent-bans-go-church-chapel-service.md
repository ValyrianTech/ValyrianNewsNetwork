# UZ Gent bans Go Church over unnoticed chapel service

Ghent University Hospital (UZ Gent) has banned the controversial youth church Go Church from all of its grounds after the group held a religious service in a hospital chapel last weekend without the institution's knowledge. The hospital only learned of the gathering the following day, when it was shown a flyer, and says it now distances itself from the event and has agreed with church authorities that no further Go Church activities will be permitted pending investigations.

The service took place on Sunday evening, 22 November, in a chapel on the UZ Gent campus. According to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/24/gent-uz-universitair-ziekenhuis-go-church-evenement-kapel-verbod/), the hospital was not informed in advance and only discovered what had happened a day later.

## Misleading flyer pointed to the wrong building

UZ Gent says its first indication came from a flyer that named the hospital's "hoorcentrum" — a hearing centre linked to the Amplifon chain — as the venue. An internal check found that nobody at that location was aware of any event.

"There was mention of an event at the hearing centre, but enquiries showed that nobody there was aware of it," spokesperson Elien Valcke told VRT NWS. "Go Church spread misleading information about it."

Further investigation established that a gathering had indeed taken place, but elsewhere on the hospital grounds — in a chapel. Crucially, that building is managed autonomously by church authorities, who, rather than the hospital, decide what activities occur there.

## Church was told it was a 'Pentecostal group'

The church authorities have acknowledged they granted permission — but say they did not initially know who was asking. Canon Bert Vanderhaeghen said in a written response that the priest received a request on Friday evening from "a Pentecostal group" after their contract at a previous location was cancelled.

"Only later did it become clear that it was 'Go Church'," Vanderhaeghen said. "The priest then allowed only a celebration and also decided to attend it himself."

Valcke stressed that the hospital "was not aware and distances itself from the gathering." UZ Gent has now agreed with the church authorities that no further Go Church gatherings will be permitted on the campus, pending further investigations.

## A group already under scrutiny

The incident lands at a sensitive moment for Go Church. In October, a joint investigation by VRT NWS's Pano programme and the newspaper De Morgen, based on more than 30 testimonies from current and former followers and undercover reporting, accused the movement of manipulating and financially exploiting vulnerable young people — sometimes minors. Religious sociologist Wim Vandewiele said the accounts met several of the 13 official criteria Belgium uses to identify harmful sects. Go Church denies the accusations, and a judicial investigation is now under way.

According to [La Libre/Belga](https://www.lalibre.be/dernieres-depeches/2026/11/24/le-mouvement-controverse-go-church-nest-plus-le-bienvenu-sur-le-campus-de-luz-gent-D7W7JHGO7NE4XJ5YYDG2COOGOI/), the UZ Gent measures were explicitly taken pending the outcome of that judicial probe.

Go Church is a youth church of Ghanaian origin and part of the Lighthouse Group of Churches, an international network of 7,649 churches led by Ghanaian bishop Dag Heward-Mills, whom followers call "Daddy" or "prophet." In Belgium the movement previously operated under the names Pleasant Surprise Church International and Mustard Seed Chapel International. According to the organisation itself, it is active in ten Belgian cities and draws roughly 450 people to its services each week.

## Go Church celebrates 'a wonderful time'

Go Church Gent made no direct comment on the hospital's account. In an Instagram post announcing the gathering, the group wrote: "We had a blessed and joyful service in the presence of the Lord. We were filled with His presence, His love and His peace. What a wonderful time in His house!"

VRT NWS said it contacted Go Church via the Instagram channel that announced the event but had received no response at the time of publication.

## A governance gap on public grounds

The episode raises an uncomfortable question for a public university hospital: how did a group under judicial investigation come to hold a service on its campus at all? The answer lies partly in the unusual status of the chapel, which is administered by church authorities rather than the hospital itself — a separation that allowed the gathering to proceed without the institution's knowledge.

It also echoes a pattern that has followed Go Church across Flanders. When municipalities, universities and venues began cutting ties in late October, several reported that the group had booked rooms under other names or presented itself as a cultural association. The misleading flyer naming an unaware hearing centre fits that same playbook.

The wider crackdown has been swift. Over recent weeks, cities including Mechelen, Ghent and Antwerp, along with KU Leuven and several schools, have moved to bar the group, with Ghent mayor Mathias De Clercq mapping its activities across the city.

## What to watch

Several questions remain unanswered. It is not yet clear who within Go Church arranged the UZ Gent chapel booking, or on what basis the group presented itself to the priest as an ordinary Pentecostal congregation. Nor is it known whether the church authorities who manage the chapel will face any review of their autonomous arrangement.

Much will also depend on the judicial investigation tied to the Pano findings. For now, UZ Gent's ban is provisional — and the hospital will be watching the probe as closely as anyone.
