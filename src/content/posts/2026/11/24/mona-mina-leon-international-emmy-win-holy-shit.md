# Mona Mina Leon Wins First Emmy for a Belgian Actress

Belgian actress Mona Mina Leon has won an International Emmy Award for Best Performance by an Actress for her lead role in the Flemish drama series *Holy Sh!t*, becoming the first Belgian actress ever to take home the honour, [VRT NWS reported](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/24/mona-mina-leon-wint-als-eerste-belgische-actrice-international-e/). The 32-year-old was presented with the award at the 54th International Emmy Awards ceremony, held on Monday evening at the New York Hilton Midtown in Manhattan.

The win caps a milestone year for Leon, who in September had already become the first Belgian actress ever nominated in the category. The International Emmys, distinct from the Primetime Emmy Awards, honour the best television programming produced and aired outside the United States.

## A role built on empathy

In *Holy Sh!t*, an eight-episode series produced by Fobic Films for the Flemish streaming platform Streamz, Leon plays Yasmine De Laet, a young woman with Gilles de la Tourette syndrome who dreams of becoming a teacher but faces misunderstanding and mockery from pupils and colleagues because of her tics. The story turns when she reconnects with a father who has been missing for 25 years and who, seeking forgiveness, reveals he is terminally ill.

To prepare, Leon worked with the Belgian Gilles de la Tourette association to refine her portrayal of a condition still widely misunderstood. The series premiered at the Cannes International Series Festival (Canneseries) in April 2025 before its release on Streamz later that year.

## Gratitude on stage in New York

Accepting the award, Leon struck a reflective, generous tone. "I grew up with people from all layers of society, with different religions and languages. That taught me that the other person is never the problem, because the other person does not exist," she said, according to VRT NWS. She also thanked those who had shown her kindness without expecting anything in return: "It takes a lot of courage to live this life and to walk through the world with an open heart."

After the ceremony she summed up her reaction simply: "What an honour and what a surprise!"

## A strong night for Belgian content

Leon was not Belgium's only winner. The miniseries *De klas van 2000*, produced by De Mensen for the VTM GO platform, took the prize in the Short-Form Series category. Directed by Heleen Declercq and written by Geerard Van de Walle, the series tells the story of a father who comes out as gay and the consequences for his relationship with the mother of his child.

The broader ceremony reflected the global reach of the awards. Hosted by American actor Michael Urie, the 54th edition handed out trophies across 16 categories drawn from 64 nominees representing 22 countries, with the United Kingdom collecting the most awards of the night. France's *Carême* won Best Drama, Argentina's *División Palermo* took Best Comedy, and the UK's Bobby Schofield won Best Actor for *Unforgivable*, [Deadline reported](https://deadline.com/2026/11/2026-international-emmys-winners-list-1237232118/).

## Long overdue recognition

Belgian productions have enjoyed a steady run of International Emmy success over the past decade and a half, but until now those wins came in non-scripted and comedy categories. *Restaurant Misverstand* (2024), *Hoe zal ik het zeggen* (2018) and *Sorry voor alles* (2017) all won in Non-Scripted Entertainment, while *Wat als* (2014) and *Benidorm Bastards* (2011) claimed Best Comedy. Leon's victory is the first in an acting category, a distinction that carries particular weight for Flemish-language drama.

Leon's path to the stage in New York was anything but conventional. Born in 1994 and raised in Anderlecht, she studied drama at RITCS in Brussels and broke through in the VRT series *1985*, about the Bende van Nijvel murders. In a candid 2025 interview, she spoke openly about a difficult childhood and years of therapy, describing her craft as part of a broader process of healing and self-discovery.

## What to watch

The award is likely to raise the international profile of both Leon and the Flemish production sector, which has increasingly found audiences beyond Belgium through platforms such as Streamz and VTM GO. For now, the actress appears to be savouring the moment as much as the milestone. Her win—and the recognition of a series built around a misunderstood condition—signals that Belgian storytelling is finding its place on the world's most prominent television stages.
