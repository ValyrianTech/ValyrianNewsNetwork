# China, Switzerland Hold Fifth Strategic Dialogue in Nanjing

Chinese Foreign Minister Wang Yi and Swiss Vice President and Foreign Minister Ignazio Cassis co-chaired the fifth round of the China–Switzerland Foreign Ministers' Strategic Dialogue in Nanjing on 23 November, a regular high-level channel through which the two countries discuss strategic issues and expand cooperation, [People's Daily reported](http://politics.people.com.cn/n1/2026/1124/c1001-40839205.html), citing the Xinhua News Agency.

The meeting placed trade, free-market principles and China–Europe economic frictions at its centre, with Wang using the platform to appeal to Bern to help smooth Beijing's trade differences with Brussels.

## Steady progress, framed as a model

Wang, who is also a member of the Political Bureau of the Communist Party of China Central Committee, described China–Switzerland relations as "accelerating toward new and better directions" and "steadily walking at the forefront of China–Europe relations."

He pointed to the signing of the protocol to upgrade the bilateral free trade agreement, which President Xi Jinping and Swiss Federal Council President Parmelin jointly witnessed, as a signal of both sides' commitment to openness and free trade.

According to the People's Daily account, Wang laid out three principles underpinning China's diplomacy as reflected in ties with Switzerland: equality among all countries regardless of size and opposition to power politics; respect for independently chosen development paths and non-interference in internal affairs; and complementarity of strengths for mutual benefit.

## Wang presses China–EU trade message

Turning to Beijing's broader trade tensions with the European Union, Wang urged that China and Europe "break out of the so-called 'trade imbalance' narrative trap," resolving differences through dialogue and consultation and "making the pie of cooperation bigger."

He asked Switzerland to play a "positive influence" in helping China and the EU smoothly resolve their economic and trade differences — casting Bern, one of Beijing's most cooperative Western partners, as a potential bridge in managing China–EU friction.

The call comes amid persistent friction between Beijing and Brussels over trade imbalances and disputes. Switzerland's long-standing role at the "forefront" of China–Europe relations — it was among the first Western countries to recognize the People's Republic in 1950 — gives the appeal added weight.

## Cassis: one-China principle, Davos anniversary

Cassis reaffirmed that Switzerland will "firmly uphold the one-China principle," deepen mutual understanding and trust, and expand cooperation across fields.

He noted that next year marks the 10th anniversary of Xi Jinping's landmark speech at the World Economic Forum in Davos, describing it as still influential and of guiding significance. Over the past decade, Cassis said, China has become "an important force driving globalization and addressing global challenges."

Cassis also voiced Switzerland's willingness to work with China to safeguard free trade, oppose protectionism, jointly address climate change and strengthen global artificial intelligence governance — cooperation themes echoed at the previous round and now elevated in the bilateral agenda.

## A relationship institutionalized

The dialogue's rotating format — the fourth round was held in Bellinzona, Switzerland, in October 2025 — reflects a stable, institutionalized relationship. At that meeting, [China Daily reported](https://global.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202510/11/WS68ea3651a310f735438b46ed.html), Wang praised Switzerland's commitment to the one-China policy, flagged expansion of the FTA's zero-tariff categories to benefit businesses and consumers, and signalled China's readiness to extend its unilateral visa-free policy for Swiss citizens.

The new round builds on those commitments. The FTA upgrade protocol, once in force, is expected to broaden zero-tariff treatment and deepen commercial ties, while both sides have shown growing interest in emerging fields such as artificial intelligence, advanced manufacturing and the green transition.

## What's next

The fifth round produced a strong statement of principles rather than a detailed list of new signed agreements, leaving several questions open: precisely when the upgraded FTA enters into force, which product categories gain zero-tariff status, and whether Bern will formally embrace the bridge-building role Beijing has sketched for it in China–EU trade matters.

What is clear is the direction of travel. With the Davos anniversary approaching in January 2027 and China continuing to court friendly European partners, the China–Switzerland channel looks set to remain one of Beijing's most reliable conduits in the West — a small-nation partnership that both capitals are keen to present as a template for a fragmenting global order.
