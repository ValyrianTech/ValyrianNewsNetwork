# UPS Strike Halts 300,000 Parcels in Peak Delivery Week

Some 525 UPS workers in Belgium are on strike during the busiest delivery week of the year, fighting the company's plan to cut their jobs as an estimated 200,000 to 300,000 parcels pile up undelivered. The walkout, which began last Friday, coincides with Black Friday, Cyber Monday and the Sinterklaas gift-buying season — a deliberate show of force at the moment it can inflict the most pressure on the American logistics giant.

A conciliation meeting with a social mediator opened at 10:00 on Tuesday in Brussels to try to break the deadlock. As of midday, the outcome remained unknown, but unions have warned that the action will continue and escalate if talks fail.

## A plan that would cut two-thirds of the workforce

The dispute dates back to June, when UPS management announced at a works council its intention to eliminate up to 525 of roughly 800 jobs in Belgium.

"This affects drivers, parcel sorters and workers handling customs formalities," Hans Elsen, secretary of the Christian union ACV Puls, [told RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/ups-prevoit-de-supprimer-jusqu-a-525-emplois-en-belgique-11746157) at the time. The large majority of staff work at the UPS site in Diegem, in Flemish Brabant near Brussels Airport, with an additional distribution centre in Lummen, Limburg.

Unions argue the scale is disproportionate. Elsen said parcel volumes had fallen "a little, but not to such a degree" as to justify such a drastic restructuring, and accused the company of seeking to replace permanent contracts with cheaper freelancers.

"We suspect the company wants to replace fixed contracts with less costly freelancers," he said. UPS declined to comment on "speculation," saying by principle it does not respond to questions about possible proposals.

## Blockades and a growing backlog

The action entered a sharper phase on Friday 20 November, when morning teams found no work because UPS had diverted volumes to subcontractors. Workers blocked UPS depots in Diegem and Lummen, four subcontractor installations near Diegem, and the Brucargo freight zone at Brussels Airport.

"No sorting has been done for four days. We see UPS containers piling up in the parking lots," Elsen said. "Around 55,000 parcels will again be unable to be sorted," he added, bringing the total backlog to between 200,000 and 300,000 parcels, according to the [RTBF report](https://www.rtbf.be/article/greve-ups-en-plein-black-friday-cyber-monday-et-saint-nicolas-au-dela-des-centaines-de-milliers-de-colis-qui-s-accumulent-le-combat-de-525-travailleurs-pour-leur-emploi-11820245) on Tuesday.

## Why the timing matters

The strike is calibrated for maximum impact. Black Friday, Cyber Monday and Sinterklaas — the gift-giving feast celebrated on 6 December in Belgium and 5 December in the Netherlands — fall within days of one another, making this the peak retail and parcel week of the year.

The union has also sought to widen the pressure beyond UPS's own depots. On 18 November, more than 200 workers blockaded the European logistics centre of Skechers, one of UPS's largest clients, near Liège. Targeting a major customer signals that the union intends to hit the company's commercial relationships, not just its internal operations.

The dispute is unfolding against a backdrop of heightened labour tension in Belgium. On Monday 23 November, a national demonstration of 50,000 to 70,000 people protested against the "Arizona" federal coalition's policies, and postal distribution in Wallonia was disrupted as about a third of bpost workers walked out.

## What's next

Everything now hinges on the Brussels conciliation meeting. If it produces an agreement, the dispute could ease — but normalisation will not be immediate. Because sorting centres across Europe are also saturated during this peak period, Elsen warned that "there is little room to absorb this backlog."

If talks fail, the unions have pledged to continue and potentially escalate the blockades, threatening to push the backlog higher through the crucial retail week and to leave consumers waiting on Black Friday and Sinterklaas purchases.

Still unresolved is the precise distribution of the threatened cuts between Diegem, Lummen and subcontractors, whether the 525 figure is final or a ceiling — UPS has not confirmed it — and the terms of any social plan covering severance and redeployment. For the 525 workers and their families, the answer cannot come soon enough.
