# Court Revives Trump Bid to Erase Hush Money Conviction

A federal appeals court on Monday handed President Donald Trump a fresh pathway to erasing his hush money criminal conviction, reviving his bid to move the case into federal court, where he could seek to have it dismissed on presidential immunity grounds, [AP News reported](https://apnews.com/article/trump-hush-money-appeal-immunity-85d97732018b716f4fe87a554698ec02).

The ruling from a three-judge panel of the 2nd U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals reverses a lower-court decision that had kept the case in New York state court, and directs a federal judge to promptly decide whether the case should be removed to federal jurisdiction. If Trump succeeds, the case could be dismissed — potentially wiping out the only criminal conviction of a sitting U.S. president.

## What the Ruling Does

The two-page order finds that the late U.S. District Judge Alvin Hellerstein "erred in holding that removal is categorically unavailable in a state criminal case" after a federal officer has been convicted, [according to Politico](https://www.politico.com/news/2026/11/23/appeals-court-opens-door-to-moving-trumps-criminal-case-to-federal-court). Hellerstein had repeatedly denied Trump's requests to move the case, most recently in August 2026, before his death in October at age 92.

The panel set no deadline for the district judge. It also set in motion a halt to Trump's parallel appeal in New York state court, where the mid-level Appellate Division heard oral arguments on November 5 but has not yet ruled. Trump's lawyer, Robert Giuffra Jr., notified that court of the ruling and requested that "no further proceedings occur in the state courts" while jurisdiction is settled.

## The Underlying Conviction

In May 2024, a Manhattan jury found Trump guilty on 34 felony counts of falsifying business records to conceal a $130,000 hush money payment to adult film performer Stormy Daniels, whose affair allegations threatened his 2016 campaign. Trump denies the affair and any wrongdoing. The verdict made him the first U.S. president convicted of a crime.

He was sentenced in January 2025 to an "unconditional discharge" — no jail time and no financial penalty — but the conviction remains on his record. Prosecutors argued that Trump disguised reimbursements to his former fixer Michael Cohen as legal expenses, elevating misdemeanor record-falsification to felonies in service of another crime.

## The Immunity Question

At the heart of the fight is the Supreme Court's July 2024 ruling that presidents enjoy "presumptive immunity" for official acts and that prosecutors cannot use official acts as evidence that unofficial actions were illegal. Trump's lawyers argue state prosecutors relied on evidence — testimony about Oval Office conversations, workflow and social media posts — that would be shielded in federal court.

During oral argument, Judge Raymond J. Lohier Jr. voiced skepticism about the procedural maneuver, saying, "You're asking us to embrace an entire procedural mechanism that I don't see anywhere authorized by Congress," [The Guardian reported](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/nov/23/legal-path-erase-donald-trump-hush-money-conviction). Judge John M. Walker Jr. similarly asked how briefs already filed in the state appellate division would be handled.

The panel that ruled Monday was ideologically mixed: Lohier and Jose A. Cabranes were appointed by Democrats, and Walker by a Republican — underscoring that the ruling was procedural rather than an endorsement of Trump's merits arguments.

## Analysis and Implications

The decision does not overturn the verdict; it simply revives the removal pathway and pauses the state appeal. Trump's chances of success are widely seen as higher in federal court, and particularly at the 2nd Circuit, which has now sided with him procedurally twice.

A favorable removal ruling would set off what AP called a "legal merry-go-round": the case would go to a federal district court, then back to the 2nd Circuit, before any Supreme Court review. That process could take months — far faster than the state route, which Giuffra said could leave the president waiting "years."

Al Jazeera noted that the ruling leaves the jury's guilty verdict intact, but clears the way for the president to argue that state prosecutors secured his conviction using evidence shielded by presidential immunity.

## What to Watch

Four questions remain open: whether the district court grants removal, whether it survives a full 2nd Circuit review, how the Supreme Court's "official acts" definition applies to the trial evidence, and whether the removal of a post-conviction state case is authorized by statute at all.

The Manhattan District Attorney's Office, which prosecuted the case, declined to comment. A spokesperson for Trump's legal team said the president "appreciates the 2nd Circuit's prompt and correct ruling, as he continues to win against Democrat weaponization of our Justice System."
