# Trump Awards Medal of Freedom to Rudy Giuliani

President Donald Trump on Tuesday awarded the Presidential Medal of Freedom — the nation's highest civilian honor — to former New York City Mayor Rudy Giuliani, honoring a career defined by his stewardship of the city through the September 11, 2001, attacks and, more recently, by his role as one of Trump's most loyal and embattled political allies. According to [The Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/trump-medal-freedom-giuliani-976fc5b276e6e590c2d28e82a81a6e63), the ceremony was held in the Oval Office and closed to the press.

Giuliani, 82, accepted the honor in a statement, saying he was "honored and humbled to receive the Presidential Medal of Freedom from my nation and from our Commander-in-Chief, President Donald J. Trump." He invoked a favorite quotation from Ronald Reagan — "There is no limit to what a man can do or where he can go if he doesn't mind who gets the credit" — and shared the recognition with "all the men and women who worked with me, disagreed with me, and loved me — particularly those who lost their lives in the causes we undertook together."

## An Honor Rooted in 9/11 and a Mayoral Legacy

The White House framed the award around Giuliani's record as a federal prosecutor and two-term mayor. Assistant press secretary Olivia Wales said that as U.S. Attorney for the Southern District of New York, Giuliani "took down mob bosses and led a relentless war on crime," and that as mayor he "transformed New York from a crime-ridden city into one of the safest big cities in the world." After the September 11 attacks, she added, he "displayed strong American leadership in the face of evil."

Trump has called Giuliani, a fellow New Yorker, the "greatest Mayor in the history of New York City, and an equally great American Patriot." The two men stood side by side in photographs from Bedminster, New Jersey, in November 2016 and at 9/11 commemorations in the years since.

## A Career Shaped by the 2020 Election

The medal also lands at a pointed moment. Giuliani's public life has traced a steep rise and fall since he left City Hall. After a brief 2008 campaign for the Republican presidential nomination, he reemerged as Trump's personal attorney and a central figure in the effort to overturn the 2020 election, a role that carries consequences to this day.

As [The New York Post](https://nypost.com/2026/11/24/us-news/trump-to-honor-americas-mayor-rudy-giuliani-with-the-presidential-medal-of-freedom/) reported, New York courts disbarred Giuliani in July 2024 for repeatedly making false statements about the election, and Washington, D.C.'s highest court followed that September. A federal jury had earlier hit him with a $148 million defamation verdict for defaming two Georgia election workers, Ruby Freeman and Shaye Moss, whom he had accused of ballot fraud. He filed for bankruptcy before settling with them in January 2025.

Much of that legal weight has since lifted. Trump pardoned Giuliani and 76 others last November for their efforts to contest the 2020 results — a largely symbolic act, since a presidential pardon reaches only federal crimes. Weeks later, a Georgia judge dismissed the racketeering case against Trump, Giuliani and more than a dozen co-defendants after the prosecutor who inherited the file declined to proceed.

## Timing and a Promise Kept

Trump first said he would bestow the medal in 2025, shortly after Giuliani was seriously injured in a New Hampshire car crash. Minutes before that crash, Giuliani and his spokesman, Ted Goodman, who was driving, had stopped to help a woman who had flagged them down claiming to be a victim of domestic violence, state police said. Writing on Truth Social at the time, Trump said details would follow and praised Giuliani as a "great American Patriot." Tuesday's ceremony made good on that promise roughly 15 months later.

Giuliani confirmed the pending award a day earlier on Steve Bannon's podcast, telling the host he was "very, very humbled and very honored not only to receive it but to receive it from him," according to [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/nov/24/trump-giuliani-presidential-medal-of-freedom). Before the closed ceremony, the White House posted a compilation video of Giuliani online, saying he had left an "undeniable mark on American history."

After the event, Giuliani's son Andrew told reporters on the White House grounds that it was "incredibly special" to see his father receive the award from Trump. Asked whether he was surprised by the recognition given the recent controversy, he defended his father's work for Trump: "I think what Rudy Giuliani has done over the last six years will go down in history as one of the most important legal jobs that has ever been done by any attorney. To defend the president of the United States against some of the evil forces that are out there, I am so proud of my father."

## Analysis: A Medal That Cuts Both Ways

The award underscores how Trump has used the presidency's symbolic powers to reward loyalty and settle scores with the institutions that once censured his allies. In his second term, Trump has also awarded the medal posthumously to Welles Crowther, the "man in the red bandanna" who rescued people during 9/11, and to the assassinated conservative activist Charlie Kirk.

For Giuliani, the honor is a striking coda to a career that once made him a unifying national figure. Supporters see the medal as overdue recognition of a prosecutor who broke the mob's grip on New York and a mayor who steadied a shattered city. Critics see a different symbolism — a celebration of a man whom the legal system disbarred and a jury ordered to pay a nine-figure judgment, elevated at the very institutions that once held him accountable.

## What's Next

The medal does not resolve Giuliani's remaining legal exposure. Arizona is the last open front: Attorney General Kris Mayes dropped her fake-electors case after a judge ruled the grand jury never saw the relevant federal election law, but the judge left the door open to refiling, and Mayes — re-elected this month — has vowed to return to a grand jury. For now, the ceremony closes one chapter in a long and polarizing public life — while leaving the next one, as so often with Giuliani, still unwritten.
