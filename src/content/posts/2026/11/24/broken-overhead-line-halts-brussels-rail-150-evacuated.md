# Broken Overhead Line Halts Brussels Rail, 150 Evacuated

A snapped overhead contact cable brought rail traffic to a standstill on two lines through eastern Brussels on Tuesday morning, stranding a passenger train inside the Jubelpark tunnel and forcing the evacuation of around 150 people, according to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/24/treinverkeer-onderbroken-tussen-brussel-luxemburg-delta-en-bord/). The disruption halted services between Brussels-Luxembourg and Vilvoorde and between Bordet and Delta, with no trains running in either direction as technical teams raced to repair the line.

The incident struck at one of the capital's most sensitive rail junctions, feeding delays into the morning rush hour and cutting the main rail link to Brussels Airport.

## What Happened

According to Infrabel, the Belgian rail infrastructure manager, the problem arose around 07:45 between Meiser station and the entrance to the Jubelpark (Cinquantenaire) tunnel. A cable of the overhead line came loose and was left hanging over the opposite track — the "tegenspoor" — making traffic impossible in both directions.

"A cable of the overhead line came loose, and is hanging above the opposite track. As a result, no train traffic is possible in either direction," Infrabel spokesperson Frédéric Petit said.

A train that had departed from Mechelen stalled inside the Jubelpark tunnel. Because power was lost, the heating on the stranded train also stopped working. The train came to a halt in a hard-to-reach spot right at the tunnel entrance, which is why the Brussels fire brigade was called in to assist with the evacuation.

"The train stalled in a hard-to-reach place, right at the tunnel entrance. That is why fire brigade assistance was requested," Petit said. By around 10:00, the evacuation of the roughly 150 passengers was still ongoing.

## Two Lines Hit at Once

The location proved especially costly. The break occurred where two rail lines converge: the north-south Vilvoorde–Halle line via Delta, and the Brussels-Schuman–Meiser line that links the capital to Brussels Airport in Zaventem. A single fault at that node therefore cascaded across two major routes.

"Because of the unfortunate location, the incident impacts 2 rail lines," Petit said.

Infrabel dispatched technical teams to carry out an urgent repair, but the cause of the cable break was not immediately established. Petit floated one possibility: the cold weather of the previous night. "Possibly the cold weather of last night played a role in this; power cables are sensitive to freezing weather," he said, with temperatures in the region having dipped to around -2°C overnight.

## Travel Advice

National rail operator NMBS/SNCB said passengers with a valid ticket could use MIVB/STIB metro, trams and buses instead. It advised travellers heading between Brussels-Luxembourg and the airport to reroute via Brussels-North, a detour that adds significant journey time but keeps the airport reachable via the northern rail axis.

## A Recurring Vulnerability

The incident points to a persistent weak spot in Belgium's rail infrastructure. Ageing overhead lines have been a recognised maintenance concern: in June 2025, Infrabel and contractor Stadsbader Rail launched a programme to urgently renew more than 2 × 6.5 km of overhead lines on parts of the network, as [VRT NWS reported](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2025/06/30/infrabel-en-stadsbader-rail-werken-samen-aan-vernieuwing-spoorwe/). Overhead line faults have struck Brussels repeatedly in 2026, including a disruption between Brussels-North and Jette in June, [according to BRUZZ](https://www.bruzz.be/actua/mobiliteit/geen-treinverkeer-tussen-brussel-noord-en-jette-door-probleem-met-bovenleiding-2026-06-21).

Tunnel evacuations, meanwhile, are a recurring logistical challenge on the Brussels–Halle corridor. In February 2026, 360 passengers were evacuated from two stranded trains near Buizingen, [VRT NWS reported](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/02/06/brandweer-west-evacuatie-trein-halle-buizingen/).

## What to Watch

Several questions remained unanswered as of Tuesday midday. Infrabel had not determined whether the break was purely weather-related, the product of wear and ageing, or something else. No estimate was given for how long repairs would take — and with two lines affected at a critical junction, knock-on delays could ripple well beyond the immediate corridor.

It was also unclear whether any passengers required treatment for cold exposure during the evacuation from the unheated train. For commuters, the immediate concern is the afternoon and evening peak; for the network, the deeper question is how quickly a junction this important can be made more resilient.
