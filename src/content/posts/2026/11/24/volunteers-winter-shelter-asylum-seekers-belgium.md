# Volunteers Open Winter Shelter as Reception Crisis Deepens

A citizens' collective in the Walloon municipality of Grez-Doiceau will open a temporary winter shelter for asylum seekers next month, offering beds to around nine men who would otherwise face the coldest months without accommodation, [RTBF Actus reported](https://www.rtbf.be/article/ils-arrivent-dans-un-etat-d-epuisement-incroyable-ces-benevoles-qui-offrent-un-refuge-aux-demandeurs-d-asile-cet-hiver-11819907). The initiative lays bare a persistent gap between Belgium's saturated federal reception network and the people it repeatedly fails to house.

From 14 December to 7 February, the scouts' hall at Archennes will host the men, coordinated by the collective "Grez-Doiceau, Commune Hospitaliere" together with the Plateforme citoyenne du Brabant wallon. The group, made up of eight volunteers, has run a similar winter shelter for four to five years.

"The goal is to take men off the street, since these men never get a place anywhere, and to put them under shelter during the coldest months," said Pierre Scalliet, a member of the collective.

## A System Under Strain

The shelter exists because Belgium's official reception network has been unable to absorb single male asylum seekers, who since 2021 have largely been placed on waiting lists rather than given a Fedasil bed. According to [Fedasil's figures](https://www.fedasil.be/fr/statistics), the federal agency had 33,111 reception places as of 1 September 2026, with 30,582 people accommodated — a 92% occupancy rate.

The strain has been documented repeatedly by humanitarian organisations. Seven aid groups, including Medecins du Monde, Medecins Sans Frontieres and Caritas International, warned in a report that the humanitarian situation for asylum seekers is worsening, [RTBF reported](https://www.rtbf.be/article/la-situation-humanitaire-des-demandeurs-d-asile-s-aggrave-en-belgique-selon-des-ong-11713964). The organisations found that 62% of shelter requests from isolated men to the Samusocial were refused in the second half of 2025 for lack of space — a figure that rose to 75% for families. They also noted that the number of Fedasil places is shrinking even as at least 2,000 people remained in need of a bed at the end of 2025. The groups argued that housing, food, medical and psychological care, and legal assistance are fundamental rights that fall to the state and Fedasil, which "cannot offload this responsibility onto NGOs."

The picture is sharper still when set against the gaps the volunteers describe. Single male asylum seekers have been placed mostly on waiting lists since 2021, with waits stretching up to nine months for a Fedasil place. The winter shelter is designed for exactly this group — people for whom the system has no answer in the coldest months.

## Beyond a Roof

At Archennes, the support goes well beyond shelter. Volunteers provide health follow-up, laundry, transport to administrative appointments, social moments and basic French lessons, while respecting residents' privacy. Some volunteers visit regularly; others stay more in the background. The municipality makes the hall available and covers much of the heating and electricity costs.

What volunteers describe most vividly is the condition in which people arrive. "What also struck us is that they sometimes arrive in an absolutely incredible state of exhaustion," Pierre said, describing life stories that are sometimes "frightening," shared spontaneously.

Anne, a volunteer who previously hosted asylum seekers in her own home, recalled both the rewards and the limits of the work. She helped a young Eritrean woman who had left her country alone as an adolescent. "We were able to help her, support her. And it allowed her to settle in. Now she is doing really well," she said. But she also described watching a young Gambian man slide toward depression as his prospects narrowed. "We really felt very powerless as volunteers," she said.

For Anne, the motivation is rooted in a simple conviction. "We are privileged. At another time, our parents and great-grandparents were also taken in when war came to our homes. It is something normally intrinsic to human beings."

## An Appeal for Solidarity

The collective is calling on residents for support — financial donations, food and hygiene products, and a sale of organic wine to benefit the project. The Plateforme citoyenne supplies part of the basic food. But Pierre stressed that public goodwill cannot substitute for a durable solution. "Citizen support is something that is never sufficient," he said.

## What's Next

The Archennes shelter is a stopgap, not a fix. Volunteers have said they dream of being able to open year-round, a wish that points to the deeper problem: as federal capacity contracts, local and citizen initiatives are absorbing a burden the state is stepping back from.

The model does carry lessons. Grez-Doiceau's status as a "Commune Hospitaliere" shows how a municipality can complement — rather than replace — federal reception, by providing the building, covering utility costs and lending legitimacy to a volunteer collective. But such arrangements depend on local goodwill and volunteer capacity, which vary widely from one municipality to the next. That unevenness raises questions of equity: whether a person's access to shelter in winter should hinge on where they happen to be referred.

For the eight volunteers in Grez-Doiceau, the work continues regardless. "What we do is bring something extra during the winter months," Pierre said. Whether that "something extra" remains necessary next winter — and the one after — will depend on decisions taken far from the scouts' hall at Archennes.
