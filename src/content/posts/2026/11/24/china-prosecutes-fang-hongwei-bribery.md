# China Prosecutes Ex-Xi'an Party Chief Fang Hongwei for Bribery

Chinese procuratorial organs have formally filed a public prosecution against Fang Hongwei, former Standing Committee member of the Shaanxi Provincial Party Committee and former Party Secretary of Xi'an, on charges of accepting bribes, according to a notice carried by [People's Daily](http://society.people.com.cn/n1/2026/1124/c1008-40839627.html) and citing Xinhua. The announcement on 24 November 2026 moves one of Shaanxi's most prominent anti-corruption cases into the trial phase.

The Lanzhou Municipal People's Procuratorate in Gansu Province has brought the case before the Lanzhou Intermediate People's Court, the report said. The designation of a court outside Shaanxi is a standard practice in politically sensitive cases, intended to insulate proceedings from local influence.

## The Charges

Prosecutors allege that Fang exploited the convenience of his official positions to seek benefits for others and illegally accepted property "of an especially huge amount" (数额特别巨大), the most serious grading in Chinese bribery law. He is charged under the crime of accepting bribes (受贿罪).

The indictment cites his roles across a career that spanned state-owned enterprise leadership and successive local party and government posts, including:

- Shaanxi Automobile Group Co., Ltd., where he served as director and general manager, then Party Standing Committee member and chairman, and finally Party Secretary and chairman;
- Shaanxi Automobile Holding Group Co., Ltd., as Party Secretary and chairman;
- Shaanxi Heavy Duty Automobile Co., Ltd., as vice chairman and general manager;
- The Hanzhong Municipal Party Committee, as deputy secretary and mayor, then Party Secretary;
- The Shaanxi Provincial Party Committee, as Standing Committee member, and as Xi'an Party Secretary.

During the review-and-prosecution stage, prosecutors informed the defendant of his litigation rights, interrogated him, and heard the opinions of his defense counsel, according to the announcement.

## A Career That Rose Through Shaanxi

Fang, born in June 1966 and a native of Fuping in Shaanxi, built his early career inside the province's flagship automaker. He joined the Communist Party in 1987 and began work in 1989, rising to general manager of Shaanxi Automobile Group in 2002 and chairman in 2008. From there he moved into local government, serving in Baoji and Weinan before becoming mayor of Hanzhong in 2017 and its Party Secretary in 2020.

He was appointed a Standing Committee member and Secretary-General of the Shaanxi Provincial Party Committee in May 2021, and in November of that year became Standing Committee member and Party Secretary of Xi'an — the provincial capital and a key political posting. He was also an alternate member of the 20th Central Committee, making him one of the higher-profile figures caught up in the current anti-corruption drive.

## From Investigation to Trial

Fang's fall followed the established sequence for senior anti-corruption cases in China. The Central Commission for Discipline Inspection (CCDI) and the National Supervisory Commission announced on 7 November 2025 that he was under disciplinary review and supervisory investigation for suspected serious violations of discipline and law, shortly after his last public appearances in early November 2025.

On 7 July 2026, following the CCDI investigation and approval by the Central Committee, Fang was "双开" — expelled from the Communist Party and dismissed from public office. The case was then transferred to the procuratorate, where the Supreme People's Procuratorate made the arrest decision and designated Lanzhou to handle the review and prosecution.

## Part of a Broader Shaanxi Cluster

Fang's case is not isolated. He was among a wave of senior Shaanxi officials investigated in 2025, a cluster that also included Han Song, former Party Secretary and director of the Xi'an Municipal People's Congress Standing Committee, and Liu Kuanren, vice chairman of the Shaanxi Provincial CPPCC.

The pattern reflects the continuing intensity of the Communist Party's high-profile anti-corruption campaign targeting senior officials — the so-called "tigers" — and the leadership's willingness to pursue figures who once held significant regional power.

## What to Watch

With the public prosecution filed, the case now awaits trial scheduling before the Lanzhou Intermediate People's Court. The specific amount of the alleged bribes, the identities of the beneficiaries, and the details of the transactions remain undisclosed pending trial, and the trial date has not been announced.

As a state media outlet, the People's Daily report reflects official framing; no independent corroboration of the specific allegations is available ahead of trial, consistent with the opaque handling of Chinese anti-corruption cases before they reach court. How the court handles a case involving a former Central Committee alternate member — and what sentence it produces — will be watched as a signal of the campaign's current severity and reach.
