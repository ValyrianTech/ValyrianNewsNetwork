# Xi Restates China's Palestine Position in UN Day Message

Chinese President Xi Jinping sent a congratulatory message to the United Nations commemorative meeting marking the International Day of Solidarity with the Palestinian People, restating Beijing's long-standing position on the conflict and urging the world to keep the Palestinian question in focus, [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/politics/leaders/20261124/df681c37241d40ae8d6aed6bcdc044d8/c.html) reported on 24 November 2026.

The message, read to the gathering held on 23 November, contains no new initiative but reaffirms China's established position at a moment when multiple regional flashpoints are competing for international attention.

## The core of the message

Xi opened by declaring that "the Palestinian question is the core of the Middle East issue," and warned that the region's current tensions should not pull the world's attention away from the Palestinian cause.

"The current tense situation in the Middle East should not divert the international community's attention away from the Palestinian question," Xinhua quoted him as saying.

The Chinese leader said the key to resolving the issue lay in anchoring the "two-state solution" and pushing the two sides back to peace talks. He also called for upholding the principle of "Palestinians governing Palestine" in advancing governance of the West Bank and the post-war reconstruction of Gaza, and said China opposes "any land-occupying and rights-infringing acts against Palestine."

Urging the world to convert expressions of solidarity into concrete action, Xi called for a durable ceasefire in Gaza and for the protection of civilian safety.

## Continuity, not a new turn

The message is a routine restatement of policy China has voiced consistently for years. Beijing advances a position centred on the Palestinian question as the root of the Middle East crisis, a two-state solution along the 1967 lines with East Jerusalem as the capital of a Palestinian state, and support for the UN agency for Palestinian refugees.

The written form of the message — a formal congratulatory note rather than a speech — reflects the standard diplomatic protocol Beijing uses for recurring UN observances. Xinhua, the state news agency, was the sole source of the report.

The reference to rising regional tensions carries particular weight in late 2026, when other Middle East crises have at times overshadowed the Palestinian issue. Xi's emphasis that attention must not be diverted signals Beijing's concern that a wider conflagration could push the Palestinian cause to the margins.

## Balancing act with Israel

China's commitment to the Palestinian cause has run in parallel with continued diplomatic engagement with Israel. In October, Foreign Minister Wang Yi met Israeli President Isaac Herzog on the sidelines of a regional summit in Baku, where he reiterated that the two-state solution is "the correct path" to resolving the Palestinian question while urging Israel to halt and reverse settlement construction in the West Bank, according to a [readout](https://www.fmprc.gov.cn/wjbzhd/202610/t20261016_12041367.shtml) from China's foreign ministry.

That encounter underscores Beijing's effort to remain a diplomatic actor with both sides even as it cultivates a reputation as a leading voice for the Global South on Palestine — a positioning that included hosting talks among rival Palestinian factions in recent years.

## What the statement signals

The repeated emphasis on "Palestinians governing Palestine" is a pointed rejection of any external governance or trusteeship model for post-war Gaza, including proposals associated with the United States and Israel. By calling for a durable ceasefire, Beijing adds diplomatic weight to ongoing ceasefire advocacy at the Security Council, where Chinese representatives have repeatedly pushed for a larger UN role.

The practical impact, however, is likely to remain symbolic. As a written message to an annual UN observance, it reinforces China's brand as a consistent, principled supporter of Palestinian statehood but is unlikely on its own to shift the dynamics of the conflict.

The message comes ahead of the Day's formal observance on 29 November. What to watch next is whether Beijing converts the language of solidarity into concrete diplomatic initiatives — and whether the Gaza ceasefire that Xi repeatedly invokes can be made durable.

As Xi put it, "justice speaks for itself, and justice must not be absent."
