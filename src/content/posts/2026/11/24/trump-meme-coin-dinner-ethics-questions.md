# Trump Meme Coin Dinner Fell Flat as Ethics Questions Mount

When President Donald Trump hosted his third gala dinner for the largest holders of his personal cryptocurrency on Sunday, it was billed as "the most exclusive dinner in the world." But for at least one attendee, the night ended in the rain outside a Virginia golf club — and for watchdogs, it renewed pointed questions about a sitting president monetizing access to himself through a speculative digital token.

The event, held November 22 at Trump National Golf Club in Potomac Falls, Virginia, rewarded top holders of the $TRUMP meme coin with dinner, gifts and a speech from the president. Accounts from attendees, reported by [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/11/24/nx-s1-6051604/trump-crypto-corruption-meme-coin-dinner), describe a night of VIP snubs and dashed expectations — feeding criticism over the intersection of the presidency and cryptocurrency.

## A VIP Snub, Then an Ejection

Dutch crypto investor Morten Christensen, founder of the website Airdrop Alert, had attended both prior Trump meme coin events. He qualified for VIP status this time in part because the coin's value had collapsed — from a peak of more than $70 in January 2025 to under a dollar — making a top-holder spot far cheaper to reach.

At the dinner, he was turned away from the VIP area despite email confirmation, then escorted out around 7 p.m. and told, according to his account, that it was because he had shared pictures online and been in touch with the media. He waited outside in the rain. Organizers did not respond to NPR's request for comment.

"Apparently I got removed from the VIP list and I don't know," Christensen said. "There's a first for everything, and I guess it was at the Trump gala."

His sister, Gabriëlle Felen, stayed inside for the president's remarks and found them unfocused. "He jumped from topic to topic. It was interesting but just him rambling," she said. "Speaking, speaking, speaking — but not making a lot of important points I guess."

Christensen said the experience ended his run of Trump events. "That's a wrap on me on the Trump events," he said.

## A Global Guest List

Perhaps the most contentious dimension of the dinners is who attends. Christensen described meeting guests from across the world, including two people who said they worked for the crypto exchange Binance — one from Saudi Arabia, one from London — along with visitors from Ukraine, India, China and South Korea.

Blockchain analysis appears to back that up. A [report](https://www.citizen.org/article/trump-meme-coin-gala-dinner/) by the consumer advocacy group Public Citizen found that at least 66 of the 130 wallets on the final leaderboard — 51% — received $TRUMP directly from crypto exchanges that bar U.S. users, a sign their owners may live abroad. Those wallets held roughly $4 million in $TRUMP, or 44% of the board's total.

At least 15 of the top 29 VIP wallets were funded by those same U.S.-restricted exchanges. The analysis also suggested concentration: four groups appeared to control nine of the 29 VIP seats, and a single person or group seemed to run 14 other wallets. Because the coin's holders are not publicly identified, foreign nationals who cannot legally contribute to a U.S. campaign can nonetheless buy their way into a private party with the president without disclosing who they are.

## The Money Behind $TRUMP

$TRUMP launched on January 17, 2025, days before Trump's inauguration. It is a meme coin — a token driven by internet hype and backed by no underlying asset, which regulators have likened to a collectible rather than a security. Trump's CIC Digital is a partner in the venture with Fight Fight Fight LLC, and the two companies retained 80% of the coin's supply while collecting revenue from trading activity.

According to Public Citizen, Trump earned $635 million in licensing fees from the meme coin in 2025 alone, per his financial disclosure. Yet the broader investor base fared poorly: the analysis found that roughly 1 million wallets were underwater by a combined $3.2 billion or more, while the top 1% of winning wallets captured about 80% of all gains. In effect, the coin shifted wealth from late retail buyers to early insiders.

## A Gathering Oversight Storm

The dinner came as the political ground shifts beneath the president's crypto ventures. Corruption was a defining issue in the midterm elections, which handed Democrats control of Congress. Rep. Robert Garcia (D-CA), the incoming chair of the House Oversight Committee, has already issued document-preservation letters laying groundwork for investigations into the Trump family and its allies. Sens. Adam Schiff (D-CA) and Richard Blumenthal (D-CT) have demanded more information about the meme coin dinners.

The White House has defended the events as legitimate private business, arguing they are not government functions and carry no promise of official action. A White House official told CBS News the administration acts in the "best interest of the American people." Watchdogs counter that the line between private profit and public power has blurred.

"Foreign actors used to need a back door to try to influence a president," Public Citizen researcher Zach Everson told CBS News. "Trump is holding the front door open and inviting them in for a steak dinner."

There is a caveat to the foreign-buyer inference: blockchain records show where funds came from, not who ultimately controls a wallet. "All you can know with certainty is that a non-U.S. person or entity sent funds," Matt Fiebach of Entropy Advisors told CBS News.

## What to Watch

As Congress changes hands and oversight gears up, the meme coin dinners are likely to become a case study in how a president's private ventures intersect with public office. Trump has cast the crypto push in nationalist terms, framing it as competition with rivals abroad rather than personal enrichment. Whether that argument holds up under subpoena is another matter — and one that Democrats appear eager to test.

For Christensen and Felen, the questions are more immediate. Heading to the airport, Felen said she still could not quite believe she had been there, and that she wished her brother had gotten the selfie with the president that VIPs received. "So that would have been a great opportunity for Morten," she said.
