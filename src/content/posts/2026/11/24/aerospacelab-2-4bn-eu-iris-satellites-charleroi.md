# Aerospacelab Wins EUR 2.4bn EU Deal for IRIS2 Satellites

Belgian space company Aerospacelab has secured a 2.4 billion euro contract from the European Commission to build 264 satellite platforms — about 70% of the 348-satellite IRIS2 constellation meant to give Europe sovereign, secure satellite communications. The milestone was marked on Tuesday 24 November 2026, when King Philippe of the Belgians officially inaugurated the company's new "Megafactory" in Marcinelle, near Charleroi, where the satellites will be built.

The award, first announced at the international Space Summit in Paris on 10 September 2026, is being described as the largest contract in the history of Belgian space. As [RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/aerospacelab-decroche-un-contrat-a-2-4-milliards-pour-produire-70-des-satellites-europeens-d-iris-11783000) reported, the Walloon firm — founded in 2018 and based in Mont-Saint-Guibert — was charged by the European Commission with producing 264 low-Earth-orbit (LEO) platforms out of the constellation's 348 satellites.

## A constellation built for European sovereignty

IRIS2 — short for Infrastructure for Resilience, Interconnectivity and Security by Satellite — is the European Union's planned network of secure communications, joining Galileo (navigation), Copernicus (Earth observation) and Ariane (launchers) as pillars of EU space ambition. It will comprise 330 satellites in low Earth orbit and 18 in medium Earth orbit, serving all member states plus Norway and Iceland, with priority access for governments, defence forces and critical infrastructure.

The strategic rationale is direct. The war in Ukraine exposed Europe's reliance on Starlink, whose operator made unilateral decisions restricting access to critical battlefield communications — a risk the bloc is determined not to repeat. IRIS2 is designed as a European-controlled alternative, focused on encrypted, governmental and security use.

According to [RTBF's analysis](https://www.rtbf.be/article/l-europe-veut-ses-propres-satellites-l-entreprise-belge-aerospacelab-lance-leur-fabrication-a-charleroi-11820146), Europe's lag is well documented: the OECD found that US actors held roughly 76% of operational satellites worldwide in 2025, against about 9% for Europe.

## Beating Airbus and Thales

Aerospacelab won the LEO platform mandate in competition with European aerospace giants Airbus and Thales. The European Commission notably cited the company's ability to deliver in four years — unusually fast for a programme of this scale, given that Galileo and Copernicus took far longer.

"Aerospacelab wins the largest satellite manufacturing mandate of IRIS2, positioning itself to lead Europe's most ambitious satellite constellation," the company said in a statement. CEO Benoît Deper, an engineer who previously worked at NASA and the European Space Agency, framed the selection as a generational shift. "The selection of Aerospacelab for IRIS2 reflects a deliberate industrial choice, a defining moment for Europe as a whole," he said. "Institutions are now placing their trust in a new generation of space manufacturers."

In an interview with [RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/benoit-deper-patron-d-aerospacelab-ce-contrat-a-2-4-milliards-est-un-gage-de-serieux-on-s-attend-a-avoir-des-effets-ricochets-11783811), Deper acknowledged the scale of the win — and its rarity. "Contracts this size come along once every 15 or 20 years in Europe," he said, adding that the firm had been "considered in the tender with the same status as Airbus and Thales." He predicted ripple effects: "We expect several very significant announcements in the coming months."

## A Megafactory in Charleroi

The contract reshapes Aerospacelab's industrial footprint. The Marcinelle Megafactory — about 20,000 square metres, including some 7,000 square metres of production surface — will be able to produce up to 500 satellites a year and employ roughly 500 people. It is billed as the largest satellite factory in Europe and the third-largest in the world.

The company's trajectory has been vertiginous. Founded in 2018 with about five employees, Aerospacelab now employs around 500 people across Belgium and foreign sites, and Deper aims to double that headcount by the end of 2027. "We recruit across the board," he said, citing engineers, scientists, technicians and machining specialists, supported by new in-house training programmes.

Asked whether he would relocate production to cut costs, Deper was categorical. "That doesn't match my values," he said, noting that labour and energy costs are "a tiny fraction of the final product cost." The choice of Charleroi, he added, rests on the quality of available talent, institutional support and Belgium's role in Europe — backed by the region's industrial heritage.

## Timeline and what comes next

The programme is ambitious on timing. The first satellite is due for delivery in June 2030, with all 264 to be delivered by 2031; launches are expected to begin in 2029. The total IRIS2 investment exceeds 10 billion euros, described as the largest public-private partnership in the history of European space, involving the European Commission, the European Space Agency and the SpaceRISE consortium.

Yet building satellites is only part of the puzzle. Europe must also launch them — relying on Ariane 6 from French Guiana, and at times on SpaceX rockets after recent launch difficulties — and secure the radio frequencies needed for satellite-to-ground communication. Some frequencies originally allocated to European operators have passed to US groups through acquisitions; on 19 November 2026, the European Parliament's industry committee examined new allocation rules favouring European operators and secure governmental communications, an early step in the legislative process.

For Belgium, the contract cements Wallonia's place in Europe's space economy and marks a striking reversal of fortunes for a start-up that was a challenger barely a year ago. For Europe, it is a down payment on strategic autonomy — but the road to genuine independence in orbit still runs through launch capacity, spectrum and services yet to be built.
