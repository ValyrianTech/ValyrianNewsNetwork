# Guangdong Issues Pre-Appointment Notice for Seven Cadres

The Organization Department of the Guangdong Provincial Party Committee has issued a public pre-appointment notice for seven provincial-level cadres, proposing them for city party secretary and mayor positions across the province's prefecture-level cities. The notice, dated November 22 and [published by People's Daily](http://renshi.people.com.cn/n1/2026/1124/c139617-40839458.html) on November 24, opens a five-working-day public comment period running from November 23 to November 27, 2026.

## Context and Procedure

The notice is a routine but significant step in China's cadre appointment system. Under the Regulations on the Selection and Appointment of Leading Party and Government Cadres, senior personnel appointments must undergo a public notice period before they formally take effect, allowing citizens and colleagues to submit feedback or complaints about the proposed appointees.

The notice states that, following study by the provincial party committee, Fang Ming, Wang Hui, Wang Tianguang, Qin Donghai, and Guo Haoyu are "proposed for further use," while Liu Donghao and Ye Hongguang are "proposed for promotion." It was sourced from Southern Daily.

During the notice period, information and problems concerning the subjects may be reported truthfully through letters, telephone calls, visits, and other channels, according to the announcement. The notice explicitly warns that the process "must not be used for defamation or false accusation." Contact details were provided for Cadre Division Two of the Provincial Party Committee Organization Department in Guangzhou.

## The Proposed Appointees

Three cadres are proposed for the position of prefecture-level city party secretary:

- **Fang Ming**, born February 1971, holds a Doctor of Laws degree and currently serves as deputy secretary of the Provincial Discipline Inspection Commission and deputy director of the Provincial Supervisory Commission.
- **Wang Hui**, born August 1971, holds a master's degree in economics and is currently deputy party secretary and mayor of Meizhou City.
- **Wang Tianguang**, born January 1977, holds a master's degree in history and serves as party secretary and director of the Provincial Government Services and Data Management Bureau.

Four more are proposed as candidates for prefecture-level city mayor:

- **Qin Donghai**, born January 1977, of Tujia ethnicity, holds a doctorate in economics and serves as deputy secretary-general of the Provincial Party Committee and director of its Policy Research Office.
- **Guo Haoyu**, born May 1971, holds a doctorate in engineering and is party secretary and director of the Provincial Housing and Urban-Rural Development Department.
- **Liu Donghao**, born December 1974, holds a master's degree in law and is a standing committee member and executive deputy mayor of Heyuan City.
- **Ye Hongguang**, born August 1974, holds a master's degree in law and is a standing committee member and executive deputy mayor of Zhongshan City.

## Generational Renewal and Technocratic Profile

The cohort stands out for its age profile and academic credentials. All seven were born between 1971 and 1977, placing them squarely in the "post-70s" generation now being elevated to top city leadership roles in one of China's most populous and economically significant provinces.

The group is also notably technocratic, counting three doctorates — in law, economics, and engineering — alongside several master's degrees. The mix of backgrounds reflects an emphasis on professional competence in senior appointments. Fang Ming's move from the discipline inspection and supervision system to a city party secretary role is particularly notable, placing an anti-corruption official in a top political leadership position. Wang Hui, currently mayor of Meizhou, advances to a party secretary post, while Wang Tianguang and Guo Haoyu each bring specialized policy experience — in data management and housing and urban-rural development, respectively — to city-level leadership.

## What to Watch

The notice does not specify which prefecture-level cities each cadre will lead, leaving the specific assignments to be revealed in subsequent appointments. Guangdong, home to 21 prefecture-level cities, manages these appointments through its provincial party committee.

Whether any public feedback during the comment period affects the appointments also remains to be seen. The comment window closes on November 27, after which the appointments are expected to proceed to formal confirmation. The notice, a standard organizational communication, has not drawn substantive secondary analysis and reflects the routine transparency mechanisms embedded in Guangdong's leadership renewal process.
