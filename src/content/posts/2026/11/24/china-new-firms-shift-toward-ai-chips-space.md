# China's New Firms Shift Toward AI, Chips and Space

Chinese entrepreneurs registered new businesses at a faster clip in higher-value, technology-intensive sectors during the first three quarters of 2026, according to data from the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR), in a signal Beijing is reading as evidence that its push for "high-quality development" is taking hold. The number of registered enterprises in the so-called "Four New" economy — spanning new technologies, industries, business formats and models — rose 5.6% year-on-year as of the end of September, [People's Daily reported](http://finance.people.com.cn/n1/2026/1124/c1004-40839222.html).

Generative artificial intelligence was the headline growth story: 90,000 new enterprises were established in the field from January to September, up 28.1% from a year earlier. That figure appeared as the subtitle of the People's Daily report, underscoring how central AI has become to the official narrative of China's economic upgrading.

## The numbers behind the shift

The data paint a picture of a registration landscape tilting toward strategic and frontier industries:

- **Spacecraft and launch vehicle manufacturing** saw new enterprises jump **73.3%** year-on-year.
- **Integrated circuit manufacturing** grew **21.1%**.
- **Humanoid robotics** added **170,000** new enterprises, up **9.5%**.
- **Advanced manufacturing** as a whole recorded **218,000** new enterprises in the first three quarters.

Within advanced manufacturing, the eastern coastal region remained the dominant engine, contributing 138,000 new enterprises, up 2.6% year on year. Private firms accounted for 199,000 of the total — more than 91% — though their year-on-year growth was comparatively modest at 0.2%. Among 13 sub-categories of advanced manufacturing, four grew faster than 4%: high-end equipment, advanced petrochemical and new chemical materials, frontier new materials, and advanced steel materials.

"In the first three quarters, China's 'Four New' economy and advanced manufacturing maintained strong development momentum; enterprises in new industries and new tracks accelerated their growth, pooling energy for high-quality development," the report stated.

The figures are consistent with earlier SAMR data. At an [SAMR third-quarter press conference](https://cfsn.cn/news/detail/22/368978.html) in late September, the agency reported that registered "Four New" enterprises totaled 29.3 million as of the end of August, up 5.4% and accounting for 44% of all enterprises — a trajectory that aligns with the 5.6% end-September reading.

## Quality over quantity

The story's framing — "improving quality, moving toward the new" — is not incidental. It echoes a doctrine the Communist Party's flagship theoretical journal has articulated explicitly. In a [2025 commentary](https://www.qstheory.cn/20250308/477605859b89415d86e86778dbe09f8f/c.html), Qiushi argued that China "must balance the relationship between improving quality and expanding total volume," treating "effective quality improvement" and "reasonable quantitative growth" as a unified whole.

That framing helps explain why the People's Daily report emphasizes the *composition* of new registrations rather than the total count. The message is that the economy is generating the right kind of firms — in AI, semiconductors, aerospace and robotics — rather than simply more of them. The private economy's role is cast in similarly deliberate terms: the report notes that "the endogenous dynamism of the private economy has strengthened," even as its year-on-year growth in advanced manufacturing barely moved.

## What the data does — and doesn't — show

The figures should be read with care. Registrations measure entrepreneurial entry and the effect of policy incentives; they do not by themselves demonstrate profitability, output or survival. New firms may be small, short-lived, or formed to capture registration-linked benefits. SAMR's numbers, released approvingly through state media, are not independently verified against the underlying database.

The growth rates also deserve context. The 73.3% surge in spacecraft and launch-vehicle enterprises rests on a small absolute base, and the 9.5% growth in humanoid robotics reflects a large base effect, following roughly 116,000 new humanoid-robotics enterprises in the first half of the year. Generative AI's 28.1% is striking, but the marquee "hot" sectors appear to be moderating in year-on-year terms even as absolute numbers keep climbing.

Geographically, the concentration of advanced-manufacturing registrations in the east reinforces China's longstanding regional imbalances — a reminder that the upgrading story is uneven.

## What to watch

The test of this data will come in whether these registrations translate into durable output, jobs and revenue — questions the registration figures cannot answer. Two things bear watching into the fourth quarter and 2027: whether the double-digit growth in strategic sectors holds up against high base effects and global tech-market volatility, and whether the modest 0.2% growth in private advanced-manufacturing entry picks up.

For now, Beijing has a data set that supports its preferred narrative: that China's economy is not just growing, but changing shape.
