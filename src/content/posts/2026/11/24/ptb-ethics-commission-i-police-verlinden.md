# PTB Asks Ethics Commission to Weigh In on i-Police Scandal

Belgium's left-wing PTB/PVDA party said Tuesday it will table a motion in the Chamber of Representatives asking the Federal Deontology Commission to issue an opinion on whether Justice Minister Annelies Verlinden can legitimately keep exercising her duties while prosecutors investigate her role in the failed i-Police police digitisation project. The move, [reported by RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/i-police-le-ptb-demande-l-avis-de-la-commission-de-deontologie-11820318), adds fresh political pressure to a scandal that has already cost the public purse an estimated €139.4 million and left the minister's position increasingly contested.

## An Unprecedented Conflict

At the heart of the PTB's demand is a structural anomaly: the investigation into Verlinden is being led by Brussels procureur général Frédéric Van Leeuw, who reports hierarchically to the very minister under scrutiny.

"We are in an unprecedented situation where the justice system must investigate its own minister," said Sofie Merckx, the PTB's group leader in the Chamber. "How can Annelies Verlinden function in this situation? Can the justice system really investigate in full independence? As long as doubt persists, no one can have confidence in the independence of justice. An independent opinion is necessary."

The party's Dutch-speaking wing, PVDA, struck the same note through MP Greet Daems, framing the request as a question of institutional integrity rather than partisan attack. The Federal Deontology Commission is an advisory body that can issue opinions on ethical questions concerning public officeholders — giving the opposition a potential avenue to force a political reckoning even absent a criminal outcome.

## How the Scandal Unfolded

i-Police was conceived as a flagship programme to modernise the federal police's IT systems. Piloting was entrusted to French consultancy Sopra Steria in 2021, with Verlinden — then interior minister in the previous Vivaldi government — signing the public procurement contract. Delays accumulated through 2024 and 2025 while the police continued committing funds, and the project was ultimately abandoned in early 2025 under her successor, Interior Minister Bernard Quintin (MR), for lack of results.

A [damning report from the Court of Auditors](https://www.rtbf.be/article/rapport-cinglant-sur-i-police-les-depenses-totales-estimees-a-139-millions-par-la-cour-des-comptes-11807904), published on 29 October, put the total bill at €139.4 million. Of that, police committed €86.5 million while pilot projects were already significantly delayed, €20.6 million went to external consultants, and only about €42 million is considered still useful.

The legal front opened in mid-November, when Le Soir and De Standaard revealed that the Brussels general prosecutor's office was investigating Verlinden's role, including alleged misappropriation of public funds and illegal taking of interests. The case had been transferred to the parquet général after an investigating judge deemed it necessary to examine certain decisions by the former interior minister or her cabinet. The [prosecutor's office stressed](https://www.rtbf.be/article/le-parquet-general-de-bruxelles-enquete-sur-le-role-de-la-ministre-de-la-justice-annelies-verlinden-dans-l-affaire-i-police-11816419) that the step was a normal procedural one that "does not prejudge any criminal responsibility."

## A Growing Political Storm

The affair has also drawn in questions about potential conflicts of interest. Verlinden previously worked at law firm DLA Piper, which represents Sopra Steria in the related civil dispute, and her partner reportedly holds a management position at the firm. Merckx put the question bluntly: "Did the links between Verlinden and Sopra Steria play a role in the whole i-Police fiasco? That is the question everyone is asking."

A separate espionage strand has compounded the pressure: Verlinden's spokesperson and a Brussels prosecutor's office spokesperson were charged with allegedly leaking information covered by judicial secrecy. The minister denies wrongdoing, saying her spokesperson only contacted the prosecutor's office to respond to what she considered erroneous reporting.

Opposition parties have seized on the revelations. The PS's Ridouane Chahid called for the minister to "step aside so that full light can be shed," arguing that "alongside judicial responsibility, there is political responsibility." Groen's Matti Vandemaele said the accumulation of questions made her position "increasingly difficult to justify," while even Georges-Louis Bouchez of the coalition partner MR demanded that "full clarity must be established, not pirouettes."

## Verlinden's Defence

Verlinden has refused to resign, insisting she has "nothing to hide" and that she maintains her position. She argued that stepping down merely because an investigation is opened would set a precedent in which the simple fact of an inquiry leads to dismissal, and said she does not yet know the exact content of the file or of any personal accusations against her.

Her CD&V party, a member of the governing Arizona coalition led by Prime Minister Bart De Wever, continues to back her. MP Franky Demon denounced an "unprecedented attack" and stressed the presumption of innocence.

## What to Watch

The PTB motion is unlikely to pass on its own, given the coalition's continued support for Verlinden, but it keeps the issue alive and keeps pressure on the government. The decisive variable may be the Deontology Commission itself: a critical opinion would provide an independent, non-criminal basis for questioning the minister's position and could shift the political calculus.

Meanwhile, the parallel demand from several opposition parties for a full parliamentary inquiry commission remains on the table, and the criminal investigation under the procureur général is likely to run for months. At stake is not only one minister's future but a broader test of judicial independence in a case where the prosecutor investigating the minister answers to her.
