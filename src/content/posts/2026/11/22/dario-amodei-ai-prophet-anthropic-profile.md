# Inside Dario Amodei's AI Prophecy, and Its Contradictions

Dario Amodei, the co-founder and chief executive of Anthropic, has spent years warning that artificial intelligence could end or permanently stunt human civilization. This week, the same company he leads became one of the most valuable corporations on Earth. A new [Washington Post feature](https://www.washingtonpost.com/technology/2026/11/22/inside-an-ai-prophets-secretive-plans-humanitys-future/) probes that tension — casting Amodei as a self-styled "AI prophet" whose sweeping vision for humanity's future shapes not only his firm but parts of the wider AI movement, even as the incentives of Wall Street close in around it.

## A trillion-dollar contradiction

Anthropic went public on the Nasdaq on November 19 in what multiple outlets describe as the largest initial public offering in history. Shares opened at $111.5, 34% above the $83 IPO price, and the company's market value briefly blew past $2 trillion, according to [France 24](https://www.france24.com/en/business/20261119-anthropic-begins-trading-1-5-trillion-company-biggest-ipo-record-ai). The offering of roughly 980 million shares raised about $81 billion, eclipsing SpaceX's June listing.

At the opening bell, Amodei struck a note of reassurance. "Today puts the company in a stronger financial position, and in doing so, only strengthens our resolve to do the right thing," he said. He added that Anthropic would "pace the training and deployment of AI systems, taking as long as is necessary to have confidence that they are safe and will remain under human control."

The financial picture is more precarious than the debut suggested. Anthropic carries $6.2 billion in debt and is unprofitable under standard accounting rules, the network reported, and it has signed leases and deals requiring about $900 billion in computing and infrastructure spending, including $70 billion in the next 12 months.

## The gospel according to Amodei

The Post's portrait, by reporter Nitasha Tiku, describes a leader who governs through essays, long Slack memos and twice-monthly all-hands talks jokingly called "Dario Vision Quests." Inside the company, staff members known as "Ants" treat several propositions as self-evident: that AI superseding humans is inevitable, that the United States must limit China's AI development, and that Claude — the company's chatbot — might be conscious.

The company has invited more than a dozen academics, think-tank fellows and media figures to two-day briefings at its San Francisco headquarters, held under the Chatham House Rule, on how society should navigate powerful AI. Guests were asked to read an Amodei essay and the 23,000-word "constitution" guiding Claude.

"They feel it's going to be a transformative event for humanity and society needs to be prepared," Arpit Gupta, an NYU economist who attended, told the Post. "I got the sense that they're not happy with the public discourse" about AI.

The convictions run deep. A previously unreported 2022 internal memo, surfaced through court records, estimated a "20 to 30 percent chance that AGI will lead to the destruction or permanent stunting of humanity" and called capable AI "more powerful/dangerous than nuclear weapons." One former colleague recalled that Amodei used offline, "air-gapped" laptops and printers for sensitive AI essays, fearing they amounted to an "infohazard."

## A culture under scrutiny

The Post describes hiring practices that include a "culture interview" even for back-office roles. One Anthropic hiring manager texted an unsuccessful candidate that it was "a bit of an 'are you ready for the cult' interview," according to a screenshot reviewed by the paper.

The company also ran an internal investigation after Wired reported that Amodei had acknowledged in a Slack message that accepting Middle East state funding was likely to "enrich dictators." That 2025 memo, [reported by Wired](https://www.wired.com/story/anthropic-dario-amodei-gulf-state-leaked-memo/), captured Amodei weighing principle against capital: "Unfortunately, I think 'No bad person should ever benefit from our success' is a pretty difficult principle to run a business on." A leaker was later fired, an incident not previously reported, the Post said. Anthropic declined to comment to the newspaper.

Former employees describe a workforce convinced of its own righteousness. "A pervasive thing at Anthropic is: We're going to put on our capes and we're going to be the responsible ones, and we're going to accrue great power, but … because we're doing it responsibly, it's responsible to acquire more power," one anonymous former employee told the Post.

## The safety debate Amodei ignited

The profile lands amid a broader reckoning over AI risk. In September, Anthropic researcher Jacob Coxon publicly resigned, warning that AI could cause human extinction by 2030 — a claim echoed by colleagues inside the company. "We really do earnestly believe AI could kill all humans!" wrote Evan Hubinger, a lead in Anthropic's alignment division, [as The Guardian reported](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/sep/09/anthropic-researchers-ai-human-extinction).

Days later, Amodei published an essay, "We Must Pace the Frontier," calling for the industry to slow down. The appeal drew rare endorsements from rivals Sam Altman of OpenAI and Elon Musk, [according to The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/sep/13/openai-sam-altman-elon-musk-back-anthropic-calls-brakes-ai-development). Yet Amodei publicly downplayed the doomsday framing, telling CNN he is "not a doomer" and that "there's some chance that things go wrong but it's not all that high."

That gap between internal alarm and public reassurance is the story's central fault line. Critics argue transparency alone does not justify public trust. Geoffrey Irving, a former OpenAI colleague, told the Post that Amodei is "just … another AI company CEO trapped in this horrible race."

## The knives come out

Amodei's warnings have also made him enemies in Silicon Valley. The New York Times [reported](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/11/13/technology/anthropic-dario-amodei-ai-openai.html) that Anthropic and OpenAI lobbied the White House to prevent some AI models from being developed and widely shared — prompting Microsoft and then-Nvidia chief Jensen Huang to rally two dozen companies behind a letter defending open models. Amodei refused to sign. The Times described a company that "has gained a reputation for playing hardball with customers and competitors."

## What to watch

The timing could hardly be more fraught. Nasdaq erased all of its 2026 gains a day after Anthropic's debut amid an AI sell-off, and voters in dozens of local ballot measures this month rejected data centers, with newly elected Democratic governors in Texas and Georgia campaigning on restricting them.

For Amodei, the question now is whether a publicly traded, profit-driven giant can remain faithful to a mission of protecting humanity — or whether, as his own 2022 memo feared, the incentives of the market will erode it. The prophet has his capital. Whether he keeps his congregation is another matter.
