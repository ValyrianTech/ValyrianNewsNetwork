# Lang Ping Awarded FIVB's Highest Honor, the Volleyball Order

Chinese volleyball legend Lang Ping was awarded the International Volleyball Federation (FIVB)'s highest distinction, the Volleyball Order, at the 40th FIVB World Congress in Punta Cana, Dominican Republic, on 21 November, [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/sports/20261122/bc02764ba80c4522977d31d960eae595/c.html) reported. The honour recognises individuals who have made an exceptional and lasting contribution to the growth and advancement of volleyball and beach volleyball worldwide.

Lang was one of five global figures to receive the award, joining Doug Beal of the United States, Craig Carracher of Australia, Mireya Luis of Cuba and Ruedi Kunz of Switzerland — a group the [FIVB](https://www.fivb.com/legends-of-the-sport-honoured-with-volleyball-order-at-fivb-world-congress/) described as "Olympic champions, pioneering coaches and visionary leaders, all united by a passion for volleyball and a lasting impact on the sport."

## A Career That Made Olympic History

In its citation, the FIVB called Lang "a global volleyball icon whose achievements as both a player and coach have inspired generations." Known as the "Iron Hammer" for her powerful attacking play, she helped China claim Olympic gold at Los Angeles 1984, then guided the women's national team back to the top of the podium as head coach at Rio 2016 — becoming the first person in volleyball history to win Olympic gold in both roles.

Lang also led the United States women's team to silver at the Beijing 2008 Olympics. She was inducted into the International Volleyball Hall of Fame in 2002 and received the International Olympic Committee's Coaches Lifetime Achievement Award in 2025.

Her connection to the sport spans more than half a century. Xinhua noted that Lang began playing volleyball in Beijing at age 14 and has since spent more than 50 years in the game, a journey that included MVP honours at both the 1982 World Championship and the 1984 Olympics.

## 'I Don't Feel I'm Standing Here Alone'

Accepting the award, Lang delivered remarks that credited the people around her for a career defined as much by collective achievement as individual brilliance.

> "Thank you to the FIVB for inviting me and awarding me the 'Volleyball Medal.' Standing here today, honestly, I don't feel I am standing here alone. I feel my coaches, teammates, the players I coached, and everyone I've worked with over the years are all standing here with me."

She went on to thank her family, friends and fans for their support through both triumph and difficulty. "You celebrated with me, stayed with me in my hardest moments, and gave me strength," she said.

Looking ahead, Lang offered two wishes: to see more female coaches share the stage, and for more people worldwide to play the game, cheer for it and fall in love with it. "If I could do it all over again, I would still choose volleyball every time," she said.

## Honouring Five Lasting Legacies

Lang's fellow honourees were recognised for contributions spanning both the court and the boardroom. Beal coached the US men's team to its first Olympic gold at Los Angeles 1984 and later served as CEO of USA Volleyball; Carracher spent more than three decades advancing the sport in Australia and helped deliver the 2025 FIVB Beach Volleyball World Championships in Adelaide; Luis, a three-time Olympic champion and two-time world champion, captained Cuba's golden generation and now serves as FIVB Executive Vice-President; and Kunz was the driving force behind the Gstaad beach volleyball event for 26 years.

## Analysis: A Milestone Beyond the Court

The award lands at a moment of significant momentum for the sport. At the same congress, new Nielsen research indicated volleyball's global reach had climbed to 1.2 billion people — roughly one in seven people worldwide — placing it as the second most popular sport globally.

The timing is fitting for Lang personally as well. In China she remains one of the most revered figures in sport, a symbol tied to the women's volleyball team's 1980s "golden generation" and to the country's rise as a sporting power. Her US coaching stint and the 2008 Beijing Olympic final — in which the Americans beat China — stirred domestic controversy at the time, but her return to guide China to Rio 2016 glory cemented her legacy. Since stepping down from coaching after the Tokyo 2020 Olympics, held in 2021, she has continued in advisory and advocacy roles, including championing women in coaching — a cause she returned to in her acceptance speech.

For the FIVB, honouring Lang alongside four peers from four continents reinforces a deliberate international framing of the sport's heritage, one that pairs on-court dominance with decades of institutional leadership.

## What's Next

The Volleyball Order recognises a career largely complete on the court but still unfolding off it. As volleyball's global reach continues to expand and the FIVB pursues its Strategic Vision 2032, Lang's call for more female coaches on the podium may prove as enduring a legacy as her Olympic medals. What to watch: whether her advocacy translates into measurable gains in women's coaching pipelines worldwide — and how the FIVB's newly confirmed global audience shapes its next chapter.
