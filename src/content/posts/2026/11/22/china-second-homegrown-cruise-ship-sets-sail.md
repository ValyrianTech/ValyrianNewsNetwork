# China's Second Homegrown Cruise Ship Sets Sail

China's second domestically built large cruise ship began its commercial maiden voyage on Sunday, departing from the southern city of Guangzhou on a six-day, five-night itinerary to Hong Kong and Vietnam—a milestone that underscores the country's push into one of shipbuilding's most demanding segments.

The vessel, the **Adora Flora City** (爱达·花城号), left the Guangzhou Nansha International Cruise Home Port on November 22 bound for Hong Kong and Hue, Vietnam, according to [The Paper](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_34532096), which cited CCTV News. Built by Shanghai Waigaoqiao Shipbuilding under the state-owned China State Shipbuilding Corporation (CSSC) and operated by Adora Cruises, the ship is the second entry in China's indigenous large-cruise-ship program.

## A Benchmark of Industrial Capability

Large cruise ships are counted among the three "crown jewels" of the shipbuilding industry—alongside aircraft carriers and liquefied natural gas (LNG) carriers—as a measure of a nation's comprehensive manufacturing strength. A single large cruise ship comprises more than 1,000 systems, over 20,000 sets of key equipment and more than 25 million components, integrating marine engineering with hotel and hospitality systems.

China joined the small club of large-cruise-ship builders in 2023, when the first vessel, the Adora Magic City, was delivered. According to [IT之家](https://www.ithome.com/1/023/206.htm), the new ship represents a shift from proving the capability "from zero to one" to demonstrating a "from good to better" level of production.

The Adora Flora City formally was delivered in Shanghai on November 6, 2026, roughly two months ahead of the original plan. Its construction cycle was eight months shorter than that of the first ship, and its domestic content and supply rate rose by about five percentage points—metrics that point to a maturing supply chain and growing build efficiency.

## Bigger, Quieter, Smarter

The new vessel is larger than its predecessor in nearly every dimension. It measures 341 meters in length—17.4 meters longer than the Adora Magic City—with a beam of 37.2 meters and a gross tonnage of 141,900, about 6,400 gross tons more than the first ship.

The ship has 16 passenger decks and 2,130 cabins, with a maximum capacity of 5,232 guests. Its cabins are engineered so that interior noise stays below 45 decibels even under maximum operating conditions—roughly the level of a quiet study. It also adds two new desulfurization towers and a Selective Catalytic Reduction (SCR) system to meet emission-control-area requirements, along with AI-assisted paperless embarkation and humanoid robots for onboard interaction.

## A Guangzhou Flavor

For its debut season, Adora Cruises is positioning the ship squarely around southern Chinese culture. The vessel's Flower City Plaza—named for Guangzhou's nickname—draws on flowers and Lingnan aesthetics, with Cantonese porcelain, bamboo weaving and mother-of-pearl inlay woven into nearly 400 artworks across the ship.

"The biggest highlight is that it has very strong Guangzhou characteristics," Guangzhou TV reporter Diao Jiajun said after touring the ship, citing the plaza, Cantonese cuisine from the Guangzhou Restaurant and an original stage show, "The Nameless Lion King," inspired by lion-dance culture.

Those cultural touches are part of a deliberate "cruise + culture" strategy. Ahead of the maiden voyage, during a trial run reported by [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/politics/20261109/87284d9a56af4808adee47cc1893da05/c.html), Adora Cruises said it would "continue to deepen the 'cruise + culture' brand strategy while consolidating independent operating capability," and the company has made clear that Guangzhou sits at the center of its expansion plans, pledging to build the city into a core hub and strengthen the South China cruise home-port cluster.

## A Batch-Production Era

The timing of the maiden voyage carries broader weight. Both the shortened build time and higher domestic content signal that China is moving from one-off capability toward serial production. According to IT之家, a third domestically built cruise ship has already begun forward concept design, marking the program's entry into a batch-construction phase.

Chen Gang, chairman of Waigaoqiao Shipbuilding and the cruise project's chief designer, described the progress as building out a research-and-development system, a production-design standard, and a dual domestic-and-international supply chain. "We will follow the principle of building one generation, designing one generation and researching one generation," he said.

## What's Next

The maiden season runs through January 2, 2027, with six international itineraries spanning short, medium and long routes. It includes a nine-day, eight-night Southeast Asia sailing and a first-ever 17-day, 16-night "Maritime Silk Road Discovery Journey" covering Singapore, Malaysia, Brunei, the Philippines, Indonesia and Vietnam.

Much still hinges on the demand side. Profitability, occupancy rates and competition from established international cruise operators remain open questions that the maiden-voyage coverage did not address. The ship's operational and safety record over its first season will also be closely watched.

For now, the Adora Flora City stands as a consumer-facing symbol of a deeper industrial story: China's steady advance into the most complex corners of high-end manufacturing.
