# Belgium's budget impasse drags into a Sunday scramble

Belgium's federal budget negotiations dragged into Sunday with no new meeting scheduled and no sign of a breakthrough, as Prime Minister Bart De Wever prepared to report to King Filip on Monday for the fourth time this month. The five-party "Arizona" coalition remains deadlocked over the 2027 budget, with Vooruit refusing to accept steep savings on workers and pensioners unless the country's largest fortunes also contribute, according to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/liveblog/oplossingen-zoeken-op-zondag-groundbreaking-kunnen-miranda-p~1793247532380/).

## A familiar standstill — with rising stakes

Despite the apparent paralysis, there were flickers of movement. Vooruit chair Conner Rousseau confirmed on Instagram that his party was "meeting and looking for solutions. On a Sunday. Groundbreaking" — a wry post illustrated with a photo of Miranda Priestly, the demanding editor from *The Devil Wears Prada*. A source described it as an internal party meeting. De Wever's own Instagram offered a cryptic note about his cat, Maximus: "Paw to the left, tail to the right, perfectly balanced, now above all don't move."

The impasse is not new. In autumn 2025, the same coalition struggled to find roughly €10 billion in savings before reaching a deal of €9.2 billion on 24 November. This time, negotiations collapsed over the weekend of 16 November, when Vooruit alone refused to work from the proposed savings table. De Wever offered his resignation to the King on 2 November; the monarch held it under advisement and granted extra time rather than accept it, keeping pressure on the negotiators, [VRT NWS reported](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/02/geen-begrotingsakkoord-politieke-crisis-overzicht/).

## The fault line: who bears the burden

The central dispute is how to divide the pain. Vooruit insists that the "broadest shoulders" — millionaires and high earners — contribute substantially. De Wever floated lowering the threshold for the securities tax (effectentaks) from €1 million to €750,000, but Vooruit reportedly rejected the idea. The other four parties — N-VA, CD&V, MR and Les Engagés — consider the table on offer fair.

Tensions sharpened after Vooruit vice-premier Frank Vandenbroucke went on VRT NWS's *Terzake* on 16 November and attacked his coalition partners, a moment analysts described as the low point of trust. Vandenbroucke criticised fiscal benefits for top footballers worth some €253 million and accused MR's Georges-Louis Bouchez of carrying far more weight than he does. CD&V's Vincent Van Peteghem hit back, accusing Vandenbroucke of spreading "incorrect information" and creating "unnecessary unrest."

Party leaders have tried to project calm. "What is on the table today is indeed a fair agreement. Four of five parties say so," N-VA chair Valerie Van Peel told Radio 1. "We are standing on the edge of a precipice, and every politician must now realise that." MR's Bouchez has said he will do everything to pull the country out of the crisis, while Rousseau insists he still wants a deal — if the burden is fairly shared, [De Standaard reported](https://www.standaard.be/politiek/vooruit-voorzitter-rousseau-wil-nog-steeds-akkoord-mr-voorzitter-bouchez-wil-alles-doen-om-uit-crisis-te-raken/164077237.html).

## The economic backdrop: rising deficits and debt

The political standoff is unfolding against a worsening fiscal picture. The European Commission's autumn forecast projects Belgium's deficit rising to 5.5% of GDP in 2027 and 5.7% by 2028, with the debt ratio climbing to 111.2% of GDP this year, 113.4% in 2027 and 115.8% in 2028, according to the [European Commission](https://economy-finance.ec.europa.eu/economic-forecast-and-surveys/economic-forecasts/autumn-2026-economic-forecast-shows-continued-growth-amid-higher-inflation_en). By then, only Italy, Greece and France would carry a larger debt mountain.

The lack of a 2027 budget deal means Belgium will start next year on a provisional-twelfths regime, under which the government may spend only one-twelfth of the current year's budget each month. The federal government has already approved a first tranche covering January to March, with Budget Minister Van Peteghem tasked with submitting it to the Chamber.

Rating agencies have so far held their ground. Fitch reaffirmed Belgium's A+ rating with a stable outlook on 20 November, as VRT NWS reported, noting that "complex, long policy discussions are not unusual in Belgium" and that a similar standstill last year was resolved. Moody's and S&P had already kept their scores unchanged the previous month. Fitch nevertheless warned of a "debt snowball" toward 2030 if nothing is done.

## What's next: another visit to the palace

De Wever must report to King Filip again on Monday, even as union protests and PVDA mobilisation are planned for the same day. The King's continued refusal to accept a resignation keeps pressure on the negotiators while buying time — a pause Cabinet watchers see as deliberate.

Political scientist Carl Devos captured the mood on *Terzake*: "I have the feeling they are looking at each other and waiting for each other's phone to ring. And if no call comes, we head into big trouble this way, Monday at the King's."

Notably, no party currently wants new elections. Vlaams Belang has filed a motion of no confidence, and Groen and PVDA continue to attack the coalition — but the prevailing instinct, as Devos put it, is that "nobody in this country believes new elections would offer a solution, given our history of long government formations." For now, the country waits for a phone to ring — or for the King's next act.
