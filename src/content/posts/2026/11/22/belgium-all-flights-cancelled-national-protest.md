# Belgium grounds all flights Monday for national protest

All flights at every Belgian airport will be cancelled on Monday 23 November 2026, as a group of air traffic controllers joins a national demonstration against the federal government's austerity plans. Air traffic control provider Skeyes said regular service could not be guaranteed, closing Belgian airspace from 07:00 to 21:00, [VRT NWS reported](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/20/maandag-23-november-betoging-hinder/).

The shutdown is expected to affect roughly 60,000 travellers at Brussels Airport (Zaventem) alone, which had anticipated 429 passenger flights across the day, and it sets off a cascade of disruption across public transport, waste collection and childcare.

## Context: the eleventh national action

The demonstration is organised by Belgium's three main trade union federations — the Christian ACV, the Socialist ABVV and the Liberal Synova — and is at least the eleventh national action since Prime Minister Bart De Wever's government took office. The unions are resisting savings plans that reportedly include a possible suspension of automatic wage indexation, pension cuts and a VAT increase, and are seeking to keep pressure on ongoing budget negotiations.

Demonstrators will gather at Brussels-North station from 10:00, with speeches at 10:30 and a march departing at 11:00 toward Brussels-South station along the Small Ring, avoiding the city centre.

## The aviation shutdown

Skeyes confirmed that regular air traffic control cannot be maintained because a portion of its controllers will take part in the protest. The provider stressed that minimum service for emergencies and other safety-critical flights remains guaranteed, stating that "the safety of air traffic remains the absolute priority under all circumstances."

Brussels Airport had expected 59,500 passengers between 06:00 and 22:00 across 429 flights — 213 departing flights carrying 28,000 passengers and 216 arriving flights carrying 31,500. Airports, airlines and partners are being informed directly, and travellers are advised to contact their carrier.

## An industry left angry — and blindsided

The late notice drew sharp criticism. Brussels Airlines said it learned of the closure only in the late afternoon of 21 November, calling the communication "extremely late" and lamenting that passengers "again have to bear the consequences of a strike." The airline noted that until just a few hours earlier, the expected impact on Brussels Airport operations had been "almost nil."

BAR Belgium, the trade federation representing around 25 airlines active in the country, including Brussels Airlines and TUI fly Belgium, was blunter, [according to VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/21/brussels-airlines-betreurt-extreem-late-communicatie-over-stak/). "After 11 airspace-related strikes in as many months, any notion of exceptional action has disappeared," it said, describing the walkout as "excessive, irresponsible and completely disproportionate" and warning it undermines Belgium's credibility as a reliable destination.

Brussels South Charleroi Airport described the consequences as "disastrous" and urged authorities to draft a coordinated plan to preserve the competitiveness of all Belgian airports. Employers' organisation Voka renewed its call for minimum-service rules, with CEO Frank Beckx arguing that "it cannot be that a strike in one link of the airport chain can shut down all air traffic."

## Wider disruption across the country

Beyond aviation, Flemish public transport operator De Lijn expects that four in ten buses and trams will not run, with the heaviest impact in Antwerp and Flemish Brabant provinces, where only about 54 percent of services will operate. In Antwerp, roughly 40 percent of trams will run, compared with about 70 percent in Ghent and 80 percent for the Coastal Tram.

Brussels public transport operator MIVB/STIB warns of heavy disruption from both staff participation and the protest route itself, and advises travellers to check its website or app on Sunday evening for a disruption forecast. Net Brussel says several waste collection rounds may be affected, with catch-up rounds from Tuesday 24 November prioritising white and orange bags.

Childcare and municipal services will vary by municipality, with urban childcare initiatives seeing high participation; parents are advised to check with their provider. National rail operator NMBS/SNCB expects no disruption for now, and schools show no signs of major problems.

## What to watch

The strike underscores a deepening standoff between Belgium's unions and a government still struggling to close its budget. With aviation action now a recurring feature rather than a rarity, the dispute is shifting from the terms of any single savings plan to a broader argument over minimum service and the limits of the right to strike. How De Wever's coalition navigates the coming budget talks — and whether it moves to enshrine minimum-service guarantees for air traffic control — will determine whether Monday's grounding becomes a turning point or simply the latest in a lengthening series.
