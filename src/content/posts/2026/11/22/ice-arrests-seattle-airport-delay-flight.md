# ICE Arrests at Seattle Airport Delay Alaska Flight

Masked federal immigration officers detained two Mexican nationals on a crowded jet bridge at Seattle-Tacoma International Airport on Saturday morning, causing what Alaska Airlines called a "distressing" scene and delaying a Los Angeles-bound flight, according to [AP News](https://apnews.com/article/immigration-arrest-seattle-airport-ice-4748ddb6161c28853d763e835aeb31f1).

The episode, captured on widely circulated video, has become a flashpoint in the national debate over aggressive immigration enforcement at travel hubs. It drew swift condemnation from Washington state officials and renewed scrutiny of the tactics federal agents are using inside U.S. airports.

## What Happened

U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement officers conducted what the agency described as a "targeted enforcement operation" during boarding of Alaska Airlines Flight 1306, which had been scheduled to depart at 8:10 a.m. The officers arrested Ricardo Larrondo Mendoza, whom ICE said had "overstayed his visa in violation of our nation's laws."

During the confrontation, a second passenger, Sofia Hernandez Mejia, "hurled hot coffee on the officers," according to an ICE statement. ICE said she was also determined to be in the U.S. illegally from Mexico and had overstayed her visa, and she could face federal charges of assaulting a federal officer. A Department of Homeland Security source told [CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/ice-arrest-alaska-airlines-flight-seattle/) that the two detainees are a father and daughter.

Video of the incident showed officers dragging away at least one person as a woman screamed that her daughter was being taken and that she had a work permit. The arresting officers wore unmarked jackets and khaki pants.

Alaska Airlines said the officers gave no advance notice of the enforcement action, which occurred during a busy travel weekend ahead of the Thanksgiving holiday.

"We are concerned by this morning's law enforcement action during the boarding of Alaska Flight 1306," the airline said. "We recognize how distressing this incident has been for affected employees and guests." The airline added that it is committed to "treating every guest with dignity and respect."

## Political Backlash

Washington Gov. Bob Ferguson, a Democrat, called the tactics "deeply un-American" and urged the agency's abolition.

"The video is horrific and it is chilling," Ferguson said at an afternoon news conference. "We need a system of immigration enforcement and border security that is effective, ethical and humane. And ICE is not it. It is beyond reform."

Ferguson said he was looking into how ICE agents accessed the jet bridge and believes they violated a state law he signed in March limiting facial coverings on law enforcement officers.

Rep. Adam Smith, a Washington Democrat, issued a sharply worded [statement](https://adamsmith.house.gov/news/press-releases/rep-smith-statement-detainment-sea-tac-airport) calling the detention "absolutely unacceptable." He said ICE is "operating as a paramilitary force that is a profound threat to our community" and that the agency "needs to be reined in before causing more chaos and fear."

Rep. Pramila Jayapal, also a Washington Democrat, said she was "disturbed by the reports of this detention at Sea-Tac" and that her office was "working to gather more information on the situation."

The Port of Seattle, which manages the airport, said the arrest was distressing not only for the affected family but for other passengers, and that local authorities would "engage federal officials to understand more about what occurred." April Sims, president of the Washington State Labor Council, AFL-CIO, said federal agents "introduced more fear and more chaos" during one of the busiest travel periods of the year.

## A Broader Crackdown

Saturday's arrests reflect a wider expansion of airport enforcement. ICE has stepped up arrests at airports since the summer, especially targeting people who overstayed visas, following increased collaboration between ICE and the Transportation Security Administration.

The enforcement surge comes against a backdrop of record arrest numbers. DHS figures obtained by CBS News found that ICE arrested 491,000 people in fiscal year 2026 — a record — including nearly 475,000 administrative arrests, which are civil rather than criminal detentions.

The Seattle incident landed only days after a nationwide ground stop grounded flights due to a communications problem, compounding traveler anxiety. It also followed the detention of a Princeton University student and Chinese national by ICE at Ronald Reagan National Airport in Washington, D.C., after she attended a neuroscience conference.

## What to Watch

The episode raises several unresolved questions. Federal prosecutors could pursue charges against Hernandez Mejia for assaulting a federal officer, while Larrondo Mendoza faces possible deportation proceedings. Washington state officials may investigate whether the agents violated the state's facial-covering law — though the state's jurisdiction over federal agents remains an open legal question.

Alaska Airlines and the Port of Seattle have said they are reviewing whether protocols were followed, particularly how federal agents accessed the jet bridge without advance notice. And with Democrats signaling they will intensify congressional oversight of ICE, the Seattle arrest could accelerate scrutiny of the agency's airport tactics heading into a charged political season.

For now, the image of masked officers on a boarding jetway — and the political firestorm it set off — has sharpened a question the country is still wrestling with: how far immigration enforcement should reach into the spaces of everyday American travel.
