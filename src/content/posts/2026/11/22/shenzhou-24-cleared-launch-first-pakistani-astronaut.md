# Shenzhou-24 Cleared for Launch With First Pakistani Astronaut

China's Shenzhou-24 crewed spaceflight mission completed its final full-system joint rehearsal on Saturday, November 21, clearing the way for a historic launch that will carry the first foreign national — a Pakistani payload specialist — to the country's Tiangong space station. According to [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/tech/20261121/2ccb8306ec134f28aca542e069819550/c.html), all launch mission systems have passed functional checks, equipment is stable, and personnel at the Jiuquan Satellite Launch Center in the Gobi Desert are ready for liftoff.

## A Mission of Firsts

The rehearsal marks the last major pre-launch exercise before the crewed flight. Conducted under the unified coordination of the Beijing Aerospace Flight Control Center, it involved the Jiuquan and Xi'an satellite launch and control centers along with various mission tracking sites, comprehensively simulating launch preparation, liftoff and the technical states encountered during flight.

The mission's defining feature is its international dimension. A Pakistani astronaut will fly aboard Shenzhou-24 as a payload specialist, becoming the first foreign national ever to board China's orbital outpost. As [Space.com](https://www.space.com/space-exploration/human-spaceflight/china-rolls-out-rocket-for-launch-of-1st-international-astronaut-to-tiangong-space-station-video) reported, this represents a first for both China's station and for Pakistan, which will see one of its citizens reach orbit for the first time. The visitor is expected to remain aboard for a short-duration stay — roughly a week — before returning to Earth aboard the departing Shenzhou-23 spacecraft.

The milestone carries clear diplomatic weight. Chinese Foreign Ministry spokesperson Guo Jiakun highlighted the flight as a key outcome of bilateral cooperation as the two countries mark the 75th anniversary of China-Pakistan diplomatic ties.

## Winter-Proofing a Gobi Launch

The mission is unfolding in early winter, when temperatures at the Jiuquan site sit near freezing. According to [Global Times](https://www.globaltimes.cn/page/202611/1374541.shtml), engineers have deployed a layered cold-weather defense: passive thermal insulation, active heating systems, enclosed protective structures on the launch tower, and temperature-controlled air continuously supplied to the rocket's fairing and body. Insulation materials wrap critical components, and additional temperature-control systems surround propellant tanks and engine components to keep sensitive equipment within operating ranges.

"The launch procedures have also been optimized to delay the opening of the tower's rotating platforms and minimize the time the rocket and spacecraft are exposed to low temperatures," Pang Zhihao, a Beijing-based senior space expert, told the Global Times. He added that these measures, combined with real-time meteorological monitoring and contingency plans for extreme cold, are designed to prevent temperature-related abnormalities in electronic components and propellants.

The meteorological system has also been upgraded for the occasion. It conducted a historical climate background analysis, upgraded its meteorological satellite ground application system, and developed neural-network-based hourly temperature forecast products to sharpen accuracy. After days of analysis and intensive consultation, the expert panel concluded that launch-day weather would meet the mission's minimum meteorological conditions, said Zhang Fang of the Jiuquan center.

The Shenzhou-24 spacecraft and its Long March-2F Y24 carrier rocket combination was transferred to the launch area on November 17, traveling roughly 1.5 kilometers along a seamless railway in a vertical transfer lasting about 90 minutes. The nearly 60-meter-tall combination now stands ready on the pad.

## A Safety Net in Reserve

Behind the scenes, China has positioned a second spacecraft-rocket combination as part of its emergency rescue architecture. The Long March-2F Y25 rocket and Shenzhou-25 spacecraft stand by inside the vertical assembly building, ready to support a rapid rescue mission should an emergency arise involving the Shenzhou-24 crew in orbit. The arrangement reflects lessons from the country's crewed program, including the uncrewed Shenzhou-22 emergency launch that China carried out in November 2025 as its first such contingency operation in the history of its human spaceflight effort.

## Broader Significance

The Shenzhou-24 flight fits into a deliberate expansion of China's space diplomacy. Since completing the three-module Tiangong station in late 2022, China has kept the outpost continuously crewed with rotating six-month missions. Beijing has positioned the station as an "open and collaborative" platform — and the Pakistani astronaut's flight is the most visible proof point yet.

There is an operational twist worth watching. One of the three Shenzhou-23 astronauts, approaching the end of a mission launched in May, will stay aboard Tiangong for an additional half-year, ceding a seat home to the international visitor while allowing a Chinese astronaut to attempt the country's first year-long stay in orbit. That extended mission is expected to yield physiological data useful for China's longer-term ambitions, including its planned crewed lunar flights before 2030.

"The mission is expected to yield valuable scientific data and open a new chapter in international cooperation aboard the China Space Station, as China continues to embrace an open and collaborative approach to exploring the vast universe," Pang told the Global Times.

## What's Next

With the final rehearsal complete, the launch site will proceed through pre-launch functional checks, joint testing and propellant loading ahead of liftoff. China's space agency typically holds a press conference at Jiuquan a day before launch, when the full three-person crew is revealed to the public. For now, one fact is settled: when Shenzhou-24 lifts off, it will carry more than a crew — it will carry a message about who China intends to bring along into orbit.
