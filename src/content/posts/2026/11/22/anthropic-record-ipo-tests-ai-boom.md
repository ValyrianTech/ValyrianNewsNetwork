# Anthropic's record IPO tests the AI boom

Anthropic, the US artificial-intelligence company behind the chatbot Claude, completed the largest stock market listing in history on Thursday, raising roughly 81.3 billion euros for the company itself in a debut that valued it at about 1.5 trillion dollars. But the central question for Belgian investors is no longer how big the deal would be — it is whether the shares are worth owning at all.

According to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/16/anthropic-naar-de-beurs/), the company priced its shares at 83 dollars each, just under 76 euros, and offered 980 million of them on the Nasdaq. That primary sale raised about 89.1 billion dollars, or some 81.3 billion euros — more than the record 85.7 billion dollars that SpaceX collected in June. Add about 26 billion dollars in secondary sales by existing shareholders such as AMD, Broadcom, Google, Microsoft and Nvidia, and the total deal was worth about 107.3 billion dollars, or nearly 98 billion euros, making it the largest listing ever.

## A pure-play AI bet, for the first time

The listing matters well beyond the size of the cheque. As VRT economics coverage framed it, Anthropic offers ordinary savers something genuinely new: the first chance to invest directly in a company whose entire business is building AI models, rather than in the chipmakers and infrastructure firms that have driven the sector's stock market run.

That distinction is central to the pitch and to the risk. Tom Simonts, a financial economist at the Belgian bank KBC, drew the contrast with SpaceX, which already earns money from its Starlink satellites. At AI companies like Anthropic, he told VRT, "there are above all a great many investments needed, while it remains uncertain what profits will take their place."

Anthropic does have a credible growth story. Simonts noted that revenue is expected to climb from about 55 billion dollars this year to some 685 billion dollars by 2030. The company is also, in his words, part of "the leading group" in the race to build a genuinely superintelligent system, a prize that would confer an enormous advantage on whoever gets there first. "But whether it will actually succeed," he cautioned, "remains of course a big gamble."

## The caution: IPOs and the overvaluation trap

For Belgian savers weighing a purchase, Simonts's most pointed warning concerns timing rather than the business itself. KBC, he said, has issued no official investment advice on Anthropic, but he offered a statistic worth remembering: many popular companies trade as much as 50 percent below their peak share price a year after going public.

"Especially with large IPOs there is usually a lot of interest at the start, and those companies are somewhat overvalued," he told VRT. "After 12 months we often see a more realistic level, which for the ten largest US IPOs of 2026 sits about 20 percent below the subscription price." He expects Anthropic to follow a similar path, in line with SpaceX so far.

He also flagged the incentives at work behind the scenes. Investment banks and early investors, he noted, benefit from pushing Anthropic's market value higher without triggering an untenable share price, because that earns them larger fees. And the cost side is formidable: Anthropic has committed to spending some 900 billion dollars in the future on computing power and infrastructure, a figure that dwarfs its current revenue.

## A wobble in the wider AI trade

Anthropic's debut lands in an uneasy moment for the industry it leads. As Simonts put it, enthusiasm for artificial intelligence has cooled of late, and several uncertainties now hang over the sector's most valuable names.

In the United States, questions persist over the construction of new AI data centres after Democratic gains in the midterms. Investors are also digesting the resignation of Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang, a shock to sentiment around the chipmaker whose processors underpin much of the AI boom. And there is the ever-present possibility that a rival such as OpenAI or a Chinese developer releases a cheaper or better model — a risk that could reshape Anthropic's prospects overnight.

None of these forces can be predicted, Simonts conceded, but together they will do much to determine whether Anthropic's extraordinary valuation can be justified.

## What to watch

For Belgian retail investors, the practical takeaway is less about the record headlines than about temperament. Anthropic is a real company with real revenue and a plausible path to enormous scale, but it is also a speculative bet priced for a future that has to be delivered. The largest IPO in history is not the same thing as the safest investment.

What happens next will depend on factors no one can yet forecast: whether AI spending turns into durable profit, whether the sector's confidence holds through the Nvidia turmoil, and whether Anthropic can convert its place in the leading pack into something more concrete. Until any of that becomes clearer, the question VRT posed to its readers — whether to buy the shares — has no easy answer, only a considered one.
