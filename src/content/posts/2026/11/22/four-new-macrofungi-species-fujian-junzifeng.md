# Four New Macrofungi Species Found in Fujian Reserve

Researchers have identified four new species of macrofungi in the Junzifeng National Nature Reserve in Fujian Province, bringing the reserve's tally of newly described fungal species to eight and underscoring the richness of China's southern subtropical forests. The findings, led by a team under Professor Qiu Junzhi of Fujian Agriculture and Forestry University, were published in the international mycology journal *Journal of Fungi*, according to [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/local/20261122/f204d7ebbef74dd79437e2a73cf5c621/c.html).

## The Four New Species

The newly described fungi span several genera. The "near-flavo-waxy Amanita" (近黄蜡鹅膏) is a member of the *Amanita* genus, distinguished by a yellow cap with an olive tone and a white-to-cream ring. The "black-central-stripe Entoloma" (中黑条纹粉褶蕈) belongs to the *Entoloma* genus, with a grey-black cap center and dark grey striations along its margin. Two belong to the milk-cap genus *Lactarius*: the "light-brown milk-cap" (浅褐乳菇), with a cream to light-brown cap mottled with darker patches, and the "yellow small milk-cap" (黄小乳菇), whose yellow-to-orange cap features an inrolled margin.

These four join four species previously discovered at the reserve: a *Cortinarius*, a Sanming cordyceps-type fungus, a ring-patterned *Russula*, and a *Russula* named for Junzifeng itself.

## A Broader Survey Effort

The discoveries emerged from a macrofungi diversity survey launched in 2024 by the reserve in partnership with Fujian Agriculture and Forestry University. According to [China News Service](https://www.chinanews.com.cn/sh/2026/11-17/10733282.shtml), the survey recorded 213 macrofungi species in total — including the eight new species, nine new records for China, and 27 species endemic to China.

"The survey recorded 213 macrofungi species in total; besides the 8 new species, there were also 9 new records for China and 27 species endemic to China," Qiu said.

## Why Fungi Matter

Macrofungi — fungi that produce fruiting bodies visible to the naked eye — play outsized ecological roles. As decomposers, they break down dead wood and fallen leaves, driving carbon and nitrogen cycling and maintaining forest material balance. Many also form mycorrhizal partnerships with plant roots, helping plants absorb water and nutrients and resist stress. Because many groups are sensitive to environmental change, they serve as valuable ecological indicators.

The species richness confirms that the reserve's forest floor has a well-developed humus layer and ample litter reserves, reflecting high ecosystem integrity and a healthy ecological baseline, China News Service reported.

## Analysis: A Proxy for Forest Health

While a taxonomic discovery may seem niche, its significance extends well beyond the cataloguing of four new names. Qiu framed the findings as evidence of the broader resource base of southern China's subtropical forests. "The discovery of these new fungal species reflects, from one angle, the richness of fungal resources in the subtropical forest areas of southern China, and at the same time adds baseline data for clarifying the national fungal baseline and enriching China's biodiversity records," he said.

The results also feed into China's national effort to document its fungal baseline — an often-overlooked pillar of biodiversity accounting that complements more visible conservation work on plants and animals. For a country investing heavily in protected-area networks, credible species inventories provide the metrics against which conservation outcomes can be measured.

## What's Next

With eight new species now confirmed at a single reserve, further survey work may reveal still more undescribed fungi. The project's findings suggest that China's subtropical forests remain a fertile frontier for mycology — and that the health of these ecosystems can be read, in part, from what fruits on the forest floor.
