# Marina Tijssen, acid attack victim, dies at 64

Marina Tijssen, the woman who survived a sulphuric acid attack at a Delhaize supermarket in Antwerp in 2015 and spent the following decade living with its consequences, has died at the age of 64. She died of lung cancer in the early hours of Saturday at her home in Olen, her husband Frank confirmed to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/21/slachtoffer-zuuraanval-antwerpen-overleden/) and [Gazet van Antwerpen](https://www.gva.be/regio/antwerpen/kempen/olen/marina-64-slachtoffer-van-zuuraanval-in-delhaize-overleden-je-laat-een-leegte-achter-die-nooit-meer-gevuld-zal-worden/164895695.html). She was surrounded by her husband and their two children.

"Marina was an incredibly strong woman. Certainly after everything she has been through," Frank told VRT NWS. "She fought to the last minute and was always there for her family. But she had to let go."

## A random victim of an extortion campaign

On 20 February 2015, Tijssen was working as a cleaner at the Delhaize on Museumstraat in Antwerp's Zuid district when a man threw sulphuric acid into her face. Her face and neck were severely disfigured, and she spent nine days in a coma, according to [Gazet van Antwerpen](https://www.gva.be/regio/antwerpen/kempen/olen/slachtoffer-zuuraanval-delhaize-er-is-mij-een-schadevergoeding-toegekend-maar-heb-nog-niks-gezien/39945105.html).

The attacker, Dutch national Jelle Frenken, had been extorting the Delhaize chain for months, threatening exactly such an attack. Tijssen was, in the words of the reporting, his "accidental" victim — a woman targeted not for who she was but for where she worked.

In October 2016, the Brussels criminal court convicted Frenken and sentenced him to 18 years in prison plus a 10-year disposal measure, as [VRT NWS reported](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2016/10/14/zuurgooier_delhaizeveroordeeldtot18jaarcel-1-2792651/) at the time. On appeal the following year, the sentence was upheld — with the court qualifying the offence as torture rather than attempted murder, as [De Standaard noted](https://www.standaard.be/nieuws/zuurgooier-krijgt-ook-in-beroep-18-jaar-cel-voor-foltering/48156785.html).

## More than 37 operations

The physical toll was lifelong. Tijssen underwent more than 37 operations. She was completely blind in her left eye and could no longer fully close her eyes, even in sleep. Nerve damage left her with constant pain around her mouth and neck, and acid fumes had damaged her oesophagus so badly that she could no longer drink alcohol.

"The pain I feel every day was done to me by a madman, and it's not easy to accept that," she said in 2020.

Frank told VRT NWS that the first years after the attack were the hardest. "She had a lot of pain in her mouth and couldn't enjoy a glass of wine, for example. But in the last 2 to 3 years she could." The couple travelled widely, and she said she was happy they had seen so much of the world. "She never complained, and was always there for her family."

## A long fight for compensation

The case became emblematic in Belgium of the gap between a court verdict and real justice. Roughly €1 million in damages was awarded to Marina and her husband, but as of 2020 they had received nothing — the perpetrator had no assets, and the victim fund had not paid out. She was left covering many medical costs herself and was considering psychological help. A civil case before the labour court in Turnhout was pending to claim an invalidity allowance.

"The perpetrator should pay it, but he has nothing," she said at the time. She also noted that Delhaize had never intervened, despite having been warned of the threats for months.

## Cancer, and the unresolved question

In May 2023, Tijssen was diagnosed with lung cancer. The family asked doctors whether the illness could be linked to the acid attack; they said they could not determine any connection. She was treated for months, but last summer the decision was taken to stop treatment. On Thursday she asked for palliative sedation, and she died overnight at around half past one.

"I told her she could stop fighting, and that it was good," Frank said. "She was more concerned about me than about herself. She also wrote a letter for each of the grandchildren, with a photo. That does her great credit."

In a tribute posted on social media, he wrote: "With immense sadness I must say goodbye to my dearest wife. My Loeke. My life partner, my best friend and the love of my life... You leave a void that can never be filled again."

## What's next

Tijssen's death is not legally a direct consequence of the 2015 attack, but her family and the Belgian media frame it against nearly twelve years of living with its aftermath. Her case remains a reference point in debates over victim compensation and the obligations of companies that have been warned of credible threats. No information was immediately available on funeral arrangements or any further legal steps by the family.
