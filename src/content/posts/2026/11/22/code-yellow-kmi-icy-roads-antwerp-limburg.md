# Code yellow: KMI warns of icy roads in Antwerp and Limburg

Belgium's Royal Meteorological Institute (KMI/IRM) issued a code yellow warning for slippery roads on Sunday morning, covering the provinces of Antwerp and Limburg in Flanders along with Liège, Namur and Luxembourg in Wallonia. The advisory, [reported by VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/21/antwerpen-limburg-glade-wegen/), remained in force until 11:00 and urged motorists to exercise caution as frost and ice patches formed overnight.

## A frosty night behind the warning

The warning followed a night in which temperatures dipped below freezing, fluctuating between -6°C and 1°C. That range left roads vulnerable to local frost (rijm) and isolated icy patches, particularly where residual moisture had not dried.

"Given that temperatures fluctuate around or slightly below freezing during the night from Saturday to Sunday, some local frost or ice patches should be expected," the KMI warned, according to VRT NWS.

The institute singled out the north and east of the country as the higher-risk zones. "In the north and east of the country the risk is somewhat greater due to the haze and local mist banks that will form there," the KMI said.

## What the colour codes mean

Under the KMI's colour-code framework, code yellow is the lowest of three alert levels—yellow, orange and red—and signals that dangerous weather is possible. The public is advised to stay attentive when outdoors or on the road. It is a routine designation in Belgium during the colder months, triggered whenever overnight temperatures approach or fall below the freezing mark, often combined with fog, mist or lingering damp on road surfaces.

## A cold but clearing day

Despite the frosty start, conditions were expected to improve through the morning. Once the local mist banks lifted, sunshine broke through in the north of the country. Forecasters put the daytime high at around 6°C with a minimum near 0°C, with dry and cloudy conditions prevailing.

## What to watch for

The warning lapsed at 11:00 as daytime temperatures climbed above freezing and roads dried out—a typical lifecycle for these short-duration advisories. Commuters in the affected provinces were still encouraged to allow extra time and drive to the conditions during the morning window.

With winter now setting in, further code yellow warnings for slippery roads are likely whenever overnight frost recurs, so travellers should check current conditions before setting out.
