# Chinese Jewelers Cut Gold Prices as Bullion Slides

Several major Chinese gold jewelry brands cut the per-gram price of their purity-gold ornaments by 20 to 34 yuan on 21 November, lowering quotes to roughly 1,056-1,068 yuan per gram, according to [CCTV Finance reporting republished by The Paper](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_34529710). The reductions followed a sharp drop in international gold prices during the previous trading session and triggered a rebound in foot traffic and sales at retail counters in Shanghai.

## Context

In trading on 20 November, both international gold futures and spot prices fell below US$3,550 per ounce intraday, with gold futures at one point dropping nearly 5 percent. Because Chinese jewelry pricing is closely pegged to international bullion benchmarks, moves in overseas markets transmit quickly to domestic retail quotes. The cuts announced on 21 November were described as the latest leg in a broader 2026 correction in gold prices rather than an isolated event.

## Key Developments

The price decline quickly fed through to consumer behavior. Shopping districts across Shanghai saw noticeably higher foot traffic at gold jewelry stores, with brands rolling out promotions to draw buyers. At the Laomiao Jingronglou cultural flagship store, gram-priced products were discounted by 100 yuan per gram, and the weekend was expected to bring a peak in customer visits.

"The price of jewelry gold floats down with international gold prices, making the per-transaction amount relatively more cost-effective," said Deng Guanyue, a salesperson at the Dunhuang Gold Shanghai Yuyuan flagship store.

Hua Yi, sales manager at the Laomiao Jingronglou cultural flagship store, said the store's gram-priced products were reduced by 100 yuan per gram and that the weekend would likely usher in a surge of shoppers.

The rebound underscores the price sensitivity—and cultural anchoring—of Chinese gold jewelry demand, which is tied to occasions such as weddings, where families traditionally purchase sets of gold pieces as gifts.

## Analysis

Industry insiders say that in the short to medium term, attention should focus on U.S. Federal Reserve rate-hike expectations and the trajectory of the U.S. dollar, both of which have weighed heavily on bullion this year. Over the longer term, U.S. real interest rates, the dollar's direction, and global central bank gold purchases will continue to shape the overall rhythm of the gold market.

The dynamic cuts both ways for the industry. Lower prices can revive volumes by drawing in price-conscious buyers, but sustained declines risk compressing margins for retailers holding inventory bought at higher levels. Whether the weekend buying surge proves durable, or was a short-lived promotional bump, remains an open question.

## What's Next

The direction of the correction will likely hinge on the Fed's December policy decision and the dollar's near-term path. For now, Chinese consumers appear to be treating the dip as an opportunity—a pattern consistent with gold's enduring role as both adornment and store of value in the domestic market.
