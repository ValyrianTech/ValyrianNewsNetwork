# Virginia Reports First Measles Death of 2026 in Young Child

A young child in Virginia has died after testing positive for measles, state health officials announced, marking the state's first measles-associated death of 2026 and the first such fatality in Virginia in nearly five decades. The death came just days after the United States lost its hard-won measles elimination status, a grim milestone that underscores how far the highly contagious virus has resurged amid declining vaccination rates.

The [Virginia Department of Health](https://www.vdh.virginia.gov/news/public-relations-contacts/2026-news-releases/vdh-1st-measles-death-2026/) reported the death on Friday, November 20, saying only that the child was between 0 and 4 years old. The department declined to release further details — including the child's exact age, vaccination status, and county of residence — to protect the family's privacy.

"This death is Virginia's first measles-associated death in 2026," the department said in its announcement. "To protect the family's privacy, VDH will not provide any further information regarding this death."

## A Death Days After a National Setback

The Virginia fatality did not occur in isolation. According to [AP News](https://apnews.com/article/measles-child-dies-virginia-e0c572252ece06a106cbe4d890e9fd70), the death was reported just days after the U.S., along with Mexico and Bolivia, lost its designation as having eliminated the virus — a status the country had held since 2000.

The Pan American Health Organization announced that setback on Tuesday, November 17, noting that measles had spread at the highest rate in more than 30 years, largely among the unvaccinated. "Measles elimination is not a trophy that is won once and placed on a shelf," PAHO director Dr. Jarbas Barbosa said at a news conference, according to the [Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/measles-elimination-status-us-mexico-b6694152aca12e8cf4293d9f50090ec9). "It is an achievement that must be safeguarded every day by closing immunity gaps and ensuring we reach every community."

Elimination status is awarded when a country goes more than 12 months without continuous local spread of the virus. The designation is largely symbolic, but its loss signals an erosion of public health preparedness — and experts warn that measles, among the most infectious diseases known to medicine, is often the first to reemerge when vaccination coverage falters.

## Virginia's Outbreak, Concentrated in the Center

Within Virginia, the virus has taken a particular toll. The state has reported 220 measles cases this year, most of them concentrated in central Virginia, and the vast majority were among people who were not vaccinated, according to AP. Of the 220 cases, 179 were in unvaccinated people, 39 were in people whose vaccination status was unknown, and two were in vaccinated people, state data show. The outbreak has centered on rural Buckingham County, which has recorded 127 cases — the most in the state — while neighboring Charlotte County has reported 34, according to the [Progress-Index](https://www.progress-index.com/story/news/healthcare/2026/11/20/child-from-virginia-is-first-measles-related-death-of-year/92884950007/).

Children have borne a disproportionate share of the burden: 40 of the state's cases were among children aged 0 to 4 and 75 among those aged 5 to 12. The Virginia Department of Health has repeatedly warned that children younger than 5, pregnant people, and those with weakened immune systems face heightened risk of severe complications, including pneumonia, brain inflammation, and death.

"Measles can cause serious illness, including pneumonia, brain inflammation, complications during pregnancy, and death," the department said. "Measles virus can also damage immune cells ... causing additional vulnerability to health."

## A National Toll That Keeps Climbing

The Virginia death pushes the national tally of state-reported measles fatalities to at least 12 in 2026, according to AP. Pennsylvania has reported eight deaths, Wisconsin two, and New York one. The U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention has tracked more than 5,800 measles cases nationally this year — by far the most of any year since 1991, according to AP.

The scale of the outbreak has alarmed public health experts, who point to a widening gap between federal and state death counts. The CDC has officially confirmed only six measles-associated deaths in 2026, using national mortality data rather than state figures — a discrepancy that has fueled criticism of the federal response, the Progress-Index reported.

"Runaway measles outbreaks in the United States, as well as the needless deaths, are all due to low measles vaccination coverage," Noel Brewer, a University of North Carolina public health professor who chairs a committee that presented U.S. measles data to PAHO, told the [Wall Street Journal](https://www.wsj.com/health/u-s-loses-measles-elimination-status-as-historic-resurgence-sickens-thousands-b95e8780). "We've gotten comfortable letting children be stopped by a deadly disease and die from it."

Separately, immunologist Frederic Bertley, chief executive of the Center of Science and Industry, told [Journal Arta](https://journalarta.com/en/2026/11/22/virginia-child-measles-associated-death-2026/) that the state had not recorded a measles-related death since 1978, calling measles "a growing problem for the United States."

## Vaccination as the Line of Defense

State health officials have stressed that vaccination remains the most effective protection. Two doses of the measles, mumps, and rubella (MMR) vaccine provide lifetime protection and are approximately 97% effective at preventing measles, according to VDH. Experts say that a community vaccination rate of roughly 95% is needed to prevent outbreaks.

Officials are now bracing for the possibility of further spread as families and friends gather for Thanksgiving next week and for December holidays. AP reported there has been "concern about the virus spreading" during the holiday season, even as experts note that most adults do not need a measles booster shot, though some may be good candidates for one.

This month, the CDC also issued [new guidance on measles and pregnancy](https://www.cdc.gov/measles/media/pdfs/2026/11/measles-in-pregnancy.pdf), noting an increased risk of miscarriage, congenital infections, and health problems such as pneumonia for pregnant women.

## What's Next

Virginia's measles dashboard is scheduled to be updated on Tuesday, November 24, to reflect the death. As the nation confronts its worst measles year in more than three decades, the coming weeks will test whether vaccination campaigns and public health messaging can slow transmission — and whether the country can begin the long path toward regaining its elimination status.

For now, the Virginia death stands as a stark reminder of what experts say was preventable. Children under 5, pregnant people, and the immunocompromised remain the most vulnerable, and health officials continue to urge families to check their vaccination records and get the MMR vaccine if they have not received the full two-dose series.
