# China's Han Zheng to Attend COP31 Summit, Visit Georgia

Chinese Vice President Han Zheng will travel to Türkiye from November 6 to 12 as Special Representative of President Xi Jinping to attend the Antalya Leaders' Summit of the 31st Conference of the Parties (COP31) to the UN Framework Convention on Climate Change, and will then pay an official visit to Georgia, a Chinese Foreign Ministry spokesperson announced Wednesday, according to [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/world/20261104/75fb1ccd477f4868b2b3f615a570acdd/c.html).

## Context

The Antalya Leaders' Summit is the heads-of-state and government segment of COP31, convened by Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan for November 11 and 12, 2026, under the theme "Towards a Sustainable, Resilient and Equitable Future," the [UNFCCC](https://unfccc.int/cop31/high-level) states. It constitutes the first part of the High-Level Segment for Heads of State and Government, giving world leaders a platform to announce new climate commitments and launch initiatives at the start of the broader COP31 conference, which runs November 9 to 20 in Antalya.

Han served as China's top-ranked vice premier from 2018 to 2023 and was a member of the Politburo Standing Committee until 2022. Since assuming the largely ceremonial vice presidency in 2023, he has frequently been deployed as Xi's personal envoy to high-profile diplomatic events — a role that lends weight to Beijing's presence at this summit.

## Key Developments

The announcement comes after a busy period in China's relations with Tbilisi. On June 9, 2026, Xi and Georgian President Mikheil Kavelashvili jointly announced the elevation of bilateral ties to a comprehensive strategic partnership on the 34th anniversary of diplomatic relations, the [Chinese Embassy in Georgia](https://ge.china-embassy.gov.cn/eng/xwdt/202606/t20260609_11940748.htm) reported.

"No matter how the international landscape evolves, China always views its relations with Georgia from a strategic and long-term perspective," Xi said in his message at the time. The upgrade built on a 2023 "strategic partnership" and a 2017 free trade agreement, with China now among Georgia's largest trading partners and a major infrastructure investor.

## Analysis

Dispatching a figure of Han's standing — rather than a minister — signals Beijing's intent to project high-level engagement on climate. China is the world's largest greenhouse-gas emitter and the dominant global supplier of solar panels, batteries and electric vehicles, making its posture at COP31 central to the negotiations. Han's presence positions China as a constructive player even as it faces Western pressure over emissions and clean-technology trade.

The summit also offers a platform for diplomacy on the sidelines. Türkiye is a key node in the Belt and Road Initiative, and Han is expected to engage Erdoğan's government on trade, the Middle Corridor and energy.

The Georgia leg underscores the momentum of the June 2026 partnership. Georgia has grown more strategically important as a transit hub along the Middle Corridor — the China–Central Asia–South Caucasus–Europe route — particularly as overland paths through Russia have become less viable since the 2022 invasion of Ukraine. The closer relationship has nonetheless drawn concern from Georgia's pro-Western voices, who worry about implications for Tbilisi's ties with Washington and Brussels. Georgian Dream officials counter that the country can maintain free trade with both China and the EU simultaneously.

## What's Next

Han's travel window places him in Antalya for the Leaders' Summit on November 11 and 12, with the Georgia visit following immediately after. Key questions remain open: whether Han will deliver a national statement or unveil new Chinese climate commitments at the summit, which bilateral meetings are planned on the sidelines, and what specific agreements — on trade, Middle Corridor infrastructure or the Anaklia port project — may emerge from the Georgia stop. How Georgia's Western partners respond to the visit will also be watched closely in Brussels and Washington.
