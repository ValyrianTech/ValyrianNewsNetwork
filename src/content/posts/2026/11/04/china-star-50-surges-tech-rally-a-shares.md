# China's STAR 50 Surges 9.9% as Tech Rally Lifts A-Shares

Chinese stocks staged a sharp, broad-based rally on Wednesday, with the technology-heavy STAR 50 index climbing 9.92% and the ChiNext growth board gaining 6.40%, as a semiconductor-led advance drove turnover back above 2 trillion yuan for the first time since September 22. More than 4,600 individual stocks closed higher, according to [Sina Finance](https://finance.sina.com.cn/tob/2026-11-04/doc-inixqaaw6203693.shtml), which confirmed the closing figures.

The move capped a dramatic session that began with a soft, choppy open and early profit-taking, before a powerful afternoon reversal swept the market. The intraday swing was first flagged by [Cailianshe, republished by Sina News](https://news.sina.com.cn/c/2026-11-04/doc-inixqaaw6185980.shtml), which reported the STAR 50 up more than 10% and ChiNext up nearly 7% during the session.

## A Reversal Built on Semiconductors

The benchmark Shanghai Composite closed at 3,686.17, up 2.16%, while the Shenzhen Component rose 4.22% to 11,335.69. The ChiNext settled at 2,544.73. Turnover across the Shanghai, Shenzhen and Beijing markets reached roughly 2.05 trillion yuan, up about 336 billion yuan from the prior session.

The day's standout was semiconductor equipment. Jingyi Equipment, Torrens, Huafeng Test & Control, Zhongke Feice and Piotech all hit their 20% daily limit, while sector leader NAURA Technology closed limit-up at 10%. ACM Research Shanghai advanced 19.82% and Hwatsing Technology gained 17.97%.

The rally had a fundamental anchor. Sina Finance reported that 14 Chinese semiconductor-equipment companies posted combined revenue of about 75.94 billion yuan in the first three quarters of 2026, up 19.3% year-on-year, with net profit up 59.5%. Order backlogs were robust, with ACM Research Shanghai's in-hand orders reaching 170.73 billion yuan, up 88.2%, and leading equipment firms' delivery schedules reportedly booked into the first half of 2027.

## Gold and Storage Chips Join the Advance

Precious metals stocks rallied in parallel. Xiaocheng Technology rose 20%, and Xingye Yinxi, Shanjin International, Zhaojin Gold, Western Gold, Shandong Gold, Sichuan Gold and Chifeng Gold all hit limit-up. The move followed the World Gold Council's November 3 report that China's third-quarter gold demand reached 192 tonnes, up 12% year-on-year and 23% quarter-on-quarter.

Storage chip names also surged: Beijing Junzheng gained 17.8%, Puya Semiconductor 16.38% and Changxin Technology 13.63%. Co-packaged optics stocks advanced, with Eoptolink up 13.35%.

Not every sector participated. The baijiu liquor sector weakened in the afternoon, with Huangtai Liquor falling 4.66%, while oil and gas names slipped, led by CNOOC down 3.10%. Banks were also soft, with Agricultural Bank of China lower intraday.

## Why the Rally Mattered

The proximate catalyst, according to same-day market commentary carried by Sina Finance, was the resolution of US midterm election uncertainty, with projections indicating Democrats would retake both chambers of Congress. A softer dollar, falling long-end Treasury yields and reduced policy uncertainty were seen as supportive of risk assets and dollar-denominated metals alike.

The reversal also marked a shift from a period of weakness. Commentary referenced oversold conditions and a 22-session streak of net ETF inflows as supporting the rebound. The session's breadth — 45 of 56 industry sectors in positive territory, with 427 stocks gaining more than 9% and only 20 falling more than 9% — underscored how widely the recovery spread.

Domestic technology policy added tailwinds. On the same day, 20 organizations including the China Academy of Information and Communications Technology, Huawei, Alibaba, Tencent and Xiaomi jointly released a self-discipline convention on AI-agent development, calling for shared infrastructure and broader deployment across industries.

## What to Watch

Brokerage commentary framed November as a policy-expectation-driven month. Everbright Securities, quoted by Sina Finance, said the market would likely "continue to oscillate," with month-end policy signals potentially becoming a mid-term watershed as expectations build for December's Central Economic Work Conference.

Whether the rally extends into a sustained recovery or proves a single-session rebound remains the central question. With earnings season concluded and overseas central-bank meetings and geopolitical risks still on the calendar, analysts expect continued volatility — and, for now, structural rather than broad-based opportunity.
