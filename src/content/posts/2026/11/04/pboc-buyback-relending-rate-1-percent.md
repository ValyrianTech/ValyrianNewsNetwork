# PBOC Cuts Buyback Relending Rate to 1%, Makes It Permanent

China's central bank cut the relending rate for its stock buyback and shareholding-increase facility by 25 basis points to 1%, effective November 4, and reclassified the program from a temporary backstop into a long-term institutional arrangement. The People's Bank of China said the move is intended to "maintain stable capital-market operations" and to step up support for share buybacks by listed companies and stake increases by major shareholders, [Xinhua News reported](https://www.news.cn/fortune/20261104/b8eeda3bdfdd4540871cf217c7014112/c.html).

The cut is the fourth reduction since the facility was created in October 2024, when its rate stood at 1.75%. The rate was lowered to 1.5% in May 2025, to 1.25% in early 2026, and now to 1%.

## What the facility does

The relending tool channels cheap central-bank funding through banks to qualifying listed companies and their major shareholders for stock buybacks and shareholding increases. Eligible institutions comprise 21 national banks and 5 systemically important city commercial banks. The PBOC supports 100% of the eligible loan principal for a one-year term that can be extended at the bank's request.

Crucially, banks are permitted to add a reasonable markup over the relending rate, allowing them to price loans to reflect client risk while the central bank effectively transfers the interest spread to lenders. The PBOC encouraged institutions to accelerate lending to strengthen listed companies' market-value-management capabilities.

## From emergency backstop to standing institution

The rate cut formalised a decision taken at a November 2 joint symposium convened by the PBOC with the National Financial Regulatory Administration and the China Securities Regulatory Commission. As [The Paper](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_34389860) reported, the policy "changed from a temporary arrangement to a long-term institutional arrangement, with the relending rate cut from 1.25% to 1%."

Tian Xuan, dean of Peking University's Guanghua School of Management, argued that the reclassification matters more than the rate cut itself. "The significance of this adjustment is not just the 25 basis point cut, but the qualitative change in the tool's functional positioning and its institutionalisation," he told [21st Century Business Herald via Sina Finance](https://finance.sina.com.cn/roll/2026-11-03/doc-inixmnqa6803574.shtml), "meaning the tool shifts from emergency backstopping to a normalised arrangement."

That shift addresses a persistent complaint from corporates: that an explicitly temporary tool was too uncertain to build into annual capital plans. By making it a standing arrangement, the PBOC is aiming to make buybacks a routine financing option rather than a crisis response.

## Quota demand surges

The PBOC signalled ample firepower. After the November 2 symposium, banks submitted relending quota applications exceeding 200 billion yuan (around 2000亿元), and the central bank said it will continue to fully meet banks' needs.

Take-up has grown steadily. Signed loan contracts reached about 440 billion yuan as of November 2, with cumulative disbursement exceeding 280 billion yuan, up from 390 billion yuan signed and 210 billion yuan disbursed at end-June 2026.

The country's securities regulator has recorded 902 listed companies disclosing 1,110 buyback and increase-loan announcements. Including applications from state capital operators China Guoxin and China Chengtong, total applied quota has reached roughly 440 billion yuan.

## Efficiency is now the test

The scale of the program masks a starker trend. In the facility's second year, from October 2025 to October 2026, new A-share buyback and increase-loan applications totalled just 366 with an upper limit of about 81.6 billion yuan, down roughly 51% and 46% year-on-year respectively. Total A-share buyback amounts, however, rose 13.8% year-on-year to 157.4 billion yuan, indicating companies increasingly fund buybacks from their own balance sheets.

Tian pointed to a conversion gap that suggests further effort is needed on utilisation rather than pricing. "Although over 280 billion yuan has been disbursed versus 440 billion yuan signed, the conversion rate is only about 64%, with nearly 40% of quota stuck in contracts, showing considerable room to improve capital-use efficiency," he said.

He recommended that policymakers shift emphasis from "expanding quota and cutting rates" toward "raising conversion rates and improving use-oriented guidance" — including differentiated risk-weighting, green approval channels, lower financing thresholds, and assessment metrics based on actual disbursement rather than signed amounts.

## The wider support package

The buyback facility operates alongside the Securities, Fund and Insurance Companies Swap Facility, which lets non-bank financial institutions swap assets for central-bank liquidity to invest in equities. That tool, rolled over several times, currently stands at a balance of 105 billion yuan and is also being optimised.

## What to watch

Attention now turns to how quickly the 200 billion yuan in fresh quota applications converts into actual purchases, and whether the long-term status reverses the year-on-year decline in new applications. Analysts also question how banks will balance markup pricing against take-up among smaller-capitalisation companies, which increasingly account for demand as large caps shift to self-funding.

The signal the PBOC is sending, though, is unambiguous: its willingness to underwrite A-share stability is no longer framed as a temporary fix.
