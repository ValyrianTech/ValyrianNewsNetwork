# Connecticut Bakers Revive Election Cake Tradition

In an era when American elections are more often associated with anxiety than celebration, a group of Connecticut bakers spent the final days before the 2026 midterms trying to change that - with flour, sugar, and frosting. The fifth annual Election Cake Bake-Off, held Oct. 26 at Connecticut's Old State House in Hartford, revived a colonial-era tradition that once made voting day a community festival, according to [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/11/03/nx-s1-6026494/election-cake-bake-off-hartford-connecticut). The event offered a lighthearted, nonpartisan counterpoint to a tense political season, blending civic pride with culinary heritage that dates back more than 250 years.

## A Tradition Rooted in Colonial Connecticut

Election cake is one of the oldest baking traditions in the American Northeast. The practice dates to 1771 in Hartford, when Connecticut was still a colony and an early pioneer of self-government. Because residents in the region could elect their own officials, election day became a joyous occasion marked by parties, parades, and - above all - cake.

"It was definitely something that Connecticut, as a colony and then later as a state, celebrated and really felt attached to," Nicole Sousa, events and outreach manager at the Old State House, told NPR.

The original 18th-century recipes reflected the scale of those celebrations. A traditional election cake called for roughly 30 quarts of flour, 10 pounds of butter, 14 pounds of sugar, 12 pounds of raisins, three dozen eggs, and more than a quart of wine or brandy - a dense, alcohol-soaked fruit cake designed to feed crowds of voters. The tradition was later preserved in *American Cookery*, widely recognized as the first American cookbook, where author Amelia Simmons included a scaled-down version of the recipe.

## A Star-Spangled Winner

This year's contest crowned a mother-daughter duo led by Sophia Grestini of Amy's Cakes LLC in Cheshire, Connecticut, [Patch](https://patch.com/connecticut/hartford/old-state-house-names-election-cake-winner-see-who-won) reported.

The winning cake was a four-layer showpiece alternating between sweet lemon-blueberry and traditional spiced sponge flavors. It was decorated in red, white, blue, and gold with election-day symbols - an "I voted" pin, a ballot box, and an American flag - alongside handcrafted fondant donkey and elephant figures representing the two major parties, topped with a bald eagle sculpted entirely from frosting. The design also incorporated a reference to the nation's 250th anniversary, Patch reported. Judges evaluated entries on presentation, creativity, and taste.

The prize: the title of official Connecticut Election Cake of 2026, plus a commission to bake the Old State House's birthday cake in May 2027.

## Baking for the Love of It

What distinguishes the bake-off from the sharp edges of modern campaign politics is its spirit of good-natured competition. Sousa described the entrants as fierce but joyful.

"They're in it to win it for sure. But they're also in it for the love of Election Day, the love of baking and the love of the idea of election cakes," she told NPR.

That ethos is at the heart of the event's purpose. Rather than treating voting as a chore or a source of dread, the bake-off frames civic participation as something to be shared and savored.

"That's what we're trying to do with the election cake contest - we're trying to bring that joy back to Election Day," Sousa said. "It should be fun, it should be a way to show that you're contributing to your country and part of your community."

## Why It Matters

In a polarized political climate, small civic rituals can carry outsized meaning. The election cake revival is part of a broader effort to reframe voting as a communal celebration rather than a partisan battleground - a reminder that participation in democracy has historically been a source of local pride.

The bake-off also introduces a younger generation to a slice of culinary history that might otherwise fade. By transforming a dense colonial fruitcake into elaborately decorated, patriotic showpieces, today's bakers are both preserving and reinventing the tradition.

## What's Next

With the 2026 midterms now underway, the Old State House's bake-off has already done its part to lighten the mood. The winning baker will return in May 2027 to craft the historic building's birthday cake - ensuring the election cake tradition lives on, one celebration at a time.

For Sousa and the organizers, the measure of success is simpler than any ballot tally. "It should be fun," she said. For a few hours each fall in Hartford, it is.
