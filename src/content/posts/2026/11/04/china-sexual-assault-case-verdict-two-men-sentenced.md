# Two Men Sentenced in China Sexual Assault Case

A court in Shanxi Province on Wednesday sentenced two villagers to prison for repeatedly raping a mentally ill woman who went missing for more than a decade and gave birth to multiple children, a case that has drawn sustained national scrutiny over how China's justice system treats vulnerable women.

The Zuoquan County People's Court convicted Zhang Mou'guo of rape and sentenced him to 11 years and six months, adding a one-year term for molesting a child, for a combined sentence of 12 years, according to [Sina News](https://news.sina.com.cn/c/2026-11-04/doc-inixprpa6253073.shtml). Co-defendant Zhang Mou'lin was convicted of rape and sentenced to 10 years. Both men declined to appeal in court, and roughly 60 people attended the hearing as observers.

The verdict closes one chapter of a saga that began in 2024, when Bu Mou — a master's-degree holder diagnosed with schizophrenia — was found living in a rural household in Heshun County, 13 years after she disappeared from her family home. But the sentencing has done little to quiet the broader controversy: the man she lived with, who fathered children with her, was never prosecuted.

## A Decade-Long Disappearance

Bu, born in 1979, earned a master's degree in engineering from Yanshan University in 2008. She was diagnosed with schizophrenia the following year and was hospitalized from March to June 2009, continuing outpatient treatment until at least 2011. On May 26, 2011, she went missing from her home in Yuci District, Jinzhong City.

According to the family and court documents reported by [Yangzi Evening News](https://www.yzwb.net/news/zntt/202601/t20260123_314442.html), a villager found a disheveled Bu drinking dirty water by the roadside in late July 2011 and took her home. Days later she met Zhang Moujun, went to his house for lunch, and stayed. Within two to three months, the two began a sexual relationship.

In February 2013, Bu gave birth to the first of several children. In 2015, Zhang Moujun sent another infant to a more affluent family for a 40,000 yuan fee, citing family hardship. No marriage was ever registered between the two.

## The Two Convictions

The criminal cases against the two neighbors emerged from that investigation. Prosecutors said Zhang Mou'guo went to Zhang Moujun's home four times between mid-2020 and spring 2024 and had sex with Bu, knowing her mental condition left her unable to express her will correctly. On two of those occasions, Zhang Moujun was not at home.

Zhang Mou'guo was also convicted of molesting Zhang Moujun's young daughter in the summer of 2022 after drinking. Zhang Mou'lin was found to have assaulted Bu three times between 2022 and 2024, on each occasion while she was alone watching television in bed.

A December 2024 appraisal by the Shanxi Provincial Forensic Psychiatric Appraisal Center determined that Bu suffers from schizophrenia and has "no sexual self-defense capacity" — a legal finding that she cannot recognize or protect her own sexual inviolability.

Both men confessed and accepted punishment at trial, according to the reports. The case was first tried in a closed session on December 29, 2025.

## The Unprosecuted Partner

The sentencing of the two villagers has renewed attention on a decision that sparked widespread anger in January 2026, when the Heshun County Procuratorate announced it would not prosecute Zhang Moujun, the man who took Bu in.

In its decision, prosecutors concluded that his sexual relationship with Bu and their shared child-rearing were "essentially different from rape" because his subjective aim was to build a family. They characterized his sending a child away for 40,000 yuan as civil adoption with "trivially slight circumstances and negligible harm, not deemed a crime."

The reasoning drew sharp criticism, as [The New York Times Chinese edition](https://www.nytchina.com/china/20260130/china-woman-mental-illness-rape/) reported, because it appeared to treat Bu's inability to consent differently depending on the presumed intent of the man involved. Critics noted the apparent tension: prosecutors cited an expert finding that Bu lacked the capacity to defend herself sexually when charging the two villagers, yet set that same finding aside for the man who lived with her.

"Whether it constitutes rape no longer depends on whether she has the ability to consent, but on whether the other party successfully packaged the sexual relationship into a seemingly normal, stable, socially accepted life relationship," lawyer Yan Senlin wrote in a post that was later deleted, according to the Times.

For Bu's family, the convictions of the two villagers do not resolve the core grievance. Her brother, Bu Xiansheng, has said the family filed an incidental civil claim attached to the criminal case. He told reporters the two children remain in Zhang Moujun's household and that the two families have had no contact.

## What's Next

Bu remains hospitalized for psychiatric treatment, and her family says her condition has improved markedly since her return. "Even if I told her, in her current state she might not understand much, but her condition is definitely much better than when she just came back — now the family all recognize each other," her brother said.

The case has become a touchstone in a broader domestic debate over protections for people with mental disabilities, the legal definition of consent, and the rural practice of "taking in" vulnerable women. With both defendants declining to appeal, the criminal proceedings against the two villagers appear settled — but the questions the case raised about the unprosecuted partner, and about how a missing woman could live undetected for 13 years, remain unresolved.
