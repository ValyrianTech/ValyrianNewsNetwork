# 911 Audio of Father Who Killed Bend School Shooter Released

Deschutes County authorities on Tuesday released the 911 audio recording from the October school shooting in Bend, Oregon, offering an unfiltered window into the frantic seconds before a local father confronted and fatally shot a teenage gunman at Silver Rail Elementary School. The 78-second recording captures Kaylub Giamanco, a 36-year-old carpenter and father of two students at the school, calling for help even as he moved to intervene in the Oct. 15 attack that left four people dead.

The release came amid intense public interest in the case, ongoing scrutiny of school safety protocols, and a widening national debate over the role of armed civilians, according to [OPB](https://www.opb.org/article/2026/11/03/bend-oregon-silver-rail-elementary-school-shooting-crime-911-call/), the Oregon public broadcaster that first obtained and reported on the audio.

## A Father's Frantic Call

In the recording, Giamanco's voice is urgent and at times obscured by a dispatcher's questions and other emergency calls in the background. "Oh my God, someone's at my daughters' school with a gun," he says. "I have a gun, and I will shoot them right now if I have to."

The dispatcher repeatedly urges him to stay on the line and not to "do anything," promising that help is en route. Giamanco, driving home for leftover tacos when he happened upon the scene, instead shouts the school's name and describes his approach. "This is my daughter's school!" he says. "I'm about to go kill this kid. He's in the school!"

As the call continues, Giamanco announces, "Entering the building. Right now!" and then, "God damn it, they're [expletive] shooting people! Come on!" His end of the line then goes silent as the dispatcher asks for a description of the shooter.

Portions of the audio are garbled. In a previous exclusive interview with [OPB](https://www.opb.org/article/2026/10/23/bend-oregon-kaylub-giamanco-silver-rail-elementary-school-shooting/), Giamanco recalled telling the dispatcher, "I'm sorry, I have to go in. People are going to die," before ending the call. Those exact words are not audible on the released tape, though not all of his side of the conversation was captured.

## The Attack and the Intervention

The shooting began shortly before 1 p.m. on Oct. 15, when a 16-year-old former student carrying a shotgun entered Silver Rail Elementary, a campus of more than 400 children. The gunman fired into a special education classroom, killing three children — ages 5, 8, and 11 — and a 25-year-old teacher. Two others were seriously injured, including 7-year-old Saylor Graham.

Giamanco, who had been driving along the road bordering the school, spotted the gunman and followed him onto the grounds. Inside, he found a teacher struggling with the shooter and fired, striking the teenager. He then fired two more times as the gunman scrambled for his weapon. Both of Giamanco's daughters survived the attack.

Bend Police Chief Mike Krantz praised the courage of both Giamanco and the teacher at a press conference hours after the shooting. President Donald Trump later called Giamanco "a great hero that went in and did the business."

## A Rare Civilian Intervention

The episode stands out for its rarity. Armed civilians stopping mass shooters is exceptionally uncommon — in roughly 600 U.S. cases since 2000, it has happened only a few dozen times, and experts who study mass shootings told OPB they were unaware of any prior instance involving a school.

The case has also injected Giamanco into the national gun-control debate. Deschutes County reported a rise in concealed handgun license applications following the incident. Giamanco, who describes himself as an independent, has said he does not want to make the tragedy political. "I teach my children that we love, we accept everybody," he told OPB. "I don't want to make this a political situation."

## A Community Grieves and Rebuilds

Silver Rail Elementary has remained closed through at least Nov. 6, while other Bend-La Pine schools reopened on Oct. 21. A district survey found that 86 percent of Silver Rail families want to return to the campus.

The shooting has intensified local demands for security changes, including school resource officers, cameras, bulletproof glass, and stronger locks. Giamanco has proposed a motocross fundraiser to support school safety improvements. "We have smart homes. We have smart cars," he said. "Why can't we have smart schools?"

## What's Next

The released audio largely corroborates Giamanco's public account and is unlikely to alter the official narrative of the response. Police have credited him with saving lives, and no charges against him have been indicated.

Open questions remain, including how the teenage gunman obtained the shotgun and whether any warning signs were missed. For Giamanco, the public exposure has come with a heavy emotional toll. He told OPB he still relives the day and grieves for the victims he could not reach. "I want to apologize for not saving their babies," he said. "The gratitude for saving people's children is awesome, but there were ones that I didn't save."
