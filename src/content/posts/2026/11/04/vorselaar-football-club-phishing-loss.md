# Phishing Drains Vorselaar Football Club's Accounts

Amateur football club Ons Genoegen (OG) Vorselaar has fallen victim to a telephone-based phishing fraud that drained large sums from its bank accounts, the club has confirmed. The theft came to light just before the weekend of 31 October–1 November and has been described by the club board as "a drama for OG Vorselaar," according to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/04/og-vorselaar-phishing-voetbalclub-drama/).

Regional broadcaster [RTV](https://www.rtv.be/actualiteit-en-nieuws/phishers-halen-100000-euro-van-rekening-voetbalclub-og-vorselaar), citing reporting by Gazet van Antwerpen, put the loss at up to €100,000 — a figure the club has confirmed in substance but declined to confirm in precise amount.

## How the fraud unfolded

According to chairman Glenn Van de Vel, the fraudsters relied on social engineering rather than a mass email campaign. "Fraudsters phoned an employee. They had that person carry out certain actions using a pretext, and in that way gained access to the accounts," Van de Vel told VRT NWS.

The targeted staff member is said to be deeply affected by the incident. Once the theft was discovered, the club raised the alarm with its bank's fraud team and with the police, and is now attempting to recover the stolen funds. Those recovery efforts, RTV reported, "look unlikely to yield much for now."

OG Vorselaar was founded in 1992 and plays in first provincial, a level of Belgian amateur football. For a club of that size, the loss represents what RTV described as "a heavy blow."

## Painful timing for a club with plans

The fraud could hardly have come at a worse moment. Earlier this year the club presented an ambitious future plan to the municipal authorities, proposing to replace ageing facilities — infrastructure the club says "has had its best time" — with an entirely new complex. A public participation process ran during the summer of 2026, and the municipality is currently examining what financial support it can offer, with clarity expected when the multi-year plan is adjusted later this year.

The theft directly erodes the club's own savings pot just as that partnership was taking shape. "For an association it is always a drama when someone touches the savings pot, but it comes at a very unfortunate moment alongside a process we have been running to see how we could cooperate around their future plans," said Vorselaar mayor Simon Dens (Koester 2290). "That makes it extra regrettable that this has now happened. We hope, together with the club, that some sums can still be recovered."

Despite the setback, OG Vorselaar intends to continue talks with the municipality and hopes its operations and future plans will not be jeopardised. The club has declined to state exactly how much money was stolen.

## A national problem

The OG Vorselaar case fits a broader pattern of phishing and social-engineering fraud across Belgium, where criminals increasingly target trusted insiders through sustained phone manipulation and fake payment requests. For victims such as small non-profit clubs, recovery often hinges on how quickly funds are blocked and on the discretion of the bank — which explains the cautious hope voiced by both the club and the mayor rather than confidence that the money will be returned.

Because the fraud appears to have involved manipulating an employee into carrying out certain actions, the case may also turn on how the bank classifies the transaction — a distinction that can determine whether the club is treated as a genuine victim or as having authorised the transfers.

## What to watch next

The exact amount lost remains unconfirmed, with €100,000 the reported ceiling figure. No arrests or suspects have been reported, and the police investigation is ongoing. The central questions now are whether any funds can be recovered, how the theft affects the club's contribution to its planned new complex, and whether the municipal partnership — and the club's very survival — can proceed as intended.
