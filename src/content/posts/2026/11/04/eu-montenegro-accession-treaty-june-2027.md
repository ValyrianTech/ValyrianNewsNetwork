# EU Eyes Montenegro Treaty by June 2027

The European Union is aiming to sign an accession treaty with Montenegro by June 2027, putting the small Adriatic nation on course to become the bloc's 28th member state — potentially by 2028. The target, confirmed by Enlargement Commissioner Marta Kos as the European Commission published its 2026 Enlargement Package on 4 November, would end more than a decade of enlargement stagnation and break the ice for a new wave of candidates.

According to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/03/europese-unie-wil-in-juni-volgend-jaar-toetredingsverdrag-ondert/), Kos said Montenegro's accession negotiations could be wrapped up by the end of this year, with the treaty finalized in the spring and signed in June. Membership would then follow around 2028, after roughly a year of ratification by all 27 national parliaments.

"Montenegro is on track to become the 28th member of the EU in 2028," Kos said, according to [Vijesti](https://en.vijesti.me/news-b/politika/834791/Kosovo-Montenegro-on-track-to-become-EU-member-in-2028--accession-treaty-likely-in-summer-2027).

## A Frontrunner Ready to Close

Montenegro has long been the most advanced Western Balkan candidate. It uses the euro unilaterally, has aligned closely with EU foreign policy, and has steadily closed negotiating chapters. Of the bloc's negotiating chapters, it has provisionally closed 21, and the Commission now assesses that it has met all the conditions to close the remaining ones — subject to unanimous approval by EU member states.

"The accession treaty [could be] finalized in the spring and signed in June," a senior EU official said, as [EU Reporter](https://www.eureporter.co/world/montenegro/2026/11/04/montenegro-first-in-line-for-eu-enlargement-accession-treaty-could-be-signed-in-june-2027/) noted. The official added that the remaining technical documents would be sent to the Council shortly, paving the way for negotiations to conclude in December.

Its small size — Kos compared its population to that of the Belgian city of Antwerp — makes integration administratively easier. "I am sure we will bring the population on board," she said.

The main risk is a bilateral objection from Croatia, which has raised outstanding issues with its neighbour and has tried in recent months to slow the process. Because accession requires unanimity, any single member state can stall a candidate. The Commission, however, appears confident the dispute can be resolved.

"Croatia has highlighted that it has a number of expectations in respect of Montenegro, which Montenegro is working to address," the senior EU official said. "We do not expect this issue to become an obstacle on the roadmap."

## Four Frontrunners

Beyond Montenegro, Kos identified four frontrunners: Montenegro, Albania, Moldova and Ukraine. Each is at a different stage, and each faces distinct obstacles.

**Albania** has been one of the fastest movers, opening all negotiating clusters in just over a year and provisionally closing its first four chapters. It aims to finish all chapters by the end of 2027, but must reverse a recent reform slowdown. "In the past year, Albania has really sprung into action with all kinds of reforms," Kos said. "The question now is what impact the street protests against the government will have on their progress." [EU Reporter](https://www.eureporter.co/economy/enlargement-economy/2026/11/04/albania-could-conclude-eu-accession-talks-by-2027-but-must-reverse-reform-slowdown/) noted that the Commission has flagged environmental controversies — including a proposed luxury eco-resort on Sazan Island — as a test of Albania's alignment.

**Moldova**, a candidate only since 2022, has opened 25 of its 33 negotiating chapters this year. Kos has called it the best-performing candidate, praising its determination to push through difficult reforms such as the integrity checks on judges, despite Russian pressure and a divided domestic electorate.

**Ukraine** is progressing "under Russian bombs." Kos said the Commission hopes to open all remaining negotiation clusters by the end of 2026, and that a joint working group launched in October will help advance reforms, with an interim benchmark assessment expected in the first half of 2027. On the prospect of membership within five years, she was conditional: "I believe it can be done if we help Ukraine. Although that will also depend on how long the war lasts."

## Serbia's Opening

Serbia offers the most dramatic political story. Once the furthest along after Montenegro, it regressed in fundamental areas under the government of Aleksandar Vučić — on freedom of expression, public administration and academic freedoms. Of 35 chapters, 22 have been opened, but no new cluster has opened since December 2021.

That may now change. In late October, the student-led Student List won a historic parliamentary election with around 53% of the vote, ending more than a decade of Vučić's dominance. The result followed nearly two years of mass protests sparked by the November 2024 collapse of a railway station canopy in Novi Sad, which killed 16 people.

"A new chapter can begin for Serbia. The door to the EU is open," Kos said, according to EU Reporter. "If they are serious and really want to carry out major reforms, then things could go very quickly for Serbia."

Analysts caution, however, that political change in Belgrade may not translate quickly into geopolitical realignment. Bojana Zorić of the EU Institute for Security Studies noted that the student movement had declared its opposition to sanctions against Moscow, and that shifts on Kosovo, Russia sanctions or ties with China appear unlikely in the near term.

## A Geopolitical Push

Brussels is increasingly framing enlargement as a security instrument rather than a purely technical process. The 2026 package, for the first time, includes a separate chapter on security and defence. "Where countries that are making progress are left waiting, space for instability is created. Enlargement is a direct investment in Europe's security," EU foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas said.

Commission President Ursula von der Leyen struck a similar note: "The significant progress achieved by some candidates over the last year allows us to turn the promise of enlargement into reality."

Kos invoked the scale of the moment, comparing it to the 2004 "big bang" that brought in ten new members. "We haven't seen this since... I feel that more and more European leaders realise it is important to bring all those countries on board, if we want to protect ourselves in these geopolitical times."

## What to Watch

The road ahead remains contingent on unanimity and ratification. Key milestones include the conclusion of Montenegro's negotiations in December 2026, Croatia's stance, and the finalization and signing of the treaty in spring and June 2027. Beyond Montenegro, the pace of Albania's reforms, Ukraine's cluster-opening, and whether Serbia's new government converts its democratic opening into durable institutional change will determine whether the EU's enlargement promise becomes reality — or stalls once more.

For now, the bloc appears closer to admitting a new member than at any point since Croatia joined in 2013 — a signal aimed as much at the Western Balkans and at Kyiv as at Brussels' own citizens.
