# China Releases 2026 Internet Development Blue Books at Wuzhen

China officially released the *China Internet Development Report 2026* and the *World Internet Development Report 2026* blue books on November 4 at the 2026 World Internet Conference Wuzhen Summit, offering an authoritative annual survey of internet development trends at home and abroad, according to [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/tech/20261104/70a0e431a5fd4e349d3b409768343d51/c.html).

The volumes were compiled under the lead of the China Academy of Cyberspace Studies (中国网络空间研究院), a research institution attached to China's cyberspace administration apparatus. Their release marks the tenth consecutive year the blue books have been published globally since 2017, cementing their status as a flagship intellectual output of the World Internet Conference and a widely watched reference on the state of the global internet.

## A Decade of Annual Publication

Since the first edition in 2017, the blue books have been released each year at the Wuzhen Summit, held this year from November 2 to 5 in Wuzhen, Zhejiang Province. The reports are pitched as an authoritative window onto internet development trends, spanning infrastructure, the digital economy, and — increasingly — artificial intelligence.

## China's Report: Policy, Plan and AI

The *China Internet Development Report 2026* takes Chinese practice as its starting point, summarizing the country's internet development outcomes. An opening overview chapter surveys the state of China's internet and looks ahead to future trends, while subsequent chapters examine information infrastructure, the digital economy, and online content.

The report's compilers highlight three defining features. First, it is grounded in Xi Jinping's thought on building a "cyber power" (网络强国), reflecting the spirit of the 20th Party Congress and its plenary sessions and showcasing a year of progress on the cyber-power agenda. Second, it presents key measures by regions and departments implementing the national 15th Five-Year Plan, laying out strategic deployment for the next five years and beyond. Third, it captures China's advances in the AI era — from policy-making and industry application to the industrial ecosystem and security governance.

## The World Report: A Global Perspective

The *World Internet Development Report 2026* adopts a global outlook, summarizing internet development worldwide and analyzing conditions in major countries and regions. Beyond its overview chapter, it covers information infrastructure, information technology, and the digital economy.

Its three distinguishing features center on international cooperation, regional comparison, and the AI transition. The report presents the latest progress in building a community with a shared future in cyberspace, as international organizations and major countries pursue cooperation on key cyberspace issues. It summarizes development characteristics across Asia, the Americas, Europe, Africa, and Oceania as a reference for global internet construction. And it highlights how major countries are incorporating AI into national strategies, accelerating research and industrial application, setting governance rules, and vying for leadership in the field.

## A Vision for Shared Development

A responsible official from the China Academy of Cyberspace Studies said the institution would remain devoted to research on major internet issues. The Academy, the official added, is willing to strengthen exchanges and cooperation with research institutions, universities, and think tanks around the world — contributing wisdom and strength to building a community with a shared future in cyberspace and to advancing internet development for the benefit of humanity.

The release underscores how the Wuzhen Summit has become a platform for China to present its vision of internet governance, with the 2026 edition placing artificial intelligence — and the competition to shape its rules — at the center of the global internet agenda.
