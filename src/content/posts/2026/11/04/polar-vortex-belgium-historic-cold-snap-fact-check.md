# Polar vortex: is Belgium facing a historic cold snap?

Alarmist social media posts circulating in early November warned that a "historic" polar vortex would plunge Belgium and Europe into an exceptionally early deep freeze within days. According to an [RTBF fact-check](https://www.rtbf.be/article/un-vortex-polaire-va-nous-plonger-dans-une-vague-de-froid-historique-ces-prochains-jours-on-a-verifie-l-info-11808356) published on 4 November, the reality is more nuanced: the polar vortex is indeed real and its current disturbance is unusually early, but the claim of an imminent historic cold snap over Belgium is false.

## What is true: the vortex exists and is disturbed early

The polar vortex is a normal seasonal feature — a large mass of cold air in the stratosphere over the North Pole. "The polar vortex has a seasonality," climatologist Xavier Fettweis of the University of Liège told RTBF. "It is born in October and dies next spring. At the very start of its life it is still very weak and begins to mature to a peak in December, January, February."

Fabian Debal, principal meteorologist at the Royal Meteorological Institute (IRM), confirmed that forecasts do project a significant disturbance of the vortex in the coming days — and that its timing is exceptional. "The forecasts indeed envisage a significant disturbance of this polar vortex, with fairly high reliability... It is indeed very early, since the earliest event was around 28 November 1968 exactly. Here we are in early November, so it is very early, that is true."

## What is false: an imminent Belgian cold wave

Where the viral posts go wrong is in the leap from a stratospheric disturbance to a certain, immediate cold catastrophe on the ground. "It is not certain," Debal said. "The low-altitude disturbance could very well occur in other regions of the world, like North America or other parts of the Northern Hemisphere... And if there is a lower-altitude impact, it could then occur more toward the beginning of December, for example, so several weeks after the start of the disturbance in the stratosphere."

The cooling actually expected in Belgium over the coming days is simply seasonal, and unrelated to the vortex. "On social media, everyone associates this cold with a polar vortex that would be disintegrating and bringing very cold air here, which is not at all the case," Fettweis said. "It is just seasonal weather. In November we have forgotten it can freeze, but it is completely normal."

## The science behind the hype

Belgian meteorological outlets had already flagged the unusually early vortex weakening in late October. According to the [Belgische Meteo Club Belge](https://www.bmcb.be/l/early-split-polar-vortex/), ECMWF projections suggested that westerly winds at around 30 kilometres altitude could slow from roughly 22–23 m/s to about 12 m/s — nearly half their usual seasonal speed. But the club explicitly cautioned that "between a stratospheric disturbance and a genuine winter offensive over Belgium, the path remains long and strewn with uncertainties."

That caution is echoed by the seasonal outlook from [Météo Belgique](https://www.meteobelgique.be/previsions/belgique/tendances-saisonnieres/2678-tendances-pour-lhiver-2026-2027), which foresees a first part of winter that is mild and often disturbed, with Atlantic weather systems returning in the second decade of November in a relatively mild atmosphere.

## Clickbait versus climate reality

Both specialists pointed to the commercial logic behind the alarmist posts. "We see from time to time this kind of more sensational announcement or the winters of the century that are sometimes announced," Debal said. "But we, as meteorologists, refer to what science produces... these methods often bring much more nuanced conclusions."

Fettweis put the claims in a broader climate context. "On social media, every winter we exaggerate, we predict the winter of the century and in fact nothing happens," he said. "Global warming dominates the signal and our winters are increasingly mild... This winter will likely be very warm and very mild and very wet."

## What to watch

The scientific takeaway is not that the polar vortex is irrelevant, but that its effects are slow and uncertain. Any surface impact from the current stratospheric disturbance would more likely arrive in early December — if it reaches Western Europe at all — rather than this week. For now, Belgium's weather is behaving seasonally, and the experts' advice is simple: trust the science-based forecasts and be wary of messages that are more alarming than the meteorology warrants.
