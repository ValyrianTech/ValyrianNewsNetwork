# Mypension.be Restores Pension Date and Supplementary Pension Services

Belgium's federal pension portal, Mypension.be, has come back online after several hours of disruption, restoring two services that citizens rely on to plan their retirement: the ability to view one's earliest possible pension date and, for those building up a supplementary pension, the future net amount of that second-pillar pension. The restoration marks a return to normal after [heavy traffic overwhelmed the site](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/04/vroegste-pensioendatum-en-bedrag-aanvullend-pensioen-bekend-via/) on the day the features launched.

## What's Now Available

From 4 November 2026, users can consult their earliest pension date directly on the portal. For anyone accruing a supplementary (second-pillar) pension, the site now displays the future net amount of that pension expected at the statutory pension age — aggregated across all pension plans. Previously, the portal only showed the supplementary pension already accrued up to that point; it now offers a total picture, expressed in net figures.

One major piece of the puzzle remains pending. The net amount of the statutory pension will only become visible in the second half of 2027. The exception applies to anyone who can already retire in 2026, or whose earliest pension date falls within 2026: for them, the statutory pension net amount is already visible, because the federal pension reform has no impact on those leaving the workforce this year.

## Behind the Surge in Interest

The renewed functionality arrives amid intense public attention driven by Belgium's federal pension reform, which reshapes the rules governing when citizens can retire and how much they will receive. According to the [Federal Pension Service (FPD)](https://www.sfpd.fgov.be/nl/veranderingen/pensioenhervorming), the reform fundamentally changes how pension dates and amounts are calculated, which requires the calculation engines behind Mypension.be to be completely rebuilt — meaning some data is temporarily unavailable while estimates are restored.

The overload was a direct symptom of that interest. "Quite a few Belgians had already surfed to mypension.be, causing the website to go down after a few hours," VRT NWS reported, before the site came back online.

## What the Reform Means for Retirees

The reform, spanning 2025 to 2029, introduces a bonus/malus system that rewards those who work longer and penalises some who retire earlier than the statutory age without meeting career conditions. Yet the Federal Pension Service has clarified that those who can retire in 2026 but choose to postpone are not affected: the reform has no impact on their pension date, and they incur no malus regardless. Civil servants' pension amounts also continue to be calculated on the basis of their current reference period.

Because of the added complexity, the Federal Pension Service reports longer-than-normal waiting times and is asking the public for patience. Priority is being given to people retiring in 2026 or 2027.

## What's Next

Citizens seeking clarity are being directed to three official sources: the FPD's pension reform page, the [RSVZ page on the 2025–2029 pension reforms](https://www.rsvz.be/nl/faq/pensioenhervormingen-2025-2029), and the [FPD's Mypension.be information page](https://www.sfpd.fgov.be/nl/mypension-be).

The key date to watch is the second half of 2027, when the net statutory pension amount is expected to become visible for the general public. Until then, Mypension.be offers citizens a valuable but still partial picture of their retirement finances — enough to plan the timing of retirement, but not yet the full sum they will live on.
