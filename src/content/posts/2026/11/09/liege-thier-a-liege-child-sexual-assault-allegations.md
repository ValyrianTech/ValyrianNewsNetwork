# Liege Parents Allege Child-on-Child Sexual Assault at School

Parents at the Thier-a-Liege primary school in the Belgian city of Liege have come forward to denounce sexual assaults committed by children against other children, and accuse the school leadership and local authorities of turning a blind eye, according to an [RTBF investigation](https://www.rtbf.be/article/a-l-ecole-du-thier-a-liege-des-parents-denoncent-des-agressions-sexuelles-entre-enfants-et-accusent-des-adultes-d-avoir-ferme-les-yeux-11811391). The allegations, which span several years, raise serious questions about safeguarding and how the school handled reports of abuse among pupils.

The case came to light after the school took part in a booklet on preventing school bullying, produced in part for a project publicised through RTBF's children's news programme Les Niouzz. Following that presentation, the broadcaster received around ten emails from parents describing bullying, physical aggression and sexual aggression at the school. Three families agreed to testify anonymously, with all first names changed.

## What the families describe

One mother, identified as Sybille, said her 11-year-old son had been bullied for years before an alleged sexual assault in the summer of 2024. She described an incident in which another child, a long-time harasser, approached her son from behind as he bent to tie his shoelace and inserted pointed fingers into his anus.

"My son was extremely shocked, it hurt him, he felt violated," Sybille said. When the boy reported the incident to his teacher, the teacher allegedly told the aggressor only: "Haven't you done enough naughty things like that already?"

The school's director later sent a collective email to all parents of fifth-year primary pupils acknowledging a "game" called "1000 ans de douleurs" ("1000 years of pain"), inspired by a scene from the manga Naruto, which involves inserting two fingers into another pupil's anus by surprise. The email described it as "a physical, psychological aggression and an attack on intimacy" that "must be forbidden," and noted it appeared to have been going on since at least the previous year.

"I am struck to learn that the leadership was aware and that this had been happening for at least a year," Sybille said. "And then it's entirely contradictory: if it's a physical, psychological aggression and an attack on intimacy, then it's not a game!"

A second father, Gabriel, said his daughter Darlene was touched in the school's mixed toilets, where "the doors no longer close." He said the director asked the girl's aggressor to give her a drawing to apologise, and that Darlene had to say "thank you" - after which, for the director, the incident was closed.

A third family said their son reported repeated touching of intimate parts by the same boy over several years, in the toilets, the playground and sometimes in class under the table. According to their lawyer, Me Aurelie Kettels, the children tried to be heard by writing in a suggestions box, but were then reprimanded by the director and accused of harming the child who had assaulted them.

## Complaints and a wall of silence

One family filed a police complaint after their son's assault. The third family filed a criminal complaint for "non-assistance a personne en danger" - failure to assist a person in danger - targeting the school leadership, the City of Liege and the PMS centre (a psycho-medico-social support service). They ultimately withdrew their child from the school. "It's the victim who had to leave," Kettels said, adding that the boy has developed significant difficulties and that his trust in the school's adults has been destroyed.

The lawyer said the City of Liege's response letter minimised the facts, describing them as "discoveries and games" rather than sexual assault.

The City of Liege and the former school director, who was in post at the time of the facts, declined to respond to RTBF. The former director left her position at the start of the 2026-27 school year and now works in the City of Liege's public education cabinet. Alderman for public education Julie Fernandez Fernandez also declined to answer the broadcaster's questions.

## A wider, under-estimated phenomenon

The case points to a broader problem that extends well beyond one school. Liege psychologist Suzanne Aubinet, who specialises in violence between children, said there is little doubt about how such acts should be classified. "From the moment one is constrained, blocked or held, and surprise is used to insert anything into genital parts, there is little doubt that it is sexual violence," she told RTBF.

She warned that the phenomenon remains taboo and poorly understood. "We don't want to see this," she said. "It's unbearable, so we don't look." She also pointed to a language gap that silences victims, noting that adults rely on "watered-down words," and to a training deficit: courses on sexual violence between children "don't exist" in the curricula of professionals who work with children.

No official statistics exist for Belgium, but in France it is estimated that 30% of sexual violence against children is committed by other children, according to [FFCriavs](https://www.ffcriavs.org/boite_outils/documents/310/).

## What's next

Liege municipal party Vert Ardent issued a statement saying the testimonies "worry us deeply" and demanding that full light be shed on the situation and that the City be fully transparent about what it knew and what measures it has taken. The party recalled its 2025 motion, passed unanimously by the communal council, to train people in contact with children to detect violence and strengthen prevention. "To the families, we believe you," it said.

Several questions remain unanswered: whether the Liege prosecutor's office has opened an investigation into the complaints, how many children were involved as victims and as alleged aggressors, and whether the city will translate its stated commitments into concrete training and prevention measures. For the families who spoke out, the central concern is whether the institutions that were meant to protect their children will finally be held to account.
