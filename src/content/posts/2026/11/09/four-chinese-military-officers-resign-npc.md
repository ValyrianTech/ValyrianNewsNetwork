# Four Chinese Military Officers Resign From NPC

Four Chinese military officers have resigned from the National People's Congress (NPC), the first military deputies to step down since 2023 and a move that brings the total number of military deputies removed from or resigned from the legislature over that period to 50, [Caixin reported](https://www.caixinglobal.com/2026-11-09/four-chinese-military-officers-resign-from-national-legislature-102502702.html). The resignations were confirmed in an official announcement as the Standing Committee of the 14th NPC concluded its 25th session in Beijing on Nov. 6.

The four officers — Zhang Gong, Guo Jianjun, Wu Yuhong and Meng Qingbin — hold the rank of major general or its naval equivalent, rear admiral. Before these resignations, 46 military deputies had already been removed from the legislature since 2023.

## What Was Announced

According to NPC Standing Committee Announcement No. 20, published by the official Xinhua News Agency, the election committee of the Central Military Commission's National Defense Mobilization Department accepted the resignations of Zhang and Guo; the navy's election committee accepted Wu's resignation; and the People's Armed Police (PAP) election committee accepted Meng's. Their qualifications as NPC deputies were terminated under the relevant regulations.

The 50 total departures involve deputies from both the People's Liberation Army (PLA) and the PAP. By rank or naval equivalent, they comprise 22 generals, 18 lieutenant generals and 10 major generals — meaning the overwhelming majority are flag officers from the senior ranks of the armed forces.

## Who the Officers Are

Zhang Gong is the most prominent of the four. A career naval officer, he served as chief of staff of the North Sea Fleet's naval aviation force and deputy chief of staff of the East Sea Fleet before being promoted to rear admiral in June 2019. He became commander of the Jiangxi provincial military command in 2022, first appeared publicly as commander of the Guangdong provincial military command in July 2023, and joined the provincial party standing committee that September. As recently as March 2025 he called for Guangdong to harness its electronics, AI and new-energy industry clusters to strengthen military capabilities.

Guo Jianjun, born in December 1965 in Lushan county, Henan province, held a series of political and disciplinary posts, including director of the political department of the Qinghai provincial military command, political commissar of the PLA Rocket Force University of Engineering and political commissar of the Ningxia military command, where he joined the autonomous region's party standing committee in June 2022. In January 2026 the Ningxia legislature announced he had been transferred out of the region.

Meng Qingbin served as political commissar of the PAP's Guizhou and Sichuan contingents before becoming political commissar of the Gansu provincial military command by August 2024. Wu Yuhong, a native of Hunan province promoted to rear admiral in December 2018, led the shore-to-ship missile formation during the military parade marking the 70th anniversary of the People's Republic of China in October 2019.

## A Softer Route Out

A notable detail is that all four **resigned** rather than being formally **recalled** — a legally distinct and administratively softer route. The distinction is significant: it suggests handling via internal discipline rather than formal criminal removal, preserving face and legality while allowing Beijing to quietly remove figures under scrutiny.

Analysts have compared the cases to that of former foreign minister Qin Gang, who resigned his NPC seat in 2024 after being removed from office without a public criminal charge. No official reason has been given for the four departures.

## The Broader Purge

These resignations are not isolated. They are the latest visible manifestation of an unprecedented, years-long purge of the senior ranks of the PLA and PAP, driven by Xi Jinping's anti-corruption campaign and his drive to ensure absolute political loyalty within the armed forces.

According to a February 2026 report by the Center for Strategic and International Studies, more than 100 senior PLA officers from virtually all areas of the armed forces have been removed or gone missing since 2022. On Jan. 24, 2026, the Ministry of National Defense announced that top general Zhang Youxia and Joint Staff chief Liu Zhenli were under investigation — leaving only one of the six generals named to the Central Military Commission in 2022 in place. In May 2026, former defense ministers Wei Fenghe and Li Shangfu were sentenced to death with two-year reprieves on corruption charges.

The numbers tell the story: PLA and PAP deputies have fallen from 281 at the start of the term in 2023 to 229 — a reduction of 52 (50 with terminated qualifications, plus two who transferred to local delegations).

## Timing and Signals

The resignations came just days after the October 2026 Party plenum, at which the Communist Party [filled 10 Central Committee vacancies](https://www.caixinglobal.com/2026-10-30/chinas-communist-party-fills-10-central-committee-vacancies-102499045.html) and [elevated a military leader to the Politburo](https://www.caixinglobal.com/2026-10-30/china-elevates-military-leader-to-politburo-ratifies-party-expulsions--102498954.html). Read together, the moves suggest the ongoing consolidation and rebuilding of the military leadership as Xi reconstitutes the high command ahead of the next Party Congress.

The provincial-command profile of three of the four officers is also notable. Zhang, Guo and Meng all held provincial military command posts, several of them concurrently serving on provincial Party standing committees — reflecting the intersection of military and civilian political power that has long been a concern in Beijing.

## What to Watch

Key open questions remain: whether any official charges or disciplinary actions will be brought against the four; whether their concurrent provincial party roles were also terminated; and how the pace of military resignations in 2026 compares with earlier waves of the purge.

What is already clear is that the attrition of military NPC deputies has become a primary mechanism for removing purged officers — quietly, legally and without the glare of public trial. For Xi's campaign to remake the PLA in his image, the resignations are less an ending than another step in a continuing process whose full scope remains hidden from public view.
