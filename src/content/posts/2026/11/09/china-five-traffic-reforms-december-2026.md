# China Expands Online Car Registration to Imported Vehicles

China's Ministry of Public Security will roll out five new traffic-management reform measures nationwide from December 1, 2026, extending a digital registration system that already covers domestically produced cars to imported passenger vehicles and adding tools aimed at businesses. The measures, announced by the ministry's Traffic Management Bureau and reported by [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/politics/20261109/2001b1a2890141cdb08ae387c3177931/c.html), continue a years-long push to move vehicle procedures from service windows onto the phone.

The centerpiece is a service allowing buyers of imported passenger cars to complete license-plate selection and new-vehicle registration entirely online through the Jiaoguan 12123 app. According to Xinhua, the change builds on an existing one-stop service for domestic passenger vehicles and relies on newly shared pre-inspection data from market regulators, joining earlier data-sharing arrangements with tax and financial-regulation departments.

"After purchasing an imported passenger vehicle, the public can complete all plate-selection and registration business online in one stop through the Jiaoguan 12123 app, no longer needing to handle business at service windows," the ministry said in the announcement, describing the goal as "achieving registration and plate issuance without leaving home."

## Context: A Reform With a Long Runway

The measures extend a template China has been testing incrementally. On November 1, 2025, the ministry launched new-car registration "as one thing" for domestic passenger vehicles alongside six related one-network services, according to a [Jinan Bendibao](https://m.jn.bendibao.com/live/83067.shtm) summary of the October 14, 2025 announcement. That rollout let buyers complete vehicle selection, insurance and plate registration through the app after automakers performed pre-delivery inspections at the factory — which is why imported cars, built abroad, were excluded at the time.

The December extension closes part of that gap by sharing pre-inspection data from market regulators. [Haibao News](https://hb.dzwww.com/p/p6iT5yHux6.html) noted that the 2025 rollout was framed as a shift from "running to many departments to efficiently accomplishing one thing," a State Council initiative to consolidate multi-agency, multi-window procedures into single online transactions.

The scale of the underlying system is considerable. According to figures cited by Xinhua, China had 480 million motor vehicles and 570 million licensed drivers as of the end of October 2026. The Jiaoguan 12123 app has more than 610 million users, logging roughly 27 million daily visits and 4.47 million daily transactions. The ministry says it has introduced more than 170 reform measures over recent years, saving citizens and enterprises over 100 billion yuan and eliminating more than 4 billion paper documents, with cross-province services used more than 200 million times.

## Four Measures Aimed at Enterprises

While the imported-vehicle change affects individual buyers directly, four of the new measures target businesses and the auto industry:

- **No paper business license required.** Enterprises can present electronic business licenses when conducting vehicle business such as new-car registration and used-car transfers, after the system connects to market regulators' electronic license database.
- **Corporate online accounts.** Companies holding electronic business licenses can register as corporate users on the app to query vehicle status, violations, accidents and business reminders for the firm and its branches.
- **Online temporary plates for test vehicles.** Automakers can apply online for temporary plates for test vehicles through the internet traffic-safety integrated service platform, avoiding trips to the vehicle-management office.
- **Pilot "ask-as-you-do" AI assistant.** In municipalities directly under the central government, provincial capitals, autonomous-region capitals and cities with independent planning status, the ministry will pilot an intelligent customer-service assistant called Jiaoguan Xiao'an, enabling users to complete transactions while chatting.

## Analysis: AI Meets Administrative Reform

The AI pilot is the most notable signal in the package. Rather than a standalone chatbot, Jiaoguan Xiao'an is designed around an "ask-as-you-do" model — an attempt to let users complete procedures conversationally rather than navigating menus, reflecting a broader push to embed AI assistants into government service platforms. Whether the approach reduces friction or simply shifts it into dialogue will depend on how well it handles the edge cases that drive people to service windows in the first place.

The enterprise measures, meanwhile, are quietly consequential for the automotive supply chain. Online test-vehicle plates reduce turnaround friction for automakers, while corporate accounts and paperless business licenses simplify compliance for dealerships, fleet operators and insurers — the 30,000-plus social agencies the ministry says already handle traffic business.

The larger backdrop is data integration. Each measure depends on linking systems across tax, financial-regulation and market-regulation departments. That cross-agency plumbing is what makes "one thing" services possible; it is also where data-privacy and interoperability questions will concentrate as the model expands.

## What's Next

The ministry said it will formulate supporting regulations and organize information-system upgrades to ensure the measures take effect on schedule. Key unknowns remain: the specific pre-inspection data market regulators will share for imported vehicles and how it will be verified; which cities form the first AI-pilot cohort; and whether further reforms — such as extending the "one thing" model to other vehicle categories or cross-border scenarios — are planned.

For China's hundreds of millions of drivers and the businesses that serve them, the direction is clear: fewer windows, more app. The question is how smoothly the newest layer lands when it goes live December 1.
