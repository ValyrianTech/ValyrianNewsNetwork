# Taiyuan High-Rise Fire Kills 14 in Liuxiang District

A fire that swept through a high-rise apartment building on Liuxiang South Road in downtown Taiyuan, Shanxi province, has killed 14 people, according to [Xinhua News Agency](https://www.news.cn/politics/20261109/1a5c042176c14cf587fecbe2d63b47cd/c.html). The blaze broke out at the Haosheng International Apartments in the Liuxiang Subdistrict of Yingze District at 16:59 on November 8, Xinhua reported, and ranks among the deadliest building fires the city has seen in years.

## What Happened

CCTV's field reporting, republished by [Phoenix News](https://news.ifeng.com/c/8xuMdNbsibb) under a Xinhua byline, said the fire broke out on the afternoon of November 8 at a high-rise on Liuxiang South Road. By the time reporters reached the scene, the open flame had been largely extinguished and firefighters were carrying out search-and-rescue operations. Some of those injured were taken to hospital for treatment. The cause of the fire remains under investigation, authorities said.

The casualty toll was confirmed in an official statement on the morning of November 9, relayed by Shanxi provincial departments to Xinhua. The agency has not released the identities of the victims, nor the number and condition of the injured.

The building sits on Liuxiang South Road in the heart of a historic and heavily trafficked commercial district in Taiyuan's central urban core, an area that draws dense foot traffic and houses a mix of commercial and residential occupancy.

## Renewed Focus on High-Rise Safety

The disaster lands on sensitive timing: November 9 is China's 35th National Fire Prevention Day, when localities typically stage firefighting drills and public-safety campaigns. The fire struck the evening before the annual observance.

It also echoes an earlier tragedy in the same city. On March 28, 2026, a high-rise commercial building near Qinxian North Street in Taiyuan's Xiaodian District caught fire, killing three people and injuring 23, nine of them seriously, as [Jiemian News](https://m.jiemian.com/article/14179038.html) reported. That building housed ground-floor barbecue restaurants, eateries and snack shops, with karaoke bars and internet cafes on the upper floors. Witness video showed flames erupting above a ground-floor barbecue shop and climbing rapidly up the exterior wall — an illustration of the so-called "chimney effect" that makes high-rise fires so dangerous.

Just weeks before that March blaze, the Xiaodian District government had published a fire-safety registration notice listing shopping malls, hotels, public entertainment venues, and high-rise public buildings — office buildings and apartment buildings included — as key fire-safety units, Jiemian reported. High-rise structures, with their complex layouts, can allow fire to spread rapidly and create grave danger and heavy casualties once a blaze takes hold.

## Analysis: A Pattern Under Scrutiny

The Haosheng apartments fit precisely the category of building that regulators have sought to track more closely: a tall, mixed-use structure in a crowded central district. That the fire occurred so soon after another deadly high-rise blaze in the same city will intensify questions about whether safety inspections and rectification drives have translated into real protection.

Following several high-rise fires nationally, localities have launched special inspections targeting buildings under exterior-wall renovation, internal renovation, fire-facilities equipment and day-to-day fire-safety management. Industry associations have urged stronger controls against the chimney effect. Yet the recurrence of fatal fires suggests the gap between regulation on paper and enforcement in practice remains wide.

## What to Watch

Several key questions remain unanswered. The number and condition of the injured have not been released, nor has the exact cause of the fire. Investigators have not said whether the building had fire-safety violations or what its precise occupancy mix was. It is also unclear whether official accountability measures, arrests or rectification orders will follow.

For now, the confirmed toll stands at 14 dead — a figure that may rise as search-and-rescue and hospital updates emerge. As a fast-moving disaster story, details are subject to revision as provincial authorities release further statements.
