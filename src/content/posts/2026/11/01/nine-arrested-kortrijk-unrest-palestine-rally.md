# Nine Arrested as Kortrijk Unrest Erupts at Palestine Rally

Nine young people were administratively arrested and 41 others identified and fined after hours of clashes in central Kortrijk on Saturday evening, as a recurring pro-Palestine wake and an organised counter-presence collided in the streets of the West Flanders city. Three police officers were injured in the scuffles, which began around 19:00 and spread from the Veemarkt to the nightlife district before calm returned roughly two and a half hours later.

The police zone VLAS, which covers Kortrijk, said the arrests included both minors and adults drawn from both opposing groups. All nine face local exclusion orders and potential prosecution by the West Flanders prosecutor's office, according to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/10/31/kortrijk-onrust-pro-palestina-protes-en-tegenprotest/).

## How the evening unfolded

According to police spokesperson Thomas Detavernier, the trouble traces back to a pamphlet circulated on social media earlier in the week calling for a pro-Palestine protest. That appeal, he said, drew little response on its own. "Actually very little attention was paid to it, few young people turned up for it," Detavernier told VRT NWS.

What changed the situation was a counter-mobilisation. "Among others, hard cores of various football clubs and nationalist youths responded. They moved through the shopping streets in a group and chanted slogans," he said. "As a result the pro-Palestine protest gained more supporters and there were repeated scuffles."

Regional broadcaster [Focus-WTV](https://focus-wtv.be/nieuws/acht-jongeren-opgepakt-na-onrust-en-vechtpartij-in-centrum-van-kortrijk) reported that a nationalist-oriented group travelled to the city after calls, circulated through various channels, to, in their own words, "protect" Kortrijk, joined by youths with links to several football clubs. Police had been on heightened alert that day amid separate online calls to protest "against education" in several West Flanders cities, though no effective demonstration on that theme materialised in Kortrijk.

Around 19:00 the counter-group reached the Veemarkt, where the weekly pro-Palestine wake was taking place — this week also attended by youths with a migration background. The two groups began to provoke each other, and confrontations broke out at multiple locations in the city centre, including the Lekkerbeetstraat.

Detavernier described a sustained game of cat and mouse. "The cat-and-mouse game lasted the whole evening," he said. Police called in reinforcements from other West Flanders zones and the federal police to keep the groups apart and prevent further escalation.

## Gathering ban and a firm response

At around 20:30, Mayor Ruth Vandenberghe (TBSK) imposed a prohibition on public gatherings within the ring around the city centre. "It is absurd that so many police resources have to be deployed," she said. By about 21:30, order had been restored.

The mayor made clear that those who seek confrontation will face consequences. "Whoever comes to Kortrijk deliberately to seek confrontation or disrupt public order must know there are consequences," she said. "Our police services were prepared and acted firmly. We continue to work for a safe city where everyone can move freely and without fear."

## Two-track enforcement

The case illustrates Belgium's parallel administrative and judicial enforcement tracks. The nine arrests are administrative in nature — a rapid public-order tool that removes individuals from the scene and is typically paired with a local exclusion order. Separately, judicial reports (processen-verbaal) have been drawn up for the prosecutor's office, which will decide on any charges.

The 41 others identified by police each face a municipal administrative sanction (GAS) fine of up to EUR 500. On Sunday morning, a security consultation brought together the mayor, police zone VLAS and the West Flanders prosecutor's office to coordinate the follow-up.

## What to watch

The final tally of nine arrests consolidates the picture: early local reporting on Saturday night cited eight at around 20:30, reflecting an evolving count rather than a contradiction. Several questions remain open. The identities and affiliations of those arrested and fined have not been published, and the prosecutor's office has yet to set out any charges.

The bigger question is structural. The Veemarkt wake is a weekly fixture, not a one-off, and Saturday's unrest followed a familiar pattern seen in Belgian and wider European cities — local activism meeting an organised counter-presence, with football "hard cores" acting as a mobilising network. Whether the city and police adjust their approach to recurring gatherings, and how the prosecutor's office handles the dossiers in the coming weeks, will determine whether this weekend's flashpoint remains an isolated episode or becomes part of a longer pattern.
