# ACA Open Enrollment Opens With New Rules and Cost Pressures

Open enrollment for 2027 Affordable Care Act health insurance plans opened November 1 in most states and runs through January 15, 2027, but consumers selecting coverage this season face a markedly different marketplace than in years past. According to [NPR and KFF Health News](https://www.npr.org/2026/11/01/nx-s1-6023423/aca-open-enrollment-health-insurance), shoppers can expect continued premium increases, possible additional identity checks, potential delays in working with a broker, and new limits on which immigrants can receive federal subsidies.

A handful of state-run exchanges, including those in Idaho, Connecticut, and Massachusetts, started their enrollment windows earlier, and some states have allowed "window shopping" so consumers can preview premium rates before committing.

## A Shrinking Marketplace

A continued decline in overall enrollment is expected, driven primarily by the expiration of enhanced subsidies at the end of 2025. Policy experts say the end of those subsidies led millions of people to drop or lose coverage through the first months of 2026. A [KFF analysis](https://www.kff.org/affordable-care-act/how-has-aca-marketplace-enrollment-changed-across-states-in-2026/) found that February effectuated enrollment fell to 19.2 million people in 2026 from 21.8 million a year earlier, a 12% year-over-year decline and the first drop in seven years.

The Trump administration credits its fraud-reduction efforts for the falling numbers, pointing to the August cancellation of policies covering 760,000 enrollees suspected of improper sign-up. A small number of those enrollees have since regained coverage.

## Identity Checks and Fewer Brokers

One of the biggest changes for consumers involves increased scrutiny of both enrollees and the agents who help them. New rules outlined by the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services apply only to consumers using agents or brokers to enroll through the federal marketplace, which serves 29 states.

All such consumers will need a one-time confirmation code or link to authorize a specific agent to enroll them or make changes on their behalf. So-called "high-risk" consumers — first-time applicants, those changing their state of coverage, those working with a new agent, or those with a monthly premium of $30 or less — must verify their own identity before authorizing an agent.

Joshua Brooker, who oversees an insurance agency in Pennsylvania, warned that "up to 1 in 5 consumers is going to have to do extra identity proofing" and may simply give up. Georgetown University researcher Stacey Pogue noted that "some people may have trouble not because they're not real, but because they don't have a credit file, or have moved, or have changed names."

Separately, CMS in September issued a rule placing a moratorium on brokers new to offering ACA coverage. Agents not registered with the federal marketplace in 2026 will not be able to assist with 2027 enrollments until February at the earliest. The National Association of Benefits and Insurance Professionals sued the administration on October 15 over the moratorium, saying it unfairly targets some 19,000 agents, and Health Agents for America filed a separate suit seeking a restraining order.

## Costs and Subsidies

This year, after Congress did not renew the expiring enhanced subsidies, most people buying ACA coverage saw their costs rise, and higher earners lost subsidies entirely. Premiums are slated for double-digit increases for 2027 — at 15%, slightly lower than last year's average increase of 26%.

Subsidies remain income-based and cut off for households earning more than four times the federal poverty rate — $63,840 for a single person, $86,560 for a couple, and $109,280 for a family of three. Out-of-pocket maximums will rise about 13% in 2027, to $12,000 for individuals and $24,000 for families, with lower caps for households up to 2.5 times the poverty level.

"For most folks, overall affordability will stay about the same," said Louise Norris, a health policy analyst for HealthInsurance.org. "That's not to say it's great, but if you were able to manage the premiums this year, it should be similar to that." Still, Anne Petry, who owns an Illinois insurance agency, warned, "The biggest issue we will face is affordability. There definitely will come a breaking point for people."

The One Big Beautiful Bill Act, passed in 2025, also strips subsidy eligibility from many lawfully present immigrants, including H-1B work visa holders, refugees, asylees, people with temporary protected status, and those with pending green-card applications. Green-card holders, certain Cuban and Haitian immigrants, and citizens of Micronesia, the Marshall Islands, or Palau can still receive subsidies.

## What to Watch

Agents and experts urge consumers not to let plans auto-renew and instead compare premiums, deductibles, and other costs, noting that several insurers — including Cigna — have dropped all or part of their ACA coverage areas. Because broker slots may fill faster this year, Pogue advised reaching out early.

"If you just sit on the sidelines and let the policy auto-renew, you might miss out on what could be a better opportunity," Norris said. With open enrollment running into mid-January, the coming weeks will show whether the crackdown and cost pressures further reshape a marketplace already shrinking from its 2025 peak.
