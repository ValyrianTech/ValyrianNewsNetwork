# Brussels CPAS Decry Van Bossuyt Circular Consultation Gap

Brussels public social welfare centres (CPAS) have accused federal Social Integration Minister Anneleen Van Bossuyt (N-VA) of issuing a major policy circular on the activation of social assistance without informing or consulting the frontline services that must apply it. The new rules, published on 30 October 2026, tighten how CPAS assess whether recipients of the integration income (revenu d'intégration, or leefloon) are genuinely "disposed to work" — but the federations representing those centres say they learned of the text through the press.

## A Circular Nobody Saw Coming

Sébastien Lepoivre, president of the Brussels CPAS federation, said he discovered the circular "avec étonnement" — with astonishment — and stressed that the federations were neither informed nor consulted on the planned provisions, according to [RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/les-cpas-bruxellois-denoncent-une-absence-de-concertation-pour-la-circulaire-van-bossuyt-11808545).

The grievance echoed immediately in Wallonia. "This circular is a surprise for us," said Alain Vaessen, president of the Walloon federation of CPAS within the Union of Towns and Municipalities. "We regret not having been consulted, especially since this is a circular that has a great impact on the front line of social work," he told Belga, as [La Libre](https://www.lalibre.be/dernieres-depeches/2026/10/30/les-cpas-wallons-surpris-par-la-circulaire-van-bossuyt-et-son-absence-de-concertation-TLQMK7QR6ZELDCOIYXA22HRUKI/) reported.

## What the Circular Actually Does

According to the official [press release](https://www.mi-is.be/fr/nouvelles/nouvelle-circulaire-relative-la-disposition-travailler-des-beneficiaires-du-revenu) from the Social Integration ministry, the circular dated 29 October provides CPAS with "concrete guidelines to assess and monitor the disposition to work in a uniform and coherent manner." It replaces point 1.5 of the general circular of 18 March 2024 and is intended to strengthen the activation of integration-income recipients and increase their chances of durable employment.

As detailed by [Le Soir](https://www.lesoir.be/779888/article/2026-10-30/anneleen-van-bossuyt-adresse-une-circulaire-aux-cpas-nous-avons-ete-trop), the circular sets out several concrete criteria. Beneficiaries may lose or be denied aid if they refuse, without valid reason, an available accommodation solution, an adapted and feasible schedule, a training course or a support program. Language learning becomes an explicit criterion: recipients must demonstrate steps to improve their linguistic skills. Health problems no longer automatically exempt a person — CPAS are told to focus on what remains possible, such as part-time or adapted work. Students receiving the income must remain disposed to work and may be expected to be available on weekends and holidays.

Cultural or religious reasons cannot constitute an exception, the minister specified — citing as an example the idea that women should not work.

"We have been too lax for too long," Van Bossuyt declared. "Our social protection is there for those who need it. But rights come with duties. We cannot accept that people hide behind excuses to avoid going to work."

## Timing Fuels the Dispute

The federations' objection is as much procedural as substantive. Wallonia's Vaessen noted that the circular arrives while the government is still reforming the law on the individualised social integration plan (PIIS), which contains provisions on the work obligations of beneficiaries. "We therefore have a circular that in some way precedes a law whose revision is not yet complete," he said.

The Walloon federation has launched a first impact study, to be discussed at its steering committee on 4 November. The Flemish CPAS federation also reacted negatively, calling the circular devoid of "any added value" and rejecting the minister's premise of laxism, according to La Libre.

## Stigmatisation and the Front Line

Lepoivre went further than procedure, accusing the minister of stigmatising populations already "highly exposed to discrimination," as well as the sworn social workers who accompany them. A modern insertion policy, he argued, should combat structural hiring discrimination based on origin, presumed nationality, religion, gender or age "with as much vigour as it encourages access to employment — rather than waiting on forced efforts from only the most precarious people, and even more so if they are of foreign origin."

He stressed that CPAS already carry out strict monitoring and follow-up work, and that the new recommendations demand a "rigorously balanced" implementation.

> "Les CPAS continuent à partager sans ambiguïté l'objectif d'accompagner intensivement les bénéficiaires vers la formation et l'emploi, lorsque cela est possible. Mais on peut néanmoins rester inquiet de certaines orientations."

## Analysis: The Activation Agenda Meets the Front Line

The circular is a concrete expression of the "Arizona" coalition's drive to activate social benefits — a politically potent theme as Prime Minister Bart De Wever pursues federal budget consolidation. By framing the measure as a correction of past laxism, the minister is staking out ground that plays well politically. But the pushback reveals a structural tension in Belgian social policy: a federal minister can tighten eligibility rules through an administrative circular, yet it is local CPAS staff — and the vulnerable people they serve — who bear the consequences, all while facing discrimination in a labour market that the circular does little to address.

The CPAS retain case-by-case assessment and the final decision, leaving room for interpretation. Critics argue that discretion is exactly what the circular seeks to narrow — and that doing so before the PIIS reform is finalised risks locking in rules the parliamentary process might have softened.

## What to Watch

The Walloon federation's 4 November impact study will offer the first structured assessment of the circular's practical consequences. Whether the federations translate their criticism into a legal challenge or selective non-application remains open. And the interaction with the pending PIIS reform will determine whether the circular is eventually superseded, amended, or entrenched. For now, the minister has her criteria in place — and the services expected to apply them are still asking why they were not at the table.
