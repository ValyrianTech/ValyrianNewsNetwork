# Anderlecht Roaster Crowned World Coffee Champion

A Brussels-based coffee roaster has been crowned world champion, bringing a global title back to the Belgian capital. Benjamin Brassart, who co-founded the Boo Coffee Roasters workshop in Anderlecht, was named 2026 World Coffee Roasting Champion on 27 June 2026 at the World of Coffee Brussels event held at the Heysel, becoming the first Belgian ever to win a title at that level.

The achievement, explored in detail by [RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/dans-son-atelier-a-anderlecht-se-cache-un-ancien-graphiste-devenu-champion-du-monde-de-torrefaction-11807772), confirms Belgium's arrival in a discipline long dominated by more established coffee nations.

## A win on home soil

The championship, part of the World of Coffee Brussels gathering organised by the Specialty Coffee Association, pitted around 30 national champions from Europe, Asia and the Americas against one another over three days of competition. Brassart faced roasters representing countries including China, Greece, Austria, Brazil, Japan and Chinese Taipei.

According to [La DH/Les Sports+](https://www.dhnet.be/regions/bruxelles/2026/06/29/benjamin-brassart-de-boo-coffee-roasters-a-anderlecht-premier-belge-champion-du-monde-de-torrefaction-je-ne-realise-pas-encore-PUQWJVUYHZDBRAHP5GC4Y62ZJI/), the Ath native — who moved to Anderlecht, where he set up his workshop in 2024 — won after three days of competition at what the newspaper described as the largest international convention dedicated to specialty coffee. He received the trophy from the 2025 champion, Frenchman Mikaël Portannier.

The official results published by the [World Coffee Championships](https://wcc.coffee/latest-news/wcrc-winners) list Brassart first, followed by Li Zhong Xiang of China in second and Thanasis Angelopoulos of Greece in third.

## Technique over luck

What distinguishes a roasting champion is not the bean but the craft. In the world final, competitors were handed identical, blind coffees and judged purely on how well they roasted them — meaning the margin between victory and defeat rests entirely on technique and instinct.

"I managed to produce the best roast on the coffees given," Brassart told [RTBF's short-format report](https://www.rtbf.be/short/anderlecht-un-bruxellois-est-champion-du-monde-de-torrefaction-6714).

His path to the title was unlikely. A former graphic designer in advertising, Brassart discovered specialty coffee almost by chance, drawn in by the fruity notes he had never associated with the drink. He worked as a barista before turning to roasting. "Roasting is a lot of training," he told RTBF. "It's like athletic preparation."

[L'Avenir](https://www.lavenir.net/regions/bruxelles/anderlecht/2026/06/27/boo-coffee-roasters-champion-de-belgique-de-torrefaction-chaud-bouillant-pour-sa-coupe-du-monde-a-bruxelles-sans-nous-le-cafe-est-comme-un-caillou-MSWAVLNENZDA3KIL34JIBHTXCI/) reported that Boo operates from the fourth floor of the former COOP flour mill along the canal quays in Anderlecht, roasting beans sourced from Brazil, Rwanda, Guatemala, Burundi and Colombia. The workshop was born in 2024, the same year Brassart co-founded it.

## Why it matters

Brassart's victory is more than a personal milestone; it is a signal for Belgium's specialty coffee scene. The country only held its first national roasting championship in late 2025, making it a very young entrant on the world stage. Winning a world title so soon underscores how quickly the local scene has matured.

"Winning this championship is a form of global recognition," Brassart told RTBF. "It has opened up quite a few opportunities for us — a fine showcase internationally, but also in Belgium."

The specialty coffee sector has been growing steadily. Coffees are graded on a strict scale, with those scoring 80 or above earning the "specialty" label, a philosophy built on transparency from farmer to cup. As demand for higher-quality, traceable coffee rises across Europe, a Belgian world champion gives the domestic scene a recognisable figurehead.

## What's next

Brassart will hold the title until the next championship, scheduled for 2027 in New Orleans, where he will be expected to hand the trophy to his successor. For now, the golden coffee-bean cup sits on a shelf in his Anderlecht workshop, between samples of green beans still waiting to be roasted.

The wider question is whether one title can translate into a lasting shift in Belgium's coffee culture — and whether the country's young specialty scene can build on the momentum a world champion provides.
