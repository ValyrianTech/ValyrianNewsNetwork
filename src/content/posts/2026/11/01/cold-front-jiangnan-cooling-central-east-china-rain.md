# Cold Front Ends Jiangnan Heat as East China Braces for Rain

A strengthening cold air mass pushed further south across China on Sunday, ending an unusually warm spell and plunging parts of Jiangnan and southwestern China into a damp, rapid cool-down, while central and eastern regions braced for widespread rain. According to [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/politics/20261101/feb2cd65f8a946c18ee5c515175bdcd5/c.html), local temperature drops in Jiangnan could exceed 10°C, with highs in many areas falling to around 20°C.

The forecast, originally issued by China Weather Network at 07:50 and republished by Xinhua at 08:27, marks a sharp seasonal turning point after days of late-autumn heat across southern China.

## A Rapid Reversal

The defining feature of the event is the speed of the reversal. Just a day earlier, the subtropical high kept much of southern China unusually warm, with parts of southern Jiangnan and South China topping 30°C—conditions forecasters described as somewhat extreme. Now, that warmth is collapsing.

On 1 November, the cooling pushed into Sichuan, Chongqing, Guizhou and western Jiangnan, with highs generally dropping 4–8°C and locally more than 10°C. The cooling center shifts south of the Yangtze River on 2 November, with eastern Yunnan, Guizhou and Jiangnan all seeing highs slide.

The city-level numbers illustrate the swing. Changsha's high was still 29.5°C on 31 October but will plunge to 18°C by 2 November. Hangzhou and Nanchang will see highs of only 19°C on 3 November—their coolest days since the start of the second half of the year. In southern Hunan and central Jiangxi, single-day drops could exceed 10°C.

## Widespread Rain Moves South

Alongside the cooling, the cold air is driving a large-scale precipitation event across central and eastern China, with the rain zone shifting steadily southward. Over 1–2 November, light-to-moderate rain covers much of the Huang-Huai, Jianghuai, Jianghan, and northern and western Jiangnan regions.

Local heavy rain is forecast in parts of western Anhui, eastern and southwestern Hubei, northern and western Hunan, southeastern Guizhou, southeastern Yunnan, and western and northeastern Guangxi. Some southern areas could see rainstorms, the meteorological department warned.

On the northern and western fringes, the system is bringing snow or rain-sleet to parts of the western Kunlun Mountains in southern Xinjiang, southeastern Tibet, the northern Sichuan highlands, eastern Inner Mongolia, southeastern Heilongjiang, and central and eastern Jilin.

## A Multi-Phase Cold Onset

The Sunday forecast is the latest phase in a broader cold-season onset that began at the end of October. As [CCTV News reported](https://www.yangtse.com/news/txs/202610/t20261029_407271.html), two cold-air branches—one from the east and one from the west—moved south in succession, cooling most of the country north of the Nanling Mountains and pushing the rain belt southward.

That transition was already visible in the north. On 31 October, Harbin's high reached only −0.5°C, the first full day below freezing in the second half of the year, while strong north winds with gusts of 7–11 on the Beaufort scale swept across the Northwest, North and Northeast.

The same system also produced a major snow event on the central-eastern Tibetan Plateau from 28–31 October. Driven by the residual vortex of a Bay of Bengal cyclone interacting with a westerly trough, blizzard-to-severe-blizzard conditions struck southern Tibet's high-altitude areas, with Nyalam recording 63.3 mm of precipitation on 30 October.

## What It Means

Meteorologically, the event reflects a textbook mid-autumn pattern: cold air from the north colliding with the retreating subtropical high, producing a sharp temperature reversal and a southward-migrating rain belt. The swing from 30°C-plus heat to sub-20°C highs within days is notable even by the region's variable autumn standards.

The pattern also carries practical consequences. Jiangnan and southwestern China are in the autumn harvest and winter-planting window, and persistent overcast rain—known locally as lianyinyu—can disrupt late-rice harvesting and drying as well as rapeseed planting. Large day-to-day temperature drops and damp cold raise the risk of respiratory illness, while heavy rain, slick roads and reduced visibility create travel hazards.

## What to Watch

The immediate concern is the rain. The meteorological department urged the public to follow warning and forecast information, carry rain gear and guard against slippery roads over the coming two days. Looking further ahead, southern China is expected to remain rainy and overcast next week, with cold air and a periodically strengthening warm-moist airflow bringing marked rainfall from Guizhou to northern Jiangnan.

The key questions now are whether the predicted drops materialize as forecast, how long the persistent southern rain lasts, and whether the China Meteorological Administration escalates rainstorm or cold-wave warnings as the system evolves.
