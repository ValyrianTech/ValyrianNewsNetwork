# Belgian Workers Take Home €8.47 More Net Per Month

Belgian employees will see an extra €8.47 net per month on their payslips from 1 November 2026, as a reform of the personal income tax takes effect. The increase comes because employers must withhold less *bedrijfsvoorheffing* — the corporate withholding tax that serves as a monthly advance on personal income tax — from gross salaries. [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/10/30/meer-loon-vanaf-november-aanpassing-bedrijfsvoorheffing/) reported the figure, calculated by HR services provider Liantis, as the first visible phase of the De Wever government's flagship tax package.

## Context: "Work must pay"

The measure is the centrepiece of the so-called *Arizona* coalition's promise that *werken moet lonen* — "work must pay more." In its 2025 coalition agreement, the government committed to raising the tax-free allowance (*belastingvrije som*) in stages, so that a growing share of income escapes personal income tax altogether. The allowance had stood at €11,170; under the reform it rises to €11,550 for the 2026 income year, with the aim of reaching between €15,300 and €15,600 by 2030.

The underlying problem the reform targets is Belgium's *werkloosheidsval* — the unemployment trap. For years, critics argued that the financial gap between working and receiving benefits had narrowed to the point where taking a job barely paid off. The government has said its aim is to guarantee workers at least €500 a month more than those living on benefits.

## How the €8.47 is calculated

Because the tax-free allowance rises, employers withhold less from each monthly salary. The legal footing is the personal income tax reform law enacted on 15 July 2026, together with the royal decree amending the withholding tax on 27 September 2026.

The adjustment formally applies to the entire 2026 income year, not just November onward. The effect on the earlier months of 2026 will be reconciled through the 2027 tax return, meaning the true annual impact may differ from the monthly headline figure.

## Not everyone gains — and some lose

Liantis cautioned that the €8.47 increase applies cleanly only to workers without dependent children. Family circumstances and parallel reforms materially change — and in some cases reverse — the gain.

| Situation | Net monthly effect |
|---|---|
| No dependent children | +€8.47 |
| One dependent child | +€13.47 |
| Two dependent children | +€7.47 |
| Three dependent children | +€3.47 |
| Four or more children | Slight net loss |
| Five children | −€18 |

The divergence stems from the simultaneous overhaul of child tax allowances. The government is moving toward a flat per-child supplement to the tax-free allowance, phased in over time. The first child's allowance rises by €5 a month, but the supplements for subsequent children fall.

A second drag is the *huwelijksquotiënt*, the marriage quotient — a tax advantage for married and legally cohabiting couples where one partner has little or no professional income. It is being phased down, with the maximum amount dropping from €13,790 to €11,780 in 2026. In 2025, some 500,151 couples benefited from the arrangement. From November, the change will negatively affect the net pay of the highest-earning partner. Additional allowances on the tax-free sum — for a child under three, or for a single parent — are also no longer indexed.

## Analysis: modest gains, real distributional shifts

By raising the tax-free allowance, the reform is progressive in structure: a flat euro amount is proportionally more valuable to lower earners. But the parallel measures cut the other way. Phasing down the marriage quotient and reworking child allowances shift the burden toward single-earner families and larger households — the very groups the headline figure does not capture.

There is also an implementation risk. The VRT noted that the staged increases to the tax-free allowance are "what the government has now decided," but whether they survive the budget plans for the coming years remains to be seen — the government is still negotiating. The law is enacted; the later phases are not yet locked in.

## What's next

Workers will see the change on their November payslip. The full-year reconciliation arrives with the 2027 tax return. Further staged increases to the tax-free allowance are scheduled through 2030, alongside the continued expansion of measures such as the work bonus and the reform of the special social security contribution.

The questions to watch: whether the budget talks preserve the 2030 target, and how the distributional impact plays out across household types as the child allowance and marriage quotient changes are fully phased in.
