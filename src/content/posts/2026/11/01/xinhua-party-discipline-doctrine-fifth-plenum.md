# Xinhua Sets Party Discipline Doctrine After Plenum

China's state news agency has issued an authoritative reading of the doctrine on Party discipline adopted at the Fifth Plenary Session of the 20th Communist Party of China Central Committee, framing the strengthening of Party spirit and the enforcement of democratic centralism as the twin pillars of the leadership's self-governance agenda. The commentator article, published by [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/politics/20261101/16312ca2cb9344a990f506a00796c3f2/c.html) on 31 October and dated 1 November, is the interpretive companion to the plenum's decisions and signals which formulations the leadership wants emphasised across the Party apparatus.

## What the Plenum Decided

The Fifth Plenum convened in Beijing from 26 to 29 October and adopted the "Decision of the CPC Central Committee on Several Major Issues Concerning Persistently Advancing Comprehensive Strict Party Governance," as [CCTV](https://news.ifeng.com/c/8xfoaZobTIM) reported. The plenum's theme — Party discipline and governance — follows the Fourth Plenum of the same Central Committee, which positioned comprehensive strict Party governance as the "fundamental guarantee" for the drive to basically realise socialist modernisation by 2035. According to an analysis by Yan Xiaofeng of Tianjin University published in the [Economic Daily](https://www.yangtse.com/news/yw/202610/t20261029_407309.html), the two plenums' agendas are "closely connected and mutually supportive."

The commentator piece distils the Decision into two headline formulations, which it attributes to the plenum itself rather than to the agency's own gloss: "Strengthening Party spirit is the fundamental task of comprehensive strict Party governance," and "implementing democratic centralism is the inherent requirement of comprehensive strict Party governance." The piece opens with a blunt assertion of stakes: "Getting China's affairs done well depends on the Party, and on comprehensive strict Party governance."

## Party Spirit as a Lifelong Discipline

The commentary builds its case around a quotation attributed to Xi Jinping, the Party's general secretary: "Party spirit, in the final analysis, is a question of standpoint." It describes Party spirit as the cornerstone on which a member or cadre "establishes themselves, their careers, their words and their virtue."

Crucially, the piece insists that this quality cannot be acquired passively. Party spirit "cannot naturally strengthen with seniority in the Party, nor with promotion in position," it states, but must be forged continuously through strict inner-Party life. The formulation is crystallised in one of the piece's most quotable lines: "Joining the Party organisationally happens once in a lifetime; joining the Party ideologically is a lifelong matter."

The commentary places this in the context of the Party's long-standing warnings about the "Four Tests" and "Four Dangers" — the risks to a party that has governed for decades — arguing that the more complex the situation, the more necessary it is to temper Party spirit. Yan Xiaofeng's Economic Daily analysis made a related point, invoking Xi's "Six How to Always" framework for the "unique challenges of a big Party" and noting that the Party now counts more than 100 million members and 5.4 million grassroots organisations.

## Democratic Centralism and the Power Problem

The second pillar concerns organisation. The commentary describes democratic centralism as "the Party's fundamental organisational principle and leadership system, and an important marker distinguishing Marxist parties from other parties." Democracy and centralism, it argues, are mutually conditioning and neither can be dispensed with.

Applied to governance, the piece calls for improving mechanisms that constrain how power operates within Party committee and leading Party group teams, and for standardising how "top leaders" exercise power — a notable emphasis on the accountability of the most senior local and departmental officials. It closes this section by invoking a classic Mao-era aspiration: a political situation with "both centralism and democracy, both discipline and freedom, both unity of will and personal ease and liveliness."

CCTV's expert coverage reinforced the anti-corruption dimension. Zhuang Deshui, deputy director of the Public Policy Research Centre at Peking University, said the plenum had systematised experience with the integrated approach of ensuring officials "dare not, cannot and do not want to be corrupt," and called for a power mechanism in which the granting, exercise and supervision of authority all follow rules — putting "power in the cage of the system." Hong Xianghua, a professor at the Central Party School, described the Party's combination of popular oversight and self-revolution as a distinctive contribution to Marxist party-building.

## Analysis: Messaging as Policy

The genre matters. A "Xinhua Commentator" article is not reportage but interpretive messaging: its function is to define the orthodox reading of a plenum document for officials who will study it in the months ahead. That the two pillars — Party spirit and democratic centralism — were attributed directly to the plenum elevates them from commentary to doctrine, and their emphasis tells officials where to concentrate.

The recurring language of "perseverance" and permanence fits the wider governing logic. Yan Xiaofeng quoted the 20th Party Congress report's warning that "comprehensive strict Party governance is always on the road; the Party's self-revolution is always on the road," and that there must be no mood of slackening or weariness. The piece also situates the campaign relative to the 15th Five-Year Plan period, 2026 to 2030, framing Party discipline as the guarantee for delivering the modernisation programme.

For observers, the most operationally significant detail may be the stress on restraining top leaders' discretion. It dovetails with the anti-corruption apparatus and suggests that the next phase of Party building will focus less on headline investigations than on the institutional plumbing that governs how senior officials make decisions.

## What to Watch

Over the coming weeks, expect a wave of "study and implement" sessions across provinces, ministries and state enterprises, along with further commentator and theorist articles unpacking the Decision's specific provisions. The key questions are how the plenum's language on constraining top-leader power is translated into enforceable rules, and how the drive is sequenced against the economic priorities of the 15th Five-Year Plan. The Party's own framing leaves little doubt about the intended signal: rigorous self-governance is presented not as a corrective episode but as a permanent condition of governance.
