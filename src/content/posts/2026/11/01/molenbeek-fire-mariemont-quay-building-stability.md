# Fire ravages Molenbeek building, stability compromised

A major fire broke out in the early hours of Saturday in a mixed-use building on the Quai de Mariemont in Molenbeek-Saint-Jean, Brussels, leaving the structure's stability compromised and sending one cleaning worker to safety with severe smoke inhalation. The Brussels fire brigade was called around 03h40 and found the building fully ablaze on arrival, according to [RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/molenbeek-un-violent-incendie-quai-de-mariemont-une-personne-gravement-intoxiquee-11808527). The fire was brought under control by around 06h40, but operations continued into the afternoon as crews worked to reach the last remaining hotspot.

## A complex, mixed-use building

The structure housed a warehouse, offices and a party and events hall — a combination that increases fuel load and complicates both evacuation and firefighting. Firefighters had to contend with thick smoke, intense heat and the risk of further structural failure.

One person, a member of a cleaning team, was trapped on the second floor. She signalled her presence using a flashlight and was evacuated by aerial ladder, fire service spokesperson Walter Derieuw told RTBF. Three of her colleagues managed to leave the building on their own. The victim was treated for severe smoke inhalation.

"A person on the second floor signalled their presence with a flashlight. We were able to evacuate them using an aerial ladder," Derieuw said. The roof of the building collapsed on Saturday morning, [RTL Info](https://www.rtl.be/actu/regions/bruxelles/violent-incendie-bruxelles-une-personne-gravement-intoxiquee-par-les-fumees/2026-10-31/article/805115) reported.

## Precarious operation as stability fails

The building's compromised structure forced a cautious, technically demanding operation. "The building's stability is compromised. Our teams are therefore working with the greatest caution and are calling on heavy machinery to reach the last hotspot in complete safety," Derieuw [explained](https://www.rtbf.be/article/incendie-quai-de-mariemont-a-molenbeek-la-stabilite-du-batiment-est-compromise-11808588). Crews were waiting for a heavy demolition shear to cut through scrap metal and reach the final seat of the fire.

Firefighters attacked the flames from aerial ladders used as water towers and from the building's flanks, deploying a water cannon. Water was also pumped directly from the nearby canal to reinforce supply — a common tactic for large blazes near Brussels waterways.

The Brussels fire brigade (SIAMU) deployed two officers, four pump trucks, two aerial ladders and a tanker truck. The Brussels-West police zone established a wide security perimeter around the building. Residents of an adjacent apartment block spontaneously evacuated their homes and were received by police in a nearby building. The water utility Vivaqua and the gas and electricity operator Sibelga were also on site. Propagation of the flames to a neighbouring warehouse was narrowly avoided.

## Cause under investigation

By midday the fire was completely extinguished, though the intervention continued. The origin of the blaze remains undetermined, and authorities have not disclosed the victim's identity or exact medical condition beyond the severe smoke inhalation.

The fire comes amid a broader fire-safety context in Molenbeek, which has seen several blazes in recent months, including a September 2026 fire in a disused canteen and a June 2026 apartment fire linked to electric scooters. The mixed-use configuration of the Quai de Mariemont building — combining storage, office and event space in a single structure — echoes concerns raised repeatedly about fire prevention in multi-purpose venues.

## What to watch

Investigators are expected to focus on the origin of the fire and on whether building-safety requirements were met for a venue hosting events. The structural damage will require assessment before the site can be secured or restored. For now, one person's quick thinking with a flashlight, and the crews' use of heavy machinery, helped avert a far worse outcome.
