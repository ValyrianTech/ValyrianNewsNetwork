# Ningbo Zhoushan Port Tops 40 Million TEU Again

Ningbo Zhoushan Port has surpassed 40 million twenty-foot equivalent units (TEU) in annual container throughput for a second consecutive year, reaching the milestone on November 10 — 22 days earlier than in 2025, according to [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/fortune/20261110/d007347efa3f46a2b8bc09f5bc744381/c.html). The early arrival of the mark reaffirms the port's standing as China's largest by cargo tonnage and one of the busiest in the world, and reflects a year of buoyant export growth for the Chinese economy.

The port company attributed the performance to an expanding foreign trade base, a wider inland and global network, and the regularized operation of a new Arctic shipping route that shortens transit times to Europe.

## Context: A Port Already at the Top

The milestone builds on a rapid climb. Ningbo Zhoushan first crossed 40 million TEU in December 2025, becoming only the third port globally — after Shanghai and Singapore — to do so, and the second in China. For the full year 2025, it handled 43.87 million TEU and became the first port in the world to exceed 1.4 billion tonnes of annual cargo throughput, a position it has now held for 17 consecutive years.

Reaching the same container mark 22 days earlier than last year signals that 2026 throughput is running ahead of the previous record pace.

## The Drivers Behind the Growth

The port's business department said the expansion of China's foreign trade and a shifting export mix have supplied abundant cargo while raising expectations for speed and reliability. In the first three quarters of 2026, China's goods trade imports and exports reached 39.94 trillion yuan, up 18.8% year-on-year.

"The continuous expansion of foreign trade scale and the shift of export structure toward 'new and better' bring abundant cargo sources to the port, while also raising higher requirements for channel timeliness and stability," an official at Ningbo Zhoushan Port Co., Ltd. said, according to Xinhua.

On the infrastructure side, the port has extended its reach inland through sea-rail intermodal transport. It now operates 115 sea-rail routes reaching 16 provinces and 71 prefecture-level cities, with intermodal volume exceeding 1.8 million TEU so far this year. Its ocean-going network has grown to more than 310 container shipping routes connecting over 700 ports in more than 200 countries and regions.

Efficiency gains have followed. Strengthened coordination with customs, maritime and border inspection authorities has cut the non-operational time of container ships. In September, daily average container handling exceeded 140,000 TEU, and the single-month total reached a record 4.27 million TEU. "In the face of continuously growing container operations, Ningbo Zhoushan Port has kept improving infrastructure carrying capacity; deepwater coastline, specialized berths, and collection/distribution facilities support each other," the port official said.

## The Arctic Bet

A distinctive element of the port's 2026 strategy is the China-Europe Arctic Express, which entered regularized operation this year. Eight voyages were completed during the navigable season, carrying high-value-added goods — energy storage cabinets, power batteries and solar modules — across the Northeast Arctic Passage. One-way transit to Europe takes about 20 days, nearly half the time of the traditional Suez Canal route.

The route offers exporters a seasonal alternative to established chokepoints, a consideration of growing strategic weight as global shipping lanes face congestion and geopolitical pressure.

## A Global Ranking Shift

The throughput milestone comes against a broader realignment of global container traffic. According to shipping data firm Alphaliner's first-half 2026 rankings, reported by [AMZ123 via Maijiazhijia](https://mjzj.com/article/fxp08mj8g1kx), Ningbo Zhoushan handled 22.90 million TEU in the first six months, up 8.8% year-on-year, overtaking Singapore to take second place globally in a half-year ranking for the first time. Shanghai remained first with 28.74 million TEU.

It marked the first time in history that Chinese ports have held the top two half-year spots worldwide, reinforcing the "Shanghai–Ningbo" dual-core hub position. Analysts attribute the shift to hinterland manufacturing advantages, river-sea intermodal capability and dense ocean-going trunk line frequency, concentrating container traffic along the East China Sea–Hangzhou Bay coast.

## What to Watch

The port has not published a full-year 2026 throughput target, but its first-half pace and record September suggest it could exceed 2025's 43.87 million TEU. Whether Ningbo Zhoushan holds the global number-two position for the full year will depend on fourth-quarter performance against Singapore.

The bigger picture is one of supply-chain resilience. China's first-ten-month trade rose about 20% year-on-year to 44.81 trillion yuan, providing a strong tailwind for the country's ports. The early arrival of the 40 million TEU mark, the record September and the Arctic route's maturation together point to a port system positioning itself not just as the world's largest by volume, but as an increasingly sophisticated node in global trade — one that is diversifying its routes even as it deepens its domestic reach.
