# Zoë (33) death: case nears assize trial five years on

Five years after Zoë Snoeks (33) fell roughly 30 metres to her death from the Le Hérou rock formation in the Ardennes, the Turnhout council chamber has moved her husband a step closer to standing trial for murder. The chamber has forwarded the case file to the prosecutor-general with a view to referral to the Court of Assizes, according to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/10/zoe-val-rots-ongeval-moord-raadkamer-doorverwijzing/).

The decision means the council chamber sees sufficient indications to pursue the case, following the position of both the Antwerp public prosecutor's office and the victim's family. The final say on whether Joeri J. (39) will actually face a jury now rests with the Chamber of Indictment (kamer van inbeschuldigingstelling).

## A fall, and a dispute over what happened

On the morning of 2 November 2021, Snoeks and her husband travelled early to the Le Hérou rock in Nadrin, part of the municipality of Houffalize in Belgium's Luxembourg province, on the banks of the Ourthe river. Snoeks, a beautician and social-media influencer from Meerhout in Antwerp province, fell dozens of metres and died at the scene.

The Luxembourg public prosecutor's office initially treated the death as a tragic accident. According to her husband, Snoeks lost her balance while enjoying the view.

Her parents rejected that account. They pointed to tensions in the marriage and filed a civil-party complaint with an investigating judge in Antwerp, triggering a fresh judicial investigation. That inquiry grew into a dossier of thousands of pages, with investigators reconstructing the final months of the marriage, interviewing numerous witnesses and carrying out a reconstruction at the scene.

## A case built on circumstantial evidence

No hard material evidence exists. There is no camera footage of the fall, no DNA traces and no eyewitnesses to what happened on the rock. Instead, the prosecution's case rests on a set of behavioural and circumstantial elements.

One of the final investigative steps was a specialised sound test at the scene, which examined whether a scream would have been audible to the husband standing nearby. "A scream that was impossible to miss" was the conclusion, [HLN reported](https://www.hln.be/meerhout/parket-wil-weduwnaar-van-influencer-zoe-snoeks-33-die-stierf-na-fatale-val-voor-assisen-na-geluidstest-schreeuw-was-wel-hoorbaar~a85d9831/). The husband had said he neither saw nor heard the fall because he was distracted by the couple's dogs.

Investigators also questioned his conduct afterwards. According to the prosecution, he took a longer route down to reach her and did not immediately alert emergency services. Another element concerns a video Snoeks was recording shortly before her death: investigators established that the video file on her phone had been deleted, though they could not recover the images. A polygraph test indicated deception, but it is not admissible as direct evidence in Belgian law.

## Arrest, release, and a legal turning point

Joeri J. was arrested on suspicion of murder in late October 2023, nearly two years after the fall, and was held in pre-trial detention for almost a year. At the time, he maintained that his wife had slipped while posing for a photo, and that he had warned her to be careful, [HLN reported](https://www.hln.be/binnenland/bijna-twee-jaar-nadat-zoe-33-van-rots-in-ardennen-viel-zit-weduwnaar-in-cel-had-haar-nog-zo-gezegd-voorzichtig-te-zijn~aeed58f2/) at the time of his detention. He was released in October 2024 without an ankle tag or conditions, though he remained a suspect.

The Antwerp public prosecutor's office concluded in 2025 that the evidence pointed to a deliberate act — that Joeri J. had taken his wife to Le Hérou that morning intending to push her from the rock. The prosecutor sought referral to the Court of Assizes for murder. The defence has consistently maintained the death was a tragic accident, and the accused denies any involvement.

## What's next

The case now passes to the prosecutor-general, which must bring it before the Chamber of Indictment. That body will make the final decision on referral to the assize court, where a jury would weigh the question that has hung over the dossier for five years: whether Snoeks fell, or was pushed. Referral requires only "sufficient indications of guilt" — a lower threshold than the proof needed for conviction, and no verdict has been reached.

As [RTV](https://www.rtv.be/actualiteit-en-nieuws/5-jaar-na-dood-zoe-snoeks-staat-weduwnaar-van-meerhoutse-influencer-stap) noted, the indications of guilt are considered sufficiently serious to seek a referral, even as the accused continues to insist on his innocence. Whichever way the Chamber of Indictment rules, the case is likely to turn on circumstantial evidence — and, given the five-year delay, the defence may also invoke the reasonable-time argument. For Snoeks's family, who forced the reinvestigation through their civil-party complaint, the prospect of a full jury trial is the closest they have come to answers.
