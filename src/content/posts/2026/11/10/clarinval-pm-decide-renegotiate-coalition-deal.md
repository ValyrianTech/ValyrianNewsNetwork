# Clarinval: PM to Decide Within Days on Renegotiating Deal

Belgian Deputy Prime Minister David Clarinval said Tuesday that Prime Minister Bart De Wever will decide "in the coming days" whether to keep budget talks within the current coalition agreement or reopen the entire deal, as the five-party government scrambles to close a deep budget gap before a mid-November deadline. The remarks, made in an interview with Bel RTL, expose the fragility of the so-called "Arizona" coalition and amount to a pointed signal from Clarinval's Francophone liberal party, MR.

Speaking to journalist Martin Buxant, Clarinval framed the choice starkly. "At a certain point you have to choose: either we negotiate within the framework of the coalition agreement, or we cross those red lines," he said, according to [21news.be](https://www.21news.be/nl/david-clarinval-als-we-opnieuw-over-een-regeerakkoord-moeten-onderhandelen-wordt-het-moeilijk-om-maandag-een-akkoord-te-bereiken/). "That is up to the prime minister to decide in the coming days."

## A Government on the Clock

The federal government led by De Wever (N-VA) took office after the 2024 elections as a five-party coalition: the Flemish nationalist N-VA, the Francophone liberal MR, the Francophone centrist Les Engagés, the Flemish socialist Vooruit and the Flemish Christian democrat CD&V. It must now find roughly 10 billion euros in savings and revenue by 2029 to bring Belgium's public finances under control — among the weakest in the eurozone.

The pressure intensified after budget talks in the inner cabinet collapsed on 3 November, prompting De Wever to offer his government's resignation to King Filip. The King held the resignation under advisement, consulted the five majority party presidents, and on 6 November refused it, asking the premier to immediately resume negotiations and report back on 16 November. According to [Business AM](https://businessam.be/de-wever-zet-begrotingsgesprekken-voort-na-bouchez-en-vandenbroucke-is-vandaag-les-engages-aan-de-beurt/), every party president had pledged to make concessions.

## The Two Paths

Clarinval's intervention sets out two scenarios. The first is to continue talks within the existing coalition agreement while respecting each partner's red lines. The second is to reopen and renegotiate the agreement itself, giving the parties room to move beyond the positions now blocking a deal.

The deputy premier acknowledged that a full renegotiation would cost precious time. "It is clear that it will be difficult to reach an agreement by next Monday if we have to renegotiate the coalition agreement," he said. A renegotiation would also pull the party presidents into the talks, as MR president Georges-Louis Bouchez has demanded.

At the heart of the dispute is a fiscal rule MR says is written into the coalition agreement. The MR argues that new revenues — higher or new taxes — may account for at most one-ninth of the total budget effort, roughly 1.1 billion euros of the 10 billion. Vooruit disputes that interpretation and wants the "strongest shoulders" to contribute around 1.5 billion euros. "If we want to go considerably above that, we will probably have to negotiate a new coalition agreement," Clarinval said.

## Bouchez at the Table

The remarks followed a striking development on 9 November, when De Wever began a fresh round of one-on-one talks and, for the first time, allowed each deputy prime minister to bring a "plus one." MR sent Clarinval alongside Bouchez — the party president widely dubbed the "shadow premier" — for a session lasting about two hours. Vooruit sent deputy premier Frank Vandenbroucke with his chief of staff for a meeting of more than an hour. On Tuesday it was Les Engagés' Maxime Prévot's turn at the premier's office, as [De Morgen](https://www.demorgen.be/politiek/na-bezoekje-van-bouchez-hoe-wil-de-wever-verder-met-de-begrotingsopmaak~b64875bb/) reported.

Clarinval dismissed suggestions that Bouchez was there to keep him on a leash. "Georges-Louis and I form a tandem, a duo. We are completely on the same line," he said.

On substance, Clarinval confirmed that a proposed VAT increase is no longer on the table — "we are glad about that" — and reiterated MR's opposition to fresh taxes, arguing the middle class ultimately bears the heaviest burden. He cited revenues of 267 billion euros against expenditures of 293 billion euros, an annual deficit of 26 billion, and pointed to spending on pensions, health care and automatic indexation as the core problem.

## Analysis: A Veiled Ultimatum

Clarinval's framing is less a neutral briefing than a public pressure tactic. By presenting the choice as "current framework versus full renegotiation," the MR is raising the stakes and signalling it will not surrender its one-ninth tax cap without reopening the entire agreement. That shifts the burden onto De Wever to pick a path that keeps the coalition intact.

The central axis remains the MR–Vooruit split, with Les Engagés, which favours a balance between savings and new revenue, caught in between. King Filip's 16 November report-back date is the key forcing mechanism, though it does not require a final deal — only demonstrable progress. De Wever has cancelled attendance at a European summit in Dublin to stay at the table.

Opposition parties are circling. Vlaams Belang and the Francophone socialist PS have both called for early elections, arguing the government cannot govern. Clarinval rejected that prospect outright: "Of course not! No one at the negotiating table wants elections."

Despite the tensions, he struck a confident note about the premier and the coalition. "I have confidence in this premier, I have confidence in this government, and I am convinced we will find an agreement," he said. Whether that agreement comes within the current framework — or requires tearing up the deal that created this government — is now De Wever's call to make.
