# Ninth CIIE Deal Value Hits Record $85.4 Billion

The ninth China International Import Expo (CIIE) closed in Shanghai on November 10 with an annualized intended transaction value of US$85.45 billion, a record high and up 2.3% from the previous edition, according to [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/world/20261110/d9af7bb249a54dfab83988fdaa0e7250/c.html). The figure cements the expo's status as the world's largest import-themed trade platform and a flagship vehicle for Beijing's "high-level opening-up" agenda, held in the opening year of the 15th Five-Year Plan.

The headline number is striking, but the underlying trend tells a more nuanced story: the absolute record rose even as the pace of growth slowed sharply from a year earlier.

## A Record, With an Asterisk

The intended value is measured "on an annual basis" (按一年计意向成交额), a CIIE convention that allows year-on-year comparison. It is a gauge of intent rather than realized commerce — non-binding expressions of interest rather than audited contracts — so it signals sentiment and platform scale more than it measures actual trade flows. CIIE does not release audited per-deal breakdowns.

The 2.3% year-on-year increase compares with the eighth edition's US$83.49 billion, which itself grew 4.4% over the prior year and was then a record, as [21st Century Business Herald](https://www.21jingji.com/article/20251110/herald/bc1a72756882a8998c6d6efaed56ecae.html) reported at the time. In other words, the record keeps climbing, but the rate of increase has roughly halved — the sharpest deceleration in three years.

## A Platform of Unusual Scale

The expo, held November 5–10 at the National Exhibition and Convention Center in Shanghai, drew 162 countries, regions and international organizations, with 4,142 overseas companies exhibiting. The enterprise exhibition area exceeded 370,000 square meters for the first time, according to [CCTV News](https://www.yangtse.com/news/yw/202610/t20261029_407294.html).

Nearly 200 companies have exhibited at all nine editions — the so-called "full-attendance" exhibitors (全勤生) — growing from modest 9-square-meter stalls to booths of more than 100 square meters, and shifting from exhibitors to investors embedded in Chinese supply chains.

Foreign firms' strategies have visibly evolved over the nine years, moving through three phases: an early "in China for global" model that leveraged cost advantages for export-oriented production; "in China for China," serving the vast domestic market; and a newer "in China for global" in which local research and development feeds global markets. Medtronic's Cao Shan (曹珊), vice president of corporate affairs and communications for Greater China, said locally developed and locally innovated products "have already gone back overseas to serve patients globally."

## Why It Matters

The expo's timing gives it outsized political weight. As the first edition held in the opening year of the 15th Five-Year Plan (2026–2030), it serves as a deliberate signal of China's continued openness amid global trade tensions, supply-chain diversification and Western "de-risking" pressures. State media framed the record as validation of that agenda and of foreign confidence in the Chinese market.

That framing runs up against the decelerating growth figure. A record absolute total paired with slower expansion suggests the platform is maturing and that foreign appetite, while substantial, is not accelerating at its earlier pace — a reading consistent with a global environment in which companies are diversifying manufacturing footprints even as they deepen local operations in China.

The exhibitor testimonials underscore a pragmatic, localization-driven approach rather than a purely export-oriented one. Schneider Electric's Gu Yue (古月), vice president and head of marketing for China and East Asia, said the company used the CIIE's limited window to showcase how digital technology can empower industrial life-cycle renewal. Herbalife China's Yang Lan (杨兰) described the expo as a "loudspeaker" for products and health-management solutions, adding that "deepening roots in China is itself the foundation for consolidating our global layout."

## What's Next

The immediate question is whether the expo's intended deals translate into actual trade. Because the figures are expressions of intent, the real test will be in realized imports over the coming year — and in whether the growth rate stabilizes or continues to cool. For Beijing, the broader challenge is sustaining the openness narrative when the underlying numbers, while record-setting, point to slowing momentum.

Judged purely on scale, the ninth CIIE delivered. Judged on trajectory, it left a more complicated picture for the 10th edition to answer.
