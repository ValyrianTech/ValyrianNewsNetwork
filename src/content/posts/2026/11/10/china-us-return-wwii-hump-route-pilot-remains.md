# China, US Military Hold Repatriation Ceremony for Remains of WWII Hump Route Pilot

China and the United States held a military repatriation ceremony in Chengdu, Sichuan Province, on 10 November, returning a batch of remains of American airmen who flew the "Hump Route" to support China's resistance against Japan in World War Two, according to [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/world/20261110/bd47d65589d64cabb208d2ef00b45422/c.html).

The remains were recovered in areas along the route's flight path, Beijing's Ministry of National Defense said in a statement released through its official @国防部发布 account. The handover was presented as a gesture grounded in shared wartime history and as a stabilising factor in a bilateral relationship otherwise marked by strategic rivalry.

## A Wartime Lifeline Across the Himalayas

The Hump Route was one of the main aerial corridors connecting China with Allied forces between 1942 and the end of the war, as [Guancha](https://www.guancha.cn/JunShi/2026_11_10_907730.shtml) recounted in its report on the ceremony. It ran from Assam in India eastward across the Himalayas, the Gaoligong and Hengduan mountain ranges and the Salween, Nu, Lancang and Jinsha rivers into China's Yunnan plateau and Sichuan Province.

The passage took its name from the camel-hump-like peaks that pilots had to clear, with terrain generally rising between 4,500 and 5,500 metres and summits approaching 7,000 metres — above the service ceilings of much of the aircraft involved. The route stretched roughly 500 miles.

Early operations relied on fewer than 30 aircraft, mostly Douglas C-47s that had never been designed for high-altitude, heavy-load flying, later supplemented by the larger Curtiss C-46 Commando. By the final months of the war, hundreds of aircraft were delivering an estimated 650,000 to 685,000 tonnes of fuel, ammunition, medicine and supplies, with monthly tonnage peaking above 70,000 tonnes in 1945.

The human cost was severe. US statistics indicate that between 509 and 594 aircraft crashed, more than 1,300 aircrew were confirmed dead, and hundreds more went missing. The route earned the grim nickname "Aluminum Valley" because of the wreckage glinting on the mountainsides — a wartime correspondent wrote that pilots could navigate by the trail of smoking debris below them.

## A Long-Standing Track of Cooperation

The ceremony follows a precedent set in October 2015, when China handed over the remains of a Hump Route pilot recovered from the Ruoguo Glacier in Bomê County, Nyingchi, Tibet. That C-87 transport aircraft had crashed some 72 years earlier, and the handover took place at the Jianchuan Museum's "Flying Tigers" hall in Dayi County, Chengdu, as the [Chinese Consulate General in New York](https://newyork.china-consulate.gov.cn/lghd/202508/t20250819_11692610.htm) documented in a commemorative series.

Cooperation on locating missing US personnel in China has continued largely uninterrupted even as wider ties have fluctuated. The US Defense POW/MIA Accounting Agency's work in China focuses on World War Two losses from the Flying Tigers and the Hump Route, as well as the Korean War and Cold War shoot-downs. The two militaries have periodically held consultations on the search effort.

## Humanitarian Framing, Strategic Message

Beijing's statement was carefully worded. It described the search cooperation as "a vivid embodiment of the history of China and the US fighting shoulder to shoulder against Japanese fascism during WWII," and said China had long assisted the US "in a humanitarian spirit." It added that China is willing to continue practical cooperation in the field, "adding positive factors to building a constructive and strategically stable China-US relationship."

That final phrase is the operative diplomatic formula. By anchoring the gesture in humanitarian and historical terms, Beijing can offer cooperation that is insulated from — and presented as a counterweight to — current frictions over trade, technology and security. The symbolism is low-cost but deliberately chosen: a reminder of a time when the two countries were allies rather than competitors.

Domestic reaction reflected the careful line Beijing must walk. On Guancha, readers broadly supported honouring the fallen airmen on humanitarian and historical grounds, while debate turned on how much credit Washington should receive for its wartime aid to China — a sign that commemorating shared history and maintaining strategic distance can coexist, but not without friction.

## What to Watch

The number of remains and the precise excavation sites were not disclosed. No public statement from the US Department of Defense or the DPAA on the ceremony was immediately identified, and it is unclear whether the handover is tied to a specific earlier recovery mission.

What is clear is that the repatriation track remains one of the few channels where humanitarian framing reliably overrides geopolitics. Whether such gestures can be scaled into broader confidence-building — or remain isolated symbolic moments — will depend on a relationship that both capitals continue to describe in guarded terms.
