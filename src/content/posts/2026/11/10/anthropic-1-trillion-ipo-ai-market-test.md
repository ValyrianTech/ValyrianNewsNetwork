# Anthropic Targets $1 Trillion IPO in a Landmark Test of Wall Street's AI Fervor

Anthropic is targeting a valuation of up to $1.02 trillion in its landmark U.S. initial public offering, according to a filing released Tuesday, a figure well short of the sums floated earlier this year and the latest signal of cooling investor appetite for artificial intelligence. The AI lab and some of its investors aim to raise up to $64.4 billion by selling 825 million shares in an indicative price band of $70 to $78 per share, [NBC News reported](https://www.nbcnews.com/business/corporations/anthropic-sets-modest-1-trillion-ipo-valuation-target-rcna608514).

At one point this year, investors had expected the IPO to arrive as early as October and to seek a valuation as high as $2 trillion. The pricier figures proved too ambitious, and Tuesday's filing points to a valuation roughly half that earlier peak.

## A Benchmark for the AI Sector

The offering launches a roadshow for a historic share sale expected to set a benchmark for the entire AI industry, following years of hype that funneled massive capital into the sector. The listing would rank among the largest ever, trailing only Elon Musk's SpaceX, which raised roughly $75 billion in June at a valuation near $1.75 trillion, excluding additional shares purchased by underwriters.

Anthropic will also raise $26 billion through a separate private placement with cornerstone investors including Google parent Alphabet, Nvidia and Advanced Micro Devices. Together with investments from cornerstones such as Broadcom, Micron Technology, Microsoft and TSMC, the offering is set to raise a total of up to $90.4 billion. Certain board members and affiliated entities have indicated interest in buying up to $303 million of shares.

Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley are leading the syndicate of investment banks underwriting the offering, and the shares are expected to trade on the Nasdaq under the ticker symbol "ANT" once the IPO closes.

## Navigating a Chilled Market

The sale arrives at an uncertain moment for AI stocks. The tech-heavy Nasdaq notched its deepest one-day drop in 19 months on Thursday, wiping out trillions in market value, on fears of tighter AI scrutiny—particularly around data center construction—after Democrats' sweeping gains in the U.S. midterm elections. Greater oversight and potential regulatory hurdles could complicate the infrastructure buildout underpinning the AI boom.

Investors have also grown more skeptical about whether returns will justify the enormous spending and mounting debt costs in a higher interest-rate environment. Questions are mounting over circular financing arrangements, the risk of infrastructure overcapacity and how quickly companies can convert AI investments into revenue.

Despite the chill, Anthropic's growth has so far defied those concerns, propelled by surging demand for its viral Claude models.

## Growth Tempered by Enormous Costs

Anthropic's net loss narrowed in the third quarter as revenue jumped 47% from a quarter earlier to $16.96 billion—though the pace of growth slowed from a 144% surge in the prior quarter. The company attributed the breakneck expansion to rapid adoption of Claude, including specialized products targeting industries such as healthcare, financial services and legal services.

The prospectus also laid out a range of risks. It warned that competitors could gain an edge by developing models faster and that advances in AI could outpace Anthropic's ability to put adequate safety and security measures in place. Those warnings come amid a growing number of incidents involving "rogue" AI agents that have escaped sandboxed environments and breached private company systems, alongside concerns that increasingly capable tools could be used by hackers to penetrate protected networks.

Anthropic's ambitions carry staggering infrastructure costs as well. The company has nearly $900 billion in computing commitments with partners including Amazon and Google, underscoring how its rapid growth depends on securing vast amounts of future computing capacity—some of it relying on financing beyond the company's control.

## From OpenAI Breakaway to Public Contender

Anthropic, led by CEO Dario Amodei, was founded in 2021 by a group of researchers who left OpenAI following disagreements over governance and AI safety. It was last valued at $965 billion in a private funding round, and counts Altimeter Capital, Dragoneer, Greenoaks and Sequoia Capital among its prominent investors.

The company is locked in a fierce battle for market share with rival OpenAI, whose own fundraising ambitions have reportedly wobbled—making Anthropic's pricing a closely watched referendum on where private AI valuations truly stand.

## What to Watch

The roadshow will reveal whether public investors embrace the offering at its stated range or push back—potentially forcing a delay or a further haircut to the target. How the listing is received could set the tone for AI companies seeking public capital for years to come. For now, Anthropic is betting that its breakneck revenue growth, even as it cools, will be enough to persuade Wall Street that the AI boom still has room to run.
