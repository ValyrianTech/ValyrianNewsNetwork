# Trump: FBI Probing Nvidia CEO's Ties to Alleged Spy

President Donald Trump said Monday that the FBI is investigating Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang's relationship with a woman U.S. intelligence agencies believe to be a Chinese spy, dramatically escalating a fast-moving scandal now encircling the world's second-most valuable company and its celebrated leader.

Speaking at an impromptu briefing at the White House, Trump said he had called Huang earlier in the day "to see if he's feeling all right," and defended the executive. "He had absolutely no idea that the person in question was a spy," Trump said, adding, "I really believe him." According to [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/business/corporations/trump-fbi-probe-nvidia-jensen-huang-spy-rcna608439), the president added: "I know the FBI is looking into it, in terms of something else, and I think that he'll be in good shape."

## Context

Nvidia, founded by Huang in 1993, is the dominant designer of the artificial intelligence chips powering the global AI boom. Its market capitalization sits at roughly $4.3 trillion to $4.5 trillion, and Huang, 63, is one of the world's ten richest people, with a net worth estimated at $155 billion to $160 billion.

At the center of the controversy is Alice Huang (also known as Huang Le), a Chinese citizen and executive at Megaspeed, a Singapore-based cloud computing firm. U.S. intelligence officials have concluded she was recruited by China's top spy agency. Jensen Huang and Alice Huang are not related, despite sharing a surname.

The U.S. has long restricted the export of advanced chips to China, citing national security and military concerns — restrictions that intensified with the 2022 export controls. Megaspeed is suspected of smuggling Nvidia AI chips to customers in China in violation of those rules.

## Key Developments

The scandal erupted over the weekend. On Sunday, Nvidia's board [announced](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/tech-news/nvidia-ceo-jensen-huang-investigation-rcna608148) it was "initiating an independent investigation in light of recent reporting that founder and CEO Jensen Huang has been the target of a foreign intelligence effort." A committee of independent directors, assisted by outside counsel, will oversee the review. The board said it has "no reason to believe that either national security or NVIDIA secrets were compromised."

At the same time, Huang addressed employees directly, [acknowledging](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/a-message-to-our-company) an extramarital affair. "I was unfaithful to my wife. My actions hurt her and our children. There is no excuse for what I did," he wrote, saying he supports the investigation and will cooperate fully. "I love our country and our company. I am committed to protecting NVIDIA and supporting America's national security."

Separately, the Justice Department is conducting a criminal investigation into Megaspeed, according to a source familiar with the matter. The probe — led by the department's National Security Division, alongside the FBI and the Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security — is examining whether Megaspeed helped ship Nvidia AI chips to China and whether China recruited Alice Huang both to spy and to assist the smuggling. Megaspeed has denied wrongdoing.

Markets responded sharply. Nvidia shares closed down about 4.2% on Monday, erasing roughly $190 billion to $200 billion in market value and making the stock the worst performer in the Dow and the Nasdaq, even as the broader market gained.

Reaction from Washington was swift and bipartisan. House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries, D-N.Y., called for "a full briefing" on the matter, while Sens. Elizabeth Warren, D-Mass., and Jim Banks, R-Ind., said in a joint statement that Congress should "take decisive action" and that the news "makes these national security concerns even more urgent and raises important questions NVIDIA must answer." Rep. John Moolenaar, R-Mich., chairman of the House Select Committee on China, has requested a briefing from Huang set for Nov. 18.

China rejected the allegations. Foreign Ministry spokesperson Guo Jiakun said Monday that "the so-called Chinese spy allegation is unsubstantiated. It is nothing but smears against China."

On Tuesday, a shareholder class-action lawsuit, Kistenmacher v. Nvidia, was filed in federal court in California just minutes after the market close, alleging the company and Huang misled investors.

## Analysis

The affair has fused two strands of anxiety that rarely intersect so publicly: the personal conduct of a corporate titan and the national security stakes of the AI supply chain. For Nvidia, the risk is not merely reputational. Huang has been the architect and public face of the company's global strategy, and analysts warn that even speculation about his future could unsettle investors.

"There is clearly risk that Jensen could be removed as CEO depending on the board investigation, and this will add risk to the stock as he is so important to the Nvidia story and strategy globally," analyst Dan Ives said, according to NBC News. Daniela Hathorn of Capital.com noted that "given Huang's central role in Nvidia's success and the wider AI investment narrative, even speculation about leadership uncertainty could unsettle investors."

Trump's public embrace of Huang is notable. The president has positioned himself as a transactional steward of U.S. chip policy, and his backing signals that the White House is not, at least for now, treating Huang as a liability. Nvidia has said Huang will remain CEO, and a spokesperson insisted that "today's news will not slow" the company.

The most consequential unanswered question is what, if anything, Alice Huang obtained. Nvidia says it has found no reason to believe secrets were compromised, but that conclusion rests on a board investigation that is only beginning. U.S. intelligence conclusions reported by major outlets have not been independently verified, and Alice Huang has not commented publicly.

## What's Next

Huang is expected to appear before the House Select Committee on China on Nov. 18, where lawmakers are likely to press him on Megaspeed, export controls, and what he knew about his associate's alleged ties. The shareholder lawsuit adds a fresh legal front, and additional suits are likely.

The matter may also surface diplomatically: Trump is expected to meet with Chinese President Xi Jinping in the coming weeks, and the Nvidia dispute could complicate an already fragile effort to manage U.S.-China tech tensions. For now, the president's vote of confidence has bought Huang time — but the board investigation, the Justice Department probe, and Congress will ultimately decide how much of it he has.
