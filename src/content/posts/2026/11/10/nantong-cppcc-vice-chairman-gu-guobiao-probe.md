# Nantong CPPCC Vice Chairman Gu Guobiao Under Probe

Gu Guobiao, a member of the Party group and vice chairman of the Nantong Municipal Committee of the Chinese People's Political Consultative Conference (CPPCC) in Jiangsu province, is under disciplinary review and supervisory investigation for suspected serious violations of discipline and law, the Jiangsu Provincial Commission for Discipline Inspection and Supervision announced on 10 November 2026. The brief bulletin, carried by [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/legal/20261110/5c12a16d0d47409f801ca004a180eb44/c.html) at 15:24 local time, is the standard disclosure format that opens a Party disciplinary case but stops short of detailing any alleged wrongdoing.

## Context

Gu, a native of Nantong born in August 1965, built most of his career inside the city's administrative and organizational apparatus. According to his official biography carried by [China Economic Net](http://district.ce.cn/newarea/sddy/202101/23/t20210123_36251766.shtml), he began as a primary school teacher in Haimen county in 1984, joined the Communist Party in October 1994, and rose through the Nantong Municipal Party Committee's Organization Department from the late 1990s through the mid-2000s.

From 2006 to 2011 he served as Organization Department chief in Chongchuan district, later adding the post of Party secretary of the Chongchuan Economic Development Zone. He then moved to Hai'an, first as Organization Department chief and deputy Party secretary, then as acting county head in 2014, county head in 2015, county Party secretary in 2017 and, after Hai'an was reclassified as a city, municipal Party secretary in 2018. He held that post, concurrently with the Hai'an Economic and Technological Development Zone Party secretary role, until January 2021, when he was elected vice chairman of the 12th Nantong Municipal CPPCC. He was re-elected to the same position on the 13th CPPCC in February 2022.

## Key Developments

The announcement was carried quickly by regional outlets, including [The Paper](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_34445389) and [The Beijing News](https://www.sohu.com/a/1099646874_114988), both of which reproduced the provincial commission's statement without adding further detail. No information has been publicly disclosed about the specific conduct under investigation, the sums allegedly involved, or whether Gu has been placed under *liuzhi* — the retention-in-custody measure used in supervisory investigations.

## Analysis

The phrasing of the notice — "suspected serious violations of discipline and law" (涉嫌严重违纪违法) — is the fixed formulation Chinese discipline commissions use when an official is formally placed under review, and signals corruption or disciplinary charges rather than a routine administrative matter. Under the framework of the 2018 Supervision Law, such a review is conducted by the supervisory commission at the corresponding level; because Gu's most senior post is at the prefecture level, the case has been taken up by the provincial commission rather than the city-level body.

While the CPPCC is a political advisory body without executive authority, Gu's earlier career placed him at the center of local governance in one of Jiangsu's more economically active prefecture-level cities. His decade in charge of personnel appointments in Chongchuan and Hai'an, and his four years as Hai'an's top Party official, gave him responsibility over land allocation, project approvals and cadre appointments — the very areas where local Party secretaries most often face scrutiny in corruption inquiries.

Jiangsu, a wealthy coastal province, has seen a steady flow of disciplinary cases at municipal and county levels, and Nantong in particular has been the site of several such investigations. Most of these cases are disclosed through short provincial bulletins of the kind published on Tuesday, rather than through the higher-profile national announcements reserved for provincial-ministerial officials.

## What's Next

Under standard procedure, the Jiangsu commission's review will determine whether Gu is expelled from the Party and referred to prosecutors, whether he is cleared, or whether the case is closed with internal disciplinary sanctions. Such reviews can take months, and the commission is not obliged to publish interim findings. What remains to be seen is whether the investigation remains confined to Gu or draws in other officials from his time in Hai'an and Chongchuan — a question that will shape how the case is read locally in Nantong and across Jiangsu's cadre ranks.
