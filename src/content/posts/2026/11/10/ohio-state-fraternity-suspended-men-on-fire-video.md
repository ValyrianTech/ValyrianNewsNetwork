# Ohio State Suspends Fraternity After Men Set Ablaze on Video

Ohio State University has placed the Sigma Alpha Epsilon fraternity on interim suspension after a video circulated online appearing to show two young men engulfed in flames at an off-campus event near campus, according to [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/us-news/ohio-state-university-investigating-fraternity-video-appears-show-two-rcna608313). The incident occurred Saturday, Nov. 7, 2026, and is now under investigation by the university, Columbus police, the city's arson unit, and the fraternity's national headquarters.

## How the incident unfolded

The footage shows a man in a gray sweatsuit on the ground with his pants on fire, as [the New York Post reported](https://nypost.com/2026/11/10/us-news/ohio-fraternity-suspended-after-terrifying-video-shows-two-men-on-fire/). He then gets up and tackles another man in a matching outfit, igniting the second person's clothing. Both men run as others work to stamp out the flames, and at least one bystander is seen struggling with a fire extinguisher.

A second video taken from a different angle appears to show the second man's pant leg was already burning before the tackle, according to the research compiled on the incident.

Columbus police said they received no calls for service about the incident on Saturday. They were alerted only after the video began circulating online the following day.

## Officials respond

University spokesperson Benjamin Johnson confirmed the university is aware of the episode and that the individuals involved are receiving medical care, though their conditions remain unclear.

"Ohio State is committed to a safe and healthy environment and expects students and student organizations to uphold that commitment and the university's values," Johnson said.

Columbus police said they are coordinating their review with the university and the Columbus Division of Fire's Arson Unit to determine whether any criminal acts occurred. Sgt. Joe Albert, a police spokesperson, confirmed the fiery scene appeared to unfold at a Saturday event but said the only report police received concerned the video circulating online.

Sigma Alpha Epsilon's national leadership said it is conducting its own inquiry. "We take this matter seriously and are actively investigating the circumstances surrounding the incident while working closely with university officials and local law enforcement," CEO Steve Mitchell said in a statement.

## A fraternity with a troubled history

The episode has renewed attention on Sigma Alpha Epsilon, one of the nation's oldest and largest fraternities. Founded in 1856 at the University of Alabama, SAE was described in a 2013 Bloomberg report as the "deadliest frat" in the country, having been linked to nine deaths tied to drinking, drugs, and hazing between 2006 and 2013 — more than any other Greek organization, according to the research compiled on the incident.

In 2014, the fraternity's national headquarters eliminated its pledge system in favor of a "True Gentleman Experience" after a series of deaths and misconduct incidents. The organization's guiding principle describes a true gentleman as one "whose conduct proceeds from good will and an acute sense of propriety."

The incident is not SAE's first brush with hazing allegations. In 2022, an event at the University of New Hampshire resulted in 46 members being charged with hazing, though three defendants were later acquitted after a judge ruled the conduct did not meet the legal threshold for a hazing charge.

## A national reckoning over Greek life

The Ohio State episode arrives amid heightened scrutiny of fraternity conduct nationwide. A Cornell University student alleged in 2024 that seven Chi Phi fraternity brothers drugged and gang-raped her, and police in late October 2026 raided fraternity houses at West Virginia University as part of a hazing probe.

The timing of the Ohio State incident was especially conspicuous: the university hosted a major football game against Oregon on Saturday, and many bystanders in the footage appeared to be wearing Ohio State jerseys.

## What's next

Key questions remain unanswered, including whether the two men were fraternity members or pledges, how the fraternity was connected to the event, and what the incident's medical and legal consequences will be. A hazing investigation is reportedly underway, though officials have not confirmed whether the episode was a hazing ritual.

The university's interim suspension will remain in effect while the parallel inquiries proceed. For now, Ohio State and SAE's national leadership have both pledged to hold accountable anyone found responsible — a promise that, given the fraternity's history, will be closely watched.
