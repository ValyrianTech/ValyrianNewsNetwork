# Deerlijk mother (35) suspected of killing two babies

A 35-year-old woman from Deerlijk in West Flanders has been placed under investigation for the manslaughter of two of her own children — who died as infants in 2014 and 2015 — and for the attempted manslaughter of a third child who survived a life-threatening incident in March 2025. The West Flanders public prosecutor's office confirmed the case on Tuesday, after Het Nieuwsblad first reported it, as detailed by [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/10/vrouw-35-deerlijk-verdacht-van-doodslag-op-haar-2-eigen-kinderen/). The woman, identified by the media only as M.V., denies any involvement.

## A decade-old mystery reopened

The facts reach back more than a decade. In 2014 and 2015 the woman lost two children: a daughter who died at seven months and a son who lived only 25 days. At the time, neither death was treated as suspicious.

"Those deaths were not considered suspicious at the time," Griet De Prest, spokesperson for the West Flanders prosecutor's office, told VRT NWS.

After the deaths, the mother gave interviews about the loss of her children and organised a benefit event to commemorate them. In a later interview, she attributed the deaths to "a rare genetic abnormality in which signals from the heart are not transmitted to the brain, causing the heart to stop."

## IVF conception removed the genetic explanation

The investigation was set in motion in March 2025, when a baby boy of about five weeks old was taken to Ghent University Hospital (UZ Gent) with severe breathing problems. Doctors found no clear medical explanation and alerted the prosecutor's office.

"The investigation started after a doctor at UZ Gent notified the prosecutor's office, because there were questions about the circumstances in which the five-week-old baby was admitted to hospital," De Prest said.

That re-examination brought the earlier deaths under a new light. According to [KW.be](https://kw.be/nieuws/criminaliteit/moord/vrouw-35-uit-deerlijk-verdacht-van-doodslag-op-eigen-kinderen-parket-voert-onderzoek/), investigators consider the injuries to the third child suspicious precisely because a genetic abnormality could not have been the cause. The boy was conceived through IVF using an egg cell from the mother's partner — not her own — meaning there was no possible genetic link between the suspect and the child.

"The investigating judge in Kortrijk detained a 35-year-old woman from Deerlijk on 23 April 2026 on suspicion of the manslaughter of her seven-month-old daughter and her 25-day-old son. In addition, the woman was placed under suspicion of attempted manslaughter of her then five-week-old baby, a boy. She has since been placed under electronic monitoring," the prosecutor's office said.

## Denial and a disputed polygraph

The woman's lawyer, Emeline Vanhooren, said her client denies everything and is cooperating fully.

"My client denies any involvement. We are awaiting the results of the investigation. It is being conducted very extensively and calmly. She is providing every possible cooperation," Vanhooren told VRT NWS.

VRT NWS reported, citing its editorial information, that the woman had asked to take a polygraph test and that the result was "deceitful" — that is, not in her favour. The prosecutor's office has not formally confirmed the details of that test, and [HLN](https://www.hln.be/binnenland/moeder-35-die-verdacht-wordt-van-dubbele-doodslag-en-poging-doodslag-op-eigen-pasgeboren-babys-schreeuwt-onschuld-uit-maar-leugendetector-zegt-iets-anders~a00adff1/) also reported on the polygraph angle. All medical files of the babies have reportedly been seized and are being examined by experts.

## Where things stand now

The woman is not in pre-trial detention. According to [Focus-WTV](https://focus-wtv.be/nieuws/vrouw-uit-deerlijk-in-verdenking-gesteld-van-doodslag-op-eigen-kinderen-ruim-10-jaar-geleden), she has been under electronic monitoring since July. The surviving child is under the supervision of the juvenile judge and is staying with the other parent. A contact ban is in place: the mother may have no contact with the child.

## Analysis: why this case is unusual

Several elements set this investigation apart. Belgian law distinguishes between being "placed under suspicion" and being convicted; the probe is at an early stage and no verdict has been reached. The decision to reopen decade-old deaths rested on a subtle evidentiary shift — the realisation that the explanation once offered for two infant deaths could not, by definition, apply to a third child conceived with a donor egg. The electronic-monitoring arrangement suggests the investigating judge did not deem the suspect a flight risk or an immediate danger, though she faces serious charges.

If sufficient serious indications of guilt are established, the case could eventually go before the assize court, the only Belgian court with a jury, which handles serious intentional homicide. That outcome is not assured.

For now, the prosecutor's office stresses that the judicial investigation continues under the investigating judge in Kortrijk, and media note the case remains sensitive, with the family having asked for calm and privacy.

All persons are presumed innocent until proven guilty.
