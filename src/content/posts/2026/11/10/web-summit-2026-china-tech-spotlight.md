# Web Summit 2026 opens with China's tech in spotlight

The 2026 Web Summit opened in Lisbon on the evening of November 9, with Chinese technology companies and innovations taking a prominent — and, according to organizers, record-setting — role at the global gathering, [Xinhua reported](https://www.news.cn/tech/20261110/87e4710e78f94e76ae9217b599f287b0/c.html). The four-day event runs through November 12 at the MEO Arena in Portugal's capital, drawing roughly 70,000 tech executives, entrepreneurs, investors and policymakers from around the world.

Artificial intelligence is the summit's central theme. As AI accelerates from software applications into the physical world, focus areas include embodied intelligence, AI agents, robotics and the integration of AI with physical industry. Delegates are also weighing questions of AI investment returns, regulation and the technology's impact on employment.

## A record Chinese presence

China's tech sector emerged as one of the summit's defining features. According to organizers, this year's Chinese delegation is the largest in the conference's history, Xinhua reported. Representatives from Unitree Robotics, AgiBot (Zhiyuan Robotics), Galbot, ModelBest, Xiaomi, Tencent, XPeng AeroHT and Pony.ai are attending, discussing innovations spanning artificial intelligence, robotics and smart mobility.

"The future tech superstars are increasingly coming from China," Web Summit co-founder and CEO Paddy Cosgrave said at the opening ceremony, adding that Chinese companies are shaping the future of global technology development to a greater degree.

The symbolism was theatrical as well as strategic. Dancing humanoid robots from AgiBot opened the conference on Centre Stage, putting China's AI hardware firmly in the spotlight. [Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/2026/11/09/web-summit-lisbon-2026-chinas-robotos-take-to-the-stage-as-tech-conference-launches) reported Cosgrave telling an audience of up to 12,000 people: "The superstars of the future are not coming from California; they're coming from China." He added that "Chinese companies are building more of our shared future than anyone else," referring to open-source technology.

## Geopolitics on the sidelines

The summit is unfolding against a backdrop of deepening US–China tech rivalry, with artificial intelligence at its core. Washington has sought to restrict exports of the most powerful semiconductor chips to China. Euronews noted that Cosgrave's remarks — contrasting China with California — landed squarely within that competition.

A parallel storyline dominated conversation on the sidelines: US chip giant Nvidia is probing its own CEO, Jensen Huang, after reports that he was the "target of a foreign intelligence effort" tied to an alleged relationship with a Chinese spy. Asked about the controversy on stage, Cloudflare co-founder and CEO Matthew Prince called it "very salacious," saying it remains to be seen whether Huang will be remembered as "a hero or villain," while noting that "Jensen did a lot to cement the AI ecosystem."

## AI agents take the stage

Beyond the geopolitics, AI agents — systems designed to take autonomous actions to assist humans without step-by-step instructions — featured prominently. Manus AI, originally founded in China, demonstrated a 30-second AI-generated video produced with its new 2.0 video technology from a prompt of just 13 words.

Tao Zhang, co-founder and chief product officer of Manus, said AI is reshaping how he builds his own engineering team. "We found the people we are looking for; it's not just a good attitude. We don't need more people; we need people who can work with AI," he said, according to Euronews.

Web Summit expects close to 70,000 attendees, including about 900 speakers and more than 2,000 startups. Cosgrave described it as the most international edition of the Lisbon event, with every European country represented.

## Background and what to watch

First held in Dublin in 2010, Web Summit moved its flagship event to Lisbon in 2016 and has become one of the world's most important technology gatherings. China's official engagement with the conference is more recent: [Foreign Policy](https://foreignpolicy.com/2025/11/11/web-summit-lisbon-china-technology-paddy-cosgrave/) reported that Beijing made its Web Summit debut at the 2025 edition, with the prior year's event drawing nearly 72,000 attendees and 268 government delegations from 82 countries. The 2026 edition appears to mark a substantial escalation of that presence.

Several questions remain as the summit continues through November 12. Observers will be watching for concrete announcements, deals or partnerships emerging from the Chinese companies' participation, and for how US policymakers and commentators respond to the prominence of Chinese firms at a major Western-hosted tech conference. Organizers have yet to publish an official breakdown quantifying the "largest-ever" Chinese delegation, and the specific speaker, exhibitor and booth-space numbers that would allow a direct comparison with 2025 are not yet public.

For now, the message from the opening night was unambiguous: at a conference designed to showcase the future of technology, the firms and machines on center stage increasingly carry Chinese names.
