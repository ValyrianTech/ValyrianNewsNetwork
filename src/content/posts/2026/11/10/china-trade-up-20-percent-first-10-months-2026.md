# China Trade Up 20% in First 10 Months of 2026

China's goods trade grew 20 percent year-on-year in the first ten months of 2026, reaching 44.81 trillion yuan, as October alone posted a striking 31.2 percent jump, according to [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/fortune/20261110/9afec94b24df4805b3d8797a5d884bf3/c.html), citing data released by the General Administration of Customs (GACC) on 10 November. The figures point to accelerating momentum, with high-tech exports driving a visible upgrade in the composition of Chinese trade.

## The Numbers Behind the Growth

Total trade for January–October reached 44.81 trillion yuan. In October, single-month trade totaled 4.87 trillion yuan, up 31.2 percent year-on-year. Exports rose 17.6 percent to 25.99 trillion yuan, while imports grew faster still at 23.6 percent, reaching 18.82 trillion yuan — a gap of six percentage points that underscores Beijing's push to present a rebalanced, import-friendly trade posture.

The headline figure continues an upward trajectory through 2026. Trade for the first seven months had totaled 30.13 trillion yuan, up 17.3 percent, as reported at the time by [CNR](https://news.cnr.cn/native/gd/kx/20260807/t20260807_527751704.shtml), before the pace accelerated to 20 percent by October.

## A Structural Shift Toward High-Tech Exports

The composition of exports tells the more consequential story. Mechanical and electrical products reached 16.81 trillion yuan, up 25.2 percent, and now account for 64.7 percent of total exports — a 3.9 percentage point increase over the same period last year. Within that category, some segments posted explosive, triple-digit growth:

- **Integrated circuits:** +101.9 percent
- **Storage components:** +132.5 percent
- **3D printers:** +104.8 percent
- **Digital cameras:** +41.4 percent
- **Industrial robots:** +17.8 percent

The so-called "new three" products — electric vehicles, lithium batteries and solar products — rose 46.5 percent, while electric motorcycles and bicycles grew 33.3 percent and wind turbine components 19.7 percent.

This evolution reflects a broader transformation in China's export competitiveness, from labor-cost-driven goods toward innovation-driven products. As a [Guangming.com commentary](https://guancha.gmw.cn/2026-07/31/content_38920984.htm) noted, China's trade has moved through three phases: the "old three" of apparel, furniture and home appliances; the "new three" of electric vehicles, lithium batteries and solar products; and now a "new new three" of robots, artificial intelligence and innovative drugs — a shift from "factor-driven" to "innovation-driven" competition.

## Officials Tout Resilience and the 'China Opportunity'

Lü Daliang, director of the Statistical Analysis Department at the General Administration of Customs, framed the results as evidence of economic resilience. "Since the start of this year, in the face of various shocks and challenges, our economy has pushed forward against pressure, moving toward innovation and improvement," he said. "Market expectations and business confidence have continued to improve, foreign trade has maintained a strong growth trajectory."

On imports, Lü emphasized diversification. "In the first 10 months, our imports from more than 150 countries and regions grew; imports from ASEAN, Latin America, and Africa all reached their highest levels for the same period in history," he said. "China's ultra-large-scale market continues to provide 'China opportunities' to countries around the world."

## Analysis: What the Data Really Shows

The figures are, on their face, robust — and the acceleration from 17.3 percent in July to 20 percent in October is a genuine signal of strengthening final demand. But the headline numbers also demand careful reading.

The explosive growth in integrated circuits and storage components — both above 100 percent — likely reflects more than pure volume gain. Semiconductor price surges and the front-loading of trade ahead of anticipated tariff and export-control actions may inflate these figures, and the source data does not disaggregate volume from price effects. The October jump of 31.2 percent in particular warrants caution; such a spike can reflect timing effects rather than a durable new baseline.

The notable gap between import growth (23.6 percent) and export growth (17.6 percent) is politically as well as economically significant. It supports Beijing's narrative of actively opening its market and rebalancing trade, a counter to international criticism of China's persistent trade surplus. Yet the data did not include a breakdown by trading partner or overall surplus figures — details that would be essential to any full assessment of the balance with the United States and European Union, where trade and technology frictions persist.

## What to Watch

Three questions will shape how this trade performance is judged in the months ahead: how much of the growth is genuine volume versus price and timing effects, especially in semiconductors; how the bilateral picture with the US and EU evolves amid continuing tech restrictions; and whether the triple-digit growth in chips and storage components proves sustainable or proves to be a one-off surge. For now, China's trade machine is running hot — and increasingly, it is running on higher-value goods.
