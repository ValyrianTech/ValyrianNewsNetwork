# Where China Sent 800 Billion Yuan of Policy Capital

China had fully deployed its 2026 tranche of 800 billion yuan (about US$119 billion) in "new policy-based financial instruments" by the end of October, directing the money exclusively into project capital for major investments, according to [Xinhua](https://english.news.cn/20261028/9f6029a55e7f4d0183771cb64dfde249/c.html). A field investigation published by People's Daily on Monday and carried by [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/fortune/20261123/148b3a2de8e743ab9336850ca96a8475/c.html) traces the money to projects spanning artificial intelligence, the digital economy, green and low-carbon energy, biomedicine, the low-altitude economy and consumer infrastructure, with a deliberate tilt toward private enterprises.

The instrument matters because it targets the scarcest constraint in Chinese infrastructure finance: the equity share a project must raise before commercial bank loans can follow. As one enterprise maxim quoted in the reporting puts it, "Whether a project can start early depends on whether the capital can arrive early."

## What the instrument actually is

These tools are not ordinary lending. They are hybrid fiscal-monetary instruments funded by the central bank's Pledged Supplementary Lending and channeled through China's three policy banks, used solely to top up project capital. At roughly 8 to 13 times leverage, according to analyst estimates cited in the reporting, the 800 billion yuan is expected to support 8 to 10 trillion yuan of total investment over three to four years.

It is also the largest round of a recurring counter-cyclical tool. The instrument was first launched in 2025 at 500 billion yuan; the 2026 Government Work Report raised the allocation to 800 billion yuan explicitly to "attract more private capital," Xinhua reported.

## How the money was split

China Development Bank deployed 365 billion yuan, leveraging projects with total investment of 5.5 trillion yuan. The Export-Import Bank of China deployed 120 billion yuan, helping leverage more than 1.7 trillion yuan in investment. The Agricultural Development Bank of China deployed 315 billion yuan, supporting projects with total investment of about 4.39 trillion yuan.

Sectoral allocation skewed heavily toward strategic emerging industries. CDB put 179.01 billion yuan — 49% of its total — into AI, the digital economy, green and low-carbon development, biomedicine and consumption. Exim Bank supported more than 220 projects in sectors such as the digital economy and AI, roughly half its allocation. The Agricultural Development Bank backed almost 2,000 projects in AI, the digital economy, the low-altitude economy, consumption and green sectors, with nearly 200 billion yuan.

The private-sector emphasis is notable. CDB directed 127.99 billion yuan (35.1% of its allocation) to projects involving private investment or private capital; Exim Bank backed more than 210 such projects, accounting for almost 40% of its allocation; and the Agricultural Development Bank supported nearly 400 projects with over 65 billion yuan.

[Caixin](https://finance.caixin.com/2026-10-28/102498285.html) added a geographic dimension: CDB and Exim Bank each directed more than 70% of lending to 11 large provincial economies, including Anhui, Zhejiang and Sichuan.

## Three projects, three bottlenecks

The People's Daily investigation uses three case studies to show how the money behaves on the ground.

In Ulanqab, Inner Mongolia — one of eight national nodes under the "East Data, West Computing" program — CDB funding supported the third phase of a zero-carbon smart computing center operated by Vianet. Approval and disbursement took ten days. "The deployment of CDB's new instruments put an accelerator on the third-phase project," said Liu Yuankun, general manager of Vianet's Inner Mongolia base. "Only when funding doesn't break can computing power keep its momentum." Hao Chunyu, a CDB Inner Mongolia client manager, described two selection principles: alignment with the national green-computing strategy and a tilt toward private enterprises with urgent funding needs. He added that his branch's supported computing projects "grew by a dozen at once," with capital shifting away from traditional energy and mining.

In Shandong's Binzhou high-tech zone, the Export-Import Bank provided a 46.7 million yuan instrument with a seven-year term — matched to the long research-to-market cycle — for a domestically developed woven large-diameter artificial blood vessel, aimed at import substitution for aortic surgery. "Medical device innovation requires cracking hard technical nuts AND crossing the funding hurdle," said chief engineer Li Fujie. "The policy-based financial instrument gave us a 'reassurance pill.'" The full process, from documentation to funds arriving, took seven days.

In Jingzhou, Hubei, the Agricultural Development Bank deployed 157 million yuan as project capital for a 1.576 billion yuan smart agriculture project spanning 1,075 mu, a joint venture between a private and a state-owned developer. "The project has good prospects, but it's too large — relying on the company's own funds alone, the capital gap is hard to fill in time," said Yu Lifa, president of the bank's Jingzhou branch. Once built, the project is expected to draw 500,000 visitors a year, generate 500 million yuan in sales and create 3,500 jobs, with phased operations targeted for May and June 2027.

## What it is meant to move

The tool's real test is whether disbursement turns into what officials call "physical workload" — visible construction and investment that shows up in economic data. Early signs are encouraging. Wen Bin, chief economist at China Minsheng Bank, said the rollout, together with major infrastructure and urban renewal projects, "could stimulate demand for electronic information products, electrical machinery, high-end equipment and certain raw materials." Zhang Di, chief macro analyst at China Galaxy Securities, noted that in September, entrusted loans rose 80.2 billion yuan year-on-year and medium- and long-term corporate loans rose 890 billion yuan, a high since April — likely from accelerated infrastructure and the instrument's follow-on financing.

## Open questions

The figure that draws least attention may be the most telling: each round has grown, from 500 billion yuan in 2025 to 800 billion in 2026. That escalation signals persistent weakness in private investment demand and softening growth, consistent with the "strong supply, weak demand" diagnosis economists have offered through 2026. The concentration of more than 70% of lending in 11 wealthy provinces also raises the question of whether weaker regions — which have thinner pipelines of bankable projects — are being left behind. And because the instrument is project capital, not a grant, project quality and repayment ultimately determine whether the leverage works as advertised. Official coverage stresses strict screening across policy alignment, technical maturity, repayment sources and risk mitigation — an implicit acknowledgment of those risks.

For now, the message from Beijing is about speed and direction: capital that can move in days rather than weeks, pointed squarely at the industries the state has designated as strategic. Whether that produces durable investment or simply another round of stimulus is the question the next set of economic data will answer.
