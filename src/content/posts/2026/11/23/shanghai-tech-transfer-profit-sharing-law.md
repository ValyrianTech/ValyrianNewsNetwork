# Shanghai Codifies Tech Transfer Profit-Sharing in Law

Shanghai has publicly released a revised regulation on commercializing scientific and technological achievements, writing the rights and benefit-sharing arrangements among researchers, institutions, service providers and enterprises directly into local law. Passed by the 35th session of the Standing Committee of the 16th Shanghai Municipal People's Congress, the *Regulations on Promoting the Transformation of Scientific and Technological Achievements* (《上海市促进科技成果转化条例》) takes effect on 1 December 2026, according to [The Paper](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_34537359), which reported directly on its publication.

The revision adds 22 new articles and a dedicated chapter on the supply of scientific and technological achievements, and rewrites 10 existing articles. Its most notable innovation is codifying the entitlement of "technical managers" — the intermediaries who match researchers with industry — to a share of commercialization revenue, a step intended to lift a profession long constrained by precarious, commission-based pay.

## A profession written into statute

Technical managers, historically called "science matchmakers," broker deals between labs and companies. Their income has largely depended on one-off commissions, and deal cycles run from six months to several years. "Three lean years, then one fat one" is how the industry describes the pattern, and market institutions often cannot pre-negotiate revenue-sharing terms with universities.

New Article 20 pushes back against that structure by allowing technical managers to participate in the distribution of transformation gains as agreed, earning returns through cash remuneration and equity incentives. A companion Article 39 strengthens national technology-transfer talent-training bases to cultivate high-level technical managers. The regulation also encourages transfer institutions to take equity stakes and promotes a "services-for-equity" scientist-partner model.

"Without a very clear incentive mechanism, it's impossible to do this long-term," said Zhao Pu, Shanghai general manager of the tech-service firm Green Cheng Technology, describing the strain on practitioners. Liu Qunyan, a professor at East China Normal University and chief engineer of the Shanghai Higher-Education Science & Technology Development Center, said China has no unified legal system for the role — "it looks like a profession, but it's hard to really call it one." He called the regulation's inclusion of technical-manager cultivation in local law "of major, breakthrough significance."

The Shanghai Municipal Science & Technology Commission said letting technical managers share in transformation revenue "helps raise professional recognition and sense of value" and attract talent to the field. Relevant departments are reportedly drafting implementing opinions on deepening benefit-sharing reform and operational guides for full-ownership empowerment.

## From "how to transform" to "what to transform"

The regulation also shifts legislative attention upstream. A new chapter on achievement supply and an Article 16 requirement make applied research funded by public money treat commercialization prospects as part of project approval, specifying transformation obligations, deadlines and licensing conditions up front. The aim is to break the habit of producing results first and thinking about commercialization later.

Grounding this push is a decade of reform. The regulation enshrines a "full-ownership empowerment plus contractually agreed benefits" model and creates a separate asset-accounting system for job-related achievements so they are not lumped with ordinary state assets — addressing a key "dare-not-transfer" barrier. Government-established research institutes and universities that transfer, license or invest such achievements may award no less than 70% of net income, or the equivalent share or contribution ratio, to the achievers.

The regulation also encourages state-owned enterprises to build transformation-focused assessment systems with long-cycle, differentiated evaluation, treating research and commercialization spending as equivalent to profit in performance reviews. "Where do high-quality results come from? Certainly not from scientists' guesswork — more from coupling with enterprises and industry," Liu said.

## Why Shanghai matters

The reform builds on a decade of institutional change, including the 2015 national revision of the science and technology achievement transformation law and a three-year pilot from 2020 in which 40 universities and institutes nationwide granted researchers ownership or long-term use rights over job-related work. Shanghai Jiao Tong University alone completed 356 empowerment cases worth 317 million yuan, and its 2022 contract value was nearly four times that of 2019, [Shang Jie News reported](https://m.163.com/dy/article/J8QEAI06055040N3.html). In 2024, the ruling Communist Party's Third Plenum explicitly called for strengthening the technical-manager workforce.

Shanghai is a leading national hub for university- and hospital-led commercialization. Its 2025 technical contract value from research institutions reached 62.289 billion yuan, a 12-fold increase over 2016, and it has ranked first nationally for years in license, transfer and equity-investment modes. Liu said the university sector still accounts for less than 10% of enterprise technology-transaction value — a gap the new supply-side measures aim to close.

Liu expects the measures to influence national system-building over the next three to five years, describing them as a potential "Shanghai solution" for commercialization reform. Whether the national framework is revised to mirror Shanghai's technical-manager provisions — and how the 70% reward floor interacts with existing state-asset and tax rules — remain open questions to watch.
