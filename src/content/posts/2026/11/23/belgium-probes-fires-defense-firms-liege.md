# Belgium Probes Three Fires at Defense Firms Near Liège

Belgian civilian and military intelligence services are investigating whether three fires at military-linked companies around Liège are the work of a new far-left arson campaign or an act of Russian sabotage, [Het Laatste Nieuws](https://www.hln.be/binnenland/extreemlinks-geweld-of-russische-sabotage-inlichtingendiensten-onderzoeken-drie-verdachte-branden-bij-militaire-bedrijven-rond-luik~addb6d85a/) reported on Monday. According to the newspaper, the State Security Service (VSSE) and military intelligence (ADIV) are examining a possible link between the three incidents, all of which struck firms tied to the arms industry.

The case has thrust a politically charged attribution battle into the open, pitting a government minister's early claim of far-left violence against a party's flat denial—while investigators reportedly keep the Russian-sabotage hypothesis on the table.

## A claimed campaign against the "war industry"

An organisation calling itself the **International Fire Faction Against War** has claimed responsibility for the fires online, describing them as part of "a campaign against the war industry."

The first occurred on the night of 30 to 31 August 2026 at BATS (Belgian Advanced Technology Systems), located in the Sart-Tilman science park in Angleur. The Liège prosecutor's office judged the fire intentional and said it was reportedly started at three distinct locations, with no casualties, according to [RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/l-incendie-des-batiments-de-la-societe-bats-au-sart-tilman-revendique-de-maniere-anonyme-sur-internet-11778812). BATS, founded in 1984, makes defence and homeland-security electronics—notably battlefield surveillance radars—and is a key supplier to the Belgian army and an approved supplier to NATO and the European Commission. A claim posted on 1 September on a militant website asserted that BATS is "100% Israeli" and owned by Israel Aerospace Industries, arguing that this "suffices to justify reducing Bats to ashes."

The second incident came around 30 September, when the collective claimed an arson attack on a car belonging to Mockel, a firm producing high-precision mechanical components, including for the military sector. In its communiqué, the group said Mockel supplies FN Herstal and John Cockerill Defence.

The third targeted Reul Parts / Reul Sport in Dison, in the Plenesses industrial zone of Liège province, on the night of Saturday 7 to Sunday 8 November. Reul Parts produces off-road vehicles for military use and exhibited at BEDEX, the Belgian arms fair. The group claimed five vehicles were burned, three of them military; local press reported that two vehicles were affected with limited damage, according to director Benoît Reul ([Vedia](https://www.vedia.be/info/dison-un-incendie-se-declare-dans-latelier-automobile-reul-sport-aux-plenesses/214893); [Sudinfo](https://www.sudinfo.be/id1222491/article/2026-11-08/un-incendie-se-declare-au-zoning-des-plenesses-dison-deux-vehicules-ont-pris-feu)). It was described as the collective's third claimed arson in Belgium.

The group also claimed a related action abroad: a fire on 29 October in Switzerland against the firm producing Brügger & Thome riot-control launchers. In its communiqué, circulated via the anarchist media network ([Info Libertaire](https://www.infolibertaire.net/nouvelle-action-incendiaire-contre-la-guerre-et-lindustrie-de-larmement-en-belgique/)), the collective warned firms profiting from war that "making money from people's death will cost you dearly from now on," and issued "a call to all autonomous factions of fire and sabotage."

## Political blame, and a denial

After the BATS fire, Defence Minister Theo Francken (N-VA) moved quickly to attribute the blaze. On X he wrote that "the extreme left is setting fire to the offices of the Liège tech company Bats," adding that the extreme left is "becoming more violent by the day" and that the PvdA—the Flemish branch of the PTB—is "the political arm of this extremist violence."

PTB chairman and federal MP Raoul Hedebouw rejected the accusation outright. "On what is this accusation against the PTB based? On nothing. No facts. No evidence. No link," he said. "The PTB has nothing to do with these fires."

For its part, the Liège prosecutor's office said after the BATS fire that it was "much too early" to say more, with a judicial investigation to establish the circumstances and the perpetrators.

## The Russian-sabotage hypothesis

The intelligence inquiry is reportedly weighing the far-left arson theory—bolstered by the existence of an explicit online claim—against possible Russian involvement. That framing reflects a wider European pattern of suspected Russian hybrid operations targeting defence-industrial sites. Since mid-2026 there has been a series of suspicious fires and incidents at European defence firms and military installations attributed to—or suspected of being—Russian operations using hired "disposable agents," [De Morgen](https://www.demorgen.be/nieuws/wegwerpagenten-saboteren-in-heel-europa-wapenfabrieken~bd528e80/) reported. Similar questions have been raised over incidents at European defence companies and military bases, [Doorbraak](https://doorbraak.be/incidenten-bij-europese-defensiebedrijven-en-militaire-basissen-toeval-of-russische-sabotage) noted.

That backdrop helps explain why investigators have not ruled out a foreign hand even where an anarchist group claims an action.

## What to watch

No culprit has been officially established, and the prosecutor's investigation is ongoing. Several questions remain unresolved: the true nature, size and possible foreign direction of the "Fire Faction" network; the discrepancy over how many vehicles burned in Dison; and whether the three Belgian fires are the coordinated work of one cell or a looser, decentralised milieu.

For Belgium, the stakes extend beyond the individual blazes. The incidents highlight the vulnerability of defence-linked industrial sites—an increasingly sensitive front line as European governments ramp up arms production and confront a widening campaign of suspected hybrid attacks.
