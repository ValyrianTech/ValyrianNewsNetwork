# CrowdStrike to Buy Zscaler in $44B Cybersecurity Mega-Deal

CrowdStrike has agreed to acquire cloud-security company Zscaler in an all-stock transaction valued at roughly $43.9 billion — a deal widely described as $44 billion — creating one of the industry's largest cybersecurity companies, the two firms announced Monday. The merger unites CrowdStrike's dominance in endpoint protection with Zscaler's cloud-based network security and Zero Trust architecture, positioning the combined company to defend enterprises in what both call the "AI era."

According to [AP News](https://apnews.com/article/crowdstrike-zscaler-cybersecurity-ai-artificial-intelligence-cf5a66aaa14f98763eabfb767ff8727c), the deal is the biggest acquisition CrowdStrike has made to date and ranks among the largest in the sector's history. It arrives after a string of high-profile hacks targeting banks, supermarket refrigeration systems, and cryptocurrency platforms that have forced the White House to overhaul its cybersecurity leadership and raised alarms among officials worldwide.

## Deal Terms and Scale

Under the agreement, Zscaler shareholders will receive 1.135 shares of CrowdStrike common stock for each Zscaler share, representing an implied value of approximately $260.41 per share and a 34% premium to Zscaler's closing price on Friday, November 20. Following completion, existing CrowdStrike shareholders are expected to own about 84.1% of the combined company, while Zscaler shareholders will hold approximately 15.9%.

[CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/23/crowdstrike-zscaler-aquisition-ai-cyberattacks.html) reported that the combined company is expected to begin with more than $10 billion in annual recurring revenue (ARR), with Zscaler contributing $3.9 billion of ARR as of its most recent fiscal quarter. CrowdStrike estimates the acquisition will expand its total addressable market by more than $185 billion by calendar year 2029. The deal is expected to close in the second half of CrowdStrike's fiscal year 2028, which begins in February 2027, subject to Zscaler shareholder approval and regulatory clearance.

## Market Reaction: A Tale of Two Stocks

The announcement drew sharply divergent reactions on Wall Street. CrowdStrike shares fell sharply — CNBC reported a 10% drop on Monday, while AP noted the stock tumbled more than 7% before the opening bell. Zscaler shares surged, with AP reporting a 20% jump and CNBC citing a gain of more than 15%. The split reflects investor debate over whether the all-stock deal represents fair value or an overpayment by CrowdStrike, whose own shares had climbed roughly 80% this year as of Monday's open.

As of Friday's close, the two companies' combined market value exceeded $265 billion — still behind the sector leader, Palo Alto Networks, at $282 billion.

## A Strategic Bet on AI-Era Security

According to [CrowdStrike's official announcement](https://ir.crowdstrike.com/news-releases/news-release-details/crowdstrike-acquire-zscaler-bringing-network-security-and-zero), the acquisition moves CrowdStrike into network security, described as cybersecurity's largest single addressable market segment. As users, applications, and AI agents increasingly connect directly to the cloud, legacy firewalls and VPNs built to protect the data center cannot inspect that traffic. Zscaler's Security Service Edge (SSE) and Zero Trust Exchange sit inline between every user, agent, and application to enforce Zero Trust on every connection.

"Security used to be about keeping the adversary out. Today, safety is required to let AI in," CrowdStrike CEO and founder George Kurtz said in a statement. "With Zscaler, we're building cybersecurity's operating system: a single control plane to deploy AI, control AI, and enable any agentic application to run safely. We see a path to become the largest AI safety and security company the world has ever seen."

Zscaler CEO, chairman and founder Jay Chaudhry framed the tie-up as a natural extension of an existing partnership. "In the AI era, Zero Trust security has become non-negotiable, and AI agents are rapidly becoming the new weakest link," he said. "Zscaler and CrowdStrike are complementary and this is the natural evolution of our longstanding partnership." Chaudhry is expected to continue leading the Zscaler business and will join CrowdStrike's board.

The two firms were already closely intertwined, with one of CrowdStrike's largest technology alliances spanning more than 2,000 shared customers and 16 integrations. Roughly 8,000 Zscaler customers are not yet CrowdStrike customers — a significant cross-sell opportunity. Once combined, CrowdStrike will unite endpoint security with Zero Trust SASE, converging device and network telemetry into a single data set to train its AI-driven "SafeMind" security models on an adversary's full attack path.

## Consolidation in a Transforming Industry

The deal caps a wave of consolidation across cybersecurity as firms race to keep pace with AI-powered threats. Google announced plans in 2025 to acquire Wiz for $32 billion, while Cisco shelled out about $28 billion for Splunk in 2024. Palo Alto Networks has also spent billions on cyber and AI businesses, including a $25 billion deal for CyberArk that closed earlier this year.

Cybersecurity experts have warned that artificial intelligence is turbocharging attacks by making it faster and easier to find software flaws. Reports of advanced AI models deploying autonomous agents to break into other organizations have further stoked concern, while recent outages affecting Microsoft, the Federal Aviation Administration, and JPMorgan Chase have pushed businesses to accelerate patching and upgrades of aging systems.

Analysts see the Zscaler deal as a major AI security opportunity. "The addition of ZS to CRWD's platform could bring more breadth in an era of vendor consolidation and, more importantly, network security to position the company more effectively for rising demand for AI Sovereignty," JPMorgan analysts wrote, according to CNBC.

## What's Next

The transaction still requires approval by Zscaler shareholders and regulatory review — no small hurdle given heightened antitrust scrutiny of large technology and security mergers. Jay Chaudhry and his affiliates, representing roughly 34% of Zscaler's outstanding shares, have entered into voting agreements in support of the deal, a strong signal of confidence. Until closing, both companies will continue to operate independently.

The deal was announced alongside better-than-expected quarterly results from both firms: CrowdStrike reported fiscal third-quarter earnings of 34 cents per share on $1.58 billion in revenue, topping estimates, while Zscaler's fiscal first-quarter results also surpassed expectations. For investors and enterprises alike, the coming months will test whether combining the endpoint and the network into a single platform delivers on the promise of a more unified defense — or proves a costly bet in the fast-moving race to secure the AI era.
