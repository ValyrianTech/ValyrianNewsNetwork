# China's Lingsheng Supercomputer Retains Global No.1

China's fully domestic "Lingsheng" (LineShine) supercomputer has held on to the top spot in the latest global TOP500 ranking, extending a lead of roughly 21 percent over the second-place US system, El Capitan, on the benchmark used to judge the world's most powerful computers. The result, confirmed in the November 2026 [TOP500 list](https://www.top500.org/lists/top500/2026/11/), marks the first time a China-based machine has led the list for two consecutive editions in nearly a decade.

## A Record-Breaking Benchmark Run

According to the TOP500 list, LineShine achieved 2.198 exaflops on the HPL benchmark, using 13,789,440 cores — the highest core count of any system on the list. El Capitan, installed at Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory in California, held second place at 1.809 exaflops. LineShine is deployed at the National Supercomputing Center in Shenzhen and was built by the Shenzhen Cloud Computing Center.

The system is the world's first supercomputer to exceed 2 exaflops of sustained double-precision performance, and the first to do so using only CPUs, without the GPU accelerators that underpin the top US and European machines. It first reached No.1 in June 2026, ending El Capitan's reign atop the list, and has now retained the position.

## The 'Glass Room' Story Behind the Win

In a commentary republished by [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/tech/20261123/1a34db3741064122969758bdaf25be7a/c.html), the outlet's People's Daily piece framed the achievement as the product of decades of self-reliance. It recalled the 1980s, when high-performance computers imported into China had to be housed in a transparent room, with keys held by the foreign vendor and all computing materials subject to their approval.

The commentary noted that Chinese systems — Sugon, Tianhe and Sunway — progressively earned China a place among the few nations able to build high-end computers, with Tianhe-2 and Sunway TaihuLight each topping the TOP500. It said the industry then faced "harsh technological blockade" and became "one of the most suppressed technology fields," but did not stall.

Lu Yutong, chief designer of the Lingsheng system and director of the National Supercomputing Center in Shenzhen, said the team had "reconstructed an autonomous, controllable, safe, reliable and sustainably evolvable supercomputing technology development path."

## Full-Stack, GPU-Free Architecture

What distinguishes Lingsheng, according to the commentary, is that its innovation spans the entire chain — chip, network, storage, system software and application ecosystem — rather than a single breakthrough. Instead of adding GPU accelerator cards, the design embeds an AI matrix acceleration unit inside the CPU itself, an approach Chinese media describe as "changing lanes to overtake." The domestic LX2 processor integrates high-bandwidth memory, and the system uses a self-designed high-speed interconnect, tiered storage and full-stack software.

Turing Award laureate Jack Dongarra, quoted by Chinese science outlet Science & Technology Daily, called the system a sign of "the light of hope for a new system architecture leading to AI for Science."

## Beyond the Benchmark

The People's Daily commentary stressed that the real measure of the achievement lies in applications, noting the system has supported 22 "ten-million-core-class" full-machine-scale applications and adapted more than 220 domain applications. Researchers have used it for large-scale training of remote-sensing models and for virtual screening of hundreds of billions of compounds per day in drug discovery.

As [Interesting Engineering](https://interestingengineering.com/science/china-lineshine-worlds-fastest-supercomputer) reported, the 21 percent lead over El Capitan underscores how far China's domestic hardware has advanced despite US export controls on high-performance processors and GPUs aimed at Chinese supercomputing entities.

## What to Watch

While Lingsheng tops the list by raw performance, the United States still dominates the TOP500 by sheer number of systems, and the Chinese entry is the only new major system reported from China this edition. The commentary itself pointed to future challenges: scaling the industrial supply chain, meeting green and efficiency targets, and converting computing power into broader economic advantage — including through the national "East Data, West Computing" initiative. How quickly those applications mature may matter more, in the long run, than the benchmark crown itself.
