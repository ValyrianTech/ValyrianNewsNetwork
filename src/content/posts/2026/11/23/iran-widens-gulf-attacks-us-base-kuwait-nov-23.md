# Iran Widens Gulf Attacks, Striking US Base in Kuwait

Iran widened its military campaign across the Persian Gulf on Wednesday, firing on a US air base in Kuwait and launching multiple attacks on targets in Bahrain in what appeared to be retaliation for an American strike the day before, according to [AP News](https://apnews.com/article/iran-us-kuwait-gulf-attacks-bahrain-b1f0b530cffe951e6fcc1e27b2e1af94). The strikes broke a roughly two-month lull in Iranian attacks on US facilities and landed as American allies in the region scrambled to prevent a return to all-out war.

The early-morning assault on Ali Al Salem Air Base in Kuwait and the barrage against Bahrain marked a significant expansion of a conflict that had largely been confined to exchanges between Washington and Tehran. Gulf states hosting American forces are now directly exposed to a war that regional officials fear could grow more devastating than any previous round.

## A Tit-for-Tat Escalation

The attacks followed a familiar and dangerous sequence. A US fighter jet struck a Revolutionary Guard missile launch site Tuesday on Qeshm Island in the Strait of Hormuz, a US operation that itself followed an Iranian attack on an American radar installation in Bahrain earlier that day, AP reported.

Iran said its recent strikes — including those on other US facilities in Bahrain on Sunday and Friday — were responses to US drone surveillance operations and an attack on Iranian communications towers. In the attack on Ali Al Salem, the Iranian military fired at air defense systems and drone and missile launch platforms, according to a report on Iran's state broadcaster.

Kuwait's military said its air defenses intercepted drone attacks and did not immediately report any damage. The Kuwaiti Foreign Ministry condemned the repeated Iranian attacks as "criminal," saying they threatened the country's security and stability. Bahrain's military said it intercepted a number of attacks from Iran but gave no information on damage or casualties.

The base has been a repeated target. It had been struck at least 30 times by Iran, damaging or destroying at least 15 aircraft hangars, numerous warehouses and 10 buildings near a residential area, according to [an analysis by The Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/iran-war-us-base-damage-trump-26e0f05e01d6f56ff46f7c1a14384f7c).

## Allies Race to Avert a Wider War

The new strikes came as Middle Eastern allies accelerated diplomatic efforts to prevent a resumption of full-scale conflict, even as pressure grew from Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu for the Trump administration to launch a joint air campaign on Iran, regional officials said.

Government and security officials from the Persian Gulf countries, as well as Pakistan and Turkey, have engaged in concerted diplomacy — including phone calls with senior US officials — in a bid to avert renewed war, according to four regional officials briefed on the matter who spoke on condition of anonymity. The goal is to counter Netanyahu's push for the US to restart the war or allow Israel to launch airstrikes on Iran's nuclear facilities and missile capabilities.

American allies in the region have been concerned that a recent visit to the US by Israel's Mossad chief, Roman Gofman, would tilt President Donald Trump's thinking in favor of restarting the war. Gofman, who is close to Netanyahu, met with CIA Director John Ratcliffe in Washington before traveling to Miami for a meeting with US envoy Steve Witkoff. The CIA declined to comment.

Qatar's prime minister, Sheikh Mohammed bin Abdulrahman bin Jassim Al Thani, had met with Trump at the White House the week before in a bid to bring the US and Iran back to the negotiating table.

## The Blockade and Iran's Calculus

The wider war began Feb. 28, 2026, when Israel and the US attacked Iran, killing its supreme leader, Ayatollah Ali Khamenei. Since then, Iran has regularly fired on American facilities and interests in neighboring Gulf countries, as well as energy infrastructure and shipping. Since summer 2026, the US has maintained a naval blockade of Iran's ports, and American military escorts have helped restore the passage of some oil tankers through the Strait of Hormuz.

Yet Iran has continued to attack tankers as they attempt to transit the strait. The United Arab Emirates state-owned oil company ADNOC said Wednesday that one of its tanker crew members was missing after an attack in the strait on Tuesday, while 11 others were injured and receiving medical care.

With its economy suffering under the blockade, experts say the recent attacks suggest Tehran may be growing weary of the status quo. "Iran appears to be pursuing a strategy of deliberate, calibrated escalation," wrote Danny Citrinowicz, a researcher at Israel's Institute for National Security Studies and a former Iran specialist in Israel's military and intelligence services. "The objective is not necessarily to trigger an all-out war, but to steadily increase the military, economic, and political costs of maintaining the blockade."

Others warned of the dangers of the accumulating incidents. "Even if neither side intends to enter a broader confrontation, the accumulation of military incidents across multiple fronts increases the risk of miscalculation and escalation beyond what either side originally intended," wrote Hamidreza Azizi, senior Iran analyst at the International Crisis Group.

## Mixed Signals From Washington

Trump has sent mixed messages on his next steps, saying new strikes are "highly unlikely" but also recently saying the military was "locked and loaded." A US official said the administration's campaign of economic pressure on Iran has been effective to the point that Trump does not need to restart major combat operations. That official said the US remains in close contact with its Gulf allies and has options to ensure Iran does not obtain a nuclear weapon.

Meanwhile, the commander of the US military's Central Command, Adm. Brad Cooper, met Wednesday in Tel Aviv with Israel's military chief of staff, Eyal Zamir, and Gofman, underscoring the close coordination between Washington and Jerusalem even as Gulf capitals press for restraint.

## A Region Bracing for Wider Damage

Iran's Arab neighbors have become increasingly concerned that any new round of fighting would be more devastating than before. Regional officials believe Iran and its allies would retaliate not only against US military facilities but also against other infrastructure, including oil facilities, power and desalination plants, and undersea cables that are essential for financial interactions and communications between the region and the rest of the world.

They also worry that a new round of strikes would prompt Yemen's Iranian-backed Houthi rebels to disrupt traffic through the Bab el-Mandeb Strait, a vital shipping route connecting the Red Sea with the Gulf of Aden. Saudi officials have briefed allies that the conflict with the Houthis could last another six months, according to a diplomat familiar with the matter. The Houthis control a significant swath of Yemen, including the capital of Sanaa, and have made territorial gains along the Red Sea coast, rattling the kingdom with missile and drone attacks targeting Saudi infrastructure, including Riyadh's King Khalid International Airport. But thus far, Trump has resisted the push from his ally for US strikes on the Houthis.

## What to Watch

The coming days will test whether Iran's calibrated strikes remain limited or tip the region back into the all-out war that Gulf diplomats are working desperately to prevent. Two factors loom largest: whether Trump yields to Israeli pressure for a renewed air campaign, and whether Tehran decides the costs of the blockade have grown too high to bear.

For now, the widening geography of the conflict — from the Strait of Hormuz to Kuwait's air bases to the skies over Bahrain — is itself the message. Each new front raises the price of the standoff for every party involved, and each new incident raises the risk that a miscalculation, rather than a decision, becomes the trigger for a broader war.
