# Ohio Baby Shower Shooting Kills 3; Suspect Charged

A baby shower in a Cleveland suburb turned deadly over the weekend when an exchange of gunfire left three people dead and a fourth wounded, and on Monday a 29-year-old man was charged with murder in the attack, according to authorities.

The shooting unfolded around 6 p.m. on Saturday, Nov. 21, at Pandora's Magical Events Venue on the 5300 block of Ridge Road in Parma, Ohio, [The Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/baby-shower-shooting-parma-ohio-0f6cfb9a24747695ba6be1259f5e7f0f) reported, citing Parma police. Responding officers found that four people had been shot during what police described as an "exchange of gunfire." Three were pronounced dead at the scene; a 39-year-old woman was shot in the leg and is expected to survive, with injuries described as non-life-threatening.

## Suspect Charged, Held on $1 Million Bond

On Monday morning, Deangelo Townsend Jr., 29, appeared in Parma Municipal Court, where Judge Timothy Dobeck charged him with murder, ordered him held on $1 million bond, and scheduled a preliminary hearing for Dec. 3, [Cleveland 19](https://www.cleveland19.com/2026/11/21/parma-police-fire-called-out-active-shooting-situation-ridge-road/) reported.

Townsend's attorney entered a self-defense argument, telling the court that the shooting was justified and that Townsend is a cousin of the pregnant woman for whom the shower was held. The attorney also said Townsend has no criminal record, holds a full-time job, and that three of his four children live with him.

The charging decision came roughly 48 hours after the shooting — faster than early wire reports, which had said the man in custody had not yet been charged.

## The Victims

The Cuyahoga County Medical Examiner identified the three people killed as Daviontae D. Pate, 19, of Cleveland; Jayson Lamont Townsend, 45, of Euclid; and Leasia England, 17, according to Cleveland 19.

Police said Pate was the father of the unborn baby for whom the shower was being held, and that Jayson Townsend was the child's maternal grandfather — the expectant mother's father. Authorities have not publicly disclosed England's relationship to the family. The expectant mother was not physically injured.

According to Parma Police public information officer Scott Traxler, Pate was one of the two people involved in the exchange of gunfire. Preliminary information indicated Pate was not invited to the shower, Traxler said, and his arrival was followed by an argument that escalated into the shooting.

## A Quiet Street, a Shocking Scene

Officers secured both firearms used in the shooting and said there was no ongoing danger to the public. In a statement, the department extended its "deepest condolences to the families and loved ones of those who lost their lives in this tragic incident," [Fox News](https://www.foxnews.com/us/three-killed-one-injured-shooting-ohio-baby-shower-police-say) reported.

Neighbors described a chaotic scene on a block that residents say is normally quiet. John Mayhugh, who lives across the street from the venue, told Cleveland 19 he had never seen so many officers.

"I have never seen so many cops — they came out of everywhere," Mayhugh said. "It's sad that a baby shower had to turn into that, spill out onto the streets and shut the road down."

## Investigation Ongoing

Police have characterized the violence as an apparent domestic dispute, but key questions remain unresolved. Investigators have been reviewing surveillance footage and witness statements, and ballistics testing may be needed to establish which firearm fired which fatal rounds — a determination that could be central to assessing the self-defense claim.

At the heart of the case is a question prosecutors will ultimately have to weigh: whether Townsend was the aggressor or was acting in self-defense. Under Ohio law, self-defense can provide a justification for the use of deadly force when a person reasonably believes it is necessary to prevent imminent harm.

## What's Next

Townsend's preliminary hearing on Dec. 3 is expected to clarify how the murder charge advances and how the self-defense argument is treated. Cuyahoga County prosecutors must also decide whether the charge proceeds as filed or whether the justification claim changes the case's trajectory.

For now, a celebration meant to welcome a new child has become the subject of a homicide investigation — and a community across the street is left to make sense of a shooting that, as one neighbor put it, was "really shocking" to find at the end of his street.
