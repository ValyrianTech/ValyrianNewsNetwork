# Hong Kong Completes 1,500-Seat Election Committee Lineup

Hong Kong has finalized the full lineup of its 1,500-member Election Committee, after all 723 contested seats were filled in a subsector general election held on November 22, according to [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/gangao/20261123/5f65397248014477a811144d064fc2e1/c.html). A total of 856 candidates competed across 31 subsectors, with results announced that evening.

The Election Committee is the central pillar of Hong Kong's revamped political architecture. Under Annexes I and II of the Basic Law, it is empowered to elect the Chief Executive, elect 40 of the 90 Legislative Council (LegCo) members, and nominate candidates for both posts.

## How the Committee Is Assembled

The 1,500 members are drawn from five major sectors of 300 seats each: industry, commerce and finance; the professions; grassroots, labour and religious groups; LegCo members and district organizations; and Hong Kong deputies to the national bodies. Membership is formed through three routes — ex-officio appointment, nomination by designated bodies, and direct election.

According to the official results, 341 people were confirmed as validly registered ex-officio members and 156 were validly nominated, while 259 candidates were returned uncontested. The remaining 723 seats were decided by ballot on November 22 — the only portion that required a public vote in this cycle.

A [Global Times report carried by 商业观察网](https://www.sygcnews.com.cn/gat22/130156.jhtml) published during the nomination period noted that the broader 2026 electoral calendar envisions roughly 982 seats filled through balloting, with the remainder determined by ex-officio and designated-body routes. The new committee's five-year term runs from February 1, 2027 to January 31, 2032.

## Lee Hails 'Healthy Competition'

Chief Executive John Lee Ka-chiu issued a statement after the results were announced, saying the election "fully demonstrated healthy competition among patriots who are responsible, capable and effective," and highlighted what he called the "healthy democratic development under the 'patriots governing Hong Kong' principle."

Lee thanked voters for turning out and for "selecting the best among the best," expressing hope that all committee members would uphold the principle and perform their duties to contribute to the steady and enduring practice of "one country, two systems." He also linked the new committee's work to the implementation of Hong Kong's first-ever five-year plan, which aligns the city with the national development blueprint.

The nomination period, which ran from October 7 to October 20, drew strong interest. Authorities received 1,115 candidate nomination forms and 40 designated-body nomination forms, the Global Times report said — roughly 305 forms arrived on the opening day alone. LegCo members Tan Zhenguo and Bu Guoming described the volume of candidates as evidence that "healthy competition has become the norm" in this cycle, with many fresh faces bringing deep industry credentials into the race.

## The Foundation of Hong Kong's Political System

Supporters of the overhauled system frame the Election Committee as foundational infrastructure for governance. Writing in the *Sing Tao Daily*, wholesale and retail sector LegCo member Shiu Ka-fai likened the body to the bedrock of a skyscraper: "a high building rises from the ground — the Election Committee is the foundation of Hong Kong; only when the foundation is solid can it stand firm."

Cai Guanshen, a standing committee member of the national CPPCC and president of the Chinese General Chamber of Commerce, stressed that the election is an important part of implementing "patriots governing Hong Kong" — and argued that members must help translate the national blueprint into momentum for the city. Liu Zhaojia, an adviser to the national association studying Hong Kong and Macao affairs, said the participation of elites and talent would boost public interest and raise expectations for the incoming Chief Executive and LegCo.

## Context: A System Reshaped in 2021

The Election Committee assumed its current form after Hong Kong's 2021 electoral overhaul, which expanded it from 1,200 to 1,500 members and gave it wider powers — notably the ability to elect 40 LegCo seats and to nominate candidates for both the Chief Executive and LegCo. The changes were designed to institutionalize the "patriots governing Hong Kong" principle following the unrest of 2019.

This election is the first of three major contests in the current cycle. In July 2026, Lee said Hong Kong would hold the November Election Committee vote, a Chief Executive election in March 2027, and a District Council election before the end of 2027.

## Analysis and Implications

The completed Election Committee lineup clears the way for the March 2027 Chief Executive race — the most consequential test of the new system to date. Because the committee both nominates and elects the Chief Executive, its composition effectively shapes the field of candidates who can enter the race.

The emphasis officials place on "healthy competition" and broad representation reflects an effort to project legitimacy and effectiveness. Whether that framing resonates with a public whose confidence in institutions has been tested will bear watching as the city moves toward the 2027 vote.

## What's Next

The new Election Committee formally convenes on February 1, 2027, and its first major task will be the Chief Executive election in March. Much of the committee's credibility will hinge on how it performs its nominating and electoral roles — and on whether the broad, sector-spanning representation invoked by officials translates into governance that addresses the practical concerns of residents, from housing to economic opportunity.

For now, the math is settled: all 723 contested seats are filled, and Hong Kong's electoral machinery is primed for its next, higher-stakes test.
