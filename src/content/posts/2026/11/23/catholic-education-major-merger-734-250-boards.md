# Catholic schools plan major merger of 734 boards

The Catholic education network in French-speaking Belgium is preparing a sweeping internal restructuring that would cut the number of its school organising bodies from 734 to 250, [Le Soir reported](https://www.lesoir.be/784293/article/2026-11-23/enseignement-catholique-une-grande-fusion-dans-les-cartons) on Monday. Driven by a crisis in volunteering and mounting financial pressure, the network's umbrella body wants to pool resources and professionalise the management of schools that together educate roughly one pupil in two in the Fédération Wallonie-Bruxelles.

At the heart of the plan are the *pouvoirs organisateurs* (POs) — the local non-profit bodies that administer one or more schools in the free network. They receive subsidies, hire staff, build and maintain buildings, and look after the well-being of pupils and teachers. There are currently 734 of them, and their sizes vary enormously.

## Why the network wants to act now

According to [BX1](https://bx1.be/categories/news/lenseignement-catholique-en-route-pour-une-grande-reforme/), only 25% of POs say they can guarantee their financial survival over the long term. The network's coordinating body, the Secrétariat général de l'enseignement catholique (SeGEC), argues that grouping them would generate savings by pooling the resources currently spent on functions performed by volunteer administrators or school directors.

The stated goal is to professionalise how school boards are run. By mutualising administrative functions, the network hopes to free up money and expertise for the schools themselves while offering teachers greater mobility across the consolidated structure.

The plan is already moving. School directors have been approached, and 20% have begun a voluntary merger path, BX1 reported. The SeGEC is targeting 250 POs within ten years.

## A "boss" rather than a head teacher

Le Soir's report centres on an emblematic case: the Collège Jean XXIII in Woluwe-Saint-Pierre, a cluster of four primary and secondary schools with about 130 staff and some 1,700 pupils. The college is recruiting a *general director* — a role that has existed for four years — with a corporate-style job description calling for a university degree, a "school culture", leadership, and skills in management, human resources, IT, and prevention and safety.

As the paper put it, the college is not recruiting a pedagogical director but "a boss". The shift captures the broader philosophy behind the restructuring: moving away from volunteer, hyper-local governance toward fewer, larger, professionally staffed entities.

## The wider budget debate

The merger push sits inside a larger, more politically charged debate about whether to merge entire school *networks*, not just their organising bodies. That question was explored in a September [RTBF "Parlons Solutions"](https://www.rtbf.be/article/parlons-solutions-comment-sauver-l-ecole-fusionner-les-pouvoirs-organisateurs-c-est-souhaitable-11786545) discussion, where the prevailing view was sceptical about the savings a full network merger could deliver.

Alexandre Lodez, secretary-general of the SeGEC, argued that even handing over buildings would not make a network merger cheaper. "Even if it were given the buildings, it would cost €1,200 more per child… in my commune that would be about €500,000 more in total," he said. The RTBF piece also noted that teaching salaries — €7.9 billion, about 80% of the regional education budget — are not the main cost driver; buildings and operating costs are.

Élisabeth Degryse, president of the Fédération Wallonie-Bruxelles, has described a full network merger as an "illusory objective", insisting the real question is what such a merger would be *for*. The regional government has not officially taken up the network-merger question.

## Precedent for consolidation

Pooling organising bodies is not new, merely smaller in scale. In 2018, the Catholic schools of Flémalle, Grâce-Hollogne, Horion-Hozémont and Seraing merged their boards into a single organising body covering 13 schools and 2,700 pupils, [RTBF reported](https://www.rtbf.be/article/fusion-des-pouvoirs-organisateurs-de-13-ecoles-liegeoises-9813648) at the time. The new body's president, Philippe Thiry, framed it as a matter of professionalisation: the structure allowed the network to create six cross-cutting units for buildings, finance, teaching and pastoral care, staffed by specialists serving every school.

## What to watch

The restructuring raises difficult questions. How voluntary will "voluntary" mergers remain for POs that are financially unviable? How will the identity and autonomy of parish-rooted schools be preserved inside larger entities? And what happens to the roughly two-thirds of POs that sit above the target of 250?

The plan also intersects with a wider education-budget crisis and ongoing social tension in the sector — including a national demonstration in Brussels on the very day the restructuring was reported. For a network that educates half of French-speaking Belgium's pupils, how it answers those questions will shape school life for years to come.
