# Belgian Coalition Splits Again Without Budget Deal

Belgium's federal coalition government broke up again on Monday morning without reaching an agreement on the 2027 budget, leaving the country without a spending plan as a months-long political standoff drags on. Prime Minister Bart De Wever (N-VA) travelled to the palace to report to King Filip but, according to [HLN](https://www.hln.be/binnenland/regering-opnieuw-uiteen-zonder-begrotingsakkoord-de-wever-bij-koning-maar-zal-geen-ontslag-aanbieden~a9cb0fe2/), will not offer his government's resignation.

The decision to stay on marks a reversal from 2 November, when De Wever did tender his resignation — which the King held *in beraad* and ultimately refused, sending the prime minister back to the negotiating table. This is his fourth report to the monarch this month.

## A Coalition Fracturing Along One Fault Line

The so-called "Arizona" coalition — named for the colour of its parties' logos and made up of N-VA, MR, Les Engagés, CD&V and Vooruit — has been deadlocked over the budget for weeks. The structural tension is built in: Vooruit is the only left-of-centre party in a broadly right-leaning five-party government, and it has become the lone holdout.

The inner cabinet (*kernkabinet*) of deputy prime ministers convened at 9:30 a.m. at the prime minister's office on the Wetstraat and broke up without a deal before 11:30 a.m. Vice-Premier David Clarinval (MR) said Vooruit had blocked the talks and "did not accept the equilibriums," claiming that Health Minister Frank Vandenbroucke (Vooruit) had even demanded apologies at the negotiating table.

The central sticking point remains the contribution of what Belgian politics calls the "broad shoulders" — high earners and wealth. De Wever's latest proposal reportedly revisited the *effectentaks* (securities tax), currently levied on accounts above €1 million. Under one option, all of a single person's securities accounts would be aggregated to prevent avoidance by spreading assets across accounts, and the threshold could be lowered.

## Tensions Boil Over at the Table

The morning was further inflamed by Vice-Premier Maxime Prévot (Les Engagés), who posted an Instagram image labelling Vandenbroucke "FAKE NEWS," accusing him of lying about a supposed *indexsprong* (index jump) affecting teachers' purchasing power. Prévot's spokesperson later insisted the post targeted disinformation circulating on Francophone social media and was "not a personal attack" on Vandenbroucke.

Budget Minister Vincent Van Peteghem (CD&V) struck a more conciliatory note on arrival, saying he hoped "there is now enough courage among everyone to actually keep working" toward a deal. The rupture came against the backdrop of an N-VA party congress on Sunday, where De Wever warned that "these times demand not pyromaniacs but firefighters."

## The Fiscal Stakes

The stakes extend well beyond party politics. Belgium's deficit ran at roughly 5% of GDP last year — well above the EU's 3% ceiling — and De Wever has spoken of a package of around €10 billion now under negotiation, with a further €20 billion in savings needed to bring the debt ratio down.

Because no budget has passed, the country is governing on provisional twelfths (*voorlopige twaalfden*) for 2027. The first tranche covering January to March was approved on 20 November. On the same day, credit rating agency Fitch kept Belgium at A+, but cautioned that the debt could snowball toward 2030 if reforms stall — while noting that "complex, long-lasting policy discussions are not unusual in Belgium."

## What's Next

By not resigning, De Wever keeps the government alive rather than triggering snap elections, meaning the negotiation continues. But there is little sign of a quick breakthrough: reporting suggests Vooruit intends to push matters to the very end of the deadline, an "attrition battle" that could stretch into next week. Vlaams Belang and others are already calling for early elections.

For now, Belgium remains a country without a budget, its government intact but gridlocked — and its prime minister buying yet more time from the palace.
