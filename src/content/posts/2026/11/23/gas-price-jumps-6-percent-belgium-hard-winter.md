# Gas Price Jumps 6% as Belgium Braces for a Hard Winter

The European gas price shot up nearly 6 percent on the morning of 23 November 2026 to around 93 euros per megawatt-hour, driven by early winter cold, weak wind output and unusually low reserves. Energy experts warn the benchmark could breach the symbolic 100-euro mark within days — and that Belgian households should prepare for a fresh shock to their energy bills, [Het Laatste Nieuws reported](https://www.hln.be/binnenland/gasprijs-stijgt-6-procent-experts-vrezen-moeilijke-winter-mogelijk-komende-dagen-al-boven-symbolische-grens-van-100-euro-per-megawattuur~aab0b386/).

## A price three times higher than a year ago

The 23 November spike is not an isolated event but the latest surge in a year of turmoil. Before the United States and Israel went to war with Iran in February 2026, European gas traded below 30 euros per megawatt-hour. Since then, strikes on key refineries and liquefied natural gas (LNG) infrastructure in the Middle East have sharply reduced exports from the region, and the European benchmark climbed past 87 euros on 13 October before [crossing 100 euros per megawatt-hour on 28 October](https://nos.nl/artikel/2637039-gasprijs-stijgt-door-boven-de-100-euro-per-megawattuur), the highest level since the 2022 gas crisis.

After a brief dip in early November, prices are climbing again. At roughly 93 euros per megawatt-hour, gas now costs about three times what it did a year earlier — a fact that has unsettled households across Belgium and its neighbours.

## Why reserves are so thin

The central problem, experts say, is storage. Belgium's gas reserves stood at just 67.3 percent of capacity — 5.12 of 7.61 terawatt-hours of working volume — on 11 November, according to [gasvoorraden.nl](https://gasvoorraden.nl/land/belgie). Europe-wide, reserves were around 73 percent, the lowest level since records began in 2011 and below even the crisis year of 2021.

"The European gas reserve is far too low for this time of year," energy expert Kenneth Vansina of comparison site Mijnenergie.be told HLN. Gas trader Matthias Detremmerie echoed the concern, and both experts warned that a sustained cold snap or a further loss of Gulf supply could push prices past 100 euros per megawatt-hour in the coming days.

The cold is already biting. Early winter weather has lifted heating demand while low wind speeds have curbed renewable generation, leaving gas to fill the gap at exactly the moment stockpiles are thinnest.

## What it means for household bills

How quickly the spike reaches Belgian households depends on the type of energy contract they hold. Under a dynamic contract, prices track the wholesale market almost daily; under a variable contract, they adjust every few months; and under a fixed contract, they hold until the deal expires.

Roughly 8 percent of households have a dynamic contract, about 37 percent a variable one and 54 percent a fixed one. Variable gas tariffs in Belgium already rose by an estimated 23 percent from 1 November, following October's peak, meaning many families are absorbing higher costs just as winter sets in.

Vansina has been warning households for weeks not to wait. "We received strikingly many worried phone calls in recent days from people wondering what they should do," he said in late October. "My advice is clear: don't wait any longer to switch to a fixed contract."

## A structural, not a seasonal, squeeze

Analysts stress that the current situation is severe but not a repeat of 2022, when prices peaked near 340 euros per megawatt-hour after Russia's invasion of Ukraine. European gas consumption has fallen and supply has diversified, which has cushioned the blow.

Yet the underlying fragility is structural. Europe has swapped dependence on Russian pipeline gas for heavy reliance on seaborne LNG, much of it from the United States and the Gulf, exposing it to global competition. Europe and Asia are again bidding against each other for scarce cargoes, and US gas remains several times cheaper than the European benchmark — a gap that reflects the continent's limited alternatives.

Some experts argue the deeper lesson is to use less gas altogether. "Move away from natural gas and switch to renewable electricity," energy expert Kris Voorspools urged earlier this year, pointing to a long-term shift that would reduce Europe's exposure to volatile import markets.

## What to watch

The immediate question is whether the 100-euro threshold is breached again in the coming days — and how long the cold, low-wind spell lasts. Much also hinges on whether Gulf LNG flows stabilise or worsen amid an unresolved conflict.

For Belgian households, the practical window is narrowing. Those still on variable or dynamic contracts face the steepest exposure, while fixed-contract holders will feel the pinch only when their deals come up for renewal. With reserves thin and winter just beginning, the pressure on both bills and budgets looks set to build well before spring.
