# China Fiscal Revenue Up 4.9%, 'People' Spending Outpaces

China's national general public budget revenue reached 19.57 trillion yuan in the first ten months of 2026, up 4.9% year-on-year, while tax revenue grew faster than the headline figure and spending on livelihoods and human capital climbed at a quicker pace, according to data released by the Ministry of Finance on November 20 and reported by [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/fortune/20261123/caac6a4a6f3e42f8b6ec7b913b412243/c.html).

The figures paint a structural story rather than a volume one: revenue growth is increasingly anchored in taxes tied to real economic activity, and expenditure is tilting toward social security, health and childcare—consistent with Beijing's "invest in people" (投资于人) policy push that favors human-capital outlays over infrastructure-heavy stimulus.

## Revenue Quality Improves

Of the 19.57 trillion yuan in total revenue, national tax revenue accounted for 16.26 trillion yuan, growing 6%—outpacing overall revenue growth of 4.9%. Non-tax revenue, by contrast, fell 0.2%. That "one up, one down" (一升一降) pattern, as detailed in the [Ministry of Finance release](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_34521489), signals that revenue growth now depends more on taxes linked to economic activity rather than on fees, fines and asset sales.

"In Jan–Oct, tax revenue growth outpaced overall revenue growth, while non-tax revenue turned to a 0.2% decline," said Li Xuhong, vice president of the Beijing National Accounting Institute, in the Xinhua report. "This 'one up, one down' shows revenue growth relies more on tax revenue connected to economic development; revenue quality and the tax-fee structure continued to improve, providing a more solid foundation for fiscal sustainability."

By region, Eastern China revenue rose 3%, Central China 1%, Western China 4.3% and Northeast China 2.4%—with most regions continuing to grow.

## Taxes Reflect an Economy in Motion

The tax breakdown maps onto activity across the economy. Domestic value-added tax rose 5.3%, driven by continued industrial and service-sector growth and an uptick in producer prices. Import VAT and consumption tax jumped 13.2% on faster import growth, while corporate income tax rose 5.9% on stronger profits.

Two lines stand out. Personal income tax climbed 22.9% overall—including a striking 89.6% rise in October alone—which authorities attributed partly to strengthened collection of personal income tax on offshore trusts. Securities transaction stamp duty surged 54.1%, a direct reflection of active capital-market trading. Export VAT and consumption tax refunds totaled 1.96 trillion yuan, 144.2 billion yuan more than a year earlier, supporting exporters.

## Spending Tilts Toward 'Investing in People'

National general public budget expenditure reached 22.8 trillion yuan, up 1%. Beneath the modest headline, the composition tells the story: social security and employment spending rose 4.8% and health spending rose 7.8%—the latter driven mainly by disbursement of childcare subsidies. Housing security spending rose 2.9% and education 1.4%, while science and technology spending grew just 0.5%.

"The spending structure continues to optimize, and the 'invest in people' orientation is more pronounced," Li said.

The central-local split is equally telling. Central-level expenditure grew 5.4% while local-level spending edged up just 0.2%—reflecting Beijing's intent to bear more of the spending burden and ease pressure on debt-laden local governments.

## A Modest, Selective Expansion

The overall posture is more deliberate than aggressive. According to [Securities Times coverage](https://finance.eastmoney.com/a/202611213936556114.html) carried by Eastmoney, the 4.9% revenue growth was 0.2 percentage points slower than in the first three quarters, even as the cumulative figure stayed positive. Total expenditure growth of just 1% is similarly restrained.

The "people-first" tilt, moreover, is concentrated rather than broad: health and social security grow robustly, but science and technology (+0.5%), education (+1.4%) and local spending (+0.2%) remain soft. With spending at roughly 76% of the full-year budget after ten months, market analysts expect the fiscal authorities to accelerate disbursement in the fourth quarter. Guangfa Securities macro analyst Wu Qiying noted that incremental tools are now in place—including 550 billion yuan in local government debt quota and completed policy-financing instruments—and that spending should pick up.

## What to Watch

Analysts frame the period around three features: "higher-quality taxes, livelihood priority, and the center stepping up," which they say corroborates the broader economic recovery. Li called for continued implementation of a "more proactive fiscal policy" alongside faster development of a local tax system, balancing growth support, livelihood protection and risk control.

Three questions will shape the narrative into year-end and the 2027 budget: whether October's revenue deceleration hints at softer momentum; how the offshore-trust tax enforcement affects high-net-worth capital behavior; and whether the childcare-subsidy surge proves a one-off disbursement or a sustained structural commitment. The answers will determine whether China's fiscal shift from quantity to quality holds through the year ahead.
