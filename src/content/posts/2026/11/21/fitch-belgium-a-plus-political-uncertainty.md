# Fitch Keeps Belgium at A+ but Warns on Political Risk

Ratings agency Fitch confirmed Belgium's long-term sovereign credit rating at A+ with a stable outlook on Friday, but attached a pointed warning about the country's "high political uncertainty" as a federal budget standoff drags on, [RTBF reported](https://www.rtbf.be/article/fitch-maintient-la-note-belge-a-a-mais-epingle-une-incertitude-politique-elevee-11818867). The decision completes a sequence of 2026 reviews in which all three major agencies held Belgium's ratings in place while signalling a deteriorating fiscal trajectory.

Fitch grounded the rating in Belgium's "diversified and prosperous economy," per-capita income and governance indicators well above the median for peers rated "A," and its euro-area membership. Those strengths, the agency said, are "counterbalanced by very high and rising public debt, as well as by a political and institutional context that complicates fiscal consolidation efforts."

## A recurring Belgian pattern

Fitch was careful to frame the impasse as familiar rather than fatal. "Complex, interminable political debates are not rare in Belgium," the agency noted, adding that "an equally tense impasse last year also ended up being resolved, and we expect a similar outcome this time." Yet it named a specific trigger that could worsen the picture: "the prospect of a faster rise in public debt relative to GDP than our current forecast, for example due to a failure to agree on fiscal consolidation measures."

Belgium's debt level is among the highest of countries in the "A" category, and Fitch reiterated the risk of a "snowball effect" from 2030, when rising interest costs could outpace growth.

## The fiscal picture behind the warning

The warning lands against a difficult backdrop. Belgium posted an overall government deficit of 5.2% of GDP last year — the highest in the eurozone — with public debt exceeding 107% of GDP, according to data from the Central Economic Council cited on the same day by [La Libre](https://www.lalibre.be/dernieres-depeches/2026/11/20/le-cce-pointe-des-finances-publiques-belges-preoccupantes-et-adresse-ses-recommandations-G5KRH4ZJ75HAPDEKQ4RLFNBAMU/). The European Commission projects debt could climb above 137% of GDP by 2036 at unchanged policy.

Fitch acknowledged reforms aimed at controlling social spending — on unemployment, the return to work of the sick, and pensions — but flagged that the personal income tax reform, combined with higher interest charges, will push the deficit up again. On growth, it warned about energy prices, to which Belgium is unusually exposed because of its chemicals and pharmaceuticals industry, and about wage indexation, which could erode any recovery in wage competitiveness.

## Same day, same message on the budget

Adding to the symbolism, the federal government approved the first tranche of provisional twelfths for January, February and March 2027 on the same day, [La DH reported](https://www.dhnet.be/actu/belgique/2026/11/20/crise-budhetaire-le-gouvernement-federal-approuve-la-premiere-tranche-des-douziemes-provisoires-DUJVXDG4EVACRKUYHQYD2VOQFM/). The mechanism lets the state operate without a full budget — releasing one-twelfth of the previous year's spending each month — and its use means Belgium will again run on stopgap financing into 2027. Budget Minister Vincent Van Peteghem (CD&V) was tasked with presenting the tranche to the Chamber.

The rating review also completes a calendar of 2026 verdicts: Moody's maintained Belgium's rating on 10 October, and S&P kept it at AA- with a stable outlook on 24 October. Fitch's A+ sits a notch below S&P's assessment, but the collective message from the agencies is one of a trajectory under strain rather than an imminent downgrade.

## De Wever's warning to his coalition

Prime Minister Bart De Wever seized on the review as a lever in the standoff. "Given the efforts this government has already made, Fitch decided to maintain, for now, the rating of our country at a stable level," he said. "But the warning that accompanies it is clear: if the train of reforms stops, the rating will fall and interest rates will rise. We cannot rest on our laurels. I hope everyone will fully grasp the measure of this warning."

The political context is tense. The "Arizona" coalition — N-VA, MR, Les Engagés, CD&V and Vooruit — has been locked over the 2027 budget, with Vooruit demanding contributions from "the broadest shoulders," i.e. the wealthiest. De Wever offered his resignation to King Philippe in early November; the King refused and set reporting deadlines, granting one more week on 16 November. On Friday, De Wever and Vooruit president Conner Rousseau met for three hours, [according to RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/budget-federal-conner-rousseau-et-bart-de-wever-se-sont-entretenus-pendant-trois-heures-ce-vendredi-11818982), as the prime minister prepares another report to the King on 23 November.

Foreign Minister Maxime Prévot (Les Engagés) pointed to the substance at stake: "Yesterday, there was nearly €1.2 billion on the table — nearly 12% of the budget effort — that was going to be borne by the richest 5% of the population."

## What to watch

The immediate question is whether De Wever can deliver a budget agreement by his 23 November report to the King. A deal would reinforce the prime minister's argument that Belgium reforms when pressed and would ease the pressure Fitch has flagged; failure could trigger a government crisis, raise borrowing costs and push the debt trajectory closer to the scenario the agency warns against. Fitch's own expectation of a "similar outcome" to last year's impasse offers the government a measure of reassurance — but the agency has now made clear, in writing, what it will be watching.
