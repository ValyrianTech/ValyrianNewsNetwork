The coastal municipality of Knokke-Heist is turning to an emergency plan to guarantee beach bars on its sand for summer 2027, after a legal defeat this autumn forced it to scrap all 25 of its beach concessions. Under the new approach, the town will lose four beach bars, a children's park and roughly 400 beach cabins, [VRT NWS reported](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/21/knokke-noodplan-strandbars-zomer-2027/).

The municipality said it is "doing everything to have beach establishments on the beach again next summer and to offer maximum legal certainty to all involved," according to a press release quoted by VRT NWS. It will launch a sharply simplified market consultation procedure and apply the rules of the provincial spatial implementation plan (PRUP) for the beach and dike to the letter.

## How the process collapsed

The trouble dates to late August 2026, when Knokke-Heist awarded 25 beach concessions for 2027-2035 under a public tender driven by European rules. The award left six established operators without a spot, and controversy followed swiftly.

On 23 October, Belgium's Council of State [suspended the awards](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/10/23/strandbars-geschorst-knokke-heist/) in the Knokke, Het Zoute and Duinbergen zones, vindicating four operators — Lekkerbek Beach, Blue Buddha, Albertstrand and Yssis Beach. The Council's ruling cited three main problems. It found the town had not examined "with due care" whether the exceptionally high concession fees offered by some candidates were realistic and feasible. It also flagged concepts that appeared to breach urban planning rules, and questioned the nine-year concession terms, renewable for up to 18 years, saying the town had not justified such long periods.

Only the Heist zone awards initially survived. But on 29 October the town withdrew all 25 concessions — including those in Heist — effectively resetting the entire dossier to zero and ruling out a beach bar opening in time for Easter 2027.

## Strict application of the PRUP

The cuts now announced flow directly from adhering strictly to the provincial spatial plan. Under the PRUP, beach bars, children's parks, beach cabins and event zones together may occupy no more than half the length of the central recreation beach.

That beach runs roughly five kilometres, from the Baai van Heist to the Directeur Generaal Willemspark, between the watersport clubs Anemos and River Woods. That means about 2.5 kilometres of the busiest stretch must remain open and publicly accessible.

The result is a reduction from 24 beach bar parks to 20, plus one children's park — a measured retreat that the municipality frames as the price of legal certainty.

"We realise this hits everyone involved hard, especially current operators," said Bert De Brabandere, alderman for the beach from the Inzicht party, in comments carried by VRT NWS. "But we want beach bars on our beach again next summer. The PRUP says at least half of the busiest part of the beach must remain publicly accessible. We are now applying that strictly."

## A tight timeline

The path to a rebuilt beach season is narrow and conditional. This month, the municipal council must approve amended rules for sub-concessions. Before the Christmas holidays, the college of aldermen is to approve new concession guidelines. Early in 2027, candidates submit proposals and the college awards sub-concessions; in spring, operators apply for environmental permits. Only then can bars be rebuilt — provided, the town cautions, that no fresh procedures derail the process.

The town's current concessions expire on 31 December 2026 and cannot be extended. It rejected a one-year stopgap because each operator would need a new environmental permit, which cannot be granted for structures that breach the PRUP.

## What to watch

Several questions hang over the dossier. It is not yet clear how the roughly 400 cabin holders will be compensated or relocated, nor how space will be allocated if demand outstrips supply. The beach cabin question is treated separately, with new rules from 2027 limiting each address to a single cabin and banning subletting.

There is also the risk of renewed litigation: any losing candidate could return to the Council of State and again push the opening past summer 2027. And because the PRUP is a provincial instrument for West Flanders, the strict application could ripple to other Belgian coastal towns, though that remains unconfirmed.

For now, Knokke-Heist has chosen legal certainty over abundance — a smaller, more compliant beach that it hopes will reopen on time.
