# De Wever, Rousseau Talk 3 Hours as Trust Hangs in Balance

Belgian Prime Minister Bart De Wever and Vooruit leader Conner Rousseau spoke for roughly three hours on Friday evening, in the most significant attempt yet to break a weeks-long deadlock over the federal budget. But according to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/liveblog/de-wever-en-rousseau-hebben-elkaar-3-uur-lang-gesproken-niet-du~1793247532380/), nothing is known about the content of the conversation, and it remains unclear whether trust between the two has been restored.

## A Coalition at an Impasse

The meeting comes after the budget negotiations collapsed entirely on Sunday 15 November over a proposed austerity package of roughly 10 billion euros. Within the inner cabinet, Vooruit was the only one of the five coalition parties unwilling to continue working on the basis of the proposed table.

The Arizona coalition brings together five parties: De Wever's N-VA, Vooruit, CD&V, MR and Les Engagés. The atmosphere soured further in the days that followed, largely due to television interviews and social media posts from Vooruit. VRT NWS reported that Deputy Prime Minister Frank Vandenbroucke's appearance on the current affairs programme *Terzake* on 16 November in particular caused deep resentment among coalition partners.

During the past week, De Wever held discreet contacts with the four other parties, but not with Vooruit, deliberately keeping the socialist party waiting. On Friday, Rousseau confirmed there had been contact with N-VA chairwoman Valerie Van Peel and said he was awaiting an invitation from the prime minister. That invitation ultimately took the form of the direct three-hour conversation that evening.

## The Fight Over "Broad Shoulders"

At the heart of the conflict is the contribution demanded of the so-called *sterke schouders* — the "broad shoulders" of high earners and large fortunes. Vooruit is demanding a substantial contribution from millionaires and large assets, targeting people with more than 1 million euros in financial assets, excluding their home.

De Wever saw a possible compromise in lowering the threshold for the securities tax from 1 million to 750,000 euros — an idea Vooruit has so far rejected. The party also objects to the tax advantages enjoyed by top footballers, a benefits package reportedly worth 253 million euros, which it says has been off the table in talks. Other savings proposals that drew resistance included cuts to the indexation of wages and pensions and savings in healthcare.

Speaking on Friday, Rousseau framed his party's position as a matter of fairness. "We are asking for something very reasonable," he said, according to VRT NWS. "We will remain reasonable too. But that works both ways. We do not want to accept just anything. If we had wanted an N-VA agreement, I would have bought an N-VA membership card."

Van Peel pushed back, insisting the deal on the table was already fair. "What is on the table today is indeed a just agreement. That is what four of the five parties say," she said. "We are standing on the edge of an abyss, and every politician must now realise that."

## Economic Pressure Mounts

The political standoff is unfolding against a deteriorating fiscal backdrop. On 17 November, the European Commission projected that Belgium's budget deficit would rise to 5.5 percent of GDP in 2027 and 5.7 percent in 2028 under unchanged policy, with public debt climbing from 111.2 percent of GDP in 2026 to 115.8 percent in 2028. Only France, Italy and Greece would carry a larger debt burden. Because no federal budget agreement exists, the Commission could only factor in regional measures.

On Friday, credit rating agency Fitch kept Belgium's rating at A+, citing the country's diversified economy and describing complex, protracted policy discussions as not unusual in Belgium. But it warned of a "debt snowball" by 2030 if action is not taken in time.

"The warning attached to this is clear: if the reform train stalls, the rating will fall and interest rates will rise," De Wever said in response.

With no 2027 budget in place, the government also approved the first tranche of provisional twelfths covering January to March, allowing the state to keep running on a monthly twelfth of this year's budget. Budget Minister Vincent Van Peteghem was tasked with submitting the tranche to the Chamber of Representatives.

## What to Watch

De Wever must report to King Filip again on Monday 23 November — already the fourth time this month the prime minister has been summoned to the palace. A trade union demonstration is also planned in Brussels the same day.

So far, all parties say they want to keep negotiating and are not pushing for the government to fall or for new elections. But there is still no concrete schedule for the coming days, and no invitation to a new inner cabinet has been sent. The three-hour conversation may have reopened a channel between the two key figures — but whether it rebuilt the trust needed to close a 10-billion-euro gap remains, for now, an open question.
