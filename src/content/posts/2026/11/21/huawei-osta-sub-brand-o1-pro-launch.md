# Huawei Launches AI Phone Sub-Brand OSTA From 5,499 Yuan

Huawei on Friday unveiled OSTA, its first new smartphone sub-brand since it sold off Honor six years ago, launching an AI-focused handset priced from 5,499 yuan ($825) as it moves to reshape its premium lineup around artificial intelligence. The O1 Pro, the brand's debut device, runs Huawei's HarmonyOS operating system and tops out at 8,799 yuan ($1,318) for its highest-specification version, with the announced prices applying through Dec. 31, according to [Caixin Global](https://www.caixinglobal.com/2026-11-21/huawei-launches-ai-focused-smartphone-sub-brand-osta-102507371.html).

The launch marks Huawei's return to a multi-brand smartphone strategy for the first time since November 2020, when U.S. sanctions cut off its access to advanced chips and forced it to sell Honor to a Shenzhen government-backed consortium. The decision carries historical weight: under its old Huawei-plus-Honor strategy, Huawei dominated China's handset market, shipping 124.9 million units in 2020 for a 38.3% share — roughly the combined volume of the No. 2 through No. 4 players, per IDC data cited by [Guancha](https://www.guancha.cn/CaiJing/2026_11_20_909334.shtml).

## An 'AI-native' flagship, not a price fighter

Richard Yu, chairman of Huawei's consumer business group, framed OSTA as the company's answer to the AI era, drawing an explicit parallel to the e-commerce brand Huawei built during the mobile-internet age. The brand aims to "become a pioneering technology brand favored by consumers in the AI era," Yu said. The OSTA acronym itself stands for Original, Smart, Technology and Accessible.

At the center of the O1 Pro is an on-screen AI assistant called "Xiao Xingmang," built on Huawei's Celia platform. The company claims the phone connects more than 2,100 system capabilities and over 500 ecosystem skills — including Alipay, JD, Taobao and Xiaohongshu — allowing users to orchestrate services through natural-language conversation and, in some cases, bypass app navigation entirely. A "Xiao Yi Space" memory base with a four-tier progressive architecture is designed to retain long-term user habits.

The device ships with HarmonyOS 7 "AI Pioneer Edition" and a Kirin 9030S chip. Its hardware leans on a distinctive "wide slab" form factor with a 6.39-inch 1.5K LTPS OLED display in a 16:9.5 aspect ratio, a 96.1% screen-to-body ratio and 6,500 nits of peak local brightness. Cameras include a 200-megapixel 1/1.28-inch main sensor, a 50-megapixel 3.7x periscope telephoto and a second-generation "Red Maple" spectral lens. A 7,000 mAh silicon-anode battery supports 66W wired and 50W wireless charging, and the phone carries IP68 and IP69 water-resistance ratings, according to [Huawei's official product page](https://consumer.huawei.com/cn/osta/phones/o1-pro/).

Crucially, the O1 Pro is a close hardware sibling of Huawei's Pura X View. The two share the same Kirin 9030S chip, 6.39-inch 120Hz OLED wide screen, 7,000 mAh battery, 200MP main camera and side fingerprint sensor. But the OSTA model drops the Beidou satellite short-messaging feature, downgrades the panel from LTPO to LTPS, and loses an 8MP ultrawide — while matching most of its sibling's core specifications and, most notably, carrying no Huawei logo on its back.

## Why competitors breathed a sigh of relief

Before the launch, market expectations were that OSTA would inherit Honor's old positioning: no Huawei badge, a focus on the 3,000–5,000 yuan value segment, and a starting price likely under 4,000 yuan. Instead, the O1 Pro landed at 5,499 to 8,799 yuan, aligning with — not undercutting — mainstream Android flagships. OPPO's Find X10 and vivo's X500 both start at 5,499 yuan, while Xiaomi's 18 Pro begins at 5,999 yuan. The O1 Pro's entry price even sits 700 yuan above the adjusted Honor Magic8.

That pricing reflects a market transformed by a global memory super-cycle. According to TrendForce data cited by Guancha, conventional DRAM contract prices rose 90%–95% quarter-on-quarter in early 2026, with NAND flash up 55%–60%. A 12GB+256GB memory package reportedly climbed from about 500 yuan a year earlier to roughly 2,200 yuan — the first time in smartphone history that memory cost more than the processor. Richard Yu himself acknowledged that memory price hikes added over $200 in cost per phone.

The broader market shows a classic "volume down, price up" pattern. China's smartphone shipments fell 15.4% year-on-year in the third quarter of 2026 to about 57.9 million units — the sixth consecutive quarterly decline — even as the premium segment above $600 expanded its share from 31.1% to 38.3%. Huawei was the biggest beneficiary, with shipments up 17.3% to about 12.2 million units and a 21.1% share, the only top vendor with double-digit growth, IDC data shows.

## The logo question and a scale bet

Perhaps the most consequential detail is what the O1 Pro lacks: the Huawei name. At prices above 5,000 yuan, brand premium is a hard component of product competitiveness. Buyers of the similarly specced Pura X View are paying for a widescreen plus the Huawei brand plus HarmonyOS — a three-in-one certainty. OSTA keeps the screen, chip and system but removes the brand pillar, leaving a zero-awareness new name to justify flagship pricing.

That gap is hard to quantify in benchmarks but decisive at the point of purchase. Building trust, channels and reputation for a brand-new label will take time, and the AI narrative has yet to prove it can independently drive upgrades. Industry surveys suggest more than 70% of users turned off most AI features six months after buying an AI phone, and IDC China research manager Guo Tianxiang has noted that agent phones remain constrained by fragmented ecosystems.

Yet Huawei is clearly betting on scale rather than a clearance experiment. JD.com and Huawei OSTA signed a strategic cooperation agreement targeting 70 billion yuan ($10.5 billion) in all-category sales on JD.com over the next three years — a signal that OSTA is meant to grow into a significant business.

## What's next

The strategic intent appears less about igniting a price war than about testing AI-native experiences and the distinctive wide-slab form factor at the mid-to-high end, protecting Huawei's Mate and Pura flagships rather than cannibalizing them. Unlike the Honor era, when self-developed Kirin chips gave Huawei extraordinary pricing freedom, today's flagship silicon must first serve the high-end narrative, so OSTA benefits from cost amortization through shared mainboards and chips — not from a disposable pricing weapon.

The open question is whether Huawei will eventually push OSTA down-market into the 3,000–4,000 yuan tier, which would intensify pressure on Honor, Xiaomi, OPPO and vivo. For now, competitors can breathe easy. But the first-sales data — and whether a phone without the Huawei logo can command a flagship price in a shrinking market — will determine how long that relief lasts.
