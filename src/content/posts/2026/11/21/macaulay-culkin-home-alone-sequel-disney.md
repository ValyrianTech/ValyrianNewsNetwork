# Macaulay Culkin to Return in New 'Home Alone' Sequel

Bad news for the Wet Bandits: Kevin McCallister is coming home. More than three decades after he last played the role, Macaulay Culkin is set to reprise his signature character in a new "Home Alone" sequel in early development at Disney, [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/pop-culture/pop-culture-news/macaulay-culkin-set-return-kevin-mccallister-home-alone-sequel-rcna610574) confirmed.

The project will be written and produced by Seth Rogen and his longtime partner Evan Goldberg through their Point Grey banner, according to people with knowledge of the plan. Disney declined to comment, and no director, production start or release date has been announced.

## How the Sequel Came Together

The idea, notably, originated with Culkin himself. [Deadline](https://deadline.com/2026/11/home-alone-macaulay-culkin-seth-rogen-evan-goldberg-1237230258/) reported that the actor pitched his concept directly to Disney Motion Pictures chief David Greenbaum over the summer of 2026, after online chatter about a possible return, framing the story from "an older dad's POV." Digital outlet Puck first broke news of the project on Nov. 20, 2026.

The premise echoes remarks Culkin made during an onstage conversation with "Home Alone" director Chris Columbus at the Academy Museum in December 2025, timed to the film's 35th anniversary. There, he sketched out a sequel in which Kevin is a father himself.

"He's raising his kid and they don't really get along, he's working all the time," Culkin said, [according to The Hollywood Reporter](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-news/macaulay-culkin-home-alone-chris-columbus-sequel-new-movie-1236450093/). "There's one of two ways you can do it. One, he actually leaves the kid behind by mistake; he calls up his mom like, 'So sorry, I get it now.' Or I leave him behind on purpose, like, 'Oh, that made me the man I am today.'"

He added that he envisioned Kevin as "like a widower or something like that," and said he "wouldn't be completely allergic" to returning as the character, provided the project was right.

## A Franchise That Never Left

The original "Home Alone," written by John Hughes and released in 1990, cast a nine-year-old Culkin as the youngest McCallister child, accidentally left behind when his family flies to Paris for Christmas. He defends the family's Chicago-area home from two bumbling burglars played by Joe Pesci and Daniel Stern. The film grossed $285.8 million domestically on an estimated $18 million budget, making it one of the most successful comedies of its era. Culkin returned for the 1992 sequel "Home Alone 2: Lost in New York," but has not appeared in the franchise since.

The property has since passed to Disney, which inherited it through its 2019 acquisition of most of 20th Century Fox's film assets; it now sits under 20th Century Studios. Four later installments were produced without Culkin, most recently 2021's "Home Sweet Home Alone," which was widely regarded as a misfire. That history helps explain the studio's interest in a legacy sequel headlined by its original star.

The timing may also factor in. Deadline noted that the original "Home Alone" re-entered the Nielsen top-10 streaming movies chart the week of the news, landing at No. 9 with 177 million minutes viewed on Disney+. Culkin has also recently reprised the character in a McDonald's "Home Alone" Meal advertising campaign, available at participating U.S. restaurants starting Dec. 1.

## Why It Matters

The project fits a broader Hollywood strategy of reviving beloved 1980s and 1990s properties with their original stars, a formula studios and streaming platforms increasingly favor as established intellectual property carries less risk than untested ideas. Pairing that nostalgia with Rogen and Goldberg—the comedy duo behind "Superbad," "Pineapple Express" and "Neighbors"—signals a tonal bet on humor that can appeal to both the original film's aging fans and a new generation.

## What to Watch

Much remains unsettled. The sequel has not been greenlit to production, no director is attached, and Chris Columbus, who helmed the first two films, has not been confirmed for a return. Disney's silence leaves the details beyond Culkin's involvement—the final story, the rest of the cast and the timeline—officially unconfirmed. For now, the project is best understood as an early-stage bet that one of the holiday season's most enduring characters can carry a franchise once more—with Kevin, at last, on the other side of the front door.
