# China's Open-Source AI Models Pass 10 Billion Downloads

China's homegrown open-source artificial intelligence models have surpassed 10 billion cumulative global downloads and now lead the world in both download volume and the number of derivative models, according to data from the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology (MIIT) reported by state media. The milestone marks a shift from playing catch-up to helping shape the global open-source AI ecosystem, driven by architectural innovation, domestic-compute breakthroughs and real-world industrial deployment.

The figures were published by [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/politics/20261121/d02ffe3d36144ceea8847030acd97936/c.html), citing a People's Daily report by journalist Gu Yekai. The same report noted that China's daily average token calls—the basic units of data processed by AI models—have surpassed 500 trillion, and that Chinese models accounted for 119.1 trillion tokens in weekly calls on the global open-source aggregation platform OpenRouter during 9–15 November, ranking first worldwide for a 29th consecutive week.

## A Shift From Catching Up to Setting the Pace

The numbers reflect more than market share. "A batch of world-leading open-source models continues to emerge, leading globally in downloads and number of derivative models, guiding the global AI open-source ecosystem," said Huang Qi, Party Secretary of the Institute of Scientific and Technical Information of China, in comments carried by Xinhua.

In the AI field, "open source" more precisely means "open weights": releasing a model's trained parameter values so that developers can download, run and fine-tune it locally or on private servers. That matters for data-privacy-sensitive users and lets developing countries access frontier capabilities at low cost. Crucially, open-sourcing does not mean abandoning commercial returns—firms can still monetize through cloud inference, agent platforms and fine-tuning services.

Central to the momentum is a wave of new flagship releases. Beijing-based Moonshot AI disclosed the full weights of its Kimi K3.1 Pro model, described as the largest-parameter open-source model in the world, while Tencent released its new-generation Hunyuan Hy4 large language model with improved instruction-following, long-text understanding and deep-research capabilities.

## Innovation Beneath the Surface

The competitive gains rest on genuine technical advances. In early 2025, DeepSeek's model series innovated a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architecture that sharply cut inference costs and compute demand—an approach that has since become a direction explored across the global AI field. Kimi K3 introduced a self-developed "hybrid linear attention" mechanism that makes very long texts cheaper to process and very large models more stable to train.

"We reconstructed key components of the architecture from the ground up, giving model training a new space for evolution," said Yang Zhilin, founder and CEO of Moonshot AI. "Chinese open-source models are becoming benchmarks in global model makers' evaluations and important samples for overseas research institutions."

Equally significant is progress in domestic compute. In June, Meituan released a trillion-parameter model trained and run entirely on Chinese-made hardware—the first such model to complete full-process training and inference on a domestic five-figure accelerator cluster, according to the company's [technical blog](https://tech.meituan.com/2026/06/30/LongCat2.0.html). In November, Meituan followed with LongCat-2.5, a 1.6-trillion-parameter native multimodal model also trained entirely on domestic compute, moving such models from "can run" toward "runs fast and runs economically."

"We gradually overcame challenges in deterministic computing on neural network processors and improving compute utilization, verifying the feasibility of large-scale domestic training," said Cai Xunliang, head of Meituan's LongCat model, as cited by [Yibang Power](https://www.ebrun.com/20261102/718021.shtml).

## From the Data Center to the Factory Floor

The practical effects are already visible in industry. At display-maker BOE, an AI system on production lines spots microscopic defects in real time, generates repair plans and triggers equipment to fix them, closing a full quality-management loop. The company built its "Blue Whale" display model on top of domestic open-source foundations, integrating manufacturing experience and production-line data across R&D, manufacturing and quality management.

"We independently developed BOE's Blue Whale display model on a domestic open-source foundation, integrating industrial manufacturing experience and production-line data to cover R&D, production and quality scenarios, driving multiple AI applications across our factories," said Jiang Xinqun, BOE senior vice president and co-CTO.

At the individual level, developer Wu Ruimeng built an AI demo in just 25 minutes on the no-code platform "Merda" after a Taobao shop owner asked for anime-style car wraps—a concept he adapted from virtual try-on tools. The demo sold for 8,000 yuan (about $1,100). "I describe the requirements in natural language, the model creates a customized application, and AI fills in the gaps in my abilities," Wu said.

The influence extends overseas. In Singapore, a multilingual model called "Sea Lion V4" was built on Alibaba's Qwen open-source base, covering 119 languages and pre-trained on 36 trillion tokens, tailored to Southeast Asia's complex language environment. For Liu Sili, senior director of Singapore's AI programme, cooperation with Chinese firms is "an important marker of advancing AI accessibility and making it more representative of Southeast Asia."

## Broader Context and Open Questions

The download milestone arrives on the heels of the 2026 World Internet Conference Wuzhen Summit, whose theme—"Open Source, Openness, Co-building and Sharing"—placed open-source AI at the center of China's digital governance agenda. International media at the summit framed the push as part of broader efforts to advance global AI cooperation, with outlets in Namibia and Sierra Leone noting the potential for developing countries to become AI builders rather than mere users, according to [China News Service](https://www.chinanews.com.cn/gj/2026/11-03/10725454.shtml).

"The possibility of AI accessibility must be built on a solid open-source foundation," said Zhang Yunquan, a researcher at the Institute of Computing Technology, Chinese Academy of Sciences. "China is turning the vision of inclusiveness into concrete action."

### Caveats and the Road Ahead

Some caution is warranted. The 10-billion-download and token-volume figures originate from MIIT and the reporting outlet, and such self-reported metrics can be inflated by re-downloads and mirror sites. Token volume is an emerging, firm-defined indicator with no standardized global baseline, so cross-platform comparisons should be treated carefully. The celebratory framing also omits the geopolitical friction surrounding the topic—including US-China tensions over chip export controls and a dispute in which Washington has accused Moonshot of distilling a rival's models, allegations the company has not publicly conceded.

The next phase will test whether China's open-source lead translates into durable commercial and strategic advantage. What to watch: whether domestic-compute training scales to ever-larger models, how Western developers and regulators respond to the growing footprint of Chinese open weights, and whether the "AI inclusiveness" narrative translates into concrete adoption across the Global South. For now, the download numbers make one thing clear—China's open-source models are no longer a footnote in the global AI story.
