# Anthropic's Record IPO: Should You Buy the Hype?

Anthropic, the artificial intelligence company behind the Claude chatbot, completed the largest stock market listing in history on 19 November, raising roughly $81.3 billion and securing a valuation of about $1.5 trillion, according to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/16/anthropic-naar-de-beurs/). The offering eclipsed the previous record held by SpaceX, but it also raises an increasingly pressing question for ordinary investors: is this a defining opportunity or a costly gamble in an overheated AI boom?

## The Deal in Numbers

Anthropic priced its shares at $83 each — just under €76 — only hours before trading began, as it confirmed in its [official press release](https://www.anthropic.com/news/anthropic-prices-initial-public-offering). It offered 980 million shares, generating about $89.1 billion from the primary offering alone. That topped the $85.7 billion SpaceX raised in June.

The deal grew further as several major technology companies — AMD, Broadcom, Google, Microsoft and Nvidia — sold parts of their existing stakes, adding about $26 billion. That brought the total transaction to roughly $107.3 billion.

On its first day of trading on the Nasdaq Global Select Market, the stock closed at $97.17, up 17.1 percent from the offer price, after surging as much as 38 percent intraday before pulling back. That pattern — an early spike followed by a retreat — is precisely the dynamic some analysts warned about.

## A Safer Alternative to OpenAI

Anthropic was founded in 2021 by Dario Amodei and his sister Daniela Amodei, alongside others, positioning itself as a more ethical and safer alternative to OpenAI. The company has consistently marketed AI safety as central to how the technology should be developed. Dario Amodei rang the Nasdaq opening bell on listing day, reiterating that theme.

The company's operational trajectory is striking. As VRT NWS reported, revenue is expected to climb from about $55 billion this year to $685 billion by 2030. But those figures come with a hefty price tag.

## The Bull and Bear Case

Tom Simonts, a financial economist at KBC, offered a notably cautious assessment. Comparing Anthropic to SpaceX, he highlighted a fundamental difference in business models.

"The big difference is that SpaceX already has a revenue model thanks to its Starlink satellites. With AI companies like Anthropic, there are mainly a lot of investments needed at the moment, while it remains uncertain what profits will replace them," Simonts said.

The upside, he conceded, is substantial if Anthropic breaks through. "If Anthropic ever becomes the first to develop a superintelligent system — one that not only answers questions but can independently take actions — then the company has a gigantic advantage," he said. "Anthropic is part of the leading group in the race to achieve that goal. But whether it will actually succeed remains, of course, a big gamble."

That gamble carries a steep infrastructure bill. Simonts noted that Anthropic has committed to spending about $900 billion in the future on computing power and infrastructure — making it, in his words, "certainly a risky investment."

## The Valuation Question

Perhaps the most important caution for retail investors concerns valuation. Simonts warned that popular companies frequently trade well below their debut highs a year later.

"Especially with big IPOs, there is usually a lot of interest at the start and those companies are somewhat overvalued. After 12 months we often see a more realistic level — for the 10 biggest IPOs of 2026 in the US, that is about 20 percent below the subscription price," he said. "The chance is high that this will also be the case for Anthropic, in line with SpaceX so far."

He also flagged an incentive problem at the heart of the listing. "Investment banks and early investors naturally have an interest in pushing up Anthropic's market value without causing an untenable share price. That earns them higher fees."

## Headwinds on the Horizon

The broader environment for AI investment has cooled somewhat, Simonts noted. In the United States, uncertainty surrounds the construction of new AI data centres following the Democrats' midterm election wins. The market is also watching chipmaker Nvidia after CEO Jensen Huang resigned in November, a move that rattled the sector. And there is always the risk that a better or much cheaper AI model emerges from competitor OpenAI or from China — "things nobody can predict, but which will determine Anthropic's future," Simonts said.

## What to Watch

Anthropic's record debut cements its position among the leading players in the AI race and signals that public markets are willing to price in extraordinary growth expectations. The offering is expected to close on 20 November, subject to customary closing conditions, and underwriters hold a 30-day option on up to 147 million additional shares.

For retail investors weighing a purchase, the lesson from history is clear: the largest listings tend to arrive with great fanfare and equally great volatility. Whether Anthropic defies that pattern depends on forces no one can yet predict — the pace of AI adoption, the cost of computing, and the emergence of a competitor that rewrites the rules.

As Simonts put it bluntly: KBC has no official investment advice on Anthropic, and for most ordinary investors, the prudent course may be to watch the opening act before deciding whether to step onto the stage.
