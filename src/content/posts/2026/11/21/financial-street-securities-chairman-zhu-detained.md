# Financial Street Securities Chairman Zhu Detained in Probe

Financial Street Securities said on 20 November that its chairman, Zhu Yanhui, is under investigation and has been placed under *liuzhi* detention by the Supervisory Commission of Beijing's Xicheng district, according to a filing with the Hong Kong Stock Exchange. The measure came less than a month after Zhu was reappointed to a fresh three-year term on 23 October — a striking reversal for a brokerage chief reconfirmed just weeks earlier.

## What the Filing Says

The disclosure was made to the Hong Kong bourse on Friday, as [Caixin](https://finance.caixin.com/2026-11-20/102506997.html) reported. The company said it had not received any request from the commission to assist with the investigation or provide data, and that group business operations remain normal.

The board said it was assessing arrangements for the roles Zhu had held and that other directors and senior management were performing their duties normally. Based on currently available information, it said it did not expect a material impact on the business.

*Liuzhi* is a form of designated custody introduced under China's 2018 Supervision Law, used by supervisory commissions in graft investigations. It permits the detention of a suspect for a defined period during an investigation and is separate from ordinary criminal detention. The measure does not by itself establish guilt; it is a stage in a process that may lead to Party disciplinary action and, potentially, criminal charges.

## A Career Spanning Brokerage and State Group

Zhu, born in 1975, graduated in July 1996 from the Beijing University of Civil Engineering and Architecture with a bachelor's degree in civil engineering, and joined Financial Street Group in 2002. He became chairman and executive director of the brokerage in December 2022 and formally took office as chairman on 8 September 2023.

Beyond the chairmanship, Zhu served as chairman of the board's Strategy and Investment Decision Committee and of its Risk Control and Oversight Committee. He concurrently held senior posts at the state-owned parent, serving as Deputy Party Secretary, Vice Chairman and General Manager of Financial Street Group, and as chairman and general manager of Beijing Huarong Comprehensive Investment Co. Those overlapping roles mean his detention raises questions about who will assume his duties across the wider group.

Financial Street Securities was founded in 1992, listed in Hong Kong in 2015, and changed its name from Hengtai Securities in 2025. It is ultimately controlled by Beijing's Xicheng district through the Financial Street Group — which explains why a district-level supervisory commission, rather than a central or securities regulator, is handling the case.

## Compliance Pressure and Thin Margins

According to [Caixin Global](https://www.caixinglobal.com/2026-11-20/beijing-brokerage-chief-put-under-investigation-102507183.html), the firm was fined 1.4 million yuan by the People's Bank of China's Inner Mongolia branch for anti-money-laundering failures and was penalized by three regional regulators.

The brokerage has also had a turbulent recent history. Prior Caixin coverage referenced a prolonged employee-shares dispute, a judicial auction of equity in 2020, a penalty that year for illegally lending 36 client accounts to a margin-financing operation, and the termination of a planned merger with Tianfeng Securities. Zhu arrived in late 2022 amid that leadership churn.

Financially, the picture is mixed. First-half operating income rose 10.7% to 1.8 billion yuan, but net profit fell to 240 million yuan — a thin result for a mid-sized brokerage that points to margin pressure and the cost of compliance issues. Brokerage and wealth-management revenue grew, while trading and investment banking declined.

## Part of a Wider Campaign

The case is the latest involving a senior financial executive amid a sweeping anti-corruption campaign targeting China's financial sector. Caixin Global cross-referenced the July 2026 investigation of former CSRC Vice Chairman Fang Xinghai and a March 2026 case involving a former CSRC Hubei bureau head — a pattern that has ensnared senior regulators as well as executives in recent years.

The speed of the reversal — from reappointment to detention in four weeks — underscores the unpredictability of the campaign and its reach into district-controlled financial institutions. For investors, it adds a governance risk premium to smaller state-linked brokers whose leadership is closely tied to local government structures.

## What to Watch

The filing discloses only that a district supervisory commission issued a *liuzhi* notice. It offers no detail on the alleged misconduct — whether it is connected to the firm's AML fines, its prior equity and employee-share disputes, or the parent group's wider dealings.

Key questions lie ahead: who will take over Zhu's roles at both the brokerage and Financial Street Group, whether group-level governance is affected beyond the listed entity, and whether this is an isolated case or part of a broader probe touching Xicheng-district state assets. Until those questions are answered, the firm's investors and counterparties have little to go on beyond the company's assurance that business continues as normal.
