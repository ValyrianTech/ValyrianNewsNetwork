# COP31: Belgium, 20+ Nations Fight to Keep Fossil Phase-Out

Belgium and roughly twenty other countries are pressing to reinstate an explicit commitment to phase out oil and gas in the final text of the COP31 climate summit in Antalya, Turkey, after the reference vanished from the latest draft, [RTBF reports](https://www.rtbf.be/article/cop31-a-antalya-la-belgique-et-une-vingtaine-de-pays-haussent-le-ton-pour-reinscrire-la-sortie-du-petrole-et-du-gaz-dans-le-projet-d-accord-11818457). The standoff has pushed negotiations more than 18 hours past the scheduled close, with no final agreement in sight as of Saturday midday.

## How the Deal Slipped

The flashpoint is the "cover decision," the overarching political statement that crowns each COP. A first draft published on Monday had included options to accelerate a fossil fuel phase-out. But the revised text presented by the Turkish presidency on Thursday dropped any explicit mention of phasing out coal, oil and gas, according to [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/sustainability/climate-focus-cop31-draft-deal-strips-fossil-fuel-references-2026-11-20/).

In its place, the draft pivots toward electrification, targeting a rise in electricity's share of global final energy consumption to 35% by 2035, up from roughly 23% today, and elevates carbon capture, utilisation and storage (CCUS) as a central mitigation pillar. Critics note the electrification goal does not require that the electricity be generated from clean or low-carbon sources.

The retreat comes three years after COP28 in Dubai, where countries agreed for the first time to "transition away" from fossil fuels. Successive COPs have failed to make that commitment more specific.

## A Coalition Digs In

Belgium joined the push alongside a group of ambitious nations that have adopted, or are about to adopt, national fossil-fuel transition roadmaps. Climate Home News named Panama, France, Belgium, Finland, Sweden, Spain, Vanuatu, Côte d'Ivoire and Guatemala among those refusing to accept the text as it stood, while RTBF also cites the Marshall Islands, the Netherlands, Germany, Switzerland and Pacific small island states.

Separately, France, Panama and the Solomon Islands released an open letter calling for the final decision to reference a "clear transition away from fossil fuels" and "immediate clean electrification."

Belgium's federal climate minister, Jean-Luc Crucke, insisted the fight is not over. "The final text is not yet approved. It changes hour by hour, and it remains a demand of Europe, a demand of all the countries here," he said. "It goes well beyond Europe. So we'll fight to the end."

The Netherlands' climate minister, Stientje van Veldhoven, framed the coalition as a signal to negotiators. Panama's environment minister, Juan Carlos Navarro, went further, warning his country would block any text that fails to reflect immediate action. For the Marshall Islands, whose environment minister Jess Gasper Jr. spoke of survival rather than politics, the stakes are existential.

## The EU's Warning

EU Climate Commissioner Wopke Hoekstra warned that the bloc will not sign off on a deal without change. "There will be no agreement" if the draft does not evolve, he said, adding that negotiators are "close to the finish line, but not there yet."

The pressure reflects a deeper divide. COP decisions require consensus among nearly 200 parties, and a bloc of oil-and-gas producers has resisted naming fossil fuels explicitly, favouring the CCUS emphasis instead. Environmental groups have criticised the carbon-capture pivot as expensive and of uncertain efficacy, pointing to the roughly 700 energy and carbon-capture lobbyists registered at the summit and warning that surging power demand, including from AI data centres, is being used to justify prolonged fossil reliance.

Climate finance remains another fault line: developing countries accuse wealthy nations of casting themselves as climate champions while evading historical responsibility, and the COP30 pledge to triple adaptation finance by 2035 remains vague. Reuters noted the revised draft also weakened language on adaptation finance.

## What to Watch

The key question is whether a third draft, still awaited, will reinstate explicit oil-and-gas phase-out language or settle on an electrification-plus-CCUS compromise. As the talks drag on, [RTBF notes](https://www.rtbf.be/article/cop31-les-negociations-se-prolongent-les-divergences-persistent-sur-le-texte-final-11818976), the presidency must find a text acceptable to every party before the final gavel falls. Whether Panama and its allies follow through on their threats to block a weak deal will determine whether COP31 defends the COP28 baseline or quietly dilutes it.
