# Francophone Magistrates Demand Justice Minister Resign

The francophone association of Belgian magistrates has publicly called on Justice Minister Annelies Verlinden (CD&V) to resign — or at least temporarily step aside — over the i-Police scandal, [VRT NWS reported](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/21/franstalige-magistraten-vragen-ontslag-verlinden-ipolice/). In a letter to the College of Attorneys-General, l'Association Syndicale des Magistrats (ASM) argues that the minister's position has become institutionally untenable because she now oversees the very prosecutorial hierarchy investigating her.

"That is unthinkable," said Marie Messiaen, a judge at the Labour Court in Mons and a board member of the ASM, speaking on VRT NWS's De Ochtend on Radio 1. "The impartiality of the magistrate is a fundamental principle of justice."

## The Rule-of-Law Argument

As Justice Minister, Verlinden exercises authority over the College of Procureurs-Generaal — the body that is now following up on an investigation into decisions she made while serving as Interior Minister. That overlap, the ASM says, breaches judicial impartiality and erodes public confidence in the justice system.

The organisation was careful to stress that the presumption of innocence fully applies and that the minister's potential criminal liability "is not at issue here." Rather, the objection is structural: a minister who supervises prosecutors cannot credibly do so while those prosecutors examine her own conduct.

"To be clear, we are not a political association and have no political interests," Messiaen said. "This is really about protecting the rule of law." She added that the demand is not necessarily for outright resignation. "That does not have to be a resignation... There could also be a temporary replacement. But that is not for us to decide."

## How a Procurement Project Became a Crisis

The i-Police programme was launched at the end of 2021 with ambitious aims: digitise police work, merge disparate data systems into a single information platform, and detect risks and criminal networks faster. Then-Interior Minister Verlinden signed a EUR 299 million contract with French IT consultancy Sopra Steria.

In December 2025, her successor Bernard Quintin (MR) [tore up that "monster contract"](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2025/12/26/digitalisering-politie-mislukt-monstercontract-verbroken/) after years of invoicing — roughly EUR 76 million in total — with "no tangible result." The Court of Audit later [reported](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/10/28/audit-rekenhof-i-police/) that the project cost the taxpayer EUR 139.4 million, nearly double earlier estimates, including EUR 21.6 million in software licences that are now largely useless.

In January 2026, the Brussels prosecutor's office opened an investigation into possible embezzlement of public funds and conflict of interest. A key fact sharpened the scrutiny: before entering government, Verlinden had acted as a lawyer for Sopra Steria.

## Investigation Moves Up the Chain

In mid-November, the Brussels parket-generaal [confirmed](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/16/onderzoek-minister-verlinden-i-police/) that it is investigating Verlinden's role in the dossier. The procedure is prescribed: when an investigating judge believes a serving minister may have committed offences, the Brussels attorney-general must open an investigation.

The parket-generaal stressed that this is a "normal procedural step" and "does not in any way prejudge the existence of any criminal responsibility." Verlinden has consistently maintained she has nothing to hide and says she herself asked to be heard.

Earlier in the fallout, the spokesperson of Minister Verlinden and a spokesperson for the Brussels prosecutor's office were placed under suspicion for breach of professional secrecy.

## No Precedent, Mounting Pressure

The ASM's intervention injects judicial authority into what has until now been a largely political pressure campaign. It also adds a linguistic-community dimension to a federal crisis: a francophone magistrates' body lending weight to calls for a CD&V minister's departure.

"The legislator did not provide for this situation and there is no precedent," Messiaen said. "That is why we address the responsibility of the government: something must happen, because as things stand now, it doesn't work."

Opposition parties have already pushed for Verlinden to go. Vlaams Belang and Groen want her to step aside pending the investigation, while Anders is demanding a parliamentary investigative commission. Verlinden's coalition partners have so far stopped short of calling for resignation, focusing instead on the judicial process. N-VA's Maaike De Vreese has said everyone is innocent until proven otherwise but that the probe must be thorough given the EUR 140 million of taxpayer money involved; Vooruit has called the waste "completely unacceptable" but declined to prejudge the outcome.

## What's Next

The decisive moment comes on 1 December, when the Chamber of Representatives holds a joint hearing with Verlinden, Quintin and the Court of Audit. The parket-generaal's investigation continues, and a summons for questioning remains possible. The central question now is whether the ASM's intervention tips coalition partners from a wait-and-see stance into open calls for the minister to step aside — and whether a scandal that began as a procurement failure ends as a constitutional test of how Belgium investigates the official who oversees its prosecutors.
