# Anthropic's Record $81B IPO Lands, Then Tech Sell-Off Bites

Anthropic became the most valuable company ever to go public on Thursday, pricing the largest initial offering in history and briefly crossing $2 trillion in market value in its Nasdaq debut. Yet the triumph lasted barely a day: a deepening rout in artificial-intelligence stocks pulled the S&P 500 into a correction on Friday and dragged Anthropic's own shares down 8.5%, [according to the Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/stock-markets-anthropic-ai-chips-boom-c4c3f4878b12989347f8a930ed813dad).

The contrast captures the central question hanging over the AI boom: whether Anthropic's blockbuster listing signals enduring investor conviction in artificial intelligence, or whether the simultaneous sell-off shows the trade has finally peaked. The trading data suggests both — the offering succeeded on its own terms while the sector it embodies entered a sharp retreat.

## A record debut

Anthropic, the San Francisco-based maker of the Claude AI assistant, sold 980 million shares at $83 each, raising roughly $81 billion. A separate $26 billion private placement with corporate backers brought the total raised to about $107 billion — an extraordinary haul for a company not yet six years old, [AP reported](https://apnews.com/article/anthropic-ipo-nasdaq-ai-b6a74b8949021207dc162fcc876c4649). The offering overtook SpaceX's June listing as the biggest IPO on record.

The stock, trading under the ticker ANT on the Nasdaq Global Select Market, jumped as much as 34% intraday on its first day, pushing its valuation above $2 trillion before it closed up 17% at $97.17. On a fully diluted basis, the company was valued at roughly $1.5 trillion at the IPO price.

"It's a big win for the tech sector after a white-knuckle time with Nvidia," said Dan Ives, a financial analyst at Yorkville Ives & Co. "The pricing shows strong demand among investors."

Investor appetite was fierce, with demand reportedly running about six times the available supply. Employees who sold vested stock became millionaires overnight, and the fortunes of CEO Dario Amodei and his sister, President Daniela Amodei, swelled by billions.

## The sell-off arrives

The mood turned within 24 hours. On Friday, a broad retreat in AI and semiconductor stocks sent the S&P 500 down 3.7% to 6,910.39, its worst weekly loss in more than 18 months and the first correction since March 2025. The tech-heavy Nasdaq composite slumped 5.3% to 22,617.73, tumbling into negative territory for the year, while the Dow fell 1.9% to 48,147.33.

Chip stocks bore the brunt. Advanced Micro Devices fell 13.3%, Micron Technology 13.9% and Broadcom 12.2%, while the PHLX Semiconductor Index dropped 10.7%. Nvidia, the poster child of the AI boom, slid 14% — its ninth down day in 10 — and has lost more than 30% of its value since the sudden ouster of CEO Jensen Huang amid a possible Chinese spying scandal earlier this month. The pain spread globally, with Japan's Nikkei and South Korea's Kospi each falling more than 5% overnight.

Anthropic was not immune. Its shares closed at $88.91 — still above the $83 IPO price but roughly 23% below the $115.10 peak it set the day before. "The whole notion that AI can continue to spend unlimited amounts of money and support the stock market, I think the notion of that is ending as we speak, that's what's being priced right now," said Michael Antonelli, market strategist at Baird. "But I don't think that means that the overall bull market that started in October 2022 is over."

## The fragility beneath the hype

The sell-off reflects anxieties that have been building for months. The AI trade has been losing steam over worries that the sector's towering valuations and enormous spending are unjustified. Fears of a bubble have been sharpened by the midterm elections, in which candidates advocating a slowdown in data-center construction won at the federal and state levels — a result that could crimp future demand for the chips and infrastructure underpinning the industry.

Anthropic's own financials expose the tension at the heart of the boom. Revenue has exploded — from $386 million in 2024 to $4.585 billion in 2025 and $16.286 billion in just the first half of 2026 — yet the company remains deeply unprofitable. It has taken on $6.2 billion in debt and disclosed commitments to spend roughly $900 billion on computing power and infrastructure in the coming years, including $70 billion in the next 12 months.

The IPO gives Anthropic a way to fund that expansion without taking on more debt, a critical advantage as lenders grow anxious about the sector's borrowing. Its Public Benefit Corporation structure and safety-first mission remain part of its pitch; Amodei used the debut to argue the two can coexist. "If we can show it's possible to make the right choices on safety and still build an incredibly successful company," he said, "others in our industry will be inspired to do the same."

## What to watch

Anthropic's reception is now a bellwether for the rest of the industry. Rival OpenAI postponed its own IPO until 2027, and how Anthropic trades in the coming weeks will shape expectations for that listing. If Anthropic qualifies for early index inclusion, it could enter major benchmarks as soon as December, amplifying passive-fund exposure to the AI trade.

Analysts caution that tech debuts are often followed by volatile periods — SpaceX, the previous record-holder, rose nearly 50% in its first three days before falling below its IPO price a month later. Market valuation for AI companies will be "up and down for many years to come, but more up than down," said Rich Lesser, global chair of Boston Consulting Group.

For now, the picture is more uncertain. As Bob O'Donnell of TECHnalysis Research put it, the Anthropic launch is a critical step in reassuring the market about AI's potential, "but it's really just the beginning."
