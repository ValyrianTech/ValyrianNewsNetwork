# New Permit Advances Saint-Pierre Hospital Move to Wavre

Walloon Minister for Spatial Planning Francois Desquesnes has issued a new permit for the relocation of the Clinique Saint-Pierre from Ottignies to a greenfield site in Wavre, [RTBF reports](https://www.rtbf.be/article/demenagement-de-la-clinique-saint-pierre-d-ottignies-a-wavre-un-nouveau-permis-a-ete-delivre-11818730). The decision revives the long-planned move, which had been complicated by appeals against the first permit granted in June 2026.

## A Decision Re-examined From Start to Finish

The minister revoked the June permit and issued a fresh, 188-page reasoned decision after reviewing the entire file, the broadcaster reported. That review weighed environmental questions — soil artificialisation, water management, noise and impact on flora and fauna — alongside urbanistic and architectural considerations and the grievances raised by local residents.

For the hospital, the new permit is an opportunity to move forward. Site clearing is scheduled for December 2026, and barring setbacks, work on the site's access roads and a new motorway slip road is due to begin next summer. The new hospital is to occupy a site at Louvranges, at the crossroads of the N25 and the E411 motorway in Brabant wallon.

## What the Project Entails

The planned facility is substantial. According to [RTBF's earlier coverage of the June permit](https://www.rtbf.be/article/feu-vert-a-la-construction-d-un-nouvel-hopital-a-wavre-la-clinique-saint-pierre-d-ottignies-espere-lancer-les-travaux-en-2027-11738377), the future hospital will have 441 beds and 129 day places, plus a crèche able to accommodate 42 children. The project also includes a new motorway exit connecting the E411 to the N25 towards Louvain-la-Neuve.

The cost is estimated at €480 million. The Walloon Region is expected to cover roughly half through subsidies, and the clinic will finance the remainder through its own funds and borrowing. It hopes to raise between €70 and €80 million from the sale of assets, including its 7.7-hectare site in Ottignies, and it is negotiating the balance with banks.

## Preparing the Ground

Work on the Wavre site is already under way. Preventive archaeological excavations carried out in September 2026 [revealed nothing notable](https://www.rtbf.be/article/des-fouilles-archeologiques-sur-le-site-de-la-future-clinique-saint-pierre-a-wavre-11786891), according to RTBF. More recently, part of the former Domaine du Blé, a well-known venue on the edge of the N25, was demolished. The building brought down housed a restaurant and a nightclub; the hotel portion will be kept and converted to host the polysomnography service and several ancillary services of the future hospital, including occupational medicine and archives.

Philippe Pierre, the clinic's medical director and general coordinator, framed the milestone as the fruit of long effort. "This is the culmination of nine years of preliminary work, reflection, corrections, dialogue with the AVIQ … as well as with the City of Wavre and that of Ottignies for the reconversion of the site," he said when the first permit was granted.

The financing remains the project's tightest constraint. "In these times, with inflation, with bank wariness, with demands for guarantees at every turn when hospitals are non-profits … it's frankly difficult," Pierre acknowledged. He has also stressed the will to build quickly: "We have the ambition and firm will to build in four years. Because the longer it takes, the more it will cost."

## Opponents Weigh a Final Appeal

Residents who filed appeals against the first permit have now been informed of the ministerial decision. They intend to take time to read it and look for a flaw that might allow them to obtain its suspension or annulment. Their last resort is the Council of State.

Opposed to the project from the outset, neighbours of the Louvranges site maintain that the land chosen is unsuitable for a new hospital and that alternatives were not sufficiently studied. They also argue that the project as a whole will cost the public purse too much — around €240 million in Walloon subsidies, roughly half the estimated final bill — and that the future infrastructure will bring them nuisances in terms of noise, light pollution and visual impact.

## What to Watch

The minister's decision does not end the dispute. If an appeal to the Council of State suspends the permit, the December 2026 clearing and the summer 2027 access works could be delayed, pushing back a construction timeline that targets a four-year build and an inauguration in 2031.

Two further unknowns loom over the project: the bank financing, which the clinic hopes to conclude by the end of January 2027, and the reconversion of the Ottignies site, whose sale is meant to bankroll a large share of the new hospital. For now, however, the green light stands — and the bulldozers are due on site within weeks.
