# Yellow River Enters Ice-Flood Season in Inner Mongolia

Ice floes appeared on the Inner Mongolia section of the Yellow River on the morning of 21 November, formally opening the region's 2026-2027 "ice-flood" season — the annual winter period when ice formation, jams and breakup can dam the river and raise water levels to flood stage, [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/politics/20261121/cc7576dc4df049cfa6459b20925d2641/c.html) reported.

According to the Inner Mongolia Autonomous Region Flood Control and Drought Relief Headquarters Office, the first ice run was detected at 08:00 across the reach stretching from the Wujunniu section in Baotou city to the Lamawan Bridge section in Hohhot. Ice-floe density was measured at roughly 5%, with a cumulative ice-run length of 34 kilometres.

The office said the first-ice date was on par with the long-term average but two days later than last year, a shift it attributed to recent cold-air processes that lowered temperatures across the region.

## A Routine Milestone With Real Stakes

While the announcement is a scheduled seasonal status update rather than an emergency, it carries operational weight. The river has only begun to carry ice floes; it has not yet frozen over. The most dangerous phases typically come later, when the channel freezes solid and, more critically, during the spring thaw, when upstream meltwater and broken ice can pile up against still-frozen downstream reaches.

The Inner Mongolia section of the Yellow River spans seven league and city jurisdictions along the river's great northern bend. The headquarters office said it had already convened an ice-flood prevention work conference, issued a formal notice governing the 2026-2027 season, and published the names of administrative officials responsible for ice-flood prevention at league, city, banner and county levels.

"Going forward, [we] will closely track changes in weather and ice conditions, strengthen joint consultation and assessment, issue warning information in a timely manner, urge localities along the Yellow River to strengthen patrols and defence of key reaches, prepare for emergency rescue, and ensure the peaceful freeze-up of the Yellow River's Inner Mongolia section," the office said, according to Xinhua.

## Why the Bend Is So Vulnerable

The Inner Mongolia reach sits at the river's northernmost bend, running roughly south to north. Because of the latitude and temperature gradient between upstream and downstream, the river freezes from the north downstream toward the south upstream in winter and thaws in the reverse order in spring. When the upstream section breaks up first, meltwater and broken ice surge against downstream stretches that are still frozen, creating ice jams and dams that can block the channel and send water levels surging even at modest flow volumes.

Those dynamics have caused severe disasters historically. According to [Baidu Baike](https://baike.baidu.com/item/%E9%BB%84%E6%B2%B3%E5%87%8C%E6%B1%9B/499733), in the century between the Yellow River's 1855 course change and 1955, ice-flood levee breaches occurred in 29 years — roughly once every 3.5 years. The 1955 breach at Liwuzhuang inundated 360 villages, affected 177,000 people and flooded 880,000 mu of farmland.

## Building on Last Season's Playbook

Modern prevention blends levee patrols, upstream reservoir regulation, flood-diversion structures and mechanical icebreaking, an approach that has sharply reduced disasters in recent decades. The prior season offers a template: the 2025-2026 first ice run was detected on 19 November 2025 at 08:00 on the Baotou reach, at roughly 10% density and one day earlier than the long-term average, [CNR and Science and Technology Daily](https://tech.cnr.cn/gstj/20251120/t20251120_527435536.shtml) reported at the time. That season concluded on 14 March 2026 when the Inner Mongolia reach fully thawed after a 44-day campaign, during which authorities coordinated reservoir releases and diverted 288 million cubic metres of ice-flood water to ease pressure on downstream channels, [Sina, citing People's Daily](https://news.sina.cn/znl/2026-03-15/detail-inhrafps3412302.d.html), reported.

The 2026-2027 first-ice date of 21 November is therefore two days later than the previous year's 19 November, matching the headquarters office's statement.

## What to Watch

The immediate priority is a safe freeze-up, known in Chinese as *fenghé*. The key questions will unfold over the coming months: whether the river freezes on schedule and how extensive the ice cover becomes; whether ice jams or dams form at known risk points, particularly during the spring thaw; and how reservoir regulation and icebreaking operations are deployed if water levels spike.

The Inner Mongolia reach is a critical agricultural and industrial zone, including the major city of Baotou, making safe freeze-up and thaw essential to protecting levees, farmland and infrastructure along the bend. For now, the seasonal clock has started — and a coordinated regional machinery has moved into its winter posture.
