# China's High-Tech FDI Surges as Overall Inflows Slip

China attracted 606.93 billion yuan in foreign direct investment in the first ten months of 2026, a 2.4 percent year-on-year decline, according to data released by the Ministry of Commerce on November 20. But beneath the softer headline lies a sharp structural shift: foreign capital is pouring into high-tech industries at a record pace, even as broader inflows cool.

According to [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/fortune/20261120/b9ac995d51e343c69047c295b08872e3/c.html), the January-October period saw 52,853 new foreign-invested enterprises established nationwide, down 1.7 percent year-on-year, while actual utilized foreign capital fell 2.4 percent to the 606.93 billion yuan figure. The data offers a snapshot of how global investment trends and Beijing's industrial priorities are reshaping the world's second-largest economy's capital inflows.

## The Headline Number Masks a Deeper Story

On the surface, the figures extend a multi-year contraction. China's inbound FDI fell 24.7 percent in 2024 and 9.5 percent in 2025, when actual utilized investment totaled 747.69 billion yuan, or about $106.92 billion, according to [Xinhua reporting on government statistics](https://english.www.gov.cn/archive/statistics/202601/23/content_WS69737bf4c6d00ca5f9a08bd4.html).

Yet the 2.4 percent decline for the first ten months of 2026 is the shallowest in this cycle, suggesting the slump may be bottoming out. By sector, manufacturing drew 143.04 billion yuan in foreign investment, while the services sector attracted 450.38 billion yuan.

## High-Tech Inflows Surge

The standout data point is the performance of high-tech industries, which attracted 252.83 billion yuan, up 31.3 percent year-on-year. As [China Daily](https://www.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202611/20/WS6b002b26e4b03ebf8519dc42.html) reported, high-tech now accounts for 41.7 percent of all utilized foreign investment, up 10.7 percentage points from a year earlier.

Growth was concentrated in strategic sub-sectors. Aerospace vehicles and equipment manufacturing jumped 128.8 percent, science and technology achievement transformation services rose 73.3 percent, computer and office equipment manufacturing climbed 48.8 percent, electronic and telecommunications equipment manufacturing increased 35.7 percent, and research and development and design services grew 23 percent.

The pattern aligns tightly with Beijing's push to cultivate "new quality productive forces," an industrial strategy that channels capital toward advanced manufacturing, electronics, and clean-technology supply chains. Foreign investors are increasingly backing the sectors China's policy priorities favor, even as aggregate flows shrink.

## Source Countries Diversify

Investment from key source countries also shifted. Canada led with a 105.7 percent year-on-year increase, followed by France at 35.1 percent, Switzerland at 23.5 percent, and the United States at 9.6 percent, according to the ministry data. The figures include investment routed through free ports, an important caveat: some capital attributed to a given country may be channeled through intermediary jurisdictions such as Hong Kong or Singapore, complicating precise attribution.

The rise in European and Canadian inflows suggests those firms are expanding exposure to China even amid persistent US-China tensions, offering a partial offset to "de-risking" by some Western companies.

## Policy Push to Stabilize Investment

The data lands against the backdrop of an active government campaign to reverse the multi-year slide. In June 2026, the commerce ministry and other departments released a [15-measure action plan](https://english.www.gov.cn/news/202606/22/content_WS6a38e97dc6d00ca5f9a0bb55.html) across five areas to stabilize and optimize foreign investment utilization.

The plan prioritizes expanding market access in services, finance, and healthcare, while streamlining cross-border mergers and acquisitions, data flows, and domestic reinvestment. Vice Minister of Commerce Ling Ji said the plan adds pilot programs in vocational training, schools, and high-level universities, while allowing more foreign financial institutions to manage risks via treasury bond futures. With manufacturing access restrictions now fully lifted, the policy focus has shifted decisively toward the services sector.

## What to Watch

The full-year 2026 figure, expected in January 2027, will show whether the FDI contraction has genuinely bottomed out. Two risks loom. First, the concentration of investment in high-tech and dual-use sectors could draw greater scrutiny from Western governments restricting outbound technology investment. Second, the success of the services-opening agenda will determine whether the overall decline reverses in 2027.

For now, the story is less about the headline number than about direction: foreign capital may be shrinking in aggregate, but it is increasingly flowing into the strategic industries Beijing most wants to build.
