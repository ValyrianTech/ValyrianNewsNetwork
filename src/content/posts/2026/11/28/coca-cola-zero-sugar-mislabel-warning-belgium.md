# Coca-Cola Reissues Zero Sugar Mislabel Warning in Belgium

Coca-Cola has renewed a consumer warning in Belgium about bottles of its sugar-free Coca-Cola Zero Sugar that carry the wrong label, showing the regular, sugar-containing Coca-Cola Original Taste instead. The company, acting in consultation with the federal food safety agency FAVV, urged consumers — particularly people with diabetes and those with the metabolic disorder phenylketonuria (PKU) — to check their products carefully.

The renewed alert, [reported by VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/28/coca-cola-waarschuwt-voor-verkeerd-etiket-petflesjes-coca-cola-z/), marks the second labelling problem in about a month and expands an earlier recall to a new batch of bottles.

## Which Products Are Affected

Two specific lots are involved. The first is a set of 12-packs of 250 ml PET bottles bearing lot number 274AN2, which were sold exclusively at the Colruyt and Okay supermarket chains. The second is a set of 6-packs of 500 ml bottles with lot number 295AN2, which were distributed through various points of sale.

In every case, the bottles actually contain the sugar-free Zero Sugar drink and feature the correct black bottle cap normally associated with that variant. Only the front label is wrong, advertising the sugar-containing Original Taste.

According to [HLN](https://www.hln.be/binnenland/coca-cola-waarschuwt-voor-verkeerd-etiket-op-flesjes-coca-cola-zero-sugar~abc03b39/), the mix-up means shoppers can identify the true contents by looking for the black cap rather than trusting the label alone.

## Why the Mislabelling Matters

The product itself poses no safety risk for the general public; the issue is purely informational. But for two specific groups, the wrong label could lead to confusion.

People with diabetes are asked to be extra vigilant, since the actual drink is sugar-free and thus differs from what the label states. For them, a label showing sugar could prompt unnecessary alarm or distort dietary and insulin decisions.

The second group is people with phenylketonuria (PKU) or a sensitivity to phenylalanine, an essential amino acid the body cannot produce on its own. Coca-Cola Zero Sugar contains the sweetener aspartame, a source of phenylalanine, which people with PKU must strictly limit. Because the incorrect label omits the Zero Sugar branding, those consumers might unknowingly take in a product containing the sweetener.

Coca-Cola stressed that the measure is a warning, not a recall — meaning the product remains safe to consume and does not need to be returned.

## A Repeat of an October Recall

The warning extends an issue that first surfaced in late October. At that time, Coca-Cola Europacific Partners Belgium, working with FAVV, [recalled](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/10/27/coca-cola-zero-terugroepactie/) 12-packs of 250 ml Coca-Cola Zero Sugar bottles from lot 274AN2 sold at Colruyt and Okay, with a best-before date of 28 February 2027. Consumers could return those products for a refund.

The November update concerns a limited lot of 500 ml plastic bottles produced in the same month on the same production line — widening the pool of consumers who need to check their purchases.

## What to Watch

The recurrence on a single production line within the same month raises questions about quality control at the bottling facility, though Coca-Cola has characterised the latest action as a limited extension rather than a systemic problem. The company has not publicly detailed how the labelling error occurred.

For now, the practical advice is straightforward: consumers holding Coca-Cola Zero Sugar bottles from either lot should verify contents by the black cap, and those in the at-risk groups should treat the front label with caution. It remains to be seen whether FAVV will issue its own independent statement or whether further lots will be pulled into the widening labelling review.
