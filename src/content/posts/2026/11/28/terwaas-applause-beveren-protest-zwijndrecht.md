# 'Terwaas': applause in Beveren, protest in Zwijndrecht

The merged Flemish municipality of Beveren, Kruibeke and Zwijndrecht has a permanent name: **Terwaas**. But a day after the choice was unveiled on live television, residents were divided along old municipal lines — approval in Beveren, resentment in Zwijndrecht — [VRT NWS reported](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/28/terwaas-reacties-inwoners/). The reaction reflects the fault lines that have run through the merger since it took effect on 1 January 2025 under the provisional name Beveren-Kruibeke-Zwijndrecht (BKZ).

## A name chosen by vote

Terwaas was announced on the evening of 27 November during a 45-minute live show on regional broadcaster TV Oost, broadcast from the cultural centre Ter Vesten in Beveren. It won decisively in a public vote open to all residents aged 16 and over, held from 12 October to 22 November, in which [14,583 residents took part](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/27/beveren-kruibeke-zwijndrecht-krijgt-nieuwe-naam/) — roughly 20% of eligible voters. Terwaas beat four other finalists: Polderwaas, Scheldeweelde, Scheldewende and Welde.

The shortlist itself was the product of a lengthy process. Residents submitted nearly 5,000 proposals during the summer, which a jury of 12 members from the various advisory councils screened. Former VRT journalist Jan Balliauw, a native and honorary citizen of the area, moderated the jury and said the discussions proceeded "with exceptionally much respect", noting that three motifs kept recurring in proposed names: the Schelde river, the polder landscape and the Waasland region.

Mayor Marc Van de Vijver (CD&V) welcomed the outcome. "Terwaas is not the capital of the Waasland, but it is the largest municipality of the Waasland. Economically, too, it is the most important municipality of the Waasland," he said. "It is beautiful as a family name for the 13 different sub-municipalities, which are all part of the Waasland."

## Applause in Beveren, protest in Zwijndrecht

In Beveren the dominant mood was positive. "I think the new municipal name rolls off the tongue nicely. Polderwaas was my first choice, but I have no problem with Terwaas," said Maarten Merckx at café 't Gaverland in Melsele. Paul Van de Perre noted that "Terwaas does echo Beveren-Waas a little, which used to be the name of the municipality." The name leans deliberately into Waasland identity and places the largest town at its heart.

In Zwijndrecht the tone was sharper. "We do not feel that we, as Zwijndrecht, are represented in Terwaas," said Jules at café Toerist. Yannick De Laet would have preferred "something without Waas", saying he and his friends feel more connected to the Schelde and to Antwerp. Manuel put it more bluntly: "This name does not suit our municipality."

That sensitivity has deep roots. Zwijndrecht sits in Antwerp province, while Beveren and Kruibeke lie in East Flanders, and the town never wanted to merge: in a [September 2023 referendum](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2023/09/17/grote-volksraadpleging-in-zwijndrecht-inwoners-mogen-vandaag-st/), 80% of the 6,602 residents who voted rejected the merger, though the result was not binding.

There is also a generational split. "I notice there was a lot of support for the name Terwaas among the older generation, while the younger generation associates more with names that contain Schelde," said Lode Doggen. At youth centre Togenblik, the preferred pick was "Waaskantje", a nod to the fictional Dutch village in the TV series New Kids. It did not make the final five. "I think a name was deliberately removed, because Waaskantje really lived among young people and was not among the last five," said Axel Reyns — who nonetheless resigned himself to it: "From now on I live in Beveren Terwaas, so good for me."

Not everyone in Zwijndrecht objected. "I think it sounds modern and it still has something authentic," said Judy Proost, who voted for Terwaas. Karina agreed: "It's short and concise, easy to pronounce." And some in Beveren were unimpressed: "Beveren must stay Beveren and Melsele Melsele," said Rens Van Rooij, who voted for Polderwaas.

## Why a name touches a nerve

For Prof. Trui Steen of KU Leuven's Public Governance Institute, the intensity of feeling is unsurprising. She [told VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2024/02/23/waarom-nieuwe-namen-voor-fusiegemeenten-zo-gevoelig-liggen/) that residents of merging municipalities share two core fears: a loss of identity — "does my municipality not become too small, as a sub-municipality within a larger whole?" — and literal distance, or how far people must travel for services. Names that are too narrow, too broad or too playful, she warned, tend to misfire.

Terwaas sits squarely in that tension. It amplifies the Waasland identity that resonates in Beveren and leaves the Schelde and Antwerp identity of Zwijndrecht under-represented — the same divide that has shadowed the merger from the start. The low turnout adds another layer of friction: only about one in five eligible residents voted, so the winning name reflects the preference of a minority of the population.

## What's next

The name is not yet legally in force. The gemeenteraad takes formal note of it on 15 December, after which a decree by the Flemish Parliament must ratify it. Mayor Van de Vijver expects that approval around mid-April 2027, from which point the municipality will officially operate as Terwaas. The 13 sub-municipalities — including Zwijndrecht — keep their own names and postcodes; only the overarching municipal name changes.

Whether the new label can bridge the divide the merger created remains to be seen. For many residents, as one put it, the name they actually use will continue to be that of the town they live in.
