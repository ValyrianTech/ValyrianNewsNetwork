# China Deploys 'Six Friendlies' Workplace Rules

China's five leading labor, health and business bodies have jointly issued national guidelines for building "family-friendly workplaces," unveiling for the first time at the policy level a comprehensive "Six Friendlies" framework designed to help employees balance work and family life as the country confronts mounting demographic pressure.

The All-China Federation of Trade Unions (ACFTU), the Ministry of Human Resources and Social Security, the National Health Commission, the China Enterprise Confederation/China Enterprise Directors Association, and the All-China Federation of Industry and Commerce jointly released the *Guidelines for Building Family-Friendly Workplaces (Trial)*, the ACFTU announced on 27 November, as [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/politics/20261128/8af01bbc1b194a6c8e9df831579c5da3/c.html), citing People's Daily, reported on 28 November.

## What the Guidelines Define

The document defines a family-friendly workplace as one where an employer, "by improving and optimizing work-management systems, creates a safe and dignified working environment conducive to employees' physical and mental health," supporting both men and women in balancing work, family and personal life while ensuring they can work "in a state of physical and mental well-being."

The centerpiece is the "Six Friendlies" framework, which the guidelines describe as the first such set of measures proposed at the national policy level. According to the [All-China Women's Federation's Women's Voice](https://k.sina.cn/article_5952915705_162d248f906703u8d9.html) platform, which published a detailed breakdown, the six dimensions are:

1. **Concept and attitude friendliness** — embed family-friendly principles into corporate culture and key work plans, publicize channels for employees to report work-family needs, and advocate that male and female employees share family responsibilities.
2. **Career development friendliness** — refrain from cutting pay or limiting advancement because an employee is preparing for pregnancy, giving birth, raising children, or caring for elderly or sick relatives; guarantee equal access to training, promotion and awards; and support women returning to work after childbirth.
3. **Maternity and reproductive protection friendliness** — legally guarantee maternity and paternity leave and other reproductive rights, pay maternity insurance, protect employees preparing for pregnancy from occupational hazards, and provide daily care for pregnant and breastfeeding staff.
4. **Caregiving support friendliness** — implement birth-reward leave, parental childcare leave and nursing leave, and provide childcare and after-school services, including temporary care, holiday care and third-party childcare support.
5. **Work arrangement friendliness** — guarantee rest and leave rights, offer flexible or remote working arrangements, simplify emergency-leave approval, and encourage family-oriented welfare leave such as paid time off on Children's Day for employees with children under 14 and leave to accompany children taking entrance exams.
6. **Occupational health friendliness** — provide written disclosure of occupational hazards, conduct regular health checks including gynecological and breast exams, and offer targeted protection during menstruation, pregnancy, breastfeeding and menopause.

## A Milestone in a Long Build-Up

The guidelines did not emerge in isolation. In June 2023, the same five bodies launched a nationwide campaign to collect model cases of family-friendly workplaces organized around the "Six Friendlies" standards, establishing the conceptual foundation the 2026 document now formalizes at the national level.

In 2024, the State Council issued measures to accelerate the development of a birth-support policy system and build a "birth-friendly society." The effort gained provincial momentum in April 2026, when [Jiangsu Province](https://www.workercn.cn/c/2026-04-28/8789939.shtml) rolled out its own pilot guideline across four dimensions and 24 specific requirements, setting a three-year cultivation plan and a five-year scaling goal — a provincial forerunner to the national framework.

Earlier in 2026, policymakers at the annual Two Sessions signaled full implementation of childcare subsidies, one year of free preschool education, and improvements to birth-leave policy, underscoring the demographic urgency behind the initiative.

## Shared Responsibility, Tripartite Approach

The guidelines emphasize a tripartite mechanism among government, employers and workers. They clarify the joint responsibilities of employers and the departments of trade unions, human resources, health, enterprise confederations and industry and commerce in policy supply, resource support and service guarantees, establishing mechanisms for communication, coordination, information sharing and joint advancement.

Notably, the framework frames childcare as a shared responsibility rather than a women's issue alone, advocating that men and women alike take on family duties. To make the standards actionable, the guidelines provide concrete practice cases tailored to different scenarios, giving employers replicable operational templates.

## Analysis and Implications

The move signals a shift from voluntary corporate experimentation to formal national policy guidance — though as a "trial" document, it remains largely directive rather than uniformly mandatory. Its emphasis on flexible work, expanded leave and employer-provided childcare aligns with Beijing's broader effort to lift a persistently low birth rate by reducing the career and financial penalties associated with raising children.

The explicit focus on shared parental responsibility could, if implemented, ease the disproportionate caregiving burden long shouldered by Chinese women and help retention of female talent. Yet the initiative's success will hinge on enforcement and employer capacity: small and medium-sized enterprises, which dominate China's employment landscape, may struggle to fund childcare facilities or absorb flexible staffing. The reliance on "replicable templates" and pilot models suggests the government intends a phased, incentive-driven rollout rather than immediate universal mandates.

## What's Next

With the national framework now in place, attention turns to provincial and municipal implementation, the development of supporting incentives, and how employers — particularly in the private sector — respond to the new standards. Whether the "Six Friendlies" translate from paper into lasting workplace change will be the real measure of a policy aimed at one of China's most consequential social challenges.
