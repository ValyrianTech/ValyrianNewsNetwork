# Belgian Cyclist Viaene (16) Seriously Hurt in Spain Crash

Robrecht Viaene, a 16-year-old Belgian cycling prospect riding for Remco Evenepoel's R.EV Brussels Cycling Academy, was found seriously injured and unconscious by the roadside after a training ride in Spain on Thursday, [VRT NWS reported](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/28/ik-ben-met-de-helikopter-afgevoerd-maar-herinner-me-niks-meer/). The young rider was airlifted by helicopter to the university hospital in Alicante, where he remains in intensive care. On Saturday morning he posted an update from his hospital bed saying his condition is now stable, though he has no memory of what happened.

## A Long List of Injuries

Viaene's own account, shared on Instagram two days after the accident, laid out the severity of the crash in stark detail. He suffered four broken ribs, a broken sternum, a broken collarbone, two broken jawbones, a broken nose and two broken vertebrae. He also sustained a bilateral pneumothorax—a collapsed lung on both sides—a lung contusion on his right side and a small brain haemorrhage.

"I was found unconscious by the roadside and was taken by helicopter to the university hospital in Alicante, where I am now in intensive care," Viaene wrote. "My condition is currently stable."

His injuries are serious enough that a return to Belgium will only be considered once his medical condition allows it. For now, he remains under close observation in Alicante.

## No Memory, and a Police Inquiry

The exact circumstances of the accident remain unclear. Viaene said he cannot recall any of it: "I can't remember anything from the accident, so I have no idea what happened. The police are now investigating."

VRT NWS noted that it is not yet known whether another vehicle, the road conditions or a solo fall were involved, though the fact that he was discovered unchecked along the roadside suggests he may have been riding alone when he went down.

## A Rising Name in the Development Ranks

Viaene, born on 18 December 2009 and a former Belgian national champion in the novices (nieuwelingen) category, races for R.EV Brussels Cycling Academy, the development squad founded by Evenepoel, according to his [ProCyclingStats profile](https://www.procyclingstats.com/rider/robrecht-viaene). The team has been building its profile in recent seasons, adding former professional Dimitri Peyskens as a team leader and trainer—[WielerFlits reported](https://www.wielerflits.nl/nieuws/dimitri-peyskens-stapt-in-opleidingsploeg-van-betrokken-remco-evenepoel/) that Evenepoel himself remains closely involved, attending team-building sessions and sitting in the squad's WhatsApp group.

The R.EV project has positioned itself as an ambitious pathway for young Belgian riders, and Viaene's result sheet—including a podium finish at the Gp Kermesse de Laneuville and a top-15 general classification at the Triptyque Ardennais—marked him as one to watch.

## Why Spain, and Why It Matters

The setting of the crash is familiar to anyone in the sport. Spain's Costa Blanca region, around Alicante, is a favoured winter training base for professionals and development riders alike, prized for its mild climate and varied terrain. That a 16-year-old was training there in late November reflects the increasingly internationalised, year-round nature of elite development cycling.

The accident also lands at a sensitive moment for the sport. Concern over the safety of young riders—particularly those training on open roads far from home—has grown in recent years, and a crash of this severity involving a teenager is likely to sharpen those questions. Development teams face a difficult balance between giving talented riders exposure and protecting them.

## What's Next

For Viaene, the immediate focus is recovery. "The coming period will therefore be all about recovery," he wrote. "I want to thank everyone for all the support and the many messages I have received!"

The police investigation may eventually clarify what caused the crash, but there is no timeline for that. His long-term prognosis and any return to competition remain undetermined pending his recovery. For now, the cycling world will be watching a promising young career paused—not ended—and hoping the road back is a full one.
