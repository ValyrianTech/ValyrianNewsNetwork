# Gold Falls Below $3,500 as Silver Slips Under $40

Spot gold briefly tumbled below the psychologically important $3,500-per-ounce mark on Wednesday, November 25, and spot silver fell below $40 an ounce for the first time in more than a year, capping a punishing stretch for the metals that has defied their traditional role as safe havens. According to [The Paper](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_34558960), spot gold was down 2.09% at $3,480.99 an ounce at the time of publication, extending its year-to-date decline to nearly 20%, while spot silver dropped 3.51% to $39.468.

The move was all the more striking because it coincided with a sharp sell-off in US equities. Rather than pulling investors toward defensive assets, the market turmoil helped pull money out of gold, exposing how rising interest rates, a firm dollar and a scramble for cash have reordered the usual relationships between asset classes.

## A Hot Inflation Print That Locked In a Rate Hike

The immediate trigger was October's US inflation data. The core Personal Consumption Expenditures (PCE) Price Index — the Federal Reserve's preferred gauge — rose 3.3% year-over-year, up from 3.1% in September and above market expectations of 3.2%, [FXStreet reported](https://www.fxstreet.com/analysis/gold-falls-below-3-500-to-its-lowest-since-september-2025-after-core-pce-202611251738). Headline inflation accelerated to 3.8% from 3.7%, and both measures rose 0.3% month-over-month.

Coming in the final PCE release before the Fed's December meeting, the data effectively locked in another rate hike. Markets have almost fully priced in a 25-basis-point increase in December, with roughly 55% odds of another move as early as January, according to the CME FedWatch tool cited by FXStreet. The Fed last raised rates in September.

"With inflationary pressures remaining significantly above the Fed target, the likelihood continues that the Fed will raise interest rates moving forward, which adds pressure to gold," David Meger, director of metals trading at High Ridge Futures, told [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/25/gold-metals-federal-reserve-inflation.html). "That's been one of the main themes that remains a headwind for the gold market, and today's PCE data did nothing to allay those concerns."

Higher rates raise the opportunity cost of holding gold, which yields nothing, while a stronger dollar makes dollar-priced bullion more expensive for buyers using other currencies. Both forces have been working against the metal all year.

## Selling Gold to Cover Stocks

The second, more unusual driver came from the equity market. As investors fled a resurgent sell-off in AI-related stocks, some were forced to liquidate gold holdings to cover losses and meet margin calls — a dynamic loosely described as selling gold to prop up stocks. FXStreet noted that gold's failure to benefit from worsening risk appetite highlights a market in which liquidity needs and tightening expectations are outweighing traditional safe-haven demand.

"Investors are covering shorts going into a major US holiday, which is driving the price action in gold today," Ilya Spivak, head of global macro at Tastylive, told [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/27/gold-metals-silver-markets-federal-reserve.html). "Gold continues to be sold to raise cash in this sentiment-sensitive environment amid risk aversion."

Economist David Rosenberg, writing separately, had flagged the broader pattern earlier in the month, describing gold as behaving "increasingly like a risk asset, which is an entirely new dynamic," [Business Insider reported](https://www.businessinsider.com/gold-price-crash-why-metal-is-down-inlation-interest-rates-2026-11). He compared the liquidation to 2008 and the aftermath of the September 11 attacks.

A resilient US economy left the Fed little reason to relent. Third-quarter GDP was revised up to 4.3% annualized, and weekly jobless claims fell to 189,000, FXStreet reported — data that reinforced the case for keeping monetary policy restrictive.

## A Recovery, but a Fragile One

By Thursday and Friday, the selling had eased. Spot gold rose 0.3% to $3,591.37 an ounce, reclaiming the $3,500 level, while US gold futures gained 1.5% to $3,589.50, CNBC reported. Spot silver recovered 1.6% to $41.88, back above $40, and platinum and palladium also advanced.

The rebound was helped by a US holiday lull that calmed some of the AI-driven jitters that had forced the earlier liquidation. Even so, the fundamental backdrop — a Fed still leaning toward tighter policy — remains in place.

## Why Analysts Remain Split

The split among forecasters reflects genuine uncertainty about whether this is a cyclical stumble or a structural turning point.

Several major houses remain bullish on gold's longer-term prospects. UBS Global Wealth Management reiterated its constructive view, maintaining an "attractive" rating and forecasting $5,000 an ounce by December 2027, citing central-bank reserve diversification, high sovereign debt, persistent geopolitical uncertainty and Chinese demand. Goldman Sachs expects further Fed hikes in December 2026 and March 2027, which it says will keep weighing on Western gold-ETF demand, but still projects gold could reach $5,600 an ounce by the end of 2028, according to The Paper.

Chinese brokerages took a more cautious near-term line. Citics Securities argued that a short-term rebound depends on a pullback in rate-hike expectations, while a "weak dollar trade" could emerge if US equities suffer a deeper correction in 2027 — potentially handing gold more durable allocation buying. Zheshang International said the focus should be on the policy path after December and on 2027 real-rate trends, while noting that central-bank buying and reserve diversification still support long-term demand.

## What to Watch

The path forward hinges on a handful of variables. The December Fed decision is the most immediate, but the durability of any rebound may depend on whether inflation cools and whether the AI-led equity volatility subsides. If risk-off sentiment intensifies and investors again need to raise cash, gold could once more be treated as a source of liquidity rather than a refuge.

For now, the episode is a reminder that gold's safe-haven reputation is conditional. In a world of elevated rates, a strong dollar and stretched positioning, even the oldest store of value can be sold when the market decides cash is the more valuable commodity.
