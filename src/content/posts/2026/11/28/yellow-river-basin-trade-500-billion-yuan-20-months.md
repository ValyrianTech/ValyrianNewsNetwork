# Yellow River Basin Trade Tops 500B Yuan for 20 Straight Months

The nine provinces and regions along China's Yellow River basin recorded combined import and export value of 6.26 trillion yuan in the first ten months of 2026, up 18.7 percent year-on-year, according to customs statistics reported by [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/fortune/20261128/18bfb43032304ea0a753816df6888828/c.html), citing CCTV. It marks the 20th consecutive month in which the region's monthly trade has surpassed 500 billion yuan, underscoring the basin's growing weight in China's foreign trade map.

The milestone comes roughly five years after Beijing published its Outline of the Plan for Ecological Protection and High-Quality Development of the Yellow River Basin in October 2021, a strategy that has framed the region's economic expansion around upgraded industry and greener trade.

## A Shifting Trade Mix

The headline figure is matched by a notable change in what the region buys and sells. Integrated circuit exports rose 111 percent year-on-year, while exports of the so-called "New Three" products, electric vehicles, lithium batteries, and solar cells, climbed 26.4 percent, according to the same customs data.

The tilt toward higher-value and green categories reflects deliberate policy rather than a simple rebound. [CCTV/ Phoenix News](https://news.ifeng.com/c/8xg7Zey6FPc) reported in a five-year review that from 2021 to the end of September 2026 the basin's cumulative trade reached 35.53 trillion yuan, with annual trade rising from 5.46 trillion yuan in 2021 to 6.51 trillion yuan in 2025, an average annual growth of about 4.5 percent.

Over the same period, New Three exports more than tripled, from 478.2 billion yuan to 1,447.6 billion yuan, an average annual growth of 31.9 percent, becoming a key engine of overall export growth. Imports of integrated circuits, computer parts, and other high-tech goods topped 500 billion yuan in the first three quarters of 2026, up 28.7 percent, while bulk commodity imports rose from 752.59 billion yuan to 1.03 trillion yuan.

## Opening Up and Who Drives the Trade

The basin's trading ties have widened as well. The region's trade partners expanded from 231 to 256 countries and regions over five years, and the number of enterprises with recorded import-export activity rose from 87,000 to 120,000, according to the five-year review.

Private firms have been the standout force. Their share of the region's trade climbed from 53.8 percent to 64.1 percent, with average annual growth of 9.2 percent, positioning them as the stabilizing backbone of foreign trade.

Within the region, Shandong continues to play the role of the basin's "ballast stone," with its share of trade rising from 51.8 percent to 54.4 percent. Qinghai leads the basin in trade growth at an average annual 22.2 percent.

"Since the start of this year, the Yellow River basin's import structure has continued to improve, with rapid growth in high-tech imports and strong momentum in the manufacturing sector's shift toward high-end, intelligent, digital, and green development," said Xin Yang, deputy director of Jinan Customs, in the review. "This provides strong support for the high-quality development of the region's foreign trade."

## Momentum Building Into 2026

The full-year outlook is reinforced by early-2026 figures. As reported by the [21st Century Business Herald](https://www.21jingji.com/article/20260314/herald/b0b7af4df1d9593ec478fc5060100918.html), citing customs data, the nine provinces, Qinghai, Sichuan, Gansu, Ningxia, Inner Mongolia, Shanxi, Shaanxi, Henan, and Shandong, posted combined goods trade of 1.09 trillion yuan in the first two months of 2026, up 15.4 percent year-on-year to a record high for the period.

Six of the nine provinces grew, with Shaanxi, Inner Mongolia, Henan, and Ningxia leading at 69 percent, 38.6 percent, 28.2 percent, and 23.9 percent respectively. Machinery and electrical products, a high-value category, grew 17.3 percent in those two months and accounted for 61 percent of exports.

## What to Watch

The 18.7 percent growth in the January-to-October window is markedly stronger than both the 15.4 percent seen in the first two months of 2026 and the roughly 4.5 percent five-year average, a sign that momentum has accelerated in recent months rather than cooled.

A caveat remains: the primary report gives a single headline figure without a province-by-province breakdown for the January-to-October period. The contextual figures come from the five-year review and earlier 2026 data points, which are consistent in direction but not directly comparable to the latest cumulative total.

For Beijing, the numbers feed directly into the wider thesis of the 2021 Yellow River plan, that a belt of inland manufacturing and trade hubs, anchored by Shandong's coast and Sichuan, Henan, and Shaanxi's inland centers, can convert ecological protection and industrial upgrading into export competitiveness. Whether the region can sustain above-500-billion-yuan monthly trade through the final months of 2026 will be the next test of that thesis, as interest rate moves abroad and rising trade tensions weigh on demand.
