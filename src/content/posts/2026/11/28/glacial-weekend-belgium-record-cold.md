# Glacial Weekend as Belgium Shivers After Record Cold

Belgium is bracing for a weekend of glacial temperatures, with forecasters warning of widespread frost and sub-zero conditions across the entire country following one of the coldest November nights in decades. According to [Le Soir](https://www.lesoir.be/785415/article/2026-11-28/des-temperatures-glaciales-attendues-ce-week-end), Saturday will be cold but largely sunny, with maxima of between -1 and 4°C, before cloud and rain sweep in from the west overnight.

## A Record-Breaking Cold Spell

The weekend chill caps an unusually cold late-November period. [RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/record-de-temperature-negative-enregistre-a-uccle-11822029) reported that Friday 27 November was the coldest day of the year at Uccle, the Royal Meteorological Institute's national reference station, where the mercury fell to -4.9°C. It was the coldest November reading there since 24 November 1998, when the temperature dropped to -7.4°C.

Earlier in the week, the cold was even more severe in localized pockets. Forecaster Luc Trullemans, writing for [Sudinfo](https://www.sudinfo.be/id1232038/article/2026-11-27/elles-devraient-traverser-la-belgique-luc-trullemans-confirme-le-redoux-sera-de), described a sharp contrast across the country and warned that the brief milder spell ahead would be short-lived, with snow potentially returning to the Ardennes heights in early December.

## What to Expect This Weekend

Saturday's daytime conditions will be bright but biting. The IRM forecasts a fairly sunny day with some high cloud over the western half of the country, the east staying clear into late afternoon, and a moderate south-easterly wind.

Saturday night, the picture changes. Cloud will become abundant and a weak precipitation zone will cross the country from west to centre, with minima of between -4 and 2°C reached in the evening.

Sunday brings a marked shift. The sky will be generally very cloudy as the disturbance advances eastwards, with precipitation falling mostly as rain. On the highest Ardennes summits, however, forecasters warn of sleet, rain turning to snow, or freezing rain. Maxima will range from 0 to 3°C in the east while climbing to 5 to 9°C in the west.

## Why It Matters

The cold is not an isolated episode but the tail end of Belgium's first genuine winter offensive of the season. The extreme lows earlier in the week — down to -14°C in the eastern valleys of Liège and Luxembourg provinces — resulted from a combination of a cold air mass, a perfectly clear sky and very weak wind, which allowed intense nocturnal radiative cooling, RTBF explained. This "cold hole" effect makes valley bottoms markedly colder than surrounding slopes.

Weekend frost carries practical consequences: icy roads, a freezing-rain risk on Sunday in the Ardennes, and impacts on vulnerable populations, agriculture and transport. The IRM had earlier issued a yellow warning for slippery conditions during the cold snap.

## A Brief Reprieve, Then More Cold

A south-westerly flow will bring milder air after the weekend rain, with a temporary mild spell early in the week beginning 30 November and maxima reaching up to 9–10°C. But according to Trullemans, that relief will be short-lived. From around 4 December, renewed cooling is expected, with precipitation possibly turning to sleet or snow on the Ardennes heights over the weekend of 5–6 December.

Forecasters caution that this remains a developing outlook. As Sudinfo noted, "the return of more frequent night frosts and the risk of snowfall in the Ardennes remain to be confirmed in the coming forecasts." For Belgians, the message is clear: winter has arrived early, and it is not finished yet.
