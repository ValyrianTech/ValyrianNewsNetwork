# China Signals Market Backstop With 'National Team' Pledge

Premier Li Qiang chaired a State Council executive meeting on November 27 that pledged to intensify the "national team's" role in stabilizing the stock market and to optimize Central Huijin's quasi-"stabilization fund" with "sufficient liquidity guarantees," according to the [official readout](https://finance.eastmoney.com/a/202611273941564045.html) of the meeting. The deployments were read by state media as five clear market-stabilization signals, and they landed with immediate effect: the benchmark Shanghai Composite closed up 3.73%, the Shenzhen Component jumped 7.24%, and the growth-focused ChiNext index surged 11.61%, with more than 5,000 individual stocks ending the day higher.

## A Cabinet-Level Endorsement

The weekly State Council executive meeting sets the direction of China's cabinet policy, and its readout on capital markets marked a notable elevation in language. The meeting declared that "the capital market bears on the overall economic development situation," framing equities as central both to corporate financing and industrial upgrading and to household assets and property income. As [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/fortune/20261127/73e4b00aade74e069e6c0b1df8311520/c.html) put it in its analysis, "stabilizing the stock market is stabilizing expectations."

The most closely watched line committed the government to "intensify the 'national team's' market-stabilizing role, further optimize Central Huijin's quasi-'stabilization fund' function, and provide sufficient liquidity guarantees." It was the first time the colloquial term "national team"—long used in market circles to describe state-linked buyers—had appeared in a State Council readout, a formalization that analysts said reinforced the state's commitment to backstop equities.

## The Five Signals

Xinhua distilled the meeting into five signals. Alongside the overall-development framing, the government said it would "implement existing policies well and plan and introduce incremental policies"—a hint of further stimulus in reserve. It pledged to improve policies guiding insurance funds, enterprise annuities, and social-security funds into the market to "continuously strengthen medium- and long-term investment forces." And it called for strengthening prevention of and response to "imported risks," acknowledging the A-share market's exposure to overseas volatility.

The reform agenda extended beyond stabilization: the meeting urged deeper capital-market reform, a multi-tiered market, full-chain regulation, and a crackdown on financial fraud and irregular share reductions to protect investors.

## Flows Back the Rhetoric

The policy language was accompanied by striking fund-flow figures cited by Xinhua. Since early October, listed-company buybacks and share increases totaled nearly RMB 60 billion (about 600亿元), far exceeding prior levels. Equity ETF net subscriptions reached nearly RMB 800 billion (近8000亿元), lifting total ETF scale to roughly RMB 2.9 trillion (2.9万亿元), with RMB 400 billion in net subscriptions into broad-based blue-chip ETFs tracking the CSI 300 and SSE 50.

Valuation arguments featured prominently in the state-media framing. Xinhua noted that ChiNext's trailing price-to-earnings ratio had fallen to 25 times—below the Nasdaq's 28 times—at a historic low, while the STAR 50 index's P/E dropped to 53 times excluding loss-makers. ChiNext and STAR 50 ETFs each drew more than RMB 100 billion in net subscriptions, a pattern Xinhua characterized as "buy more as it falls."

Sell-side economists quoted in the coverage amplified the message. Xie Yaxuan, head of China Merchants Securities' research center, said the meeting positioned the capital market within the broader economic-development picture. Zhao Wei, chief economist at Shenwan Hongyuan Securities, said the "national team," various stabilization funds, and medium- and long-term capital were boosting stabilization synergy. Liu Yu, chief economist at Industrial Securities, described the effort as building a risk "breakwater" that integrates domestic and external risk defense.

## Regulatory Follow-Through

The securities regulator moved on the same day to operationalize the pledges. On November 27, the China Securities Regulatory Commission, chaired by Wu Qing, convened an enlarged party committee meeting to convey and study the spirit of the State Council executive meeting and to map out how it would carry out the measures, [China Securities Journal reported](https://finance.eastmoney.com/a/202611283942146954.html). The commission said it would "promptly study and introduce relevant incremental policies" in coordination with other authorities and would "actively and fully" support the stabilization effort.

## What to Watch

The immediate test is whether the rally holds and whether the promised "incremental policies" materialize in concrete measures from the CSRC and other regulators. The explicit "imported risks" language signals that Beijing's stabilization push is partly defensive—a hedge against spillover from global technology-stock volatility and shifting US monetary conditions—even as it maintains capital-market opening.

A caveat is warranted: the accounts are drawn from Xinhua, the official state news agency, so the framing reflects government messaging, and the analyst commentary comes from sell-side economists with an institutional interest in market optimism. Independent confirmation of the specific fund-flow figures was not available from a second, non-state source. What is clear is that Beijing has now put its most explicit language yet behind the equity market—and moved quickly to show it means it.
