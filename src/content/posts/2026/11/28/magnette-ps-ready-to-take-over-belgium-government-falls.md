# Magnette: PS 'Ready to Take Over' If Government Falls

Socialist Party (PS) leader Paul Magnette has declared that his party is ready to take over should Belgium's federal government collapse, as the governing "Arizona" coalition remains locked in a protracted budget crisis. In an interview with [Le Soir](https://www.lesoir.be/785405/article/2026-11-28/paul-magnette-si-le-gouvernement-tombe-est-pret-prendre-la-releve) published on 28 November 2026, the Francophone socialist leader said: "If the government falls, we are ready to take over." The remarks position the PS as the credible alternative at a moment when the federal executive has already come close to falling once and faces a decisive budget meeting on Monday.

## A coalition on the brink

The Arizona coalition — named after the colours of the US state flag, which happen to match the party colours of its members — has governed since February 2025 under Prime Minister Bart De Wever of the Flemish nationalist N-VA. It groups N-VA, the Francophone liberal MR, the centrist Les Engagés, the Flemish Christian-democrat CD&V and the Flemish socialist Vooruit.

The government's structural weakness is that it lacks a full right-wing majority, making the left-leaning Vooruit mathematically indispensable. That dependence has become the fault line of the budget standoff, as Vooruit resists austerity measures it argues fall disproportionately on workers, pensioners and patients.

The crisis came to a head in early November. After budget negotiations collapsed overnight, De Wever went to King Philippe to tender his resignation. According to [RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/direct-crise-dans-le-gouvernement-arizona-bart-de-wever-a-remis-sa-demission-au-roi-qui-reserve-sa-decision-et-convoque-les-presidents-de-parti-11809122), the King kept the resignation "in deliberation", deplored the absence of a "credible" budget agreement, and launched consultations with the five party presidents before giving De Wever roughly ten more days to break the deadlock.

As of late November, no federal budget for 2027 has been agreed — raising the spectre of provisional twelfths (*douzièmes provisoires*) to keep the state running into the new year, a mechanism De Wever had previously ruled out. De Wever has now summoned the five party presidents to a fresh budget meeting on Monday 30 November, with Vooruit's attendance uncertain.

## Magnette backs Vooruit's stand

In the Le Soir interview, Magnette offered a full-throated defence of the coalition's left-wing partner, arguing that Vooruit's demands are legitimate. "The Prime Minister has known from day one that this is a centre-right government, but with a left-wing party making up the numbers because they don't have a full right-wing majority," he said. "So he cannot not listen to them. They have already made enormous concessions. And now they are being asked to pay the balance of the bill by making all the effort on healthcare, by touching wages, while almost nothing is asked of the multinationals making excess profits and the very largest fortunes."

The framing is the PS's core political weapon: fiscal justice. Magnette has repeatedly argued that the deficit should be closed by taxing large fortunes and multinationals rather than through spending cuts. In a post on X on 23 November, he wrote that "the efforts demanded must be more fairly distributed" and that "the time has come to make multinationals and the very largest fortunes contribute" — adding that "contrary to what the right-wing parties say, it is possible!", [as reported by 21News](https://www.21news.be/paul-magnette-le-temps-est-venu-de-faire-contribuer-les-multinationales-et-les-tres-grandes-fortunes/).

This is not a sudden conversion. At the PS's political bureau in Gosselies in September, Magnette struck a similar note, telling reporters that the socialists were "always ready" and that "it is better to have no government than to have a bad government," as reported by [La Libre](https://www.lalibre.be/belgique/politique-belge/2026/09/07/paul-magnette-ne-craint-pas-la-chute-de-larizona-mieux-vaut-pas-de-gouvernement-quun-mauvais-gouvernement-CVMQVPSRXBEL3LBM7FAYD6HIAQ/). He also unveiled an alternative PS programme worth around €16 billion, built largely on a tax on large fortunes that the party estimates could raise some €6 billion on its own.

## Positioning, not precipitating

The November interview escalates the PS's language from being "always ready" to an explicit claim of being able to "take over" — a meaningful shift in tone, though not in substance. Magnette has consistently insisted the PS does not seek the government's fall. That framing lets the socialists present themselves as responsible while benefiting politically from the government's dysfunction.

The PS's fortunes, however, hinge on Vooruit inside the coalition. By amplifying Vooruit's demands, Magnette reinforces the left bloc's bargaining position and pressures De Wever toward concessions on wealth taxes that the MR and N-VA resist.

An alternative path has also been floated. In early November, Flemish liberal leader Frédéric De Gucht said his party Anders — the former Open VLD — was ready to replace Vooruit in the coalition, a scenario that would move the government to the right, [21News reported](https://www.21news.be/crise-politique-anders-pret-a-remplacer-vooruit-au-gouvernement/). CD&V leader Sammy Mahdi has dismissed such a majority as "totally ungovernable," [arguing it would amount to the same as early elections](https://www.sudinfo.be/id1231237/article/2026-11-25/une-majorite-avec-anders-serait-totalement-ingouvernable-sammy-mahdi-refuse), while political scientist Dave Sinardet has weighed the likelihood of a new coalition formula, [judging a rapid alternative majority with Anders unlikely](https://www.sudinfo.be/id1231199/article/2026-11-25/crise-budgetaire-le-scenario-dune-nouvelle-coalition-avec-anders-vraisemblable).

## What to watch

The immediate test comes on Monday 30 November, when De Wever convenes the five party presidents. Whether Vooruit attends — and on what terms — will determine whether the Arizona coalition survives into 2027 or Belgium heads toward early elections.

Beyond the budget arithmetic, the deeper question is one of political direction. Magnette has suggested De Wever might even allow the economy to deteriorate to argue for the country's "ungovernability" and push confederalism — a charge that reflects the enduring divide between Flemish nationalists and Francophone socialists.

For now, the PS is waiting in the wings. Its message to voters is that it is both a responsible opposition and a government-in-waiting. Whether that posture becomes reality depends less on Magnette than on the five parties still trying, against the odds, to hold the Arizona coalition together.
