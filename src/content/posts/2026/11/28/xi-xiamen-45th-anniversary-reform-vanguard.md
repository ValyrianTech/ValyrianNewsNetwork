# Xi Frames Xiamen at 45 as Reform Vanguard

China's official media marked the 45th anniversary of the Xiamen Special Economic Zone (SEZ) with a long feature portraying the southeastern port city as a living showcase of President Xi Jinping's drive for "higher-standard reform and opening up." The piece, published Saturday in the latest issue of *Outlook* (《瞭望》) magazine, uses the anniversary to tie Xiamen's institutional innovation record directly to Xi's personal legacy and current policy agenda, according to [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/politics/leaders/20261128/c5b885bff15d4edbb46e2193d78b3de4/c.html).

## A City at the Center of Xi's Biography

The feature is built around Xi's long association with Xiamen. It recalls his arrival in Fujian in 1985 — "Xiamen was my first stop," Xi said at the 2017 BRICS Business Forum — and credits him with leading the drafting of the *1985–2000 Xiamen Economic and Social Development Strategy*, described in the piece as the earliest 15-year economic development plan compiled by a Chinese local government.

The narrative is anchored in remarks Xi made during an October 2024 inspection of Xiamen, in which he said the requirements for reform and opening up are now "higher than before, in depth and breadth." Xi called on Fujian and Xiamen to "steadily advance institutional opening up" and "produce more institutional and policy outcomes" to bolster high-level opening. The article treats those words as the organizing principle for the entire anniversary narrative.

## Institutional Innovation as the Selling Point

According to the report, Xiamen now carries 43 national-level pilot mandates, spanning the National Comprehensive Reform Pilot, a service-industry opening-up pilot, the BRICS New Industrial Revolution Partnership Innovation Base, a cross-strait integrated development demonstration zone, and its role as a maritime Silk Road pivot city. A June 2023 central plan gave the city "list-based bulk authorization" to push reforms in key areas.

The feature reports that Xiamen has accumulated 84 nationwide-first reform results and 280 institutional practice results, and completed 14 reform-area legislative acts in nearly three years. It cites a striking official figure: of 107 reform experiences promoted nationally from comprehensive pilot cities since 2023, "Xiamen experience" accounts for 59.8%. Xiamen officials and central ministries are said to have promoted 64 of those experiences in four batches, with 15 original reform outcomes absorbed into national law and regulations.

The flagship example offered is the *Xiamen SEZ Personal Bankruptcy Protection Regulation*, which took effect November 1, 2025. The article describes it as the most legally complex regulation since Xiamen gained SEZ legislative power, and as China's first bankruptcy law to carry the word "protection" in its name — a detail framed as a signal of balanced treatment for creditors, debtors and stakeholders.

## Opening Up at the Frontier

Beyond domestic reform, the piece leans heavily on Xiamen's role in "institutional opening up" — aligning rules and standards with international norms. It highlights a Xiamen-led proposal to add chemically graded barite powder to the International Maritime Solid Bulk Cargoes Code, adopted by the International Maritime Organization and credited with saving exporters roughly two months of sea time and about 700,000 yuan per voyage.

Customs figures are deployed to reinforce the export-liberalization message. According to the report, Xiamen's aviation off-site bonded maintenance import-export value exceeded 680 billion yuan (about $95 billion) as of August 2026, ranking first nationally. For the first three quarters of 2026, the city's total import-export value reached 790.4 billion yuan, up 10.7% year-on-year — a record for the period — with trade to Belt and Road partners up 17.7% and to BRICS countries up 30.4%.

## Cross-Strait Integration and Its Limits

A significant portion of the feature is devoted to cross-strait integration, casting Xiamen as the front line for drawing Kinmen — the Taiwan-held islands just offshore — into a "same-city living circle." The article notes that the Xiamen section of the Xiamen-Kinmen Bridge had its main sea-span linked up on November 10, 2026, and is due to open to traffic by year-end. It also cites record "mini three links" passenger volumes of more than 1.8 million this year, including over 1.45 million Taiwan compatriots.

The piece frames the bridge, power and gas interconnection projects as steps toward serving "the great cause of national reunification." It is worth noting that the report presents this connectivity largely from the mainland side: the Kinmen section of the bridge has not received approval from Taiwan's authorities, a point the official narrative does not address.

## Analysis: A Proving Ground on Message

Read as political communication, the anniversary feature does more than recount Xiamen's record — it stitches the city's individual milestones into a national doctrine. "Institutional opening up" has become a signature concept in Chinese economic policy, moving the emphasis from goods and tariff liberalization toward alignment of rules, standards and management practices with international norms. Xiamen functions as the doctrine's proving ground, and the piece is calibrated to show that the strategy produces measurable, replicable results.

The framing also serves Xi's biography. By returning to his 1985 arrival and the development strategy he helped draft, the article presents today's "higher-standard" agenda as a continuation of a decades-long personal project rather than a response to current economic pressures. That rhetorical move strengthens continuity and authority — and steers attention away from the headwinds, including softer domestic demand and persistent cross-strait political friction, that the celebratory tone leaves out.

Because all the figures cited are self-reported by state media and municipal officials, and no independent audit is provided, the statistics should be treated as official claims rather than neutral findings. Yet the underlying verifiable developments — the bridge milestone and Xi's October 2024 inspection — are corroborated by independent reporting.

## What to Watch

Two dynamics will test the narrative in the months ahead. The first is whether the models celebrated here — the personal bankruptcy regime, the standards-setting diplomacy, the bonded-maintenance customs innovations — translate into durable national policy or remain showcase pilots. The second is whether the cross-strait "connectivity" vision advances beyond the mainland side, given Taipei's non-participation. For now, Beijing is using Xiamen's 45th year to signal a simple message: the reform agenda is deepening, and the city on the strait is meant to prove it.
