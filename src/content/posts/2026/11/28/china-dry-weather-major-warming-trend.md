# Central and Eastern China to Stay Dry Before Major Warm-Up

A cold air mass is still working its way across China on Saturday, driving sharp cooling in the northeast, but forecasters say the chill will give way to a rapid and pronounced warming trend across the country's central and eastern regions from late November into early December, [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/politics/20261128/bf4df80450b64a8ba343088b2a33f8fc/c.html) reported, citing the China Weather Network.

According to the forecast, temperatures north of the middle and lower reaches of the Yangtze River will rebound quickly over the next two days, with the most striking warmth expected at the turn of the month.

## A Clear, Dry Pattern—With Snow in the North

Over the next three days, most of China will remain under clear and dry skies, but the far north and northwest will see frequent snow, the report warned. Snowfall is expected to be persistent in Xinjiang and Heilongjiang, where precautions are advised.

The Central Meteorological Observatory forecast heavy snow or sleet in parts of the high-altitude areas of northern Xinjiang on Saturday, with blizzards possible in some spots. Light rain is expected in parts of the Shandong Peninsula, south-central Hainan Island, and eastern Taiwan Island.

On Sunday, moderate-to-heavy snow or sleet is forecast for parts of northern northern Xinjiang, northeastern Inner Mongolia, and western Heilongjiang, with blizzards possible in the high-altitude Ili River Valley. By Monday, snow is expected to spread across most of northern Xinjiang, northeastern Inner Mongolia, central-eastern Heilongjiang, central-eastern Jilin, southern Qinghai, and eastern Tibet.

## Dryness Peaks, Then Temperatures Swing

Dryness will peak on Saturday afternoon, with minimum relative humidity forecast at 20% to 30% across North China and the Huang-Huai region, and 30% to 40% in Jiangnan and South China. The China Weather Network advised the public to stay hydrated.

The temperature swing has already been dramatic. On Friday, cooling spread across eastern Northwest China, North China, and the Huang-Huai area. At 2 p.m., some areas of central-southern Inner Mongolia, Shanxi, and northern Hebei had dropped more than 6°C compared with the same time the previous day, with local declines of around 10°C, leaving many afternoon temperatures below 10°C.

On Saturday, the cold air pushed into the northeast, Huang-Huai, and Jiang-Huai areas. Cooling in the northeast is particularly pronounced, with some areas dropping 4°C to 10°C, and maximum temperatures in western and northern Heilongjiang falling below freezing.

## Warmth Returns, Then Peaks in the South

Beginning Sunday, temperatures north of the middle-lower Yangtze will rebound, and warmth will return to many places. Around November 30, northern temperatures are expected to reach recent highs, with the 20°C-plus maximum-temperature zone expanding to Henan, Shandong, and even southern Hebei.

The southern peak arrives on December 1 and 2, when maximum temperatures in many parts of Jiangnan and South China are forecast to reach 25°C or higher. By December 3, Guangdong and Hainan are expected to approach or reach 30°C, bringing a distinctly warm feel.

## Analysis: A Classic Cold-Front Rebound—With More to Come

The pattern reflects typical cold-front dynamics: a cold air mass moving east and southeast across China first cools the north and east, then gives way to warming as southerly and subsiding air returns. The contrast between dry, clear conditions in the central and eastern heartland and active snowfall in Xinjiang and Heilongjiang underscores the geographic diversity of China's early-winter weather regime.

The forecast also carries practical public-health implications. The low relative humidity—20% to 40%—raises concerns about dry air, elevated fire risk, and the need for respiratory hydration, while the sharp temperature swings between day and night make seasonal colds more likely.

The China Weather Network specifically warned that temperature fluctuations in North China and the northeast will be large over the coming period, advising the public to check forecasts regularly, adjust clothing accordingly, and guard against colds.

## What's Next

Looking further ahead, a new cold air mass is expected to arrive around December 4, bringing large-scale cooling to central and eastern China. Forecasters cautioned that the lead time is long and the outlook should be monitored for changes.

For now, the immediate picture is one of sharp contrasts: snowbound northern frontiers, a parched and clear heartland, and a south bracing for unseasonably warm early-December temperatures—all within the space of a single week.
