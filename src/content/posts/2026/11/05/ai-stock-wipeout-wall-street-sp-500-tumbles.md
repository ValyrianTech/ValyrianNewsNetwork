# AI Stock Wipeout Sends Wall Street Reeling, S&P 500 Tumbles

Wall Street suffered one of its steepest one-day declines in more than a year on Thursday, as a broad selloff in artificial-intelligence and semiconductor stocks dragged the S&P 500 down more than 3% and wiped out the market's best session in three months. Nvidia, the market's heaviest weight, fell sharply while chipmakers and data-center operators plunged, sending the CBOE Volatility Index spiking to its biggest jump in over two years.

## The Numbers

According to [AP News](https://apnews.com/article/stocks-markets-ai-midterm-oil-trump-7ee9c212805aa5e70cf34d25aab7a36e), the S&P 500 fell 3.2% after an opening loss accelerated through the morning, putting the index on track for its worst drop since President Donald Trump's "Liberation Day" tariffs shook markets worldwide in April 2025.

The Dow Jones Industrial Average dropped roughly 1,200 points, or about 2.4%, while the tech-heavy Nasdaq Composite fell about 5%, leaving it on the brink of correction territory. The PHLX Semiconductor Index (^SOX) dived roughly 9% to 11%, with [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/live/stock-market-today-thursday-november-5-dow-sp-500-nasdaq-oil-rises-chip-stocks-141439950.html) reporting that chip losses erased roughly $2 trillion in market capitalization in a single session.

The scale of the reversal was striking given that just a day earlier the market had posted its strongest day in three months, rallying on relief after the U.S. midterm elections.

## The AI Trade Cracks

The carnage was concentrated in the companies that have powered the AI boom. Nvidia fell 8.3% — its weight on the S&P 500 is so large that a 1% move has a bigger index impact than nearly any other U.S. stock. Advanced Micro Devices tumbled 15.9%, Micron dropped about 12%, and CoreWeave — which rents cloud access to AI chips — sank 14.9%.

Power and infrastructure suppliers tied to data centers were hit just as hard. GE Vernova, which supplies equipment for data-center power, fell 9.1%, while Constellation Energy slid 9.2% and Vistra sank 15.4%, according to AP. Applied Digital dropped 8.8% and Cipher Digital fell 21%.

The selling reflects a persistent worry: that the massive, often debt-financed buildout of AI data centers may not generate returns commensurate with its cost, particularly as borrowing money has grown more expensive. As AP put it, such stocks have been "swinging for months" on fears the construction of further data centers could slow.

## A Political Shock Lands on Top

Unlike a pure valuation correction, Thursday's drop collided with a genuine political regime change. Tuesday's midterm elections handed Democrats a nationwide sweep, including wins in Republican strongholds and key data-center hubs such as Texas and Georgia.

That result, [Reuters](https://finance.yahoo.com/energy/articles/ai-data-center-stocks-dip-113427491.html) reported, raised the prospect of greater scrutiny of the AI buildout — more hearings and investigations, and potentially fewer tax incentives and more responsibility for power and water costs. It also introduced the chance that governments could pressure developers to pause construction, which would mean fewer chip purchases and less of the electricity demand that has been driving much of U.S. economic growth.

"The AI buildout will become more expensive, not disappear," said Mark Malek, chief investment officer at Siebert Financial. "Fewer tax incentives and greater responsibility for power and water costs will put pressure on returns, making margins more important for investors. The most vulnerable AI companies are the borrowers, not necessarily the biggest spenders."

## A Tale of Two AI Trades

The selloff was anything but uniform, and its internal divergence offered the clearest window into what investors are actually pricing.

Even as the S&P 500 fell sharply, roughly two out of every five stocks in the index rose — a sign of just how concentrated the market has become. AppLovin soared 30.2% after the marketing platform beat revenue and profit expectations and expanded a buyback program to up to $3.6 billion. Tapestry rallied 6.7% on stronger profit and raised guidance.

Most tellingly, the biggest hyperscalers moved the opposite way from the rest of the AI complex: Amazon and Microsoft each jumped about 6%, and Alphabet rose 4.6%. The logic, as Art Hogan, chief market strategist at B. Riley Wealth, explained, is that slower data-center construction actually protects the value of assets the hyperscalers already own and curbs their enormous capital spending.

"The hyperscalers already have data centers, so those assets are more valuable now because there's not going to be an overbuild," Hogan said. "If there's a slower pace of data-center build-out, then there's going to be a slower pace of CapEx spending."

## Cross-Asset Signals

The flight to safety showed up across markets. The yield on the 10-year Treasury fell to about 4.84% from 4.90%, extending a slide from above 5.30% last month, as investors sought the relative safety of government bonds. Bitcoin fell more than 5%, briefly dipping below $76,000.

Oil rebounded after a sharp prior-day decline, with Brent crude rising about 2.2% to roughly $96.70 a barrel, leaving intact the uncertainty over an ongoing U.S.-Israeli war with Iran. Reports on the U.S. economy were actually encouraging — fewer workers applied for unemployment benefits and productivity improved more than expected over the summer. The Bank of England, meanwhile, raised its main interest rate for the first time in three years, citing Iran-war-driven inflation.

The selling was global. South Korea's Kospi tumbled 5.3% and Japan's Nikkei 225 fell 1.8% as tech and AI-linked names came under pressure worldwide.

## What to Watch

The central question is whether this is a one-day shakeout or the start of a broader correction. The market's fate is increasingly hostage to a handful of AI megacaps, and any sign that data-center spending is genuinely slowing — particularly in earnings guidance from Nvidia and the hyperscalers — could extend the pain.

Analysts caution that a Democratic Congress may investigate rather than legislate, and that stock prices ultimately follow profits and interest rates more than who controls Washington. But with valuations stretched, borrowing costs elevated and a war-driven inflation backdrop, the margin for error in the AI trade looks thinner than it has in some time. Investors will get their next test on Friday, when the government releases its monthly employment report.
