# Brooklyn Fire Kills 6 as Battery Fears Mount

A fast-moving, three-alarm fire tore through a two-story row house in Brooklyn's Sunset Park neighborhood early Thursday, killing six people and injuring nine others, including three firefighters and two police officers, according to the [FDNY](https://apnews.com/article/fatal-fire-brooklyn-new-york-bb3c0c9753d0e64ec994cc892f32170e). More than 200 first responders rushed to the scene on 58th Street between 6th and 7th Avenues, where flames were already blowing out every window.

All six victims were adults. Four were pronounced dead at the scene and will be identified by the Medical Examiner; two others — a man and a woman in their 50s — died while being transported to the hospital, [ABC7 New York](https://abc7ny.com/post/6-dead-3-alarm-fire-tears-sunset-park-home-brooklyn/20017265/) reported.

## A Fire Met at the Front Door

The call came in just after 1:30 a.m., and units arrived within about three minutes and 18 seconds to find heavy fire on both the first and second stories. "Upon arrival, we had fire on every floor," Kevin Woods, the FDNY's chief of fire operations, said at a news conference near the charred home. "Fire blowing out every window, meeting us at the front door."

The blaze escalated from a two-alarm to a three-alarm response as heavy smoke and flames poured from the building. Crews made aggressive pushes into the cellar, first, and second floors, but the fire partially collapsed the second-floor staircase, trapping occupants upstairs — forcing firefighters to use a portable ladder inside the home.

Firefighters rescued four people in all, including two who were trapped behind window bars at the rear of the two-unit building. Officials said those two would have died without the FDNY's swift action.

The fire, which destroyed one home and spread to at least one adjoining house, was brought under control in about three hours. The New York City Department of Buildings ordered a third damaged home vacated, and eight displaced residents were relocated to hotels with help from the American Red Cross.

## A Neighborhood in Mourning

Mayor Zohran Mamdani, who took office in January, visited the scene and pledged the city's support. "This was a devastating fire," he said. "It will take a long time for this neighborhood to mourn the neighbors they lost this morning and to recover from this immense grief, and as they do so and as we rebuild the city of New York will be here for them every step of the way."

The nine injured were all expected to survive. Among them, a man was treated for burns to his feet and a woman for smoke inhalation — both rescued from the building — while the firefighters and police officers suffered minor injuries.

## Battery Evidence, But No Confirmed Cause

Fire marshals are still investigating what sparked the blaze, and officials stressed that no cause has been determined. A lithium-ion battery was found at the scene, and a hazmat team responded, but it was unknown whether the device was related to the fire, the fire department said.

Mamdani said it was "too early" to determine the exact cause, but he pointed to a broader pattern of battery-linked dangers. "E-bike fires as a whole have increased to more than 400 fires this year," he told a local news broadcast, according to ABC7. "They are something that can cause people to lose their lives as well as their livelihoods. We are really looking at every avenue we have to keep them safe."

Accounts of how many batteries were found have varied. CBS News New York reported that hazmat teams were called after a lithium-ion battery was discovered, noting that the FDNY said such devices are not unusual inside homes.

## A Pattern of Deadly Battery Fires

The Sunset Park tragedy landed one week after another fatal fire that officials linked to a lithium-ion battery. That blaze at the Patterson Houses in the Bronx killed a mother and her three children — one of them a 10-year-old, according to [CBS News New York](https://www.cbsnews.com/newyork/news/brooklyn-fire-sunset-park-deadly-residential-building-fdny/).

Before Thursday's fire, seven people had died in lithium-ion battery fires in New York this year, ABC7 reported. The Associated Press, citing fire officials, said more than 400 fires in the city this year have been linked to lithium-ion batteries, leading to at least six deaths and more than 70 injuries.

The surge has been driven largely by e-bikes and e-scooters used by delivery workers, whose batteries are often charged indoors and overnight — sometimes with mismatched or aftermarket chargers. The devices can fail violently through a phenomenon known as thermal runaway, producing intense, fast-spreading fires that leave occupants little time to escape.

Fire officials also flagged a structural vulnerability specific to Sunset Park: its dense stock of early-20th-century "row-frame" homes that are made entirely of wood beneath vinyl siding. As [FOX 5 New York](https://www.fox5ny.com/news/6-killed-after-suspected-lithium-ion-battery-fire-traps-residents-inside-burning-brooklyn-home) reported, Chief Woods explained that the fast-moving flames were fueled by that construction, and officials feared the fire could race down the entire row of attached houses. The home that burned was built in 1915, according to city property records.

## What's Next

The FDNY fire marshal's office is conducting a comprehensive investigation into the cause of the blaze, and the Medical Examiner will formally identify the four victims pronounced dead at the scene. The outcome of that probe — and whether a battery is ultimately confirmed as the source — will shape how the city weighs new restrictions on the lithium-ion devices at the center of its mounting fire crisis.

For now, the neighborhood is left to reckon with a grief that arrived in the dead of night, in a fire that officials say could have claimed more lives were it not for the crews who met it at the door.
