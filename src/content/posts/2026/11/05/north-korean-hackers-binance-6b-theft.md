# North Korean Hackers Likely Behind $6.3B Binance Theft

Cybersecurity and blockchain intelligence firms have concluded that North Korean state-linked hackers were likely responsible for a record-setting cryptocurrency theft from Binance, the world's largest crypto exchange. Roughly $6.3 billion in digital assets were moved out of Binance wallets across ten blockchains in what analysts call the biggest crypto heist in history. The finding, later echoed by the FBI, renews concerns that Pyongyang is using stolen digital assets to fund its weapons programs.

## What happened

Binance disclosed the breach on October 30, saying hackers had stolen more than $6 billion in what appeared to be the largest cryptocurrency heist on record. According to [AP News](https://apnews.com/article/binance-cryptocurrency-security-hack-north-korea-550e9b876550f71f07098494fe735beb), the company reserved more than $6.34 billion of its own funds to cover stolen assets and worked with blockchain partners to freeze roughly $3 billion in affected assets. Deposits were unaffected, while withdrawals were suspended and expected to resume within a day or two.

Blockchain intelligence firm TRM attributed the attack to TraderTraitor, described as North Korea's elite hacking unit. "An attacker moved approximately USD 6.36 billion out of Binance wallets on ten blockchains, most of it in about 20 minutes," TRM said. "Several features of this attack match earlier North Korea-linked heists." The firm added: "We make this judgment with high confidence." Chainalysis, led by co-founder and CEO Jonathan Levin, corroborated the assessment.

"We are working nonstop to resolve this," Binance co-CEO Richard Teng said in a video posted to X. He said the vast majority of user assets sit in offline cold wallets and are moved to a hot wallet only when a user makes a withdrawal.

## FBI confirms, then political ambiguity

The [FBI](https://apnews.com/article/binance-crypto-hack-north-korea-fbi-883f18140c1e21ce0c38781e72cbe315) said Saturday that the theft was orchestrated by North Korean hackers tied to "malicious cyber activity" by TraderTraitor — the same actors behind a $1.5 billion Bybit hack in 2025. The bureau found TraderTraitor was converting stolen assets primarily into ethereum and asked exchanges and analytics firms to block transactions linked to identified addresses to prevent laundering.

Yet the political messaging was less certain. Asked whether North Korea was involved, President Donald Trump told reporters at the White House, "We're looking at that." Days later, at a Mar-a-Lago Halloween party, he downplayed certainty: "I'm not concerned. We're learning about it. We don't know if it's North Korea."

## Pyongyang pushes back

North Korea flatly denied involvement. In a statement carried by [AP News](https://apnews.com/article/north-korea-binance-cryptocurrency-heist-01c9b80d938dd67c747e268c410f265d), Kim Yo Jong, the influential sister of leader Kim Jong Un, called the FBI announcement "a trite provocation" and "an absurd rhetoric."

"This is not the first time that the U.S. has attempted to spread misunderstanding of the DPRK in the international community, blindly linking the DPRK with the 'cyber crime' incidents whenever they occur on an international scale," she said. On the same day, North Korea said it had tested a new cluster warhead for strategic cruise missiles.

## How it may have happened

The hack has raised questions about why stronger approval controls were not in place. "This looks like one signature that gave the attacker a never-expiring, full-access token to a Binance hot wallet," said Kok Tin Gan, co-founder and CEO of cybersecurity firm NyxLab. Teng confirmed the hot wallet was affected and said attackers used complex tactics, including a "self-destruct mechanism" to impede the investigation.

## The bigger picture

North Korea has increasingly turned to crypto theft to raise hard currency amid stringent sanctions. In December 2022, South Korea's spy agency estimated North Korean hackers had stolen about $1.1 billion in crypto and other virtual assets over the previous five years. The Binance heist dwarfs that total and the prior Bybit breach.

The theft lands at a sensitive moment for Binance, which has a turbulent history. Founded in 2017 by Changpeng Zhao, the exchange became the world's largest by trade volume. Zhao was sentenced to prison in 2024 after pleading guilty to violating the Bank Secrecy Act, then pardoned by Trump in October 2025 — a sequence that has drawn scrutiny of the company's political ties. As [AP News](https://apnews.com/article/binance-changpeng-zhao-hack-north-korea-trump-world-liberty-3255f83c8ca64131bd52ca9b4033c17c) noted, Binance — long accused of abetting crypto criminals — has now become a victim. Binance denies any quid pro quo.

The recovery race is underway. About half the stolen assets were frozen, but analysts expect hackers to launder the rest through mixers, tumblers, smaller exchanges and decentralized finance over months. "It's bank robbery, in this case, at the speed of the Internet," said Ari Redbord, chief policy officer at TRM Labs. "However, we can also track and trace those funds in real time... It's just this cat and mouse game."

## What to watch

Attention now turns to whether Binance resumes withdrawals on schedule and honors its promise of full reimbursement, and whether investigators can claw back more than the roughly $3 billion already frozen as attackers convert assets to ethereum. The episode also sharpens a geopolitical question: whether Washington will firm up its North Korea attribution and push for new counter-cyber cooperation and sanctions. As security experts warn, an attack of this scale may not remain the record for long.
