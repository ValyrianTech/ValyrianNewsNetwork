# Cold Air, Rain to Send China Temperatures to New Lows

A strong cold air mass combined with widespread precipitation will drive temperatures to new lows for the second half of 2026 across large parts of China, with heavy rain soaking the south and snow bearing down on the north over the coming days, according to a forecast bulletin from the [China Weather Network distributed by Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/politics/20261105/163dbaeeb88f4d4fab9ccc6033d81251/c.html).

The forecast, issued at 09:23 on 5 November and drawing on projections from the Central Meteorological Observatory, covers the period from 5 to 10 November and warns of travel hazards and potential secondary disasters from prolonged rainfall.

## Rain Spreads, Then Expands

Over the next two days (5-6 November), the area of precipitation across central and eastern China will shrink somewhat, with parts of the Jiangnan region seeing stronger rainfall and Northeast China experiencing frequent snowfall. The picture changes markedly on 7 November, when the rain band is expected to expand significantly, bringing notable snow to northern parts of North China and intensifying rain along the middle and lower reaches of the Yangtze River.

According to the bulletin, much of the south can expect moderate-to-heavy rain, with locally torrential downpours in eastern Guizhou and elsewhere. In the north, moderate-to-heavy snow or sleet is forecast for parts of the Ili River Valley, northeastern Inner Mongolia, Heilongjiang, and northern and eastern Jilin, with freezing rain possible locally in eastern Heilongjiang.

Into the weekend, the system is projected to strengthen. Northern Shanxi, northern Hebei, mountainous northern Beijing, and southwestern Liaoning could see blizzards or severe blizzards, while southern Anhui, southern Hubei, eastern Guizhou, northern Hunan, northern Jiangxi, and northwestern Zhejiang face heavy-to-torrential rain.

## Temperatures Hit New Lows Nationwide

Driven by the cold air and compounded by the wet conditions, temperatures across most of the country will fall to new lows for the second half of the year over the 5-10 November window. The chill will be felt first and hardest in the northeast: Changchun's high is forecast to drop below freezing on 6 November, the first sub-freezing high there this half-year, while Shenyang's high will reach only 3C on 7 November, described as chilly as late November.

In North China, the temperature trough is expected on 6-8 November, with cities such as Beijing and Shijiazhuang possibly seeing highs fall below 10C. Many areas are set to begin their transition into meteorological winter. The northwest sees its lows on 7-9 November, with Xining's high on 8 November forecast at just 4C, while the Huang-Huai region will see highs slip to just above 10C on 8 November.

Cooling reaches the south a little later, with lows around 9 November. Guiyang and Kunming could see highs of only around 10C, while Hangzhou and Wuhan hover near 15C. Combined with the rain, the effect will be a damp, penetrating chill. Even South China will see the heat recede: Guangzhou, Nanning, and Haikou are forecast to drop to around 25C on 9-10 November, becoming relatively comfortable.

The arrival of the cold snap fits a familiar seasonal pattern. A cold air process at the end of October already brought strong winds and cooling to Inner Mongolia, the Northeast, and North China, as [China News Service reported](https://www.chinanews.com.cn/sh/2026/10-29/10722533.shtml), and the latest system accelerates the country's shift toward winter.

## Risks for Travel and Daily Life

The meteorological department urged the public to guard against secondary disasters that continuous and locally intense rainfall could trigger, particularly in the mountainous Jiangnan region, where landslides and flooding are longstanding concerns. It also advised attention to traffic safety as rain, snow, and ice affect roads, rail, and air travel, especially in North and Northeast China where blizzards and freezing rain are in the forecast.

For the south, the damp chill raises heating and health concerns, and residents are advised to add clothing to stay warm. The winter wheat belt in the Huang-Huai region is entering dormancy, and early-season snow and low temperatures could affect late-autumn crops and greenhouse operations.

## What to Watch

Forecasts of this kind are model-based and subject to revision as the event approaches. Key questions to watch are whether the Central Meteorological Observatory upgrades the episode to an official cold-wave (Hanchao) warning given the projected temperature drops, and how the cooling affects the start of the heating season in northern cities.

The event coincides with the approach of Lidong, the start-of-winter solar term that falls around 7 November, making this the first significant cooling episode of the season and a signal that winter is arriving ahead of schedule across much of the country.
