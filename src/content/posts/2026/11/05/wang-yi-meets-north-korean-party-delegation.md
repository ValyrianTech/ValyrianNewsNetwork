# Wang Yi Meets North Korean Party Delegation in Beijing

Chinese Foreign Minister Wang Yi met in Beijing on 5 November with a delegation of North Korea's Workers' Party of Korea (WPK) led by Kim Song-nam, the latest in an unusually dense sequence of high-level exchanges between Beijing and Pyongyang. According to [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/politics/leaders/20261105/cc7dbfa0637d4b2ab3b4d26b01336f0a/c.html), the two sides pledged to implement the "important consensus" of their top leaders and to push China-DPRK relations to develop "deeper, more substantively and favorably."

Wang, a member of the CPC Political Bureau and director of the Office of the Central Commission for Foreign Affairs, received Kim — a WPK Political Bureau member, secretary of the WPK Central Committee, and director of the party's International Department — in a meeting that the [International Department of the CPC Central Committee](https://www.idcpc.gov.cn/ttxw_2992/wzxw/202611/t20261105_170566.html) described in fuller detail than the standard Xinhua dispatch.

## A relationship being consolidated

The meeting's substance lay less in new commitments than in the work of translating summit-level agreements into concrete cooperation. Wang pointed to Xi Jinping's June 2026 state visit to Pyongyang — his first since 2019 — as having "charted the way forward for the development of China-DPRK relations in the new era."

Wang restated Xi's "three won'ts" formulation: that China's high regard for traditional friendship, its support for Kim Jong Un's socialist cause, and its determination to safeguard shared interests and the strategic environment "will not change." The full IDCPC readout also recorded Wang's pledge to "push China-DPRK relations to develop deeper, more substantively and favorably, better benefiting the two countries and two peoples."

The Xinhua summary distributed to all media carried a shorter version, noting that Wang "introduced the main situation of the CPC 20th Central Committee's Fifth Plenum" and that Kim "expressed the DPRK's willingness to follow the will of the two countries' top leaders and make unremitting efforts to develop friendly DPRK-China relations."

## A party-to-party channel front and center

Unusually for a foreign-policy readout, much of the meeting concerned party governance. Wang briefed Kim on the CPC's Fifth Plenum, held 26–29 October, whose most important outcome was the adoption of a decision on "persistently advancing full and rigorous party self-governance." He offered to strengthen exchanges of "party-governance and state-governance experience."

Kim reciprocated, congratulating the CPC on the plenum and expressing willingness to deepen exchanges on party-building. That framing reflects a channel Xi explicitly endorsed in June, when he called for expanding "party-to-party exchanges at all levels" — a low-cost, high-symbolism instrument that gives Pyongyang a way to engage Beijing outside the nuclear and security file.

## Dandong and the economic opening

Kim's remarks also reached back to Kim Jong Un's surprise one-day visit to the Chinese border city of Dandong on 24 October, timed to the 76th anniversary of the Chinese People's Volunteers entering the Korean War. As [Lianhe Zaobao](https://www.zaobao.com.sg/news/world/story20261024-9889052) reported, Kim toured the CPV Memorial Hall and a Dandong automobile plant, and both sides floated expanding trade and person-to-person exchanges on the occasion of the New Yalu River Bridge's opening.

That bridge, completed in 2014 but idle for more than a decade, formally opened to traffic on 15 October 2026 — a milestone analysts read as a signal of renewed cross-border economic cooperation. In Beijing, Kim Song-nam cited the Dandong trip as "once again proving the strategic character" of DPRK-China friendship, "forged in blood and made firmer in the course of history."

## What's next

The tempo of engagement is striking. Kim Jong Un visited Beijing in September 2025; Xi made a state visit to Pyongyang in June 2026; Wang Huning, the CPPCC chairman, visited the DPRK in July and accompanied Kim in Dandong in October. [Yonhap](https://cn.yna.co.kr/view/ACK20261104001800881) reported that Kim Song-nam's delegation arrived in Beijing on 3 November, received by Sun Haiyan, vice-minister of the CPC International Department, and Ri Ryong-nam, the DPRK ambassador to China.

Notably absent from the Chinese readout were any references to denuclearization, the Korean Peninsula, or the United States — a deliberate framing that presents ties as stable and deepening around "socialism" and "development" rather than security bargaining. The visit also ran parallel to North Korea's launch of multiple missiles toward the East Sea on 4 November, a reminder that diplomacy and military signaling continue side by side.

Observers will watch whether the meeting prepares further high-level exchanges — possibly around the 65th anniversary of the 1961 China-DPRK Treaty of Friendship, Cooperation and Mutual Assistance — and whether it yields concrete border and trade deliverables. For now, the message Beijing and Pyongyang chose to project is one of continuity: a strategic relationship being steadily bound tighter through party channels and economic opening.
