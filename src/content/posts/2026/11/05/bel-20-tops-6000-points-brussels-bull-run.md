# Bel-20 Tops 6,000 Points as Brussels Bull Run Roars

Belgium's benchmark Bel-20 index crossed the 6,000-point threshold for the first time in its 35-year history on Thursday morning, climbing to 6,031 points shortly after the Brussels bourse opened. The milestone extends a record run that saw the index close at 5,979 points the previous day, and marks a symbolic return to the heady levels last seen before the 2008 financial crisis, [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/05/bel20-index-voorbij-kaap-van-6-000-punten/) reported.

## A "Finally" Moment for Brussels

For financial economist Tom Simonts of KBC, the round number carries weight beyond the arithmetic. "We are finally at a level where we participate again," he told VRT NWS, describing a sense that Belgium has caught up with its larger European peers. "We have finally reached the level where we participate with the other indices and where we have surpassed the level of before the financial crisis."

The Bel-20 tracks the 20 largest companies on the Brussels exchange, Euronext Brussels. Launched in 1991 at a base value of 1,000 points, it took 18 years to surpass its 2007 record, a barrier finally cleared in August 2025 when the index hit 4,760 points, [according to VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2025/07/31/brusselse-bel-20-slaat-pas-na-18-jaar-de-financiele-crisis-achte/). Since that breakthrough, the index has climbed more than a quarter — rising roughly 27% from 4,760 to 6,031 points in about 15 months.

## The "Classic Champions" Behind the Climb

Simonts attributes the surge chiefly to four heavyweight constituents. "AB InBev posted strong figures. The banks, and therefore also KBC, keep doing well. UCB and Argenx have become champions in the pharma and biotech sector," he said. Smaller index members have also performed well and are finally being valued correctly by investors, he added.

The rally is notable for what it lacks: Belgium has no artificial-intelligence, semiconductor, or broader technology champions of the kind that have powered Wall Street. "Recently we have generally excelled in the absence of big players," Simonts said. "That put us at a disadvantage, and we now compensate with an abundance of solid companies doing well. They may not be technology champions, but they are classic champions."

## A Post-Midterms Rotation Toward Blue Chips

The record arrived one day after the US midterm elections, and Simonts sees a connection. "The midterms certainly play a role," he said, pointing to a rotation away from tech and AI stocks and back toward "blue chip" names. The Dow Jones — an index of 30 classic companies — rose more than 3.1% the day before, outpacing the tech-heavy Nasdaq, a movement now visible on European exchanges as well.

He also argued that geopolitical turbulence has done little to dent appetite for Belgian equities, because many Bel-20 companies sit far from the sectors most exposed to those pressures, such as energy. "There is still more than enough support," he said. "The geopolitical situation has an impact on, for instance, the energy crisis, but many companies within the Bel-20 are far removed from that."

## Is the Rally Justified?

On the question of whether 6,000 points reflects an overshoot, Simonts is unequivocal. "I think this is a justified movement. The Belgian stock exchange is not expensive, so there is no reason to say this rise is exaggerated and unrealistic."

Yet the new all-time high comes against a changed tax backdrop. Belgium's capital gains tax — the "solidarity contribution" introduced by the De Wever government — took effect on 1 January 2026, imposing a 10% levy on realised gains on financial assets above a €10,000 annual exemption. The tax creates a fresh incentive for investors to crystallise profits at record levels, a tension Simonts acknowledged. "Normally I would say you should hold your shares when things are going well, as they are now," he said. "But of course there is, since recently, the capital gains tax. Through that tax you can ask yourself whether you shouldn't sell your shares with profit now — although that of course runs counter to long-term investing."

## What to Watch

Several open questions will determine whether 6,000 becomes a plateau or a launchpad. The rally leans heavily on four heavyweight stocks, making concentration risk a genuine factor. The index's lack of tech exposure has been a persistent growth handicap, and whether the current rotation toward classic blue chips proves durable remains uncertain. And with Belgians recently shifting from net savers toward active investors, the record could just as easily accelerate retail inflows as trigger a profit-taking wave.

For now, the Bel-20 has its headline. After years of lagging, the Brussels market is, in Simonts's word, participating again.
