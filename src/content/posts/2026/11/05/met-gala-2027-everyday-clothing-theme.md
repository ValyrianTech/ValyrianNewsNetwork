# Met Gala 2027 to Celebrate Everyday Clothing

The Metropolitan Museum of Art's Costume Institute announced Thursday that its spring 2027 exhibition and the accompanying Met Gala will be themed "extraORDINARY: Fashion and the Poetics of the Everyday," a show celebrating jeans, T-shirts, puffer jackets and humble materials like paper and plastic, according to [AP News](https://apnews.com/article/met-gala-new-theme-2027-galliano-d30048b6dec7598158993aba9315c552). The announcement marks a sharp pivot from a glittering John Galliano retrospective that was scrapped in August amid an outcry over the British designer's past antisemitic statements.

## A Show Assembled Under Pressure

The replacement exhibit is being organized by Andrew Bolton, the Costume Institute's curator in charge, who acknowledged the scramble to build a new concept after the Galliano show was canceled under pressure from donors, city officials and the Jewish community.

"I had no Plan B, to be honest," Bolton told the AP. "There wasn't another exhibition already researched and ready to go." He said the idea had occurred to him while working on the prior year's "Costume Art" exhibition but had remained merely "a twinkle in my eye."

Bolton framed the new concept not as a hasty substitute but as a deliberately challenging show with contemporary resonance. "I wanted it to be as challenging and as provocative and as beautiful as a John show would have been," he told [Vogue](https://www.vogue.com/article/the-met-gala-2027-theme-is-extraordinary-fashion-and-the-poetics-of-the-everyday). "That was really important to me: to come up with an idea that wasn't a substitute. And it had to have some sort of contemporary resonance."

## Two Directions, One Theme

The exhibition approaches its subject from two angles. The first elevates archetypal garments — tees, jeans, trenches and puffer jackets — while the second examines how humble, even "disposable" materials, from paper to aluminum to plastic, are transformed into wearable art. Named galleries include "Familiar Forms" and "Common Matter."

"Everyday clothes carry extraordinary amounts of meaning," Bolton told the AP. "We have a favorite T-shirt, we have a favorite pair of jeans, and we wear them until they literally fall apart. And those meanings develop through wearing."

Featured designers include Jun Takahashi of Undercover, Kunihiko Morinaga of Anrealage, Jonathan Anderson of Loewe, Virgil Abloh of Off-White, Gianfranco Ferré, Burberry, Pierpaolo Piccioli for Moncler, Maison Margiela, Comme des Garçons, Matthieu Blazy of Bottega Veneta, Junya Watanabe, Helmut Lang, Vetements and Ellen Hodakova Larsson.

Bolton said a paper dress can demand as much skill and labor as a silk gown. "The cost of a material tells us very little about the craftsmanship required to transform it," he told Vogue. "It will make us question: what do we overlook, what do we discard, what makes something worth keeping?"

## The Galliano Cancellation

The original retrospective, announced with fanfare in late July 2026, was scrapped about a month later. The museum had promised to confront Galliano's "derogatory conduct that fundamentally altered his career and public reception," a reference to racist and antisemitic comments from 2010 and 2011 that went viral and led to his conviction in an antisemitism trial in France. He was fired as creative director of Christian Dior and later spent a decade at Maison Margiela.

The 2027 gala, scheduled for May 3, would have fallen on Yom HaShoah, Holocaust Remembrance Day, intensifying objections to a tribute. New York City Council Speaker Julie Menin, the daughter of a Holocaust survivor, had met repeatedly with museum leaders to voice opposition and called the cancellation "the correct decision, as the harm and pain this was causing was simply unacceptable," the AP reported.

Galliano said in a statement at the time that he remained "fully accountable for the pain caused by my words in the past" and "deeply sorry" for the hurt caused to the Jewish and Asian communities. Anna Wintour, Vogue's global editorial director who oversees the gala and has long supported Galliano, called his decision "very courageous" and said both he and the museum had her full support.

## Dates and Sponsorship

The Met Gala is traditionally held the first Monday in May and serves as the Costume Institute's star-studded fundraiser. The 2027 gala is set for May 3. The exhibition opens to the public May 9, 2027, and runs through January 9, 2028. Dress code and celebrity hosts will be announced at a later date.

The show is being staged by the London-based design studio 1009, intended to create a dialogue between the installation and the visitors moving through it. It is sponsored by Instagram, with major funding from Moncler and additional support from Condé Nast; education programs are made possible by Jordan Roth.

Bolton told Vogue he would like visitors to wear "their favorite t-shirt or their favorite pair of jeans or their favorite hoodie" through the show. "There'll be a humanity to it," he said.

## What to Watch

With the theme now set, attention turns to the gala's celebrity hosts and dress code, which the museum has yet to reveal. The broader question — whether a show built on quiet, familiar garments can command the same cultural wattage as the blockbuster it replaces — will be answered when the doors open in May.
