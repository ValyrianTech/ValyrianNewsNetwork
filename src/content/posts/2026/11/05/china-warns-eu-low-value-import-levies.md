# China Warns EU Over Levies on Low-Value Imports

China's Ministry of Commerce said on November 5 that it is "highly concerned" about the European Union's escalating charges on low-value imported goods, wading into a deepening trade dispute over the rules governing cross-border e-commerce parcels. The statement, delivered by ministry spokesperson Huang Ling at a regular press conference in Beijing, came days after a new EU per-parcel fee took effect, stacking on top of a customs duty already in force since July. [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/fortune/20261105/59dfce3d7424459b8fb7d02fea97f9e2/c.html) reported the remarks.

The two charges together bring fixed per-parcel costs on low-value shipments to roughly 5 euros — a significant burden for the direct-to-consumer model that has powered the rapid growth of Chinese platforms such as Temu and SHEIN across Europe.

## A Two-Layer Cost Structure

The EU's 3-euro flat-rate customs duty took effect on July 1, 2026, after the Council gave final approval on February 11, 2026, abolishing the long-standing duty exemption for parcels valued under 150 euros. Under the interim regime, the duty is levied per item category — identified by tariff sub-heading — rather than per parcel, and runs until July 1, 2028. [The Council of the EU](https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/press/press-releases/2026/02/11/council-gives-final-green-light-to-new-customs-duty-rules-for-small-parcels/) illustrated the methodology with a simple example: a parcel containing one silk blouse and two wool blouses holds two distinct item categories, meaning 6 euros in duty is owed.

A separate 2-euro per-parcel handling fee took effect on November 1, 2026. According to Xinhua, the two charges apply cumulatively to low-value imports, lifting the combined fixed cost to about 5 euros per parcel.

The EU framed the measures as a response to unfair competition. Because such parcels previously entered the bloc duty-free, European sellers argued they were undercut by low-cost imports shipped directly to consumers.

> As global e-commerce booms, EU customs rules must keep pace. Abolishing the out-of-date exemption for small parcels will help support EU business and shut down avenues for unscrupulous sellers.
>
> — Makis Keravnos, Minister of Finance of Cyprus, whose country held the rotating EU Council presidency at the time of the February approval

## The Scale of the Flow

The stakes are substantial. According to the European Commission, 4.6 billion small packages entered the EU market in 2024, and package volumes have doubled every year since 2022. Crucially, **91% of small shipments arrive from China** — meaning the new charges fall disproportionately on Chinese exporters and the platforms that serve them.

Beijing's response has been measured. Rather than threatening retaliation, Huang emphasized the value of cross-border e-commerce as a legitimate form of trade.

> China is highly concerned about the EU's relevant new rules. Cross-border e-commerce, as a new form and model of international trade, provides diversified choices for global enterprises and consumers. We hope the EU will prudently assess the impact of the relevant policies and create favorable conditions for China-EU cooperation in e-commerce and other fields.
>
> — Huang Ling, spokesperson for China's Ministry of Commerce

## Analysis: A Careful Balancing Act

The tone of Huang's statement is itself revealing. By expressing concern and urging a "prudent assessment" rather than announcing countermeasures, Beijing appears to be keeping the dispute contained — a signal that China is reluctant to let trade friction with the EU escalate while it manages other economic pressures.

The measures arrive amid a broader Western push against the de minimis model that Chinese platforms built their growth on. The United States revoked its own de minimis exemption for China and Hong Kong in May 2025, then suspended it for all countries in August 2025 — creating a transatlantic squeeze on low-value direct shipping.

Early market signals suggest the costs are already reshaping behavior. Reports indicate Temu and SHEIN have begun scaling back European advertising and shifting logistics strategies in response to the higher per-parcel costs.

## What's Next

The 3-euro interim duty is scheduled to run until July 1, 2028, when it is expected to be replaced by normal customs tariffs once the EU's planned customs data hub becomes operational. Negotiations between the Council and the European Parliament on the broader customs reform package — including the creation of a new EU customs authority — remain ongoing.

For now, implementation is uneven. Reports indicate that Italy has repeatedly postponed its application of the 2-euro surcharge for non-EU low-value parcels, highlighting the operational complexity of rolling out the regime uniformly across member states.

The core questions are whether the combined charges will meaningfully curb parcel volumes from China, and whether Beijing's consultative posture holds if the economic impact on its platforms deepens. For a trade relationship already tested by tariffs and subsidy disputes, the parcels regime has become a new front — one measured in euros per parcel, but with consequences far larger than the sums involved.
