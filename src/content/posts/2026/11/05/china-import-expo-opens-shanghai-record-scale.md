# China's Import Expo Opens in Shanghai at Record Scale

The ninth China International Import Expo (CIIE) opened in Shanghai on Thursday, running through November 10 at the National Exhibition and Convention Center, as organizers reported record participation in what Beijing presents as the definitive symbol of its commitment to high-level opening-up. According to [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/fortune/20261105/602d0af8b0ee41059f11cc95206ef284/c.html), participants from 162 countries, regions and international organizations are taking part, and the total exhibition area surpassed 440,000 square meters for the first time.

Held in the opening year of China's 15th Five-Year Plan, the expo is being framed by state media as an "oriental appointment that benefits the world" — a platform through which, in Xinhua's framing, foreign firms access the Chinese market and China signals its continued integration into global trade despite what it describes as a complex and volatile external environment.

## Context: A flagship fair in a new plan cycle

The CIIE was launched in 2018, when President Xi Jinping announced it as "a major decision China made to advance a new round of high-level opening-up and a major move to proactively open its market to the world." It has since become an annual fixture and China's flagship import-themed trade fair, positioned as a showcase for market opening and a node in the country's "dual circulation" economic strategy.

The 2026 edition carries additional significance because the plan's outline devotes a dedicated chapter to expanding high-level opening-up and explicitly calls for the expo to be "continuously run well." A China Pavilion themed "Opening new prospects, co-winning a new future" is designed to present the economic vision of the five-year plan period.

## What's new: records on scale and first-time exhibitors

This year's figures mark several firsts. A record 4,142 foreign enterprises are exhibiting, and for the first time more than 300 Fortune Global 500 and industry-leading companies are taking part. More than 2,000 firms are attending for the first time, while nearly 200 companies and institutions have participated in all nine editions — the so-called "full-attendance" exhibitors.

Foreign exhibition delegations exceed 90, with their combined exhibition area up nearly 8% year-on-year. Medical equipment and healthcare, consumer goods, and agricultural and food products were the zones fully expanded this year, which organizers say reflects the breadth of Chinese consumer demand.

US companies remain the largest single national contingent, with exhibition space exceeding 50,000 square meters and ranking first for an eighth consecutive edition — up 1,500 square meters from last year. Canadian and New Zealand firms occupy their largest-ever footprints, and Australian exhibitors expanded their space by 25% year-on-year. Canada is this year's guest country of honor.

The US corporate presence is notable given continued friction in bilateral trade relations; organizers point to it as evidence that commercial engagement endures beneath the political noise.

## Innovation and the 'ecosystem' pitch

Organizers report more than 480 representative innovation achievements on display, with "global debut, Asia premiere and China debut" events at record highs. The innovation incubation zone gathers 633 technology startup projects from around 40 countries and regions, up roughly 20% from the previous edition.

Qualcomm, exhibiting for a ninth straight year, is showing a 6G terminal prototype publicly for the first time. "Each year's participation is not only keeping an appointment but a journey of co-innovation and co-growth with Chinese industry partners," Meng Pu, chairman of Qualcomm China, was quoted as saying by Xinhua. Schneider Electric, a "full-attendance" exhibitor, has now built four World Economic Forum-designated "lighthouse factories" in China; its executive vice-president Yin Zheng said China has become its second-largest global market, most important supply-chain base, and one of four R&D bases.

Germany's NürnbergMesse — a major trade-fair organizer that typically runs events rather than attends them — is exhibiting in its own right for the first time. Its chief executive, Peter Ottmann, told Xinhua that China is "both a major global source of products and innovation and an important market for international firms."

## Inclusion: zero tariffs and free booths

The expo continues to reserve free booths for exhibitors from least-developed countries, whose booth area rose 17% this year, with the Asia-Africa products zone upgraded to help African countries capture zero-tariff benefits. China has extended full zero-tariff treatment to 53 African countries with which it maintains diplomatic ties.

Crediting the policy, importer Xinrongmao reported South African citrus imports up 50% year-on-year. Peruvian large-berry fruit — shipped via the 23-day direct sea link from Chancay Port to Shanghai under the upgraded China–Peru free trade agreement — will have its global debut at the fair.

Grace Mbabazi, chief executive of Rwanda's M&M Sozo, said free booths "greatly reduced our costs, making it possible to travel across the ocean to exhibit" — a point echoed by Zambia's UN envoy in Geneva, who said that "regardless of economy size, all exhibitors have equal opportunities to showcase and connect."

## Analysis: Certainty as a counterweight

The expo's messaging is unmistakably political as much as commercial. Xinhua explicitly contrasts a "century of change" and a turbulent global environment with the CIIE as a source of "certainty" — positioning the annual fair as a stabilizing anchor for a global economy buffeted by trade tension and geopolitical fragmentation.

The companion Hongqiao International Economic Forum, held concurrently under the theme "Stable cooperation, jointly building an open world economy," will release the *World Openness Report 2026* with its openness index. Notably, it will also debut the *Export China Capability Development Report* — described as the world's first research study that, from the perspective of trading partners, catalogs the opportunities in the Chinese market. The framing shift from "what China buys" to "what partner countries can sell, and how to sell it better" suggests a deliberate effort to present the expo as a two-way platform rather than a showcase for imports alone.

According to [Guangming Daily](https://news.gmw.cn/2026-11/05/content_39085865.htm), the organizers emphasize that exhibitors increasingly graduate from visitors to investors — a "from attending the expo to growing roots in China" narrative that underpins claims of long-term value. The paper reported that 169 companies and 30 foreign exhibition bodies have attended every one of the nine editions.

## Caveats and what to watch

The expo's headline figures originate primarily from Xinhua and state-affiliated media, are consistent across outlets, but are official statistics without independent third-party audit. The framing of the expo as a benevolently world-benefiting platform is a state-media editorial stance; readers should weigh it against broader debates over Chinese market access, trade policy and geopolitical tensions that the primary sources do not address.

What to watch in the coming days: the openness index numbers released by the Hongqiao Forum, the outcome of trade-matching sessions among the 4,142 foreign exhibitors, and whether the "expo to investment" conversion yields measurable commitments. Within China's broader push to stabilize foreign investment and consumption in the first year of the 15th Five-Year Plan, the CIIE's ability to convert pageantry into signed deals will be the real test of its "growing magnetism."
