# China's Han Zheng to Attend COP31 Summit, Then Visit Georgia

China announced on 4 November that Vice-President Han Zheng will travel to Turkey next week to attend the leaders' summit of the 31st Conference of the Parties (COP31) to the UN Framework Convention on Climate Change, before making an official visit to Georgia. The trip, running from 6 to 12 November, will see Han represent President Xi Jinping as his special representative at the Antalya Leaders' Summit, according to [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/world/20261104/75fb1ccd477f4868b2b3f615a570acdd/c.html), China's state news agency.

## A Signal of Political Weight

The decision to dispatch the vice-president — a former senior Politburo Standing Committee member — rather than an environment minister is notable. It signals the political importance Beijing attaches to a summit taking place at a fragile moment for global climate cooperation, and positions China to shape the narrative on the sidelines.

Han's role as Xi's special representative places him at head-of-state-equivalent level for the gathering. The Antalya Leaders' Summit, hosted by Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan, runs on 11 and 12 November under the theme "Towards a Sustainable, Resilient and Equitable Future," forming the first part of the High-Level Segment for heads of state and government, according to the [UNFCCC](https://unfccc.int/cop31/high-level). COP31 itself runs from 9 to 20 November in Antalya.

The summit convenes amid weak global momentum. In September 2026, the UN acknowledged for the first time that the world is set to overshoot the 1.5°C target within a few years. COP31 also features an unusual arrangement: Turkey hosts the conference while Australia leads the official negotiations.

## Climate Finance at the Forefront

As the world's largest carbon emitter, China is a pivotal player in any global outcome. Its 2035 nationally determined contribution targets include raising the non-fossil share of energy consumption from 20% to about 30%, reaching roughly 3,600 gigawatts of solar and wind capacity, and making electric vehicles the mainstream transport mode.

The most urgent issue on the table, climate justice groups say, is adaptation finance. A UN report this week found that money flowing to help poorer countries cope with the climate crisis has been flat or falling since 2022, with flows reaching at most $32bn in 2024 against an estimated need of $305bn annually by 2035 for 155 developing nations, [The Guardian reported](https://www.theguardian.com/global-development/2026/nov/04/funding-poorer-countries-climate-crisis-adaptation-un-report). A newly established loss and damage fund is also already overwhelmed: 119 countries requested $2.8bn in the first round against initial funding of just $342m.

How China positions itself on these finance debates — and whether it offers new commitments — will be closely watched, particularly as the United States has retreated from international climate leadership.

## The Georgia Leg

The second phase of Han's trip, an official visit to Georgia, underscores China's incremental expansion into the South Caucasus. The two countries upgraded to a strategic partnership in July 2023, building on a free trade agreement signed in 2017.

According to [Carnegie Politika](https://carnegieendowment.org/russia-eurasia/politika/2023/08/whats-behind-chinas-strategic-partnership-with-georgia), the partnership advances China's push for the Middle Corridor — a trade route running through Central Asia, the Caspian and the Black Sea or via Turkey — as an alternative to the northern route through Russia disrupted by the war in Ukraine. It could also draw Chinese involvement in Georgia's Anaklia port, which sits on the shortest route from China to Europe. For Tbilisi, the relationship serves a multi-vector foreign policy, balancing Russia while extracting leverage from the EU.

## What to Watch

Han's travel window — arriving before the leaders' summit opens on 11 November and departing on its final day — leaves limited time for the Georgia visit, suggesting that leg may be brief or extend slightly beyond the stated dates.

For Beijing, the twin trip projects an image of a responsible major power engaged on both climate and connectivity. Whether that translates into concrete finance commitments at COP31 — and tangible infrastructure deals in Tbilisi — are the questions that will define the trip's legacy.
