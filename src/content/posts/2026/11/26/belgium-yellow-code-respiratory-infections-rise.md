# Belgium Enters Yellow Code as Respiratory Infections Rise

Belgium has been placed under yellow code for respiratory infections, the second of four alert levels, as Covid-19, influenza, RSV and pneumococcus circulate more heavily with the arrival of the winter season. The federal public health institute [Sciensano](https://www.rtbf.be/article/covid-grippe-toux-les-infections-respiratoires-augmentent-sciensano-place-la-belgique-en-code-jaune-11821521) announced the move on 26 November, stressing that pressure on the healthcare system remains under control.

"Every year, during the winter months, we see a peak period characterised by greater circulation of respiratory viruses (flu, Covid-19, RSV and pneumococcus), which leads to an increase in respiratory infections and greater pressure on the healthcare system," Sciensano said. "Currently, we are in yellow code."

## What the Yellow Code Means

The alert was set under the Winter Plan for Respiratory Infections, which [distinguishes four levels](https://www.health.belgium.be/fr/themes/sante/lutte-contre-maladies-infectieuses/plan-hivernal-infections-respiratoires) — green, yellow, orange and red — defined by the intensity of viral circulation and the pressure on the healthcare system.

Green corresponds to low viral circulation with healthcare pressure under control. Yellow, currently in effect, signals increased viral circulation while pressure on the system remains under control. Orange means high circulation and growing pressure, historically accompanied by a mask recommendation. Red, the maximum level, denotes very high circulation and an overload of the healthcare system.

In short, the yellow code is a routine, mid-tier precautionary upgrade rather than an emergency — the standard marker of the winter respiratory season's onset in Belgium.

## Daily Habits and Ventilation Urged

Under yellow code, the basic recommendations continue to apply: wash hands regularly with soap and water, cough or sneeze into a paper tissue or your elbow, keep vaccinations up to date and, if unwell, stay home to avoid infecting others.

Sciensano adds further advice for the yellow phase. Residents are asked to air out their homes several times a day for several minutes. Those with a ventilation system — whether extraction-based or dual-flow — should ensure it works properly, maintain it regularly and adjust its power according to the number of people in a room.

The Risk Management Group of the FPS Public Health pointed to those most vulnerable to respiratory infections who should be especially vigilant: people over 65, those with chronic illnesses, people with immune disorders, pregnant women and infants. The group stressed that vaccination remains the best protection and that it is high time to catch up if not already done.

## A Predictable Seasonal Cycle

Belgium runs this four-colour alert system every year, and the current upgrade follows a familiar pattern. During the previous season, yellow code was activated on 19 December 2025, escalated to orange code with a mask recommendation on 16 January 2026, eased as spring approached in late February, and returned to green code on 20 March 2026.

Sciensano has coordinated influenza surveillance since 2006, when it was designated by the Interministerial Influenza Committee, a mandate later extended to RSV and Covid-19. The institute integrates data from sentinel GP networks, the National Reference Centre for respiratory pathogens, winter mortality analysis and wastewater analysis, publishing results weekly in its acute respiratory infections bulletin.

Beyond monitoring, Sciensano coordinates the Risk Assessment Group, which evaluates public health risk and proposes measures to health authorities through the Risk Management Group.

## What to Watch

Neither the announcement nor the official winter plan page published specific incidence figures for the current week; those are tracked in Sciensano's interactive dashboards and weekly bulletin. The key indicators to follow are incidence per 100,000 for each virus, GP workload and hospital admissions.

If circulation intensifies and hospital pressure grows, the likely next step is a move to orange code — as happened in mid-January 2026 — which would bring a fresh mask recommendation. If the season proves mild, the code may stay yellow or revert to green.

For now, the message from health authorities is one of vigilance rather than alarm: the viruses are spreading more widely, but the healthcare system is holding. The practical takeaway is simple — get vaccinated if you haven't, ventilate indoor spaces, and stay home when sick.
