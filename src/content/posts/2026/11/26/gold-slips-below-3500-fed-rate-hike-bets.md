# Gold Slips Below $3,500 as Fed Rate-Hike Bets Bite

Spot gold broke below the $3,500-per-ounce mark intraday on November 25, its lowest level since September 2025, while spot silver fell below $40 an ounce, as a hotter-than-expected US inflation report hardened expectations that the Federal Reserve will raise interest rates in December. According to [The Paper](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_34558960), spot gold was down 2.09% at $3,480.99 an ounce at time of publication, and spot silver was down 3.51% at $39.468 an ounce. Year-to-date, spot gold has fallen nearly 20%.

## A Hawkish Inflation Surprise

The trigger was the release of US October PCE inflation data. Headline PCE rose 3.8% year-on-year, in line with expectations and up from 3.6% prior, but core PCE climbed 3.3% year-on-year—above expectations—and 0.3% month-on-month for a third consecutive month. Analysts said the reading made a December rate hike "virtually certain," and spot gold's decline widened after the data landed.

The [China Fund Report](https://finance.eastmoney.com/a/202611263940195628.html) noted that the core reading exceeded expectations of 3.2% and a prior 3.1%, hitting its highest level since November 2023, while headline PCE reached its highest since May 2023. That reinforced market expectations of Fed hikes in both December and January. Markets widely expect a 25-basis-point increase at the December 8–9 FOMC meeting.

A second and third driver compounded the selloff. A liquidity squeeze following a sharp tech-stock selloff pushed investors to "sell gold to cover stock losses," while a strengthening US dollar added further pressure, The Paper reported.

## A Dramatic Late Reversal

The intraday picture told only half the story. Despite the plunge, US markets staged a striking recovery into the close. COMEX gold, which had fallen nearly 4% intraday, closed up 0.47% on the day. The Dow Jones Industrial Average, down as much as 1,000 points intraday, closed up 0.33% at 50,182.95. The S&P 500 ended down 0.25% at 7,182.78 and the Nasdaq closed down 0.62% at 23,585.8.

Chinese assets were a bright spot: the Nasdaq Golden Dragon China Index rose 1.87%. The reversal came alongside a sharp upward revision to US third-quarter growth, with real GDP annualized revised from 3.4% to 4.3%. A Federal Reserve Beige Book released the same day showed wage and benefit growth accelerating, further reinforcing the December hike case.

## Structural Support Versus Cyclical Pain

The divergence between London spot prices and COMEX futures reflects the tension now defining the gold market: near-term cyclical headwinds against deeply structural long-term supports. Higher interest rates raise the opportunity cost of holding non-yielding gold, pressuring prices and Western ETF demand. Yet major institutions remain constructive.

UBS Global Wealth Management reiterated an "attractive" rating on gold and forecast it could reach $5,000 an ounce by December 2027, arguing gold's value lies in strategic allocation rather than short-term trading. The bank cited four long-term supports: central banks adjusting foreign-exchange reserves and reducing reliance on traditional reserve assets; high and growing global sovereign debt; persistent geopolitical uncertainty; and Chinese market gold demand.

Goldman Sachs expects further Fed hikes in December 2026 and March 2027, which it said will continue to weigh on Western gold ETF demand, but still projects gold could reach $5,600 by end-2028. CSC Financial framed the medium-term case around a "weak dollar trade," suggesting that if US equities see a deeper correction in 2027, overseas investors may shift from trimming US stocks to reducing overall US asset exposure—potentially giving gold more durable allocation buying.

## What to Watch

The immediate focus is the December FOMC meeting and the Fed's policy path afterward. CZBank International said the key questions are the trajectory of policy following the December hike and the path of real interest rates in 2027, while noting that central bank gold purchases and reserve diversification continue to underpin long-term structural demand.

For now, the November selloff illustrates how quickly the rate-hike regime can whipsaw precious metals—and how sharply the market can reverse within a single session. Whether gold can hold the $3,500 line will depend less on the metal itself than on the inflation data and central-bank signals that drive the dollar and real yields.
