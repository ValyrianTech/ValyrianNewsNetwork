# Electricity network tariffs to rise €35 a year in Flanders

Flemish households will pay about 35 euros more a year for electricity network tariffs in 2027, as the regulated grid charges rise even as wholesale energy markets swing wildly. According to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/liveblog/nettarieven-voor-elektriciteit-stijgen-volgend-jaar-met-35-euro-~1774343778229/), the electricity network tariff for an average family will climb 7.6 percent to roughly 498 euros a year — the highest level since 2018. Gas network tariffs, by contrast, will fall 3.5 percent, or about 9 euros, to some 267 euros.

The increases were approved by the Flemish energy regulator, the Vlaamse Nutsregulator (VNR), and add a structural cost to household bills at a time when energy prices and inflation are already near multi-year highs.

## What network tariffs actually cover

Network tariffs are one of three components of an energy bill, alongside the cost of the energy itself and government levies. They pay for building and maintaining the electricity and gas grids, including transmission costs paid to the high-voltage operator Elia. Because they are regulated rather than set by the market, they hit every connected household regardless of whether it holds a fixed, variable or dynamic contract — unlike commodity prices, which consumers can partly offset by shopping around or fixing a deal.

The decision on the permitted incomes of the distribution network operators was taken on 10 November 2026 (decisions BESL-2026-86 through BESL-2026-101) within the tariff methodology for 2025–2028, as set out by the [Vlaamse Nutsregulator](https://www.vlaamsenutsregulator.be/elektriciteit-en-aardgas/nettarieven/toegelaten-inkomens-en-tariefvoorstellen/2027).

## Bills vary sharply by where you live

The headline figures mask significant regional variation. Because each network operator sets its own investment policy, the bill depends heavily on the household's place of residence. In 2027, a family in the most expensive electricity network area will pay 20 percent more than in the cheapest area, up from a 19 percent gap in 2026; for gas the spread is 16 percent, slightly narrower than this year's 18 percent, [HLN](https://www.hln.be/energie/nettarieven-voor-elektriciteit-stijgen-meer-dan-7-procent-die-voor-aardgas-dalen-met-3-5-procent~a62ce8d9/) reported.

Small businesses on the low-voltage grid face a similar evolution, with electricity tariffs rising. Large businesses on the medium-voltage grid, meanwhile, will see their electricity network tariffs fall.

## The industrial discount behind the increase

A more consequential driver sits upstream of these figures. The VNR has warned that transmission costs for Flemish grid users could rise by about 20 percent in 2027 if the federal regulator CREG brings forward discounts of 52 percent and 63.5 percent for large industrial users with stable or anticyclical consumption profiles, moving them from 2028–2031 into 2026–2027. What those large users no longer pay is shifted onto other grid users — households and small firms on the low- and medium-voltage grid.

"The Flemish Nutsregulator (VNR) warns that the transmission costs paid by Flemish grid users could rise by about 20 percent in 2027," [InduBa](https://induba.be/nl/blog/netkosten-2027) reported, citing the regulator. Over the longer term, the VNR expects transmission costs for an average Flemish family could more than triple within ten years.

## A price signal at odds with electrification

The 2027 decision lands at an awkward moment for policy. Flanders and Belgium are encouraging households to switch from gas to electricity — through heat pumps, electric vehicles and induction cooking — to meet climate goals and the planned phase-out of gas toward 2050. Yet the new tariffs make the electricity grid more expensive while the gas grid becomes relatively cheaper, sending a price signal that runs against the electrification objective.

The timing is also difficult. The VRT NWS liveblog and earlier warnings tracked by [Solar & Storage Magazine](https://solarmagazine.nl/nieuws-zonne-energie/i44682/vlaamse-regulator-waarschuwt-voor-hogere-netkosten-mogelijk-20-procent-stijging-in-2027) place the increase within a broader energy squeeze: high wholesale gas prices linked to conflict in the Middle East, Belgian inflation at 5.26 percent in October, and estimated annual electricity bills for an average Flemish family climbing toward 1,546 euros.

## What to watch

The 2027 increase is structural, not cyclical: unlike volatile wholesale prices, it persists unless the tariff methodology or investment plans change. Two things bear watching. First, whether CREG proceeds with the accelerated industrial discounts — the single most consequential lever behind the higher increase, and one that raises questions of fairness between large energy users and households. Second, whether policymakers respond, given consumer groups' warnings about energy poverty and the tension between rising electricity costs and the push toward electrification.

For now, the message to households is the familiar one: network tariffs are unavoidable, but the energy and contract components of the bill remain where savings can be found.
