# China Sets 2030 Targets for Next-Gen Communication Networks

China has unveiled a sweeping plan to build "next-generation communication networks" by 2030, placing timely 6G rollout and a nearly complete low-orbit satellite internet system at the center of its digital infrastructure ambitions. The plan was jointly issued by the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology (MIIT), the Cyberspace Administration of China (CAC) and the National Development and Reform Commission (NDRC), with State Council approval, according to [Xinhua News Agency](https://www.news.cn/politics/20261126/9fe827a2d98741acb60f62d36ba89cf7/c.html).

## What the Plan Sets Out to Achieve

The "Implementation Plan for the Construction of Next-Generation Communication Networks" targets a comprehensive, performance-leading network by the end of the decade. According to the Xinhua report, the 2030 goals include "carrying out 6G network construction in due course," basically completing a low-orbit satellite internet system, continuing to optimize the layout of international submarine and land cables, achieving large-scale development of converged networks such as the industrial internet and vehicle-to-everything (V2X), and building a more complete industrial ecosystem.

Beijing defines the next-generation communication network as an intelligent, comprehensive digital information infrastructure supported by 5G-Advanced (5G-A), 6G, 10-gigabit optical networks and satellite internet. Its defining characteristics are high-speed ubiquitous connectivity, space-ground integration, cloud-network integration, intelligent agility, green and low-carbon operation, and security with controllability—a framing that positions telecommunications as the connective tissue of the economy.

## Beyond Connectivity: An AI-Centered Blueprint

The plan coordinates construction across four dimensions: basic networks, space networks, international networks and converged networks. Specific measures include actively advancing scale coverage of 5G-A networks, accelerating backbone network upgrades, promoting the coordinated development of high-, medium- and low-orbit satellite systems, and planning "BeiDou + 5G" infrastructure.

Perhaps the most telling element is the plan's explicit pivot toward artificial intelligence. It calls for evolving communication network architecture "toward being centered on supporting AI applications" and for building a nationally unified computing power monitoring and scheduling platform. It also outlines a new industrial network integrating control, network and computing—described as open and intelligent—and orderly digital upgrading and networking of road infrastructure.

On capability goals, the plan seeks comprehensive improvements in all-domain intelligence, security resilience and green, low-carbon performance. These include strengthening protection systems for critical industry information infrastructure, retrofitting communication rooms and computing facilities for energy and water savings, and coordinating the planning of the new network with water networks, new-type power grids, computing power networks, urban underground pipe networks and logistics networks.

## Building the Industrial Ecosystem

Industrial ecosystem measures focus on accelerating standards development for 6G, satellite internet, ultra-high-speed optical communication and computing-network integration. The plan also calls for strengthening technology exploration in deep space, deep earth, deep sea and distant sea scenarios, and deepening the integration of brain-computer interfaces, embodied intelligence and intelligent connected vehicles with the next-generation network.

On implementation, the plan emphasizes ministry-province coordination and support for pilot projects in key regions. It pushes for legislation including the Telecommunications Law and the Radio Spectrum Resources Law, encourages private enterprises to participate in construction in accordance with laws and regulations, and commits to stronger guarantees for land, forest, grassland, sea use and spectrum. It also calls for strengthening related academic disciplines and building a high-quality talent pool.

## A Network Already Taking Shape

The plan formalizes a buildout already well advanced in places. According to [CCTV's News Simulcast](https://lianbolu.com/item/2026-11-07/05-xin-yi-dai-tong-xin-wang-jia-kuai-jian-s/), China had built more than 26,000 industrial 5G private networks and 1,260 5G factories by early November, with the industrial internet covering all 41 major industrial categories. Coverage has extended to the Greater Khingan Mountains, the Qinba Mountains and the Bohai Sea, where more than 60 base stations now provide full-area maritime connectivity.

The frontier of the effort is 6G. At the Purple Mountain Laboratories in Nanjing, researchers are conducting multi-base-station cooperative sensing tests in which 6G base stations can "sense" object movement like radar—technology with potential applications in autonomous driving and other latency-sensitive systems.

## Anchored in the 'Six Networks' Framework

The plan operationalizes a concept embedded in China's 15th Five-Year Plan (2026–2030). As a commentary carried by [Hubei Daily](https://swj.wuhan.gov.cn/tzdt/hykd/202607/t20260722_2823807.html) explained, the modernization framework pairs the traditional physical transportation network with the "Six Networks": water networks, new-type power grids, computing power networks, next-generation communication networks, urban underground pipe networks and logistics networks. The computing power and communication networks are classified together as digital infrastructure.

The same commentary framed the next-generation communication network as the "nervous system" of an emerging super-intelligent social system, while tracing China's telecom trajectory from "1G blank, 2G following, 3G breakthrough, 4G running in parallel, to 5G leading." It described the new network as advancing full commercialization of 5G-A while deploying toward 6G, with faster speeds, lower latency, and "communication-sensing-computing-intelligence" integration across a space-air-ground-sea architecture. This year, the construction of the "Six Networks" was incorporated into the broader framework for building a modernized infrastructure system.

## What to Watch Next

Several questions will determine how the plan unfolds. The emphasis on private-sector participation and on formal legislation suggests a push toward a more codified governance model for telecom infrastructure. The explicit AI-centric architecture and unified computing power scheduling platform tie the network buildout directly to China's broader ambitions in artificial intelligence, making the telecom plan a pillar of industrial policy rather than a standalone sector initiative.

With a 2030 horizon and construction already underway in remote regions and at the 6G frontier, the next milestones to watch will be the pace of 6G standards development, the completion timeline for low-orbit satellite internet, and how quickly the "BeiDou + 5G" and computing-network integration deployments reach commercial scale.
