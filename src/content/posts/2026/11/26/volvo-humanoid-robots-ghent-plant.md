# Volvo Deploys Humanoid Robots at Ghent Plant

Volvo Cars is testing two humanoid robots at its Ghent assembly plant, making the Belgian factory the first in the carmaker's global network to trial the technology. The AEON robots, supplied by Hexagon Robotics, are learning to perform repetitive and physically demanding tasks, with the company aiming to bring them into actual production by mid-2027, according to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/26/humanoide-robots-volvo-car-gent/).

The move marks a notable step in industrial automation in Belgium and reflects a broader push across the automotive industry to harness humanoid robots for work that is difficult or ergonomically taxing for people.

## Meet the AEON Robots

The two robots now on the Ghent floor are AEON units developed by Hexagon Robotics, a division of the Swedish-Swiss Hexagon group. Standing 1.65 metres tall and weighing 60 kilograms, they move on wheels, use cameras and sensors to perceive their surroundings and grasp components, and can carry up to 10 kilograms at speeds of up to 2.4 metres per second.

A dedicated in-house Volvo team is training the robots to mimic tasks normally performed by employees. "A concrete example is placing a component into a fixed frame. The robots must learn to do this accurately and efficiently," spokesperson Barbara Blomme told VRT NWS.

Once the basics are mastered, the plan is to make the assignments progressively harder — teaching the robots to handle different components, move around the plant and communicate with the factory's automated systems.

## Heavy Work, Not Job Cuts

Volvo already relies on robot arms and other fixed robots for repetitive tasks carried out in a single position. But some physically demanding jobs require more freedom of movement, which is where the humanoid form factor comes in — the robots are designed to work within the existing factory alongside employees and other machines.

Volvo is emphatic that the project is not about replacing workers. "It is not the intention that they will replace those people. The real intention is to make production more efficient and to let the humanoid robots sometimes take over ergonomically more difficult tasks," Blomme said.

She added that staff are fully on board and were introduced to the robots earlier. "It would be strange not to work with such robots in the automotive industry. In China this has been established for longer."

## Why Ghent, and What Comes Next

In the first half of 2027, more AEON robots are due to arrive in Ghent for a larger pilot project covering a wider range of tasks, before the robots enter production in mid-2027. If the project succeeds, Volvo plans to gradually expand its humanoid robot fleet.

Ghent earned the pilot role because the plant has long played a pioneering part in innovation and technology, Blomme said, describing the robots as part of a broader effort to renew production processes.

The timing matters for the factory. Volvo Car Gent is a major Belgian automotive employer, but it produced 186,313 cars in 2024, down 19% from 230,527 in 2023, a transition year as the plant shifts toward new models.

## An Industry-Wide Shift

Volvo is not moving alone. Humanoid robots are being piloted across automotive manufacturing globally. BMW has tested Hexagon's AEON robots at its Leipzig plant, and Chinese manufacturers have adopted the technology more extensively. Hexagon's robotics division lists initial pilot partnerships with BMW Group, Schaeffler Group and Pilatus Aircraft.

The shift is not without friction. In January, a major Korean union warned that humanoid robots would not enter Hyundai factories without its consent, citing fears of job losses as the carmaker pushes ahead with the technology, [AutoWeek reported](https://www.autoweek.nl/autonieuws/artikel/grote-vakbond-waarschuwt-voor-mensachtige-robots-er-komt-geen-robot-de-fabriek-binnen/). "We will never tolerate one-sided decisions about moving production abroad, introducing new technologies (robot automation) or acting without an agreement between employer and employee," the union said.

## What to Watch

The Ghent pilot will be closely watched as a test case for how humanoid robots are integrated into European manufacturing — and how smoothly that transition can be managed alongside a workforce. Volvo has not disclosed the investment cost of the programme or whether any specific roles could be affected, beyond insisting that replacement is not the goal.

For now, the robots remain in training, learning the same tasks their human colleagues perform. What happens when the first AEON units join the production line in 2027 will say much about the pace of automation on Belgian factory floors.
