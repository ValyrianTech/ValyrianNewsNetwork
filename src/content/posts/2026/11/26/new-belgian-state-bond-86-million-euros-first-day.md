# New Belgian State Bond Raises 86 Million Euros on Day One

Belgium's new state bond drew 86 million euros in subscriptions on its first day of sale, the Federal Debt Agency announced on Thursday, underscoring continued appetite among savers for safe, government-backed returns in a low-interest environment. The one-year tranche again proved the most popular, attracting more than 56.6 million euros on Wednesday, according to [Het Laatste Nieuws](https://www.hln.be/mijn-geld/nieuwe-staatsbon-is-in-trek-86-miljoen-euro-opgehaald-op-eerste-dag~acca6fbe/).

The brisk opening day, however, fell well short of the exceptional September issue, which pulled in 193 million euros on its first day and eventually raised more than 1.255 billion euros in total — the best result since the so-called Van Peteghem bond.

## A three-tranche offering with higher yields

This December issuance offers three maturities for the first time — one, five and ten years — whereas recent issues offered only two. The one-year bond pays 2.95% gross, or 2.065% net after 30% withholding tax; the five-year tranche yields 3.60% gross (2.52% net), and the ten-year tranche 4.10% gross (2.87% net), [HLN reported](https://www.hln.be/binnenland/nettorendement-nieuwe-staatsbons-bekend-van-2-065-procent-op-1-jaar-tot-2-87-procent-op-10-jaar~a9326a59/) when the rates were announced on 23 November.

The first-day breakdown tracked the same pattern: alongside the 56.6 million euros in one-year subscriptions, savers placed 18.7 million euros in the five-year bond and 10.8 million euros in the ten-year bond.

The net yields are higher than at previous issues — and considerably more attractive than Belgium's average regulated savings account rate of about 1.52%, as tracked by [Spaarvergelijker.be](https://spaarvergelijker.be/nieuws/staatsbon-december-2026-2-065-procent/).

## Why savers are being paid more

The elevated yields reflect what financial markets are demanding to lend money to Belgium. According to VRT NWS, as relayed by Spaarvergelijker.be, the absence of a budget and the ongoing political crisis are pushing up the premium investors require — a dynamic that ultimately flows through to the coupons offered to retail savers.

The Debt Agency has also signalled that there is ample savings capital among Belgian households that can be mobilised. The bond carries a 100% issue price, repays principal at 100% at maturity, pays an annual coupon, and is available from a minimum of 100 euros and multiples thereof — features that have made the product a recurring favourite among risk-averse savers.

## A familiar competitive dynamic

As in September, commercial banks are again stepping forward with alternative savings products designed to match the state bond's yield, HLN noted. That competition gives savers a genuine choice between the certainty of a fixed, government-backed coupon and the flexibility of a bank deposit.

The subscription window runs from 25 November to 3 December through banks, while commission-free online subscriptions via the Debt Agency's Grootboeken at staatsbons.be remain open until 2 December, subject to possible early closure. The issue and settlement date is 4 December 2026.

## The fine print worth reading

While the state bond offers capital protection, it is not without caveats. Coupons are subject to 30% withholding tax. Holders who sell early on the secondary market receive the market price, which may be above or below the purchase price — and rising interest rates can depress the value of the longer five- and ten-year tranches. Secondary-market trades also incur a 0.12% stock exchange tax (capped at 1,300 euros), while a 10% withholding tax on capital gains applies under the law of 6 April 2026, and a 0.15% securities account tax may also be due.

For savers weighing the trade-offs, the calculus is straightforward: the state bond suits those who can lock up their money for a fixed term and prefer a known return, while a regulated savings account remains more flexible for funds that may be needed at short notice.

## What to watch

With subscriptions open until early December, attention now turns to whether the new issue can approach September's headline total of 1.255 billion euros. A first-day haul of 86 million euros suggests solid but more measured demand — a sign that Belgian savers remain keen on government paper, even as banks sharpen their competing offers. The final tally, due when the books close, will show whether this issuance follows September's extraordinary run or settles into a more conventional rhythm.
