# Xi Signals Party Rulebook Push After Key Plenum

Xi Jinping, general secretary of the Communist Party of China Central Committee, has issued "important instructions" on the work of intra-party regulations, setting the direction for the next phase of the Party's institutional building and treating rule-based governance as the fundamental method of running the Party. The instructions were conveyed and studied at the National Conference on Intra-Party Regulations Work, held in Beijing on 24–25 November, according to [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/politics/leaders/20261125/0b429f3662ae427cb3df80c556237c63/c.html), the official state news agency.

The dispatch is a routine but high-signal entry in China's political calendar. It translates the agenda of October's 20th Central Committee Fifth Plenary Session into concrete priorities for the Party's regulatory system, signalling that the leadership's institutional-building drive continues at full pace into the new period.

## A Governing Doctrine, Reaffirmed

Xi said the Central Committee had, over the past five years, planned and advanced governance according to regulations from a strategic, whole-of-Party perspective, with solid results in building the intra-party regulatory system. Going forward, he called for adherence to "treating governance-by-regulations as the basic method of running and disciplining the Party," focusing on strengthening Party leadership and Party building, shoring up shortcomings and strengthening weak links, and continuing to advance the regulatory system "with high quality."

At the heart of the instructions were four priorities, each framed as a call to pay "more attention" to a particular task: the systematic integration of institutions, institutional study and education, institutional execution, and the coordination between intra-party regulations and state laws and regulations. Together they describe a system meant to "provide stronger institutional safeguards" for the Party's sustained comprehensive and strict governance, ensuring it remains what Xi called "the strong leading core of the cause of socialism with Chinese characteristics."

## Cai Qi Delivers the Leadership's Message

Cai Qi, a member of the Politburo Standing Committee and head of the Central Secretariat, attended the conference and delivered a speech, describing Xi's instructions as "sweeping in vision and rich in content, with strong political, guiding, and targeted significance." He said the instructions pointed the way forward for intra-party regulations work on the new journey.

Cai framed the doctrine in doctrinal terms, calling adherence to governing the Party by institutions and by regulations "one of the core tenets of Xi Jinping Thought on Party Building." He urged that institutional execution be made a "rigid binding constraint," that study and education of intra-party regulations be strengthened, and that the responsibility system for enforcement be strictly implemented. Shi Taifeng presided over the meeting, with Li Ganjie and Li Shulei also in attendance.

## The Post-Plenum Context

The instructions follow the Fifth Plenary Session, held 26–29 October, which adopted a major decision on persistently advancing comprehensive and strict Party governance, as [reported](https://www.forestry.gov.cn/lyj/1/szxx/20261029/694246.html) by Xinhua. That decision set the overall theme; Xi's instructions on intra-party regulations are the implementation-level follow-through, converting the plenum's political direction into work priorities for the regulatory system.

The emphasis on rule-based governance is a long-running signature of Xi's party-building doctrine. A [Xinhua background commentary](https://www.news.cn/politics/leaders/20261027/855f3ab636f842d39ca0014a3c313724/c.html) traces the lineage to the Politburo's adoption of the "Eight-Point Regulation" on work style on 4 December 2012 — the first important intra-party regulation of the post-18th Congress era. Over the new era, the Party has issued its first Central document dedicated to regulatory-system building, convened two national conferences on the topic, and compiled three five-year plans for regulation-making.

## From Volume to Effectiveness

The most revealing language is about maturity. Xi's call to strengthen "system integration" so that rules form "an organic whole" reflects a shift the official commentary describes as moving from "whether we have regulations at all" to "whether they actually work," and from "isolated advances" to "coordinated interlocking." In practice, that points to fewer headline rules and more effort spent making the existing framework coherent and enforced.

The backdrop is an anti-corruption campaign that shows no sign of easing. According to the commentary, discipline inspection and supervision organs filed 538,000 cases and disciplined 412,000 people in the first half of 2026, including 74 officials at the provincial-ministerial level or above. Against that record, the stress on "institutional execution" reads less as rhetoric than as a live governance priority — the enforcement gap is the problem the new emphasis is designed to close.

The explicit call to coordinate intra-party regulations with national laws and regulations is likewise significant. It reflects an ongoing project to align Party rules with the state legal system, an effort that shapes how both are applied in practice.

## What to Watch

The instructions are unlikely to produce dramatic new rules in the near term. The more telling signals will be in implementation: how the Party measures the "effectiveness" of existing regulations, how the enforcement responsibility system is applied in practice, and how the party-law and state-law systems are reconciled as they mature. For observers of Chinese governance, the message is continuity — the institutional-building agenda is not winding down but entering a more technical, enforcement-focused phase.
