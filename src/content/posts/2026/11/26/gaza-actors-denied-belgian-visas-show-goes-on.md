# Gaza Actors Denied Belgian Visas, But the Show Goes On

A Belgian theatre production built around the autobiographical stories of two Palestinian actors is being staged across Wallonia and Brussels this month — even though the Belgian authorities refused to let the men enter the country to perform it. Jamal Al-Rozzi and Hossam Al-Madhoun, both from Gaza and now living in exile in Cairo, were denied entry visas, so the show *From Gaza* has been reworked to carry their voices across the border by other means.

![From Gaza production poster by Une Petite Compagnie](https://www.theatre-action.be/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/UPC_From_Gaza.png)

## A show built on lived experience

The production was designed for four performers on stage. Instead, only two Belgian actors — Emmanuel Guillaume and Philippe Dumoulin, joined by singer Aïcha Haskal — now appear, while Jamal and Hossam take part through video recordings made in Cairo. Their words are performed on stage by their Belgian colleagues, who have known them for some 30 years and have visited Gaza repeatedly.

It is the first production the two Gazan actors have staged since the war began on 7 October 2023, and the most personal. As [RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/from-gaza-des-acteurs-palestiniens-interdits-d-entree-en-belgique-pour-jouer-le-role-de-leur-vie-j-ai-ete-prive-de-visa-pour-une-raison-ridicule-11821067) reports, Jamal said he wanted to show the human consequences of the war — not only the death toll, but the fear, uncertainty and depression, and the personal milestones lost, such as his daughter's wedding, which did not unfold as he had dreamed.

"When we cried, we weren't playing a role," he said from Cairo. "I was Jamal the person, the father, not the actor."

## A second refusal, and a disputed reason

According to a communiqué from the creative team dated 19 October and published on the venue page of [Les Riches-Claires](https://lesrichesclaires.be/saison/from-gaza/), Jamal Al-Rozzi's visa was refused a second time on 15 October. The stated reason was a missing work permit — which, the team argues, Belgian law waives for international artists on tours of fewer than 21 days per quarter. Their tour counts 14.

"Our lawyer is categorical: this refusal is abusive," the team wrote. "Rules are being invented as they are invoked against them." The company said it would not file a new application or appeal.

Jamal said the decision rested on doubt that he would leave Belgium, and that he was judged on his name and origin. "They saw the name and the origin. 'Gaza? No, there is no guarantee you'll leave,'" he said in the RTBF report. He noted he had visited Belgium about 15 times without ever breaching visa or stay rules, and had left Egypt twice in two and a half years — evidence he says was ignored.

## Part of a broader pattern

The refusal fits a wider policy. As RTBF notes, 13 Gazan students and researchers holding Belgian university scholarships were similarly denied entry in September and October 2026. The refusals rest chiefly on the risk of permanent settlement and a feared "domino effect" invoked by federal asylum and migration minister Anneleen Van Bossuyt of the N-VA.

For Emmanuel Guillaume, the decision amounts to censorship. "The visa refusal is a political choice of Europe," he said. "It is also a form of censorship; they don't want to hear what they have to say." He described the team's reaction as one of shame: "Inhumanity is not only abroad, but also in our own country."

## Absence made visible

Rather than hide the missing performers, the production places their absence at the centre of the staging. Their recorded testimonies are extended on stage by the Belgian actors, and the performance keeps returning to the question shared by all exiles: will we ever be able to return home?

The November tour has taken the show to Viroinval, Dinant, Seraing, Limelette, Forzée, Cuesmes, Brussels, Charleroi and La Louvière, with a performance at Les Riches-Claires in Brussels on 23 November. The production is a partnership between Une Petite Compagnie and Palestine's Theater for Everybody, supported by the Fédération Wallonie-Bruxelles, Wallonie-Bruxelles International and a successful crowdfunding campaign.

## What's next

The November run is drawing to a close, though the company has signalled that a new tour is being prepared. For now, the piece stands as a quiet act of defiance: a show about lives interrupted, performed by those left behind, for audiences asked to listen in place of the men who could not come.

"Being on stage, as an actor, is our life, our soul, our energy," Jamal said. "And I was deprived of it for a ridiculous reason."
