# Kanal-Centre Pompidou Opens in Brussels

After more than three years of delays and a budget that swelled from €130 million to roughly €230 million, the Kanal-Centre Pompidou museum opens to the public on Saturday 28 November in Brussels, transforming the city's landmark 1930s Citroën garage into one of Europe's largest museums of modern and contemporary art. King Philippe and Queen Mathilde are due to preview the building on Thursday, a day before the public is admitted, [RTBF reported](https://www.rtbf.be/article/kanal-centre-pompidou-ouvre-ses-portes-ou-quand-l-ancien-garage-citroen-devient-un-immense-musee-d-art-moderne-et-contemporain-11820254).

![Drone image of Kanal-Centre Pompidou, the former Citroën garage along the Brussels canal](https://files.ocula.com/rf/d1/d13c7141-e6bf-4333-ab00-48509ecc2078/6-drone-image-of-kanal.jpg)

## A garage reborn as a museum

The project converts 40,000 m² of industrial heritage along the Brussels-Charleroi canal into a vast cultural complex. From the outside, the building retains the rounded glass-and-metal forms that once made it resemble a ship moored beside the waterway. Inside, a new five-storey building houses the exhibition galleries, the lowest of which descends nine metres below ground, while a 200-metre-long central nave has been preserved as a space for civic gathering.

Architect André Verstraeten, who has managed the building for the Fondation Kanal since 2017, said the guiding principle was to preserve the garage's identity. "I find it has an extraordinary new life," he said, according to RTBF. "The goal we set with the architects was to preserve as much as possible the historic appearance of the building." The original access ramps once used by the "deux-chevaux", "DS" and "Ami-6" cars were kept, and the historic metal roof structures were decontaminated, repainted and reinforced to carry new contemporary glazing — work deliberately kept invisible.

## A nine-year saga of overruns

The opening caps a turbulent near-decade. The Brussels-Capital Region purchased the building in 2015, and a partnership convention with the Centre Pompidou was signed in 2017. The museum was initially slated to open in 2022 at a cost estimated at €130 million. The final bill now stands at roughly €230 million, of which the region has committed about €185 million, 95% of which has already been paid.

The project was dogged by a funding crisis that at one stage threatened to halt the redevelopment. As [RTBF detailed](https://www.rtbf.be/article/le-musee-kanal-sauve-mais-recalibre-dans-son-financement-11681290), the new Brussels government promised the museum would open "in good conditions" and could expect an additional €50 million to finish the works, but it also signalled that the region's annual subsidy would be cut sharply from 2029. Annual operating costs have been described as the highest in Belgium, straining the wider cultural sector.

The pressure is set to intensify. Xavier Rey, director of the Centre Pompidou, warned that time is short to make the model sustainable. "Kanal has five years to refine its cultural model; it's crucial, because public funding will be only 10 million in 2029, versus 28 million today," he said, according to RTBF.

Yves Goldstein, the foundation's managing director, has insisted there is no turning back. "This project will happen. This place can only be the museum we have been building for nine years," he said, as [BruxellesToday reported](https://www.bruxellestoday.be/actualite/Kanal-pompidou-programme-ouverture.html). "What I ask of the political world today is to respect the contracts we signed to allow us to complete the opening and the first two years of programming." Goldstein announced in May that he would step down in early 2027, [RTBF noted](https://www.rtbf.be/article/fondation-kanal-le-directeur-general-yves-goldstein-annonce-son-depart-debut-2027-mais-se-defend-de-quitter-le-navire-11719580), while denying he was "quitting the ship."

## A strategic partnership with Paris

The museum's cultural programme rests on its alliance with the Centre Pompidou, which is itself closed for at least five years for renovation. The partnership, [announced in 2017](https://www.lalibre.be/culture/arts/2025/05/20/officiel-ouverture-de-kanal-le-28-novembre-2026-FT2KRDK7FFALTGEH4DN7U25NBQ/), gives Kanal access to a collection of some 150,000 works spanning 1905 to the present, plus curatorial advice. In return, Kanal pays the Paris institution €1.98 million a year under an agreement running through 2031.

The arrangement matters for a museum that does not yet own enough works to programme on its own. Kanal currently holds just 144 works by Brussels artists and relies heavily on loans.

## Ten exhibitions, more than 250 artists

The inaugural programme features ten exhibitions with works by more than 250 artists. The headline show, "A truly immense journey" (28 November 2026 – 9 January 2028), explores movement and migration through more than 350 works drawn largely from the Pompidou collection, with artists including Henri Matisse, Alberto Giacometti and Wolfgang Tillmans, according to the [Centre Pompidou](https://www.centrepompidou.fr/en/program/calendar/event/9F8VhjO).

Artistic director Kasia Redzisz said the theme of movement felt deeply tied to Brussels. "The creativity was never missing," she said, as [Ocula reported](https://ocula.com/magazine/art-news/brussels-kanal-centre-pompidou-announces-programme/). "What was missing was an institution able to hold it, to question it, to evolve." She added: "I hope we will develop a reputation for interdisciplinarity and a commitment to telling the stories that will maybe be overlooked."

Other shows include "An infinite woman: Black archives in two acts", revisiting the circulation of images of Mangbetu women as a colonial symbol and their reappropriation by Afro-descendant artists; Joëlle Tuerlinckx's "La première fois"; "NO SHOW", in which 20 Brussels artists ask whether a museum can entertain; and "Right?" by Banu Cennetoğlu, who renders the Universal Declaration of Human Rights as golden letter-balloon bouquets that slowly deflate.

## More than a museum

Kanal has been conceived as a neighbourhood and civic hub as much as an art institution. It houses a brasserie called "La Carrosserie" — built with local bodywork craftsmen — a gastronomic restaurant, a bakery, a playground designed by the Turner Prize-winning collective Assemble, and a rooftop arranged like a ship's deck overlooking the canal. Half of its programming is free, and the building is open from 8am to midnight six days a week, designed in the words of architect Stephen Bates "to feel like a public space with institutions attached." Children from 27 neighbouring schools were welcomed as trial visitors ahead of the opening.

## What to watch

The museum's opening is a landmark for Brussels, placing the capital alongside other European cities that have used major cultural institutions to drive urban regeneration — Kanal is billed as roughly one and a half times the footprint of the Guggenheim Bilbao. But the harder test begins after the ribbon is cut. With public subsidy set to fall by more than half by 2029, Goldstein's successor will need to attract private partners and prove the museum's cultural and economic impact. For now, the garage that once serviced Citroën cars has definitively changed its public: motorists have given way to art lovers, visitors and the curious.
