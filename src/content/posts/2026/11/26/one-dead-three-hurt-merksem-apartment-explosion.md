# One dead, three hurt in Merksem apartment explosion

One person has died and three others were taken to hospital after an explosion and fierce fire tore through an apartment building on the Winterling in Merksem, a district of Antwerp, on Thursday morning. The fatal victim was found in the affected apartment only after firefighters had extinguished the blaze, and the cause of the explosion remains under investigation, [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/26/explosie-merksem-appartement/) reported.

The fire service received the emergency call at 8:25 a.m., and the first crews were on scene roughly five minutes later. Nine residents were evacuated and sheltered in a building across the street, while the apartment on the fourth floor was completely destroyed.

## Rapid response limits the damage

Because the local fire station is "around the corner" from the Winterling, firefighters reached the building quickly and brought the fire under control. Fire brigade spokesperson Gert Wollaert said the structural layout of the affected apartment helped crews reach the seat of the fire fast.

"There was an explosion on the fourth floor of an apartment building," Wollaert said, according to [HLN](https://www.hln.be/merksem/een-dode-bij-brand-in-appartementsgebouw-door-explosie-in-merksem-dovo-ter-plaatse~a95c06ae/). "We were on scene five minutes later, because our fire station is just around the corner. We got the fire under control quickly because the front of the apartment was blown open, which let us reach the seat of the fire fast. That also kept the damage to the other apartments limited."

Wollaert added that the fourth-floor apartment was completely wrecked but the building itself remained structurally stable. "Three people were taken to hospital with light smoke inhalation," he said. "Nine residents were moved to a sheltered workshop across the street, where they are being received. After clean-up work they will be able to return to their apartments."

The blast blew out the glass front of the top apartment. As [De Morgen](https://www.demorgen.be/nieuws/een-dode-bij-ontploffing-en-uitslaande-brand-in-appartementsgebouw-in-merksem-drie-slachtoffers-naar-het-ziekenhuis~b95c06ae/) reported, neighbouring apartments sustained mainly broken glass and smoke damage rather than structural harm.

## A powerful bang, then fire

Antwerp police confirmed the death and described the sequence of events. Spokesperson Wouter Bruyns said the incident began with a violent blast followed immediately by fire.

"It must have been a powerful bang," Bruyns said, according to VRT NWS. "Immediately afterward a fierce fire followed. The smoke was visible from far away and caused hindrance on the motorway."

Residents of the building were evacuated straight away and received in the neighbourhood. Smoke drifting across the area disrupted traffic on the nearby highway during the morning.

The Belgian bomb disposal unit, DOVO (Dienst voor Opruiming en Vernietiging van Ontploffingstuigen), was deployed to the scene because of the explosive nature of the incident. The federal service is routinely called upon when explosives or unexploded ordnance may be involved, in order to rule out or handle any remaining hazard.

## Drug link ruled out

Police were quick to set aside one of the most common explanations for explosions in the Antwerp region. Illegal synthetic-drug laboratories have become a recurring hazard in parts of Belgium, particularly around Antwerp and in Limburg, where production can trigger blasts and fires.

This incident, police stressed, is not one of them. According to HLN and De Morgen, authorities stated that no drug-related activity is involved and that there is no question of a drug lab at the site.

What did cause the explosion is not yet known. Investigators have not indicated whether a gas leak or another domestic fault is suspected, and the identity of the deceased had not been released as of Thursday afternoon.

## What to watch

The immediate task for emergency services is stabilising the building and confirming it is safe for residents to return. Those moved to the shelter across the street are expected to go home once clean-up operations are complete.

The investigation into the cause is the central open question. Confirmation of the explosion's origin — and whether it will be classified as accidental — will shape how authorities respond, including whether any building-safety or gas-infrastructure checks follow. Until then, the focus stays on the victim's family and the residents displaced from their homes on a cold late-November morning.
