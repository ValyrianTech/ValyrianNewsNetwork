# China Economic Roundtable Spotlights High-Level Opening Up

China's flagship "Economic Roundtable" programme has devoted its 39th instalment to the country's strategy of high-level opening up, convening customs, free-trade-port and research officials to make the case that deeper openness remains the driving force behind the world's second-largest economy as it enters the opening year of its 15th Five-Year Plan. The episode, released by Xinhua on 26 November 2026, drew on field reporting from Ningxia, Guangxi and Hainan to argue that expanding imports, attracting foreign investment and institutional reform are reinforcing one another.

## Trade momentum that runs on imports

The roundtable rests on a striking set of figures. According to [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/fortune/20261126/db6f0d4f9cf84060a39c9a85709b5e64/c.html), China's total goods trade grew 20% year-on-year in the first ten months of 2026, with imports rising 23.6% — continuing to outpace export growth. The headline numbers are corroborated by [China News](https://www.jwview.com/jingwei/html/11-10/696466.shtml), which reported customs data showing total trade of RMB 44.81 trillion over the period, with exports up 17.6% and imports up 23.6%.

The emphasis on import growth is deliberate. Officials framed rising inbound trade not merely as domestic demand but as evidence of "orderly, unilateral opening" — a signal that Beijing wants to be seen as a buyer as well as a supplier. At the regional level, the figures are sharper still: Ningxia's trade with Belt and Road partner countries climbed 35.3% in the first three quarters, while Belt and Road trade overall reached RMB 20.37 trillion, up 17.1% and accounting for 51% of China's total trade.

## Hainan as a testbed for institutional opening

The centrepiece of the roundtable's argument was the Hainan Free Trade Port, which has now moved to full island-wide independent customs operations. Wang Fengli, deputy director of the Hainan provincial deep-reform office, reported that the "zero-tariff" commodity tax-line coverage has been raised from 21% to 74%, while "second-line" outbound declaration items have been simplified by more than 60%. The results, he said, are visible in investment: new foreign-invested enterprises grew 31.1% year-on-year through the end of September, with landmark projects including Siemens Energy's first gas-turbine assembly base in Danzhou and Singapore's Fulong Group hospital in the Lecheng pilot zone.

The province is also positioning itself as a services hub. A one-stop aircraft maintenance base handled more than 100 inbound aircraft repairs in 2026, and Wang said stacked policies on bonded maintenance and fast customs clearance can cut maintenance costs for overseas airlines by roughly 10–15%.

## From gatekeeper to rule-shaper

A recurring theme was the shift from merely opening markets to shaping the rules that govern them — what officials call "institutional opening up." Li Kaiyi, head of Yinchuan Customs, described an ambition to move customs functions "from gatekeeper to participant and builder of rules," citing plans to build a TIR (international road transport) hub for Central Asia and to study the impact of the EU's carbon border adjustment mechanism on exporters.

Analysts see the emphasis on rules and standards as an effort to align with high-standard international trade norms while hedging against protectionism. Zhang Yan, a researcher at the Xi Jinping Economic Thought Research Center, argued that China would use "the certainty of steadily expanding opening up to hedge against the uncertainty of the external environment," describing Chinese growth as a "stabilising anchor" for the world economy. She also flagged the rise of cultural exports — online literature with roughly 250 million overseas users and game exports that have topped RMB 100 billion for six consecutive years — as a growing services-trade strength.

## Analysis: openness as a hedge

The roundtable is an official Xinhua production and reflects Beijing's own framing, so its optimistic tone should be read as policy advocacy as much as reporting. Yet the underlying trade figures are independently verified, and the focus on imports, foreign investment and services suggests a genuine strategic pivot: as external demand and geopolitics grow more uncertain, China is trying to anchor growth in its own consumption and in the appeal of its market.

Zhang's four-point proposal on risk control — strengthening foreign-related legal safeguards, building early-warning mechanisms for trade and supply chains, protecting overseas interests, and running "stress tests" in Hainan and pilot free-trade zones on cross-border data and financial opening — reveals the balancing act at the heart of the strategy. Opening up, in this telling, is to be widened but also carefully fenced.

## What's next

The 15th Five-Year Plan devotes a dedicated chapter to expanding high-level opening up, covering the orderly opening of sectors and regions, trade innovation and foreign-investment attraction. With trade growing at a double-digit clip and Hainan's institutional dividends still unfolding, the coming year will test whether China can convert record trade volumes into the kind of durable, rules-based openness that foreign investors say they need.
