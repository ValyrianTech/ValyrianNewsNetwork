# Macy's Thanksgiving Parade Postponed for First Time Ever

For the first time in its century-long history, the Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade will not march on Thanksgiving Day. Organizers postponed the 100th edition of the iconic event from Thursday, November 26, to Saturday, November 28, citing severe weather, according to [The Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/macys-thanksgiving-parade-postponed-weather-cdc870899c8dc2d32a0ac3d86119b3ba). The decision came as a powerful, multi-region storm system battered much of the United States over the holiday, bringing heavy rain, high winds, coastal flooding and snow to both coasts and the central states.

The rescheduled parade will kick off at 8:30 a.m. Saturday, Macy's said. The company said it moved the event "out of an abundance of caution" and following consultation with city officials about the severe weather.

"Our team is now working through the complex operational logistics required to bring the Parade to life on Saturday, and we will share additional details as they are finalized," Macy's said in a statement.

## A Century Without a Postponement

The postponement is unprecedented. A Macy's spokesperson said it was the first time the event has been postponed in its history. The parade has been canceled only three times — in 1942, 1943 and 1944 — during World War II. The parade balloons have been grounded due to weather only once, in 1971.

The 2026 edition was to be a milestone celebration, featuring 100 inflatables, floats and featured balloons, including the return of the Blue Hippo character and the debut of a Bluey balloon. The postponement unfolded while the parade's iconic balloons were well on the way to being inflated, with Macy's representatives repeatedly putting off on-site interviews ahead of the official announcement.

## Safety First, Mayor Says

New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani said Thursday's winds would be strong enough to snap tree limbs and down power lines, and he stressed that rescheduling the parade was the right call.

"Now, I know that many New Yorkers, myself included, as well as millions around the world, were looking forward to the parade being held tomorrow," he said. "Generation after generation has watched SpongeBob and Spider-Man soar down our streets and has taken in Garfield floating down Sixth Avenue. And I know that this change of plans will cause inconveniences, yet safety has to come first."

The National Weather Service issued a high wind warning and a coastal flood warning for Thursday into Friday for the New York area. The city banned empty tractor-trailers from bridges Thursday morning because of the wind.

Those warnings reflect a broader concern that has shaped parade logistics for decades. The city's wind-safety rules date to the 1997 parade, when high winds sent a balloon careening into a metal pole, leaving a woman in a coma. Each inflatable character has a distinct risk profile that includes its size and weight and optimal altitudes for differing wind speeds.

## A Nationwide Storm

The parade postponement was only one facet of a storm that disrupted the holiday across the country. Wind gusts were expected to reach 50 mph (80 kph) or higher along the Interstate 95 corridor, with the weather service posting wind advisories from northern Georgia to most of the mid-Atlantic to southern New England.

All of New Jersey was under a state of emergency, declared by Gov. Mikie Sherrill on Wednesday night, as were parts of New York, following a declaration from Gov. Kathy Hochul. More than 500,000 homes and businesses were without electricity Thursday morning, with the majority of outages in East Coast states, according to the outage tracking website poweroutage.us.

Coastal villages and cities from North Carolina through New Jersey flooded as the high tide rolled in Thursday morning. Roads were closed and emergency workers rescued people from cars stranded in water. The nearly 18-mile (29-kilometer) Chesapeake Bay Bridge-Tunnel in Virginia was closed for several hours because of high winds, and NC 12 — the only highway connecting Hatteras Island on the North Carolina Outer Banks to the mainland — was closed after seawater breached the dunes and covered the two-lane road. In Norfolk, Virginia, tornado warnings blared as winds topped 50 mph.

Inland, winter storm warnings and watches were posted for parts of Missouri and Illinois, with up to 13 inches (33 centimeters) of snow expected around St. Louis. Snow fell as far south as Florence, Alabama.

On the West Coast, king tides drove coastal flood warnings from Southern California to the San Francisco area to the Oregon and Washington coasts, AP [reported](https://apnews.com/article/thanksgiving-weather-storm-macys-king-tides-e8bc9bc454c933dacffe89b1139c4d3c). King tides occur when the gravitational pulls of the moon and sun align to raise water levels, and their effects this year are being amplified by an ongoing "super El Niño," which has warmed the ocean and increased the volume of seawater.

A video showed a massive wave tearing down a portion of the pier in Pacifica, California, on Wednesday. Officials had closed the L-shaped pier in the summer after finding cracks in the structure. North of San Francisco, Marin County officials issued an evacuation warning for beachfront properties in Stinson Beach, later lifted.

## Disrupted Plans and a Silver Lining

NBC, which airs the parade, said it would instead broadcast a rerun of the 2025 edition on Thanksgiving Day. It was not immediately clear how much of the event's expansive lineup would shift to the new date. Representatives for Broadway shows including "The Rocky Horror Show," "Wicked" and "Hamilton" said their performers will take part Saturday. The University of Connecticut's marching band, set to perform for the first time, will also make the rescheduled event. But the University of Alabama said its Million Dollar Band will head home to perform at a football game instead.

Will Coss, a Macy's executive, said organizers are working with bands and other performers and are "optimistic that we're going to be able to maintain the parade that we set out to produce and to deliver to New York City on Thanksgiving."

"Everyone understands what we're up against and is rallying behind us to create the best and most successful 100th parade," he said.

For at least one family, the delay proved a stroke of luck. Sarah Collins and her youngest son had planned a New York weekend trip to celebrate his recent 12th birthday, but their flight from Florida arrives Friday — meaning the parade wasn't part of their original plans. Now it is.

"It's been his lifelong wish to go to New York and see all the Christmas stuff," she said.

## What to Watch

The storm was expected to hamper air travel at eastern U.S. airports Thanksgiving Day into Friday morning, with reduced visibility, greater turbulence, strong winds and aircraft icing, the weather service said — though the days leading up to Thanksgiving are typically far busier on the roads and in airports than the holiday itself.

The central question now is whether Saturday's rescheduled parade can proceed safely as the region recovers from the storm's passage. Macy's has expressed confidence, but the same wind-safety protocols that prompted this week's historic decision will govern the balloons again on Saturday. For a tradition that has weathered a century without ever changing its date, the coming days will determine whether the 100th parade arrives on time — just two days late.
