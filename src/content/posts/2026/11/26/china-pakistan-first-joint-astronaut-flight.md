# China, Pakistan Mark First Joint Astronaut Flight

Chinese President Xi Jinping and Pakistani President Asif Ali Zardari exchanged congratulatory messages on November 25 after a Pakistani astronaut became the first foreign national to fly aboard a Chinese Shenzhou spacecraft and board the Tiangong space station, according to [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/politics/leaders/20261125/33abf5073d3a45efac9ef4a6b69b786c/c.html). The milestone, which both governments cast as a symbol of their "all-weather" partnership, marks the first joint flight of Chinese and foreign astronauts in the history of China's crewed space program.

The Shenzhou-24 mission lifted off from the Jiuquan Satellite Launch Center in northwest China on the morning of November 25, carrying commander Tang Hongbo, fellow Chinese astronaut Wu Yi, and Pakistani payload specialist Muhammad Zeeshan Ali, [Euronews/AP reported](https://www.euronews.com/2026/11/25/china-sends-first-foreign-astronaut-to-its-space-station-as-pakistani-joins-shenzhou-24-cr). The capsule separated from its rocket and entered its planned orbit, and crowds gathered at Jiuquan hours earlier for a send-off ceremony.

## A First for Both Programs

For China, the flight moves Tiangong from an independently built station toward an openly operated, internationally accessible platform. For Pakistan, which lacks independent crewed spaceflight capability, it delivers the country's first astronaut — a national figure who can conduct in-orbit experiments and, officials hope, open a broader road into space engineering.

Ali, a Pakistani Air Force pilot holding the rank of lieutenant colonel, was born in August 1986 and was selected in April 2026 along with a second candidate, according to a [Pengpai/澎湃新闻 profile](https://news.qq.com/rain/a/20261124A0A5X700). The two emerged from a pool of about 20 candidates drawn from Pakistan's pilots and scientists, screened on criteria including physical health, psychological resilience, and tolerance for high gravitational acceleration.

Speaking at a pre-launch press conference, Ali described an intensive preparation process in China. "I learned Chinese and built friendships with my Chinese colleagues. Now I can communicate with the Chinese crew members in Chinese," he said. Reflecting on the bond between the crews, he added: "We trained shoulder to shoulder; the friendship between us is the embodiment of China–Pakistan friendship: higher than mountains, deeper than the sea, sweeter than honey, broader than space."

## Science on Board

Ali is serving as a payload specialist on a short-duration mission, with his in-orbit work focused on joint China–Pakistan space science experiments. According to the Pengpai report, his main task involves a photosynthetic cyanobacteria experiment, part of four bilateral projects in biotechnology and materials science. The crew overall will carry out more than 90 scientific experiments and technology tests, state media reported, as cited by Euronews/AP.

Pakistan's national space agency, SUPARCO, collected more than 300 research proposals from universities in Karachi, Lahore, and Islamabad, ultimately selecting four experiments — all biotechnology projects aimed at food security and climate change, according to the Dawn reporting cited by Pengpai. Because Ali is on a short stay, he is scheduled to return to Earth aboard the Shenzhou-23 spacecraft rather than Shenzhou-24.

## Messages of Friendship

In his congratulatory message, Xi framed the mission as a milestone in a partnership approaching its 75th anniversary. "China and Pakistan are all-weather strategic cooperative partners. In the 75 years since the establishment of diplomatic relations, China–Pakistan friendship has remained rock-solid and unbreakable," Xi said, per a [Ministry of Foreign Affairs](https://www.mfa.gov.cn/wjdt_674879/gjldrhd_674881/202611/t20261125_12066779.shtml) transcript. "Our astronauts joining hands to reach the heavens is a vivid manifestation of the profound China–Pakistan friendship and a new important achievement of bilateral cooperation."

Xi also positioned Tiangong as an open platform. "China's space station is a ship of peace, friendship and cooperation. China is willing to work with all countries, including Pakistan, to hold high the banner of the peaceful use of outer space, deepen international space cooperation, improve global outer-space governance, and let the fruits of space exploration better benefit all of humanity," he said.

Zardari, in his reply, thanked China and described the cooperation as historic. "On the occasion of the 75th anniversary of Pakistan–China diplomatic relations, the two countries have carried out historic space cooperation, with Pakistan's first astronaut entering China's space station," he said, adding that it would "further consolidate Pakistan–China friendship."

The reaction in Pakistan was immediate. Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif called the mission a source of national pride, writing on X: "This is not just one astronaut's journey, but the journey of 240 million Pakistanis." Planning Minister Ahsan Iqbal, speaking to reporters at SUPARCO, said, "The sky is no longer our limit; it is our launching pad," and, asked whether Pakistan could one day send astronauts independently, replied: "Certainly that day will come."

## Why Pakistan, and What Comes Next

Analysts say Pakistan's selection as the first partner reflects both deep strategic trust and a long track record of space cooperation — from decades of satellite launches on Chinese rockets to remote-sensing and BeiDou navigation collaboration, and Pakistan's 2023 membership in the China-led International Lunar Research Station, following a Pakistani CubeSat's 2024 flight to the Moon aboard Chang'e-6.

The arrangement also carries broader geopolitical weight. The International Space Station is expected to retire around 2030, potentially leaving Tiangong as the only long-term crewed platform in low Earth orbit. By opening first to partners, China signals that its station is not a closed national laboratory but an open platform for global scientists — a counterpoint to the US-led space order as both countries press toward crewed lunar landings, with China targeting 2030 and NASA aiming for 2028.

For now, several questions remain open: the precise length of Ali's stay, the experimental results from the four joint projects, and whether the Shenzhou-23 arrangement sets up China's first planned one-year continuous in-orbit stay. What is already clear is that a partnership long anchored in trade, infrastructure, and defense has extended its reach into orbit — and that both governments intend the milestone to be remembered as more than a single flight.
