# Eurostar Strike Disrupts Brussels–London Services

A surprise strike by staff on the Eurostar network disrupted cross-Channel rail services on Wednesday morning, 25 November, cancelling several Paris–London trains and delaying departures from Brussels-Midi, according to [RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/trafic-eurostar-perturbe-par-une-greve-surprise-un-train-deja-annule-au-depart-de-la-gare-du-midi-11820827). The operator described the cause as "an unforeseen strike by some members of our staff" and acknowledged that traffic was affected in both directions.

## What Happened

Eurostar announced on its X account that "today's cancellations are due to an unforeseen strike" and that circulation was impacted in both directions between Paris and London. In the Belgian capital, at least one train was cancelled at departure from Bruxelles-Midi, while other Brussels–London services were delayed in both directions from the morning.

On the Paris–London axis, [20 Minutes](https://www.20minutes.fr/monde/royaume-uni/4265593-20261125-eurostar-plusieurs-trains-annules-entre-paris-londres-mercredi-raison-greve-surprise), citing Eurostar and AFP, reported that trains scheduled at 07:01 and 08:01 from London were cancelled, along with departures from Paris at 07:37, 08:39 and 09:10. The disruption struck the morning peak, hitting commuters and business travellers hardest.

## Company Response

Eurostar asked passengers whose train was cancelled not to travel to the station. "We ask customers whose train is cancelled not to come to the station, because they will not be able to travel," the company said in a statement to AFP. Affected customers may change their trip to another date or obtain a full refund under the company's commercial policy, and Eurostar apologised for the inconvenience caused.

The operator also called on trade unions to continue discussions directly with the company, which it said "remains open and willing to continue a constructive dialogue." Notably, Eurostar did not publicly specify the reasons behind the walkout, leaving the underlying grievance unconfirmed.

## Why It Matters

Beyond its historic Paris–London link through the Channel Tunnel — opened on 14 November 1994 — Eurostar also serves Belgium, the Netherlands and Germany, and some of those routes showed delays on Wednesday morning. Eurostar is a subsidiary 55.75% owned by SNCF Voyageurs, and the Brussels–London connection is a key route for the Belgian market, where the company employs a significant workforce.

The episode adds to a pattern of last-minute industrial action affecting the operator in recent years. It also comes against a backdrop of corporate change, including Eurostar's order of 50 new Alstom trains with the Charleroi site involved, and earlier restructuring that trimmed roughly 140 jobs, including about 50 in Belgium.

## What to Watch

Several questions remain open. It is not yet clear what specific grievances drove the strike, which unions were involved, whether a normal timetable was restored the same day, or how many passengers were affected network-wide beyond the listed Paris–London departures. Whether the action is linked to the company's recent legal-status change or to ongoing restructuring affecting Belgian staff has likewise not been established. For travellers, the immediate advice is to check their booking status and avoid stations if their train has been cancelled.
