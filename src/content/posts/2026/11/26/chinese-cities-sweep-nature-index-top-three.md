# Chinese Cities Sweep Top Three of Global Science Ranking

Chinese cities have filled the top three places of a global ranking of scientific research output for the first time, according to the 2026 Nature Index Science Cities supplement published online on 26 November. Beijing retained first place — a position it has now held for ten consecutive years — followed by Shanghai in second and Nanjing in third, which [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/tech/20261126/d2b4315198734faaa93030ff2ea697c9/c.html) reported as the first time Chinese metropolises have swept the podium.

The result caps a decade of rapid ascent for Chinese research institutions and is being framed by state media as a milestone in the country's emergence as a global science power. Independent analysis of the same dataset tells a parallel story of relative decline in the United States and parts of Europe.

## What the Ranking Shows

The supplement measures research output across 145 natural-science and health-science journals tracked by the Nature Index, which tallies author contributions — a metric known as Share — to a curated set of high-quality publications. According to [Science and Technology Daily](https://www.stdaily.com/web/gdxw/2026-11/26/content_619539.html), the 2025 global top ten overall was led by Beijing, followed by Shanghai, Nanjing, the New York metropolitan area, Guangzhou, the Boston metropolitan area, the San Francisco Bay Area, Wuhan, Hangzhou and Baltimore-Washington.

The year-on-year gains were pronounced. Nanjing leapt from fifth to third, overtaking both the New York and Boston metropolitan areas. Guangzhou climbed from sixth to fifth, and Hangzhou advanced from tenth to ninth. In the natural sciences specifically, Chinese cities took seven of the top ten places, with Hefei joining the list at tenth.

Beijing's dominance extends across disciplines. The capital ranked first in chemistry, physical sciences, and earth and environmental sciences, while the New York metropolitan area led in the biological sciences. In the health sciences — a category added to the index in 2022 — US cities remained dominant, holding five of the top ten spots, with Boston leading and Beijing the highest-ranked Chinese city in fifth place. The [China News Service](http://www.ce.cn/xwzx/gnsz/gdxw/202611/t20261126_3307966.shtml) also noted that the 2026 supplement explores advances in smart-city technology, featuring Beijing, Helsinki, London and Singapore as case studies.

## A Decade at the Top

Beijing's ten-year run at No. 1 was matched by its status as the fastest-growing city in the natural sciences by adjusted Share over that period, a measure that smooths out annual fluctuations in article volume. Shanghai and Guangzhou placed second and third for growth.

"Beijing has consistently ranked first in the Nature Index in physical sciences, chemistry, and earth & environmental sciences, while also making rapid progress in biological and health sciences," Simon Baker, editor-in-chief of the Nature Index, said. "Beijing's success is a microcosm of China's overall rise in the Nature Index. More than half of the cities on this year's Nature Index Science Cities top-ten list are still Chinese cities."

The city-level milestone builds on a broader national trend. According to [Nature Index](https://www.nature.com/nature-index/news/nature-index-research-leaders-united-states-losing-ground-china-lead-expands-rapidly) reporting on its 2025 Research Leaders list, China overtook the United States for the top country spot in 2023. In 2024, China's Share reached 32,122 against 22,083 for the US — a lead that more than quadrupled in a single year. China's adjusted Share jumped 17.4% in the year to 2023 while the US fell 10.1%.

## Why the Gap Is Widening

Analysts point to structural forces behind the shift. China has roughly four times the population of the United States and has been outpacing it in science, technology, engineering and maths PhD graduates since the early 2000s. A 2021 report from Georgetown University's Center for Security and Emerging Technology predicted Chinese universities would produce about 77,000 STEM PhDs per year by 2025, compared with 40,000 in the US.

"China now employs more researchers than both America and the entire EU combined," Stephen Ezell, vice-president for global innovation policy at the Information Technology and Innovation Foundation, told Nature. "If America is going to hold ground, we're going to have to recognize that the race is real."

US research funding has also come under pressure. Nature's reporting linked accelerating American decline to proposed cuts under the second Trump administration — including roughly 40% to the National Institutes of Health, 57% to the National Science Foundation and 55% to the Environmental Protection Agency — alongside policy disruptions in higher education. Observers have warned these could accelerate China's gains and trigger a brain drain from West to East.

"We're going through a national crisis," Caroline Wagner, a science policy researcher at Ohio State University, told Nature. "It's more than a malaise."

## What to Watch

The sweep of the top three is symbolic, but the underlying numbers carry weight. Chinese cities now dominate the natural-sciences leaderboard, hold a commanding national lead in overall Share, and are closing the gap in health sciences where the US remains strongest.

Two caveats temper the picture. First, the 2026 city ranking reflects research output from calendar year 2025, so it predates any effects of the most recent US policy shifts. Second, the Nature Index measures publication in a curated set of high-quality journals — a widely used but imperfect proxy for the full breadth of a country's research capacity. Framings differ sharply between Chinese state media, which emphasize national achievement and momentum, and Western outlets, which stress relative decline and funding crises; both, however, draw on the same dataset.

The next few years will test whether the trend holds. Much depends on whether the United States can stabilize research funding and retain talent, and whether China can convert its publishing volume into the kind of breakthrough science that defines global leadership. For now, the scoreboard reads unmistakably in China's favor.
