# China Sets 2030 Target for Next-Gen Communications Network

China has issued an implementation plan to build a "comprehensively covered, leading-performance" next-generation communications network by 2030, a push that formally aligns the country's telecom build-out with its transition between the 14th and 15th Five-Year Plans. The plan, the 《新一代通信网建设实施方案》, was issued by the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology (MIIT) together with the Cyberspace Administration of China and the National Development and Reform Commission, with State Council approval, according to [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/tech/20261127/31719540007541c1ba640c9156663524/c.html), which republished a report from Economic Information Daily.

The roadmap places 5G-Advanced (5G-A) and 6G mobile networks, 10-gigabit optical networks, and satellite internet at its core, and organizes work around building "four networks," upgrading "three capabilities," and executing seven major engineering programs. It is the clearest official statement yet of how Beijing intends to sequence its move from the world's largest 5G footprint toward the next technology generation.

## What the plan commits to by 2030

Under the plan, 6G network construction is to be carried out "in due course," while 5G-A and 10-gigabit optical networks are deployed in key regions. The low-orbit satellite internet system is to be basically completed, and the space network is to form global service capability. International submarine and terrestrial cable layout will be continuously optimized, and industrial internet and vehicle-to-everything (V2X) networks are to reach large-scale development.

The plan is built around four pillars — basic networks, space networks, international networks, and converged networks — alongside three capability upgrades: all-domain intelligence, security and resilience, and green, low-carbon development. [Cailianshe](https://www.cls.cn/detail/2544801), citing CCTV News, described the next-generation network as an intelligent, integrated digital information infrastructure providing sensing, transmission, storage, computing and intelligence services, built on 5G-A/6G, 10-gigabit optical networks and satellite internet as its core support.

## A "nervous system" for China's digital infrastructure

MIIT's Information and Communications Development Department framed the network as foundational to the broader economy. A department official called the next-generation communications network the "nervous system" of economic and social operations, providing solid support for the digital-intelligent development of water networks, new-type power grids, computing networks, urban underground pipeline networks and logistics networks. The official said a "moderately advanced" build-out integrating space, air, ground and sea — and combining sensing, communication, computing and intelligence — is significant for accelerating network evolution, seizing the high ground of the digital industry transformation, and supporting high-quality economic and social development.

That framing links the plan to China's wider "new infrastructure" and "national computing network" strategies, which treat connectivity as a precondition for AI-driven demand rather than an end in itself. A defining feature of the document is its explicit aim to "adapt to and empower AI innovation," pointing to the fusion of communications with computing and AI.

## The industry base and the 6G runway

The plan arrives on top of a substantial existing base. According to MIIT data cited by Xinhua, China had built 5.254 million 5G base stations and more than 33.4 million gigabit-capable network ports as of the end of September. The first-phase technical trials for 6G have been fully completed, and hollow-core optical fiber and 50G-PON technologies are described as reaching internationally leading levels.

Du Ying, deputy director of the Wireless and Mobile Communications Research Institute at the China Academy of Information and Communications Technology, said the network will combine existing technologies with new ones, including "generation-leaping" 6G and integrated sensing-communication-computing-intelligence technologies for new applications. She noted that China has formed more than 300 key technology reserves in the 6G field and is fully carrying out second-phase trials, adding that the plan will lay a solid foundation for the third-phase pre-commercial equipment research and development and system networking trials.

## An economic multiplier

The plan is also presented as an industrial policy lever. According to the estimate reported by Xinhua, the next-generation communications network is expected to drive roughly 7 trillion yuan in total upstream and downstream output over the next five years.

Xia Qingying, an analyst at Wanlian Securities, argued that information infrastructure investment will remain at a high level during the 15th Five-Year Plan period, keeping operator capital expenditure elevated while accelerating 5G-A scale deployment and 6G industrialization. That, she said, will drive continuous upgrades of optical access and backbone bearer networks, sustaining incremental demand for optical fiber and cable and related communications equipment.

Corporate players are already positioning. ZTE has continued to strengthen satellite communications investment, working with operators and partners on direct-to-device NR-NTN in-orbit validation. China Mobile plans to systematically build 5G-A large-uplink capability and advance 6G technology trials, while China Unicom, working with Beijing University of Posts and Telecommunications, has completed small-scale optical switching networking at the Shanghai Lingang intelligent computing center — an approach it says shortens average iteration time by up to 8.5% compared with an all-electrical switching architecture.

## What to watch

The MIIT said it will coordinate implementation, regularly review the progress of major projects and investment, strengthen monitoring and evaluation, and broaden participation to mobilize investment by enterprises.

For industry watchers, the near-term signals will be the pace of 5G-A rollout, the transition of 6G trials from the second to the third phase, and the commercial build-out of low-orbit satellite internet — the arena where China's ambitions most directly intersect with global satellite broadband competition. The 2030 target gives the plan a clear finish line, but the sequencing of investment and the maturity of pre-commercial 6G equipment will determine whether that line is reached on schedule.
