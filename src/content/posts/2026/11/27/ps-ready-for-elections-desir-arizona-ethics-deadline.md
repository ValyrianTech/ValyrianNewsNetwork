# PS 'Ready for Elections' as Désir Warns Arizona on Ethics

Belgium's francophone Socialist Party (PS) remains "always ready to go to elections," federal MP Caroline Désir said on Friday, as she accused the country's embattled "Arizona" coalition of preparing to miss its own 1 December deadline for an agreement on extending abortion and euthanasia rights.

Speaking on La Première radio, Désir said the PS would table its own legislative texts in Parliament in the hope of assembling an alternative majority, according to [RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/le-ps-est-toujours-pret-a-aller-aux-elections-declare-caroline-desir-du-ps-qui-rappelle-l-arizona-a-ses-engagements-sur-l-ivg-et-l-euthanasie-11821975), which interviewed her on its Matin Première programme. Her remarks land as Prime Minister Bart De Wever's government remains paralysed by a budget crisis that has pushed Belgium toward the brink of early federal elections.

## A Deadline Set to Be Missed

The De Wever government had promised a "global agreement" on sensitive ethical questions — widening the legal term for abortion (known in French as IVG) and extending euthanasia to people suffering from dementia — by Tuesday, 1 December. With that date now days away, Désir said Justice Minister Annelies Verlinden had confirmed to Parliament that no government deal had been reached, and that the matter was not even on the Council of Ministers' agenda on Friday morning.

"She told us she had worked a great deal but that she had no agreement at government level. Besides, the point isn't on the Council of Ministers' agenda this morning. So necessarily, she won't make it for 1 December," Désir said.

She accused the coalition of having "blocked democratic debate within Parliament" — refusing to debate an opposition IVG text by invoking an obscure procedural article — in favour of a promised "big ethical package" she described as a catch-all bundle of unrelated sensitive dossiers.

"It obviously smelled of a big bargaining session between the various majority parties, on dossiers that a priori are not linked to one another: we're talking about individual rights, the autonomy of individuals… very sensitive ethical dossiers," she said. "It seemed a little hazy to us. But now, once again, they are not respecting their own deadlines and they continue to take Parliament hostage."

## 'We Never Hope for Chaos'

Désir was careful to insist the PS does not wish to see the country descend into instability. "No, we never hope for chaos in our country. What we are witnessing today is a lamentable spectacle for us," she said.

Her electoral rhetoric nevertheless signals a party positioning itself as a government-in-waiting. Recent polling appears to encourage that calculation. A late-November [LN24/Dedicated survey](https://www.lalibre.be/belgique/politique-belge/2026/11/23/sondage-ln24dedicated-des-elections-anticipees-feraient-souffrir-le-mr-OIVLWG2S4NCSXJTXKXOA5DMZTY/), with fieldwork from 5 to 16 November, found the PS leading voting intentions in Wallonia, with the MR and, to a lesser extent, Les Engagés losing ground.

Désir acknowledged that if those polls materialised at the ballot box, the Flemish nationalist N-VA would be "unavoidable." But she argued its programme is "very difficult to reconcile" with the PS's. "We are not at all on the same wavelength — notably on socio-economic matters, but not only. It's a nationalist party. It would be excessively difficult," she said.

## A Troubled Coalition

The ethical dossiers are the latest flashpoint in a government that has been unravelling for weeks. In early November, the King was forced to intervene in the budget crisis, rejecting De Wever's resignation offer and asking him to keep negotiating. The effort needed to balance the budget has risen to about 2% of GDP — a level De Wever himself called "unheard of since the start of this century."

More recently, De Wever has conducted bilateral talks with coalition partners CD&V, Les Engagés and MR while excluding the Flemish socialists of Vooruit, whose relations with the other partners have deteriorated sharply. Vooruit's president, Conner Rousseau, has conditioned his party's continued participation on a guarantee of wage-indexation protection and a "millionaires' tax."

On Friday, Rousseau reportedly dismissed a meeting convened by De Wever as "a masquerade." A scenario floated by Flemish liberal leader Frédéric De Gucht — replacing Vooruit with his Anders party in the majority — has been widely dismissed. According to [La Libre](https://www.lalibre.be/belgique/politique-belge/2026/11/27/anders-a-la-place-de-vooruit-une-voie-tres-etroite-pour-sauver-le-gouvernement-de-wever-2QGJLAJ6NNCR7AR7BG5SKNK45I/), multiple majority sources said the obstacles to such a move are "nearly insurmountable," with one minister saying flatly: "This track doesn't exist."

## What's Next

Désir's intervention shifts the pressure back onto the coalition on two fronts: the budget and the ethics package. Whether the PS can actually assemble an alternative parliamentary majority for its abortion and euthanasia texts remains an open question, given Belgium's electoral arithmetic. But the political signal is clear — the Socialists are preparing for the possibility that the Arizona government does not survive, and they intend to be ready if voters are called back to the polls.

For now, all eyes are on Tuesday. If the 1 December deadline passes without an agreement, it will become the clearest evidence yet that the coalition is struggling to govern — and the strongest argument yet for the early elections Désir says her party is prepared to contest.
