# China Appoints Lyu Wuqin Deputy Public Security Minister

China's State Council on Friday appointed Lyu Wuqin as Deputy Minister of Public Security, elevating a career police and anti-smuggling official to a vice-ministerial post just seven months after he joined the ministry's senior ranks. The announcement, issued through the Ministry of Human Resources and Social Security and carried by the state news agency [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/politics/20261127/3fd46c9152ca41488f30c6e7ec9a7e62/c.html), also reshuffled leadership at the Xinjiang Production and Construction Corps and removed a senior executive from China Media Group.

The same notice appointed Yang Hongbin as Deputy Commander of the Xinjiang Production and Construction Corps (XPCC) and Deputy General Manager of the state-owned China Xinjian Group, and named Ren Guangpeng as the corps' Deputy Political Commissar. It removed Xing Bo from the post of Deputy Director of China Media Group, the state broadcasting conglomerate that operates CCTV, China National Radio and China Radio International. No reasons were given for the removal, in keeping with standard practice for such notices.

## A Rapid Rise

Lyu's promotion is the most closely watched element of the batch. Born in September 1967 in Hejin, a county-level city in Shanxi province's Yuncheng municipality, Lyu holds a university education and spent much of his career inside the public security apparatus before a stint at the customs service.

According to [Caixin](https://china.caixin.com/2026-04-21/102436444.html), Lyu served as deputy director of the Ministry of Public Security's General Office around 2013 and as director of its Audit Bureau around 2018. In early 2019, when the ministry restructured its internal departments, he became the first director of the newly created Food and Drug Crime Investigation Bureau. He later headed the ministry's News and Publicity Bureau and served as director of the Anti-Smuggling Bureau at the General Administration of Customs before returning to the ministry.

His appointment as Minister-Assistant of Public Security was announced on 21 April 2026, a move the English-language [China Daily](https://www.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202604/21/WS69e70ae9a310d6866eb44a1c.html) reported at the time. That role filled a vacancy left when Liu Zhongyi stepped down in January 2026. At the ministry, Lyu held the rank of First-Class Police Commissioner and sat on the ministry's Party Committee.

A second [Caixin report](https://china.caixin.com/2026-11-27/102509174.html) noted that Lyu, now 59, had taken up two new posts in seven months — a pace of advancement that analysts read as a signal of strong institutional backing. A deputy minister's portfolio carries substantially more authority than that of a minister-assistant, and the makeup of Lyu's career points toward oversight of specialized investigation, anti-smuggling or food and drug enforcement.

## Xinjiang and Media Changes

The XPCC appointments fill command and political roles at an organization that blends economic production with local governance in Xinjiang. Yang Hongbin had already been a member of the corps' Party Standing Committee since at least mid-2025, while Ren Guangpeng had served as a Standing Committee member and head of the corps' Discipline Inspection Commission. The reshuffle follows the investigation of former XPCC Deputy Commander Li Xu, who was placed under review by the Central Commission for Discipline Inspection in January 2026 and subsequently expelled from the Party and public office.

The removal of Xing Bo from China Media Group marks a notable change in the conglomerate's top editorial leadership. Appointed to the role in February 2023, she was a long-serving executive who had previously headed the group's General Editorial Office and its International Exchange Bureau. As with the other decisions in the notice, no explanation accompanied her departure.

## A Broader Pattern

The notice lands amid a sustained period of turnover in China's security and regional leadership. The Ministry of Public Security has seen repeated reshuffles of ministers-assistant and deputy ministers through 2025 and 2026, including the appointment of Ling Zhifeng as a deputy minister in mid-2025 and Jia Lijun as political director in September 2025.

The personnel churn has extended beyond Beijing. On 26 November, one day before the State Council notice, the Sichuan Provincial People's Congress Standing Committee approved the appointment of Yu Lintao as the province's public security director, replacing Huang Ruixue, who died in office. Yu had previously served as vice-mayor of Dalian and chief of the city's public security bureau, and earlier worked in the ministry's Criminal Investigation Bureau.

## What to Watch

State Council personnel notices formally ratify decisions already reached within the Party and state personnel system, and are treated as routine administrative instruments. Their significance lies in the patterns they reveal. For Lyu, the key question is which portfolio he will oversee within the ministry's leadership — an announcement that would clarify whether his rapid ascent is tied to specialized enforcement or a broader command role.

For the XPCC, the appointments appear to be a routine leadership refresh in a strategically sensitive region, though whether they presage deeper consolidation of governance and discipline in Xinjiang remains to be seen. And for China Media Group, the absence of a stated reason for Xing Bo's removal leaves open whether it reflects retirement, rotation or internal restructuring — a point likely to be watched as the conglomerate's editorial leadership settles.
