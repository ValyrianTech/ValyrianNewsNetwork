# Gas Leak in Ixelles Near Brussels Inner Ring Road

A gas leak broke out Friday morning on the Place du Champ de Mars in the Brussels municipality of Ixelles, close to the inner ring road, prompting a response from emergency services and the evacuation of a nearby building. According to [RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/ixelles-fuite-de-gaz-pres-de-la-petite-ceinture-bruxelloise-11822097), the incident was confirmed by the Brussels-Capital/Ixelles police zone, and no injuries have been reported.

## Context

The Place du Champ de Mars sits in the heart of Ixelles, just south-east of central Brussels and adjacent to the petite ceinture — the ring of boulevards encircling the historic city centre. It is a dense, busy urban area near major thoroughfares, which explains the swift emphasis on keeping traffic and pedestrians away from the scene.

## Response and Developments

Brussels firefighters and Sibelga, the operator of the Brussels gas and electricity distribution network, were dispatched to the site to stop the leak. "The Brussels firefighters and Sibelga are on site to seal the leak," said Walter Derieuw, spokesperson for the Brussels fire service.

A safety perimeter was established around the affected area, and one building was evacuated as a precaution. The police zone asked the public to avoid the square and the surrounding streets while crews worked.

The cause of the leak was not immediately stated, and it was not immediately clear when the leak would be fully sealed or how long the road closures and evacuation would last.

## Analysis

The incident follows the standard playbook for gas leaks in the Belgian capital: firefighters secure a perimeter and evacuate nearby buildings while the network operator locates and closes the valves feeding the affected pipe before repairing it. Comparable episodes in recent years — including a leak at Place Royale in 2024 and one at the RTBF/VRT site on Boulevard Reyers in 2025 — were likewise contained without casualties.

For a city crisscrossed by an ageing gas distribution network, such incidents are a recurring test of coordination between emergency services and Sibelga. The quick evacuation and public advisory suggest the response followed routine procedure, though the underlying cause — whether excavation work, an ageing pipe or a valve fault — remains to be established.

## What's Next

Residents and commuters can expect the square and nearby streets to remain partly closed until the leak is sealed and the area declared safe. Motorists are advised to follow police directions and use alternative routes, particularly around the inner ring road, while the operation continues.
