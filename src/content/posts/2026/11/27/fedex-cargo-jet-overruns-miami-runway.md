# FedEx Cargo Jet Overruns Miami Runway, Snarling Travel

A FedEx cargo jet overran a runway at Miami International Airport on the eve of Thanksgiving, forcing the closure of all but one runway and triggering hourslong flight delays during one of the busiest travel periods of the year. No injuries were reported, but the incident has renewed scrutiny of runway safety at an airport still reeling from a deadly cargo crash less than three months ago.

The aircraft — a McDonnell Douglas MD-11 operating as FedEx Flight 910 from Memphis, Tennessee — came to rest in the grass beyond Runway 26R at approximately 8:40 p.m. local time on Wednesday, November 25, according to [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/us-news/fedex-airplane-overruns-runway-miami-airport-temporarily-closing-1-run-rcna611417). The plane used the airport's shortest runway, arriving in rainy, windy conditions that reduced visibility to about seven miles with a reported 13-mph tailwind.

## What Happened

The two people aboard the aircraft were unhurt. Emergency crews from the Miami-Dade Sheriff's Office and Miami-Dade Fire Rescue responded and reported no signs of fire or smoke.

In a statement, FedEx confirmed the incident and said the crew was safe. "FedEx Flight 910 from Memphis to Miami experienced an issue upon landing Wednesday evening at Miami International Airport," the company said. "We are grateful that our crew is safe and there are no reported injuries. We are coordinating closely with investigating authorities."

The airport announced the closure in a post on X, noting that all runways were shut except Runway 9R. "Passengers scheduled to travel tonight are encouraged to check directly with their airlines for the latest flight updates before heading to the airport," the airport said. "We appreciate our passengers' patience as we work to safely resume normal operations."

The Federal Aviation Administration issued a ground stop for departures bound for Miami until at least 9:45 p.m., then reported hourslong delays stretching into Thanksgiving morning. By Thursday afternoon, the National Transportation Safety Board confirmed it had opened an investigation into the runway excursion.

## A Witness Account

An airport employee who spoke to [CBS News Miami](https://www.cbsnews.com/miami/news/miami-international-airport-ground-stop-fedex-md-11-runway-excursion-faa/) described a harrowing landing. "They tried as hard as they could to stop, but I don't know if it was the weather because the rain and the wind were really bad, and they just couldn't stop in time and went right off the runway and veered off into the grass."

Traveler Dennis Pastorizo told the station he was stunned by the disruption. "I'm kind of shocked," he said. "I didn't think that a delivery plane would cause a two-hour delay for a flight to the other side of the country, especially on one of the busiest [travel days] of the year."

## A Familiar and Troubling Location

The overrun carries particular weight because of where it occurred. On September 6, an Amazon Prime Air Boeing 767 cargo plane overran a runway at MIA, breached the airport's perimeter fence and struck vehicles on a nearby road, killing at least five people and injuring others before catching fire, as [the New York Post](https://nypost.com/2026/09/06/us-news/amazon-boeing-767-plane-crashes-at-miami-airport-terrifying-video-shows/) reported at the time.

Three of MIA's runways converge at the airport's west end, so the FedEx jet came to rest near the spot where the Amazon aircraft careened across the road — even though it used a different runway, according to the [ABC News](https://abcnews.com/US/wireStory/fedex-airplane-overruns-runway-miami-airport-temporarily-closing-138460484) wire report.

The episode is the latest in a string of ground incidents at MIA this year. Less than two weeks before the FedEx overrun, a Copa Airlines flight and a UPS plane collided on a taxiway, with the Copa wing striking the UPS nose. The pattern may sharpen questions from safety observers about the airport's runway configuration and operational safeguards.

## The MD-11 Question

The FedEx aircraft is an MD-11, a tri-jet freighter design that has drawn fresh attention in cargo aviation. FedEx had returned MD-11s to service earlier this year after the FAA approved a Boeing fix for the fleet, following a deadly crash of a UPS MD-11 in Kentucky in which an engine separated on takeoff, killing three pilots and 12 people on the ground.

No mechanical cause has been established in Wednesday's overrun, and investigators have not attributed the incident to the aircraft type. Still, the timing — with the MD-11's recent return to service — will likely draw attention as the NTSB and FAA examine braking performance, wet-pavement conditions and the role of the tailwind on the airport's shortest runway.

## What's Next

Weather is a suspected factor in the overrun, but investigators have not confirmed a cause. The NTSB's investigation will focus on the aircraft's landing rollout, the runway's condition and length, and the crew's decisions in deteriorating conditions.

For travelers, the immediate disruption has eased, with operations continuing under delays. For MIA, the deeper concern is a broader one: a second cargo overrun in under three months, at an airport where runways already converge near a public road, is likely to intensify calls for a hard look at how one of the nation's busiest cargo hubs manages safety on its apron and approaches.
