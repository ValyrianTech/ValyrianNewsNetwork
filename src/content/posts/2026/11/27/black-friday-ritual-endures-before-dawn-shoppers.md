# Black Friday Ritual Endures as Shoppers Line Up Before Dawn

Shoppers migrated from Thanksgiving tables to store aisles on Friday, lining up outside Macy's Herald Square in Manhattan before sunrise for a Black Friday tradition that has survived weeks of early online discounts, a turbulent economic backdrop and the rise of artificial-intelligence shopping tools. According to [AP News](https://apnews.com/article/black-friday-deals-shopping-stores-online-21e10113de5d5941024b3220bec29433), the day remains the single biggest foot-traffic event on the U.S. retail calendar even as digital spending shatters records.

## A ritual that refuses to fade

Retail technology firm Sensormatic Solutions confirmed that Black Friday still generates more foot traffic at physical stores than any other day of the year, despite growing competition from e-commerce and brands rolling out steep holiday discounts online weeks in advance. Enough die-hard traditionalists still prefer to hit the shops the morning after Thanksgiving that lines formed well before opening.

Among those waiting were Nita Lawrence and her two nieces, visiting from Miami. They were disappointed that Macy's annual Thanksgiving Day parade had been postponed to Saturday because of high wind warnings, but found consolation in promotions that included handbags marked down 25% to 40% and sneakers reduced by 20% to 50%.

"We do Black Friday every year, but this Macy's is much bigger. This is special," Lawrence said. "We're going to do some shopping and then we are going to the airport. Today, I am buying for me."

## Online spending sets the pace

Even as the crowds gathered in person, the holiday season is increasingly being shaped online. U.S. consumers spent a record $7.1 billion online on Thanksgiving Day, a 10.4% increase from last year, led by gaming and electronics, according to Adobe Digital Insights. Adobe forecast that online shoppers would spend a record $12.9 billion on Black Friday itself, up 9.2% from 2025.

The National Retail Federation expects U.S. sales in November and December to rise 4.2% year-over-year to $1.013 trillion, [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/business/retail-consumer/black-friday-test-retailers-tight-budgets-meet-ai-savvy-shoppers-2026-11-27/) reported. Part of that increase, however, reflects inflation rather than volume growth; the trade body estimates higher prices account for about half of the forecast expansion.

## Inflation weighs on the mood

The shopping season is unfolding against a backdrop of voter frustration over gasoline prices tied to the U.S. war with Iran and high inflation overall. The good news for retailers is that consumer confidence jumped in November as gasoline prices continued a steady decline.

Still, the strain is visible in individual budgets. Aime Becker and her spouse, Tabatha Ferguson, visiting Manhattan from Illinois, expected mayhem at Macy's and were surprised to find the crowds calm. The couple estimated they had spent about $4,000 on their New York trip and planned roughly $1,000 on holiday gifts, but said rising food costs and daily necessities were squeezing their finances. They now shop at discounters like Walmart and Aldi, and even a salvage store that sells damaged or near-expiry goods.

"We have no savings like there's nothing to save because it's all gone," Becker said.

Atlanta resident Anna Butler, a retired schoolteacher, took a different view after buying six pairs of jeans at the flagship Macy's. "The economy is not good, but I am comfortable," she said, citing retirement and Social Security benefits.

## AI and essentials reshape the cart

The 2026 season marks a notable shift in how Americans shop. Adobe found that traffic to online retail sites from links in AI-powered tools more than doubled compared with a year earlier, and Reuters reported that nearly one in five consumers now begin their shopping with AI tools, according to Salesforce. Walmart CEO John Furner said customers using its Sparky AI assistant spent 40% more per order, while Amazon CEO Andy Jassy said shoppers using Alexa spent 50% more.

Shoppers are also using the sales to stock up on everyday necessities. Online sales of personal hygiene products were 40% higher from Nov. 1 to Nov. 22 than the October average, household cleaning products spiked 33% and clothing basics rose 27%, per Adobe. Julie Barber, chief merchandising officer at Walmart U.S., said customers were "being very intentional about how they're shopping."

"It's not just a moment where shoppers are buying gifts. They're also buying things that they need, and so that's going to help drive up a lot of the spend," said Caila Schwartz, retail and consumer insights leader at Salesforce.

## What to watch next

The five-day window from Thanksgiving through Cyber Monday will be closely scrutinized for clues about consumers' financial staying power heading into the new year. Analysts will watch whether deeper discounts — Salesforce said November deals were 8% bigger than last year's — can keep pulling shoppers toward registers, and whether AI-assisted comparison shopping continues to shift the balance between digital and physical retail. For now, the pre-dawn line at Macy's suggests that even in an age of one-click deals, some rituals are worth waking up for.
