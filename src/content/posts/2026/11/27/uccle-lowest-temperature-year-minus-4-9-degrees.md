# Uccle Records Year's Lowest Temperature at -4.9°C

Belgium's reference weather station in Uccle (Ukkel), Brussels, recorded the lowest temperature of the year so far on Friday morning, with the mercury dropping to -4.9°C. The reading, announced by KMI meteorologist David Dehenauw, marks the coldest November temperature at the station [since 24 November 1998](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/27/laagste-temperatuur-van-het-jaar-gemeten-in-ukkel/), when it fell to -7.4°C.

## A cold snap driven by easterly air

The reading comes amid an unusually early winter cold snap that has held Belgium in its grip for several days. According to [Nieuwsblad](https://www.nieuwsblad.be/binnenland/we-stevenen-af-op-de-koudste-novembermaand-van-deze-eeuw-hoe-uitzonderlijk-is-deze-vroege-winterprik/165053332.html), the cold is being funnelled in by an easterly air circulation between a high-pressure area over Scandinavia and a fairly active depression over Italy. That brings cold, dry continental air from Central and Eastern Europe — the opposite of Belgium's usual mild, moist westerly maritime flow.

"Usually we have an air circulation from the west over the sea in our country. That's why our winters are often milder and wetter than is currently the case," KMI meteorologist Frank Deboosere told the newspaper. "We are now dealing with an easterly circulation. That brings the cold from Central and Eastern Europe. That's why it is now colder and drier here than normal."

## Record low, but not the coldest night

The figure carries an important nuance. The -4.9°C minimum was reached only after 7:00 a.m., which means it does not count as the year's coldest *night* — the night officially ends at 7 a.m. Overnight the minimum in Uccle was -4.2°C, whereas the previous Tuesday night recorded -4.6°C, which remains the coldest night of the year. Nor is it the coldest *day* of the year, which is determined by the lowest average daily temperature rather than the overnight minimum.

Dehenauw announced the figure on X at around 8:40 a.m. "-4.9 degrees in Uccle in the meantime... Lowest temperature of the year there so far," he [wrote](https://www.nieuwsblad.be/nieuws/laagste-temperatuur-van-het-jaar-gemeten-in-ukkel-komende-nacht-tot-10-in-de-ardennen/165183712.html).

## Slippery roads and a sunny but freezing day

Code yellow for slippery roads was in force across all of Flanders until 11:00 a.m. on Friday, as wet road surfaces froze and local freezing fog formed. The day otherwise remained cold and sunny, with maxima between -2°C on the Ardennes heights and 1-2°C across most other regions, under a weak easterly wind. Forecasters warned of a further bitterly cold night into Saturday, with temperatures down to -1°C at the coast, around -3°C in the centre of the country and locally as low as -10°C in the eastern Ardennes.

## A month on track for the coldest since 1998

The cold snap has also pushed November as a whole toward unusual territory. Deboosere noted that the monthly average temperature so far stood at 6.1°C against a normal of 7.2°C — a notable deviation that reflects how consistently mild recent Novembers have been over the past 25 years. The month was on track to be the coldest November since 1998, when the average was 3.7°C.

Still, Deboosere was careful to keep the cold in perspective. Breaking the 1998 record would require an implausible average of -4°C for the rest of the month, which he said "certainly will not happen." Measured against the full historical record back to 1833, 2026's November is likely to rank around 50th coldest — far from the extremes of November 1921 (average 1.8°C) or 1858 (0.9°C).

The frost has also touched agriculture, though [only to a limited extent](https://www.nieuwsblad.be/nieuws/vrieskou-treft-landbouw-beperkt-maar-bloemkool-en-knolselder-dreigen-schade-op-te-lopen/165184256.html), with cauliflowers and celeriac at risk of damage.

## A return to milder weather

The easterly circulation is expected to persist through the weekend, keeping temperatures hovering around freezing. A gradual warming is forecast from Sunday, when the usual westerly circulation returns, pushing maxima back toward 6-7°C by the following week and a return to normal averages.

For now, the Uccle reading stands as a reminder of just how far a single shift in air circulation can pull Belgian weather away from its maritime norm — an early taste of winter that, while striking for recent decades, remains modest against the deeper freezes of the country's longer climate history.
