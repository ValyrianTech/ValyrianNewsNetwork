# Hong Kong Ferry Hits Structure Near Tsing Yi, 25 Injured

A passenger ferry travelling from Hong Kong's Sheung Wan district to Shekou in Shenzhen struck a hard object in the waters off Tsing Yi on Friday morning, injuring 25 people, five of them seriously, according to the latest official account. All of the injured were reported conscious, and rescue crews rushed to the scene as a large fleet of ambulances stood by.

![Injured passengers are treated after a passenger ferry struck a structure near the Tsing Yi oil depot, with more than 20 people injured and four reported more seriously hurt.](https://newsstatic.rthk.hk/images/mfile_1879461_1_20261127104845.jpg)

According to [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/gangao/20261127/453583155848469ca67d4631bf7d1c75/c.html), police received the report at 9:21 AM local time. The vessel was sailing to Shekou when it hit what the agency described as a "hard object" near the Tsing Yi oil depot (青衣油庫). The Fire Services Department deployed fireboats and a mobile casualty treatment vehicle, while numerous ambulances took up position at the scene. Authorities said the cause of the accident is under investigation.

## An Evolving Casualty Count

Early and later reports offered different figures as the rescue and assessment operation progressed. In its first dispatch on the incident, [RTHK](https://news.rthk.hk/rthk/ch/component/k2/1879461-20261127.htm) reported that more than 20 people had been injured, all conscious, with four in a more serious condition. In a subsequent update, the broadcaster [revised that tally upward](https://news.rthk.hk/rthk/ch/component/k2/1879468-20261127.htm), reporting that 25 people on board had been injured, five of them more seriously hurt — three sent to Princess Margaret Hospital (瑪嘉烈醫院) and two to Yan Chai Hospital (仁濟醫院).

Xinhua's figure of at least 24 injured, with four in serious condition, appears to be a mid-morning snapshot. The exact nature of the collision also remains the subject of slight variation in official accounts: Xinhua referred to a "hard object," while RTHK described the ferry as suspected to have struck "dockyard facilities" (船塢設施). Police have not publicly resolved whether the vessel hit another ship or a fixed marine structure.

## A Busy Industrial Waterway

Tsing Yi is one of Hong Kong's principal industrial islands, home to oil depots, dockyards and, nearby, the vast Kwai Tsing Container Terminals. The waters around the island carry heavy marine traffic and sit adjacent to some of the busiest shipping lanes in the Pearl River Delta — the very conditions that make navigational safety a persistent priority for the Marine Department and the ferry operators who ply these routes.

The Sheung Wan–Shekou crossing is a long-established cross-boundary passenger service linking Hong Kong Island with Shenzhen's Nanshan District. It is a fixture of the Greater Bay Area's commuter and business network, carrying a steady flow of passengers between the two cities. The collision therefore carries significance beyond the immediate scene: any disruption to, or scrutiny of, this route touches the wider web of cross-border transport coordination.

A passenger ferry striking a fixed structure near a fuel facility also raises questions about navigational clearances, visibility and the physical proximity of high-demand passenger routes to industrial marine infrastructure. The authorities' investigation is expected to weigh human, mechanical and environmental factors in turn.

## What's Next

The immediate priority remains the treatment and assessment of the injured, and the confirmation — expected from a police or Fire Services statement — of a final, authoritative casualty tally. After the most recent revision, that count stands at 25 injured, five of them seriously. Beyond that, attention will turn to the vessel's identity and operator, its passenger capacity and the specific circumstances of the collision.

Hong Kong's Marine Department and the operator are likely to face questions over whether the route's alignment posed any structural hazard, and whether safety procedures were observed. For the thousands of passengers who cross between Hong Kong and Shenzhen every day, the answers will matter well beyond the waters off Tsing Yi.
