# Gold Falls Below $3,500 as Fed Hike Bets Bite

Spot gold broke below the psychologically significant $3,500-an-ounce level on November 25, 2026, and spot silver slipped under $40 an ounce, as a hotter-than-expected US inflation reading hardened expectations of a Federal Reserve interest-rate hike in December. Gold fell 2.09% to $3,480.99 an ounce, its lowest level since September 2025, while silver dropped 3.51% to $39.468 an ounce, also its weakest since September 2025, according to [The Paper](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_34558960), the Shanghai-based news outlet that first reported the moves.

The declines cap one of the most dramatic reversals in precious-metals markets in recent memory. Gold had surged to an all-time high of $5,598.88 an ounce in January 2026 and has now fallen more than 37.5% from that peak, while silver sits roughly 67% below its record of about $121.68 an ounce, [Jiupai Finance reported](https://news.qq.com/rain/a/20261125A0FUKZ00). Year to date, spot gold is down close to 20%.

## The inflation trigger

The proximate catalyst was US October inflation data. The headline Personal Consumption Expenditures (PCE) price index rose 3.8% year on year, matching expectations but up from a prior 3.6%. More important for markets, core PCE—the Fed's preferred inflation gauge—climbed 0.3% month on month for a third consecutive month and 3.3% year on year, above consensus. Analysts cited by The Paper said the prints make a December rate hike "almost certain," and gold extended its losses after the release.

The mechanism is straightforward. Gold yields no interest, so rising rates raise the opportunity cost of holding it and make interest-bearing assets such as bonds relatively more attractive. A stronger US dollar, meanwhile, lowers the dollar price of gold—a dynamic [Guancha](https://www.guancha.cn/GuanJinRong/2026_11_26_909995.shtml) laid out in detail.

## A liquidity squeeze, not just a rate story

The rate narrative alone does not fully explain the speed of the fall. A concurrent crash in technology stocks and the associated pullback in the AI trade created margin and liquidity pressures, prompting investors to "sell gold to cover stocks"—a "sell the winners to fund the losers" dynamic that added another leg to the decline. In a vivid illustration of the chill in the tech complex, Anthropic—the first frontier AI company to list—broke below its $83 issue price just four trading days after its Nasdaq debut, falling to $76.6, down 12.6%, with its market value at risk of shrinking by nearly $200 billion, [Guancha reported](https://www.guancha.cn/GuanJinRong/2026_11_25_909838.shtml). The combination of forced liquidations, a stronger dollar and hawkish rate expectations proved too much for prices that had already run to extraordinary heights earlier in the year.

Silver's steeper fall reflects its dual identity as both a monetary and an industrial metal. With demand tied to solar panels, electronics and other high-tech manufacturing, silver was hit by worries about the tech-cycle outlook on top of the rate-driven pressure on precious metals generally.

## Institutions split on what comes next

Forward views from major houses diverge sharply. CITIC Securities argues that a short-term rebound hinges on whether rate-hike expectations are revised lower, with gold's near-term fund performance pulled by that debate. Over the medium term, it points to the "weak-dollar trade": if 2027 brings a deeper US equity correction, overseas investors may shift from trimming US stocks to cutting overall US asset exposure, giving gold more durable allocation buying.

Zheshang International says the focus should be on the policy path after the December hike and on 2027 real-rate trends, while noting that central-bank gold purchases and reserve diversification continue to support long-term allocation demand.

UBS Global Wealth Management is more constructive, reiterating an "attractive" rating on gold and forecasting the price will reach $5,000 by December 2027. UBS argues gold's investment value lies more in strategic allocation than short-term trading, citing four long-term supports: central banks continuously adjusting foreign-exchange reserve structures and reducing reliance on traditional reserve assets; high and growing global sovereign debt; persistent geopolitical uncertainty; and Chinese-market gold demand.

Goldman Sachs takes a more nuanced view, expecting the Fed to hike again in December 2026 and March 2027—which would continue to suppress Western gold-ETF demand—while still projecting gold could reach $5,600 by the end of 2028.

## What to watch

The December Fed decision is the single most important near-term variable. A confirmed hike could extend gold's weakness; a softer path could spark the rebound CITIC describes. Beyond that, investors will watch 2027 real-rate trends, whether a deeper US equity correction drives a shift out of US assets, and whether robust physical demand—including a reported wave of Chinese buying even as prices fell—can stabilize the market against ETF outflows. Silver's recovery, meanwhile, may hinge as much on the AI and technology-cycle outlook as on Fed policy itself.
