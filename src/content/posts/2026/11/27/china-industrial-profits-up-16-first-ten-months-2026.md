# China Industrial Profits Up 16.0% in First Ten Months of 2026

Profits at China's industrial enterprises above designated size (规模以上工业企业) rose 16.0% year-on-year in the first ten months of 2026, reaching a combined 6.737 trillion yuan, according to official data published by [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/fortune/20261127/bbfacfb59c844ad091908f7c8a963619/c.html) on 27 November. The result marks an acceleration from the January-September reading of 15.5% and keeps earnings in double-digit territory, while October alone surged 20.1% year-on-year — a full 5.7 percentage points faster than September.

## A Headline Built on a Narrow Base

The 16.0% gain is less uniform than the surface number suggests. The result is carried overwhelmingly by a single cluster of electronics and high-technology manufacturing, even as several traditional industries posted steep declines.

According to the [National Bureau of Statistics (NBS)](https://www.news.cn/fortune/20261127/5c703108d1744e0ea68c6f507a4700a6/c.html), the computer, communications and other electronic equipment sector grew profits by a factor of 1.0 — a doubling year-on-year — contributing 9.7 percentage points to the overall figure. Demand for artificial intelligence computing power was the primary engine, driving continued growth in memory chips and related peripheral manufacturing.

"Demand for AI computing power continued to increase, driving continued profit growth in memory chips and related peripheral manufacturing… an important support for the relatively rapid growth of above-scale industrial profits," said Yu Weining (于卫宁), Chief Statistician of the NBS Industrial Statistics Department.

High-tech manufacturing as a whole grew profits 50.9% year-on-year — 34.9 percentage points above the broader industrial average — with large-scale intelligent computing center construction lifting optical fiber and cable manufacturing, and demand for copper and aluminum pushing non-ferrous metals up 71.7%, together adding another 4.6 percentage points.

## Divergence Beneath the Surface

Stripping out electronics and non-ferrous metals removes more than 14 of the 16 percentage points of growth, exposing deep pressure elsewhere. Automobile manufacturing profits fell 26.0%, non-metallic mineral products dropped 46.7%, electricity and heat production and supply fell 18.4%, agricultural and sideline food processing declined 19.5%, and ferrous metal smelting and rolling fell 14.2%. The electricity, heat, gas and water production and supply sector as a whole contracted 14.8%.

The split points to a structural transition in which new growth drivers are racing ahead while many traditional industries undergo adjustment — a divergence visible in the same data release, which shows mining profits up 38.8% and manufacturing up 17.5% even as power generation contracted.

## Quality-of-Growth Warnings

Several balance-sheet indicators suggest demand-side and cash-flow pressures beneath the headline. Revenue rose 6.7% year-on-year to 118.41 trillion yuan, but accounts receivable grew 10.5% to 30.45 trillion yuan and finished-goods inventory climbed 12.0% to 7.54 trillion yuan — both outpacing revenue growth. The average receivables collection period lengthened by 1.4 days to 72.2 days, and finished-goods inventory turnover slowed to 21.2 days.

The operating revenue profit margin did improve, rising 0.45 percentage points to 5.69%, helped by a 0.42-yuan reduction in cost per 100 yuan of revenue to 85.06 yuan — a sign of ongoing cost control across industry.

## By Ownership

Among ownership categories, shareholding (joint-stock) enterprises led with 21.4% profit growth to 52.43 trillion yuan, followed by private enterprises at 11.4% and state-controlled enterprises at 13.0%. Foreign and Hong Kong/Macao/Taiwan-invested enterprises were near-flat at 0.2%, underscoring the uneven recovery across ownership types.

## What's Next

The data point to continued policy support into late 2026. The NBS interpretation called for strengthening macro-policy regulation, further smoothing economic circulation and cultivating new growth drivers to consolidate stable industrial operation.

The key question is durability. The electronics and AI-driven boom sits against a backdrop of external technology restrictions, meaning its longevity depends partly on domestic substitution capacity for advanced chips. Meanwhile, the sharp contractions in automobiles, construction materials and power generation will test whether new-economy growth can continue to mask legacy-sector weakness — or whether broader industrial momentum begins to flatten.
