# House Search at Bruine Mehdi as Sixth Complaint Filed

Belgian police have carried out a house search at the Ghent home of content creator Mehdi El Hadri, better known online as 'Bruine Mehdi', and seized several laptops and a mobile phone. The search, conducted on Thursday 26 November, coincided with the filing of a sixth complaint of possible sexual offences against the 32-year-old, [VRT NWS reported](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/27/contentcreator-bruine-mehdi-huiszoeking-nieuwe-klacht/).

## Context

The case began in early November, when the East Flanders prosecutor's office opened a preliminary inquiry (*opsporingsonderzoek*) into possible sexual offences after three women filed complaints with police. The allegations first circulated on social media, amplified by model and activist Flore Pensaert, before moving into the judicial system. According to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/11/_bruine-mehdi_-ontkent-aantijgingen-van-grensoverschrijdend-gedr/), the complaints followed days of online uproar over the creator, who had risen to wider fame through his participation in the TV quiz *De Slimste Mens ter Wereld* on Play.

Mehdi's lawyers issued a statement formally denying the allegations, and advocate Sanne De Clerck described the online reaction as an "online bonfire" and a "witch hunt", insisting that only a court can judge what did or did not happen.

## Escalating Investigation

The number of complaints climbed steadily. By mid-November, [two additional complaints](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/17/bruine-mehdi-vierde-klacht/) had brought the total to five, confirmed in part by the prosecutor's office and reported by VRT NWS from good sources.

The latest developments mark a significant escalation. According to [De Morgen and Belga](https://www.demorgen.be/nieuws/huiszoeking-uitgevoerd-bij-bruinemehdi-ook-zesde-klacht-ingediend~b3ffada8/), the house search was carried out with the suspect's consent — a requirement because the case remains a preliminary inquiry led by the prosecutor rather than a full judicial investigation involving an investigating judge. Electronics were among the items seized. The prosecutor's office and the defence declined to confirm or detail the search on Friday.

De Morgen noted that *Het Nieuwsblad* reported police also found handcuffs and a balaclava, though this detail was not confirmed to Belga. The sixth complaint, unlike the earlier ones, was filed without the involvement of Pensaert.

## Trial by Social Media

The case has become a flashpoint in Belgium over the tension between social-media accountability and due process. Legal experts have warned that public naming and the sharing of allegations can complicate the investigation — and potentially even benefit the accused.

"It could be that a suspect ends up receiving no sentence, or a lower one, because of the trial by social media," Liesbet Stevens of the Institute for the Equality of Women and Men told [Gazet van Antwerpen](https://www.gva.be/binnenland/experts-over-ophef-rond-bruinemehdi-en-rol-van-activiste-flore-pensaert-wat-heb-je-bereikt-als-hij-daardoor-geen-straf-krijgt/164484116.html). Human rights professor Sofie Royer warned that publicly gathering complainants could let the defence argue their accounts were aligned, while Stevens noted that suspects, once warned, can erase chats and images cited in testimonies.

Royer argued that "the trial by social media runs counter to the way we have agreed to resolve conflicts in society." She also suggested that Pensaert's approach could potentially be qualified as defamation.

## What's Next

A presumption of innocence applies, and the case remains at the investigative stage. Key questions remain open: whether the inquiry will be upgraded to a full judicial investigation with an investigating judge, the substance of the sixth complaint, and whether further complaints will follow.

For now, the seizure of devices signals that investigators are moving to secure digital evidence — a step that may shape how the case develops in the weeks ahead.
