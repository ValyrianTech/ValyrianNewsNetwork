# Indonesia Opens RMB Clearing Bank in Jakarta

A renminbi clearing bank in Indonesia officially opened in Jakarta on 12 November, with the Bank of China serving as the clearing bank, according to [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/20261112/890658499f2348449a05894659e1d5c9/c.html). About 100 representatives from Chinese and Indonesian government departments, enterprises, and financial institutions attended the ceremony at Bank Indonesia, the country's central bank. The launch is expected to facilitate cross-border trade and investment settlement in renminbi, lower transaction costs for businesses, and advance the internationalization of the Chinese currency in Southeast Asia.

## A New Pillar in the RMB Network

Twelve local and foreign banks — including Bank of China, Industrial and Commercial Bank of China, China Construction Bank, Mandiri Bank, and Bank Negara Indonesia — are serving as participating banks in the clearing house under authorization as cross-currency market makers, with trading infrastructure reported as ready.

Bank of China President Zhang Hui described the new institution as "a new important pillar of BOC's global RMB service network." He said the bank would build "four bridges" — cross-border payments, capital financing, green finance, and financial innovation — to improve clearing efficiency and enrich RMB financial products, citing support for mineral downstream processing, energy infrastructure, advanced manufacturing, the digital economy, and green transition around Indonesia's "Golden Indonesia 2045" vision.

## Trade Flows Already Shifting

According to [China News Service](https://www.chinanews.com/gj/2026/11-12/10730988.shtml), Zhang reported that from January to September 2026, Bank of China's domestic institutions recorded 609 billion yuan in cross-border RMB settlement with Indonesia, up 34 percent year-on-year. Bank of China is currently the largest bank in Indonesia's RMB business and the main channel for local RMB clearing.

At the ceremony, Bank Indonesia Governor Destry Damayanti said that with the clearing bank's launch and the maturation of the local currency transaction mechanism, enterprises can settle trade directly with China in renminbi, which "helps reduce reliance on third-party currencies, maintain exchange rate stability, and strengthen the regional economy's resilience against external shocks." She added that Bank Indonesia would continue deepening cooperation with China and further promote the use of the renminbi in Indonesia's foreign exchange market.

Indonesia's Coordinating Minister for Economic Affairs, Airlangga Hartarto, noted that China is one of Indonesia's largest sources of non-oil and gas imports and that expanding settlement in currencies other than the US dollar would significantly ease the external pressures the country faces. He encouraged Indonesian enterprises to make full use of the clearing mechanism and its derivative instruments, including RMB credit and panda bond issuance, to broaden financing channels.

## A Decade of Deepening Financial Ties

The launch caps a multi-year effort to link the two financial systems. Zhou Kan, Chargé d'Affaires of the Chinese Embassy in Indonesia, noted that China has been Indonesia's largest trading partner for 13 consecutive years and among its top three investment sources for 10 consecutive years. He traced a sequence that began with the 2021 launch of the bilateral local currency settlement mechanism and continued with the 2025 renewal of the bilateral currency swap agreement and the achievement of cross-border QR code payment interconnection this year.

The swap agreement, renewed in February 2025, was set at 400 billion yuan (878 trillion rupiah) and runs for five years, [according to](https://www.chinanews.com/cj/2025/02-07/10364665.shtml) China News Service. The next stage came in September 2025, when the two central banks formally launched a bilateral Local Currency Transaction (LCT) framework expanding settlement to all balance-of-payments items. Under that framework, China-Indonesia local currency settlement reached US$6.23 billion in January-July 2025 — 45 percent of Indonesia's total local currency settlement with all trading partners, [Xinhua reported](https://www.workercn.cn/c/2025-09-11/8603407.shtml).

## Analysis: De-dollarization by Infrastructure

The clearing bank matters less as a single event than as institutional plumbing. A network of RMB clearing banks around the world underpins offshore renminbi markets by giving foreign institutions a domestic counterpart for accounts, settlement, and liquidity. Bank of China was the first RMB clearing bank globally, in Hong Kong in 2003, and has since taken on the role in Malaysia, the Philippines, Cambodia, and now Indonesia.

For Indonesia, the arrangement is a way to insulate its economy from US dollar volatility while deepening ties with its largest trading partner. For Beijing, it is a further step in a broader campaign to reduce dependence on the dollar in trade and investment, particularly across Southeast Asia and Belt and Road partner countries. The emphasis on RMB credit and panda bonds signals that the next phase is as much about financing as about payments.

## What to Watch

The immediate test is uptake — whether Indonesian firms move beyond settlement into RMB-denominated borrowing and hedging at scale. Watch Bank Indonesia's progress on RMB foreign-exchange instruments and whether the country's panda bond pipeline accelerates. The broader question is how quickly a nascent offshore RMB market in Jakarta takes shape, and whether it can add a genuine second settlement currency to one of Asia's largest bilateral trading relationships.
