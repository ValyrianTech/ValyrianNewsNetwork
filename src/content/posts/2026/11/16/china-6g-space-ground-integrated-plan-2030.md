# China Sets 2030 Target for 6G in Space Integration Plan

China intends to launch commercial 6G services "at an appropriate time" by 2030 and is advancing a space-ground integrated network architecture that fuses satellite and terrestrial systems, according to specialist plans under the country's 15th Five-Year Plan (2026–2030). The strategy aims to deliver ubiquitous connectivity spanning air, space, and sea, and positions Beijing to shape global 6G standards and deployment as research and field trials accelerate, [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/tech/20261116/33ea36d3987d4b9d9439b1637bda5d7b/c.html) reported, republishing a report from Economic Information Daily.

The plans—the National Informatization 15th Five-Year Plan and the Information and Communications Industry Development 15th Five-Year Plan—set out targets to complete by 2030 "a new-generation communications network with complete coverage and leading performance," timely commercial 6G launch, and integrated advancement of satellite-terrestrial network fusion.

## Context: From internet of everything to intelligent connection

6G represents the next upgrade of communications networks into what planners describe as an "intelligent foundation" for the digital economy—a shift from 5G's "internet of everything" to 6G's "intelligent connection of everything." China has been building toward this transition for years. Its IMT-2030 (6G) Promotion Group, established by the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology (MIIT) in 2019, was among the world's earliest dedicated 6G research bodies, and Beijing has made the technology a priority across successive policy documents.

The new plans place original and disruptive technological innovation at the center of the agenda. According to [Xinhua Finance](https://cnfin.com/yw-lb/detail/20261029/4487905_1.html), the Information and Communications Industry Development 15th Five-Year Plan calls for accelerating core 6G technology R&D and continuing 6G technology trials. Alongside that push, the plans set broader targets: information and communications industry revenue reaching RMB 4.1 trillion by 2030 and cumulative information infrastructure investment of RMB 3.8 trillion.

## Narrative: Prototypes, tests, and demonstrations

The strategy has been matched by a burst of industry activity in recent weeks. At the 9th China International Import Expo, Qualcomm publicly displayed for the first time a 6G terminal prototype compliant with the latest 3GPP definition. Qualcomm China chairman Frank Meng said the company is working with China Mobile, ZTE, and other Chinese partners, and that in September 2026 Qualcomm and China Mobile completed interoperability testing between a U6G-band base-station prototype and the 6G terminal prototype—marking, in his words, 6G key technologies moving "from research exploration into a new stage of systematic verification."

At the 2026 World Internet Conference Wuzhen Summit, operators showcased a dedicated 6G technology exhibition. China Telecom and China Unicom presented a "6G ubiquitous intelligent-benefit network," an AIDN architecture integrating next-generation communications with computing power, direct connection to Tiantong satellites, and low-altitude economy capability systems—sketching an integrated air-space-ground-sea innovation map.

Du Ying, deputy director of the China Academy of Information and Communications Technology's Wireless and Mobile Communications Research Institute, framed the transformation sharply. Service objects, she said, are expanding from humans, machines and things to intelligent agents, while service space expands from the ground to air-space-ground—"a leap from '5G internet of everything' to '6G intelligent connection of everything.'"

The satellite industry is positioning itself at the center of this shift. Liu Chang, co-founder of GalaxySpace, said deeper integration of communications, navigation, remote sensing, and computing power will be a major trend during the plan period, with breakthroughs in space computing power and space energy injecting momentum into an integrated communication-navigation-sensing-computing service system.

## Upstream breakthroughs and operator commitments

The ambition is being backed by advances deeper in the supply chain. China Telecom Research Institute, fiber-maker YOFC, and ZTE jointly completed the world's first 1,000-km hollow-core fiber bidirectional real-time transmission verification. On a 1007.48 km link, the team achieved simultaneous bidirectional transmission at 64 Tb/s each way—128 Tb/s total—[Securities Times](https://stock.stockstar.com/SS2026110600009293.shtml) reported, describing it as a milestone in next-generation optical communications. China Telecom said the test verified hollow-core fiber's practical capability in long-distance scenarios.

On the operator side, China Mobile launched a "6G Smart Application Co-creation Action" to explore ten high-value 6G scenarios, including networked robots, AI terminals, immersive communications, and air-space-ground-sea integration. Wang Xiaoyun, a Chinese Academy of Engineering academician and China Mobile's chief engineer, said fewer than 30 months remain until 6G standards freeze—pointing to a window around early 2029—and that the industry must plan ahead so demand better guides network design. China Mobile said it will allocate R&D cooperation funds at the RMB 1 billion level and deploy a RMB 10 billion-scale industrial investment fund to cultivate specialized enterprises and accelerate commercialization.

## Analysis: Standards race and the integration dividend

China's approach carries strategic weight beyond its domestic market. With 6G standards expected to freeze around 2029 and commercial launches clustering near 2030, the country that shapes the underlying architecture—particularly how satellite constellations integrate with terrestrial networks—will influence equipment markets globally for a decade. China's push on satellite-terrestrial fusion, phone-direct-to-satellite applications, and standards-aligned prototypes with multinational partners like Qualcomm signals an intent to lead that architecture rather than merely adopt it.

The space-ground dimension is especially consequential. China is organizing commercial trials of satellite IoT services, with two private enterprises already approved for pilot operations, and MIIT has said it will support more qualified applicants. Companies including GalaxySpace and Guoxing Aerospace are working on direct-to-device connectivity and in-orbit computing. Satellite internet, phone-direct-to-satellite services, and the low-altitude economy are widely seen as among the earliest 6G commercialization directions—markets that blend telecom, aerospace, and industrial policy.

Government support is being structured to match. MIIT convened a symposium on accelerating next-generation communications network construction, proposing to coordinate planning and policy, resolve land, power, pipeline, corridor, and spectrum factor-guarantee issues, improve investment mechanisms, and guide more social capital into the sector.

## What's next

Several open questions will shape how quickly China's 6G vision becomes commercial reality. The exact regulatory timeline for 6G spectrum allocation—including the U6G and 6 GHz bands—remains unannounced. The precise meaning of "timely" commercialization leaves room for first launches before or after full standards maturity. And the commercial viability of hollow-core fiber beyond long-haul trials will depend on cost curves yet to be proven at scale.

What is clear is the direction. With targets set, prototypes tested, operators committing capital, and standards work entering its final stretch, China is treating 6G not as a distant research program but as near-term industrial policy—one that ties its satellite, optical, and telecom ambitions into a single integrated network. How that architecture is standardized, and how it interacts with competing Western 6G initiatives, will determine whether the 2030 "intelligent connection of everything" becomes a shared global framework or a bifurcated one.
