# Bitcoin Slides Below $70,000 as Strong Dollar Bites

Bitcoin has dropped below the $70,000 mark, the first time it has traded under that threshold since August, as a strengthening US dollar and persistently high interest rates pull investors away from riskier assets. The world's largest cryptocurrency dipped below $70,000 (about €63,200) in the hours before [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/13/waarde-van-bitcoin-duikt-onder-kaap-van-70-000-dollar/) reported on the move on Friday, while Ethereum — the second-largest cryptocurrency — slid beneath $2,000 (roughly €1,800).

The decline is not an isolated crypto event but the digital-asset dimension of a broader macro-financial squeeze. A resurgent dollar, rising US inflation and a global tech-stock rout have combined to sour the mood toward speculative holdings.

## A Safe-Haven Flight From Risk

As VRT NWS explained, cryptocurrencies are "taking hits from economic uncertainty," which is driving investors toward less risky holdings. The dynamic is classic risk-off behavior: when volatility rises and safer yields become more attractive, capital retreats from assets that offer no income stream.

The retreat built on weeks of turbulence in equity markets. Global stocks sold off heavily after the Democrats' victory in the US midterm elections on November 3, with technology shares hit hardest on fears of stricter regulation. The turmoil deepened when Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang was forced to step down over personal scandals — a shock that wiped roughly $1.9 trillion (about €1.7 trillion) off the chipmaker's value and rippled through the index funds and pension portfolios that hold it.

"This is a technical move, as Bitcoin broke down through its 200-day moving average of about $73,000, which has caused a slew of liquidations and new shorts to develop," Rich Rosenblum, co-founder of crypto trading firm GSR, said, according to Bloomberg.

## Why a Strong Dollar Hurts Bitcoin

Beneath the immediate selling lies a structural mechanism that ties Bitcoin's fortunes to US monetary policy. US inflation, driven by high energy prices, continues to climb; the consumer price index came in higher than expected on Tuesday, November 10. That has hardened expectations that the Federal Reserve will raise interest rates further to cool prices.

Higher rates make holding dollars — for instance in US Treasury bonds — more attractive. Those are safe investments, and the resulting demand lifts the value of the US currency. The dollar index, which measures the greenback against six other major currencies, has climbed above 103 points, its highest level since April 2025, according to VRT NWS.

Bitcoin, priced in dollars and currently inversely correlated with the currency, feels the pinch directly. High real yields also raise the opportunity cost of holding non-yielding assets — a drag that weighs equally on gold and on Bitcoin.

The pressure has intensified rather than eased. By Monday, [FXStreet](https://www.fxstreet.com/cryptocurrencies/news/crypto-today-bitcoin-ethereum-xrp-face-increasing-selling-pressure-etf-outflows-202611161030) reported Bitcoin trading around $64,439, having touched an intraday low near $62,006 the previous week — levels not seen since mid-July. Ethereum traded near $1,807.

## ETF Outflows Sap the Institutional Cushion

The slide has been compounded by a steady leak of institutional capital. Spot Bitcoin exchange-traded funds recorded slightly more than $1 billion in outflows last week through Friday, FXStreet reported, marking a third consecutive week of withdrawals and bringing cumulative outflows to $54.6 billion. Assets under management fell to $86.2 billion. Ethereum spot ETFs have now bled capital for seven straight weeks, with $399 million withdrawn in the latest period.

Those outflows matter because ETFs have functioned as the institutional backbone of the recent crypto cycle. "ETFs, stablecoin liquidity, and corporate treasuries have served as the primary capital pillars supporting this Bitcoin rally," Tim Sun, a senior analyst at HashKey, told Decrypt. "With all three coming under simultaneous pressure, the market is losing the marginal buying power that previously propelled prices higher."

## Analysis: A Test of Crypto's Macro Maturity

The episode is a reminder that Bitcoin, for all its reputation as "digital gold," still trades like a high-beta risk asset when the macro weather turns. Its inverse relationship with the dollar in this environment exposes how much of its valuation is a bet on loose money and cheap liquidity rather than on any intrinsic store-of-value function.

The technical picture reinforces the caution. With Bitcoin below its 200-day moving average and key exponential moving averages clustered overhead near $76,000, any rebound faces layered resistance. Crypto-specific drags — including the record late-October Binance hack, attributed to North Korea-linked actors, and capital being drawn toward Anthropic's initial public offering — add to the headwinds.

Yet some analysts see exhaustion rather than collapse. Bitcoin's relative strength index sits deeply oversold, and Sun noted that a concentrated liquidation of leveraged long positions — rather than a wholesale rejection of the asset — sparked the most recent leg down.

## What to Watch

The near-term direction hinges almost entirely on the Federal Reserve. Markets have priced roughly a 93.5% chance of a 25-basis-point rate hike at the Fed's December 9 meeting, according to CME FedWatch data cited by FXStreet. A hotter-than-expected inflation reading would keep upward pressure on the dollar and downward pressure on crypto; a softer print or a dovish shift could offer relief.

Two further catalysts loom: Nvidia's earnings on November 17 and the progress of Anthropic's IPO, both of which could swing risk appetite across markets — and drag crypto along with it. For now, a stabilization in ETF flows may be the clearest signal that the worst of the sell-off has passed.
