# Bruges Man Sets 69-Hour Belgian Bar-Sitting Record

A 28-year-old Bruges man has been crowned Belgian champion of *toogzitten* — bar-sitting — after remaining on a barstool for 69 uninterrupted hours. Dylan Debaene took his seat at Café De Waert on Friday morning and stood up only on Monday at 6am, according to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/16/dylan-debaene-zit-69-uur-op-een-barkruk-in-brugge-en-is-belgisch/), breaking the previous record of 60 hours by a full nine.

## A study in persistence

The attempt began on Friday at 9am. As [VRT NWS reported](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/13/dylan-debaene-wil-61-uur-aan-de-toog-hangen/) ahead of the event, Debaene had set himself an initial target of "at least 61 hours" to clear the 60-hour mark. He was permitted five minutes per hour to stretch his legs and visit the toilet — and could bank those breaks up to three times over, to accumulate at most a quarter of an hour of rest. Otherwise, the rule was simple: stay seated.

He had compression stockings and medical supervision on standby. "The GP and physiotherapist are on standby," he said before beginning. In the end, he did not need their help.

The challenge was not a passive one. From his stool, Debaene joined a card game, a quiz, a communal breakfast and a dance evening; DJs played through the night, and he brought earplugs. Sunday evening, during the Belgium–Italy football match, he crossed the 60-hour threshold. On Monday, after a final countdown, he rose at the 69-hour mark — nine hours beyond the previous record.

"They were intense and beautiful hours, surrounded by family and friends, carried and supported by them," Debaene told VRT NWS. "But especially the last few hours were very intense. Only my father and I remained. We did it together, so I'm very grateful for that." His grandparents set an alarm for 3.30am to witness the final stretch.

## A personal and charitable mission

Debaene lives with ankylosing spondylitis — an inflammatory arthritis affecting the spine — and a tremor. Rather than dwelling on the difficulty, he framed the feat as a message of inclusion and support for Belgium's hospitality sector. "At the same time I want to encourage everyone to come outside and seek each other out," he said. "I myself suffer from a muscle and joint disease and also a tremor. Because of that I know that isn't self-evident for everyone."

Asked whether the condition hindered him, he was candid: "I can't say I suffered much. It was mainly the fatigue and the noise in the café that made it hard. To sit in a café for so long so sober was actually quite confronting."

The attempt was also a fundraiser. Proceeds from the linked benefit action go to vzw Boven De Wolken, a non-profit that supports parents of "star children" — children who have died. Local outlet [KW](https://kw.be/nieuws/economie/horeca/hij-doet-het-dylan-debaene-28-uit-brugge-verpulvert-belgisch-record-toogzitten-er-was-nooit-een-dood-moment/) captured the mood of the achievement under the headline "Er was nooit een dood moment" — "There was never a dull moment."

## The wider toogzitten tradition

Bar-sitting records are a recurring, self-organised endurance craze in Belgium, particularly in West Flanders, where local media track successive "Belgian records" as they change hands among cafés and regulars. It is an informal, media-recognised tradition rather than a federation-sanctioned sport — a blend of personal endurance, community bonding and local fundraising that fits comfortably within Flemish café culture.

## Bearing "the same as few glasses"

For his part, Debaene was eager for rest. "It's time to sleep... I'm going home, switching off my phone and withdrawing like a caveman for a while. The afterparty will be without me." With his record banked and his cause served, the new champion left his barstool — and went in search of his bed.
