# One in Three Belgians Struggled to Pay the Mortgage

One in three Belgians had difficulty paying their mortgage at least once over the past year, according to a survey commissioned by VDK Bank, as [VRT NWS reports](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/16/1-op-de-3-belgen-had-voorbije-jaar-minstens-1-keer-moeite-om-woo/). The finding, drawn from responses by 2,200 Belgians and first covered by Het Laatste Nieuws, points to mounting financial strain on households rather than outright default.

The distinction matters. The figure does not describe people who can no longer pay their loans at all, but moments when the household budget becomes too tight to meet the housing instalment comfortably. Those pinch points cluster in the more expensive months of the year, when extra costs pile up.

## Where the pressure bites

"I spontaneously think of September, back to school," Steven Wauters, commercial director of VDK Bank, told VRT NWS. "But also extra costs, like paying membership fees for hobbies, and so on."

The survey paints a picture of households trimming spending to stay afloat. Half of respondents say they are cutting back on holidays and leisure. A third are economising on clothing and food, and one in five are scaling back even on water, electricity, gas and mobility.

"Those are, after all, the basic needs that are being cut back on at that moment," Wauters said.

He points to three forces behind the squeeze: a rising cost of living, persistently high property prices, and higher interest rates that have made mortgages more expensive to repay.

## Renters under similar strain

The pressure is not confined to homeowners with a loan. Among renters surveyed, 40 percent said they had difficulty paying rent at least once a year. Rents, too, have been on an upward path, driven partly by indexation.

Wauters argues that renters are structurally more exposed. "People who rent often do so because they lack the means to buy a home," he said. "They are therefore already in a segment that is financially somewhat more vulnerable."

The rate environment has compounded the problem for borrowers. As [Test Aankoop explains](https://www.test-aankoop.be/geld/hypotheekleningen/nieuws/impact-stijgende-rente-op-woonkrediet), the European Central Bank's rate rises have pushed up mortgage rates, and borrowers on variable-rate loans can see their monthly instalments climb when their rate is revised.

## A market still under pressure

Property prices remain a structural drag on purchasing power. [Immoweb's price monitor](https://blog.immoweb.be/nl/kopen/laagste-stijging-in-5-jaar-belgische-vastgoedprijzen-nemen-dit-jaar-toe-met-29/) has charted continued pressure on what Belgian households can afford, even as the pace of price growth has cooled from the peaks of recent years. Longer loan terms have become a common workaround, stretching affordability at the cost of more interest paid over time.

## Not a default crisis

It would be wrong to read the survey as evidence of a wave of defaults. Belgian repayment problems have historically remained limited. Figures from the Central Credit Register for Individuals (CKP), cited by [Credit Expo](https://www.creditexpo.be/2-op-3-belgen-sloten-ooit-al-lening-af/), have shown that the overwhelming majority of mortgage borrowers pay on time, with arrears affecting only a small fraction of loans. The picture the VDK survey captures is more subtle: occasional liquidity crunches rather than structural insolvency.

That reading is consistent with the way the findings are framed. The difficulty is concentrated in specific months and tied to irregular expenses, not to a lasting inability to service debt.

## What to watch

For households, the practical test will be whether the strain remains episodic or hardens into persistent arrears as higher rates work through variable-rate loans and rents continue to be indexed upward. The survey offers a snapshot rather than a trend line: it is a single, bank-commissioned poll, and the full methodology, including sampling and margin of error, has not been disclosed. Still, its broad contours line up with independent data on living costs and borrowing.

The coming months will show whether the expensive months of the calendar translate into longer-term stress. For now, the picture is one of stretched budgets rather than broken ones, with a third of mortgage-holders and a larger share of renters feeling the pinch at least once a year.
