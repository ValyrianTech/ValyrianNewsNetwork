# Three Children Hit at Zebra Crossing in Charleroi

Three young children were struck by a car as they crossed a zebra crossing in Couillet, a sub-municipality of Charleroi, during Monday morning's school run. The driver fled the scene and is being actively sought by police, according to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/16/ongeval-charleroi/). One of the girls is fighting for her life after being resuscitated at the scene.

The collision happened shortly after 7am on Rue de Villers, near the local cemetery, when a Seat approached "at very high speed" as the children were crossing, VRT NWS reported. The car may have been damaged in the impact.

## What we know about the victims

The Dutch-language public broadcaster identified the injured as three girls aged 4, 5 and 6, who were walking to school with their mother. The mother was also pushing a fourth child in a stroller at the time.

VRT NWS initially reported that the baby in the stroller had also been hit, but later corrected the record to confirm the infant was not struck. One girl suffered an open fracture to her arm. A second sustained severe head trauma and had to be resuscitated at the spot; her condition is described as life-threatening. No further information was immediately available on the condition of the third girl.

French-language outlet [Sudinfo](https://www.sudinfo.be/id1226929/article/2026-11-16/accident-couillet-une-maman-et-ses-quatre-enfants-de-moins-de-7-ans-renverses-le) reported a slightly different account, describing a mother and her four children, all under the age of seven, as the pedestrians involved. It said two children were seriously injured, with one's life in danger, and noted that no update was available on the mother or the other two children. The discrepancy between the two accounts — whether three or four children were hurt — had not been fully resolved by midday.

A large emergency response was mobilised, with multiple ambulances and SMUR (mobile intensive-care) teams dispatched to the scene. Rue de Villers was closed to traffic to allow rescue workers and police to work.

## Manhunt under way

The driver did not stop after the collision, a hit-and-run offence known in Belgium as vluchtmisdrijf or délit de fuite. Charleroi police are analysing surveillance-camera footage in an effort to identify the vehicle and its driver. As Sudinfo reported, an automobile expert was also expected on site to carry out technical observations and help establish how the accident unfolded. No details about the driver's identity have been released.

## A stricter legal framework

If caught, the driver would face penalties under Belgium's revised Criminal Code, which applies to offences committed on or after 1 September 2026. Leaving the scene of an accident involving injuries carries a level-2 penalty: imprisonment of six months to three years and a fine of €4,000 to €50,000, according to [legal analysis from Novex Advocaten](https://novexadvocaten.be/fr/delit-de-fuite-belgique-sanctions-interdiction-de-conduire-defense/). A driving disqualification of at least three months — and up to five years, or permanently — is mandatory, with reinstatement requiring fresh theory, practical and psychological exams.

Crucially, Belgian law requires a driver to stop and identify themselves even when the accident was not their fault. The public prosecutor can also immediately withdraw a fleeing driver's licence for 15 days, extendable to three months by the police court. Because the vehicle's driver remains unidentified, any compensation for the victims' bodily injury would typically fall to Belgium's Motor Insurance Guarantee Fund.

## Why this resonates

The incident struck at one of the most sensitive points in Belgian public life: the safety of children on the daily walk to school. A zebra crossing — the very infrastructure meant to protect the most vulnerable pedestrians — became the site of the collision, and the driver's decision to leave the scene has sharpened public concern.

The crash also lands amid a period of heightened attention to road safety and traffic enforcement in Wallonia. Whether the crossing's design, speed limits on Rue de Villers, or enforcement in the area will come under scrutiny remains to be seen, but accidents at school-run crossings routinely prompt calls for tougher measures.

## What to watch

Several questions remain open. The most urgent is whether police locate the driver and the Seat, and whether CCTV footage yields an identification. The exact number and condition of the injured children, along with any update on the mother, should become clearer as hospitals and authorities release information. A statement from the Charleroi prosecutor's office is also expected as the investigation advances.

For now, the priority is the child whose life remains in danger, and a community waiting for answers about how a routine morning walk to school turned into a manhunt.
