# Lukaku Retires From Belgium's Red Devils at 33

Romelu Lukaku, Belgium's all-time record goalscorer, announced his retirement from international football on Monday morning, ending a 16-year Red Devils career that spanned four World Cups and three European Championships. The 33-year-old striker revealed the decision in an Instagram post, just a day after sitting out Belgium's 4-1 Nations League win over Italy without being substituted on.

According to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/16/romelu-lukaku-stopt-als-rode-duivel/), Lukaku leaves the national team with 135 caps and 94 goals — placing him fourth on the all-time list of international goalscorers, behind only Cristiano Ronaldo, Lionel Messi and Ali Daei.

## A Statement of Gratitude

Lukaku's message struck a reflective tone, emphasising gratitude over grievance.

"16 years," he wrote. "Over the past 16 years I gave everything for the national team. I experienced every possible emotion. I played with great teammates and I will always be grateful for that."

He added: "It was a fantastic journey and I enjoyed every moment. To the Belgian fans: thank you for supporting the team. I am very proud to have represented my country at 4 World Cups and 3 European Championships."

"I will never forget those moments. I am at peace with my decision to stop. It was great, it was a joy, and I am very grateful."

## The Context Behind the Decision

While the announcement came suddenly, it followed months of tension between the record scorer and the new coaching regime under head coach Mark van Bommel, appointed in 2026.

Van Bommel implemented a high-intensity, high-pressing style that demanded relentless movement and work-rate — a system analysts noted was an awkward fit for the more static, physical Lukaku. Under the Dutchman, Lukaku featured in only three matches — two substitute appearances against Italy and Turkey and one start in the 4-1 defeat to France — scoring once, against the Turks, and was hampered by injury. He missed the November away game against Turkey with hip problems.

The decisive moment appeared to arrive on Sunday. Declared fit and named in the squad for the Italy match, Lukaku warmed up for 30 minutes during the second half but was never brought on. Belgian supporters had honoured him beforehand with a large tifo alongside Kevin De Bruyne. Van Bommel defended the omission, saying the striker had been injured for a few days and would only be used "in case of high emergency."

## A Career of Records

Lukaku made his debut on 3 March 2010 at just 16 years, 9 months and 18 days, in a friendly against Croatia under Dick Advocaat. He went on to become the defining striker of Belgium's celebrated "Golden Generation," a side that also featured Vincent Kompany, Eden Hazard, Kevin De Bruyne and Thibaut Courtois.

His goals helped end Belgium's long absence from major tournaments, and he qualified the country for the 2014 World Cup in Brazil. The high point came with a bronze medal at the 2018 World Cup in Russia. He scored at a remarkable rate of roughly one goal every 92 minutes for his country, finishing 5 caps short of Axel Witsel and 22 shy of record cap-holder Jan Vertonghen.

## A Generation in Transition

The retirement accelerates a generational handover. Axel Witsel (37) announced his own international retirement in September 2026 after 140 caps, and other Golden Generation members are departing as a new cohort takes the field.

Recognition poured in from teammates and fellow athletes. "Thank you for everything, my brother," wrote goalkeeper Thibaut Courtois, while Jeremy Doku called him "a legend." Belgian basketball star Emma Meesseman simply posted: "King." As [Sporza](https://sporza.be/nl/2026/11/16/rode-duivels-zwaaien-romelu-lukaku-uit-met-lof-grote-meneer-legende-en-zoveel-mooie-herinneringen~1794823321688/) reported, the tributes reflected a dressing room paying farewell to one of its defining figures.

## What's Next

Belgium finished second in its Nations League group behind France and is preparing for the quarter-finals. Van Bommel, reacting to the news, paid tribute to his departing striker's legacy.

"We respect Romelu's decision," the coach said. "Romelu wrote history with the Red Devils, not only through his performances and goals, but also through what he meant to the group."

For Lukaku, the focus now shifts to club football with Turkish side Fenerbahçe, whom he joined in summer 2026. Whether Belgium can replace its most prolific goalscorer — and eventually whether the 100-goal milestone might have been within reach — are questions that will hang over the Red Devils as the post-Lukaku era begins.
