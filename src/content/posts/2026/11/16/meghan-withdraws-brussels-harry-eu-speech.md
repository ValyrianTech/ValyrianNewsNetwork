# Meghan Silent in Brussels as Harry Speaks to EU Alone

Meghan, the Duchess of Sussex, withdrew from a planned speech at the European Parliament in Brussels on Tuesday, leaving her husband Prince Harry to address the chamber alone — just days after her father was arrested in the United States on federal charges of possessing child sexual abuse material.

The couple had been invited by the parliament's Intergroup on Children's Rights for a joint 15-minute keynote on children's digital safety. Instead, [VRT NWS reported](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/10/harry-en-meghan-europees-parlement/), Meghan stepped back at the last moment, saying through a source that she felt "not strong enough" to speak publicly so soon after the case against her father became public.

## A Father's Arrest

Thomas Markle Sr., 82, was arrested on November 6 near Tampa, Florida. According to a [US Department of Justice press release](https://www.justice.gov/usao-cdca/pr/thomas-wayne-markle-arrested-federal-complaint-charging-him-possessing-sexually), he faces one count of possession of child pornography after FBI agents searched two Los Angeles-area storage units he had rented since 2008. Investigators seized roughly 78 items, including slides, film negatives and books, and described images allegedly depicting Markle with prepubescent girls.

If convicted, Markle faces a statutory maximum sentence of 20 years in federal prison. He has denied the allegations, telling the Daily Mail he is "not a pedophile." A criminal complaint contains only allegations, and all defendants are presumed innocent until proven guilty.

"These allegations are extremely serious, and we will prosecute Mr. Markle to the fullest extent of federal law," First Assistant US Attorney Bill Essayli said in the DOJ statement. FBI Director Kash Patel said the bureau acted to protect children, adding that Markle "allegedly targeted innocent children and sought to steal their innocence."

The case spans several jurisdictions. Philippine police reportedly found a large quantity of child abuse images during a raid on Markle's apartment in Cebu in late October, and the investigation reportedly began after his son, Thomas Markle Jr., alerted authorities. On November 7, a California court placed Markle under electronic monitoring in a Florida care facility and ordered a psychiatric examination amid concerns about possible dementia — which will determine whether he can be questioned.

## A Controlled Public Appearance

The Brussels visit was the Sussexes' first joint working engagement in Europe since their return to England. Meghan accompanied Harry throughout, holding his hand as they entered the venue, but did not speak. Journalists were pre-briefed that the couple would not answer questions, and they avoided the press.

After his address, Harry opened an exhibition and visited an art installation memorialising children who died as a result of online bullying. In his speech, [Harper's Bazaar reported](https://www.harpersbazaar.com/celebrity/latest/a74386852/prince-harry-european-parliament-speech-childrens-online-safety-meghan-markle-father-arrest/), he asked why society can build extraordinary technology "yet still struggle to make it safe for a child." He said all children "deserve to feel safe wherever they are" — in a classroom, on the playground or online — while cautioning that they should not be kept off the internet entirely.

Harry made no reference to his father-in-law or the case during his remarks.

## Why Meghan Stays Silent

Meghan's decision to avoid speaking appears deliberate and legal in nature. VRT reported that she likely remained quiet to avoid compromising the judicial proceedings against her father — consistent with her stated rationale for having stayed silent about the allegations for years.

In late October, Meghan confirmed she had known of the abuse allegations since 2019, when she was informed by British police, and said she kept her children away from her father to protect them. She called the alleged abuse "disgusting" and said she hoped "justice will be done and that the protection of innocent children remains paramount."

Her silence in Brussels also reflects the tricky reputational timing. As royal commentator Pascale Mertens told VRT, "the timing is especially bad." With the couple rebuilding ties with the British royal family after their move back to England, the visit tested how they manage a private crisis in a highly formal, high-visibility European setting.

## The Wider Picture

There is a stark irony in the setting. The Sussexes have long campaigned on mental health and child protection, previously working with the United Nations and the World Health Organization. The Brussels engagement — focused on shielding young people from online harms in the age of social media and artificial intelligence — sits squarely within that advocacy. Yet it unfolded against allegations that touch the very same theme.

Reports say Meghan's mother, Doria Ragland, is cooperating with the FBI investigation, adding another layer of family strain. The couple's public posture — present, united, but silent on the case — appears designed to keep the focus on their cause while allowing the legal process to run its course.

## What to Watch

The immediate question is whether Meghan will make any public statement on the case before the proceedings conclude, or maintain her silence. Much also depends on the pending psychiatric examination: its conclusions could shape whether Markle can be questioned and whether he is fit to stand trial. Meanwhile, US investigators have not yet publicly identified victims in the United States, and the parallel Philippine inquiry continues.

For now, the Brussels appearance offers a study in careful crisis management: a message delivered, a controversy sidestepped, and a family's private turmoil kept just behind the curtain of a very public stage.
