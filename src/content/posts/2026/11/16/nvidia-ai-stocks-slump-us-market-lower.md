# Nvidia, AI Stocks Slump Again, Dragging US Market Lower

The U.S. stock market fell again on Monday, Nov. 16, 2026, as Nvidia and other artificial-intelligence-linked shares extended a recent slide, according to [AP News](https://apnews.com/article/stocks-markets-ai-nvidia-oil-us-35786b8f539ec95f16ae0500f71cfcc7). The S&P 500 dropped 1.2% and was on course for a third straight loss, while the tech-heavy Nasdaq composite led the decline with a 1.7% fall and the Dow Jones Industrial Average was down 395 points, or 0.8%, as of midday Eastern time.

Nvidia, the most influential name in the AI trade, sank 2.3% and was once again the heaviest weight on Wall Street.

## A Leadership Shock at the Center of the AI Boom

The decline is not simply a routine pullback. It comes after Jensen Huang, Nvidia's co-founder and longtime chief executive, resigned on Nov. 12 amid a scandal involving an alleged spy, AP reported.

"Now, following Jensen Huang's resignation as Nvidia's CEO last week, both the chip company and the broad industry are facing a more uncertain future without the leather-jacketed man who became one of AI's biggest celebrities," AP wrote.

The company moved quickly to steady itself. Nvidia's board named Chief Financial Officer Colette Kress as interim CEO while she retains her finance role, and appointed Harvey C. Jones as the first-ever chair of the board, according to the company's [official announcement](https://nvidianews.nvidia.com/news/nvidia-announces-ceo-transition). Board chair Harvey Jones said the board has "full confidence in Colette and the leadership team" and expects operations, customer commitments and the product roadmap "to continue without interruption."

Kress, who joined Nvidia in 2013 after roughly 25 years in finance roles at Cisco and Microsoft, framed the transition as continuity. "NVIDIA's strength has always come from its people," she said, adding that she is committed to "building on the extraordinary foundation Jensen created over the last 33 years."

## The Sell-Off Is Broader Than One Company

The pain spread well beyond Nvidia. AP reported that Broadcom fell 3.1% and was on track for a second straight loss, while Advanced Micro Devices dropped 4.6%. CoreWeave, which rents out cloud access to Nvidia chips, tumbled 5.2%, and GE Vernova, which helps power AI data centers, fell 3.2%.

Those declines suggest investors are repricing the entire AI complex, not just one company. "The market was already worried about this risk before what happened at Nvidia," Zhiwei Zhang, president and chief economist at Pinpoint Asset Management, said in a commentary. "Now the risk has become even more serious."

## Two Fears Compounding

Monday's moves reflect the convergence of two overlapping anxieties.

The first is company-specific. Huang's departure removed the figure most closely associated with Nvidia's rise, leaving an interim leader and no named permanent successor at a moment when the company faces intense scrutiny over export controls and its dealings involving China.

The second is structural. Critics have long argued that AI stock prices climbed too far, too fast. That worry was on full display earlier this month, when a brutal sell-off sent the S&P 500 down 4.4% in a single session and pushed the Nasdaq into correction territory, [AP reported](https://apnews.com/article/stocks-markets-ai-midterm-oil-trump-7ee9c212805aa5e70cf34d25aab7a36e) at the time.

Politics has added fuel. Sweeping Democratic victories in this month's midterm elections raised worries about possible restrictions on further construction of AI data centers — a concern that matters because a data-center slowdown would mean fewer chip purchases and less electricity demand. AI has been driving much of U.S. economic growth, so any pullback in that spending ripples outward.

## A Strong Earnings Season, Overshadowed

What makes the AI-driven swings especially striking is that the broader corporate backdrop has been healthy. Companies in the S&P 500 are on track to deliver earnings per share for the summer nearly 32% higher than a year earlier, according to FactSet — the third straight quarter of growth above 25%. Corporate profits and interest rates are the two big levers that set stock prices over the long term, and on the earnings side the picture remains solid.

Interest rates, however, are a headwind. The yield on the 10-year Treasury eased to 5.14% from 5.15% late Friday but remains well above a low of 4.80% reached earlier this month. Higher yields make borrowing more expensive for everyone and have already hurt the housing industry through elevated mortgage rates. Oil offered a modest counterweight: Brent crude fell 2.6% to $93.35 a barrel, easing some upward pressure on inflation, though prices have been volatile amid uncertainty over the war with Iran.

Abroad, the picture was mixed. South Korea's Kospi fell 3.6% and Japan's Nikkei 225 dropped 2.4% — two of the world's sharpest losses — on tech weakness and a report that Japan's economy contracted in the latest quarter.

## What to Watch Next

The next major test arrives Tuesday, when Nvidia reports its latest quarterly results after the U.S. market closes, with interim CEO Kress set to address Wall Street. Investors have grown accustomed to Nvidia beating estimates and guiding well above expectations, so the bar remains high even as confidence in the company's leadership has been shaken.

Beyond the numbers, several open questions loom: whether the board's search for a permanent CEO restores confidence, whether export-control scrutiny raised in Washington escalates into formal action, and whether Democratic policymakers move to slow data-center construction. Each of those threads will help determine whether this is a passing bout of turbulence for the AI trade — or the beginning of a deeper reassessment of what the world's hottest stocks are really worth.

For now, the message from Wall Street is clear: the AI boom's most important company is facing its most uncertain moment in years, and the market is paying attention.
