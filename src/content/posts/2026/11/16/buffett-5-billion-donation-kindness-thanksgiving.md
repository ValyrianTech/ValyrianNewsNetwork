# Buffett Gives $5.5 Billion, Praises Kindness

Warren Buffett on Monday said he is donating about $5.5 billion in Berkshire Hathaway shares to four foundations, using a particularly short annual Thanksgiving letter to tell shareholders that kindness "compounds, and at a better rate" than any financial investment he and the late Charlie Munger ever made. The gift, [CBS News reported](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/warren-buffett-donation-berkshire-hathaway-kindness/), is roughly four times as large as his donation a year earlier.

The 96-year-old investor converted 6,150 Class A Berkshire shares into 9,225,000 Class B shares, then distributed 3,690,000 of those to The Susan Thompson Buffett Foundation and 1,845,000 shares each to The Sherwood Foundation, The Howard G. Buffett Foundation and the NoVo Foundation, [according to CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/16/warren-buffett-donates-5point5-billion-thanksgiving.html). The latter three are run by his children — Susie, Howard and Peter Buffett — while the Susan Thompson Buffett Foundation honors his late first wife.

## A Thanksgiving Tradition, Scaled Up

The gift extends a multi-decade philanthropic plan. Buffett has committed to giving away the vast majority of his fortune, most famously through a 2006 pledge to gradually donate his Berkshire shares. In recent years the annual Thanksgiving-week donations have become the most visible expression of that promise, with Buffett increasingly handing giving decisions to his children as he accelerates the timeline.

After this latest round, Buffett still owns 182,140 Class A shares — worth roughly $162 billion. He reiterated in the letter that all of his Berkshire stock will eventually go to the four foundations, with a stated goal of increasing the size of the grants each year.

Buffett wrote that the gifts signal his confidence not only in his children but also in Berkshire's leadership. "My children have given thoughtfully to worthy causes for decades, and I trust 100% both their abilities and their fidelity," he said. He added: "You heard plenty from me in September about my confidence in Berkshire. That confidence is as strong as ever."

## A Message Built on Kindness

The letter's centerpiece was not the money but the counsel. Buffett returned to a theme he introduced a year earlier, when he wrote that kindness is "costless but also priceless." This time he sharpened the point with an investor's logic.

"Last Thanksgiving I wrote that kindness is costless but also priceless," he wrote in the [shareholder letter](https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/news/nov1626.pdf). "A year later, I would only add that it compounds, and at a better rate than anything Charlie and I ever bought."

He closed with a call to action: "This Thanksgiving, thank one of those selfless people in your own life. Better yet, be a benefactor for someone else."

The tone reflected Buffett's long habit of crediting mentors. He noted that he and Munger "received beyond measurement costless benefits from a few teachers such as Ben Graham, Joe Rosenfield and Tom Murphy," adding that "because of this we not only prospered mightily but were able to pass along many of the benefits we received."

Berkshire CEO Greg Abel, who took over from Buffett at the end of 2025, echoed the sentiment. "Warren continues to share his wisdom with us all during this holiday season," Abel said. "His example reflects the kindness he encourages in all of us."

## A Legacy Shaped in Transition

The donation lands during the first full year of Berkshire's leadership transition away from Buffett. He stepped down as CEO at the end of 2025 and relinquished the chairman's role in September 2026, when his son Howard succeeded him; Abel runs the company day to day. The Omaha-based conglomerate, which owns GEICO and holds major stakes including Apple, is valued at close to $1.3 trillion.

Markets greeted the news mildly. Berkshire shares rose about 0.4%, up $2.09 to $595.32, in early trading, CBS News reported. The stock has climbed almost 20% in 2026 and has been viewed as a safe haven as investors weigh concerns about the artificial-intelligence buildout, corporate drama and geopolitical risk — outperforming the S&P 500 for the year by roughly 7.5 percentage points, per CNBC.

That steady performance, coming as Buffett's formal roles recede, underscores a central aim of his recent communications: reassuring shareholders that the institution he built is in capable hands. By tying the gift to both his children's philanthropic judgment and Abel's stewardship, Buffett used a charitable announcement to deliver a message about continuity.

## What to Watch

The immediate question is timing. With more than $160 billion in Berkshire stock still to distribute and a stated intention to increase annual grants, the pace of future donations will draw close scrutiny — both from the nonprofit world and from investors watching the company's share structure. Business Insider has reported that Buffett intends for his children to distribute his entire fortune by 2034, a timeline that would require sustained, large-scale giving in the years ahead.

For now, the letter stands as a characteristic Buffett document: brief on specifics, long on values, and framed around a philosophy he has spent a lifetime arguing pays dividends. Whether measured in dollars or in the goodwill he urged this holiday, the message was plain — that the returns on kindness, in his view, remain the best investment of all.
