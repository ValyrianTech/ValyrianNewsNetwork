# Childminder Held After Six-Month-Old Baby Dies

A 33-year-old registered childminder from Sijsele, a village in the West Flanders municipality of Damme near Bruges, has been taken into custody after a six-month-old baby in her care died, according to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/16/onthaalmoeder-sijsele-damme-cel-baby-zes-6-maanden-overleden/). The woman is being held on suspicion of "violent acts resulting in death" (*gewelddaden met de dood tot gevolg*), a serious charge under Belgian criminal law.

## How the case unfolded

The events date to the beginning of November but only became public on Monday. On Friday 6 November, the baby boy lost consciousness while at the childminder's home. The childminder called the emergency services, which took the infant to Ghent University Hospital (UZ Gent).

It was doctors at the hospital who set the criminal investigation in motion. "There, doctors alerted the police because they found the circumstances in which the baby was admitted suspicious," a spokesperson for the public prosecutor's office, De Prest, told VRT NWS. The baby died in hospital the following day, Saturday 7 November.

## Detention and appeal

The childminder has already appeared before the *raadkamer*, the pre-trial chamber that rules on detention. That chamber decided to keep her in custody under electronic monitoring. The public prosecutor's office, however, appealed against that decision.

Pending the ruling of the *Kamer van Inbeschuldigingstelling* — the indictment chamber that hears such appeals — the woman remains in custody. The charge carries the highest stakes in the case, and Belgian courts weigh pre-trial detention more heavily in cases involving a death.

## Investigation continues

The public prosecutor's office has stressed that key questions remain unanswered. "The precise circumstances of the events are not yet clear at this moment and are being further investigated," the prosecutor's office said.

Among the outstanding questions are the findings of forensic examinations into the cause of death, the specific acts alleged to have caused it, and what evidence underpins the charge. Prosecutors have not named the childminder or the victim, and no further official information about prior complaints or oversight inspections has been released.

## A spotlight on home-based childcare

The case draws attention to the standing of *onthaalmoeders* — registered childminders who look after children in their own homes, distinct from crèche-based daycare centres. In Flanders they must be officially registered and fall under the oversight of Opgroeien, the region's agency for child and family services.

High-profile court cases in recent years have periodically renewed scrutiny of safety and supervision in this form of childcare, fuelling debate about inspection standards and the conditions under which home-based carers operate. The details specific to this case, including whether any warning signs were missed, remain unknown.

## What to watch

Attention now turns to two decisions. The first is the indictment chamber's ruling on whether the childminder should remain in pre-trial detention or be released under electronic monitoring. The second is the outcome of the investigation itself, which will determine what charge, if any, is ultimately pursued.

For the childminder's family, the child's family and the local community in Sijsele, the immediate focus is on a death that prosecutors have so far described only in outline. As more details emerge from forensic reports and witness accounts, the full picture of what happened on the morning of 6 November is expected to take shape — and with it, the legal reckoning.
