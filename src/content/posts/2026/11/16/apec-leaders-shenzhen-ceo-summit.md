# 12 APEC Leaders to Attend Shenzhen CEO Summit as Openness Tops Agenda

Twelve leaders from APEC economies will attend the APEC CEO Summit in Shenzhen this week, as China prepares to host the 33rd APEC Economic Leaders' Meeting on November 18-19 under the theme "Building an Asia-Pacific Community, Promoting Common Prosperity." The confirmation, announced at a November 15 press conference in Shenzhen, places China's message of open regional cooperation at the center of the Asia-Pacific's flagship economic gathering.

Li Qingshuang, Vice Chairman of the China Council for the Promotion of International Trade (CCPIT), said the CEO Summit would run November 17-18 at the Qianhai International Convention Center. According to [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/world/20261115/5667a85ff3214962a5bbc18ba3b9fe13/c.html), President Xi Jinping and the leaders of Australia, Canada, Chile, Hong Kong (China), Indonesia, South Korea, Malaysia, Mexico, Russia, Thailand, and Vietnam will attend and deliver speeches, remarks, or take part in thematic discussions.

## A Gathering of Business and Government

The summit is drawing significant corporate firepower alongside its political headliners. [China Daily](https://cn.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202611/15/WS6af95256e4b0e14252fcb22a.html) reported that 115 Fortune Global 500 companies will send representatives, with the United States accounting for the largest foreign contingent at nearly 20 percent of overseas participants. Leaders of several international organizations and business associations from economies including the US, Russia, and Japan are also expected to attend.

Companies from 13 non-regional economies, such as the United Kingdom, France, Germany, and Switzerland, are also taking part — an "Asia-Pacific Plus" configuration that organizers say reflects the summit's reach beyond the region itself.

## Openness as the Framing Theme

Chinese state media have built the summit's messaging around a single idea: that openness is the lifeblood of Asia-Pacific cooperation. In a [study notes feature](https://www.news.cn/politics/leaders/20261115/945dc91910694ea8ad0e11442f5a63bc/c.html) published by Xinhua, the outlet traced President Xi's past APEC remarks, recalling his 2021 statement that "openness is the lifeline of Asia-Pacific cooperation" and his call to uphold open regionalism and build an open Asia-Pacific economy.

The piece reached further back, citing Xi's 2013 remarks at the APEC CEO Summit — "The Pacific Ocean is vast because it has no natural barriers; we should not set artificial barriers for it" — and his Bali formulation that for member economies, "openness brings common progress, closure brings self-isolation."

Those principles have been linked to concrete policy. Xinhua noted China's continued shortening of the negative list for foreign investment, expanded unilateral opening, high-quality implementation of the Regional Comprehensive Economic Partnership (RCEP), and Belt and Road cooperation that deepens both physical infrastructure links and the "soft connectivity" of rules and standards.

Anchoring the scale of what is at stake, Xinhua observed that APEC member economies account for nearly 40 percent of the global population, more than 60 percent of global economic output, and close to half of total global trade.

## Why Shenzhen, and Why Now

The choice of Shenzhen is itself part of the message. The city — long nicknamed Pengcheng, or "Roc City" — sits at the heart of the Greater Bay Area, one of the world's densest clusters of manufacturing, finance, research, and logistics. Chinese officials have pointed to the city's humanoid robotics and artificial-intelligence sectors as evidence of the technological ambition the summit is meant to showcase.

The timing reinforces the link between domestic strategy and regional outreach: the summit coincides with the opening year of China's 15th Five-Year Plan, a framing Beijing uses to connect its own development priorities with the wider Asia-Pacific agenda.

## Analysis: Competing Narratives, Shared Stakes

The summit unfolds against a backdrop of rising protectionism, fragmented trade arrangements, and intensifying technology competition — pressures that give the "openness" theme both urgency and tension. As Xinhua's notes recall, Xi warned in Lima in 2024 against unilateralism and protectionism, urging an "open and interconnected" Asia-Pacific cooperation architecture.

Two narratives are converging on Shenzhen. Beijing's official framing emphasizes shared prosperity, open regionalism, and China's role as host and consensus-builder. International observers, meanwhile, are watching for substance on trade facilitation, supply-chain resilience, digital economy rules, and — for the first time formally on the CEO Summit agenda — artificial intelligence governance. Analysts have floated the possibility of US-China cooperation on standards for AI deployment in third countries, though such outcomes remain speculative.

For the region's businesses, the most concrete signal may come from the 2026 APEC CEO Summit Shenzhen Initiative — the first time in the summit's 30-year history that a results document will be issued in the form of an initiative. Its contents will indicate whether the rhetoric of openness translates into commitments.

## What to Watch

Three questions will define how the week is judged. First, whether the Shenzhen Initiative delivers measurable commitments rather than declarations. Second, whether the high-level attendance translates into bilateral momentum on trade and technology, given the presence of leaders from across the region and major economies. Third, whether APEC's long-standing ambition of a Free Trade Area of the Asia-Pacific advances beyond rhetorical support.

For China, the summit is an opportunity to demonstrate that its host role can produce outcomes valuable to the whole region. For the Asia-Pacific, it is a test of whether dialogue — in the words of one foreign commentary — can still deliver tangible benefits despite deep political differences.

With leaders arriving in Shenzhen this week, the answers will begin to emerge within days.
