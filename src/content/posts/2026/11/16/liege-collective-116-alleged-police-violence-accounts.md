# Liège Collective Logs 116 Alleged Police Violence Accounts

A spontaneous citizen collective in Liège has compiled 116 testimonies of alleged police violence and illegal practices during the student and youth protests that shook the city between 1 and 24 October, [RTBF reported](https://www.rtbf.be/article/manifestations-a-liege-un-collectif-citoyen-recense-116-recits-de-violences-policieres-presumees-11815078). The findings, presented at a press conference on 12 November at the Centre Polyculturel Résistances in the Saint-Léonard district, raise pointed questions about police conduct, the treatment of minors, and the chilling effect on the right to protest.

Of the 116 testimonies, 47 involve cases of physical violence and 32 people were injured. The collective stressed that none of the accounts rests solely on social media: all derive from direct testimony, with roughly 40 coming from the victims themselves and the remainder from direct witnesses or parents.

## Context and Background

The October protests were part of a broader wave of student and youth mobilisation across Wallonia and Brussels against the education policies of Minister Valérie Glatigny, rising public transport (TEC) subscription prices, and demands for better study conditions and an accessible school system. On 7 October, mobilisation in Liège degenerated, prompting the city to issue a BE-Alert message and briefly close Liège-Guillemins station, while Mayor Willy Demeyer banned gatherings of more than three people across the city from the following morning. Protests nonetheless continued, including a [demonstration on 24 October](https://www.rtbf.be/article/liege-plusieurs-centaines-de-manifestants-dans-les-rues-pour-denoncer-la-precarisation-de-la-societe-et-les-violences-policieres-11805496) in which hundreds marched to denounce "the precarisation of society and police violence."

## Documentation and Methodology

The collective, formed "in the urgency" by associative-sector workers, caregivers, people running shifts outside police stations, youth educators, and parents, structured its collection in three strands: street medics who witnessed injuries, volunteers who gathered accounts at the exits of the Natalis and Herstal police stations, and education and youth workers who recorded young people's testimonies. Assisted by lawyers, sociologists, doctors and psychologists, the group then catalogued the data and removed duplicates using the methodology of the Observatoire des violences policières de Belgique.

RTBF said it was able to consult and authenticate video rushes underpinning some testimonies, sourced from the left-wing activist outlet "Média Rouge!". The footage reportedly shows takedowns with a knee on the neck, kicks and baton blows on people already on the ground, and particularly forceful arrests of sometimes very young people. The catalogue distinguishes "physical violence" from violence "with injuries still visible after more than 12 hours," citing tightened zip-ties that leave marks, punches after arrest, armlocks, and pressure on the neck and throat of people already immobilised.

"We have also noted psychological violence, with insults based on gender or sexual orientation. We also recorded racist insults used systematically, and arrests based on appearance," said Marc Monaco, pedagogical attaché at Jeunesse Organisée et Combative and spokesperson for the collective.

## Minors at the Centre of Concern

Fifty-three of the 116 testimonies concern minors — a dimension the collective describes as particularly legally sensitive. "Many illegal acts were committed with regard to minors," Monaco said. A 14-year-old girl reported having to undress to her underwear while a door was open; minors reported being kept zip-tied inside cells. One arrested person said that "in a collective cell designed for 14 people, there were people aged 13 to 24," with access to toilets outside the cell systematically refused. Video footage reportedly shows about a dozen young people kneeling in the rain, hands bound behind their backs, for nearly 40 minutes.

The collective cited Article 33 quater of the law on the police function, which requires that a minor deprived of liberty have the person responsible for their supervision notified as a matter of course, and Article 37(c) of the UN Convention on the Rights of the Child, which states that any child deprived of liberty shall be separated from adults.

The Liège police zone said in a statement the preceding weekend that more than 80% of parents of minors deprived of liberty were notified, attributing exceptions to minors who refused to share a responsible person's number, whose phone was discharged, or whose parents were unreachable. It confirmed that nine complaints had been filed and were being verified, and invited those concerned to lodge complaints with its internal investigations service, the Comité P, or the AIG. RTBF has previously reported that some minors spent nearly 12 hours at the police station.

## Institutional Response and Analysis

For its part, the police zone said 52 officers — 42 from Liège and 10 from outside the zone — were injured during the October protests, including one still on total incapacity to work, a reminder that the events involved violence on multiple sides and that any inquiry must weigh both protest-related disorder and alleged police misconduct.

Robin Bronlet, a lawyer and member of the Liège section of the Ligue des droits humains, said the evidence was concerning: "There have been at least several medical certificates and several testimonies, numerous and concordant enough, reporting worrying things, particularly regarding the police's use of force, the legality of arrests, and restraint methods such as handcuffs or tear gas." To date, nine complaints have been filed and 12 medical findings recorded, to the collective's knowledge.

Mayor Demeyer said he wants to leave "no zone of shadow" — "if I am brought different elements, I will examine them" — while cautioning that videos on social media "are not reliable, because they are taken out of context." He said he is ready to meet the collective to begin discussions. The tension between his scepticism and RTBF's authentication of specific footage is one oversight bodies would need to resolve.

## What's Next

The collective is calling for an independent investigation to shed full light on the events. Beyond the immediate allegations, the case carries broader stakes. Both the collective and the Ligue des droits humains warn of a "chilling effect": many young people who experienced violence believe that filing a complaint is futile, or could even backfire, which could cool the population's willingness to exercise the right to protest. "The authorities must see the problems and try to resolve them so that peace and serenity return," Monaco said. Whether the nine complaints yield a formal inquiry — and how the disputed question of parental notification is settled — will determine whether that call is answered.
