# Faulty Gas Cylinder Blamed for Maredsous Market Blast

A badly connected gas cylinder caused the explosion and fire that tore through a chalet at the Maredsous Christmas market on Sunday, the Namur public prosecutor's office confirmed on Monday, in an accident that has left three people injured, one of them fighting for their life and has renewed questions about safety at seasonal public events. According to [RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/explosion-au-marche-de-noel-de-maredsous-un-mauvais-branchement-de-bonbonne-de-gaz-a-l-origine-de-l-accident-11816175), an expert appointed by the prosecutor concluded that the faulty connection triggered a gas leak that accumulated before igniting.

## What Happened

The blast struck the Christmas market in Maredsous, a village in the municipality of Anhée in Namur province, late on Sunday morning, at around 11:30. The market had opened its doors only on Friday evening, billed as the first Christmas market of Wallonia to launch this season, making the accident all the more jarring for a region still weeks away from the heart of the festive period.

The subsequent fire required the intervention of firefighters from the Dinaphi rescue zone, covering Dinant and Philippeville. Two chalets were completely destroyed by the flames and two neighbouring chalets were damaged. The market was evacuated and closed on Sunday, both as a safety precaution and out of respect for the victims.

The prosecutor's office said on Monday that it had no further information to release at this stage, and its statement focused squarely on the technical cause rather than any suggestion of foul play.

## A Toll That Shifted

The casualty figures evolved significantly over the course of the day, a discrepancy that authorities have not fully explained.

In the immediate aftermath on Sunday, the mayor of Anhée, Pierre Rondiat, who was present on site, reported five people injured. Two were said to be in absolute emergency — one airlifted to a hospital in Liège and another, to be confirmed, to the military hospital of Neder-Over-Heembeek. A third person had been burned, while the other two were more lightly hurt. "The situation is now totally under control," the mayor said at the time.

By Monday morning, however, the prosecutor's office had revised the official toll to three injured, with one person whose life remains in danger. The shift likely reflects a reclassification by investigators or a distinction between people treated at the scene and those formally registered as injured, though no formal clarification has been issued. At the time of writing, the prosecutor's Monday statement stands as the most authoritative account.

Rondiat had also confirmed that the market would stay closed on Sunday "in consultation with the organiser and out of respect for the victims."

## Why It Matters

Maredsous is best known for its Benedictine abbey and its cheese and craft brewery, which have made its Christmas market a notable seasonal draw in the region. Like most European Christmas markets, it is built around wooden chalet-style stalls — structures in which vendors routinely rely on gas cylinders and propane for heating, cooking and food preparation.

That dependence is precisely what makes accidents of this kind a recurring fire-safety concern. A single faulty connection, as investigators now describe, can allow gas to pool unseen before a spark or flame sets it off. The fact that the Maredsous blast occurred on the market's opening weekend underscores how quickly such incidents can upend an event that local organisers spend months preparing.

The case also highlights the regulatory grey zone in which many temporary seasonal installations operate. Vendors at pop-up markets are not always subject to the same inspection regimes as permanent commercial kitchens, and the responsibility for checking gas fittings often falls to individual stallholders. While Belgian authorities have not suggested any wider systemic failure in this instance, the confirmed cause is likely to sharpen calls for clearer, standardised checks of gas equipment before markets open each winter.

## The Bigger Picture

The Maredsous accident fits into a broader pattern of gas-related incidents at public gatherings across Europe, where the combination of cold-weather heating, temporary structures and high footfall creates obvious risk. Each such event tends to prompt a round of local safety reminders, even as the underlying standards vary widely from one municipality to the next.

For now, the immediate priority is the condition of the person still in danger, and the question of how the remaining weeks of the festive season will be policed at similar events across Wallonia.

## What to Watch

The coming days are likely to bring further updates on the injured, particularly the individual reported to be in critical condition, and on whether the market reopens at all this season. Organisers and local authorities will also face scrutiny over the gas equipment used at Maredsous and at comparable markets due to open in the weeks ahead. The prosecutor's office has signalled that it will release no further detail for the moment — leaving the precise sequence of events, and the lessons to be drawn, still to be filled in.
