# Vooruit's Grit and Press Leaks Test De Wever's Patience

Four of Belgium's five coalition parties were ready on Sunday to keep negotiating a federal budget framework. One — the Flemish socialist party Vooruit — refused, leaving Prime Minister Bart De Wever to report to King Filip on Monday without a deal, according to an [analysis by VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/16/begrotingsonderhandelingen-federale-regering-waar-liggen-de-bree/). The standoff, compounded by a fresh round of press leaks, has pushed the premier to his emotional limit and plunged the "Arizona" coalition into its deepest crisis since taking office.

## A Deal Almost Within Reach

De Wever's government is trying to close a budget gap of roughly €10 billion. By the weekend, about €9 billion had been found, with the final — and hardest — billion still on the table. On Sunday afternoon, the premier convened the "kernkabinet," the inner cabinet of deputy prime ministers, to bridge the last stretch.

The framework on offer was not a final deal but a basis for detailed negotiations in the days that followed. Health-care savings, once targeted at more than €3.6 billion, had already been scaled back to roughly €2.5 billion — a significant concession. For four parties, that was enough to keep talking. For Vooruit, it was not.

## Where the Talks Broke Down

The sharpest sticking point was the index — the mechanism that ties wages and benefits to inflation. Savings on indexation account for roughly €1 billion, and Vooruit wanted that effort halved. "Touching the purchasing power of pensioners again, via the index, is very sensitive for us," Deputy Prime Minister Frank Vandenbroucke said.

Vooruit also kept pressing its signature "strongest shoulders" agenda, demanding a "small and robust effort" from the holders of very large fortunes. Among its symbolic demands is the scrapping or limiting of a social-security contribution discount that top athletes and professional footballers have enjoyed since the 1970s. That puts the socialists at odds with MR chairman Georges-Louis Bouchez, who also chairs the football club Francs Borains. As [VRT NWS detailed earlier this month](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/03/moeten-topvoetballers-sociale-voordelen-opgeven-voor-begroting/), that perk lets players contribute roughly 5.22 percent of gross salary to social security, against 13.07 percent for ordinary workers.

Other disputed items included proposals to index civil servants' wages only once a year and to scrap compensation for official holidays that fall on a weekend. Union leader Ann Vermorgen opposed the latter, noting Belgium already sits below the EU average of 12 public holidays.

## The Leak That Broke the Mood

Negotiations had already been grinding when the atmosphere collapsed after 7 p.m. Sunday. During a live broadcast, VTM journalist Hannelore Simoens cited what she described as literal messages from Vooruit, including criticism of the savings measures on the table and a jab at the premier — "Meanwhile, Bart De Wever keeps staring stoically at his paper." Vooruit categorically denies that the information came from its ranks.

Regardless of the source, the leak infuriated De Wever, who had spent weeks complaining about persistent disclosures. One insider described his reaction as "fairly emotional." "Not that there was shouting or yelling at the table, but you felt the frustrations of the past weeks were extremely high."

De Wever then explicitly asked who was willing to continue negotiating on the framework before them. Four parties said yes. Vooruit said no. The meeting was over.

## Blame Shifts to the Socialists

Les Engagés, often a Vooruit ally at the table, was blunt. "Vooruit's refusal harms that process," the party said, adding that "the constant leaking of measures still under discussion undermines trust and enormously complicates the negotiations." MR's David Clarinval was equally direct: "Today it is not the MR causing problems. Four parties agreed, only Vooruit didn't."

Vooruit chairman Conner Rousseau acknowledged the political cost on Instagram. "The other parties will point at us. I'll take that," he wrote, adding that he was "enormously disappointed that we could not convince our colleagues to make this agreement fairer." He closed with a defiant "We fight on."

Even MR — which until recently pursued the same confrontational strategy as Vooruit — can now pin the blame squarely on the socialists. Some parties suspect Vooruit may have overplayed its hand.

## The 'Great Emptiness'

The question now is what options remain for De Wever. VRT NWS political analyst Ivan De Vadder is skeptical of grand gestures. "You can bring all the party chairs around the table again, but in my view you only make it harder there," he said, warning that other topics, such as ethical issues, could get dragged in. "It is the great emptiness at the moment."

Les Engagés deputy PM Maxime Prévot told RTBF he does not expect the premier to pull the plug, arguing that elections would not solve the budget problem and could inflate the gap from €10 billion to as much as €15 billion. As of midday Monday, De Wever was delivering a status report to the King — not a resignation — with no agreement in hand.

For Belgium, the impasse is a test of whether a five-party coalition can hold together when the hardest billion remains unfound. For now, an extension of talks appears the likeliest path. But the trust around the table, already thin, has taken another blow.
