# Southern China Rain to Intensify as North Braces for Cold Turn

Rainfall across southern China will increase and intensify over the next two days, with localized rainstorms expected, while northern parts of the country prepare for a sharp turning point in temperatures as a new cold air mass moves in, according to a forecast carried by [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/politics/20261103/fbf20378243a4b6e98080458e887f93b/c.html) and sourced from the China Weather Network.

The report, citing the Central Meteorological Observatory, warns that moderate-to-heavy rain will hit parts of the Jiangnan and Jianghuai regions on November 3 and 4, with torrential downpours in isolated areas and a damp, chilly feel settling in across the south.

## Southern Rain to Peak

For November 3, moderate-to-heavy rain is forecast across eastern Chongqing, northeastern Guizhou, most of Hubei, northern Hunan, northern Jiangxi, south-central Anhui, southern Henan, southern Jiangsu, northwestern Zhejiang, southern Yunnan, southern Guangxi, and Hainan Island. Localized rainstorms are expected in northwestern Hunan and northwestern Hainan Island.

The system intensifies on November 4, spreading moderate-to-heavy rain to southern Heilongjiang, eastern Liaoning, eastern Shandong, southeastern Hubei, northern and southern Hunan, central-northern Jiangxi, southern Anhui, southern Jiangsu, Shanghai, central-northern Zhejiang, southern Yunnan, northern Guangxi, southern Guangdong, and western Hainan Island. A localized rainstorm is forecast in northeastern Guangxi.

Under the overcast and rainy conditions, temperatures will stay low, leaving residents with a pronounced damp-cold feel. Cities including Changsha, Hefei, and Wuhan are expected to see highs of only 17-19°C.

## Northeast Faces Snow and Freezing Rain

While much of the north stays relatively mild, the northeast will see frequent snow and rain. Light-to-moderate snow or sleet is forecast for November 3 in northern Xinjiang, including the Altay mountains and Tianshan range, northeastern Inner Mongolia, northern Heilongjiang, southeastern Jilin, central Qinghai, western Gansu, the northern Sichuan plateau, and eastern Tibet, with heavy snow possible in the Xinjiang Altay mountains.

By November 4, moderate-to-heavy snow or sleet is expected in eastern Inner Mongolia and central-northern Heilongjiang, with a blizzard possible in northern Heilongjiang and freezing rain in central-eastern Heilongjiang. Nationwide snow and rain are expected to weaken by November 5.

## Temperature Turning Point for the North

The more dramatic shift comes on temperatures. North China and the Huang-Huai region will still see highs above 20°C on November 3, but a new cold air mass arriving November 4-7 will drive significant cooling, with the largest drops in northern North China and the northeast — generally 6-10°C, and locally up to about 16°C.

After the cooling, northeast highs will fall below freezing, with Harbin expected to reach only -4°C on November 6. North China highs will drop to single digits, with Shijiazhuang potentially reaching just 9°C on November 7. Both would mark new lows for the second half of the year.

Southern cooling arrives later. Around November 8, highs in the middle and lower Yangtze region will fall to about 15°C, with Hefei and Nanjing possibly below 15°C — also new lows for the second half of 2026 — compounded by rain for a distinctly raw, cold feel.

## Safety Advisories

The meteorological department advised the public to watch for traffic safety during strong rain in the Jiangnan and Jianghuai regions, and to guard against the effects of heavy snowfall on transportation and agriculture in the northeast.

The forecast underscores the seasonal handover now gripping China: as the autumn rain belt lingers over the south, the first strong cold surges push south from the north, nudging the country toward its winter baseline. Residents across both regions are advised to adjust their clothing to the rapidly changing conditions.
