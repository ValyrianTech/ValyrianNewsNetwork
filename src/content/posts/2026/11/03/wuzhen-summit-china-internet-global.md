# Wuzhen Summit Frames China's Internet Story as a Global Invitation

The 2026 World Internet Conference (WIC) Wuzhen Summit opened on November 2 in Wuzhen, Zhejiang Province, and runs through November 5, according to [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/tech/20261103/489ff649f8684c80a61ffe9c3bc03d59/c.html), the state news agency. Its accompanying state-media feature frames China's internet journey as a shift from Chinese companies "going global" to offering a platform for the world to "move toward the future."

The 13th edition of the summit carries the theme "Open Source, Openness, Joint Contribution and Shared Benefits — Jointly Build a Community with a Shared Future in Cyberspace," positioning this year's gathering around artificial intelligence (AI) and open-source governance.

## A Story That Begins With a Single Line

Xinhua anchors the narrative in a now-iconic moment. On the night of September 14, 1987, scientists in Beijing drafted an email that traveled for six days before reaching West Germany on September 20 — widely regarded as China's first email sent abroad. Its body contained a single line: "Cross the Great Wall, go toward the world." The agency cast that phrase as a prophecy of China's internet connecting with the world and heading toward the future.

In 1994, China achieved full-function connection to the global internet through a 64K international dedicated line, a milestone the agency describes as the start of the country's leapfrog development in networking.

From there, the feature traces a technology progression that runs from web portals and chat software to mobile internet and "Internet Plus," and from 2G to 5G — with 6G now "moving from concept to reality." Demonstrations at the summit's "Light of Internet" expo, as documented in the feature's photographs, included remote operation of construction machinery over 6G, brain-controlled robots, and an intelligent bionic hand capable of playing the piano.

## AI Moves to the Foreground

Xinhua frames AI as the current wave bringing "intelligent new life" into daily reality — from AI pets and robotic cooking arms to automated parcel sorting and simulated robots on display at the expo.

The retrospective also returns to a set of interviews the agency conducted when the summit first settled in Wuzhen in 2014, when residents were asked what they most hoped the internet would bring. Their wishes — remote home control, 3D family images, online grocery selection — have since become everyday reality. Their 2026 wishes look further ahead: a fully intelligent senior mobility vehicle, a safe AI butler, and a network that preserves human warmth.

## A Platform, Pushed by the State

Beyond the showcase, the summit carries a governance message. Since 2014, President Xi Jinping has engaged with the Wuzhen Summit through congratulatory messages, letters, and video greetings, advocating "jointly building a community with a shared future in cyberspace," according to the feature and official framing surrounding it.

The official commentary framing the event stresses multilateralism and resistance to "unilateralism, hegemony and protectionism," with AI governance lines arguing that "AI development should not be a solo of one country but a symphony of global cooperation" and opposing the idea of AI becoming "a game of rich countries and rich people."

## Analysis: A Curated Story, Not a Breaking One

The Wuzhen Summit is China's flagship state-sponsored internet governance event, positioned as a parallel platform to Western-dominated internet governance fora. The 2026 edition reflects a shift of emphasis toward AI open-source governance.

The Xinhua feature is best understood as a retrospective, image-driven showcase tied to the event rather than independent reporting. All of the available source material is from Chinese state or state-affiliated outlets, and the coverage is uniformly aligned with official framing; no independent or international perspectives were available in the searches conducted.

That context matters for readers. The narrative of "letting the world move toward the future" is a statement of intent and soft-power messaging as much as a description of outcomes.

## What to Watch

As the summit runs through November 5, the key questions are how far the open-source AI message translates into concrete international participation, and whether the vision of a "truly multilateral" global AI governance regime gains traction beyond the platforms China already leads. For now, the story that began with a single line crossing the Great Wall has become a platform China wants the world to step onto.
