# Inside the Making of the CPC's Party-Discipline Resolution

China's state news agency has published a detailed account of how the Communist Party's latest landmark document on party self-governance was drafted and adopted, offering a rare, officially sanctioned window into the leadership's internal decision-making. The retrospective, published by [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/politics/20261102/89cdeba96f1c49cf86cb547fee7584b8/c.html) on November 2, traces the "Resolution" on persistently advancing full and rigorous party self-governance from its conception in January 2026 to its unanimous adoption at the Fifth Plenum of the 20th Central Committee on October 29.

The document — formally a "Decision" (决定), though rendered as "Resolution" in official English — runs to more than 17,000 Chinese characters across three major blocks and eight parts, and sets out more than 200 specific measures. Its full text was released on November 1, alongside General Secretary Xi Jinping's explanation of the drafting process.

## Context: A Milestone in Party Building

The document is the latest in a line of plenum-level party-building texts. Xinhua situates it alongside the 18th Central Committee's Sixth Plenum in 2016, which adopted norms on intra-party political life and supervision, and the 19th Central Committee's Sixth Plenum in 2021, which issued a historic resolution on the party's century of achievements. The Fifth Plenum of the 20th Central Committee now turns directly to self-governance — timing the move to the party's 105th anniversary and the opening of the 15th Five-Year Plan (2026–2030), a period Beijing frames as a decisive stage in building a "modern socialist country."

In his explanation of the document, Xi framed the choice of topic as a response to a foundational question of long-term rule. According to [Xinhua's release of his remarks](https://www.news.cn/20261101/2cab4ff1d3304a0583242414e3cac6c3/c.html), he invoked the 1945 "cave-dwelling dialogue" between Mao Zedong and Huang Yanpei on escaping the historical cycle of dynastic rise and fall. Mao's answer, Xi said, was to "let the people supervise the government"; the party's answer since the 18th Party Congress has been "self-revolution." "Studying the issue of persistently advancing full and rigorous party self-governance is very necessary and timely," Xi said.

## The Drafting Process

The retrospective details an elaborate, top-down drafting machinery. A drafting group was formed in January 2026, chaired by Xi himself, with Politburo Standing Committee members Wang Huning, Cai Qi and Li Xi as deputy heads. It held its first plenary meeting at the Zhongnanhai leadership compound on February 6.

The numbers Xinhua cites are designed to convey both rigor and consultation. The group commissioned 23 key research topics from 16 units, producing 28 reports. Across the consultation process, regions and departments submitted 1,173 comments — 1,076 after removing duplicates — of which 208 suggestions were incorporated through 81 edits, an adoption rate of 17.7 percent. During the plenum itself, 100 further comments yielded 13 changes. Xi chaired three Standing Committee meetings and two Politburo meetings to review the draft, and convened a symposium in Shanghai on May 6 to gather input from provincial leaders.

Xi's explanation stresses that the process was not merely technocratic. He described repeatedly emphasizing a "tone of strictness" in party governance "precisely to send the signal that it will only get stricter going forward." He also pointed to a structural concern: the growing share of party members and cadres born after the reform and opening-up era, a demographic shift that, in the leadership's assessment, places new demands on education and management.

## What the Document Contains

The Decision rests on seven principles for advancing party self-governance: serving the party's mission and tasks; taking political building as the guide; comprehensive strictness; problem orientation; upholding fundamentals while innovating; leaders setting the example; and governing the party according to rules. It identifies "three more arduous" conditions and "four challenges" — spanning governing capacity, leadership quality, cadre standards and resistance to corruption.

Substantively, the text targets several sensitive areas. It strengthens rules on how top leaders ("first-in-command" cadres) exercise power, tightens supervision over the selection and use of officials, and expands party organization into emerging sectors such as platform companies and tech firms. It also sharpens the anti-corruption framework, explicitly naming "new-type and hidden corruption" — including expected-return arrangements, proxy shareholding, so-called "white gloves" and government-business "revolving doors" — and pledges to counter corruption conducted through the internet, virtual currency, cross-border funds and blockchain.

## Analysis: Signals and Subtext

Retrospectives of this kind are an established genre in CPC political communication, and they serve several functions at once. On one level, the birth story is a factual chronicle; on another, it is a carefully staged narrative that magnifies the top leader's personal authorship and frames the document as the ideological centerpiece of the coming political cycle. The heavy emphasis on consultation statistics — the 1,173 comments, the 17.7 percent adoption rate — is meant to demonstrate that the process was collective and deliberative, even as the ultimate authority clearly rests with Xi.

External analysts read the document as part of a broader consolidation. Hong Yaonan, director of Tamkang University's Cross-Strait Relations Research Center, told Singapore's Lianhe Zaobao that the Decision forms a complete "from ideology to punishment" closed loop — defining loyalty, regulating the use of power, governing cadre selection and behavior, and then locking it all in through institutions and supervision, "with no completion deadline." That open-ended framing suggests the campaign is designed as a permanent feature of governance rather than a time-bound drive.

The timing is also telling. By tying party discipline to the delivery of the 15th Five-Year Plan and the 2035 modernization goals, Beijing is presenting clean, disciplined governance as a precondition for economic and strategic success — a link that could shape how cadres are evaluated and promoted in the years ahead.

## What's Next

The full text of the Decision, [released by Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/20261101/bd5876c937fa4cc7ab5104bf33d7bc73/c.html), now becomes the reference document for party organizations nationwide, with study and implementation expected to dominate the political calendar. The open question is enforcement: whether the document's expansive list of measures translates into concrete changes in how power is exercised at the local level, and how the leadership's stated concern about the party's changing demographics shapes recruitment and discipline in the run-up to the 21st Party Congress.
