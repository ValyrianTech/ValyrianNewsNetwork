# CIIE 'Four-Leaf Clover' Venue Ready for Record 9th Edition

Shanghai's National Exhibition and Convention Center — known as the "four-leaf clover" for its distinctive clover-shaped roofline — is fully prepared to host the 9th China International Import Expo (CIIE), which opens on November 5 and runs through November 10, according to [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/world/20261103/2308b4f782854da2896d59bab0c4aa1e/c.html). Booth construction was scheduled to finish on November 3, capping months of preparation for China's flagship import-promotion event.

This year's edition is the largest in the expo's history, with 162 countries, regions and international organizations taking part and more than 4,100 overseas companies exhibiting. Total exhibition space exceeds 440,000 square meters for the first time, while the enterprise and business exhibition area alone passes 370,000 square meters.

## A Record Field of Exhibitors

Organizers say the scale marks a new high across several measures. More than 300 Fortune Global 500 and industry-leading companies are attending — a first — alongside over 2,000 companies exhibiting for the first time. The expo will host more than 480 representative premieres, including global, Asian and China debuts, of which over 200 are global firsts.

Developed economies dominate the exhibitor roster. American companies hold the largest signed exhibit area at more than 50,000 square meters, ranking first for an eighth consecutive edition and up 1,500 square meters from last year. Firms from Germany, the United Kingdom and France follow, with French, British and New Zealand exhibitors already exceeding last year's signed areas.

On the Country Exhibition side, 57 countries and three international organizations have confirmed participation, with Moldova, Romania and the United Nations Development Programme joining for the first time. Canada serves as the guest country of honor — its second time in that role.

## Nine Editions of Continuity

Nearly 200 enterprises have attended all nine editions as so-called full-attendance exhibitors, a group organizers highlight as a signal of durable foreign confidence in the Chinese market. The CIIE was first held in 2018; the current edition is the ninth.

The venue's operating team said preparations had entered their final sprint. Zheng Yusheng, general manager of the NECC (Shanghai) Operations Center, said the venue's Wi-Fi had been fully optimized to support 100,000 people online simultaneously.

Exhibitors described the expo as a gateway rather than a one-off showcase. Liu Yanxia, chief representative of Austria's WFL, said the machine-tool maker had previously attended only specialized trade fairs and was entering CIIE to use its "spillover effect" to reach Chinese industrial customers more broadly — timing the debut to the 55th anniversary of China-Austria diplomatic relations.

## Why It Matters

Beyond the exhibition floor, the CIIE is a barometer of China's trade posture. The event runs alongside the Hongqiao International Economic Forum, themed "Stabilizing Cooperation, Building an Open World Economy," which this year debuts a dual flagship report — the World Openness Report 2026 with the latest World Openness Index, plus a first-ever Report on China's Export Capacity Development.

The expo also carries political weight. Washington's large exhibitor presence comes amid ongoing US-China trade frictions, positioning the CIIE as a channel for commercial engagement despite tariff tensions. Beijing has meanwhile touted the full removal of foreign-investment restrictions in manufacturing and national treatment for foreign firms as evidence of its opening-up commitments.

Macro trade figures underscore the backdrop: China's General Administration of Customs reported total goods trade of 39.94 trillion yuan in the first three quarters of 2026, up 18.8% year on year, with imports rising 22.3% to 16.74 trillion yuan — growth above 20% for seven straight months since March.

## What's Next

The 9th CIIE opens November 5, with organizers betting that record scale and a lineup of global product debuts will reinforce Shanghai's role as a showcase for foreign brands seeking a foothold in China. Whether the enthusiasm translates into signed deals — and sustained investment commitments — will be the test beyond the closing ceremony on November 10.
