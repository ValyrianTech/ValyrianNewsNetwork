# Former Red Devil Toni Brogno dies at 53

Toni Brogno, a former Belgian international and one of the most recognisable cult strikers in the Belgian top flight of the late 1990s, has died at the age of 53. He passed away on the morning of Tuesday 3 November 2026 from the effects of liver cancer, a diagnosis he had received at the end of 2024. His daughter Olivia announced the news in a statement on Facebook, as [Het Laatste Nieuws](https://www.hln.be/belgisch-voetbal/voormalig-rode-duivel-toni-brogno-53-overleden-aan-de-gevolgen-van-leverkanker~a199e69d/) reported.

"With deep sadness we inform you that the best father and husband in the world has passed away," she wrote. Brogno died in palliative care, surrounded by his family, a few days after a decision — taken in consultation with his oncologist — to move him to palliative sedation.

## A career built on goals

Brogno made his professional debut in 1994 at Sporting Charleroi, but it was at KVC Westerlo that he became a club legend. A 1.70-metre "pocket striker" with a nose for goals, he gradually developed into one of the most feared forwards on Belgian pitches. His breakout came in the 1999-2000 season, when he scored 30 goals in 29 matches to share the Belgian top-scorer title with Norwegian Ole Martin Årst, as [Sporza](https://sporza.be/nl/2026/11/03/voormalig-cultspits-toni-brogno-op-53-jarige-leeftijd-overleden-aan-de-gevolgen-van-leverkanker~1793707940155/) detailed.

That season also delivered Westerlo's highest-ever league finish, sixth place with 56 points. Brogno's 71 goals in 162 appearances for the Kempen club cemented his place in its history.

The top-scorer campaign earned him a move to French Ligue 1 side CS Sedan, though he never fully established himself there. Two seasons later he returned to Westerlo before spells at Charleroi, OH Leuven and Olympic Charleroi. He retired in 2009 at the age of 36.

Brogno also won seven caps for the Red Devils under coach Robert Waseige between November 1998 and April 2000, having been selected eleven times. He never scored for the national team and was notably left out of the Euro 2000 squad co-hosted by Belgium and the Netherlands. His older brother Dante was also a professional in the Belgian first division.

## A fight that drew widespread support

Brogno's illness became publicly known in October 2025, when Dante revealed that his brother was battling liver cancer. In recent months he suffered countless complications but kept fighting, HLN noted. "Until his body, already very weakened, gave out."

The football community had rallied around him during his illness. In April 2025, Brogno took the ceremonial kickoff before a Westerlo versus Charleroi match at Het Kuipje — the two clubs with which he is most associated — and received a warm, emotional reception from both sets of fans, as [Voetbalkrant](https://www.voetbalkrant.com/nieuws/2025-04-11/-westerlo-en-charleroi-steunen-ex-spits-in-strijd-tegen-kanker-voormalige-topschutter-krijgt-warm-onthaal-in-kempen) reported at the time.

"Toni was not just a player — he was our top scorer, a crowd favourite and a true fighter on the pitch," Westerlo wrote on its official channels. "Today he shows even more strength in his fight against liver cancer. And together we show that he is not alone."

## Tributes and a broader grief

His death drew tributes from across Belgian football, with regional outlet [RTV](https://www.rtv.be/regionale-sport/westerlo-legende-toni-brogno-overleden-aan-gevolgen-van-leverkanker) remembering him as "a true cult striker" and "a real club legend" in Westerlo. De Morgen, quoting his daughter's statement, recalled the family's loss in [its own tribute](https://www.demorgen.be/sport/voormalig-rode-duivel-toni-brogno-53-overleden-de-beste-vader-en-echtgenoot-ter-wereld~b1e58693/).

Brogno's passing came on a difficult day for the sport in Belgium, falling close to the death of Tenerife player Jesús de Miguel (30) following an on-field collision — both drawing major coverage across the same day.

## What's next

Official statements from Westerlo, Charleroi and the Belgian football federation were still expected in the hours after the announcement, along with details of funeral arrangements. For now, the footballing community is left to remember a striker whose 30-goal season remains one of the defining individual campaigns of the modern Belgian top flight — and a man remembered, above all, as "the best father and husband in the world."
