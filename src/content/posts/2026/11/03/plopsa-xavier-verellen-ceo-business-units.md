# Plopsa Names Xavier Verellen CEO as Group Splits Into Units

Belgian theme-park group Plopsa has appointed Chief Commercial Officer Xavier Verellen (60) as its new CEO, effective immediately, succeeding Carl Lenaerts at the helm of the Studio 100 division. The handover, announced on Tuesday, comes with a structural overhaul: the group will reorganise into separate business units, each led by its own managing director.

The change marks the third leadership transition at Plopsa in roughly three-and-a-half years and sets a new strategic direction for one of Europe's larger independent theme-park operators.

## A Change at the Top

Lenaerts had led Plopsa since October 2023, when he was brought in to restore calm following the ousting of his predecessor, Steve Van den Kerkhof. According to [HLN](https://www.hln.be/binnenland/plopsa-wisselt-van-ceo-commercieel-directeur-xavier-verellen-volgt-carl-lenaerts-op~af619ea5/), which first reported the appointment, Lenaerts previously headed the Belgian bookstore chain Standaard Boekhandel and had earlier worked in banking, at football club Club Brugge and for years in a management role at cinema chain Kinepolis.

Verellen joined Plopsa in 2023 — May of that year, according to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/03/plopsa-pretparken-krijgen-nieuwe-topman-xavier-verellen-volgt-c/) — to lead the group's commercial activities. He now takes over from one day to the next.

"I look back on the past three years at Plopsa with great pride and satisfaction," Lenaerts said in a press release. "Together with a strong and driven team we took beautiful steps and further built out the group. I am particularly grateful to everyone within Plopsa for the trust and cooperation."

## A Decentralisation Push

The board of directors has tasked Verellen with anchoring Plopsa more strongly in the local markets of its parks, bringing the company closer to visitors. To reinforce that ambition, the group will be split into business units, each headed by its own managing director — a notable shift for an organisation that had been run centrally. The reorganisation was detailed by regional broadcaster [Focus-WTV](https://focus-wtv.be/nieuws/xavier-verellen-volgt-carl-lenaerts-op-als-ceo-van-plopsa).

"Plopsa has unique parks, strong brands and above all exceptionally driven employees," Verellen said. "We have covered a strong trajectory in recent years, and the ambitions for the future remain exceptionally large. In the coming period we want to further strengthen our organisation and prepare it for the future, so that from a strong base we can realise our next growth phase."

Studio 100 founders Hans Bourlon and Gert Verhulst, the key figures behind Plopsa's parent company, publicly endorsed the appointment. "With Xavier we choose someone who already knows Plopsa and its people well while at the same time looking at the organisation with fresh eyes," they said. "We have every confidence that he, together with management and all employees, will further build out Plopsa."

## Scale and Context

Plopsa is the European theme-park arm of Studio 100, the Flemish entertainment group behind children's brands such as Kabouter Plop. Its flagship, Plopsaland De Panne on the Belgian coast, has long been marketed as the "Disneyland on the North Sea." According to VRT NWS, the group operates seven parks in Belgium, the Netherlands and Germany and holds minority stakes in three Polish parks.

The leadership churn has been unusually frequent. In February 2023, De Tijd published testimony from dozens of (former) employees about humiliation and a difficult working culture under then-CEO Steve Van den Kerkhof. As [VRT NWS reported](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2023/03/20/studio-100-schuift-ceo-van-pretparkengroep-plopsa-steve-van-den/), Van den Kerkhof was forced out in March 2023 after an independent investigation into the allegations. Lenaerts was then recruited from outside to stabilise the company.

## Analysis: An Internal Choice to Close a Turbulent Chapter

This handover is markedly different from the one that preceded it. Where Lenaerts arrived as an external figure brought in to restore order, Verellen represents continuity — an internal promotion of a known quantity to owners and staff alike. The absence of any controversy surrounding the departure is itself significant given the group's recent history.

The business-unit model is the substantive news. By devolving decision-making to managing directors tied to individual parks and local markets, Plopsa is signalling that future growth will be driven by proximity to visitors rather than by a single central strategy. For a group whose parks span three countries and multiple brands, that could sharpen local marketing and investment decisions — though it also raises questions about coordination and brand consistency.

The move also raises a familiar test: whether Plopsa has now found durable leadership. Three CEOs in roughly three-and-a-half years is a lot for any company, and the success of Verellen's tenure will depend on whether the new structure delivers both stability and growth.

## What to Watch

The next signals will be who is appointed to lead each business unit and how much autonomy those managing directors are granted. Investors and park visitors alike will also be watching whether the renewed local-market focus translates into tangible investment at individual parks across Belgium, the Netherlands and Germany — and what it means for the group's Polish minority holdings.
