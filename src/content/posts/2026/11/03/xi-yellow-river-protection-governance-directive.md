# Xi Issues Directive on Yellow River Protection, Governance

Chinese President Xi Jinping has issued "important instructions" on further advancing the protection and governance of the Yellow River basin, emphasizing the river's ecological and strategic significance, the state-run Xinhua News Agency [reported](https://www.news.cn/politics/leaders/20261102/7cd5e0cfc8ed46d9a01a865a5c4900f1/c.html) on November 2. The directive signals continued high-level priority on conserving China's second-longest river and was conveyed at a dedicated conference in Zhengzhou, Henan Province, the same day.

Xi framed the directive around the 80th anniversary of Party-led river management, stating that the Party's stewardship had "fundamentally changed the historical situation of 'two breaches in three years'" and created the "miracle of 'peaceful flow year after year'" — language that underscores the political significance Beijing attaches to flood control and water security.

## Context: A National Strategy

The Yellow River, regarded as the "mother river" of the Chinese nation, has historically been prone to severe flooding and channel shifts. The phrase "two breaches in three years" reflects a long period of disastrous floods that shaped the region's history.

The basin's ecological protection and high-quality development was elevated to a national strategy by Xi in 2019, paralleling the Yangtze River Economic Belt initiative. The Yellow River Protection Law, which took effect on April 1, 2023, established a legal framework for basin-wide ecological management and designated responsibilities across provinces.

## Key Developments

At the Comprehensive Promotion of Yellow River Protection and Governance Work Conference in Zhengzhou, Xi outlined the strategic requirements for the new era. He emphasized the approach of "focusing on protection, with governance being essential" and the water-management principles of "water-saving priority, spatial balance, systematic governance, and two-handed efforts."

The directive calls for continuously improving the "big protection, big coordination" pattern — an integrated approach requiring coordinated governance across the basin's provinces — to promote ecological protection and high-quality development, build a national ecological security barrier, safeguard the river's long-term stability, and inherit and promote Yellow River culture.

"Strive to make this mother river more and more beautiful, forever benefiting the Chinese nation and future generations," Xi said in the instructions.

Vice Premier Liu Guozhong, a member of the CPC Central Committee Political Bureau, attended the conference and delivered a speech. He said Xi's instructions are "highly political, ideologically rich, and instructive," providing "important guidance" for continuing ecological protection and high-quality development in the basin.

Liu called for strengthening the sense of responsibility and mission, jointly carrying out big protection and coordinating big governance, ensuring the river's long-term stability, strengthening conservation and intensive use of water resources, enhancing the water network's overall allocation capacity, and advancing water ecological environmental protection.

The conference reported on the situation of 80 years of Party-led river management. Responsible officials from Qinghai, Gansu, Henan, and Shandong provinces, along with the National Development and Reform Commission and the Ministry of Water Resources, spoke at the event. Officials from relevant central and state organs and provinces also attended.

## Analysis and Implications

The instructions follow a classic Chinese governance communication pattern: a top-down directive from the leadership setting the ideological and strategic tone, paired with an inter-provincial implementation conference to operationalize it. Liu's role — as Vice Premier overseeing agriculture, water, and ecological affairs — confirms the administrative channel through which the directive will be executed.

The mention of specific provinces — Qinghai, Gansu, Henan, and Shandong — maps to the upper, middle, and lower reaches of the Yellow River, reinforcing the emphasis on basin-wide coordination. The recurring "mother river" and "future generations" language ties environmental governance to national identity and long-term legitimacy.

The timing of the directive — published the day before the People's Daily front-page placement — indicates high propaganda priority and suggests the Yellow River's protection and governance will remain a central theme in China's environmental policy narrative.

## What's Next

The directive sets the strategic direction for strengthening water conservation, improving the water network's allocation capacity, and enhancing ecological protection across the Yellow River basin. Implementation will likely be driven through the relevant provinces and central agencies in the coming months. Observers will watch for the specific policy measures and funding commitments that follow the conference, as well as how the basin's provinces coordinate on cross-regional water management and ecological targets.
