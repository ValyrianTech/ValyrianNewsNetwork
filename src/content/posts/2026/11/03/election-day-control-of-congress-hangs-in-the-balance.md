# Election Day: Control of Congress Hangs in the Balance

Americans went to the polls on Tuesday, November 3, 2026, for midterm elections that will decide control of both chambers of Congress and deliver what amounts to the first nationwide verdict on President Trump's second term. More than 59 million people had already cast ballots before Election Day — nearly half the total ballots cast in the last midterm — with [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/11/03/nx-s1-5992757/election-day-live-updates-key-races) reporting that Democrats hold a double-digit advantage heading into the day based on early-voting data.

## A Referendum on the White House

The stakes are unusually high for a midterm cycle. As NPR's senior political editor Domenico Montanaro put it, "It's finally here. Election Day. Today, voting officially ends. No more talk of polls. Now, it's about results." The central question is whether a Democratic wave flips the House — and possibly the Senate — or whether Republicans hold on thanks to a surge of in-person turnout on Election Day.

## The House: Democrats Within Reach

Democrats are favored to win the House, needing to flip only three seats for control, according to NPR's analysis of the 2026 House map. The political landscape has shifted considerably in Democrats' favor, even in the past month. Republicans tried to insulate themselves with rare, mid-decade redistricting, but it is unlikely to be enough to hold the chamber.

## The Senate: A Genuine Toss-Up

The Senate is far less predictable. Democrats need to flip four seats to gain control — an outcome that was once unthinkable at the start of the cycle. Strong Democratic recruits and a difficult national environment for Republicans have made it a real possibility, per NPR's Senate map analysis. Democrats are favored to flip one seat, North Carolina, but beyond that the races are toss-ups or tilt slightly toward Republicans. Apart from Maine, the five Republican-held toss-ups are in states Trump won by double digits in 2024.

Republicans' top target is Michigan, a race between Republican former U.S. Rep. Mike Rogers and Democrat Abdul El-Sayed. If Republicans flip it, Democrats' path to a Senate majority expands from four seats to five. In Texas, Democrat James Talarico leads narrowly in polling averages against Republican Ken Paxton — though no Democrat has won statewide there since 1994.

## What Early Voting Suggests — and What It Doesn't

Early-vote data compiled by the University of Florida's [Election Lab](https://election.lab.ufl.edu/early-vote/2026-early-voting/) shows a Democratic advantage, but it may be offset by heavy Republican in-person turnout. There are so many red states and districts in play, and the GOP votes overwhelmingly in person on Election Day. High Republican turnout could limit Democrats to a single Senate pickup; low turnout among Republican voters could produce a wipeout of up to six or seven seats — and Democratic control of the Senate.

## The Bellwethers to Watch

Early returns will offer clues. Results begin arriving at 6 p.m. ET from Kentucky and Indiana, with more states following hourly. Analysts will watch Indiana's 5th District, Kentucky's 6th District, and Florida's 22nd and 27th Districts as early reads on GOP turnout and the Latino vote.

## Patience Will Be Required

While everyone wants an answer on election night, in many places the results will not be known. "It could take days. It could take weeks, but ballots are being counted and results are coming soon," NPR reported. Officials are urging patience as states count votes in what are expected to be many close races.

## What to Watch

Thirty-six governor's races are also on the ballot this year, and Trump has spent the closing days campaigning heavily in red states to turn out his base. The balance of power in Washington — and the trajectory of the president's second term — depends on how many Republicans show up Tuesday. In the Trump era, Republicans win when they capture at least 46 percent of independents, and Democrats likely need a larger share of the electorate than the 31 percent who identified as Democrats in 2024.

For now, the polls are closed to prediction and open to counting. The verdict is being cast, one ballot at a time.
