# China Doctors Warn Flu Season May Arrive Early, With Multiple Strains Circulating

Doctors in China are warning that the country's 2026 influenza season could begin earlier than usual and that several strains are likely to circulate at the same time, urging the public to step up protective measures. The warning was issued by Li Tongzeng, chief physician of the general infectious diseases department at Beijing You'an Hospital, affiliated with Capital Medical University, in a health briefing published by [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/politics/20261103/070595862d0b42748e6740deea552d1d/c.html) on November 3.

"This year's flu season may arrive early, characterized by multiple strains circulating together; the public is reminded to strengthen protection," Li said. The notice comes as China's typically busy flu period — running from November to March — gets under way, and as surveillance data point to a shifting mix of influenza A and B viruses.

## A Dual-Strain Picture

National surveillance offers the clearest view of what is circulating. According to a weekly influenza report from the China CDC cited by [China.com](https://news.china.com/social/1007/20261024/49815189.html), covering October 12–18, influenza A accounted for 66.1 percent of positive flu samples nationwide, while influenza B made up 33.9 percent. Within influenza A, the A(H3N2) subtype dominated at 80.6 percent, with A(H1N1)pdm09 at 19.4 percent; all influenza B samples belonged to the B/Victoria lineage.

That "dual-strain" pattern is not unique to this year, Li told [People's Daily Health](https://news.qq.com/rain/a/20260911A049P700). Most flu seasons see influenza A and B viruses alternate or co-circulate, he said. What matters more, he added, is the shift in the dominant virus: H1N1 predominated in the 2025 season, whereas H3N2 now leads — a change that lowers pre-existing population immunity to the circulating strain and can ease transmission.

Earlier CDC data tracked the same trajectory. The agency's surveillance network, which pools reports from national sentinel hospitals, showed a "south rising, north stable" pattern in early autumn: in the week ending September 6, China recorded 2,493 positive flu cases — 2,427 in southern provinces and just 66 in the north — with H3N2 already the leading subtype, according to the People's Daily Health report. The [China CDC's weekly monitoring bulletins](https://www.chinacdc.cn/jksj/jksj04_14249/202610/t20261008_1842096.html) have since charted a steady rise in positivity rates in both regions.

## What Co-Circulation Means for Patients

A common concern is whether catching two strains at once makes illness worse. Li said simultaneous infection with two strains does produce more severe symptoms than a single infection, but the probability of co-infection is low. Far more likely, he explained, is sequential infection — recovering from one strain and then contracting another — which exacts a heavy toll on the body and warrants heightened prevention.

Experts also point to environmental factors. Temperature swings and early cold waves in northern China reduce respiratory resistance, helping to explain why the season may start sooner than in a typical year. The pattern is not confined to China: Japan entered its flu season in October, more than a month earlier than usual, and Britain, Germany and Italy have also reported earlier-than-normal increases — suggesting a broader early-season trend across the Northern Hemisphere.

## A Vaccine Policy Shift

The most consequential change for the public this year is in the vaccine itself. China moved from a quadrivalent to a trivalent formulation for the 2026–2027 season, a shift set out in the China CDC's vaccination technical guideline released September 2, as reported by China.com. The trivalent vaccine covers H1N1, H3N2 and B/Victoria — precisely the strains now circulating.

The reason for dropping the fourth component is epidemiological. The B/Yamagata lineage has not been detected globally in recent years, and the World Health Organization has removed it from the recommended vaccine composition. In practice, the change means this year's shots match the viruses in circulation, rather than representing any downgrade in protection.

Li stressed that vaccination remains the core preventive measure. Because the body needs two to four weeks to build protective antibodies after inoculation, and protection lasts six to eight months, the optimal window is September through October — or at least before the peak. Priority groups include children under five, adults 65 and older, pregnant women, people with obesity and those with chronic conditions, all of whom face elevated risk of severe illness.

## Beyond the Shot

Public health guidance extends past immunization. Recommended measures include regular ventilation, mask-wearing in crowded enclosed spaces, hand hygiene, and adequate sleep and nutrition. People who fall ill are advised to rest at home rather than attend school, work or gatherings while infectious.

## What to Watch

With the season potentially starting early and two lineages in play, the coming weeks will show whether China's flu curve climbs faster than in a typical year. The key indicators to track are the weekly positivity rates published by the China CDC and whether H3N2 continues to dominate as colder weather settles over the north. For the public, the message from clinicians is straightforward: get vaccinated now, and do not let down guard on basic precautions.
