# Supreme Court Rejects DOJ California Ballot Bid

The U.S. Supreme Court late Monday denied an emergency request from the Department of Justice to force California to set aside and stop processing the ballots of roughly 20,000 people the DOJ claims are noncitizens on the state's voter rolls. The ruling, issued without opinion or any noted dissent, landed hours after the DOJ filed its appeal — and one day before polls opened for the 2026 midterm elections.

The application was presented to Justice Elena Kagan and referred by her to the full Court. According to [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/11/02/nx-s1-6023802/supreme-court-california-voting-ballots-trump), the order rejected the appeal just hours after it was filed. The absence of an explanation and dissents is customary for emergency applications, but it marked a decisive defeat for a federal push that had already failed at every judicial level below.

## A Standoff Building for Weeks

The dispute escalated after the DOJ delivered a USB drive to Los Angeles and Sacramento election officials in mid-October containing the roughly 20,000 names, giving officials only 48 hours to review the list and threatening to sue if the ballots were not sequestered pending citizenship verification, [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/nov/02/supreme-court-trump-california-ballots) reported. Election officials refused, setting off a rapid series of courtroom battles.

The government's claims faced immediate pushback. Los Angeles County Clerk/Recorder Dean Logan said the DOJ had not provided the evidence or methodology behind its assertions. "The Department of Justice has not provided the underlying evidence, source, or methodology supporting its assertions that individuals identified in its records are ineligible to vote," he said in a statement after receiving the list.

Courts consistently sided with California. On October 28, U.S. District Judge David Carter, a Clinton appointee, rejected the DOJ's ballot-segregation request. A divided panel of the Ninth Circuit Court of Appeals upheld that refusal. Writing for the majority, Judges Mark Bennett — a Trump appointee — and Gabriel Sanchez, a Biden appointee, warned that "there is a material risk that granting the United States its requested relief would result in disenfranchisement and could jeopardize the County's entire election." The panel's third judge, Trump appointee Patrick Bumatay, dissented.

Carter had framed the administration's conduct in stark terms. "Never before in American history has the federal government, on the eve of an election, sought to coerce a state and local government to abandon their own Constitutional elections procedures and bend to the will of the Executive Branch," he wrote, describing what he called "a broad, ongoing pattern of attempted election interference by federal officials."

## The Numbers Behind the Claim

The scale of the actual dispute appears far smaller than the DOJ's headline figure. County election officials found that many people on the DOJ list did not have active voter registrations and were never mailed ballots. Over the weekend, data scientists with the UCLA Voting Rights Project, granted access to the list through a court order in a separate lawsuit, found that 42% of the names did not appear on California's current voter roll — and that naturalized citizens were among those listed.

The broader federal campaign has drawn scrutiny as well. The DOJ issued grand jury subpoenas to California election workers and warned officials they could be criminally prosecuted for processing the ballots, a move experts say lacks legal basis. A federal judge in San Francisco issued a temporary restraining order late Friday barring the administration from threatening, intimidating or coercing election officials; the government has appealed. The administration has made a similar ballot-segregation demand in Washington state and filed criminal charges against 28 green-card holders in California over alleged fraudulent voting.

Experts note that ineligible noncitizen voting remains exceedingly rare. According to [The Associated Press](https://www.ap.org/news-highlights/elections/2026/administrations-focus-on-noncitizen-voting-has-led-to-160-arrests-experts-say-that-shows-its-rare/), a sweeping federal effort produced about 1,600 voter-fraud cases and 160 arrests nationally between January 2025 and September 2026 — a fraction of the country's more than 211 million active registered voters.

## Reactions and Implications

California's Democratic leaders welcomed the ruling. "After repeatedly losing in the lower courts, the highest court in the land firmly slapped down the Trump DoJ's baseless attack on California's electoral process — not even entertaining it," Governor Gavin Newsom said. Attorney General Rob Bonta added: "This latest decision should put an end to the administration's attempt to disrupt California's elections through emergency court orders. But our work is not over."

The Ninth Circuit's reasoning rested on established legal doctrine: the Purcell principle, which holds that courts should not alter election rules on the eve of an election, the Constitution's assignment of election administration to the states, and the National Voter Registration Act's 90-day "quiet period" barring systematic removal of ineligible voters before a federal election.

## What's Next

The Supreme Court's refusal to intervene removes the most immediate threat to California's ballot processing, but it does not resolve the underlying litigation. The DOJ's civil suit over the voter list — and its appeal of the restraining order shielding election officials — remain pending. The Washington state dispute could reach the high court as well.

For voters and election administrators, the ruling means ballots will be counted as usual through the midterms. For the broader fight over federal power in elections, it signals that the courts — including a Supreme Court with a conservative majority — have so far been unwilling to reshape election procedures in the final hours of a contested national vote.
