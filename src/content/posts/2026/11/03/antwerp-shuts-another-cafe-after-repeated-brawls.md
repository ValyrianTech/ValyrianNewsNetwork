# Antwerp Shuts Another Café After Repeated Brawls

Antwerp's acting mayor, Els van Doesburg (N-VA), has ordered the closure and sealing of a café in the Anneessensstraat, near Antwerp Central Station, for one month following a string of violent brawls and public-order violations, [Het Laatste Nieuws reported](https://www.hln.be/antwerpen/burgemeester-els-van-doesburg-n-va-sluit-zoveelste-cafe-in-antwerpen-na-verschillende-vechtpartijen~ac1f3093/). The venue will stay shut from 3 November to 2 December 2026, with a further mandatory closure over New Year's Eve.

The measure, confirmed in an [official City of Antwerp press release](https://pers.antwerpen.be/cafe-in-anneessensstraat-een-maand-gesloten-na-herhaaldelijke-vechtpartijen-en-overlast), is the latest in a sustained crackdown by the mayor on nightlife and hospitality venues linked to disorder in Belgium's largest city.

## A pattern of violence

According to the mayor's office, police have repeatedly intervened at the café for fights, threats, drunkenness and night-time noise. Violence spilled from the premises onto the street, endangering residents and passers-by.

The incidents escalated through the year. In June, a brawl left the café damaged. In September, police officers were attacked and reinforcements were needed to regain control. The owner herself asked officers to clear the venue because she could not manage her patrons.

Three days after a hearing on possible administrative measures, a serious new brawl broke out, with those involved throwing tables and chairs on the terrace. A parked car was damaged and an ambulance was called for one attendee.

Inspection reports painted a broader picture of mismanagement. At one check, 85 patrons were present where a maximum of 49 was permitted. Officials also recorded repeated noise violations—electronically amplified music was audible some 100 metres away—along with breaches of terrace regulations and smoking legislation.

## Conditions on reopening

The closure is not the only sanction. Should the café reopen, it will be subject to strict conditions for at least six months, per the city's release: it must close no later than 03:00, a recognised security guard must be present every opening day from 01:00 until closing, and a professionally calibrated sound limiter must be installed and will be checked by police.

The mayor has also imposed an additional mandatory closure from 18:00 on 31 December 2026 through 1 January 2027. "The expected crowds and increased alcohol use during the turn of the year increase the risk of new violent incidents," the mayor's office said. "With the additional closure the mayor wants to prevent violent confrontations recurring in and around the café."

Van Doesburg's stated rationale is to break a persistent cycle of nuisance, give the owner a chance to fundamentally correct her operation, and prevent new confrontations.

## A signature policy

The shutdown is emblematic of Van Doesburg's approach since she succeeded Bart De Wever, who moved to federal politics as prime minister. Public safety and nuisance enforcement have become defining priorities of her tenure, and she has made frequent use of mayoral orders to close businesses.

As [GVA noted](https://www.gva.be/regio/antwerpen/regio-antwerpen/antwerpen/antwerps-burgemeester-els-van-doesburg-gaat-malafide-handelszaken-definitief-sluiten-complexe-wetgeving-maakte-dat-zeer-moeilijk/160522269.html) in August, "almost no week goes by in Antwerp without the mayor closing a business due to persistent nuisance." Earlier closures have targeted a dance café at the Falconplein, venues on the Boomsesteenweg tied to drug dealing, and two hospitality businesses in Antwerp-Noord over drugs, illegal cigarettes and hygiene issues.

## What's next

Police will monitor compliance with the closure, and the owner faces a clear choice: meet the conditions on reopening or risk further action. The case also highlights a wider tension in Antwerp between a vibrant nightlife economy around Central Station and residents' demands for safety and quiet. How Van Doesburg balances those interests—and whether the closing spree deters disorder or simply displaces it—will shape the city's nightlife politics well into the year ahead.
