# PBOC Raises FX Deposit Reserve Ratio to 6% to Cool Yuan

The People's Bank of China will raise the foreign exchange deposit reserve requirement ratio for financial institutions by 2 percentage points, from 4% to 6%, effective November 15, 2026, the central bank announced on Tuesday. The move — its first hike of this macro-prudential tool in nearly five years — is widely read as an effort to temper the renminbi's sustained appreciation and signal a firm commitment to two-way exchange-rate stability.

## Why the PBOC Acted Now

The decision comes amid a pronounced strengthening of the yuan, which has risen for four consecutive months and reached its strongest level against the dollar since June 2022. On November 2, the onshore spot rate closed at 6.6458 per dollar, while the offshore yuan broke through the 6.65 level intraday, according to [Sina Finance](https://finance.sina.com.cn/roll/2026-11-03/doc-inixmnqe2329885.shtml). Year to date, the onshore yuan has appreciated roughly 4.91%, with the CFETS RMB index climbing to 103.77 at end-October — its highest since July 2022.

That strength has been driven largely by trade flows. Data from the State Administration of Foreign Exchange show that from January to September 2026, banks settled US$2,377.6 billion in foreign currency against sales of US$1,983.5 billion — a settlement surplus of about US$394.1 billion that has persistently released dollar supply into the market. As Wang Jinbin of Renmin University of China noted, strong exports have kept the FX settlement rate above the sales rate, continuously feeding yuan appreciation pressure.

## How the Mechanism Works

The FX deposit reserve requirement ratio governs how much of their foreign-currency deposits financial institutions must hold at the central bank. Raising it withdraws dollar liquidity from the banking system, reducing the supply of foreign currency and thereby curbing appreciation. According to [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/fortune/20261103/87be95ca6d1c44dc83f193b9a6a126c8/c.html), the PBOC framed the move as a measure "to strengthen the management of foreign exchange liquidity in financial institutions."

The scale is significant. With FX deposits at roughly US$1.2 trillion at end-September, the 2-point hike means financial institutions will park an additional US$24 billion with the PBOC from November 15, according to calculations reported by Sina Finance.

Wang Jingwen, a macro analyst at Minsheng Bank Research Institute, explained the transmission channel: raising the ratio "can withdraw FX liquidity from the banking system, restrain the expansion of FX loans, and thereby curb RMB appreciation by reducing the supply of foreign currency."

## Market Reaction

The announcement prompted an immediate pullback in the currency. The offshore yuan fell through both the 6.65 and 6.66 levels, touching an intraday low of 6.6665 — down nearly 200 pips from the prior close of 6.64724 — while the onshore rate also slipped below 6.66, according to [Eastmoney](https://finance.eastmoney.com/a/202611033919446783.html).

## A Signal, Not a Reversal

The PBOC has been careful to frame the move as smoothing rather than reversing the trend. In its October 8 "Policy Stance on the RMB Exchange Rate," the central bank affirmed it does not set a target level, does not intervene in long-term trends, and maintains a flexible, two-way floating regime — while reserving macro-prudential tools to correct what it calls "herd effects" and irrational expectations.

Governor Pan Gongsheng elaborated at the Financial Street Forum on October 19, stating that "under specific scenarios, the PBOC uses macro-prudential management tools, and even in extreme scenarios conducts direct FX intervention, to correct the market's 'herd effects' and the self-reinforcement of irrational expectations." He noted such measures conform to international rules and practice, while cautioning that with RMB FX daily turnover exceeding US$800 billion — about 80% of it offshore — central banks can hardly influence medium- and long-term trends on their own.

Guan Tao, chief economist at Huafu Securities, argued the current appreciation is fundamentally market-driven rather than the product of policy management, while warning that exchange-rate marketization easily produces "herd effects," requiring vigilance against overshooting that deviates from fundamentals.

## What to Watch

The measure takes effect on November 15, and markets will be watching whether it succeeds in cooling appreciation momentum or merely slows it. Two factors will shape the outcome: the persistence of China's export-driven settlement surplus, which remains the structural engine of yuan strength, and the degree to which appreciation expectations become self-fulfilling. The PBOC's move signals that it is willing to lean against rapid gains — but its own guidance suggests it aims to smooth the path of the currency, not to dictate its direction.
