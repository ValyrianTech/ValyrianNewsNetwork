# Schaeffler to Shut Herstal Plant, Four German Sites

German automotive and industrial supplier Schaeffler announced on Tuesday that it will cut 3,460 jobs across Europe — roughly three-quarters of them in Germany — and close five European sites, including its plant in Herstal, in Belgium's Liège province. The company, which employs some 110,000 people worldwide, linked the restructuring to what it called "the crisis in the automotive industry," citing heightened competition, weakening demand for combustion-engine parts and delays in the shift to electromobility, [RTBF reported](https://www.rtbf.be/article/l-equipementier-automobile-schaeffler-va-fermer-un-site-en-belgique-et-4-en-allemagne-11809638).

## A small plant, a heavy symbol

In absolute terms, the Belgian impact is modest. The Herstal site employs "approximately 15 employees," RTBF reported. But for Wallonia, the closure carries outsized symbolic weight — a loss of specialised industrial know-how in a region whose economy was built on heavy industry, not simply a handful of jobs.

"We are not just shutting down machines, we are destroying Walloon know-how," said Vincent Napolitano, permanent secretary of the Setca socialist union in Liège. He argued that the auto industry's difficulties have been known for years and should have prompted reflection on how to safeguard existing skills. "Giving lessons is fine; acting concretely is better," he added, directing his criticism at Walloon Economy Minister Pierre-Yves Jeholet, whom he urged to develop a genuine industrial vision.

## Germany bears the brunt

The bulk of the cuts falls on Germany, where Schaeffler plans to eliminate 2,660 posts and close four sites. According to [WELT](https://www.welt.de/wirtschaft/article6ae9899ab3f0fa60211d6f0f/autozulieferer-schaeffler-streicht-3460-stellen-und-schliesst-vier-standorte-in-deutschland.html), the affected plants include Dortmund (430 employees), Morbach (160), Berlin, where activities will cease at a site of about 270 staff, and Schwalbach, where the site will be given up and most of its roughly 385 employees shifted to Schaeffler's Frankfurt location, with about 190 positions affected by the reduction.

Further adjustments are planned at Schaeffler's toolmaking operations in Höchstadt and its special-machinery units in Erlangen and Schweinfurt, alongside capacity and structural changes in Herzogenaurach, Bühl, Höchstadt, Nürnberg and Regensburg. In total, 16 sites in Schaeffler's Europe region will be touched by the job reductions, with a dozen other European locations affected by the broader restructuring.

The programme carries a one-time cost of about €480 million but is expected to deliver annual savings of roughly €300 million by 2030, WELT reported. Roughly half the cuts fall in production, 30% in research and development and 20% in other functions.

## A string of programmes

Unions were sharply critical, framing the decision as the latest in a sequence of retrenchments rather than a strategic reset. "At Schaeffler, one cutting program now follows another, even before the consequences of the previous one have been dealt with," said Thomas Höhn, IG Metall's company representative for Schaeffler. "This is not a future concept for Germany as an industrial location."

Schaeffler's group works council chair, Uli Schoepplein, noted that German sites are especially hard hit despite the company's repeated pledges of commitment to Germany. "This commitment must show itself in concrete decisions," he said. "The program presented lacks precisely the creativity we need given the crisis." The company had already decided in 2024 and 2025 to cut several thousand jobs.

## The broader squeeze

The announcement reflects a structural transition bearing down on Europe's automotive supply chain. In its most recent quarter, Schaeffler reported revenue of €5.8 billion, slightly below the prior year, with operating profit down 7.2% to €245 million and the operating margin slipping 0.3 points to 4.2%. Its Powertrain & Chassis division — which bundles combustion-engine parts — saw profit fall 15%, while the loss-making e-mobility division narrowed its loss by 11.3% to €169 million amid rising European demand for electric vehicles.

The pattern is familiar across the sector: legacy combustion-component businesses are contracting faster than electric-vehicle operations can become profitable, squeezing suppliers that straddle both. For Wallonia, the Herstal decision is a reminder that the region's industrial base remains exposed to decisions taken far from Liège.

## What to watch

Attention now turns to how the Walloon regional government responds and whether Minister Jeholet will offer concrete support or an industrial plan for the Herstal workers. In Germany, consultations with IG Metall and the works council will determine whether redundancies are forced or voluntary and how quickly the site closures proceed. The larger question — how many more European suppliers will follow Schaeffler's path — is likely to define the continent's industrial agenda well into the decade.
