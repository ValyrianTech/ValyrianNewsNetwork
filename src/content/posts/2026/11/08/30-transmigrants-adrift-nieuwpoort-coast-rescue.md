# 30 Transmigrants Adrift Off Nieuwpoort Coast Rescued

A small boat carrying around 30 transmigrants was found drifting more than three kilometres off the coast of Nieuwpoort on Sunday morning, triggering a multi-agency rescue operation in the North Sea. The occupants themselves raised the alarm, calling emergency number 112 at 5:30 AM, according to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/08/bootje-transmigranten/).

Three rescue and police boats were dispatched to the scene, alongside an NH90 helicopter, after poor visibility at sea complicated the search. The boat was eventually located shortly before 8:00 AM.

## A Difficult Search at Sea

The incident began in near-darkness, with the exact location of the vessel unknown. An Berger, spokesperson for Belgium's federal police, which oversees the shipping police, described the immediate mobilization.

"It was clear that it was a 'small boat', but the exact location was unknown," Berger said. "All resources were immediately deployed: the NH90 helicopter flew out to find the boat and the rescue and police boats were also deployed."

Poor conditions on the water made the search challenging. "Due to the poor visibility at sea, it was not easy to find the boat, but shortly before 8 AM the NH90 was able to locate it more than 3 kilometres off the coast of Nieuwpoort," Berger added.

The vessel was carrying around 30 people, all men, according to the Agentschap Maritieme Dienstverlening, the agency that confirmed the incident.

## Occupants Refuse to Disembark

Despite the deployment of rescue services, the situation remained delicate through the morning. The boat was not taking on water, but it had lost its direction, and those on board refused to be taken off.

"It is not taking water, but it has clearly lost its direction," Berger said. "For now the people do not want to be taken off board."

The three rescue and police boats remained on site to monitor the situation, with authorities continuing to track developments closely as the morning progressed.

## Part of a Growing Pattern

The incident is the latest in a string of small-boat crossings along the Belgian coast, a phenomenon that has expanded rapidly over the past year. Historically concentrated on the French side of the border, the practice began shifting north during 2025 and 2026.

According to the Governor of West Flanders, the first successful crossing from Belgium took place on 16 January 2026, with a second on 9 February confirming the trend. By March, authorities had convened a coordination meeting and launched an action plan aimed at preventing organized crime from taking root and stopping tent camps—previously seen in France—from developing in the region.

The scale has escalated sharply. In April, five boats carrying 200 transmigrants were intercepted between Oostduinkerke and Middelkerke in a single night, prompting Governor Carl Decaluwé to activate the early alert of the provincial emergency plan as a precaution, as [VRT NWS reported](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/04/18/transmigranten-kust-provinciaal-noodplan-opgestart-overleg-gouve/).

"It is the first time that we have seen more than 200 transmigrants at sea," Decaluwé said at the time. "Most were also not wearing life jackets. That is a life-threatening situation."

Smugglers have increasingly adopted a tactic of launching multiple boats at once—a technique already used in France—making interception nearly impossible. Decaluwé compared it to the Huns "attacking en masse by the thousands."

## A Recurring Challenge

The Nieuwpoort incident echoes a similar event on 29 October, when a boat with around 30 transmigrants departed from Westende beach. Police arrived, but the boat had already reached the water; shipping police and the coast guard escorted it to the French border, where French authorities took over.

The European border agency Frontex maintains constant surveillance over the zone and alerts Belgian police to embarkations. Boats typically originate from Germany and are inflated on the spot, with smugglers relying on scout networks and favouring calm, windless conditions.

## What to Watch

As of Sunday morning, the immediate outcome for those aboard the Nieuwpoort boat remained unresolved—authorities continued to monitor the vessel rather than force a risky boarding. For Belgian coastal communities, the incident underscores a broader challenge: a growing, increasingly organized pattern of crossings that has stretched rescue services and prompted calls for deeper coordination between federal and local forces.

The coming months, with winter seas ahead, will test whether the action plan launched earlier this year can keep pace with a phenomenon that shows little sign of abating.
