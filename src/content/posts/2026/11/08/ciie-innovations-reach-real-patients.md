# How CIIE Turns Expo Debuts Into Real Patient Lives

When the China International Import Expo (CIIE) opened its ninth edition in Shanghai this week, the halls of the National Exhibition and Convention Center were again crowded with global firsts — record numbers of debut products, cutting-edge devices and headline-grabbing technologies. But a feature published by [The Paper](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_34425227) on the opening days of the expo asks a more grounded question: how do these innovations travel the "last mile" from an exhibition booth to a hospital bed, and from a product debut to a patient's daily life?

For one family in Gansu, the answer is measured in centimeters of intestine — and in a seven-year-old boy who can now go to primary school.

## From an orphan drug to a child's recovery

According to The Paper, a boy given the pseudonym Guoguo was diagnosed at age two with short bowel syndrome (SBS), a rare condition in which the small intestine is unable to absorb enough nutrients. After extensive resection, only 35 centimeters of his intestine remained — compared with the five to eight meters typical in an adult and roughly three meters in a newborn. He depended on long-term intravenous nutrition, a regimen that carries the risk of liver, kidney and other multi-organ damage.

That trajectory changed at the 2022 CIIE, when Teduglutide — a GLP-2 analogue then regarded as an "orphan drug" for digestive rare diseases — was first shown at the expo and signed on-site with the Hainan Bo'ao Lecheng International Medical Tourism Pilot Zone. Within less than six months, the drug entered "pilot early-access" at a Bo'ao medical institution. In May 2023, Guoguo became the first patient in mainland China to receive it, treated at Shanghai Xinhua Hospital in coordination with the Bo'ao facilities.

The clinical stakes were significant. Per The Paper's reporting, after a course of treatment, 12% of pediatric patients could fully wean off parenteral nutrition, while 69% achieved at least a 20% reduction in weekly parenteral nutrition volume — lowering the risks of infection and complications.

Regulatory milestones followed quickly. In February 2024, China's National Medical Products Administration formally approved the drug for SBS in adults and children aged one year and older — the country's first GLP-2 class therapy for the condition. In December 2025, it was included in China's first Commercial Health Insurance Innovative Drug Catalog. And in August 2026, an expanded indication was approved for children with corrected gestational age of four months and above.

"The child is in primary school now, his health is okay, and he still needs regular infusions," Guoguo's mother, Ms. Ma, told The Paper during the ninth CIIE. Her hopes now turn to cost: "If this drug can enter basic medical insurance in the future, our medication costs will be even lower."

## A "CIIE baby" that stays — and stays local

The Teduglutide story is one of many the expo has helped write. Boston Scientific's pulsed field ablation (PFA) cardiac system, which first entered Asia at the 2021 CIIE, was formally approved in China in 2024. Last month, 78-year-old Mr. Zou, who had lived with atrial fibrillation for decades, underwent surgery using the FARAPULSE system at Beijing Fuwai Hospital.

"If CIIE hadn't introduced these new technologies, we might still not have dared to decide on surgery," his nephew, a medical student, told The Paper. The procedure took about an hour and fifteen minutes, and Mr. Zou was discharged the next day.

Zeng Zhiyu, senior director of medical affairs at Boston Scientific China, described PFA as a transformative advance in atrial fibrillation treatment. Unlike traditional radiofrequency and cryoablation, which rely on heat or cold, PFA uses pulsed electric fields to form irreversible nanopores in target heart-muscle cells, blocking abnormal electrical signals while sparing surrounding tissue. Patients with atrial fibrillation face a stroke risk five times that of the general population, and a single procedure can now combine ablation with left atrial appendage closure to manage that risk.

Crucially, the newer FARASTAR platform, first shown at this year's expo, received NMPA approval for local production in 2026 and will be manufactured in Shanghai's Lingang area — a sign that CIIE is not only a gateway for imports but also a channel for deeper Chinese participation in global R&D and manufacturing.

## The last mile of access

Other exhibitors echoed the theme. Medtronic displayed a brain-computer interface closed-loop ecosystem for Parkinson's disease, dystonia and epilepsy; Novo Nordisk showed a "one-tube dual-chamber" weight-loss drug that made this year's CIIE "black tech" list; and Roche presented emicizumab, the only hemophilia A therapy in China suitable from children to adults regardless of inhibitor status, administered by subcutaneous injection as infrequently as once a month. The company said the drug has entered 2026 national medical insurance negotiations.

Dai Jing, a professor in the laboratory medicine department at Ruijin Hospital, affiliated with Shanghai Jiao Tong University School of Medicine, used the expo to call for tiered diagnosis and treatment. Standard-dose emicizumab requires no regular coagulation monitoring, she noted, lowering the threshold for grassroots follow-up and allowing patients to receive regular therapy closer to home. "When the last mile of tiered diagnosis is opened," she said, "innovative therapies can truly become accessible, sustainable daily management for patients."

AstraZeneca, which first set up a CIIE "stall" nine years ago, has seen more than 20 "CIIE babies" enter thousands of hospitals in China. This year it brought two China-first therapies — for hypertension and generalized myasthenia gravis — and formally established AstraZeneca Lingang Cell Technology (Shanghai) Co. in the Lingang area.

## What's next

The expo's enduring message, as The Paper frames it, is that its significance lies not only in displaying and trading innovations but in building a bridge to people's needs. China's imports have ranked second globally for 17 consecutive years, and the country is a major export destination for nearly 80 countries — a "super factory" that is also a "super market."

Yet the access gap is not fully closed. Families like Guoguo's still wait for basic medical insurance inclusion, and questions remain over how widely the new commercial insurance catalog will be adopted and how quickly local manufacturing will lower costs. For CIIE, the measure of success may lie less in the record number of debuts and more in how many of them reach the bedside.
