# China Unveils 36th News Awards and 19th Changjiang Taofen Honours

China's leading journalism bodies have announced the results of two of the country's most prestigious media honours, with 378 works receiving the 36th China News Awards and 20 journalists named winners of the 19th Changjiang Taofen Award. The results were released on 8 November by the All-China Journalists Association (ACJA) and published by the state news agency [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/politics/20261108/8ad4965145394783ae6ad4decf5f654e/c.html).

## A Sweeping Slate of Winners

The 36th China News Awards distributed honours across a wide field of print, broadcast and digital journalism. According to Xinhua, the 378 winning works comprised three Special Prizes, 71 First Prizes, 117 Second Prizes and 187 Third Prizes. The full catalogue was published separately by the agency in its [official list of winning works](https://www.news.cn/20261108/706d952f03ee4177ba336509289a236b/c.html).

The Changjiang Taofen Award, the highest honour for individual journalists in China, went to 20 recipients — 10 in the Changjiang series and 10 in the Taofen series, listed in surname-stroke order. The complete roster of names and their home outlets appears in Xinhua's [winners' list](https://www.news.cn/20261108/9237de1c98c54df0a131df18c637a293/c.html).

## The Three Special Prizes

The three Special Prizes illustrate the themes the awards body chose to foreground. A commentary by People's Daily, "The Great Rejuvenation of the Chinese Nation Is Unstoppable", took a Special Prize in the commentary category. Xinhua's own international-communication piece — "Xi Focus: How Xi's crackdown on excess reshapes China", examining the impact of the "Eight-Point Regulation" on official conduct — won in the international-communication category. The third Special Prize went to China Media Group's news report on the commissioning of China's first electromagnetic-catapult aircraft carrier, attended by Xi Jinping.

Together, the three map onto familiar priorities: ideological confidence, Party discipline and military modernisation.

## Watchdog Journalism and Storytelling Highlighted

Beyond the top prizes, Xinhua singled out several categories of work. Among the investigative and watchdog pieces it named were a Yicai report alleging that some local governments purchase export statistics at fiscal cost without substantive economic benefit, and a Dazhongwang independent investigation into the limited repair options facing owners of new-energy vehicles. Xinhua framed such reporting as "confronting problems, digging into root causes, generating wide impact and driving solutions" — presenting scrutiny as constructive problem-solving rather than adversarial.

Human-interest and international-communication work also featured. China Daily's "How China turned desert green?" and a Yiwu converged-media centre production, "Yiwu's African Dream", were cited for adapting grand themes to overseas audiences across omnimedia channels. Story-driven pieces on ordinary people, including a profile of a centenarian bookseller guarding a century-old bookshop, were praised for their communication effect.

## The Changjiang Taofen Award

The 20 individual honourees span central outlets, provincial and municipal media, and military journalism. The Changjiang series includes Shuang Chuanxue, president of Xinhua Daily; Qi Bin, director of the video department at China News Service; Luo Yingchun, deputy director of Hunan Broadcasting System; Guo Tao, head of the Chang'an Street Knowledge editorial department at Beijing Daily; and Mu Li, Latin America bureau chief for China Media Group.

The Taofen series includes Fang Lixin, Party secretary of Xinhua Daily Telegraph; Wang Binlai, director of the news coordination department at People's Daily; Liu Changfa, editor-in-chief of Chongqing Daily Newspaper Group; Tang Yaming, editor-in-chief of Shenzhen Press Group and Shenzhen Special Zone Daily; and Yan Jun, a senior editor at the PLA News Media Center.

Xinhua described the winners as firm in political stance, outstanding in professional ability and innovative in approach, noting that they had "actively devoted themselves to the systematic reform of mainstream media" and contributed to consolidating mainstream public opinion.

## Background and Process

The China News Awards and the Changjiang Taofen Award are both organised by the ACJA. The Changjiang Taofen Award traces its lineage to two separate honours — the Fan Changjiang Journalism Award, established in 1991, and the Tao Fen Award, established in 1993 — which were merged in 2005 under central regulations on national news and publishing awards. It is named after pioneering journalists Fan Changjiang and Zou Taofen.

The selection followed a formal procedure that included a public-notice period. The ACJA published the proposed 19th Changjiang Taofen winners on 23 October, opening a comment window that closed at noon on 28 October, as [China News Service](https://www.chinanews.com.cn/sh/2026/10-23/10719617.shtml) reported at the time. Members of the public could submit views through the ACJA website or by telephone, with the awards office tasked with verifying complaints before final determination.

## What to Watch

The annual awards function as more than recognition of individual excellence: they signal the editorial priorities the system wishes to reward. This year's slate — ideological commentary, anti-corruption messaging aimed abroad, military modernisation, and officially sanctioned watchdog reporting — reads as a coherent statement of those priorities heading into the next news cycle. How newsrooms respond, particularly in the watchdog and international-communication categories, will be the measure of the awards' influence in the year ahead.
