# Haren Prison: Rape, Escape and Assault Expose a System in Crisis

Within the space of just two days, Belgium's flagship prison was hit by three serious security incidents: the rape of a female detainee, the assault of four prison guards, and the escape of an inmate who simply walked out of the facility. According to [RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/viol-d-une-detenue-evasion-et-agression-d-agents-que-se-passe-t-il-a-la-prison-de-haren-11812374), the authorities insist the events are unconnected — but together they have thrust Haren, billed as the country's most modern penitentiary, into a renewed debate over overcrowding, staffing and safety.

## What Happened

The incidents unfolded in quick succession in early November. On Wednesday, a female detainee was raped by a male detainee while she was working in the prison workshop — a shared space where men and women work side by side. The Brussels prosecutor's office has opened a judicial investigation, and the penitentiary administration confirmed the assault. Support and care have been arranged for the victim and for other detainees who were present in the workshop.

The following morning, shortly before 8 am, a detainee escaped his cell and the facility "without any violence," according to RTBF. He slipped past the prison's control system by following a co-detainee who held a one-day leave permit, all under the supervision of a guard who was still in training. He was intercepted in central Brussels that afternoon and returned to custody. Later the same day, four guards were injured by a detainee with psychiatric problems who attacked staff as he was due to be examined by a doctor in his cell.

## A Chronicle of Dysfunction

For union representatives, the week's events are not anomalies but symptoms. Yasin Sarikaya, vice-president of the CSC Services publics union for the Brussels prison, points to a facility operating well beyond its limits.

"The problem is that we have a lot of new staff. Some have been trained, but it isn't enough given the size of the infrastructure," Sarikaya told RTBF. "There are 1,443 detainees while we only have 1,190 places. The number of officers doesn't keep up with the number of detainees."

He warned that the consequences are grave. "This causes serious security problems. We have repeatedly raised the alarm so that laws or systems can be put in place to reduce this overcrowding and massively recruit officers."

The penitentiary administration, for its part, says it is investigating. Spokeswoman Kathleen Van De Vijver confirmed that a judicial inquiry has been opened into the rape, and that an investigation is underway into the circumstances of the escape, with internal security procedures under review.

## A Pattern, Not an Isolated Week

The November incidents cap a difficult year for Haren. The facility was conceived as a model prison for 1,190 detainees, but an earlier investigation found 175 inmates sleeping on mattresses on the floor as overcrowding, staff shortages and rising incidents eroded normal operations. The prison's director resigned at the end of May 2026, denouncing what he called the "institutionalization of overpopulation." Vincent Spronck took over the leadership in October — only weeks before the current crisis.

The violence has been relentless. In April, staff staged a 24-hour strike over overcrowding; in June, an officer was strangled and beaten; in August, another assault prompted the plea "we want this to stop." Two off-duty guards were also attacked in the street after being recognized. The week of 4 November fits a grim, escalating pattern rather than a sudden rupture.

## Why It Matters

At the heart of the crisis is a structural dilemma. Unions argue that the justice system leans on imprisonment as a default sanction without matching resources, leaving prisons caught between too many detainees and too few, and too inexperienced, officers. The result is a facility that was meant to embody modern detention standards but now struggles to keep detainees and staff safe.

The escape raises pointed questions about how trainees are supervised and how leave-permit escorts are handled. The rape in a mixed workshop raises questions about oversight in shared work areas. And the assault on guards underscores the difficulty of managing detainees with psychiatric needs in a system under strain.

## What's Next

The immediate focus is on the investigations: the outcome of the judicial inquiry into the rape, and any disciplinary findings or procedural changes stemming from the escape. Beyond that, the political pressure is likely to intensify. Unions have long demanded mass recruitment and concrete measures to reduce overcrowding, and the Haren incidents give them fresh ammunition.

The broader question is whether Belgium's flagship prison can still serve as a model — or whether it has become a cautionary tale about what happens when ambition outpaces investment in the people and resources needed to run a modern penitentiary. For the officers and detainees of Haren, that answer cannot come soon enough.
