# China Launches Delegate Elections for 21st Party Congress

China has formally begun selecting the delegates who will attend the 21st National Congress of the Communist Party of China, a leadership gathering that will shape the country's political direction for the next five years. On 8 November 2026, a senior official of the CPC Central Committee Organization Department answered questions from Xinhua reporters on the delegate election process, one day after the Central Committee issued its formal notice launching the undertaking, according to [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/20261108/01ca558d64834f319d50b388ebfccb77/c.html).

The congress itself is scheduled to convene in Beijing in the second half of 2027, a decision made by the 20th Central Committee's Fifth Plenum. The delegate election work is slated to finish by the end of June 2027.

## A Procedural Launch With Political Weight

Although a routine feature of the party's five-yearly congress cycle, the publication of the six-part Q&A marks the formal start of the pre-congress phase of internal mobilization and political vetting. The document details the number of delegates, the electoral units they will represent, the timeline and the multi-stage selection procedure, laying out how the party intends to construct the body that will ultimately review the work of the outgoing Central Committee and elect its successor.

According to the [notice issued on 7 November](https://www.news.cn/politics/20261107/3355f2b71fd84a97a14dd44e1645098d/c.html), the 21st Congress will have **2,360 delegates**, an increase of 60 from the 2,300 who attended the 20th Congress in 2022. These delegates will be elected from **38 electoral units**, a number unchanged from the previous cycle, covering provinces, regions and municipalities, central and state organs, the central financial system, the central enterprise system operating in Beijing, and the People's Liberation Army and armed police.

## The Selection Process: Five Steps, Fifteen Percent

In the Q&A, the Organization Department explained that delegates will be chosen through a method it described as "from below upward, combining upper and lower levels, repeated deliberation, and step-by-step selection." The process unfolds across five main stages:

1. **Recommendation and nomination**, beginning at the grassroots level, with all party organizations and members encouraged to participate.
2. **Organizational investigation**, a vetting stage involving differential inspection and advance notice of the inspection to the candidates.
3. **Determination of preliminary candidates**, announced within the relevant electoral unit to solicit further opinion.
4. **Determination of a preparatory candidate pool**, decided by a vote of the party committee plenary.
5. **Conference election**, a differential election with a differential ratio of more than 15 percent.

Before the congress opens, a Delegate Credentials Committee will review the qualifications of all elected delegates.

## Political Standards Placed 'First'

The Q&A repeatedly emphasizes that political loyalty is the paramount criterion. "The political demands on party congress delegates come first; the most important thing is political firmness and loyalty to the party," the official said, adding that "those not politically qualified are vetoed with one vote."

Candidates will be assessed on their grasp of the "two establishments" and "two safeguards", the party's formulation elevating Xi Jinping's core position and centralized authority, alongside the "four consciousnesses" and "four confidences." The Q&A also stresses integrity screening, vowing to "strictly prevent 'sick nominations' and 'sick elections,'" integrating the party's long-running anti-corruption campaign into the vetting of delegate candidates.

The document explicitly invokes the vote-buying and election-rigging scandals of Hunan's Hengyang, Sichuan's Nanchong and Liaoning as cautionary examples, warning that such problems must never recur. The organization said it would intensify supervision, investigation and accountability to "ensure a clean atmosphere" around the process.

## Broadening the Party's Social Base

The Q&A sets out detailed composition requirements aimed at ensuring delegates are broadly representative. First-front production and construction workers must make up no less than one-third of delegations from provinces, the financial system and central enterprises, and no less than 15 percent of the central and state organs delegation. The document calls for representation from across the economy, science and technology, national defense, legal affairs, education, propaganda, culture, health, sports and social management.

Notably, it also asks electoral units in areas with large "new employment groups", a term referring to gig-economy and platform workers, to recommend outstanding party members from those groups. Provincial delegations must also meet minimum thresholds for women delegates and include ethnic minority members, reflecting a deliberate effort to extend the party's reach beyond traditional cadres.

## Context: A Twenty-Month Runway

The delegate election is the opening act of the congress cycle. While the current process concerns only the selection of delegates, the congress itself will elect a new Central Committee and Central Commission for Discipline Inspection, a leadership reshuffle with major implications for China's political direction. The last congress was held in October 2022, when the party unveiled its 20th Central Committee.

The heavy emphasis on centralized discipline and loyalty to Xi's core position signals continuity in the party's vetting priorities. The increase to 2,360 delegates reflects continued growth in party membership, which the Q&A places at more than 110 million members across some 5.4 million party organizations.

## What to Watch Next

With the procedural framework now public, attention shifts to the province-by-province rollout of delegate lists, which typically emerge in the months before the congress. Analysts will be watching the balance between first-front workers and career cadres, the handling of the differential-election stage, and any regional anomalies that might signal friction within the process. The completion deadline of June 2027 sets a firm timetable, leaving roughly 20 months before the congress convenes and the next leadership chapter begins.
