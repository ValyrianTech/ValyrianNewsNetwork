# China's Space Station to Host First Foreign Astronaut

For the first time in the history of China's crewed space program, a foreign national will fly to the Tiangong space station. A Pakistani astronaut will join the Shenzhou-24 mission as a payload specialist and, after a short stay in orbit of about one week, return to Earth aboard the Shenzhou-23 spacecraft, according to China's [Xinhua News Agency](https://www.news.cn/20261108/27651bfe87ad4ff5bad37fa72356c670/c.html).

The announcement, made on November 7, marks the first joint China–foreign crewed flight since the program began and positions the visitor as the first foreigner ever to board Tiangong. Chinese state media described it as a "milestone" for the country's space ambitions and a "landmark achievement" of international cooperation aboard the station.

## Context: A Long-Road Partnership

The flight is the culmination of an agreement China and Pakistan signed in February 2025 in Islamabad on the selection and training of Pakistani astronauts and their participation in China Space Station missions. The pact set in motion a rigorous three-stage selection process — preliminary, secondary and final — that ultimately produced two Pakistani astronaut candidates, as [Global Times](https://www.globaltimes.cn/page/202611/1373834.shtml) reported.

Both candidates trained in China and passed the required assessments, with one chosen for the mission and the other serving as backup. A February 7, 2026 announcement by Pakistan's military media and its space agency, SUPARCO, said the two shortlisted candidates would undergo a six-month advanced training program at the Astronauts Centre of China before one was selected for the flight. Reporting carried by the [Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/china-pakistani-astronaut-space-station-5f41ab023758265d421bb4b57ebe931f), citing Xinhua, noted that the program included intensive Chinese-language instruction.

Neither agency has officially named the selected astronaut. Reporting by [The Straits Times](https://www.straitstimes.com/asia/east-asia/pakistani-astronaut-to-join-china-space-stations-shenzhou-24-mission) noted that the China Manned Space Agency (CMSA) said in April that astronauts Muhammad Zeeshan Ali and Khurram Daud had been chosen to begin training, but which of the two will fly has not been confirmed.

## The Mission

Shenzhou-24 is scheduled to launch in November 2026, rendezvousing with the Shenzhou-23 crew already aboard Tiangong for a standard crew rotation. The Shenzhou-23 spacecraft launched in May 2026 and docked with the radial port of the station's core module, Tianhe.

Crucially, the Pakistani astronaut's one-week visit depends on an unprecedented arrangement: one member of the Shenzhou-23 crew will remain in orbit for a full year — a first for China's program — so that the Shenzhou-23 return vehicle is still available to bring the visiting specialist home. That year-long stay is expected to host China's first in-space human research program, gathering data on how the body adapts to long-duration spaceflight, a prerequisite for future crewed missions to the Moon and Mars.

## Analysis: China Positions Itself as the Post-ISS Hub

The flight carries significance well beyond the symbolism of a first. As the International Space Station nears the end of its operational life, China has moved to cast Tiangong as the leading crewed platform open to international partners — a framing Beijing has reinforced by contrasting its invite to Pakistan with restricted access to the US-led ISS.

The openness message was explicit in Chinese coverage. "The openness of China's space program is directed toward all countries willing to join hands in exploring the universe," Xinhua wrote. The agency framed the Pakistan arrangement as proof that Tiangong is a genuinely multilateral facility, citing ongoing cooperation with the UN Office for Outer Space Affairs and discussions of a "secondary expansion" of the station into a cross-shaped configuration.

The timing is diplomatically freighted. 2026 marks the 75th anniversary of China–Pakistan diplomatic relations, and the two countries describe their relationship as an "all-weather" friendship. China is Pakistan's largest trading partner and a major investor, and the astronaut program sits comfortably alongside Belt and Road Initiative cooperation. For Islamabad, sending a citizen to orbit is a prestige milestone that few developing nations have achieved.

The broader strategic frame is competition with the United States. The AP reported that China is preparing for its first crewed lunar landing by 2030, while NASA aims to return astronauts to the lunar surface in 2028. Each international partner Beijing brings aboard Tiangong strengthens its claim to leadership in a multipolar space order.

## What to Watch

Several questions remain open. The identity and scientific role of the selected Pakistani astronaut have not been disclosed, and the precise launch date for Shenzhou-24 within November 2026 has not been fixed publicly.

The mission's scientific return will also be scrutinized. A one-week visit offers limited experimental time; the more consequential research may come from the year-long Shenzhou-23 stay, which China hopes will supply core data for deep-space human adaptation.

For now, the headline is the milestone itself. When Shenzhou-24 lifts off, a program long defined by national achievement will, for the first time, carry a foreign crew member to China's station — a small step for one astronaut, and a calculated leap for Beijing's vision of a shared, and Chinese-anchored, space future.
