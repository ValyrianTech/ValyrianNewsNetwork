# Second Explosion Rocks Kortenberg Street in Days

An explosive device detonated at a home on Brouwerijstraat in the centre of Kortenberg, Flemish Brabant, on the evening of Saturday 7 November, the second such attack on the same short street in under a week. No one was injured, but the repeated blasts have unsettled residents and prompted prosecutors to examine whether the two explosions are connected.

According to a press release from the Leuven public prosecutor's office ([Openbaar Ministerie](https://www.om-mp.be/nl/artikel/persbericht-kortenberg-tweede-explosief-tegen-woning-dezelfde-straat-kortenberg)), police zone HerKo received a report of an explosion at around 21:10. The blast damaged the home's mailbox and a window, a door, and a parked vehicle. The suspects fled and remain at large.

## A Street Hit Twice

The first explosion struck on the night of Tuesday 3 to Wednesday 4 November, at roughly 23:40. As [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/04/ontploffing-brouwerijstraat-kortenberg/) reported, the front door of a home was blown out of its frame and a parked car was also damaged. No injuries were reported in that incident either.

Both scenes drew an immediate response from police zone HerKo, the federal judicial police (FGP) laboratory and the army's bomb disposal unit, DOVO. The Leuven prosecutor's office is leading the investigation, carrying out a neighbourhood canvass and examining available CCTV footage.

"The investigation continues under the direction of the prosecutor's office," prosecutor's office spokesperson Ellen Durie said after the first blast. "Among other things, a neighbourhood canvass is being carried out and available camera footage is being examined. The suspect(s) are currently being pursued."

## Residents Shaken

The incidents have rattled what long-time residents describe as a normally quiet neighbourhood. John, a Brouwerijstraat resident whose home was targeted in the first explosion, told VRT NWS of the moment the device went off.

"First I saw a light in the street, then there was a loud bang," he said. "I immediately went to get the children. They are traumatized. It was extremely frightening."

He added that he had noticed earlier suspicious activity, catching several youths in his living room about two weeks before the first blast. "It was always a calm neighbourhood, but now we are no longer staying," he said.

## Reassurance Overtaken

After the first explosion, Kortenberg mayor Alexandra Thienpont said she was shocked by the damage and urged residents not to fear a repeat attack, vowing that authorities would "get to the bottom of this," as [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/04/burgemeester-reageert-na-ontploffing-kortenberg/) reported at the time. The second explosion four days later undercuts that reassurance and is likely to intensify local pressure on police and municipal authorities.

## What's Next

Prosecutors have not attributed a motive to either attack. Targeted explosive strikes on homes in Belgium are frequently linked to drug-related extortion, settling of scores or intimidation in the narcotics milieu, though officials have made no such connection in this case. Investigators are weighing whether the two blasts are linked, whether CCTV footage can identify the perpetrators, and whether the municipality will respond with additional policing or preventive measures.

For now, the residents of Brouwerijstraat face an uneasy wait, as the search for those responsible continues.
