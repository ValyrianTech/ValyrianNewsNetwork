# China's 43rd Antarctic Expedition Sets Sail From Sanya

China's 43rd Antarctic research expedition departed from Nanshan Port in Sanya, Hainan Province, on 7 November, launching a roughly half-year deployment that will carry more than 600 personnel from over 100 domestic institutions to the southern continent, according to [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/tech/20261107/bb7040522e7449809c263bef55434a4d/c.html). The mission is expected to return in April 2027.

The expedition, organized by the Ministry of Natural Resources, relies on a three-vessel support model using the icebreakers *Xuelong* and *Xuelong 2* alongside the *Dayang*. The *Xuelong* had already departed Shanghai on 3 November and was near the equator at the time of reporting. The formal flag-off on 7 November coincided with *Lidong*, the start of winter in the traditional Chinese solar calendar — a symbolic send-off for a voyage into the austral summer.

## A First for the 15th Five-Year Plan

This is China's first Antarctic expedition of the 15th Five-Year Plan period, a detail that frames the mission well beyond the scientific. Xinhua noted that the plan lists "marine power" among 16 national "power" construction goals and includes "deep-sea, deep-earth and polar exploration" among 109 major engineering projects. Polar science, in this framing, is not a niche pursuit but a plank of national strategy and high-quality marine economic development.

The five stated task areas — which Xinhua summarizes as acceptance, foundation-laying, hard-tackling, field-testing and collaboration — map cleanly onto that ambition.

## Five Task Areas

**Acceptance.** The team will complete on-site construction wrap-up and formal acceptance of Qinling Station, China's newest Antarctic base, including its main building, communications system and sewage treatment facilities. Qinling opened on 7 February 2024 in the Ross Sea region, filling a gap in China's coverage of that sector. "We will fully complete the on-site construction wrap-up and acceptance work ... to further enhance the station's operational support and scientific research capacity," station chief Luo Guangfu said, according to Xinhua. The expedition will also advance long-term monitoring of polar oceans and polynyas.

**Foundation-laying.** The team will conduct reconnaissance and surveying for proposed supporting facilities at a Marie Byrd Land station site and prepare for upgrades to the older Great Wall and Zhongshan stations. Zhongshan station chief Jin Xinmiao noted that both stations were built decades ago and their facilities have run for a long time, so the team will monitor and assess fuel storage tanks and building safety ahead of renovation.

**Hard-tackling.** The flagship scientific push continues the Kiring subglacial lake project. Following the 42nd expedition's breakthrough — a clean hot-water drill that punched through 3,413 meters of ice, surpassing the previous international polar hot-water drilling record of 2,540 meters, as [IT之家](https://www.ithome.com/0/936/454.htm) reported — the 43rd team will drill through the overlying ice cap and conduct in-situ observation and sampling of the subglacial lake itself. Subglacial lakes are prized for the ancient climate records and isolated ecosystems they may hold. The team will also run multi-disciplinary surveys of how climate change is affecting the Southern Ocean ecosystem, using anchored submersible buoys, box grabs, biological trawls and automated monitoring.

**Field-testing.** A recurring theme is indigenous technology. The expedition will test domestic polar equipment including the AR500 large unmanned helicopter, the third-generation "Snow Leopard" 6x6 wheeled vehicle and a new-generation inland expedition pressurized building cluster. It also plans ice-field validation of the THT550 tracked high-horsepower tractor, research and development on thick-snow compaction equipment, and a prototype test of a mobile, fully modular assembled airfield.

**Collaboration.** China will conduct cooperative research with Norway, Chile and Bulgaria on the Antarctic ice sheet, geology and ice-snow environment, and has invited researchers from Thailand, France, Belgium, Portugal and China's Hong Kong and Macau regions to take part — an emphasis on openness that analysts often read as a diplomatic signal as much as a scientific one.

## Analysis: Science, Sovereignty and Self-Reliance

The expedition's structure reveals three overlapping agendas. The first is straightforwardly scientific: the Kiring lake drilling and Southern Ocean monitoring sit at the frontier of climate and polar biology research. The second is infrastructural — completing Qinling, scoping a new Marie Byrd Land site and modernizing Great Wall and Zhongshan extend China's physical footprint and year-round operating capacity across the continent. The third is industrial: field-testing helicopters, vehicles and modular habitats in one of Earth's harshest environments is a proving ground for domestic manufacturers seeking credibility for polar-grade equipment.

That combination — research, infrastructure and technology validation conducted under a single logistical umbrella — is characteristic of how China has scaled its polar program since its first expedition in 1984. The country now operates three year-round stations (Zhongshan, Great Wall and Qinling), a capability it describes as "three-station over-wintering."

It is worth noting that the headline figures — 600-plus personnel, 100-plus institutions, three ships and an April 2027 return — originate with official state media and were not independently corroborated in this reporting pass. The framing of the mission as a component of the 15th Five-Year Plan likewise reflects Beijing's own strategic presentation.

## What's Next

"Seeking scientific answers in the frozen realm, we are confident of completing the mission," said Zhang Tijun, leader and chief scientist of the 43rd expedition, per Xinhua. First Officer Zhu Pengtao of the *Xuelong 2* struck a similar note from the bridge: "Every Antarctic voyage brings both challenges and rewards."

With the *Xuelong* already approaching the equator and the Sanya contingent now underway, attention turns to whether the team can deliver on the Kiring lake sampling, finish Qinling's acceptance cleanly, and validate a new generation of Chinese polar hardware on the ice. Results from those efforts — and the state of the Marie Byrd Land site survey — will shape how the program's next phase is judged.
