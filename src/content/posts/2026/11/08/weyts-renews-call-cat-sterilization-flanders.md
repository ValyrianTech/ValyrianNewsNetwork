# Weyts Renews Call for Cat Sterilization in Flanders

Flemish Animal Welfare Minister Ben Weyts (N-VA) has renewed his appeal for cat owners to sterilize their pets, as new figures show the region's municipalities captured 12,577 stray cats last year—an average of 20 percent more than in 2018. "Sterilize your cat," the minister said in a [direct message carried by VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/06/vlaanderen-zwerfkatten-dierenwelzijn-ben-weyts/) on Friday.

## A Policy Built Since 2018

Since 2018, Flanders has required its cities and municipalities to pursue an active stray-cat policy: drafting a plan, running a dedicated reporting point, and capturing and sterilizing strays—often with the help of volunteers and animal shelters. In 2020, the region added a [general mandatory sterilization requirement](https://www.benweyts.be/nieuws/meer-dan-400000-katten-gesteriliseerd-sinds-invoering-van-verplichting) for cats.

Weyts argues the approach is bearing fruit. More than half of the cats captured last year were re-released after sterilization, while more than a quarter found a new home through adoption. Since 2018, the number of adopted stray cats has risen by 147 percent.

"That does seem to work well," Weyts said. "We note that since 2018 we catch 20 percent more stray cats. But more important is that many more of those cats ultimately find a new home."

## Sterilization Gaps Persist

The problem, the minister contends, will persist as long as owners fail to sterilize their cats—or abandon them. According to official figures, 403,981 cats have been sterilized since the requirement took effect, including 57,321 last year alone. Of the roughly 673,000 registered cats in Flanders, 81.8 percent are now sterilized, though Weyts has stressed that the mandate's target is full compliance.

To close the gap, Weyts wants to give municipalities more powers to act against persistent offenders who ignore the sterilization obligation, including the power to issue fines. "As long as people do not sterilize their cat or dump them, we are mopping with the tap open," he said. "My call is therefore very simple: sterilize your cat."

## The Cost of Stray Life

The minister emphasizes that stray cats themselves suffer most. Living outdoors in difficult conditions, they face greater risks of disease, injury and malnutrition. "A stray cat usually lives only 2 to 5 years, while a house cat often lives 10 years or older," Weyts said. "In addition, stray cats sometimes cause nuisance through noise, dirt, or by tearing open rubbish bags."

Flanders distinguishes between two groups. Cats that can still be sufficiently re-socialized go to shelters and stand a chance at adoption. Too-wild animals are sterilized and re-released to fixed locations, where volunteers provide follow-up and feeding.

## Regional Differences

Antwerp province registered the most captured stray cats last year, and the cities with the most capture operations are also located there. Flemish Brabant performs best on adoptions, with more than a third of captured stray cats there finding a new home.

With sterilization rates still falling short of the 100 percent target, the renewed appeal signals that the policy debate is shifting from setting up the system toward enforcing it—and toward persuading the remaining holdouts to comply.

## What's Next

The concrete details of the proposed fine scheme—including amounts and legal mechanisms—have not yet been specified, suggesting a follow-up policy announcement is likely. For now, the message from Flanders' animal welfare minister remains a single, repeated line: sterilize your cat.
