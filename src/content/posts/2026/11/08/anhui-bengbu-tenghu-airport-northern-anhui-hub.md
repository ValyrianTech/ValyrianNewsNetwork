# Anhui Weighs Bengbu Airport as Northern Anhui Hub

The Anhui Provincial Department of Transport has responded to a citizen proposal calling for the newly opened Bengbu Tenghu Airport to be positioned as the central aviation hub of northern Anhui, saying it will carefully study the idea and weigh it in future planning, [The Paper](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_34431951) reported on November 8.

The response, delivered through a public online message platform, neither endorses nor rejects the proposal. It is the latest signal of the tension between grassroots ambitions for regional hub status and the province's cautious, incremental approach to civil aviation planning.

## The Citizen's Proposal

The unnamed resident argued that Bengbu Tenghu Airport occupies a strong position at the geographic center of northern Anhui, sitting at a moderate distance from the region's major cities — Suzhou, Huaibei, Fuyang and Bozhou. That location, the proposal said, would let the airport "efficiently gather passengers and cargo from across northern Anhui, filling the gap in northern Anhui's aviation services."

The citizen also leaned on Bengbu's multi-modal assets. Railways, expressways and Huai River shipping converge on the city, giving it what the proposal described as natural conditions to become a northern Anhui cargo distribution and transit node. On that basis, the resident floated a "one main, two sub" (一主两副) hub structure, with Bengbu Tenghu joining Hefei Xinqiao and Wuhu Xuan airports — the former as the provincial core, the latter two as regional sub-hubs.

Under the plan, Bengbu would focus squarely on the northern Anhui market, absorbing air cargo demand for local specialty agricultural products, Chinese medicinal materials, photovoltaic components and e-commerce express parcels. The citizen further asked the province to support a cargo logistics park, air-rail transit facilities and fast corridors linking the airport to expressways, rail freight stations and Huai River port areas — part of a broader push to cultivate an airport-adjacent economy.

## A Cautious Official Reply

The Department of Transport's reply opened with a survey of the province's aviation build-out. Anhui has now completed and put into operation eight transport airports — Hefei, Huangshan, Chizhou, Fuyang, Anqing, Wuhu Xuan, Bozhou and Bengbu — giving it a "one pivot, seven branches" (一枢七支) airport system, the department said.

Bengbu Tenghu Airport, it noted, opened in April 2026 under joint provincial and municipal promotion. To support the new facility, the province has been engaging the Civil Aviation Administration of China and its East China Regional Administration to seek air-traffic rights and policy backing, while coordinating special civil aviation development funds to help launch routes and consolidate operations.

On the specific hub proposal, the department's language was carefully hedged: it will "carefully absorb and study" the suggestion and "give it comprehensive consideration" during subsequent planning and policy making.

## A New Airport, and a Crowded Region

Bengbu Tenghu Airport formally opened on April 29, 2026, becoming Anhui's eighth civil transport airport, according to [Zhong'an Online](http://ah.anhuinews.com/gdxw/202604/t20260429_9246481.html). Located in Fenghé Town in Huaiyuan County, roughly 37 kilometers from the city center, it is a 4C-class domestic regional airport with a 2,600-meter runway, a 26,000-square-meter terminal and 11 aircraft stands. Its maximum annual capacity is designed for 1.8 million passengers and 10,000 tonnes of cargo and mail. Donghai Airlines flight DZ6253 from Shenzhen operated the inaugural service, and the airport has since added routes to Chengdu, Chongqing, Haikou and Dalian.

The airport enters a crowded northern Anhui market. As [Cnr](https://www.cnr.cn/ah/kjjr/20260429/t20260429_527605181.shtml) reported, Fuyang Airport, Bozhou Airport and Bengbu Airport now form a tripartite aviation network across the region. Bengbu's strategy, local officials say, is to avoid direct competition with Hefei on passengers and with Xuzhou on cargo, pursuing simultaneous passenger and cargo development and differentiating through a closed-loop "road-rail-water-air" model.

"Bulk cargo can move via Huai River water transport, high-value cargo via air — this dual drive of 'water channel plus air corridor' can significantly cut enterprises' comprehensive logistics costs, improving supply-chain efficiency for foreign trade and manufacturing by up to 30 percent," said Xu Bin, head of the comprehensive transport section at the Bengbu Municipal Transport Bureau.

## Analysis: A Long Game, Not a Verdict

By Chinese provincial standards, "carefully absorb and study" is a familiar formulation — polite, non-committal and deferential to future planning cycles. It signals that the citizen's proposal has been heard but has not, as yet, been translated into policy.

The distinction between the citizen's "one main, two sub" framing and the province's official "one pivot, multiple branches" language is instructive. The province's own 15th Five-Year Plan (2026–2030) recommendations call for improving the "one pivot, multiple branches" system, accelerating the expansion of Hefei Xinqiao and Wuhu Xuan airports, building Suzhou and Jinzhai airports "as appropriate," and advancing early-stage work on relocating Huangshan airport. For now, that plan treats Bengbu as one regional branch in a growing system rather than a designated sub-hub.

Still, Bengbu has been building the groundwork. The city is planning a roughly 1.5 billion yuan Yangtze River Delta Integration Smart Airport Industrial Park, already added by the Provincial Development and Reform Commission to its "major project preliminary work" library, and an airport logistics park is included in the city's logistics development plan through 2030. City CPPCC members have submitted proposals on accelerating the industrial park and building an "airport new district," and Bengbu is eyeing a low-altitude economy, cold-chain logistics and high-end manufacturing clusters.

## What's Next

Whether Bengbu Tenghu becomes a genuine northern Anhui hub will depend less on a single reply than on route density, cargo volumes and the province's next round of airport planning. The province has committed to continued coordination and funding support for the young airport, but the specific hub designation remains an open question.

For now, the story is one of a city that has won its airport and is now making its case for the next step — and a province that is listening, carefully.
