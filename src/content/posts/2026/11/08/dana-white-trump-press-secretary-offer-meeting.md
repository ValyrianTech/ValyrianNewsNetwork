# Dana White to Meet Trump Monday Over Press Secretary Offer

UFC president and CEO Dana White said Saturday he will fly to Washington and meet President Donald Trump on Monday to discuss an extraordinary offer to serve as White House press secretary, a proposal that would install a sports executive with no government experience into one of the most visible roles in the administration. Trump announced the offer on Truth Social as his administration reels from sweeping Republican losses in the November 3 midterm elections, according to [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/11/08/nx-s1-6033900/ufc-dana-white-trump-white-house-press-secretary).

## An Unconventional Offer

In his post, Trump described White as "the Great Dana White, a violent Fighter and Warrior" and said he had been offered the job of "Press Secretary of the United States." The president urged White to bring along several UFC fighters as "assistants," writing: "We need these people to defend us against the Lunatic Left. Dana will be a Press Secretary like none other. Dana, please take the job!"

Speaking at a news conference in London, where he was attending a boxing event, White did not confirm whether he would accept. "I'm flying to Washington DC right now... I'm going to meet with him, we have a meeting on Monday, so I'm sure I'll have more to talk about after that," he said. Before the news conference, White posted an Instagram story of himself behind the White House briefing room podium captioned "On to the next chapter." Asked how he would balance the role with his existing jobs, he replied: "Yeah, I got a lot of jobs already."

## A Vacant Podium

The offer lands at a moment of acute turmoil for the administration. The press secretary position has been vacant since Karoline Leavitt departed at the end of August. Her replacement, Katie Zacharia, lasted only five days in October, with Trump later saying he had suggested she leave the role.

The timing is equally striking. The offer came days after Democrats won control of both the House and Senate in the midterms, and one day after Trump announced he was replacing chief of staff Susie Wiles with aide Stephen Miller, as [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/nov/07/donald-trump-dana-white-press-secretary) reported. That cascade of changes has fueled speculation that the White House is entering a period of upheaval.

## Conflicting Accounts

It remains unclear whether the offer is a genuine plan. [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/11/07/politics/dana-white-trump-press-secretary) reported, citing a source, that White had declined the offer, while one presidential aide described the post as a joke. The White House and a UFC spokesperson did not respond to inquiries on Saturday.

White's own public comments stopped short of a decision, leaving the question open until Monday's meeting. [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/07/trump-ufc-dana-white-press-secretary-offer.html) framed the situation as unresolved, noting it was unclear whether White had accepted or was merely acknowledging a joke.

## A Longtime Ally

White and Trump share a close, longstanding friendship. White spoke at the Republican National Conventions in 2016, 2020 and 2024, and he praised Trump in 2016 as "the first guy to recognize the potential we saw in the UFC." In June 2026, Trump hosted a UFC event, UFC Freedom 250, on the White House South Lawn for his 80th birthday, an event White called "a one-of-one." Trump has also recently floated rapper Nicki Minaj as a possible press secretary.

## Financial and Political Hurdles

Accepting the role would carry steep costs. White's fortune has been estimated at more than $600 million, while the press secretary job pays roughly $195,000 a year — a major financial sacrifice. And the position would place a novice communicator at the center of a White House navigating divided government after a decisive electoral defeat.

## What to Watch

The key question is whether Monday's meeting produces a decision — or whether the episode fades as a distraction from the administration's political setback. How the White House fills the press secretary post, and whether White's flirtation with the job was serious, will signal how the president intends to communicate with the public and the press as he confronts a Congress now controlled by Democrats.
