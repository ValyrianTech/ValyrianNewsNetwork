# Blizzard Blankets Qinhuangdao, Snow Above Knee-Deep

The coastal city of Qinhuangdao in northeastern Hebei Province has been buried by a severe blizzard that residents say is the worst in decades, prompting the city's highest-tier weather warning, disrupting transport and closing expressways across northern China.

The snowstorm began around midday on November 7 and continued through the morning of November 8, with the Qinhuangdao Municipal Meteorological Observatory issuing a **red-level blizzard warning** — the top of China's four-tier colour-coded system — at 00:22 on November 8, according to [The Paper](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_34432115).

"Snow started around 11 a.m. on the 7th and had still not stopped by 7:30 a.m. on the 8th. On-site measurement showed snow depth of 23 cm, with some areas deeper," resident Mr. Yuan told local media. "In my 20 years in Qinhuangdao, I've never seen snow this big."

## An Unusually Early and Intense Storm

The blizzard arrived just one day after *Lidong*, the "Start of Winter" solar term, which fell on November 7 this year. That timing — combined with the storm's ferocity — made it exceptional: several residents told reporters they had not seen comparable snow in 20 to 30 years.

The storm was part of a broad cold wave and rain-snow event sweeping northern China, driven by cold air pushing east and south from Mongolia colliding with warm, moist air — a classic setup for heavy frontal snow in North China. Qinhuangdao's location on the Bohai Sea likely added moisture and strong coastal winds to the mix.

## Official Snowfall Data

According to figures released by the Qinhuangdao Meteorological Observatory at 08:00 on November 8, covering the period from 13:00 on November 7 to 08:00 on November 8, the city's central-north saw heavy to extreme blizzard conditions, while the south saw rain and sleet turning to heavy blizzard.

City-wide average snowfall — measured as melted-water equivalent, per Chinese meteorological convention — reached 30.7 mm. District totals ranged widely: Shanhaiguan recorded 40.8 mm, Lulong 37.4 mm and Funing 30.3 mm, while Qinglong saw 23.7 mm. Snow depth reached 30 cm in Shanhaiguan and 28 cm in Haigang, the main urban district.

Thirty-nine weather stations recorded strong winds, with the maximum gust at Lulong Wutongyu reaching 28.0 metres per second — Beaufort scale 10.

## Disruption Across the City

Residents described chaotic conditions. Roads were covered in deep snow, vehicles slipped, and some cars could not leave residential compounds. Bus operations were significantly affected, and many workers could not reach their offices.

"Cars couldn't drive on the roads, so everyone walked to work today. About half my colleagues couldn't make it, and work is a bit chaotic," said a Shanhaiguan District resident posting online under the name "Half a Grapefruit." The resident added that a carport in the district collapsed under the weight of the snow; it was empty at the time.

Another resident told reporters the snow was knee-deep or mid-calf deep in places, with an icy, slippery bottom layer beneath fresh snow.

## Expressways Closed Region-Wide

The impact extended well beyond Qinhuangdao. As of 07:00 on November 8, authorities imposed large-scale expressway controls across Hebei. In the Tangshan, Qinhuangdao, Chengde and Zhangjiakou jurisdictions, all expressway toll stations were closed due to snow, with only minor exceptions — a standard safety response to severe snow and ice that nonetheless disrupts regional road freight and passenger travel.

At the national level, the National Meteorological Center issued a yellow blizzard warning at 18:00 on November 7, covering parts of Beijing, Tianjin, Hebei, Liaoning, Inner Mongolia, Shanxi, Gansu and Qinghai, and warning of heavy-to-blizzard snow with new accumulations of 5–15 cm, locally over 20 cm.

## What to Watch

A red blizzard warning authorises local authorities to suspend schools and non-essential business if necessary, and officials urged departments to take snow and frost-damage precautions. Forecasts for the morning of November 8 called for further snow in the central-south and northern areas, with new accumulation of 1–4 cm city-wide.

With temperatures expected to plunge as much as 8–11°C by the morning of November 9 under a provincial cold-wave warning, the focus now shifts to clearing roads, restoring transport and guarding against ice — and to whether this early-season storm signals a harsher-than-usual winter ahead for northern China.
