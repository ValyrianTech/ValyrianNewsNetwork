# Proximus Outage Blocks Mobile Calls for Half an Hour

Customers of Proximus, Belgium's largest telecom operator, were unable to make or receive mobile phone calls for roughly half an hour on Friday morning, an incident confirmed by both the company and the country's telecom regulator. Calls failed between about 9:45 and 10:15, after which service returned to normal, according to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/20/storing-bij-proximus-klanten-konden-tijdlang-niet-mobiel-bellen/).

## What happened

The disruption affected mobile voice services across the operator's network. Anyone who tried to call a Proximus number during the window got no connection at all, and calls between Proximus mobile subscribers failed in the same way. Both Proximus and Belgium's telecom watchdog, the BIPT (Belgisch Instituut voor Postdiensten en Telecommunicatie), confirmed that something had gone wrong with the network.

The scale of the incident was visible in outage-reporting data. According to [ITdaily](https://itdaily.be/nieuws/netwerk/proximus-telefoonnetwerk-storing/), reports on the Downdetector platform jumped from close to zero to more than 2,000 at 9:45, indicating that a large number of users were affected simultaneously. Service was back under control about half an hour later.

## Cause still under investigation

At the time of reporting, later that same morning, the root cause had not yet been established. Proximus said it was investigating further and promised to share more information as it became available. Neither the operator nor the BIPT specified whether the fault stemmed from a software or configuration issue, a hardware failure, or a capacity problem.

That an outage of this kind draws an official confirmation from the regulator is noteworthy. The BIPT oversees network reliability and service-quality obligations in Belgium's postal and telecommunications markets, and its acknowledgment that the network was at fault underscores that the incident was formally recognized rather than dismissed as isolated user complaints.

## Why a short outage matters

Proximus is the former state telecom monopoly and remains the country's dominant fixed-line provider as well as a major mobile operator. That legacy gives it a customer base broad enough that even a brief, operator-wide fault is felt across the country — from individuals unable to reach family members to businesses whose inbound calls went unanswered during the morning rush.

The incident also lands against a backdrop of heightened attention to network reliability in Belgium. For a company whose scale makes it a de facto piece of national infrastructure, resilience is an ongoing operational priority, and short disruptions tend to raise questions about how quickly a fault can be isolated and how transparently it is explained afterward.

## A contained but instructive event

By the standards of telecom incidents, Friday's disruption was brief and contained. There is no indication of a broader national emergency, and service was restored well before the working day was fully under way. The operator has not reported a wider impact on fixed-line internet or television services.

The real test will come in the follow-up. How quickly Proximus publishes a technical explanation, and whether the incident prompts any scrutiny from the BIPT, will shape how the episode is remembered. For now, the open question is simple: what took the network down, and what is being done to keep it from happening again.
