# Trump, Xi and Putin Hold Rare Nuclear Talks, No Deals

The leaders of the world's three largest nuclear powers met face to face for the first time on Thursday, gathering at a villa in Shenzhen, China, to discuss nuclear risks and the wars in Ukraine and Iran. Yet the unprecedented trilateral summit between US President Donald Trump, Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin ended with no concrete breakthroughs, binding commitments or timelines, according to [AP News](https://apnews.com/article/trump-xi-putin-nuclear-ukraine-iran-a04dc76ed8e786b087e2c1ea9598783a).

The roughly 70-minute meeting, held on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) summit, concluded with a vaguely worded "peace declaration" that affirmed "a nuclear war cannot be won and must never be fought" and pledged the three countries would "conduct a dialogue on reducing nuclear risks and preventing the proliferation of nuclear weapons." But the document, published by the [White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/briefings-statements/2026/11/u-s-russia-china-heads-of-state-declaration-toward-peace/), made no mention of Russia's war in Ukraine or the US-led war in Iran.

## A Narrower Agenda Than Advertised

Going into the meeting, Trump framed the gathering as a historic opportunity to reduce the dangers posed by the three nations' arsenals.

"We are really making history," Trump said in his opening remarks. "The United States, Russia and China are uniquely positioned to discuss how to reduce the number of nuclear weapons on Earth and also to lessen the risk of those weapons being used."

But once behind closed doors, the three leaders settled on a far more limited set of shared goals, per AP: reforming the United Nations in unspecified ways, increasing the production and supply of refined petroleum products while reaffirming the importance of "stable energy flows," reducing nuclear risks, and curbing what they called jurisdiction abuse by the International Criminal Court. The declaration asserted that state officials should enjoy immunity from ICC prosecution — a notable point of agreement given that Putin faces an ICC arrest warrant and none of the three countries is a member of the court, as [Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/19/groundbreaking-trump-xi-putin-summit-five-key-takeaways) detailed.

## Symbolism Over Substance, Experts Say

Arms control specialists were quick to caution that the summit's lofty rhetoric did not translate into meaningful progress.

"We need more than diplomatic theater and photo opportunities between Xi, Putin and Trump," said Daryl Kimball, executive director of the Arms Control Association. "And one meeting will not likely produce dramatic breakthroughs."

Tong Zhao, a senior fellow studying nuclear weapons policy at the Carnegie Endowment for International Peace, told [CNN](https://edition.cnn.com/2026/11/19/politics/nuclear-statement-out-of-trilateral-talks-mirrors-reagan-gorbachev) that the joint reaffirmation of the Reagan-Gorbachev principle — the Cold War-era mantra first enshrined in a 1985 US-Soviet statement — "is better than nothing." But he argued that the declaration "says more about how little common ground exists among the three leading nuclear powers today than about the extent of their shared interests."

Jon Wolfsthal, a former arms control official in the Obama administration now at PAX sapiens, said Trump "failed to use the summit opportunity to actually press either country on real or reliable progress," warning that "talks will not mean much" unless the three leaders set clear goals and direction.

The stakes are considerable. The last US-Russia nuclear arms pact, New START, expired in February 2026, and Trump has said he wants "a new, improved and modernized" deal that includes China. Yet Beijing's arsenal of more than 600 warheads is far smaller than the more than 5,000 believed held by each of the US and Russia — giving China little incentive to accept limits.

## A Win for a 'Multipolar' Vision

While the summit produced few tangible results, analysts say it served the strategic interests of Xi and Putin by projecting a rebalanced world order in which American dominance is no longer assumed.

For Trump, the moment was a well-timed photo opportunity — a diplomatic flex for a president whose party is reeling from a major loss in the US midterm elections, [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/11/20/china/trump-meeting-china-russia-power-rebalance-intl-hnk) noted. But for Xi and Putin, "the meeting is more likely to go down as a vindication of their vision that the world is undergoing a fundamental rebalancing of power — in their favor."

Chinese state media hailed the summit as a "new starting point" for a "new model of relationship among major powers," built on "mutual respect, peaceful coexistence, win-win cooperation, dialogue and consultation." During the talks, Xi pressed China's conditions for cooperation, including that the three powers "respect one another's sovereignty and territorial integrity" and refrain from interfering in one another's internal affairs — a veiled reference to Taiwan.

Analysts noted that Xi and Putin are unlikely to again encounter an American president as amenable to their interests as Trump, giving the moment added momentum.

## Ukraine and Iran Largely Absent

Despite the wars dominating the global agenda, neither conflict featured in the joint declaration. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said Russia's war in Ukraine "was not the central topic at the trilateral meeting," according to AP, though it dominated Putin's bilateral talks with Trump earlier.

After the summit, Trump dialed into a Berlin meeting between US envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff and Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy alongside more than 40 European leaders. Trump told the gathering that he had spoken to Xi and Putin about finding a peaceful end to the Ukraine war, Witkoff said. Trump also said he and Xi agreed that Iran cannot obtain a nuclear weapon and must reopen the Strait of Hormuz, though there were no signs he secured commitments from Xi or Putin to help resolve the Iran conflict.

## What to Watch

No date has been set for follow-up nuclear talks, though the US delegation is expected to be led by the State Department. Trump is scheduled to hold a press conference at the White House on Friday.

The next potential gathering may come at the G20 summit in Miami in December, which Trump is hosting. Xi is expected to attend, and Trump has invited Putin, who said he is considering the invitation. If Putin does attend, the three leaders could meet again within weeks — offering the clearest test of whether Thursday's symbolic gesture can be converted into something more durable.
