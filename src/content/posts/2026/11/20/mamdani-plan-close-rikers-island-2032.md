# Mamdani Sets 2032 Plan to Close Rikers Island Jail

New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani unveiled a sweeping plan on Friday to close the Rikers Island jail complex and relocate its remaining population by the end of 2032, according to [AP News](https://apnews.com/article/zohran-mamdani-rikers-island-aaf05ad46f0a999a213f2613fe39f7dc). The proposal marks the city's first comprehensive blueprint to shutter the notoriously violent facility, but it hinges on reducing the number of people behind bars and on the City Council passing a law to push back a legally mandated deadline that is now less than a year away.

The announcement came at a celebratory news conference at City Hall, where the mayor framed the effort as a reckoning with decades of neglect. "The call to close Rikers has echoed across our city for decades," Mamdani said. "What will make this chapter different is our commitment to see it through."

## A Deadline the City Cannot Meet

In 2019, the City Council passed legislation, signed by then-Mayor Bill de Blasio, requiring Rikers to close by August 31, 2027, and be replaced by four smaller, more modern jails in Brooklyn, the Bronx, Queens and Manhattan. As [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/nov/20/rikers-island-closure-plan-mamdani) reported, the city is not on track to meet that date: construction is behind schedule and the jail population has grown since 2020.

Under the new blueprint, the Brooklyn facility is expected to open first in 2029, followed by the Bronx and Queens in 2031 and Manhattan in 2032. The four jails are expected to hold roughly 4,500 beds, while about 6,600 people remained held on Rikers as of early November.

The administration wants to bring that population down to no more than 5,500 by the Brooklyn facility's 2029 opening and no more than 4,100 by the end of 2032.

## The Council Holds the Key

Mamdani cannot unilaterally change the closure date. As [Politico](https://www.politico.com/news/2026/11/12/mamdani-blueprint-rikers-island-closure-01163165) reported, the power to extend the deadline rests with the City Council, which must pass a new law, and lawmakers have signaled they will only do so in exchange for serious commitments on funding and pretrial reform.

City Council Speaker Julie Menin endorsed the push. "Delays under previous administrations have made it clear that more time is needed to fulfill the commitment the City Council made to New Yorkers in 2019," she said in a statement. "With Rikers now under federal court-ordered oversight, we must bring this troubling chapter in our city's history to an end once and for all."

## Funding and Reforms

The plan pairs the extended timeline with a slate of investments aimed at breaking cycles of incarceration. The administration has committed $146 million next fiscal year for mental health care, substance use treatment, educational and vocational programming, and victim services. It also proposes $76 million for community-based reentry and diversion programs such as supportive housing, $50 million to expand vocational and educational supports, and $16 million for crime victims and survivors.

The proposal also calls for reducing pretrial detention and court delays, modernizing the Department of Correction so its operational problems are not transferred to the new facilities, and restoring the city's online bail payment portal in 2027. The administration said it will explore renewable energy and sustainability projects as part of the island's future reuse.

## Federal Oversight Looms

Rikers has been under federal oversight since a 2015 settlement aimed at curbing violence, and last year a federal judge removed the jail from the city's direct control, placing it under an outside official appointed in January 2026. The plan commits the administration to working with the federal monitor, the remediation manager and the Board of Correction to bring the blueprint into law.

Mamdani, a Democrat, has been a vocal proponent of closing Rikers, though the longstanding plan to build new jails has met fierce pushback, including from his predecessor, former Mayor Eric Adams, who opposed federal oversight and the borough jails. The extended timeline now sets up a high-stakes negotiation with a Council that includes left-wing allies who have pressed for faster action.

## What to Watch

Whether the Council passes legislation extending the deadline to 2032, and on what terms, is the central question. Even with more time, the city must show it can meaningfully reduce its jail population—roughly half of those held face violent charges—to fit within the new facilities' capacity. The administration has promised quarterly progress updates and a transparent accounting of its progress.

"This report is only the beginning," the plan document states. "Together, these steps will result in the closure of Rikers Island, once and for all."
