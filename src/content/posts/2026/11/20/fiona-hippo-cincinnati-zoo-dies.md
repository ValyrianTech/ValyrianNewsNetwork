# Fiona the Hippo, Cincinnati Zoo Icon, Dies at 9

Fiona, the Nile hippopotamus whose improbable survival after a premature birth made her a global sensation and the emotional center of the Cincinnati Zoo, died Thursday from complications after swallowing a knit hat that was accidentally flung into her enclosure. She was 9 years old.

Her death came roughly 11 days after she ingested the hat, and just three days after the zoo publicly disclosed that she was under round-the-clock veterinary care, [the Cincinnati Zoo announced](https://apnews.com/article/fiona-hippo-cincinnati-zoo-hat-3d90d10c57698af06e5a7a67bed05608). Hippos typically live 40 to 50 years in captivity, making her loss strikingly premature.

"It is with extreme sadness that we share with you the devastating news that Fiona did not make it after multiple attempts from our amazing vets and keeper team to help her through what ended up being a medical emergency," the zoo said in a statement.

## A preventable accident

According to zoo spokesperson Michelle Curley, the hat entered Fiona's habitat when a visitor tossed it into the air and a ceiling fan caught it, catapulting the garment through a small opening above the viewing windows. The zoo has described the episode as an accident.

At first, Fiona showed no signs of distress. But her appetite began to decline a few days later, and by Monday, Nov. 16, the zoo said she was receiving around-the-clock care and was "cautiously optimistic" she would pass the obstruction, [the Associated Press reported](https://apnews.com/article/fiona-hippo-cincinnati-zoo-12f473b0792c1ef82c35be442b441f17).

Veterinarians sedated Fiona twice. On Tuesday, an ultrasound revealed bunching in her intestines, and specialists from the Columbus Zoo performed a colonoscopy using a 9-foot scope. The care team even borrowed a vibration plate from a local horse farm, hoping the shaking would help move the obstruction through her digestive tract, [the AP detailed](https://apnews.com/article/fiona-hippo-cincinnati-zoo-hat-04bef9e3d2d89f6ba4260d4daabe5c13). In a brief hopeful sign, keepers offered her a sniff of coffee — a scent she always liked — and were pleased when she responded.

Surgery was never a realistic option. Specialists advised that an operation on a full-grown hippo was too risky: hippos are difficult to anesthetize, have thick skin, a multichambered stomach and a very large gastrointestinal tract. A surgical repair would have required multiple incisions in a long, dangerous procedure.

By Thursday, those efforts had failed. "Despite multiple attempts to move the hat out of her system, it was ultimately too much to overcome," the zoo said.

## From fragile preemie to global star

Fiona was born six weeks premature in January 2017, weighing just 29 pounds (13.2 kilograms) — about 25 pounds below the lowest recorded birth weight for her species at the time. She could not stand or nurse on her own, and a zoo staffer hand-milked her mother to feed her. Against long odds, she survived, and the zoo chronicled her recovery in photos and videos that drew followers worldwide.

Her fame proved durable. Fiona inspired a "Fiona book series" used in classrooms, a line of beer, clothing lines, an ice cream flavor and a bobblehead. One couple even got engaged in front of her exhibit, with Fiona photobombing underwater in the background. Her story also marked an uplifting turn for the zoo after months of public backlash over the 2016 shooting of Harambe, the gorilla killed after a child entered his enclosure — an event that became a global pop-culture phenomenon.

"I have never known an animal so beloved in all my life," zoo board executive chair Thane Maynard said. "From the day she was born, she was like lightning in a bottle. And she will be remembered and beloved many years from now."

## A city and a state mourn

By Friday, mourners had draped a hippo statue outside the zoo with flowers, crayon-written notes and a watermelon, one of Fiona's favorite treats. One note read, "Forever our Fiona."

The tributes crossed political lines. Cincinnati Mayor Aftab Pureval called Fiona "a symbol of perseverance and joy," adding, "Our city is heartbroken, but we couldn't be more grateful that she was part of our lives." Ohio Gov. Mike DeWine said she "captured the hearts of Ohioans and those around the world," while Gov.-elect Amy Acton said her story "brought joy, hope, and a little extra magic to our state," [WKRC reported](https://local12.com/news/local/cincinnati-zoo-announces-fionas-death-after-medical-emergency-caused-by-ingesting-hat). Sen. Bernie Moreno called her "Ohio's fighter."

Usha Vance, wife of Vice President JD Vance, posted a photo of one of their children face-to-face with Fiona through the glass of her enclosure. Former first daughter Chelsea Clinton shared behind-the-scenes photos, writing that she would treasure "the smiles she gave me," [according to the AP](https://apnews.com/article/fiona-hippo-death-mourn-cincinnati-zoo-d3891935a0f3368442e546dcc37ee3e2).

For many, the grief was personal. Jenna Heery told WKRC that Fiona had been "a beacon of hope" while she was pregnant with prematurely born twins, whom she later lost. "I feel shock. It doesn't feel real," she said.

Zoo director Dave Jenike said the outcome stunned the staff. "We are in shock at the moment. We did not expect this outcome. She fought until she couldn't anymore," he said. "Our animal care team was heroic in their efforts to save her. They did everything possible, but her body couldn't fight anymore."

## A case study in exhibit design

Fiona's death has renewed attention on an inherent tension in zoo design: how to create closeness between visitors and animals without compromising safety. In 2024, a rare antelope at a Tennessee zoo choked to death after eating a plastic fruit-pouch lid, and in 2014 a polar bear at a German zoo died after swallowing a jacket dropped into its enclosure.

"It is a fine balance because at some point you have to determine when do you stop putting nets up? When do you start putting plexiglass up?" said Paul Eberhart, a lecturer in human-animal studies at Aurora University, who spoke with WKRC before Fiona's death. "One of the biggest goals with those zoos is to have a connection between the guest and the animal. And the more barriers you throw up in between those two points, the weaker that connection is going to be."

## What comes next

The zoo has not yet released a specific medical cause of death or details of Fiona's final hours, and her keepers have asked for space as they grieve. The Association of Zoos and Aquariums said the zoo provided "extraordinary 24/7 care for Fiona throughout this heartbreaking event."

Fiona's younger brother, Fritz, born at the zoo in 2022, remains part of its hippo family. The memorial outside her statue is expected to keep growing in the days ahead. For a city that watched a 29-pound preemie grow into a worldwide icon, her loss carries a simple, heavy weight — and a reminder that even the most beloved stories can turn on an everyday mistake.
