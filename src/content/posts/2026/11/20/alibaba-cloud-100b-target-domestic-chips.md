# Alibaba Cloud Holds $100B Overseas Target as Domestic Chips Advance

Alibaba Group is holding firm to its long-term goal of Alibaba Cloud generating $100 billion in external revenue by 2030, CEO Wu Yongming said, even as sweeping changes to global AI chip export rules force the company to rethink its infrastructure buildout. Speaking on the company's fiscal Q2 2027 earnings call on November 19, Wu said progress on domestically produced computing power is moving faster than expected, positioning the cloud unit to lean on homegrown silicon rather than imported AI chips.

The reaffirmation of the $100 billion target—which Alibaba ties to a profit margin above 20% for its fiscal 2031 year, or calendar 2030—comes as Alibaba navigates tightening US and global restrictions on advanced AI chip exports. According to [The Paper](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_34517251), Wu told analysts the target remains unchanged "considering the impact of all factors," adding that T-Head's three-generation GPU roadmap is accelerating.

## A Homegrown Compute Roadmap

T-Head (平头哥), Alibaba's chip subsidiary that confidentially filed in September for a Hong Kong listing, is central to the strategy. Wu outlined a rapid cadence: the Zhenwu M890 chip is fully ramping in 2026, the more powerful V900 enters mass production in 2027, and the J900 follows in 2028. As [IT之家](https://www.ithome.com/1/029/676.htm) reported, Alibaba's Qwen model family has already begun training its next-generation model on T-Head chips, while its Bailian model-service platform now runs inference on domestic silicon.

"By the first quarter of 2027, our own domestic T-Head chips can fully support ultra-large-scale model training," Wu said, according to The Paper. "In the future, the vast majority of models—our own, our partners', and our customers'—will rely entirely on domestic chips for training."

Wu framed the shift not merely as a workaround but as a strategic advantage, saying Alibaba's full-stack AI infrastructure would give it a clear edge in the domestic supply chain and make its model iteration cadence "more controllable" over the long run.

## Scarcity Ahead, Then a Rebound

Despite the confidence, Wu offered a sober near-term outlook. "2027 and 2028 will be the two years of greatest compute scarcity in China," he said, as reported by [IT之家](https://www.ithome.com/1/029/682.htm). He expects the cloud business to grow 30% to 45% in the next fiscal year—a step down from the 53% growth in AI cloud and compute services revenue recorded in the September quarter—before returning to 50%-plus growth in calendar 2028 as domestic chip output scales.

The numbers reflect both momentum and pressure. Group revenue for the quarter ended September 30 rose 10% year on year to RMB 271.5 billion, while non-GAAP net profit jumped 92% to RMB 19.9 billion. AI cloud and compute services revenue reached RMB 61 billion, up 53%, and AI-related product revenue notched its 13th consecutive quarter of triple-digit growth. Yet capital expenditure climbed 60% to RMB 50.5 billion, and China e-commerce revenue fell 5%.

## A Data-Center Target in Question

In a notable caveat, Alibaba said changes to regional compute policies could lead it to lower its long-standing target of building more than 20 gigawatts of global data-center capacity by 2032, with a corresponding reduction in its broader capital spending plan. As [IT之家](https://www.ithome.com/1/029/702.htm) reported, the company instead intends to invest more aggressively in the domestic compute supply chain.

That tension—reaffirming an ambitious revenue goal while signaling a possible cut to the infrastructure meant to support it—captures the bind facing China's AI champions. Export controls limit access to cutting-edge foreign chips, while domestic alternatives, though improving quickly, are still scaling up through the lean years Wu described.

## What to Watch

The immediate questions are whether Alibaba can bring its V900 chip to mass production on schedule in early 2027 and sustain Qwen's training cadence on domestic silicon in the meantime. The T-Head spin-off, whose valuation estimates range widely, will also test investor appetite for China's chip ambitions. For now, Alibaba's message is one of adaptation: a $100 billion cloud goal intact, but increasingly powered by chips of its own design.
