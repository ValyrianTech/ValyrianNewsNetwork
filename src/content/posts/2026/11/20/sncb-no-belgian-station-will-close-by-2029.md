# SNCB: No Belgian Station Will Close by 2029

Belgium's national rail operator SNCB has confirmed that none of its roughly 555 stations will be closed under its Transport Plan 2026-2029, regardless of how lightly a stop is used, [RTBF reports](https://www.rtbf.be/article/plan-de-transport-sncb-aucune-gare-ne-sera-supprimee-11818079). The assurance, given in the Mobility Commission of the Chamber of Representatives, defuses — at least for now — a politically charged debate over the country's sparsely used "ghost stations" and sets the framework for three years of timetable changes beginning in December.

## A reprieve for a hamlet station

The commitment is illustrated by Masnuy-Saint-Pierre, a tiny halt in the municipality of Jurbise in Hainaut that handles only around 30 commuters a day. For residents there, the train is the only form of public transport. "If I couldn't take the train, I'd be forced to buy an extra car, which doesn't suit me at all," said Marc, a local resident. "I'm not the only one; there are lots of students who go to school by train, my children go to school by train. In fact, in Masnuy-Saint-Pierre we only have the train, and I can't conceive of there no longer being a single public transport in our village."

## The politics of the 'ghost stations'

The reassurance follows a heated debate over closing lightly used stops. Last year the federal government considered shuttering around twenty stations for budgetary reasons. In April, MR president Georges-Louis Bouchez went further, arguing for closing some sixty stations — those with fewer than 100 boardings per day — saying that for such low numbers, replacement by bus could be managed. A viral social media claim that the MR wanted to close 481 of the country's stations was later debunked by [RTBF's Faky fact-checking unit](https://www.rtbf.be/article/le-mr-propose-de-fermer-60-gares-et-non-481-comme-le-denonce-ecolo-j-voici-lesquelles-11790575) as an extrapolation rather than an actual proposal.

SNCB stresses that the decision to close a station is not a simple matter of ridership figures. "It's not just ridership," said SNCB spokesperson Tom Guillaume. "There's also, for example, the offer available via other public transport. There's also the proximity, or not, of another stop, or the expected demographic evolution. […] Also the financial impact, which is ultimately fairly small — closing a station wouldn't bring the SNCB much money."

That financial argument is central. According to SNCB figures cited by Federal Mobility Minister Jean-Luc Crucke, closing a stop saves only around €20,000 a year per station in maintenance costs — a figure dwarfed by the billions in savings the federal government is seeking. "We are therefore very far from the billions in savings sought at the federal level," Crucke said.

The minister has insisted the federal coalition agreement, which allows stops to be adapted to demand and mentions the "possibility of closing old stops currently deserted by users," does not permit automatic closure based on a single ridership threshold. "I have no taboo," Crucke said. "But the agreement certainly does not provide for automatically removing stops on the basis of a simple ridership threshold."

## What changes from December

The transport plan, approved by the Council of Ministers on 5 June 2026 on Crucke's proposal, is phased over three service years: December 2026, December 2027 and December 2028. Its planned production rises to 88.4 million train-kilometres in 2027, 89.5 million in 2028 and 90.1 million in 2029.

The first phase, [effective from 13 December 2026](https://www.belgiantrain.be/fr/news/new-offer-dec-2026), brings several changes for passengers. SNCB will open up trains that previously ran empty, expanding early-morning and late-evening service at a number of stations. Extra trains will link major cities on weekdays and weekends, with expanded suburban service around Brussels, Antwerp, Liège and Charleroi. The plan also opens the new Braine-Alliance station in December 2026 and, by 2028, merges the Linkebeek and Moensberg stations, subject to Infrabel building a cycle-pedestrian path.

Further phases add extra early-morning trains to Brussels Airport from Bruges, Ghent and Brussels starting in December 2027, arriving as early as 3:30 a.m. and running seven days a week. Air conditioning is to be fitted across the entire fleet by 2032 or 2033, with the first phase beginning on 13 December. The [public service contract](https://news.belgium.be/fr/plan-de-transport-2026-2029-de-la-sncb) targets a 10% increase in the train offer by 2032, after a previous 5% expansion.

## Analysis: the limits of a reprieve

The "no closures" pledge is a significant win for defenders of rural rail, who argue the network is a public service essential to accessibility and decarbonisation, not purely a commercial operation. It also undercuts the fiscal case for closures, since the sums involved are marginal compared with general budget consolidation.

Yet the commitment is a horizon, not a guarantee. The pledge runs only to 2029, and the underlying pressures that sparked the debate — tight federal budgets and a push for commercial speed on intercity lines — have not disappeared. Bouchez has continued to argue that some trains could skip lightly used stops to speed journeys, even without closing stations, leaving room for the argument to resurface.

## What to watch

For now, commuters at Belgium's smallest stops can keep their service. The coming months will test whether the expanded early-morning and late-evening offer actually materialises on the ground, given Infrabel works and rolling-stock constraints. Whether the "no closure" assurance survives the next federal budget round — and the next coalition — remains the open question hanging over the plan.
