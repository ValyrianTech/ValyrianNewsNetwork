# China to Speed 5G Factories, Seed 6G Industrial Research

China will accelerate the buildout of 5G-enabled factories, launch a fresh round of city pilots for "5G+Industrial Internet" applications, and strengthen forward-looking research on integrating 6G technology with industry, the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology (MIIT) said, according to a [report by Xinhua and the Economic Information Daily](https://www.news.cn/20261120/451cc5b65c8d44c8a0f00c16deea86a3/c.html). The plan, announced at the 2026 China 5G+ Industrial Internet Conference held November 18–20 in Wuhan, aims to deepen the fusion of advanced connectivity with manufacturing and position China ahead in next-generation industrial networks.

## Context: A Critical Stage

Beijing frames the initiative as a move from early adoption to scale. "China's '5G+Industrial Internet' is currently at a critical stage of deepening integrated innovation and large-scale promotion and application," the report said, citing the conference. The 2026 gathering marked the seventh edition of what organizers describe as the field's leading event, functioning as a venue for policy release, commercialization, and supply-demand matching.

The push arrives as artificial intelligence reshapes industrial computing. MIIT officials said the rapid fusion of AI and other next-generation technologies with industry is placing higher demands on industrial internet networks, data quality, and platform scheduling — a shift that frames most of the ministry's new agenda.

## Key Developments: What MIIT Will Do

At the Wuhan conference, which opened November 19, MIIT outlined a multi-part program. The ministry will implement a tiered smart-factory cultivation program, support leading enterprises in building "pilot-level" smart factories, and carry out 5G standalone private network trials in an orderly manner. It will accelerate 5G factory construction, start a new batch of "5G+Industrial Internet" integrated application city pilots, and bolster forward-looking research on 6G-industrial integration.

The conference also launched the second batch of nationwide "5G+Industrial Internet" city pilots and debuted a national computing-interconnectivity "1+M+N" industry node, with Hubei serving as a regional node, according to a [conference briefing reported by Phoenix Hubei](https://hb.ifeng.com/c/8xsUKANZ5gg).

## China's Buildup in Numbers

The announcement rests on a substantial existing base. China has built 27,000 industrial 5G private networks, cultivated more than 750 "excellent-level" smart factories, established 49 pilot-level smart factories, and completed 1,260 5G factories, according to the Xinhua report. 5G applications have integrated into all 97 national economic categories, while the Industrial Internet covers all 41 industrial categories, with more than 360 influential platforms whose key systems connect over 100 million industrial devices.

The figures mark an increase from an early-November briefing, which cited more than 26,000 industrial 5G private networks — a sign of continued rapid buildout. At the provincial level, Hubei has built 200,000 5G base stations and 29,000 5G-Advanced base stations, according to the Phoenix briefing, while the Wuhan industrial internet identification top node connects 49 second-level nodes with more than 38 billion registrations.

## The June Policy Anchor

This week's push builds on a June 2026 framework. Eight departments led by MIIT issued an "Implementation Opinion on Promoting High-Quality Development of the Industrial Internet," setting 2030 targets: building 50,000 industrial 5G private networks, creating about five internationally influential comprehensive platforms, and pushing the Industrial Internet core industry's value-added output past 2.5 trillion yuan (about $350 billion). The opinion also made specific deployments for "deepening AI + Industrial Internet integration."

Looking ahead, MIIT said it will pursue intelligent, green, and integrated development, implement an Industrial Internet innovation development project, advance next-generation smart manufacturing, and coordinate network, platform, identification, data, and security systems for large-scale application. It also plans an integrated infrastructure supporting "AI+Manufacturing" that is network-connectable, data-interoperable, resource-shareable, compute-reachable, intelligence-usable, and security-controllable.

## Industry Pivots Toward AI and 6G

Companies at the conference signaled a strategic shift. Speaking to the conference, Xiao Zhe, co-founder of Black Lake Technology, said "5G+Industrial Internet" has entered a stage of small-batch, multi-variety, rapidly changing demand, making flexible response essential — and AI a central solution. "Today AI agents can understand industrial knowledge and participate in core decisions such as drawing decomposition, process planning, and production scheduling," he said, according to the Xinhua report.

Platforms are repositioning accordingly. Inspur Cloud Continent said "AI+Industrial Internet platforms" are becoming the new paradigm for industrial empowerment, having built 15 vertical large models and 43 industry agents. China Telecom is advancing multi-mode industrial cellular networks compatible with 5G, 4G, and industrial WiFi, while China Unicom is extending capabilities into embodied intelligence through its self-developed "Gewu" platform.

Security is emerging as a parallel priority. Qi Xiangdong, chairman of Qianxin Group, said China's Industrial Internet is upgrading from single-point intelligent applications to multi-agent task decomposition and process collaboration, requiring cybersecurity to keep pace. "Integrated security requires IT and OT to advance together to build 'intelligent immunity' for the Industrial Internet," he said.

## Analysis: Seeding the Next Generation

The dual emphasis — scaling 5G factories while funding forward-looking 6G research — signals a deliberate strategy. Rather than chasing generational standards after the fact, Beijing appears intent on applying next-generation networks to industrial scenarios early, seeding standards influence and ecosystem leadership. The June 2030 targets give the buildout a measurable finish line, and the conference's parallel sessions on 5G-Advanced deterministic networks, 6G frontier technology, and satellite communications show the runway extending well beyond current deployments.

The AI dimension is equally significant. As platforms shift from connection-centric infrastructure to value-creation-centric "intelligent hubs," the Industrial Internet becomes a vehicle for deploying large models and agents across manufacturing — a theme organizers placed at the center of the 2026 event.

## What's Next

The near-term test will be execution: which cities join the second batch of pilot programs, how quickly the 5G standalone private network trials scale, and whether the AI-plus-manufacturing architecture delivers on its promise of interoperable, shareable industrial data. The ministry has framed 6G work as forward-looking research rather than near-term deployment, leaving open the timeline and funding for concrete 6G-industrial projects — though the presence of a future-industries 6G fund at the conference signals early capital commitment. With 2026 as the opening year of China's 15th Five-Year Plan, this week's announcements set the tone for a buildout that will unfold over the next decade.
