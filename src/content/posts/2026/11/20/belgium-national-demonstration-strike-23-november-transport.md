# Belgium Braces for National Strike and March on 23 November

Belgium's three main trade unions are calling supporters into the streets of Brussels on Monday 23 November for a national demonstration against the federal "Arizona" government, with public transport operators across all three regions warning of significant disruption. The march, organised by the Christian CSC, the socialist FGTB and the liberal Synova, follows an earlier national demonstration in Namur and aims to press the government over the rising cost of living and its stalled budget negotiations, [RTBF reported](https://www.rtbf.be/article/manifestation-nationale-et-greve-du-23-novembre-a-quoi-s-attendre-dans-les-trains-bus-et-metros-11817872).

## A March, a Strike Notice and a Budget Deadlock

The demonstration, associations, students and pensioners are explicitly invited to join, targets the policies of the federal coalition led by Prime Minister Bart De Wever. The FGTB has filed a strike notice for the day. Unions say they are protesting both the rising cost of living and proposed budget measures; CSC president Ann Vermorgen has singled out the planned removal of public holidays that fall on weekends.

The action lands amid a prolonged budget deadlock. The federal government has still not reached agreement on a budget and is seeking roughly €10 billion in savings, [RTBF reported](https://www.rtbf.be/article/l-absence-d-accord-budgetaire-un-danger-pour-la-dette-belge-le-temps-joue-contre-nous-d-apres-l-economiste-etienne-de-callatay-11816146). After the weekend's negotiations the government still had no budget deal, with the goal remaining to find €10 billion.

A large demonstration gives the day a political charge that extends beyond transport. As the march forms in the capital, the unions are using the gathering to amplify their argument that the burden of consolidation is falling on wages, public services and social provision rather than on wealth and capital.

## Where and When the March Forms

Organisers have set out a clear schedule for the day. Logistics will be set up from 08:30, with a gathering point at Rogier from 11:00. The main march departs from Brussels-North station (Gare du Nord) at 10:00.

The day also carries a cultural dimension. The "Orchestre du Plat Pays" is calling on Belgian musicians to "make heard a music of resistance and solidarity," described as a first for Belgium and modelled on France's "Orchestre du Nouveau Monde," which drew attention at the 17 October demonstration in Paris. The planned programme is a concert of nine songs, including "Bella Ciao" and "Emmenez-moi," along with the Hunger Games anthem performed by the French ensemble, [Le Monde reported](https://www.lemonde.fr/culture/article/2026/10/20/l-orchestre-du-nouveau-monde-un-ensemble-militant-devenu-celebre-apres-la-mobilisation-du-17-octobre_6797854_3246.html).

## What Commuters Should Expect

Public transport is where most people will feel the action. In Brussels, STIB warns of strong disruption because part of its staff will join the action and because the passing of the procession will cause line interruptions and station closures. The operator says it cannot yet forecast the exact scale or which lines will be affected, and that maintained lines will be confirmed on the morning itself, shortly before 06:00, running at reduced frequency. Its Contact Center opens at 06:00 and real-time updates will follow via its website, app and social channels, [STIB said](https://www.stib-mivb.be/voyager/travaux/manifestation-nationale-le-lundi-23-novembre-2026-reseau-de-la-stib-fo). STIB advises passengers to use alternatives wherever possible.

In Wallonia, TEC expects numerous disruptions, with several routes cancelled and some SHOP sales outlets closed. Precise information by region was to be published by 20 November at 16:00 at the latest and updated throughout 23 November, the operator said, pointing travellers to its timetable search and regional network status pages, [TEC said](https://www.letec.be/disruptions/5065c59e-ffa3-45a5-d5eb-08df1ef3ae8e).

In Flanders, De Lijn's unions are taking part in the demonstration, which the operator says will mean fewer bus and tram trips across the whole of Flanders. Alternative service with adapted timetables will be available from the evening of Saturday 21 November, and cancelled trips will show as "ne circule pas" in the route planner, [De Lijn said](https://www.delijn.be/fr/content/storingen-omleidingen/staking-vakbondsactie/).

The national rail operator is the notable exception. No strike is announced at SNCB, and trains are expected to run normally on 23 November, [the federal CGRA notice said](https://www.cgra.be/fr/actualite/action-syndicale-du-23-novembre-les-entretiens-personnels-sont-maintenus).

## Beyond the Transport Networks

The disruption reaches other public services too. Bruxelles-Propreté, the Brussels waste agency, expects its services — notably waste collections — to be affected, warning that many municipalities are potentially concerned while inviting residents and traders to put their bags out as scheduled. Catch-up rounds are planned from Tuesday 24 November depending on available staff, with priority given to white and orange bags, [Bruxelles-Propreté said](https://www.arp-gan.be/fr/actualites/actions-syndicales-ce-lundi-23-novembre-les-services-de-bruxelles-proprete-perturbes).

## The Stakes Behind the March

The demonstration is the sharpest expression yet of the tension between the government's consolidation drive and the social expectations of its coalition partners. The debate over how to close the budget gap — through spending cuts or through taxation of wealth and capital — remains unresolved. Economists are split on the urgency: Louise Lambert of the Centrale Générale has argued that debt fears are overdone, noting the Belgian debt agency raised €8 billion in May against €45 billion of investor demand, while Étienne de Callataÿ of the University of Namur warns that "time is against us", with interest costs crowding out more useful spending. Lambert also argues that €24 billion in savings reduced the deficit by only €4.6 billion.

For commuters, the immediate task is practical: check the operators' apps and route planners, expect reduced or cancelled services on buses, trams and metros, and remember that the trains should still run. For the government, the march is a reminder that the budget arithmetic it is trying to settle will be contested in the streets.

## What to Watch

On the day, watch the turnout and whether it shifts the coalition's room for manoeuvre. In the weeks after, watch whether the budget impasse breaks and on what terms — and whether the unions' grievances over purchasing power and public services translate into further industrial action. The trains, at least, are scheduled to keep running.
