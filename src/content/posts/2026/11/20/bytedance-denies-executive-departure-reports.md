# ByteDance Denies Reports Two Top Executives Will Leave

ByteDance has pushed back on online reports that two of its most prominent executives — Zhang Nan, who runs the company's Jianying and Jimeng businesses, and Xie Xin, who leads the workplace-app unit Feishu — plan to leave the company. A ByteDance official told [The Paper](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_34518185) on November 20, 2026 that the information was "inaccurate." The denial came as Xie personally rejected the claims on his WeChat Moments, calling them "today's so-called exclusive fake news."

The episode draws fresh attention to leadership stability at China's most valuable technology company, which has been reshaping its organizational structure around artificial intelligence through 2026. The reported departures of a founding Douyin figure and the architect of Feishu would have been notable on their own — and their swift rebuttal underscores how closely the market now watches ByteDance's internal changes.

## What was reported — and what the company said

The rumor circulated online as an "exclusive," without a clearly identified original source; The Paper described it only as a claim that had been circulating. In its response, ByteDance gave a brief, generic denial: "The information is inaccurate."

Xie addressed the claims directly and with evident irritation. On WeChat Moments, he wrote that "today's so-called exclusive fake news has wasted a lot of my time replying to everyone" — an explicit denial of his own departure. Zhang Nan, by contrast, had not responded publicly as of press time, leaving her status addressed only through the company's official denial.

## Who the two executives are

Zhang Nan is a foundational figure at Douyin, TikTok's Chinese counterpart. Her photo-community startup was acquired by ByteDance, and her team formally joined the company in 2014. In 2020 she became the first female CEO of Douyin Group, overseeing Douyin, Huoshan, and market and brand functions. In February 2024 she announced on WeChat Moments that she would step down as Douyin Group CEO to focus on Jianying, the video-editing app known internationally as CapCut, and has since led work on AI-assisted creation, including an AI image-and-video generation product called Jimeng.

Xie Xin joined ByteDance early and built the company's HR and efficiency-engineering functions, serving concurrently as HR vice president. He was a core architect of ByteDance's OKR system and its internal collaboration tools. In 2016 he initiated the Lark project that became Feishu, building the product from scratch. When Feishu became one of ByteDance's six major business segments in November 2021, Xie reported directly to CEO Liang Rubo.

## The AI restructuring behind the speculation

The rumors did not emerge in a vacuum. In late July 2026, ByteDance announced an AI-focused organizational adjustment with far-reaching effects on both executives' remits.

Under that reshuffle, Feishu's product team was merged with the Doubao product team into a new Doubao product team led by Zhao Qi — placing Xie Xin under a peer-turned-superior. On the go-to-market side, Feishu's sales and marketing functions were merged with the Volcano Engine team to form a new enterprise-focused "Creativity Service Platform" led by Tan Dai, with Feishu's sales head Lin Chan and its strategy and marketing head Shi Zhijun reporting to him.

The company framed the moves as strengthening coordination across Doubao, Feishu, and Volcano Engine — its model, productivity, and cloud businesses respectively. But reorganizations that shrink an executive's direct product scope often invite speculation about dissatisfaction and departures, and this one did.

Zhang Nan's lower public profile since stepping back from the Douyin CEO role in 2024 has similarly fed questions about her future. She has nonetheless remained active in her AI role, including speaking about talent and AI at a conference in April 2026.

## Analysis: Why the scrutiny matters

ByteDance's central strategic bet in 2026 is AI, spanning model development through Doubao, AI creation tools through Jianying and Jimeng, and enterprise and cloud services through Volcano Engine and Feishu. Consolidating these teams reflects a push toward integrated, AI-driven products for both consumers and businesses.

For a company of ByteDance's scale, executive continuity is a signal. The departures of high-profile leaders — particularly figures as closely tied to flagship products as Zhang and Xie — would raise questions about strategic direction and internal morale. That the company moved quickly to deny the reports, and that Xie rebutted them personally, suggests ByteDance is attentive to how such narratives are read by employees, partners, and investors alike.

The episode also reflects a broader pattern across China's leading tech firms, where leadership changes and reorganizations are parsed intensively as indicators of strategic priority — especially as companies pivot hard toward AI.

## What to watch

Two questions remain open. First, Zhang Nan's silence means her status rests on the company's collective denial rather than her own word; her public statements in the coming weeks will be watched closely. Second, the durability of the July restructuring will be tested as ByteDance integrates its AI product and enterprise-service teams — a process that will determine whether the new reporting lines hold or trigger further changes.

For now, ByteDance's position is clear: the departure reports are inaccurate. Whether that settles the matter may depend on how events unfold inside one of the world's most closely watched technology companies.
