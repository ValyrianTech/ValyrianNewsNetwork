# Anthropic's Record IPO Caps the AI Boom—With Risks

Anthropic, the artificial-intelligence company behind the Claude chatbot, completed the largest stock-market debut in history on Thursday, raising about $81.3 billion and briefly touching a valuation near $2 trillion—a striking endorsement of the AI boom and a high-stakes wager on its future. The company priced its shares at $83 apiece, above the marketed range, and opened on the Nasdaq at $111.50—up 34%—before closing its first day up 17% at $97.17, according to [CNN Business](https://www.cnn.com/2026/11/19/business/anthropic-ipo-stock-test-ai).

The offering surpassed SpaceX's June listing as the biggest ever, and the debut defied weeks of turbulence in technology stocks. Yet beneath the fanfare lies a pointed question, especially for ordinary investors: after the record-breaking first day, should you buy the shares?

## A record deal, by the numbers

Anthropic sold roughly 980 million shares in total, up from an initially planned 825 million, at $83 each—above the anticipated $70 to $78 range, a sign of intense demand. The company itself sold about 956 million shares, while its seven co-founders sold roughly 24 million combined, [CNN reported](https://www.cnn.com/2026/11/18/investing/anthropic-ipo-price-stock).

On gross proceeds, the deal tops the $75 billion SpaceX raised in its initial offering, [CNBC noted](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/18/anthropic-ipo-ai-offering-price.html). When the sale of existing shares by large technology backers—AMD, Broadcom, Google, Microsoft and Nvidia—is included, the total deal value rises to roughly $107.3 billion (about €97.9 billion), as [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/16/anthropic-naar-de-beurs/) detailed. At the offer price, Anthropic carried a fully diluted valuation of about $1.5 trillion—an extraordinary figure for a company founded barely six years ago, in 2021, by Dario Amodei and his sister as a safety-focused alternative to OpenAI.

The listing was also opened unusually wide to the public: Anthropic set aside 20% of the offering for retail, or everyday, investors.

## The engine behind the growth

Anthropic's rise has been powered by its focus on business customers rather than consumers. Its Claude Code tool became an early favorite among software engineers, and the enterprise AI market grew fourfold in a single year—from $33 billion in 2025 to $177 billion in 2026, according to Menlo Ventures. That positioning made the company a darling of the AI trade. Its revenue reached $17 billion in the third quarter of 2026, a roughly twelvefold year-over-year increase, and it counts about 14,000 customers, roughly 250 of whom spend $10 million or more a year.

"They're going to just continue to embed them in more and more applications, and that makes it a very sticky business," Lauren Cassidy, chief investment officer of Founder ETFs, told CNN.

But the boom carries a heavy bill. Anthropic has committed to spend roughly $900 billion (about €820 billion) on computing power and infrastructure in the coming years—a cost burden that even a soaring revenue curve must eventually justify.

## The case for caution

For KBC financial economist Tom Simonts, the central risk is that Anthropic's business model is far less proven than that of the company it just surpassed. "The big difference is that SpaceX already has a business model thanks to its Starlink satellites," he told VRT. "At AI companies like Anthropic, above all huge investments are needed right now, while it's still uncertain what profits will replace them."

Simonts notes that big IPOs tend to be overvalued at debut, with many of the ten largest US listings of 2026 trading about 20% below their offer price twelve months later. "Anthropic is not doing badly, but it is a risky investment in any case," he said.

Much now depends on whether Anthropic can sustain its breakneck pace as a public company. Its growth is concentrated in enterprise AI, where it holds nearly 45% of US business spending against OpenAI's roughly 40%, according to Ramp—but in consumer AI, OpenAI's ChatGPT dwarfs Claude, with 1.4 billion monthly active users versus 124 million, per Sensor Tower. OpenAI is expected to go public in 2027, and cheaper open models pose a further threat to margins.

"The coding agents powered this growth," Ara Kharazian, lead economist at Ramp, told CNN. "And we kind of need to figure out what the next front is going to be."

The pressures of public life are immediate. "You go from hardly any scrutiny to everything you do, there's an analyst revaluating," said Ken Mahoney, president of Mahoney Asset Management. Even Nvidia, Google and Amazon have seen shares stumble when results merely met expectations.

The debut also came against an unsettled backdrop: the tech-heavy Nasdaq had fallen more than 10% from its record high, Nvidia was down 30% in the prior month, and markets were still absorbing the resignation of Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang.

## What to watch

Anthropic's IPO answers one question—that public markets are willing to pay dearly for a stake in the AI future—while leaving the harder ones open. Can the company convert runaway revenue growth into durable profit against a near-trillion-dollar infrastructure bill? Will its lead in enterprise AI survive intensifying competition from OpenAI and cheaper rivals? And how long can the broader AI trade withstand the volatility that has already rattled the sector?

For now, the record books have been rewritten. The task that follows—proving the valuation was deserved—is the one that will define Anthropic's next chapter.
