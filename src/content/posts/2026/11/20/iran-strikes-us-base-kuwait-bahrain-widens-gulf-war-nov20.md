# Iran Widens Gulf Attacks, Striking US Base in Kuwait

Iran widened its military operations across the Persian Gulf overnight, targeting a US air base in Kuwait and launching a series of attacks on sites in Bahrain, in what appeared to be retaliation for an American strike the day before. The escalation ended a roughly two-month lull in strikes on US facilities in the region and intensified fears that the nine-month war could spiral into a wider confrontation.

The attacks came as Middle Eastern allies accelerated a diplomatic push to prevent a return to all-out war, amid mounting pressure from Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu on the Trump administration to relaunch a joint air campaign against Iran, according to [The Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/iran-us-kuwait-gulf-attacks-bahrain-b1f0b530cffe951e6fcc1e27b2e1af94).

## A Tit-for-Tat Exchange in the Gulf

The early-morning attacks followed a US fighter jet strike on Tuesday against a Revolutionary Guard missile launch site on Qeshm Island in the Strait of Hormuz. That US strike was itself a response to an Iranian attack earlier the same day on an American radar installation in Bahrain, the AP reported.

Iran said its recent attacks — including those on other US facilities in Bahrain on Sunday and the preceding Friday — were retaliation for US drone surveillance operations and an attack on Iranian communications towers.

In the strike on Ali Al Salem air base in Kuwait, the Iranian military fired at air-defense systems and drone and missile launch platforms, according to a report carried on Iran's state broadcaster. Kuwait's military said its air defenses intercepted the drone attacks and did not immediately report any damage or casualties. The Kuwaiti Foreign Ministry condemned the repeated Iranian attacks as "criminal," saying they threatened the country's security and stability. Bahrain's military said it intercepted a number of attacks from Iran but gave no information on damage or casualties.

The base has been struck at least 30 times by Iran over the course of the war, damaging or destroying at least 15 aircraft hangars, numerous warehouses and 10 buildings near a residential area, according to an analysis by The Associated Press.

## Diplomacy Intensifies as Israel Pushes for Action

Government and security officials from the Persian Gulf countries, as well as Pakistan and Turkey, have engaged in concerted diplomacy — including phone calls with senior US officials — in a bid to avert renewed war, according to four regional officials briefed on the matter who spoke on condition of anonymity because they were not authorized to comment publicly.

The goal of the diplomatic push is to counter Netanyahu's efforts for the US to restart the war or allow Israel to launch airstrikes on Iran's nuclear facilities and missile capabilities, the officials said. Regional allies have been concerned that a recent visit to the US by Israel's Mossad chief, Roman Gofman, would tilt President Donald Trump's thinking in favor of restarting the war.

Gofman, who is close to Netanyahu, met with CIA Director John Ratcliffe in Washington before traveling to Miami for a meeting with US envoy Steve Witkoff, according to two of the regional officials. A person familiar with the situation described the visit as routine and covering a wide range of topics, including Iran and the question of military aid; the CIA declined to comment.

A US official said the Trump administration's campaign of economic pressure on Iran has been effective enough that Trump does not need to restart major combat operations, adding that Washington remains in close contact with its Gulf allies. The commander of US Central Command, Adm. Brad Cooper, met Wednesday in Tel Aviv with Israel's military chief of staff, Eyal Zamir, and Gofman.

## Calibrated Escalation or a Slide Toward War?

With Iran's economy suffering under a US naval blockade and American military escorts helping restore the passage of some oil tankers through the Strait of Hormuz, analysts say the recent attacks may signal that Tehran is growing weary of the status quo.

"Even if neither side intends to enter a broader confrontation, the accumulation of military incidents across multiple fronts increases the risk of miscalculation and escalation beyond what either side originally intended," Hamidreza Azizi, a senior Iran analyst at the International Crisis Group, wrote on X.

Danny Citrinowicz, a researcher at Israel's Institute for National Security Studies and a former Iran specialist in Israel's military and intelligence services, described an increasingly clear pattern of attacks by Iran on shipping and US facilities in the Gulf. "Iran appears to be pursuing a strategy of deliberate, calibrated escalation," he wrote. "The objective is not necessarily to trigger an all-out war, but to steadily increase the military, economic, and political costs of maintaining the blockade."

Trump has sent mixed messages about his next steps, saying at one point that new strikes are highly unlikely while also recently saying the military was "locked and loaded."

The maritime toll continued to mount. The United Arab Emirates' state-owned oil company ADNOC said Wednesday that one of its tanker crew members was missing after an attack in the strait Tuesday, while 11 others were injured and receiving medical care. Though US military protection has helped restore oil shipments from the Gulf, Iran has continued to attack tankers attempting to transit the strait.

## A War in Its Ninth Month

The exchange is the latest chapter in a conflict that began on February 28, when the United States and Israel launched a joint surprise offensive against Iran. Tehran retaliated with waves of missiles and drones across the region, targeting US embassies and military installations and striking oil infrastructure and commercial shipping. Iran also effectively closed the Strait of Hormuz — through which roughly a fifth of the world's oil passes — sending shockwaves through the global economy.

Earlier ceasefire attempts, including a Pakistan-brokered truce in April and a memorandum of understanding in June, collapsed by midsummer, after which Washington reimposed its naval blockade. In July, Yemen's Iran-backed Houthi movement stepped up attacks on Red Sea shipping, opening a second front.

Saudi officials have briefed allies that the conflict with the Houthis could last another six months, according to a diplomat familiar with the matter. The kingdom has argued that US military support could help change the course of the war in the Saudi-led coalition's favor, but Trump has so far resisted calls for US strikes on the Houthis.

## What to Watch

The coming days will test whether Gulf, Pakistani and Turkish diplomacy can produce a renewed ceasefire, or whether the pattern of calibrated strikes spirals into a miscalculation neither side intends. Much may hinge on whether Trump authorizes a renewed air campaign — or permits Israeli strikes on Iran's nuclear and missile sites — as Netanyahu reportedly urges. With the Strait of Hormuz still contested and a second maritime front simmering in the Red Sea, the economic and human costs of the war continue to mount.
