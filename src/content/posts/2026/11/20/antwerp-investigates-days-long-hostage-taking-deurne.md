# Antwerp Investigates Days-Long Hostage-Taking of 32-Year-Old

The Antwerp public prosecutor's office has opened a judicial investigation into the days-long hostage-taking of a 32-year-old man from the Antwerp district of Deurne, who was abducted last weekend and found alive and in good health on Wednesday evening. The case has drawn attention for its resemblance to the pattern of drug-related reprisal abductions that have repeatedly shaken the port city.

The investigation is being conducted jointly by the Antwerp prosecutor's office and the Federal Judicial Police of Antwerp (FGP Antwerpen), which is examining both the circumstances of the abduction and its motive, according to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/20/antwerpenaar-dagenlang-gegijzeld/).

## How the abduction unfolded

According to witness accounts reported by Het Laatste Nieuws and picked up by [MSN/Belga](https://www.msn.com/nl-be/nieuws/nationaal/ontvoerde-antwerpenaar-na-dagenlange-gijzeling-teruggevonden/ar-AA2hQijN), the man was overpowered near his home in Deurne-Noord in the night of Saturday to Sunday and dragged by force into a black car by three masked men. The perpetrators then drove off, and the man was not heard from again for several days.

He resurfaced on Wednesday evening, when he was found alive. "According to the prosecutor's office, the man is in good health," VRT NWS reported. It remains unclear why he was kidnapped and by whom; as of the reporting, no suspects had been arrested.

"The investigation into the concrete circumstances and into the cause is being conducted at full pace by FGP Antwerpen," said Kristof Aerts, spokesperson for the Antwerp prosecutor's office. "In the context of that investigation, no further information can currently be shared."

## Suspected drug-milieu motive

The prosecutor has declined to confirm a motive, but reporting suggests the abduction may be tied to the disappearance of a large shipment of cocaine from the Port of Antwerp. According to Het Laatste Nieuws, the kidnappers reportedly held the victim responsible for the disappearance of the cocaine, which he was supposed to have retrieved from the port. The reported quantity varies between outlets, with one account citing just over a tonne and MSN/Belga citing 1.5 tonnes.

That narrative fits a well-documented model within Antwerp's drug economy. The Port of Antwerp-Bruges is one of Europe's primary entry points for cocaine, and the surrounding criminal milieu has seen repeated incidents of intimidation, violence, and abduction. Victims are sometimes held for days to extract information or money, or to punish those blamed for lost or seized shipments — a pattern that makes the suspected link to a missing cocaine consignment plausible, though it remains officially unconfirmed.

## What authorities have and haven't said

The prosecutor's office has confirmed only the broad outlines: that an investigation is underway, that the man is in good health, and that no suspects have been taken into custody. Officials have given no indication of the victim's role, whether he was known to police, or what specific charges might follow. That reticence is typical in the early phase of a drug-related probe, where disclosing detail could compromise the investigation.

## What to watch for

The central open questions are who carried out the abduction and whether investigators will establish a firm motive. FGP Antwerpen's next steps — including any arrests or charges of unlawful deprivation of liberty, drug trafficking, or participation in a criminal organization — will shape how the case develops.

Beyond this single incident, the case feeds a broader concern about the spillover of drug-trade violence into Antwerp's residential neighborhoods. Deurne-Noord is not a port district, yet an abduction there unfolded in the open, near the victim's home. Whether this proves an isolated reprisal or part of a wider escalation will depend on what the investigation uncovers in the coming weeks.
