# Vandemoortele to Close Seneffe Donut Plant, 126 Jobs at Risk

Food group Vandemoortele announced on Friday its intention to halt mini-donut production at its plant in Seneffe, in the Walloon province of Hainaut, and close the site — putting 126 jobs at risk. Management cited a "price war," a 16 percent drop in sales and persistent overcapacity, and said production would be shifted to the group's facility in Reims, France, according to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/20/vandemoortele-wil-fabriek-voor-minidonuts-in-seneffe-sluiten-12/).

The announcement was made during an extraordinary works council meeting on the morning of 20 November, launching the information and consultation procedure with employee representatives — known in Belgium as the "Renault procedure" — that must run its course before any closure is confirmed.

## Why the plant is being singled out

Vandemoortele says competition in the mini-donut market has intensified sharply. "Competition in the mini-donut market has increased considerably," HR director and communications chief Marc Croonen told VRT NWS, describing a genuine price war that has steadily weakened the Seneffe site.

The financial picture, as [RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/vandemoortele-envisage-la-fermeture-de-son-site-de-seneffe-126-emplois-menaces-11818451) reported, is one of declining volumes and rising costs: sales fell by 16 percent in recent years, production is now marked by overcapacity, and operating costs have kept climbing. Management notes that 13 million euros were invested over the past eight years to modernise infrastructure, improve quality and train staff. "But that has not helped," Croonen conceded.

"The current situation pushes us to reduce costs drastically in order to reposition our products at a more competitive price level, essential in a market under strong pressure," said Claude Jacqmin, the site's interim director.

## Shock and a dispute over transparency

The decision landed hard on a workforce that, according to union representatives, had been given no warning. "Nothing suggested such a decision! ... We are in a state of shock, we don't understand at all!" said Omar Adian, a worker and union delegate.

Disclosure is now the central point of contention. Unions say management had recently spoken of continued operations and even announced future investments, including safety improvements, as recently as two weeks earlier. "If the sales declines were so significant, no one told us. There is at a minimum a transparency problem!" said Aicha Sayah, permanent secretary of the Christian union CSC.

"The staff is in shock. Some have spent their entire career there," added Gaëlle Cranenbroucke of the socialist union Setca/FGTB. She said the unions would meet management "as a united front" and press their questions, arguing that "not all solutions have been considered."

## A third blow to Seneffe in one year

The closure would be the third major setback for employment in Seneffe this year. It follows the planned shutdown at agrochemical firm Syngenta and the total cessation of activity at Alysse Food, which announced in August that it would close, threatening 108 jobs. Mayor Bénédicte Poll estimates that nearly 500 jobs will have been lost in the commune this year alone. "This is the third announcement of this kind this year, with nearly 500 jobs lost. That's a lot," she told [L'Avenir](https://www.lavenir.net/regions/mons-centre/seneffe/2026/11/20/126-postes-menaces-chez-vandemoortele-a-seneffe-OLIM5KMPTVGIDAV6HCNIBV3FXI/).

Poll has said she will respect the Renault procedure before convening the intercommunal economic development agency to examine options for reconverting the site.

## Part of a wider Belgian pattern

The Vandemoortele case fits a broader wave of industrial and logistics restructuring across Hainaut in 2026. Earlier this year, H&M Logistics announced the end of activities at its Ghlin distribution centre, threatening 440 jobs; Husqvarna put 56 jobs at risk in Ghislenghien; and Air Liquide opened a Renault procedure amid staff strikes. The cumulative effect is mounting pressure on a region already navigating a difficult industrial transition.

## What happens next

Vandemoortele says it will pursue a "constructive dialogue" with social partners to find "adequate solutions" for the 126 workers, with redeployment within the company and outplacement among the options on the table. A new meeting between management and unions is expected before the end of the year.

The key questions remain open: whether the consultation can produce alternatives to outright closure, what terms the workers will secure, and whether the shift of production to Reims represents a net transfer of jobs from Belgium to France. For Seneffe, the immediate test is whether a third closure in a single year can still be averted — or at least softened.
