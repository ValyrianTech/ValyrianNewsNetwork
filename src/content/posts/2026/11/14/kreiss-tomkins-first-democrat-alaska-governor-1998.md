# Kreiss-Tomkins Elected First Democrat Alaska Gov Since 1998

Alaska voters have elected a Democrat as governor for the first time in nearly three decades, choosing former state lawmaker Jonathan Kreiss-Tomkins in a victory that reflects a broader midterm wave lifting Democrats into statehouses nationwide, [The Associated Press reported](https://apnews.com/article/alaska-governor-democrat-midterms-c7e7176b233d5078e5c297da639e4471).

Kreiss-Tomkins, 37 and widely known as "JKT," defeated three Republicans — including one endorsed by President Donald Trump — to become the first Democrat elected Alaska governor since Tony Knowles in 1998. His win means Democrats will control the governor's office in 30 states, including several they flipped after years of Republican dominance in places such as Iowa, Georgia, Ohio and Texas.

## A Political Comeback

Kreiss-Tomkins completed a political comeback three years after leaving the state House, where he represented the 35th district from 2013 to 2023. He campaigned on affordability, full funding of public schools, and a corporate income tax on all oil and gas producers. He also called for a moratorium on new data centers until state lawmakers pass regulations governing their development.

He argued that Alaska had become "broken" under eight years of outgoing Republican Gov. Mike Dunleavy, who was term-limited and ineligible for a third consecutive term. Among the problems he cited was a state ferry system he said had been left "in shambles," with routes to many communities cut drastically. The ferry is a vital transportation link for southeast Alaska communities not connected by roads.

Throughout the campaign, Kreiss-Tomkins emphasized his willingness to work across party lines, pledging a Cabinet that would include Republicans, Democrats and independents.

"If they're competent, if they're service-minded, if they're entrepreneurial, if they're problem-solvers, you're on the team. Game on," he said at a candidate forum last month. "Let's go, and let's get Alaska back on track."

## A Coalition of Unlikely Endorsers

Perhaps the most striking feature of the race was the coalition that rallied behind the Democrat. Republican U.S. Sen. Lisa Murkowski, who has frequently clashed with Trump, endorsed Kreiss-Tomkins a week before Election Day.

She said Kreiss-Tomkins "understands that governing requires listening to people with different perspectives and finding common ground to solve the problems all of us face."

Three former governors also backed him: Democrat Tony Knowles, independent Bill Walker and Republican Frank Murkowski, the senator's father.

In a statement declaring victory, Kreiss-Tomkins said voters wanted change and "a government that works across party lines to move Alaska forward. Our administration will not waste a single day getting to work to solve problems for Alaska." He noted his campaign had drawn more than 10,000 donors.

## Ranked-Choice Voting's Role

Kreiss-Tomkins defeated Republicans Bernadette Wilson, a business owner Trump had endorsed; Dave Bronson, a former mayor of Anchorage; and Treg Taylor, a former state attorney general under Dunleavy. Because Kreiss-Tomkins cleared an outright majority on the first-choice ballot, Alaska's ranked-choice voting feature did not ultimately decide the outcome — a twist in a state where many Republicans had viewed ranked-choice tabulation as their best hope of retaining the governorship.

That view put them at odds with Trump, who has repeatedly criticized Alaska's unusual voting system, including during a rally in the state in October. Bronson congratulated Kreiss-Tomkins on social media and wished him success.

Kreiss-Tomkins finished first in August's open primary, ahead of a second Democrat, Tom Begich, who later withdrew to consolidate Democratic support. Wilson finished third in the primary.

The governor-elect said compromise "is a virtue, not a dirty word," describing the mission of the new administration as working with lawmakers to resolve longstanding problems in the state.

## Part of a National Shift

The Alaska result is one piece of a striking Democratic resurgence in state governments during the 2026 midterms. According to [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/2026-election/democrats-flip-governorships-extending-party-inroads-red-states-rcna607010), Democrats clinched a majority of governorships for the first time in 16 years, flipping seats in Iowa, Georgia, Texas, Ohio and Nevada.

As [NBC News reported](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/2026-election/jonathan-kreiss-tomkins-democrat-wins-alaska-governor-race-rcna606558) on the Alaska race, the win marks a new era of Democratic leadership across the states. Georgia and Alaska had not elected a Democratic governor since 1998, and Iowa and Ohio since 2006 — underscoring the depth of the party's gains in 2026.

The Democratic Governors Association framed the results as a turnaround. "Democratic governors and the DGA pulled off this amazing turnaround by competing and winning everywhere, staying focused on the biggest issues impacting Americans every day, and always standing up for our values," DGA Chair and Kentucky Gov. Andy Beshear said, according to NBC News.

## What's Next

At 37, Kreiss-Tomkins will be sworn in on Dec. 7. He has described his administration as one that will emphasize competence over party affiliation and focus on the practical problems facing Alaskans — from energy costs and the ferry system to fisheries protection.

His running mate is Zac Johnson, an independent and Anchorage Assembly member, who becomes lieutenant governor. Whether Kreiss-Tomkins can translate his bipartisan campaign coalition into durable governing majorities in a state long dominated by Republicans will be the central test of his tenure — and a case study for Democrats seeking to consolidate their 2026 momentum heading into future cycles.
