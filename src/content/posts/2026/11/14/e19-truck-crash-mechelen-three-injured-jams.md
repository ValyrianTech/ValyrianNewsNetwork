# E19 Truck Crash Near Mechelen Injures Three, Long Jams

Three people were injured on Saturday morning when a small truck struck a crash-attenuator vehicle protecting a mobile roadworks site on the E19 motorway near Mechelen-Zuid, sending the vehicle across the carriageway and triggering long traffic jams along one of Belgium's busiest intercity routes. The road has since been cleared and residual delays are easing.

## What Happened

According to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/14/files-e19-ongeval-vrachtwagen/), the crash occurred at around 9:30 a.m. when a small truck driving toward Antwerp ran into the "botsabsorbeerder" — a specialized crash-attenuator vehicle deployed to shield a mobile works crew. The force of the impact flung the truck to the other side of the motorway, where it collided with a passenger car and a second truck.

Three people were taken to hospital. [RTV](https://www.rtv.be/actualiteit-en-nieuws/bestuurder-zwaargewond-bij-zwaar-verkeersongeval-op-e19-mechelen-zuid) reported, citing the federal road police, that the van driver was critically injured and that two occupants of the car were also hurt. No information was immediately available on the condition of the lorry driver.

## Major Disruption During Morning Commute

[HLN](https://www.hln.be/mechelen/drie-gewonden-nadat-kleine-vrachtwagen-tegen-botsabsorbeerder-rijdt-op-e19-in-mechelen-zuid~a8c83811/) reported that the small truck was catapulted onto the right-hand lane in the collision, which left the E19 toward Antwerp seriously restricted through much of the morning.

"Er is slechts een beperkte doorgang en grote verkeershinder" — there is only limited passage and major traffic disruption — said Peter Bruyninckx of the Vlaams Verkeerscentrum, the Flemish traffic authority, as quoted by VRT NWS.

The cleanup proved to be a bottleneck in its own right. "Er is ook veel olie of brandstof op het wegdek gelopen. Dat moet eerst opgeruimd worden" — a lot of oil or fuel leaked onto the road surface, which had to be cleaned up first — the traffic centre said.

Long queues built up between Brussels and Antwerp, and drivers seeking alternate routes created secondary congestion on smaller roads between Zemst and Mechelen. The picture was complicated further by the Rupeltunnel on the A12, which was closed in both directions for scheduled works, prompting the traffic centre to advise motorists heading to Antwerp to avoid that route.

By early afternoon the motorway had been fully reopened once recovery and clean-up operations were complete, and residual delays had shrunk to roughly 15 minutes.

## Why It Matters

The E19 corridor linking Brussels with the port city of Antwerp is among the most strategically important stretches of motorway in Belgium, carrying heavy volumes of both commuter and freight traffic. Disruption there quickly ripples outward across the regional road network — as Saturday's cascading jams demonstrated when combined with the A12 closure.

The crash also highlights the persistent danger of mobile roadworks zones on high-speed motorways. The crash-attenuator vehicle did its job of shielding the works crew, but the force involved — enough to throw a light truck into adjacent lanes — underscores how high-energy such rear-end collisions can be. The cause of the crash remained under investigation, and it was not immediately clear whether following distance, signage or visibility of the works zone played a role.

## What to Watch

With one of the injured reported to be in a critical condition, the incident may attract closer scrutiny of safety arrangements around mobile works sites on Flemish motorways. Questions also remain over the full extent of the closure's knock-on effects and how the simultaneous A12 works compounded congestion on the Antwerp-bound network.
