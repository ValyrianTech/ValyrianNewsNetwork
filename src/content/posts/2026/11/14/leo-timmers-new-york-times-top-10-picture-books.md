# Flemish Illustrator Leo Timmers Makes NYT Top 10 Again

The English edition of *Kiki & Me*, illustrated by the Flemish children's author and illustrator Leo Timmers, has been named one of the 10 Best Illustrated Children's Books of 2026 by the New York Times and the New York Public Library. It is the second time the Limburg-based artist has appeared on the prestigious annual list, following his 2019 selection for the wordless picture book *Monkey on the Run*. Timmers is due to travel to New York the week after the announcement for the award ceremony.

## A once-in-a-lifetime honour, twice over

The recognition, [reported by VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/14/leo-timmers-kiki-en-ik-beste-kinderboeken-wereldwijd-nyt/), caught the illustrator off guard. Timmers said his US publisher phoned to ask whether he had a moment to talk, and that although he follows the November announcement every year, he never expected to appear on the list again.

"In 2019 I thought it would be a once-in-a-lifetime experience, but here we are," Timmers said. "It's unlikely that this happens a second time. Last time I really thought it was a once-in-a-lifetime experience."

The honour carries particular weight given its history. The New York Times and the New York Public Library have jointly published the list annually since 1952, honouring ten illustrators whose work is seen as igniting a love of reading in children. When Timmers was first selected for *Monkey on the Run*, he became [the first Flemish illustrator ever to make the list](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2019/11/01/engelse-versie-van-aap-op-straat-door-new-york-times-verkozen/).

## A book drawn from childhood

*Kiki & Me* (originally *Kiki & ik*, published in Dutch by Querido) tells the story of a young girl, Kiki, and her horse, Polly, narrated from the horse's point of view. The two are inseparable as they explore the world together and grow up, but change inevitably comes. The book is structurally unusual: it alternates between intimate black-and-white pen-and-ink drawings and vast, colourful acrylic landscapes spread across double pages, with the characters rendered very small against nature.

According to [Flanders Literature](https://www.flandersliterature.be/books-and-authors/book/kiki-me), which promotes Flemish literature abroad, the book is "his most personal yet." The New York Times praised Timmers for alternating "between colourfully painted, wordless double-page spreads, in which the duo traverses beautiful landscapes," noting that his "black-and-white pen-and-ink style emphasises the expressiveness of the horse narrator and the smaller moments that form the lasting bond between Kiki and her horse."

In an [interview with the School Library Journal](https://afuse8production.slj.com/2026/02/25/its-a-belgian-cover-reveal-day-a-talk-with-leo-timmers-about-kiki-me/), Timmers traced the story to his own upbringing. "The story is so personal because it is rooted in my own childhood. My father bought me a horse when I was about ten years old, and many of the scenes in the book are drawn directly from my own experiences," he said. He added that watching his two daughters grow up "has inevitably seeped into this story as well. I think I needed to reach a certain age to be able to tell this story."

## Two years of doubt, and a new technique

Timmers said the book took roughly two years to complete and pushed him well outside his comfort zone. "It's really two books in one. The first part is drawn in black and white with pen, then follow large landscapes painted in acrylic. They are two styles I had to make my own," he told VRT NWS. "I had to learn to draw and hatch with a pen properly."

That labour earned the book the Zilveren Penseel (Silver Pencil) 2026, a leading Dutch-language illustration prize, as well as the Kinderbuchpreis Illustration 2026. Rights have been sold in more than a dozen languages, from English and German to Korean and Japanese, per Flanders Literature.

Timmers has published children's books since 1999 and has released around 20 titles, which have been translated into more than 40 languages. He credits the New York Times recognition with opening international doors. "Such an award opens doors internationally. Since the first award in 2019, many smaller countries have also taken the risk of translating my work," he said.

## What it means

The second listing underscores the international reach of Flemish picture-book makers, an area the regional agency Flanders Literature actively supports through translation grants and rights assistance. For Timmers, the honour is less a culmination than a mandate to keep trusting his instincts. "This is real confirmation for me, especially when you try to do something different in every book. It encourages me to keep making my own choices," he said.

A follow-up book is in the works, though Timmers said it will take time: he is travelling widely for his translated titles and finishing a large mural design, and he works entirely by hand. "There's nothing like making pictures with pencil and paint on paper," he said.

Timmers will accept the award at a ceremony in New York in the coming week. Whether the list brings a third appearance remains an open question, but for now the Flemish illustrator stands among the most acclaimed children's book makers in the world.
