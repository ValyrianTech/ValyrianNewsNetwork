# Franco-Belgian Arms Ring Busted in the Ardennes, Nine Held

French and Belgian authorities have dismantled a cross-border firearms trafficking network operating in the Ardennes, arresting nine people and seizing a substantial arsenal of 178 firearms and 370 kilograms of ammunition, the prosecutor's office of Charleville-Mezières announced on Friday. The coordinated operation, carried out on Tuesday 10 November, underscores growing concern in Western Europe about arms trafficking feeding into drug networks and organized crime.

According to [RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/une-filiere-franco-belge-de-trafic-d-armes-demantelee-dans-les-ardennes-neuf-personnes-interpellees-11815544), the operation was a "large-scale judicial action" conducted in coordination between French and Belgian authorities. Among the nine arrested were eight French nationals and one Belgian national, detained by the federal judicial police (Police judiciaire federale) of Namur province.

## Indictments and Pre-Trial Status

Eight of the nine suspects were brought before an examining magistrate at the judicial court of Charleville-Mezières on Friday 13 November and formally indicted. As [ICI](https://www.ici.fr/grand-est/ardennes-08/huit-personnes-deferees-soupconnees-de-trafic-d-armes-entre-les-ardennes-et-la-belgique-annonce-le-parquet-3796224) reported, four of the eight were placed in pre-trial detention and four under judicial supervision. The Belgian national will be presented to the Belgian justice system at a later date.

The public prosecutor of Charleville-Mezières, Magali Josse, revealed the case in a press release on Friday evening. The charges cover the acquisition, possession, transport and transfer — as an organized group — of war weapons or ammunition in categories A and B, as well as money laundering and criminal association.

## How the Investigation Unfolded

The case began in autumn 2025, when the prosecutor's office was alerted to the "suspicious behaviour" of a person who was "regularly carrying out shootings with firearms" in the Sedan area, the second-largest town in the French Ardennes department. A preliminary investigation was opened and entrusted to the gendarmerie's Section de recherches de Reims and the brigade de recherches de Sedan.

Investigators soon uncovered the existence of an arms trafficking network with links across the border. In April 2026, a judicial investigation was opened against persons unknown once connections with Belgium were established. Tuesday's operation, conducted with the Namur federal judicial police, then led to the arrests and searches in both countries, which also turned up a vehicle belonging to one of the suspects.

## A Worrying Trend

Prosecutor Josse linked the case to a broader pattern of rising weapons crime. "Crime statistics reveal a worrying rise in this type of criminality," she said, as quoted by [France 3 Grand Est](https://france3-regions.franceinfo.fr/grand-est/ardennes/charleville-mezieres/un-trafic-d-armes-demantele-178-armes-et-370-kilos-de-munitions-saisis-3449532.html). "Weapons thefts, including from gun shops, have been increasing for several years, reflecting the growing organization of these criminal networks — weapons that subsequently feed networks linked to drug trafficking as well as organized crime."

The scale of the seizure — 178 firearms and 370 kilograms of ammunition — points to a wholesale-level distribution operation rather than incidental possession. The charges refer explicitly to "war weapons," suggesting the network handled military-grade material alongside category B firearms subject to authorization.

## Analysis: A Cross-Border Problem

The Ardennes straddle the France–Belgium border, with the French department adjacent to Belgium's Namur and Luxembourg provinces. The operation illustrates the practical reality that weapons and criminal networks move freely across the Schengen internal border — and that dismantling them requires coordinated action on both sides.

The case also fits a wider European pattern. Belgium in particular has faced gun-related violence linked to drug networks flowing through the port of Antwerp and cross-border trafficking routes. Arms diverted from thefts or reactivated from deactivated stock circulate through these regional criminal markets, and the Charleville-Mezières case suggests the Ardennes corridor is part of that circuit.

## What's Next

Investigations are continuing, with a focus on establishing the provenance and traceability of the 178 seized weapons — a key open question. Authorities have not yet said whether the firearms were stolen from armouries, smuggled, reactivated from deactivated weapons, or drawn from military surplus.

On the Belgian side, the federal judicial police of Namur and the Belgian justice system are expected to handle the prosecution of the Belgian national separately. Whether the Belgian federal prosecutor's office issues its own statement, and whether further arrests or seizures follow as complementary investigations proceed, remain to be seen. For now, the operation stands as one of the larger arms-trafficking busts on the Franco-Belgian border in recent years — and a reminder that the trade in illicit weapons is deeply intertwined with the region's drug economy.
