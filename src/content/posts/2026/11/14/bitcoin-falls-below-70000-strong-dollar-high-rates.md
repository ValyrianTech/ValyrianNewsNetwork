# Bitcoin Falls Below $70,000 as Strong Dollar, High Rates Bite

Bitcoin tumbled below the $70,000 mark on Friday, its lowest level since August, as a resurgent US dollar and expectations of further Federal Reserve interest-rate hikes pushed investors out of riskier assets. The world's largest cryptocurrency dropped to around $68,400 — roughly €63,200 — while Ethereum slid below $2,000 to trade near $1,920, according to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/13/waarde-van-bitcoin-duikt-onder-kaap-van-70-000-dollar/).

The move rippled across the crypto market. BNB fell about 6.3%, Solana roughly 7.7% and XRP some 3.5%, as a broad risk-off shift gripped global markets.

## Why a strong dollar hurts bitcoin

The roots of the selloff lie in US monetary policy. American inflation has remained stubbornly high, driven by elevated energy prices, and Tuesday's consumer price index came in hotter than economists had expected. That has reinforced bets that the Federal Reserve will raise rates again to bring prices under control.

Higher rates make it more attractive to hold dollars, particularly in US Treasuries, which are seen as safe havens. That demand pushes up the value of the American currency. As VRT NWS explained, the dollar index — which measures the greenback against six major currencies — climbed above 103 points, its highest level since April 2025, while the yield on the 10-year Treasury note rose to around 5.15%.

The dollar and bitcoin have long moved in inverse proportion. A stronger dollar makes dollar-denominated crypto more expensive for foreign buyers, and rising real yields raise the opportunity cost of holding a non-yielding asset like bitcoin. Cryptocurrencies tend to thrive when money is cheap and risk appetite is strong — conditions that are now reversing.

## A leverage cascade

The decline was amplified by a forced unwinding of leveraged positions. According to [CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/11/13/bitcoin-hits-12-week-low-as-dollar-rallies-on-interest-rate-outlook-liquidations-top-usd1-6-billion), liquidations topped $1.6 billion over 24 hours, with the vast majority in long positions — traders who had bet on higher prices and were forced out as the market fell. As Rick Maeda, head of markets at Laevitas, put it, once bitcoin broke the key $70,000 level, the long positions clustered below that level were forced out quickly.

The selloff was compounded by turmoil in technology stocks. Global equities had already suffered heavy losses after the US midterm elections, with tech shares hit hardest on fears of tighter regulation. Sentiment worsened after Jensen Huang resigned as CEO of Nvidia amid a spy-relationship investigation, wiping roughly $1.9 trillion from the chipmaker's market value and casting a shadow over index funds and tech-heavy portfolios.

## More pain ahead?

The pressure may not be over. Markets have priced in a near-certain 25-basis-point rate hike at the Fed's 9 December meeting, and some traders are weighing the risk of a 50-basis-point move. Jeff Mei, chief operating officer of BTSE, warned that if the Fed goes bigger than expected, bitcoin and other cryptocurrencies could have more room to fall.

Adding to the drag, US spot bitcoin exchange-traded funds have recorded net outflows for several consecutive sessions, turning net negative for the year — undercutting a key demand pillar that supported prices in previous cycles. Index-related selling, including MSCI's decision to drop Strategy (formerly MicroStrategy), and liquidity drained by a frenzy around an Anthropic IPO have further weighed on the market.

## The inflation-hedge debate

Not everyone is bearish on the longer-term picture. Some analysts argue that persistently high inflation could ultimately restore bitcoin's appeal as a hedge against currency debasement, setting up a debate between short-term, rate-driven selling and the long-standing digital-gold narrative. For now, the macro forces dominate.

## What to watch

Investors will keep a close eye on the PCE inflation report later in November and, more importantly, the Fed's 9 December meeting and its closely watched dot plot of rate projections. With the dollar near multi-year highs and rate expectations still rising, the path of least resistance for bitcoin may remain downward until the macro backdrop shifts.
