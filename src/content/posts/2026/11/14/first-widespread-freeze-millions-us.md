# First Widespread Freeze Grips Millions Across the U.S.

A one-two punch of weather systems is marking a decisive turn toward winter across the United States, delivering the season's first widespread freeze to millions of Americans while a separate parade of Pacific storms pounds the West Coast. Frost and freeze alerts stretch from eastern Oklahoma to the coastal Carolinas and as far south as southern Alabama, according to [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/weather/storms/weekend-storm-bring-seasons-first-widespread-freeze-millions-us-rcna609181).

The cold blast follows a departing storm that dumped more than a foot of snow on parts of New York State on Friday, leaving little doubt that fall is giving way to winter across much of the country.

## A Decisive Seasonal Shift

The first system's snow totals were striking for mid-November. As of Friday morning, 12.5 inches had fallen in Hartwick, New York, 12 inches in Burlington, 11.5 inches in Exeter Center and 10 inches in Cherry Valley, while multiple locations in the Catskills saw 3 to 7 inches. The wet snow gradually tapered off as rounds of rain moved through the I-95 corridor from Philadelphia to New York, and minor coastal flooding was possible along the Jersey Shore up toward Cape May.

That storm heads out to sea Saturday, but cold northerly winds behind it are whipping up lake-effect snow, rain showers and light snow across the Adirondacks, Berkshires, and Green and White Mountains in the Northeast.

## The First Big Freeze

The second and more consequential system is the weekend storm driving the first below-freezing temperatures of the season to millions. Wind chill forecasts tell the story: Chicago is expected to feel 24 and 27 degrees on Saturday and Sunday, New York 34 and 40, Atlanta 31 and 32, and Tallahassee, Florida, 35 and 32.

Frost and freeze alerts blanket a wide swath of the country, with cities including Tulsa, Oklahoma; Little Rock, Arkansas; Birmingham, Alabama; Atlanta, Georgia; and Raleigh, North Carolina, all under warnings. According to [Weatherboy](https://weatherboy.com/freeze-grips-nine-southern-states-with-colder-night-ahead/), the National Weather Service had freeze warnings in effect Saturday for parts of nine states—Alabama, Arkansas, Georgia, Louisiana, Mississippi, North Carolina, Oklahoma, South Carolina and Virginia—with a second, more widespread freeze expected Sunday morning. Preliminary observations showed Tupelo, Mississippi, falling to 28 degrees, while Birmingham, Huntsville and Jackson each reached 30, and Little Rock and Augusta, Georgia, bottomed out at 32.

The broader cold outbreak is significant in scale. [USA Today](https://www.usatoday.com/story/news/weather/2026/11/12/first-freeze-2026-states/92745151007/) reported that about 230 million people could feel below-average temperatures, citing the FOX Forecast Center, with morning lows tumbling into the 30s or lower across many areas by the weekend. AccuWeather meteorologist Brandon Buckingham said the cold air could bring the first hard freeze of the season to parts of the Midwest, Ohio Valley and South.

## Why It Matters

The freeze carries real stakes beyond the thermometer. The National Weather Service warns that [frost and freeze conditions](https://forecast.weather.gov/wwamap/wwatxtget.php?cwa=usa&wwa=Freeze+Warning) "could kill crops, other sensitive vegetation and possibly damage unprotected outdoor plumbing," urging residents to wrap or drain pipes and to protect tender plants. Several hours below freezing can end the growing season for many communities.

The timing is climatologically familiar. As [weather.com](https://weather.com/2026/10/01/science/weather-explainers/fall-first-freeze-average) explains, the first freeze typically arrives by late October in the Northeast, Midwest and Central Plains, but the Deep South and California usually wait until November—making this a normal-to-slightly-late event for the South but a sharp transition for northern regions. A freeze occurs when temperatures hit 32 degrees or lower; a "hard freeze" at 28 degrees or colder typically destroys most vegetation.

## A Split National Weather Pattern

While the East freezes, the West is dealing with the opposite extreme. A storm coming off the Pacific is bringing heavy rain from northern Washington to southern California, with heavy snow blanketing the Sierra Nevada. Isolated thunderstorms are possible for the Los Angeles metro, and rainfall could reach up to 3 inches from northern California to southwestern Oregon through Saturday. Sierra snowfall could total 12 to 18 inches above 6,000 feet, and the next system arrives in the Pacific Northwest Saturday night into Sunday.

By Sunday, the focus shifts to South Texas, where Southeast Texas into western Louisiana could see heavy rain capable of producing urban flooding. Rainfall will generally range from 1 to 2 inches, but localized areas along the southeast Texas coast could see 3 to 5 inches, affecting Houston, Galveston, Beaumont and Lake Charles. A few strong storms with damaging winds are possible from Beaumont to Brownsville.

## What to Watch

Forecasters expect the cold snap to be relatively short-lived, with temperatures moderating during the week. But the immediate priorities are practical: protecting plants, pets, pipes and vulnerable people as the season's first deep chill settles in. The key question for the days ahead is whether Sunday's second freeze materializes as forecast across the South—and how far its southern reach extends.
