# Kalbfleisch Sweeps 2026 Rossel Prize in Literary First

Anne-Sophie Kalbfleisch has won both the Prix Victor Rossel de littérature and the Rossel des lecteurs for her second novel *Lalie en l'air*, a rare double honour announced by the prize's juries on Thursday 12 November 2026. The awards, Belgium's most prestigious French-language literary prize and often likened to France's Goncourt, also recognised David Vandermeulen in comic art and Aimée de Jongh for her lifetime body of work.

The result, [reported by Le Soir](https://www.lesoir.be/782533/article/2026-11-13/prix-rossel-2026-anne-sophie-kalbfleisch-david-vandermeulen-et-aimee-de-jongh-primes), saw the three juries deliver what the newspaper called their *coups de cœur* — the literature prize, the readers' prize voted by subscribers of *Le Soir*, and the comic book (bande dessinée) categories.

## A novel of delicacy and suspicion

Published by Éditions du Rouergue, *Lalie en l'air* tells of a joyful friendship that grows between Mark, an elderly bird-lover, and Lalie, a lonely young girl in La Louvière, set against a Belgian society whose innocence has faded. The jury praised the book as "un livre tout en délicatesse, qui reproduit subtilement son époque, la Belgique des années 1990, avec ses rumeurs, ses suspicions et son écriture audacieuse à la seconde personne du singulier" — a work of great delicacy that subtly captures 1990s Belgium through its rumours, its suspicions and an audacious use of the second-person singular, [according to La Libre](https://www.lalibre.be/culture/livres-bd/2026/11/12/anne-sophie-kalbfleisch-laureate-du-prix-victor-rossel-2026-pour-lalie-en-lair-Z7BOGC56K5AX3ICUVHQSUE5IDI/).

La Libre's earlier feature on the novel linked its atmosphere to the Dutroux affair. Kalbfleisch emerged from a field of five finalists to take the literature prize — the same book then carrying the readers' vote as well, a "coup double" noted by [Sudinfo](https://www.sudinfo.be/id1224605/article/2026-11-12/prix-victor-rossel-coup-double-pour-anne-sophie-kalbfleisch-avec-lalie-en-lair).

## Twenty years in the making

In bande dessinée, Brussels author David Vandermeulen took the Prix Rossel de bande dessinée for *Fritz Haber, tome 5: Le triomphe des vaincus* (Delcourt). The series centres on the real historical figure of Fritz Haber, the German Jewish scientist awarded the 1918 Nobel Prize in Chemistry, depicted in a Germany where capitalism and science had been put at the service of war. Vandermeulen has worked on the Haber story for roughly two decades.

The Grand Prix de l'Académie Rossel de bande dessinée, awarded for an entire body of work, went to the Dutch author and illustrator Aimée (Amélie) de Jongh. Her first graphic novel, *Le Retour de la Bondrée*, was crowned in Belgium in 2016 with the Prix Saint-Michel; she has since published *L'Obsolescence programmée de nos sentiments* with Belgian scriptwriter Zidrou and the bestseller *Jours de sable* about the Dust Bowl. In 2026 she released *Dessiner Lesbos* and *Samir* (Dargaud), two albums about the fate of migrants.

## A prize steeped in history

[Le Carnet et les Instants](https://le-carnet-et-les-instants.net/2026/11/12/prix-rossel-2026/) confirmed the full palmarès, noting that four prizes were awarded across the three juries. The Prix Victor Rossel was established in 1938 by *Le Soir* in memory of Victor Rossel, the son of the paper's founder Émile Rossel, and has grown over the decades to embrace comic art and a readers' award decided by subscribers.

## Analysis: a prize that mirrors Belgian letters

The 2026 result is notable on two fronts. First, the sweep by a single novel is a signal of consensus rare in juried literary prizes, lending *Lalie en l'air* an immediate commercial and critical weight that extends well beyond the announcement itself. Second, the equal billing of literature and bande dessinée reflects the Rossel's deliberate positioning: it treats comics not as a side category but as a peer to the novel, a stance that has helped make the prize a bellwether for the French-speaking Belgian scene.

The recognition of de Jongh — a Dutch author whose work has been repeatedly embraced by Belgian institutions — also underlines the cross-border, Dutch-language dimension of a prize that is, on its face, strictly francophone. Her recent focus on migration in *Dessiner Lesbos* and *Samir* gives the Academy's lifetime award a distinctly contemporary resonance.

## What to watch

Attention now turns to how *Lalie en l'air* performs commercially and critically in the wake of the double win, and to whether the prize's growing comic category continues to attract the calibre of Vandermeulen and de Jongh. For now, the juries have delivered a verdict that celebrates range — from a delicate second-person novel about 1990s Belgium to a twenty-year graphic meditation on science at war — and that, in doing so, captures the breadth of Belgian literary and graphic talent.
