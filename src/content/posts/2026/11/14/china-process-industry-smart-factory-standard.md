# China Issues Standard for Process-Industry Smart Factories

China has approved a national standard that establishes a unified management benchmark for smart factories in its process industries, a move aimed at regularizing digitalization across heavily capitalized, high-risk manufacturing sectors. The Standardization Administration of China approved GB/T 48536-2026 - formally the "Intelligent Manufacturing - Business Management Specification for Smart Factories in the Process Industry" - which takes effect on 1 May 2027, according to [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/fortune/20261114/babcf23542ee45d1bb750908b42d5ca3/c.html), the state news agency that [reported](https://www.chinanews.com.cn/cj/2026/11-13/10731709.shtml) the measure on 13 November.

The standard is a recommended (GB/T) national code rather than a mandatory one, meaning it offers a common reference framework that guides - rather than compels - enterprises. It is intended to fill a gap in which different sub-sectors lacked a shared management language, a prerequisite for scaling digital twins, artificial intelligence, and industrial-big-data platforms across sectors that run continuous, tightly coupled production.

## What the standard covers

According to detailed reporting by [Xinhua](https://www3.xinhuanet.com/tech/20261109/9bb712ed4f9f490985dbe0df8950be36/c.html), the standard lays out a five-module business management framework spanning research and design, production and manufacturing, operations support, full-chain collaboration, and management methods. It reaches across core links including design, planning, scheduling, quality, and safety.

It also sets out five basic principles: data-driven operation, system integration, safety and environmental protection, flexible adaptation, and continuous improvement. On data, the standard requires a unified platform that collects and covers raw materials, processes, equipment, products, and emissions across the full workflow, and it calls for closed-loop, precise control of planning, scheduling, operations, and warehousing and logistics. It further encourages the use of digital twins and AI to assist scheduling optimization and the analysis of abnormal conditions.

On safety and environment, the standard embeds risk-tiered control, hazard identification and remediation, special-operations approval, and hazardous-chemicals traceability into business processes. It formalizes a "dual prevention mechanism" that pairs risk-tiered control with hazard remediation, and it promotes multi-objective optimization intended to advance safety, energy saving, and pollution prevention together.

The full-chain collaboration module pushes enterprises to strengthen internal and upstream-downstream business linkages, sharing information on procurement planning, supplier management, production scheduling, and customer service, backed by performance evaluation and continuous-improvement mechanisms.

## Why now

The standard was led by the China Electronics Standardization Institute, an institute affiliated with the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology, and was compiled jointly with more than 40 organizations - including the Building Materials Industry Information Center, the Zhejiang University Huzhou Institute, Petro-CyberWorks Information Technology, Kunlun Digital Technology, and China ENFI Engineering, according to [China Industry News](https://www.cinn.cn/2026/11-09/3DjVBveD.html).

Li Xiang, the standard's lead drafter, said process industries such as petrochemicals, coal chemicals, steel, pharmaceuticals, and non-ferrous metals share characteristics of continuous production, high process coupling, and concentrated high-risk scenarios - and that their intelligent transformation bears directly on the stability and security of industrial and supply chains. The standard, he said, was designed to break five prominent bottlenecks: inconsistent understanding of smart-factory management across sub-sectors, non-standardized processes and pervasive "information islands," shallow integration of technology with management, weak performance evaluation and continuous-improvement mechanisms, and insufficient embedding of safety, environmental, and quality compliance into business processes.

## Industry reaction and projected gains

The drafting working group estimates that adopting the standard could cut production costs by 5% to 10%, lift production efficiency by 10% to 15%, and reduce supply-chain costs by 8% to 12%, while enabling cross-domain sharing and reuse of design, production, and operational data to form a "single source of data," Xinhua reported.

Industry experts quoted by China Industry News framed the standard as a unifying force for their respective sectors. Jiang Yuan, executive deputy director of the Building Materials Industry Information Center, called it an important achievement in advancing the manufacturing-power strategy, and said it would help companies in cement, glass, and glass-fiber improve their business management systems. Wang Hua, chief technical expert at PetroChina Kunlun Digital, said it "adds a unified management language for the petrochemical industry," fixing cross-unit and cross-process collaboration rules into place.

Xiong Zhihua of China Minmetals ENFI Digital Intelligence said the standard fits the non-ferrous metals sector's needs - unmanned inspection of high-risk scenarios, predictive maintenance of key metallurgical equipment, and full-process energy and emissions control to support energy saving, carbon reduction, and ultra-low emissions. Xie Shuhong, chief engineer at ZTT, noted that optical fiber, cable, and submarine cable manufacturing are typical process manufacturing, and that the standard sets a unified business-management baseline for consolidating and replicating industry know-how.

## What's next

Representative enterprises in petrochemicals, metallurgy, and pharmaceuticals were selected for pilot testing during drafting, and feedback indicated the framework matches enterprise needs and effectively standardizes processes, improves collaboration, and strengthens risk control, the drafting group said.

Looking ahead, the State Administration for Market Regulation said it will work with the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology and other departments, industry associations, and enterprises to conduct interpretation, training, and pilot demonstrations. The China Electronics Standardization Institute will organize nationwide standard interpretation and training and select leading enterprises in sub-sectors for demonstration, aiming to spread adoption across the process industries.

The immediate test will be uptake: because the standard is voluntary, its impact will depend on how readily enterprises in capital-intensive, safety-sensitive sectors adopt its framework - and on whether a unified management benchmark translates into the measurable cost and efficiency gains its drafters project.
