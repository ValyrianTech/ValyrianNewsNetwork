# China Adds Record 51 Class-One New Drugs to Insurance

China's National Healthcare Security Administration (NHSA) on 14 November unveiled its 2026 adjustment to the National Basic Medical Insurance Drug Catalog, adding 107 new medicines — 51 of them Class-One new drugs, a record high for the number and share of genuinely novel therapies entering public coverage, according to [Xinhua News Agency](https://www.news.cn/politics/20261114/ee398c5473214b7ebd8a547e70f43e65/c.html), which reported the release from Suzhou, Jiangsu.

The new entries span oncology, rare diseases, chronic diseases and mental illness — areas of urgent clinical need. After the adjustment, the 2026 catalog contains 3,331 medicines in total, comprising 1,935 Western medicines and 1,396 traditional Chinese patent medicines. The updated catalog takes effect on 1 January 2027.

## What Counts as a 'Class-One' Drug

Under China's drug registration system, a Class-One drug is an innovative medicine that has never been marketed anywhere in the world — a genuinely novel chemical or biological entity with a clearly defined structure and demonstrated clinical value. For these drugs, inclusion in the national insurance catalog is decisive: it determines whether a therapy is reimbursable and therefore broadly accessible to patients.

The rising count of Class-One drugs entering the catalog is treated by authorities and analysts as a dual indicator — of China's domestic pharmaceutical innovation capacity and of the public payer's willingness to cover cutting-edge therapies.

The record deserves precise framing. In raw terms, the 2026 adjustment added fewer total medicines (107) than the 2025 round, which added 114, including 50 Class-One new drugs, as [China Daily](https://fashion.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202601/08/WS695f114ea310942cc499a7fd.html) reported at the time. The "record high" cited by Xinhua refers specifically to the Class-One category — 51 versus 50 — which edged past the previous benchmark in both number and proportion.

## A CAR-T Milestone Enters Basic Coverage

The headline inclusion is Ruijiolunsai injection (brand name Beinoda), a CAR-T cell therapy developed by JW Therapeutics that becomes the first such therapy covered by China's basic public insurance. CAR-T treatment — in which a patient's own T-cells are genetically engineered to attack cancer — has been transformative for certain blood cancers, but at list prices reported in the region of a million yuan or more per infusion, it had long been out of reach for most patients.

According to [时代财经, reporting via Eastmoney](https://finance.eastmoney.com/a/202611143931312747.html), the 2026 list also includes Lunkangyilongtuo monoclonal antibody (Yizekang), a world-first EGFR x HER3 bispecific antibody-drug conjugate from Baili Tianheng, which reached the catalog in its first year on the market through a newly implemented "pre-declaration" mechanism. Innovent's Mashidutai injection (Xinermei) was included under an adult type-2 diabetes indication — reported as the first Chinese-developed dual-target weight-loss and diabetes originator drug to enter the national catalog.

Analysts have framed the CAR-T inclusion as a symbolic shift in the philosophy of Chinese public insurance — from guaranteeing basic coverage toward guaranteeing quality.

## The Two-Tier Catalog Architecture

The 2026 adjustment was carried out in parallel with the Commercial Health Insurance Innovative Drug Catalog, which added 12 medicines to reach a total of 28. The commercial list is designed for high-value, high-cost therapies that sit outside the basic "guarantee-the-basics" remit but offer significant clinical benefit. Among the new entries are the world's first solid-tumor CAR-T therapy and China's first gene therapy for hemophilia, Xinhua reported.

Crucially, the 2026 round established a bridging mechanism between the two catalogs. As the [Chinese government's official policy interpretation](https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/202605/content_7069184.htm) explained in May, the adjustment introduced three new conditions under which out-of-catalog drugs could apply for basic coverage — most notably allowing drugs already listed in the commercial innovative drug catalog to apply to enter basic medical insurance. Two drugs from the 2025 commercial catalog were incorporated into the basic catalog this round.

"We encourage some high-value innovative drugs to apply to the commercial innovative drug catalog early after launch, reserving room for subsequent applications to enter the medical insurance catalog," said Huang Xinyu, director of the NHSA's medical service management department, in the government interpretation.

## Why It Matters

The 2026 adjustment is the ninth national drug catalog revision since the NHSA was established in 2018, and it lands against a broader policy backdrop. The 2026 Government Work Report called for accelerating the development of commercial health insurance and promoting high-quality development of innovative drugs and medical devices — part of an effort to build a multi-level medical security system that shares the financial burden of costly therapies.

The dual-catalog design carries a deliberate incentive structure. By giving manufacturers a pathway from commercial listing toward fuller public reimbursement, it rewards innovation while giving patients a route to broader coverage. For the public payer, it offers a way to expand access to expensive cutting-edge treatments without immediately absorbing their full cost into the basic insurance fund.

## What to Watch

The new catalogs take effect on 1 January 2027. The key questions in the months ahead will be how quickly hospitals stock the newly covered medicines, how the negotiated prices for high-cost therapies such as CAR-T are sustained, and whether the bridging mechanism accelerates the migration of innovative drugs from the commercial list into basic coverage. For patients with cancer, rare diseases and chronic conditions, the practical test of this record-setting adjustment will be measured not in catalog counts but in access at the point of care.
