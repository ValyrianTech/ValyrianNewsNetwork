# Rwandan genocide suspect, 78, held in Flemish care home

A 78-year-old man of Rwandan origin has been taken into custody at a care facility in Bierbeek, Flemish Brabant, on suspicion of genocide and crimes against humanity during the 1994 Rwandan genocide, the Belgian federal prosecutor's office has confirmed. The case is expected to go before the assizes court in Leuven and would, if it proceeds, be the first Rwandan genocide trial held in Flanders.

The suspect, identified in Belgian reporting as Jean Baptiste R., was living at an institution run by the Catholic congregation Broeders van Liefde — renamed Evara in 2026 — in the town near Leuven. According to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/14/verdachte-van-genocide-in-rwanda-opgepakt-in-bierbeek/), the federal prosecutor wants to bring him before the Leuven assizes court (hof van assisen) on charges of genocide and crimes against humanity. He remains in pre-trial detention.

## What the suspect is accused of

Jean Baptiste R. was, in 1994, the director of the *Home de la Vierge des Pauvres*, an accommodation centre for people with physical disabilities in Gatagara, Rwanda. The federal prosecutor's office says he is suspected of "serious violations of international humanitarian law, in particular genocide and crimes against humanity, in Rwanda, at least in the period from 1 April to 25 May 1994," as [HLN](https://www.hln.be/binnenland/78-jarige-verdachte-van-genocide-in-rwanda-opgepakt-bij-broeders-van-liefde-in-bierbeek~a411e000/) reported.

According to the prosecution, R. organised and attended meetings to plan and prepare the genocide against the Tutsi, and forced the staff and patients of his centre to carry identity cards stating their ethnicity. On 24 April 1994, when Interahamwe militias raided the centre, he is accused of pointing out Tutsi men who were hiding there; they were taken outside and killed. On 9 May 1994, the militias allegedly returned and, again on his indication, killed further Tutsi, mainly women and children.

## How the case came together

It was Rwanda that alerted Belgium to the man's alleged acts. "The federal prosecutor's office started an investigation and itself travelled to Rwanda to collect evidence," magistrate Ann Lukowiak told VRT NWS. In June 2026 a rogatory commission took place in Rwanda to hear witnesses and visit the Gatagara centre.

The suspect was arrested by the federal judicial police on 28 September 2026 and placed under an arrest warrant by an investigating judge in Leuven. The Leuven council chamber (raadkamer) confirmed his detention on 6 November, [BruxellesToday](https://www.bruxellestoday.be/actualite/rwandais-78-ans-suspecte-genocide-interpelle-bierbeek.html) reported, citing Le Soir and De Standaard. The case was made public on 14 November.

## A three-decade pursuit

The arrest closes a long chapter. According to BruxellesToday, Rwandan authorities began actively searching for the suspect in 1996. He is reported to have lived in the Democratic Republic of the Congo and then Tanzania before being traced to Rome by journalists from the Italian daily *La Repubblica* while subject to an Interpol Red Notice. Italy refused his extradition; at the time he was reportedly working as a receptionist at the Vatican. He was naturalised as a Belgian citizen in 2005 and moved to Belgium around 2023, after a long period in Italy.

## Shock at the congregation

Luc Lemmens, the Belgian head of the Broeders van Liefde, said the news had hit the congregation hard. "Naturally we are shocked by the nature of the allegations and the human suffering connected with such facts," he told VRT NWS.

Lemmens said candidate brothers are screened, but noted that the man joined the Brothers of Charity in Rwanda more than 50 years ago — before the genocide and long before he came to Belgium. He said he did not know whether anyone within the congregation was aware of the man's past. "If that had been the case, it certainly never reached me. I was only informed when he was recently arrested. We now hope that justice can do its work as quickly and thoroughly as possible, so that clarity comes as soon as possible."

## Belgium's unique legal role

Belgium is a former colonial power in Rwanda and, under its universal jurisdiction laws, has prosecuted genocide suspects regardless of where the alleged crimes took place. Seven trials related to the Rwandan genocide have been held in Belgium so far — all before the Brussels assizes court — resulting in a total of 12 convictions, according to HLN.

The federal prosecutor's office is currently handling 37 open files related to the genocide against the Tutsi. Across those files, six people have been placed under suspicion and four are in pre-trial detention. Jean Baptiste R. is the first of them to be arrested on Flemish territory.

## What happens next

The federal prosecutor must formally refer the case to the Leuven assizes court, and a trial date has not been set. Given the suspect's age, the pace of proceedings will be closely watched. The defence is likely to contest identification evidence and the reliability of the testimony gathered during the June rogatory commission.

Questions also remain for the Broeders van Liefde / Evara congregation about how much was known of the man's past, and whether screening procedures were ever applied to a member who joined in Rwanda in the 1970s. For Belgium, the case is the latest test of its willingness to pursue genocide suspects decades after the crimes — and the first time that reckoning will play out in a Flemish courtroom.

At stake is not only the fate of one 78-year-old man, but the principle that, for the survivors of 1994, the passage of time offers no refuge.
