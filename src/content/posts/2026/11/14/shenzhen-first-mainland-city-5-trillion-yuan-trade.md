# Shenzhen First Mainland City to Top 5 Trillion Yuan in Trade

Shenzhen has become the first city in mainland China to surpass 5 trillion yuan (roughly US$700 billion) in total foreign trade, officials announced on November 14, 2026, a milestone unveiled as the southern technology hub prepares to host the APEC Economic Leaders' Meeting. The figure was disclosed at the first press briefing of the 2026 APEC Leaders' Meeting Week, according to [Sina News](https://news.sina.com.cn/c/2026-11-14/doc-iniyskvi3041241.shtml), which cited an initial report by Jiemian News.

Zhang Feimeng, director of the Shenzhen Commerce Bureau, told reporters that the city's combined imports and exports had reached the record level, framing high-level opening-up and continuous innovation as the core engines of its trade growth.

## A Milestone on the Summit Stage

The announcement came at a special briefing for Guangdong Province and Shenzhen at the APEC news center, days before the 33rd APEC Economic Leaders' Meeting, scheduled for November 18–19 in Shenzhen under the theme "Building an Asia-Pacific Community, Promoting Common Prosperity." China is hosting APEC for the third time—after Shanghai in 2001 and Beijing in 2014—and for the first time in 12 years, a point emphasized by Wang Xi, a Standing Committee member of the Guangdong Provincial Party Committee, as [National Business Daily](https://www.nbd.com.cn/articles/2026-11-14/4647753.html) reported.

Shenzhen Mayor Li Yun said hosting the summit would bring major opportunities concentrated in three dimensions—opening-up, innovation, and cooperation—and pledged to align the city with high-standard international trade rules to spur the flow of goods, capital, technology, talent, and data.

## Imports Surge, Exports Shift Upmarket

The trade figures reveal a city buying as aggressively as it sells. In the first three quarters of 2026, Shenzhen's imports reached 2.24 trillion yuan, up 70.1% year-on-year, with durian shipments routed through Shenzhen ports from Malaysia and Vietnam rising 25.9%. On the export side, combined overseas sales of signature advanced products—3D printers, digital cameras, and smartphones—grew 15.5%.

Bilateral ties with APEC economies have tightened sharply. Shenzhen's trade with other APEC economies hit a record 3.23 trillion yuan in the first three quarters, up 40% year-on-year and accounting for about 70% of the city's total trade, according to [Southern Daily](https://www.cnbayarea.org.cn/news/focus/content/post_1341753.html). The provincial picture is similar: Guangdong's trade with other APEC economies reached 5.74 trillion yuan, up 26.4% and equal to 65.9% of the province's total foreign trade.

## Building on a Record Base

The 5-trillion mark builds on an already towering foundation. Shenzhen's full-year 2025 trade totaled 4.55 trillion yuan, up 1.4% and a record, ranking first among mainland cities, with exports posting a 33rd consecutive year of national leadership, [China News Service](https://www.chinanews.com.cn/cj/2026/01-22/10556844.shtml) reported. That year, high-tech product trade reached 1.4 trillion yuan—30.7% of the city total and 9.8 percentage points above the national average—while 62,300 enterprises conducted import-export activity, including more than 8,600 new entrants.

Private enterprises have been the driving force, contributing 3.12 trillion yuan, or nearly 70% of the city total. Trade with Belt-and-Road countries reached 1.57 trillion yuan, about 34.5% of the total, as Shenzhen diversified away from traditional markets.

## Analysis: A Showcase for the Greater Bay Area

The timing is far from coincidental. Officials have positioned the APEC summit as a platform to demonstrate the openness of the Guangdong-Hong Kong-Macao Greater Bay Area and to deepen Shenzhen's integration into Asia-Pacific supply chains. Roughly 147 Shenzhen-based enterprises are expected to participate in the concurrent APEC CEO Summit, engaging with domestic and foreign business leaders.

The milestone also carries political weight. With global trade uncertainty rising, Beijing is keen to present Shenzhen—long the laboratory of Chinese economic reform—as proof that openness and innovation can still deliver growth. The 40% surge in trade with APEC economies offers a counter-narrative to protectionist headwinds and reinforces China's pitch for deeper regional economic integration.

Yet the headline figure deserves scrutiny. The precise statistical cutoff for the "over 5 trillion" claim was not specified, and the milestone appears to rest on year-to-date customs data through roughly October rather than a finalized full-year breakdown. Analysts will watch the formal customs tally for confirmation.

## What's Next

For now, all eyes turn to November 18–19, when leaders from across the Asia-Pacific gather in Shenzhen. Beyond the summit, the question is whether the city can sustain its import surge and convert its hosting role into lasting trade and investment ties—and whether other mainland cities will follow Shenzhen across the 5-trillion threshold. For a city that has spent four decades outpacing its peers, the milestone is less a finish line than the next marker on a longer run.
