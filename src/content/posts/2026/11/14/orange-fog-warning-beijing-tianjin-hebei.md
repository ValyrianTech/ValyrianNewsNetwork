# Orange Fog Warning: Dense Fog Blankets Beijing-Tianjin-Hebei

China's National Meteorological Center (NMC) continued an orange-level fog warning on the morning of 14 November 2026, forecasting dense to thick fog across the Beijing-Tianjin-Hebei region and a band of provinces stretching from northeast China to Guangxi, with localized visibility dropping below 50 meters. The alert — the second-highest in China's three-tier fog warning system — warns of serious disruption to road, air and ferry transport during the morning travel window.

According to the warning text carried by [Sina News](https://news.sina.com.cn/c/2026-11-14/doc-iniyryfm0838040.shtml), fog is expected from the morning to forenoon of 14 November across central and southern Beijing, Tianjin, eastern and central-southern Hebei, central-northern Henan, western Shandong, central-southern Heilongjiang, central Jilin, central-western Liaoning, northern Jiangxi, southern Hunan and central-northern Guangxi.

![National Meteorological Center fog warning map for 14 November 2026, published by China Weather](http://i.weather.com.cn/images/2026/11/14/i_3e17e14a75ea48289feda048f5d0e677.jpg)

## Where the Fog Is Thickest

Within that broader area, dense or thick fog is forecast for southeastern Beijing, Tianjin, eastern and central-southern Hebei, western Liaoning, southern Heilongjiang, northern Henan and western Shandong. The most severe signal is the warning of localized "exceptionally thick fog" (特强浓雾) with visibility below 50 meters in parts of these same zones — a level that routinely triggers highway closures, flight delays and elevated road-safety risk.

[China Weather](https://news.weather.com.cn/2026/11/4859292.shtml), the official portal of the China Meteorological Administration, reproduced the full warning alongside defense guidance issued to the public. Drivers were told to control their speed given low land visibility, while airports, expressways and ferry terminals were instructed to take measures to guarantee transport safety.

## A Warning That Has Been Building

The orange alert did not appear overnight without warning. The NMC first raised the warning to orange at 18:00 on 13 November, when it forecast fog from the night of 13 November through the following morning across 15 provinces, regions and municipalities — a wider footprint that also included southern Jiangsu, southern Anhui, southern Hubei, central-southern Chongqing, central Guizhou and northern Guangxi, as [China Weather reported](https://news.weather.com.cn/2026/11/4858933.shtml) at the time.

By the 06:00 update on 14 November, that list had narrowed to 11 areas, consistent with the fog shifting and concentrating northward toward the Jing-Jin-Ji corridor and the North China Plain overnight. The prior warning had flagged localized strong fog with visibility below 200 meters; the continuation escalated the extreme threshold to below 50 meters in the worst-affected patches.

## Why Fog Forms Here in Late Autumn

The North China Plain is prone to radiation fog in late autumn and early winter. Long, clear nights allow rapid surface cooling, while relatively humid near-surface air and weak winds trap moisture in a shallow boundary layer — conditions frequently reinforced by temperature inversions. Chinese meteorology grades fog by horizontal visibility, with anything below 1 kilometer counted as fog, 50 to 200 meters as thick fog, and below 50 meters as exceptionally thick fog.

China's color-coded fog alert system runs from yellow to orange to red. An orange warning signals dense fog — generally visibility below 500 meters — that is expected or already occurring and is likely to persist, placing it one step below the most severe red tier.

## A Hazardous Prelude to a Cold Snap

The fog is unfolding within a larger cold-weather episode. Two cold-air masses moving south from 14 November are forecast to cool much of the country by 6-10°C and bring a broad band of rain and snow across central-eastern China, with heavy-to-blizzard conditions in eastern northeast China, according to [China Weather's outlook](https://news.weather.com.cn/2026/11/4858927.shtml).

That interaction helps explain the timing. The approach of cold air typically disperses fog, but in the transitional period before winds strengthen, it can also allow fog to thicken — consistent with a continued orange warning on the morning of 14 November followed by an expected improvement. For Beijing specifically, China Weather forecast light fog or mist on 14 November with reduced visibility and a high of 15°C, before north winds strengthen on 15 November, improving atmospheric dispersion and visibility, with light or spotty rain at night.

## What to Watch

The immediate concern is transport. The warning's explicit focus on drivers, airports, expressways and ferry terminals reflects the well-documented operational fallout of visibility below 200 meters — and especially below 50 meters: speed restrictions and closures on key expressways, delays and cancellations at Beijing Capital and Daxing and Tianjin Binhai airports, and ferry suspensions.

The source warnings do not enumerate specific closures, so travelers should check with local transport authorities for real-time operational impacts and confirm the precise locations of the most extreme patches, which are not specified beyond provincial-level bands.

Looking ahead, the key questions are whether any locality escalates to a red warning, how quickly north winds on 15 November clear the fog as forecast, and how the incoming cold air and rain-snow interact with any residual fog. The fog may prove a brief but hazardous prelude to a significant cold snap that will shape conditions across central and eastern China through 18 November.
