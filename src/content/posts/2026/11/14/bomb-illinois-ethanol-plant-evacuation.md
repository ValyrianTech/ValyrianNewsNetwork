# Bomb at Illinois Ethanol Plant Forces Mass Evacuation

A bomb discovered at an ethanol production facility in central Illinois forced a mandatory one-mile evacuation covering large sections of Peoria and neighboring East Peoria, with authorities warning that the blast radius around the plant's combustible chemicals could not be taken lightly. The device was "rendered safe" Friday night, but as of Saturday morning the evacuation order remained in effect while investigators scoured the facility for additional explosives and the FBI worked to identify who was responsible, according to [AP News](https://apnews.com/article/bomb-illinois-threat-peoria-b7965b246dc6f7d377d01428bd0adf31).

No arrests had been made and no injuries reported.

## A Threat That Proved Real

BioUrja Renewables, which operates the former ADM plant at 1 Edmund Street along the Illinois River, contacted Peoria Police at 1:08 p.m. Friday to report a bomb threat. A suspicious package was found about an hour later, deemed a credible threat at 6:45 p.m., and neutralized by bomb technicians at roughly 9:12 p.m., Police Chief Brad Dixon told reporters, as [WCBU](https://www.wcbu.org/local-news/2026-11-13/peoria-police-urge-evacuation-after-bomb-threat-at-biourja-renewables) reported.

The evacuation order went into effect at 7:02 p.m., urging anyone who lives or works within one mile of the facility to leave until further notice. The zone swept across much of downtown Peoria, the Southside and Warehouse District, and stretched over the river into East Peoria, encompassing several Caterpillar buildings and both the Bob Michel and Cedar Street bridges.

"This is a credible threat," Dixon said shortly after midnight Saturday. "We need to take the blast radius seriously."

Mayor Rita Ali was the first official to publicly confirm a bomb had been found. "A device has been identified. We know it's a credible threat. We know it's a bomb," she said, according to [25News Now](https://www.25newsnow.com/2026/11/14/much-downtown-peoria-shut-down-due-bomb-threat-biourja/). She cautioned that authorities did not yet know whether there was one device or several.

## Why the Blast Radius Mattered

The seriousness of the response reflected the nature of the site. BioUrja produces ethanol and alcohol and handles what Ali described as "highly combustible types of chemicals."

"If you're in the blast radius we're asking you to get out of the blast radius," Dixon said.

Dixon stressed that the case was not a hoax — a notable distinction given that bomb threats typically do not produce a genuine device. Just days earlier, hundreds of airline passengers in Europe had flights diverted or escorted by military aircraft after a series of bomb threats that officials later declared malicious hoaxes.

"Nationally when a bomb threat comes in, typically it's not legitimate... This is not the case here," Dixon said. "We found a credible package that we believe to be a threat. We're not considering this a hoax."

## A City Disrupted

The evacuation rippled through daily life across the region. Two shelters opened — Manual Academy in Peoria, where about 70 people stayed overnight, and the EastSide Centre in East Peoria. Caterpillar told employees not to report for its third shift. OSF HealthCare St. Francis Medical Center, downtown's largest employer, said it was taking "all appropriate precautions," while OSF Life Flight confirmed its medical helicopters and radio communications were unaffected.

The Peoria Civic Center paused all Saturday event operations, postponing Illinois Comic Con programming and a charity fundraiser, and country musician Jon Pardi's concert was called off at the police chief's direction. The Peoria Riverfront Museum closed until further notice.

The disruption compounded an already chaotic week: a nationwide FAA ground stop on Friday had snarled air travel, and organizers said some Comic Con guests could not arrive because of flight cancellations.

## The Investigation Ahead

The FBI's Springfield office is leading the inquiry, assisted by Peoria Police, the Peoria Fire Department, Illinois State Police, the Illinois Secretary of State and the Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives.

"We will work tirelessly to identify the individual responsible and bring them to justice," said FBI Special Agent Ryan Presley, who declined to provide further details.

As of Saturday morning, the facility had not been fully cleared. "It's a large facility, there's going to be exhaustive search of the facility. That has not been completed yet," Dixon said, adding the search would likely stretch well into the day.

"We understand that this is a disruptive process for our residents and guests," he added. "We're asking for the public's patience while we work to make this area safe."

## What to Watch

Several questions remain open: whether additional devices are found, when the evacuation order will be lifted, and who planted the bomb and why. Authorities scheduled further briefings for Saturday, and the [Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/nov/14/peoria-illinois-evacuation-bomb) reported that East Peoria's government said the evacuation zone remained in effect as of 9 a.m. Saturday.

Peoria, a city of roughly 110,000 about 150 miles southwest of Chicago, now faces the aftermath of a threat that, unlike so many others, turned out to be real.
