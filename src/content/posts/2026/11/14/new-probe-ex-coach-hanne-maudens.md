# New probe into ex-coach of Hanne Maudens

The investigating judge in Hasselt has opened a new judicial investigation into possible sexually transgressive behaviour by the former coach of Belgian heptathlete Hanne Maudens, the Limburg public prosecutor's office confirmed on Friday. The fresh probe was triggered by a new complaint Maudens filed on Monday with civil-party status, to which three additional women's testimonies have now been attached, according to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/13/nieuw-onderzoek-mogelijk-grensoverschrijdend-gedrag-door-ex-coac/).

An earlier inquiry into the same allegations had been dismissed for lack of evidence only months before. The coach, a well-known figure in Belgian top-level athletics who has not been publicly named for legal reasons, denies any wrongdoing and says the accusations are "too crazy for words."

## What is new

The substance of Maudens' own account is unchanged. The decisive new element, she says, is corroboration: her file now includes the testimonies of three women who also accuse the coach of transgressive behaviour. According to the athlete, these are two former fellow elite athletes and a woman who was treated at the man's physiotherapy practice.

"Last year I stood completely alone in the story. It was a matter of word against word," Maudens told VRT NWS. "Now there are three similar testimonies from other women that confirm my story. That makes it a completely different story."

The new complaint was lodged through her lawyer, Christine Mussche, with civil-party status (burgerlijke partijstelling), a mechanism that allows an alleged victim to seek formal recognition of offences and damages. The Limburg prosecutor's office has confirmed the investigation is open but declined to comment further on the case.

## A long procedural road

The development is the latest chapter in a case that has been building for years. Maudens, from Wetteren in East Flanders and now 29, first went to the federation prosecutor of Atletiek Vlaanderen in 2022, alleging transgressive conduct during a period when she was around 20 and training under the coach with an eye on the Tokyo Olympics. In 2024 she took her complaint to the police, prompting the prosecutor to open an investigating inquiry, which was later dropped for lack of evidence.

She has described the coach's behaviour as extending well beyond an authoritarian coaching style: inappropriate intimate touching, sexually abusive conduct during massage and physiotherapy sessions and at a training camp in South Africa, and, she says, irresponsible coaching that forced her to compete with a fractured shin at the World Championships. The coach has consistently maintained his innocence.

## Why it matters

The case has become a focal point for a wider reckoning in Belgian athletics over safeguarding and conduct. Maudens has publicly linked her decision to speak out to the turbulence surrounding star athlete Nafi Thiam, a dispute that spilled into the open and led to institutional fallout at Atletiek Vlaanderen, including the departure of its chairman, Gery Follens, who stepped down officially for health reasons after a turbulent year.

The federation operates a confidential reporting channel, an independent ethics commission and a disciplinary procedure. It has said that cases can be dismissed or conditionally dismissed, and that new evidence can lead to a file being reopened, the very logic now playing out at the judicial level, where the corroborating testimonies have changed the evidentiary picture.

## Analysis: from one voice to three

The shift from a single account to multiple comparable testimonies is significant. A single-complainant case often reduces to a credibility contest; a file with several independent accounts alleging similar conduct gives investigators a firmer footing. Maudens herself framed the change in exactly those terms, describing how her story had gone from a "word against word" dispute to something with corroboration.

Her stated aims have remained consistent: a safe and humane sporting environment, recognition of what she says happened, and space for other athletes to speak. "What is important to me is that a safe sports environment is created within top-level sport, but also beyond," she told VRT NWS. "It must be an environment where you are treated in a human way and where you can do what you love in order to achieve the desired results."

## What to watch

Maudens says she is awaiting the outcome of the investigation while focusing fully on her sport, having resumed training with an eye on the 2028 Los Angeles and 2032 Brisbane Olympic Games.

The new judicial investigation has no fixed public timetable. The key questions now are whether the prosecutor will bring the case beyond the investigative stage, how the additional testimonies are assessed, and whether the renewed scrutiny forces Belgian athletics to revisit how it handles safeguarding complaints that were previously set aside.

For now, the allegations remain allegations: no court has found the coach guilty of any offence, and he continues to deny all wrongdoing.
