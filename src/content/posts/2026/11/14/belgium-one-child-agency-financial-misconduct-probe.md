# Belgium's ONE Child Agency Faces Financial Misconduct Probe

The government of Belgium's French-speaking community has launched a formal procedure to shed light on suspected dysfunctions at the Office de la Naissance et de l'Enfance (ONE), the century-old public agency that monitors young children's health and supports families across the Federation Wallonie-Bruxelles. Minister of Childhood Valerie Lescrenier said she had been informed of "potentially serious facts" concerning "certain aspects of the body's functioning," and confirmed that one member of the agency has been removed from service, according to [RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/dysfonctionnements-a-l-one-une-procedure-ouverte-pour-faire-toute-la-lumiere-11815530).

## What Triggered the Alert

According to [Le Soir](https://www.lesoir.be/782644/article/2026-11-13/soupcons-de-malversations-financieres-lone), the alert originated in an internal "rapport d'observation" (observation report) transmitted to the minister. The newspaper reports that the matter concerns suspicions of financial misconduct (malversations financieres) and that the prosecutor's office (parquet) has been seized of the case.

The minister's communique, issued on Friday 13 November, adopts more cautious language. It refers to elements relating to "governance, management and control of certain services" that were recently brought to her attention, without publicly confirming the financial-misconduct characterization. That distinction matters: the embezzlement-type framing originates with Le Soir, while the official statement stops short of confirming it.

## Four Measures Ordered

Acting at the request of the Federation Wallonie-Bruxelles government, Lescrenier detailed a package of measures aimed at enabling a "complete, objective and independent examination of the situation," RTBF reported. They comprise four concrete actions.

The minister ordered the immediate removal from service (dispense) of a member of the agency, alongside the launch of a suspension procedure against that individual. She entrusted a temporary mission to the government commissioner to ensure continuity of the agency's management. She also launched a forensic procedure, a scientific method of collecting and analysing evidence, to establish the reality of the findings. Together, the measures are intended to constitute a complete, objective and independent examination.

Both Le Soir and [RTL Info](https://www.rtl.be/actu/belgique/faits-divers/soupcons-de-malversations-financieres-lone-le-parquet-est-saisi-la-ministre/2026-11-14/article/806570) confirm that one person has been suspended. The individual's identity and role have not been publicly disclosed.

"I do not look away. I take my responsibilities," Lescrenier said, according to Le Soir. "When it comes to the management of a public body and the use of citizens' money in the service of children and families, we have a duty of rigour, transparency and strictness," she added, as quoted across [7sur7](https://www.7sur7.be/belgique/lone-secoue-par-des-soupcons-de-malversations-financieres~a29ed9f8/) and other outlets.

## Two Parallel Tracks

A notable feature of the case is that two distinct processes are running side by side. The first is an administrative and internal track: the suspension procedure, the government commissioner overseeing management, and the forensic audit ordered by the minister. The second is a judicial track, following the seizure of the prosecutor's office. Forensic accounting is a method for preserving and analysing evidence; it can feed into or complement a criminal investigation but is separate from it.

Throughout, the language used by officials has been precautionary rather than conclusive. No one has been named as guilty, and the suspended individual has not been publicly charged. The measures are investigative and preventive in nature.

## Why It Matters

ONE is a high-profile institution founded in 1919 and headquartered in Saint-Gilles, Brussels. It organises prenatal and child consultations, home accompaniment, and the licensing of early-childhood care places, and it supervises multidisciplinary "SOS Enfants" teams. Allegations of financial misconduct at such an agency are politically sensitive both because of its mandate, children and families, and because they touch the deployment of public money.

The fact that the response combines a suspension with government-commissioner oversight underscores the perceived gravity of the matter. The minister has positioned herself as responding proactively, a framing clearly designed to pre-empt criticism about oversight of a public body in her portfolio.

## What to Watch

Several questions remain open. It is not yet publicly known who the suspended individual is, what the scale of any alleged misappropriation might be, or when precisely the internal report reached the minister's office. It is also unclear whether the prosecutor's office has opened a formal judicial investigation or formally charged anyone. ONE's own institutional response has yet to be detailed.

For now, the case turns on establishing facts through independent channels, forensic evidence and potentially judicial process, while the government seeks to reassure the public that public money intended for children and families is being safeguarded.
