# Shenzhen Says All Preparations Ready for APEC Summit

All preparatory work for the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) summit in Shenzhen is complete, a senior Guangdong provincial official said on 14 November, as the southern Chinese tech hub readies to host the 33rd APEC Economic Leaders' Meeting on 18–19 November.

Speaking at the first press briefing of the news center for APEC Leaders' Meeting Week, Wang Xi, a Standing Committee member of the Guangdong Provincial Party Committee and deputy head of the province's APEC preparatory leading group, said the city was ready to receive delegations from across the Pacific. "At present, all preparatory work is ready; with full enthusiasm, we quietly await the arrival of guests from all directions," Wang said, according to [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/world/20261114/b66193767de1474e8155e204eb9803f6/c.html).

## A City Dressed for the Occasion

Shenzhen, one of China's original Special Economic Zones and a flagship technology and manufacturing center within the Guangdong–Hong Kong–Macao Greater Bay Area, has been extensively decorated for the event. According to a [People's Daily](http://pic.people.com.cn/n1/2026/1113/c1016-40833540.html) photo report, the city is "in full bloom," with themed flower beds, banners along major roads, and APEC logos installed at subway hubs, bus stops, and border-crossing points.

The scale of the logistical build-up is considerable. The APEC Leaders' Meeting Week news center, which opened on 11 November, occupies roughly 15,000 square meters in Hall 2 of the Shenzhen Convention & Exhibition Center in Futian. As [China.com.cn](http://photo.china.com.cn/2026-11/12/content_118771362.shtml) reported, the facility includes media work areas, a technical support zone, press-conference space, a "Journalists' Home" service area, and rest and tea-break zones. It is designed to serve approximately 5,000 Chinese and foreign journalists, providing conference information, broadcast feeds, press-conference access, and smart editing and coverage services.

## Leaders Confirmed, "APEC China Year" in Full Swing

China holds the 2026 APEC chairmanship, and Beijing has used the year to stage an extensive diplomatic calendar. Chinese Foreign Minister Wang Yi said on 13 November that leaders of APEC economies — including South Korean President Lee Jae-myung — have confirmed their attendance in Shenzhen, and that China has held more than 300 events across the "APEC China Year" in the run-up to the Leaders' Meeting, according to the [Chinese Foreign Ministry](https://www.fmprc.gov.cn/wjbzhd/202611/t20261113_12059136.shtml).

The meeting will mark another chapter in a busy stretch of China–South Korea diplomacy. Speaking alongside Wang Yi in Beijing, South Korean Foreign Minister Cho Hyun noted that the two countries' leaders had already met in Gyeongju and Beijing, and would meet again in Shenzhen next week — a sequence he said would "lift bilateral relations to a new height."

## Why Shenzhen, and Why It Matters

The choice of host city is itself a statement. Shenzhen rose from a fishing village to a metropolis of global standing on the back of export manufacturing and, more recently, technology and finance. Hosting APEC there telegraphs Beijing's emphasis on open trade, technological upgrading, and regional supply-chain integration — themes that align with the city's Qianhai cooperation zone, which local officials have positioned as a hub serving APEC economies.

The summit also lands in a delicate geopolitical moment. It convenes amid persistent US–China trade friction, the ongoing war in Ukraine, and intense regional diplomacy, giving Beijing a prominent stage on which to project stability and economic openness to Pacific Rim partners. For China, the meeting is an opportunity to shape the year's agenda as chair and to showcase Shenzhen as a symbol of reform and opening.

## What to Watch

Attention now turns to the substance of the gathering: the final agenda and theme of the 33rd Leaders' Meeting, the content of the Leaders' Declaration, and which economies will be represented in person. Bilateral sideline meetings — above all any US–China exchange — will be closely watched for signals on trade and security.

One claim circulating ahead of the summit remains unconfirmed. Russian state media has reported, citing a Kremlin aide, that Presidents Vladimir Putin and Donald Trump plan to meet on the sidelines in Shenzhen — a report not corroborated by official Chinese statements reviewed, and one that should be treated with caution pending confirmation from the governments involved.

For now, the message from Shenzhen is one of readiness. With the news center open, the city decorated, and leaders confirmed, the stage is set for the Asia-Pacific's premier economic gathering — and for China's most prominent diplomatic moment of the year.
