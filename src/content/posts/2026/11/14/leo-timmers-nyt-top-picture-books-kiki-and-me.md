# Leo Timmers Earns Second Spot on NYT's Top Picture Books

Flemish author and illustrator Leo Timmers has been named to The New York Times and New York Public Library's list of the 10 best illustrated children's books of 2026 for the English edition of *Kiki & Me* — making him one of the few creators to appear on the prestigious list twice.

The announcement, published on November 13, marks Timmers' second selection following his 2019 recognition for the wordless *Monkey on the Run*. According to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/14/leo-timmers-kiki-en-ik-beste-kinderboeken-wereldwijd-nyt/), the news caught the illustrator by surprise when his American publisher called.

## A Storied Award

The NYT/NYPL Best Illustrated Children's Books Award has been given annually since 1952, with a rotating panel of three expert judges selecting 10 winners based exclusively on artistic merit. The 2026 judges were artist Edel Rodriguez, author Julie Fogliano, and librarian Kelly-Ann Smith, who weighed close to 800 picture books published in the United States this year.

For Timmers, the honor carried a sense of déjà vu. "In 2019 I thought it would be a once-in-a-lifetime experience, but here we are," he told VRT NWS. "It's unlikely that this happens a second time. Last time I really thought it was a once-in-a-lifetime experience."

## Two Books in One

*Kiki & Me* — originally published in Dutch as *Kiki & ik* by Querido — tells the story of the inseparable bond between a young girl, Kiki, and her horse, narrated from the horse's own perspective as Kiki grows up. The English edition was published by Gecko Press on October 6, translated from the Dutch by David Colmer.

The book represents a deliberate creative departure for Timmers, who is best known for his humorous, animal-led work. It alternates between sweeping, wordless acrylic-painted double-page landscapes and intimate black-and-white pen-and-ink drawings.

"Actually it's two books in one," Timmers explained. "The first part is drawn in black and white with pen, then large landscapes painted in acrylic follow. There are two styles I had to make my own. I really had to learn to draw and hatch with a pen."

The New York Times praised that duality, noting how "Timmers alternates between colorfully painted, wordless double pages, on which the duo traverses beautiful landscapes," while his "black-and-white pen-and-ink style emphasizes the expressiveness of the horse narrator and the smaller moments that form the lasting bond between Kiki and her horse."

## Two Years of Doubt, Rewarded

The recognition follows an unusually long and difficult creation process. Timmers typically produces one book a year, but *Kiki & Me* took two — a period he describes as full of struggle and multiple "crises."

"I worked on it for two years, so you have plenty of time to doubt," he told VRT NWS. "This is truly a confirmation for me, especially when you try to do something different in every book. It encourages me to keep making my own choices, to do what I myself think I should do."

In an interview with [Publishers Weekly](https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/by-topic/childrens/childrens-authors/article/101306-a-new-direction-pw-talks-with-leo-timmers.html), Timmers traced the story to his own childhood and to fatherhood. "It's the first time that I made a book that was so near to my own experiences," he said, recalling the horse he cared for as a boy and the experience of watching his two daughters grow up.

He also admitted to early nerves about the commercial gamble. "I was afraid — would all the people who buy my books follow me in this new direction? But Querido had so much confidence in the book. They started the first print run at 10,000. Now we're in the third print run. This is probably the best-selling book I have made."

## Reaching Beyond Children

The NYT honor is not the book's first accolade. *Kiki & Me* won a Zilveren Penseel (Silver Paintbrush) in 2025, a major Dutch-language prize for illustration.

Perhaps most striking is the book's crossover appeal. Timmers said adults have told him they cried at the ending — a first in his career. "Maybe, for the first time, I've made a book that's not only for children, but for a broad audience."

Timmers has published children's books since 1999 and has produced roughly 20 titles, translated into more than 43 languages. He credits the 2019 award with opening international doors, noting that "many smaller countries have also taken the risk of translating my work" since.

## What's Next

Timmers, whom [BRUZZ](https://www.bruzz.be/select/literatuur/brusselse-illustrator-leo-timmers-haalt-top-10-prentenboeken-van-new-york-times-2026-11-14) describes as a Brussels-based illustrator, is due to travel to New York for the award ceremony the week following the announcement. Timmers said he is already at work on a successor but warned it will "take some time" — he works entirely on paper, never digitally, and has been traveling frequently in pursuit of his translated titles.

"There is nothing better than making pictures with pencil and paint on paper," he said.

For Belgian children's literature, the second NYT listing is both a personal milestone and a marker of the country's growing export reach in a global picture-book market — proof that a deeply personal, formally ambitious work can travel far beyond its original language.
