# Meghan Stays Silent in Brussels as Harry Speaks Solo

Meghan, the Duchess of Sussex, attended a high-profile engagement at the European Parliament in Brussels on Tuesday but did not speak, days after her estranged father was arrested on child-pornography charges. Prince Harry delivered the keynote address alone, opening a memorial to children who have died as a result of online harm. The appearance was the couple's first overseas public engagement since returning to live in the UK.

The visit had been long planned as a joint appearance. According to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/10/harry-en-meghan-europees-parlement/), the couple had been invited by the European Parliament's Intergroup on Children's Rights to deliver a roughly 15-minute joint address on the importance of digital safety for children. At the last moment, Meghan withdrew from her portion of the keynote, reportedly feeling she was not emotionally strong enough to speak publicly after the arrest of her father, Thomas Markle Sr., 82.

## A Last-Minute Withdrawal

Thomas Markle Sr. was arrested on 6 November near Tampa, Florida, on a federal criminal complaint charging one count of possession of child pornography, according to a [US Department of Justice press release](https://www.justice.gov/usao-cdca/pr/thomas-wayne-markle-arrested-federal-complaint-charging-him-possessing-sexually). He was initially released under conditions to a dementia care facility in Lakeland, Florida, and ordered to undergo a psychiatric evaluation amid suspected dementia. He is presumed innocent; a criminal complaint contains only allegations.

A source cited by the British press said Meghan was simply not up to speaking. "She's just not strong enough, emotionally, to be doing any public speaking right now," the source said. Yet Meghan did appear: she arrived hand-in-hand with Harry shortly before 9 a.m., wearing an all-black ensemble and a remembrance poppy, and accompanied him through the memorial viewing.

Her silence was deliberate and visible. As [Town & Country](https://www.townandcountrymag.com/society/tradition/a74385342/meghan-markle-european-parliament-reporter-question/) reported, a reporter called out, "Meghan, how are you feeling and how are you coping?" The Duchess did not react, continuing to talk to the person on her left. Journalists had been briefed in advance that the couple would not answer questions.

## Harry's Emotional Address

Harry carried the engagement alone, delivering the solo speech and then speaking twice during an hour-long panel — once at the start and once at the end. He spoke of the addictive pull of technology and the vulnerability of children, and he was described as visibly emotional as he closed.

"Every time you look at your phone just remember there's a child out there who's looking at that phone twenty, thirty times more, probably considering taking their life because they don't know how to escape," Harry said, according to [Town & Country](https://www.townandcountrymag.com/society/tradition/a74386038/prince-harry-emotion-dangers-for-children-online/). He added that "we've all been asleep at the wheel" on child online safety since smartphones became widespread.

The centrepiece was the *Lost Screen Memorial*, a travelling installation first launched by the couple's Archewell Foundation in New York last year. It consists of 50 smartphone lock screens bearing pictures of children and young people who died due to online harms. Parents were present at the Brussels unveiling, including Sandra Spitaels, a Belgian mother whose 15-year-old son Glenn died by suicide in 2017. Meghan hugged her after she delivered an emotional speech.

## The Irony of the Timing

The juxtaposition was uncomfortable and widely noted. Harry delivered a speech about protecting children from harm just days after his father-in-law was arrested on allegations of possessing child-abuse material. The legal drama shadowed the event in real time: on the same day as the Brussels appearance, [Page Six](https://pagesix.com/2026/11/10/royal-family/meghan-markle-fights-back-tears-on-high-alert-at-brussels-engagement-after-dad-thomas-arrest/) reported that Thomas Markle's pretrial release had been revoked after he allegedly cut off his ankle monitor, and he was returned to custody.

Meghan has previously condemned the allegations. In an earlier written statement, she said she hoped "that justice will be done and that the protection of innocent children remains paramount," and described the alleged abuse as "disgusting." She has also said that her distance from her father, and her decision to keep him from contact with her children, Archie and Lilibet, was "to protect them."

## Why Attend at All

Analysts and observers saw the decision to appear — but not speak — as a careful line. Cancelling the trip entirely would have been a damaging blow to the couple's public advocacy platform, which has centred on mental health and online safety. Attending preserved the cause and the commitment while Meghan's silence avoided any statement that could be read as commentary on the active legal case against her father. As VRT noted, the Duchess may also have been withholding comment so as not to jeopardise the judicial process.

The Memorial itself reflects a broader legislative push. The Brussels opening was hosted by the European Parliament's Intergroup on Children's Rights, amid rising EU interest in protecting minors online as social media and artificial intelligence spread. MEP Hilde Vautmans, who spoke at the panel, publicly asked Harry to serve as a "Godfather" to the campaign; he hugged her afterwards.

## What's Next

For the Sussexes, the Brussels appearance tested their carefully managed post-UK-reset image: Harry focusing on charity work, Meghan on her business ventures and a possible return to acting. The episode drew both sympathetic and scrutinising coverage, and the legal case against Thomas Markle is far from over. The federal complaint will proceed toward indictment or resolution, his psychological evaluation continues, and a parallel Philippine investigation may yield further developments. Whether Meghan issues a fuller public statement as the case advances — and when she resumes public speaking — remains an open question. For now, she has chosen silence, and Harry has spoken for both of them.
