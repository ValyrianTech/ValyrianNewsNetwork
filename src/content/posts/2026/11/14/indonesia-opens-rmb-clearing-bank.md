# Indonesia Opens RMB Clearing Bank to Cut Dollar Reliance

Indonesia's renminbi clearing bank officially began operations at a launch ceremony at the headquarters of Bank Indonesia in Jakarta on 12 November, with Bank of China serving as the designated clearing institution. According to [People's Daily](http://world.people.com.cn/n1/2026/1113/c1002-40832949.html), about 100 representatives from Chinese and Indonesian government departments, enterprises, and financial institutions attended the ceremony, which both sides framed as a step toward reducing reliance on the US dollar in bilateral trade.

The launch marks the operational start of a clearing arrangement the People's Bank of China (PBOC) first announced on 26 June 2026, following a memorandum of understanding signed between the PBOC and Bank Indonesia on 11 June.

## What a Clearing Bank Changes

An offshore RMB clearing bank, designated by the PBOC, is authorised to provide renminbi purchase and sale services to offshore participating banks for goods trade, services, and direct investment. In practice, it gives market participants a local, direct channel to obtain and settle renminbi rather than routing transactions through a third currency—typically the US dollar—or through offshore hubs such as Hong Kong or Singapore.

Under the arrangement, 12 local and foreign banks have become participating banks as Appointed Cross-Currency Dealers. As [Kontan](https://nasional.kontan.co.id/news/gandeng-bank-of-china-bi-resmi-implementasikan-bank-kliring-renminbi-di-indonesia) reported, the 12 banks are Bank Mandiri, Bank Negara Indonesia, Bank Rakyat Indonesia, Bank CIMB Niaga, Bank Sinarmas, Bank OCBC NISP, Maybank Indonesia, China Construction Bank Indonesia, Bank Central Asia, Bank Danamon, PermataBank, and ICBC Indonesia. Bank of China's Jakarta branch also signed cooperation commitments with all of them to support clearing services and Indonesia-China local currency transactions.

## Rapid Growth in Local Currency Trade

The clearing bank lands amid a sharp rise in local currency settlement between the two countries. Kontan reported that Indonesia-China Local Currency Transaction (LCT) volume reached the equivalent of US$43.89 billion in January-September 2026, far exceeding the full-year 2025 total of US$13.19 billion. China accounted for 83.49 per cent of Indonesia's total LCT transaction volume through September, with an average of 5,951 users per month.

Indonesia has been expanding local currency settlement as a bulwark against dollar-cycle volatility affecting the rupiah. The framework was first implemented in 2018 and now covers seven partner countries: Malaysia, Thailand, Japan, China, South Korea, the UAE, and Singapore.

## Officials Frame Launch as Resilience Measure

Bank Indonesia Governor Destry Damayanti said the clearing bank would strengthen the rupiah-renminbi foreign-exchange and money-market ecosystem through three pillars: monetary operations, market development, and international cooperation.

"Strengthening this ecosystem is part of efforts to support the stability of the rupiah and strengthen Indonesia's external sector resilience," she said, according to Kontan.

Coordinating Minister for Economic Affairs Airlangga Hartarto said China is one of Indonesia's largest sources of non-oil and gas imports, and that expanding the use of non-dollar currencies for settlement would ease external pressures. He also expressed hope that wider LCT transactions would boost Indonesian exports to China, noting that Indonesia's trade balance with China currently runs a deficit.

On the Chinese side, PBOC Deputy Governor Tao Ling said the use of local currencies in trade and investment can improve transaction efficiency, reduce costs, and lower exchange-rate risk. She added that Bank of China, with its international network, can support liquidity and the smooth flow of cross-border transactions through cooperation with domestic lenders.

The Star, republishing Xinhua's report, noted that [Bank of China](https://www.thestar.com.my/aseanplus/aseanplus-news/2026/11/13/indonesia-launches-renminbi-rmb-clearing-bank) serves as the designated RMB clearing bank and that the ceremony was held at Bank Indonesia the same day.

## A Wider Pattern of RMB Internationalisation

The launch fits a broader expansion of China's RMB clearing network in 2026 and a dense sequence of China-Indonesia financial milestones. These include the 2021 bilateral local currency settlement mechanism, the 2025 local currency transaction framework and renewed currency swap agreement, cross-border QR code payment interconnectivity, and Indonesia's Panda bond issuance—CNY 7 billion in July 2026 and a further CNY 10 billion in November, according to Kontan.

Bank of China President Zhang Hui said the Jakarta clearing bank represents "a significant new fulcrum" in the bank's global RMB service network, committing to build bridges in cross-border payment, financing, green finance, and financial innovation. He said the bank would step up support for downstream mineral processing, energy infrastructure, advanced manufacturing, the digital economy, and the green transition, aligned with Indonesia's "Golden Indonesia 2045" vision.

## What to Watch

The clearing infrastructure alone does not guarantee the export gains Indonesian officials hope for; the trade deficit with China remains, and the benefits will depend on how quickly businesses adopt renminbi settlement. Market participants will also watch whether a second RMB clearing bank is designated—Deputy Governor Thomas Djiwandono had earlier indicated two were planned, though only Bank of China has been named so far.

For Beijing, the Jakarta launch is another step in advancing renminbi internationalisation and reducing dollar dependence in trade with major partners. For Indonesia, it is a test of whether deeper local-currency infrastructure can translate into greater financial resilience and stronger export competitiveness.
