# Xi Replies to Letter from 110 Australian Students

Chinese President Xi Jinping has replied to a joint letter from 110 Australian university students, encouraging them to keep contributing to China-Australia friendship, according to [Xinhua News Agency](https://www.news.cn/politics/leaders/20261106/c30de19d8a664076adae5a129f4986da/c.html). The reply, dated October 28, was made public by the state news agency on November 6.

The students—from institutions including the University of Sydney—had written jointly to Xi and Australian Prime Minister Anthony Albanese, describing a recent visit to China and expressing admiration for the country's long history, culture and technological innovation. Their letter also conveyed a wish to pass on China-Australia friendship and deepen mutual understanding between the two peoples.

## A Message to the Youth

In the reply, Xi said he was pleased to learn that the students had recently traveled to China for the "China-Australia Youth (University Students) Rowing and Dragon Boat Friendship Race," where they competed alongside Chinese university students and formed friendships that transcend national borders.

"China-Australia friendly exchanges have a long history, and the future of bilateral relations lies with the youth," Xi wrote, according to Xinhua. He called on the students to "become inheritors of China-Australia friendship and cooperation" so that the relationship can be "passed on from generation to generation, enduring endlessly."

## Deepening People-to-People Ties

The exchange reflects a broader warming in educational and people-to-people links between the two countries. According to a separate Xinhua [feature](https://www.news.cn/world/20260811/3c21bf34945a4477af53d8e3f27ca22b/c.html), data from China's Ministry of Education shows that Australian students studying in China exceeded 2,100 in 2024–2025, nearly doubling year-on-year.

That trend has been echoed in travel patterns. Australia's Travel Weekly, citing search platform Skyscanner, reported a 44 percent increase in China-related searches within Australia in the first half of the year, driven mainly by Gen Z travelers.

Institutional momentum has followed. The China-Australia Youth People-to-People Exchange Program, jointly launched by the Chinese Embassy in Australia and the Ministry of Education's Chinese-Foreign People-to-People Exchange Center, was officially inaugurated in Canberra in June 2026. It spans six thematic study-tour areas—rowing and dragon boat, youth music exchange, marine ecology, artificial intelligence, traditional Chinese medicine, and pottery and martial arts—and aims to send more than 500 Australian young people to China in 2026.

The students' visit is widely associated with the program's flagship first activity, the China-Australia University Students Rowing and Dragon Boat Friendship Challenge, held in Nanjing in September.

## Analysis: Diplomacy at the Grassroots

Xi's personal reply is a familiar instrument of Chinese public diplomacy: a gesture that signals high-level interest in people-to-people connections without committing to specific policy outcomes. By addressing the students directly and framing youth as the "future" of bilateral relations, Beijing is emphasizing the long horizon of its relationship with Australia—one that has weathered significant turbulence over the past several years.

For Australia, the exchange complements efforts to stabilize ties while managing differences on trade, security and regional policy. Educational and youth exchanges are among the least contentious channels available, and both governments have incentives to keep them active. The role of the visa-free policy, which commentators cited as encouraging Australian youth travel to China, adds a practical dimension to the diplomatic messaging.

## What's Next

The reply is unlikely to shift the broader strategic relationship on its own, but it reinforces a pattern of steady, low-key engagement. Observers will watch whether Canberra offers a reciprocal response—an Australian government statement on the letter was not immediately available at the time of reporting—and whether the exchange program's targets for 2026 are met.

For the 110 students, the message is more immediate: a presidential letter that frames their rowing and dragon-boating trips as part of something larger than a single visit.
