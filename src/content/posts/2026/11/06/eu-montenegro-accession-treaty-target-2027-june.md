# EU Aims to Sign Montenegro Accession Treaty by June 2027

The European Union aims to sign an accession treaty with Montenegro in June 2027, setting the small Balkan nation on course to become the bloc's 28th member state in 2028 — its first enlargement since Croatia joined in 2013. The target emerged from the European Commission's 2026 Enlargement Package, adopted on 4 November 2026, and was set out in an interview with Enlargement Commissioner Marta Kos published by [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/03/europese-unie-wil-in-juni-volgend-jaar-toetredingsverdrag-ondert/).

"Montenegro is on its way to becoming the next new member of the EU," Kos said. "The accession treaty is already being prepared."

## A Concrete Horizon After Years of Stagnation

According to [Deutsche Welle](https://www.dw.com/en/eu-says-montenegro-on-track-to-become-blocs-next-member-state-by-2028/a-79855011), Brussels believes accession negotiations with Montenegro could be completed as early as the end of this year, clearing the way for a treaty signature in June 2027. Because the treaty must then be ratified by all 27 member-state parliaments — a process that typically takes about a year — full membership would most likely follow in 2028 rather than 2027.

"This is testament to Montenegro's sustained efforts to advance on the European path and implement the reforms needed to turn EU membership into reality," the EU's executive said in its annual enlargement policy report.

Montenegro, a country of roughly 630,000 people — Kos compared its population to that of Antwerp — has been a candidate since 2010 and opened accession talks in 2012. It already uses the euro unilaterally and has been a NATO member since 2017, making it the most prepared of the current candidates. Even so, the Commission stresses that Podgorica must still deliver reforms, particularly on fundamental rights and financial services.

One lingering obstacle is Croatia, which has sought to slow Montenegro's progress over a set of bilateral disputes tied to the 1991–95 wars, including the Morinj detention camp, compensation for detainees and the search for missing Croatian citizens. Montenegro has moved to address some of these concerns, but the ratification of the treaty will ultimately require consensus among all member states.

## Four 'Frontrunners' Reshape the Agenda

The 2026 package identified four frontrunners — Montenegro, Albania, Moldova and Ukraine — and for the first time set out indicative roadmaps for the most advanced candidates, as [European Western Balkans](https://europeanwesternbalkans.com/2026/11/04/kos-presents-three-novelties-in-this-years-enlargement-package/) reported. Kos said 2026 marked the biggest progress on EU enlargement in 25 years, with 61 chapters opened or closed.

**Albania** aims to complete all its negotiating chapters by the end of 2027, roughly a year behind Montenegro. The Commission says Tirana must keep reforming its justice system and step up the fight against organised crime and corruption, while Kos flagged uncertainty over the impact of street protests against the government.

**Moldova**, a candidate only since 2022, has opened 25 of its 33 negotiating chapters this year. The Commission described its goal of completing reforms by early 2028 as "ambitious but achievable," though concerns remain over Russia's reaction and a domestic population divided between pro-European and pro-Russian camps.

**Ukraine** is the fourth frontrunner, though its path is the most complicated. The Commission praised Kyiv's progress despite the Russian invasion but said it must accelerate rule-of-law and democratic-institution reforms. Kos hopes all negotiating chapters with Ukraine will be opened by the end of the year.

"Ukraine is currently fighting to survive, but we set the bar just as high for them as for the other candidate countries. And that is not easy," she said. Asked whether Ukrainian membership within five years was realistic, Kos replied: "I believe that must be possible if we help Ukraine. Though it will also depend on how long the war lasts."

## Serbia's Opening After a Political Earthquake

Serbia presents perhaps the most striking shift. Progress had slowed under the government of Aleksandar Vučić, and the Commission's progress report was critical. But in late October 2026, a student-led movement defeated Vučić at the polls, and Kos said the result creates a chance for a fresh start.

"After Montenegro, they were actually already the furthest along, until steps backward were taken in recent years under the Vučić government," she said. "I have already received them here in Brussels. If they mean it seriously and really want to implement major reforms, then things could move very quickly with Serbia."

Beyond the frontrunners, the picture is more mixed. North Macedonia, Bosnia-Herzegovina and Serbia have shown slower progress; Kosovo lacks candidate status; and Georgia and Turkey remain furthest away because of democratic backsliding.

## Enlargement as a Security Imperative

The renewed push is driven substantially by geopolitics. Since Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine, integrating the Western Balkans, Moldova and Ukraine has been framed as a way to counter malign influence and stabilise the continent — a theme the Commission made explicit in this year's package.

"There is strength in numbers. A larger Europe means strengthening Europe's security, growing our geopolitical weight and our ability to project influence," said EU High Representative Kaja Kallas. "Membership is moving closer for the countries which deliver."

Commission President Ursula von der Leyen struck a similar note: "The significant progress achieved by some candidates over the last year allows us to turn the promise of enlargement into reality. Step by step, we are building a more complete, stronger Union."

Kos reached back to the EU's 2004 "big bang," when ten countries joined at once. "This is something we haven't seen since the 'big bang' of 2004," she said. "I feel that more and more European leaders realise it is important to bring all those countries on board if we want to protect ourselves in these geopolitical times."

A second novelty in the package is a new model of gradual integration, with a dialogue on closer sectoral cooperation to launch in the first quarter of 2027 — a way of giving candidates tangible benefits before full membership.

## What to Watch

The June 2027 treaty target is ambitious but conditional. The main risks are unresolved disputes with Croatia, the trajectory of the war in Ukraine, domestic political fragility in Albania, Moldova and Serbia, and the lengthy ratification process across 27 national parliaments.

For now, Brussels is projecting confidence that the long enlargement freeze is over. If the timeline holds, Montenegro would end more than a decade without a new member — and open the door for a wave of accessions that could reshape the continent's politics and security for years to come.
