# Shaanxi Stabbing Leaves 3 Dead, 2 Hurt; 500 Join Manhunt

A major criminal case in Shangnan County in China's northwestern Shaanxi Province has left three people dead and two injured, prompting a massive mountain manhunt for a knife-armed suspect who remained at large on Friday morning. The dead include two women, and the two injured are both police officers, according to [The Paper](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_34419084), citing CCTV's legal affairs channel.

As of 9:00 a.m. on November 6, the suspect had not been apprehended. More than 500 people — police, township officials and volunteers — were expected to join the search through rugged, forested terrain around Qingshan Town, with drones and police dogs deployed to track him down.

## How the violence unfolded

The sequence of events, reconstructed from witness and official accounts reported by [Beijing News](https://news.qq.com/rain/a/20261105A03O8I00), began in the early hours of November 4. At around 4:00 a.m., police received a call — reportedly placed by the suspect himself — claiming suspected prostitution at a business club in Shangnan County. When officers arrived to respond, one was wounded by the suspect.

About an hour later, at roughly 5:00 a.m., police received a report from a guard at the Railway Station Resettlement Community that a person was lying on the ground in unknown condition. Around ten minutes after that, a resident of another community about three kilometers away reported a woman lying on the ground downstairs.

According to a circulated joint investigation notice cited by Beijing News, the suspect's registered address before the crime was the Railway Station Resettlement Community — placing him near the scenes of the killings.

## A chase into the mountains

After the attack, the suspect fled by car. During the escape, his vehicle collided with a police car but kept going, sustaining severe body damage before breaking down. On the morning of November 4, searchers found the abandoned car on Shangyun Road near Lijia Gou Village.

At around 9:20 a.m., a searcher moving about 100 meters past the abandoned car into the village heard voices. Roughly 200 meters in, he saw a man walking east along a ridge line in a dark jacket, with something reflective at his right waist, apparently a metal object, talking on a phone.

"I called out to him, asked what he was doing; he didn't answer, just kept walking forward. After two or three minutes, he walked into the forest," the searcher told Beijing News. He later matched the figure to the suspect by photograph.

The notice described the suspect as short-haired and of medium height, wearing a blue suit, jeans and black leather shoes when he fled, and carrying a knife. Police warned he posed a risk of further violence.

## An arduous search

Searchers described difficult conditions. The mountain where the suspect is believed to be hiding sits at an elevation of about 800 meters, covered in dense brushwood and thick fallen leaves that make tracks hard to distinguish. Nighttime temperatures in the mountains hover around 6 to 7 degrees Celsius.

By the evening of November 5, police vehicles were stationed at every junction, and checkpoints had been set up at mountain-exit roads, with vehicles leaving the area inspected one by one, The Paper reported. The search force, initially estimated at 400 to 500 people, was expected to grow past 500 on November 6.

Shangnan County Public Security Bureau said a dedicated arrest team was working at full capacity, alongside a separate security team tasked with protecting residents, and that further case details would be released officially.

## What remains unclear

The suspect's identity and motive have not been officially disclosed, and the precise sequence and location of the three killings relative to the two residential communities remains incomplete. The condition of the injured officers has also not been publicly updated.

Shangnan County lies in Shangluo City, in southeastern Shaanxi within the Qinling mountain range — a rugged, heavily forested region that residents say is easy to hide in. The scale of the response, drawing on drones, dogs, roadblocks and hundreds of personnel, reflects the seriousness of a multi-fatality case in a remote rural area.

Authorities have urged residents to report any sightings rather than approach a suspect believed to still be armed. The coming hours will determine whether the intensified search can close in on a man who has so far eluded one of the region's largest manhunts in recent memory.
