# China Services Trade Rises 8.5% as Exports Outpace Imports

China's trade in services expanded 8.5% year-on-year in the first nine months of 2026, reaching 5.77 trillion yuan (about $810 billion), according to data released by the Ministry of Commerce on November 4. The figures, [reported by China News Service](https://www.chinanews.com.cn/cj/2026/11-04/10725782.shtml), show exports growing nearly three times faster than imports, a sign of strengthening domestic service-sector competitiveness.

Total services exports rose 14.8% to 2.27 trillion yuan, while imports climbed 4.8% to 3.50 trillion yuan. As a result, the country's structural services trade deficit narrowed by 132.7 billion yuan compared with the same period last year, to 1.23 trillion yuan.

## Transport Leads Growth

The fastest-growing category was transport services. According to [CCTV News](https://news.cctv.com/2026/11/04/ARTIUQ97Fufi93MdV4s6gURk261104.shtml), transport services trade jumped 33.9% to 1.28 trillion yuan, the highest growth rate of any sector and second in scale only to travel services and other commercial services. Transport exports grew 29.4% to 482.3 billion yuan, while imports rose 36.8% to 799.4 billion yuan.

The surge reflects China's robust goods trade and elevated international shipping and freight costs, which have made logistics a key engine of services growth.

## Knowledge-Intensive Services Shift

Beijing has been pushing to upgrade its services exports away from labor-intensive categories toward higher-value, knowledge-intensive services. The data suggests that pivot is gaining traction.

Knowledge-intensive services trade reached 2.53 trillion yuan, up 7.4%, and now accounts for 43.9% of total services trade. Knowledge-intensive exports rose 11.7% to 1.22 trillion yuan, making up 53.7% of total services exports. The standout performers were personal culture and entertainment services, which surged 69.1%, and insurance and pension services, which climbed 50.3%.

Li Jun, director of the International Services Trade Research Institute at the Chinese Academy of International Trade and Economic Cooperation, a MOFCOM-affiliated think tank, attributed the momentum to technology-driven sectors.

"Driven by AI and biotechnology innovation, exports of telecom/computer and information services, intellectual property royalties and other knowledge-intensive services have maintained steady growth," Li said. "In particular, personal culture and entertainment exports have maintained rapid growth for several consecutive months, becoming an important engine driving China's services export growth."

## Momentum Building Through the Year

The 8.5% figure caps a year of accelerating growth. Monthly MOFCOM releases tracked steadily upward: services trade rose 4.9% in January-April, 6% in January-May, and 8.2% in January-July before reaching 8.5% for the nine-month period, suggesting the sector gathered pace as 2026 progressed.

That trajectory matters for a country that is the world's largest goods trader but has historically run a structural deficit in services. A narrowing deficit, combined with faster export than import growth, points to improving competitiveness rather than mere volume expansion.

## What's Next

The services figures arrive amid broader efforts to rebalance China's economy toward higher-value, consumption- and technology-driven growth. The rapid rise in culture and entertainment exports, likely spanning gaming, streaming and digital content, signals a growing digital services footprint with implications for soft power as well as trade.

Still, challenges remain. Travel services, the largest single category by scale, depend heavily on cross-border mobility, while the transport boom is partly tied to freight costs that could moderate. Whether the knowledge-intensive shift can sustain its double-digit export growth, and continue narrowing the deficit, will be the key test in the months ahead. For now, the data offers Beijing a measure of reassurance that its services upgrade is moving in the intended direction.
