# Russell Brand Convicted of Rape and Sexual Assault

British comedian and actor Russell Brand has been convicted of one count of rape and three counts of sexual assault after a four-week trial at Southwark Crown Court in London, capping a scandal that first surfaced through a joint media investigation in 2023. The 51-year-old was cleared of two further counts of rape and one count of attempted indecent assault. He was refused bail and remanded into custody, with sentencing scheduled for Monday, November 9.

The judge, Mr Justice Bennathan, told Brand that the convictions would bring "inevitably a significant custodial term," according to [The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/culture/2026/nov/05/russell-brand-found-guilty-rape-sexual-assault). The jury — eight men and four women — deliberated for about eight and a half hours before returning its verdicts on Thursday.

## The Convictions

The charges stemmed from allegations by six women spanning roughly 1999 to 2009. Brand, who pleaded not guilty to all seven offences, was convicted on four counts relating to four women, as summarized by [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/11/05/nx-s1-6030720/russell-brand-rape-sexual-assault-prison).

He was found guilty of raping a woman he had met at a 2009 stand-up show at the Hammersmith Apollo, who later went to his home. He was also convicted of sexually assaulting three women: an MTV worker at a party in Soho in 2004, a Choice FM worker at the Capital Radio building around 2004 or 2005, and a Channel 4 worker in 2009. He was acquitted of raping the MTV worker after the party, of raping a woman he met on the fringes of the 1999 Labour Party conference in Bournemouth, and of attempting to indecently assault an MTV worker in 2001.

## Prosecutor and Police React

David Roberts, senior Crown prosecutor at the London rape and serious sexual assault unit, said the evidence showed Brand to be "a calculated sexual predator using his growing fame and power to create situations in which he could pursue women sexually regardless of what they wanted."

Detective Superintendent Andy Furphy of the Metropolitan Police, who led the investigation, praised the women who came forward. "This verdict is first and foremost a testament to the extraordinary courage, resilience and determination of the women who reported what happened to them," he said in a [statement](https://news.met.police.uk/news/russell-brand-convicted-of-rape-and-other-sexual-offences-514748). "Brand used his influence, status and fame to exploit and sexually offend against women. As his profile grew, so too did his sense of entitlement."

He added: "I hope this conviction sends a clear message that no one is above the law, no matter how famous or powerful they may be."

## A Career That Spanned Comedy, Hollywood and Politics

Brand rose to fame as a stand-up comedian around the turn of the millennium, known for shock-jock humor that also drew allegations of misogyny. He became an MTV presenter, wrote the 2007 memoir *My Booky Wook* about his heroin addiction and treatment for sex addiction, and later starred in Hollywood films including *Forgetting Sarah Marshall*. He was married to pop star Katy Perry from 2010 to 2012.

He then reinvented himself as a political commentator, launching a YouTube channel in 2014 and writing columns for The Guardian, before moving into wellness content and expressing vaccine scepticism during the COVID-19 pandemic. In recent years he found a following among parts of the American right. He has three children with his wife, Laura Brand.

## How the Case Unfolded

The allegations first became public in September 2023, when The Times, The Sunday Times and Channel 4's Dispatches published a joint investigation in which four women accused Brand of rape, sexual assault and emotional abuse. Brand denied the claims at the time, posting a video in which he said the "mainstream media" was out to get him.

The Metropolitan Police subsequently opened an investigation. Prosecutors charged Brand with multiple offences, and his trial began in October 2026. According to the Met, detectives spoke to more than 100 witnesses, took more than 400 statements and reviewed over 1,400 documents, navigating the absence of CCTV or forensic evidence given the age of the allegations.

The case has renewed scrutiny of the institutions that employed Brand at the height of his fame. The BBC said a 2025 report had examined how complaints about his conduct were handled and reiterated an apology to those affected, while Channel 4 said it recognized "the profound impact of these crimes" and commended the courage of the witnesses.

## What's Next

Brand's sentencing on Monday will determine the length of a prison term the judge has already signalled will be substantial. The Metropolitan Police said its investigation "remains ongoing," and Brand faces further rape allegations from two additional women, according to The Guardian. That means the legal reckoning may not end with this week's verdict.
