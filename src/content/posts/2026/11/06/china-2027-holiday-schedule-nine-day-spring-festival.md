# China 2027 Holidays: 9-Day Spring Festival, Fewer Make-Ups

China's State Council has released its public holiday schedule for 2027, headlined by a nine-day Spring Festival break and a marked reduction in make-up workdays — the so-called *tiaoxiu* system that has long frustrated Chinese workers. The schedule, issued on the afternoon of November 5 as [Circular Guobanfamingdian (2026) No. 10](https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/content/202611/content_7085101.htm), makes 2027 the year with the fewest make-up days in recent memory.

## A Nine-Day Spring Festival

The 2027 Spring Festival holiday will run from February 5 — Lunar New Year's Eve, a Friday — through February 13, the eighth day of the first lunar month, a Saturday. At nine days, it matches 2026 as the longest Spring Festival break on record.

Critically, the extended holiday does not come at the cost of a punishing return to work. To prevent a seven-day work stretch after the break, the government has designated two weekend make-up days: January 31 and February 14, both Sundays. As a result, the longest consecutive work stretch anywhere in the 2027 calendar is capped at six days.

[The Paper](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_34412735), citing CCTV News, described the plan as bringing back the "nine-day extended Spring Festival holiday," noting that 2027 will be the year with the fewest make-up workdays in recent years.

## Four Holidays, Zero Make-Up Days

Beyond Spring Festival, the schedule grants a noticeable reprieve across the calendar. New Year's Day runs January 1–3 with no make-up days; Qingming (Tomb-Sweeping) spans April 3–5, connecting naturally to a weekend; and both Dragon Boat Festival (June 9) and Mid-Autumn Festival (September 15) fall on Wednesdays and require no adjustment at all. Labor Day stretches five days (May 1–5) with just one make-up workday on May 8, while National Day runs seven days (October 1–7) with make-up days on September 26 and October 9.

In total, four of the year's seven statutory holidays involve no make-up workdays whatsoever.

## Why the Change Matters

The shift is rooted in a significant 2024 reform. In November 2024, the State Council amended the Measures for Public Holidays of National Annual Festivals and Memorial Days, adding Lunar New Year's Eve and May 2 as statutory holidays effective January 1, 2025. That change raised China's total statutory paid public holidays from 11 to 13 days — the most significant expansion of holiday entitlements in over a decade.

According to Liu Min, deputy director of the Regional Strategy Division II at the National Development and Reform Commission's Institute of Land Development and Regional Economy, the reform explains the longer break. "Since 2025, Spring Festival statutory holidays increased from three to four days," she said, as [reported](https://www.sohu.com/a/1097680829_121106875) by Guangzhou Labor Union. "In 2027, Lunar New Year's Eve falls on Friday February 5, and connecting with the weekend yields nine days. This nine-day holiday does not require adding extra make-up workdays on top of prior arrangements, better balancing rest rights and production order."

Liu added that the guiding principle behind the schedule is what officials call "micro-adjustment": when a holiday falls on a Tuesday or Thursday, aim to shift only one workday to connect with the weekend; when it falls on a weekend, grant compensatory rest; and except for Spring Festival and National Day, holidays falling on a Wednesday are generally left alone.

## A Response to Public Frustration

For years, the *tiaoxiu* system has been a lightning rod for public discontent. Workers have complained that shifting weekends to manufacture long breaks — then requiring make-up workdays — disrupts rest rhythms and produces "fake holidays" in which weekends are consumed to create the illusion of continuous leave. The government's deliberate reduction of make-up days in 2027 reads as a direct response to that sentiment.

The circular goes further, encouraging employers and individuals to combine paid annual leave with public holidays to form longer continuous breaks, and to stagger travel to ease congestion. It also reminds regional authorities to arrange duty rosters, security, and epidemic-control measures during the holidays.

## What to Watch

Whether the schedule delivers on its promise will depend largely on implementation. The circular's encouragement of combining annual leave with public holidays is advisory, not mandatory, and private employers may interpret it differently than state-owned enterprises. The interaction with school calendars and travel-booking cycles will also shape how the nine-day Spring Festival plays out in practice.

Still, the direction of travel is clear. After years of complaints, Beijing is signaling that it is willing to loosen the make-up-day burden — a small but symbolically significant concession to public sentiment, and one that will be watched closely when the 2028 schedule is drafted.
