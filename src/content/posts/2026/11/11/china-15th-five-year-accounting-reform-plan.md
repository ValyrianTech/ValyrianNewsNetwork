# China Unveils 15th Five-Year Accounting Reform Plan

China's Ministry of Finance on 11 November publicly released its *Accounting Reform and Development Plan for the 15th Five-Year Plan Period* (《会计改革与发展“十五五”规划》), setting the policy direction for modernizing the country's accounting profession and strengthening financial reporting standards through 2030, [Xinhua News](https://www.news.cn/politics/20261111/45cb0a3a99ac487d8d9b519152e656ae/c.html) reported.

The plan defines the development environment and overall requirements for accounting work over the 2026–2030 cycle, lays out the deployment of key tasks, and sets out the safeguard measures meant to guarantee delivery. It is a framework document rather than a single event-driven announcement, reflecting national priorities for modernizing accounting governance in the opening year of the 15th Five-Year Plan period.

## What the plan contains

According to Xinhua, the released document is structured around three major sections (三大板块) comprising a total of 10 parts. These cover the development environment and overall requirements for accounting work during the 15th Five-Year Plan period, a deployment of the key tasks, and the supporting measures designed to ensure those tasks are carried out.

At the heart of the plan is a set of seven headline goals for the five-year period. They call for the continuous consolidation of the rule-of-law foundation of accounting, stronger support from accounting talent, high-quality and efficient development of the accounting services industry, better functioning of accounting supervision, continuing improvement in the digital-intelligent level of accounting, a significantly enhanced contribution to international accounting governance, and innovative results in accounting theory building.

"Accounting is an important component of economic management, playing an important role in optimizing resource allocation, maintaining economic order, and smoothing the circulation of the national economy," the ministry stated in the plan, as relayed by Xinhua.

## Background and consultation process

The release follows a formal public consultation. The Ministry of Finance circulated a draft of the plan for comment on 2 July 2026 under document number 财办会〔2026〕19号, asking provinces, municipalities, and relevant agencies to give feedback by 26 July 2026. The [consultation letter and its two attached drafts](https://m.hainankj.cn/h-nd-1477.html) were subsequently circulated through provincial accounting platforms.

The finalized plan arrives in the opening year of the 15th Five-Year Plan period, a five-year national development cycle running from 2026 to 2030. In China's governance model, line ministries typically issue sectoral “十五五” plans to translate the broad objectives of the national Five-Year Plan into operational policy for their respective fields.

The plan continues several themes that have shaped Beijing's approach to accounting governance. These include strengthening the legal and regulatory basis for accounting (会计法), cultivating professional talent, promoting the growth and quality of the certified public accountant (CPA) firm industry, tightening supervision and inspection, advancing the "数智化" digital-intelligent transformation of accounting, and increasing China's influence in international accounting standard-setting.

## Analysis: what the goals signal

The seven goals are notable less for what is new than for where they place the emphasis. Three signal a continued push toward integration with global practice — high-quality development of the accounting services industry, a greater contribution to international accounting governance, and innovative accounting theory. The first two suggest Beijing wants Chinese firms and standards-setters to play a larger role in shaping international norms, not merely to adopt them.

A second cluster centers on the domestic regulatory architecture. Consolidating the rule-of-law foundation, strengthening talent support, and improving the effectiveness of supervision all point toward a tighter, more professionally staffed oversight system. Read together with the digital-intelligent objective, these goals imply greater reliance on data systems and automated reporting as tools of both efficiency and control.

The emphasis on accounting services as an industry to be developed to a "high-quality, efficient" standard also matters economically. Building a stronger domestic accounting services sector gives Chinese companies and auditors a firmer footing as they operate and invest abroad.

Because the Xinhua news flash summarizes the plan without enumerating its 10 individual parts or detailed sub-targets, the granular measures behind each goal remain to be seen as the ministry and local finance bureaus publish implementing guidance.

## What to watch

The immediate test will be implementation. Ministry of Finance and provincial finance departments will need to translate the plan's goals into concrete regulations, talent programs, and industry policies over the next five years. Watch for follow-on documents that detail the sub-targets behind each of the seven goals, particularly in accounting digital-intelligent transformation and participation in international standard-setting. How quickly the accounting services industry is opened, professionalized, and integrated with global practice will be the clearest measure of whether the plan delivers on its ambitions.
