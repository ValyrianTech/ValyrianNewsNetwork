# China Outlines Livelihood Measures for 15th Five-Year Plan

China will roll out a package of social-policy measures over the 15th Five-Year Plan period (2026-2030) aimed at safeguarding and improving people's livelihoods, with a focus on education, healthcare, and care for the elderly and young children, [CCTV News (央视新闻) reported](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_34452205), citing the National Development and Reform Commission (NDRC).

The report, carried on CCTV's Xinwen Lianbo (新闻联播) broadcast of 10 November 2026, frames the coming measures around the state's commitment to "focus on the people's urgent, difficult, worrying and anxious problems" and to implement "more fair, inclusive, precise and effective social policies."

## Context: Livelihood as a Policy Priority

The announcement comes in what official discourse calls the "opening year" of the 15th Five-Year Plan. The 20th Central Committee's Fourth Plenum set the plan's guiding direction, and state media have repeatedly stressed the principle that "in Chinese-style modernization, people's livelihood is paramount."

The measures reported this week represent the implementation-level detail behind the headline goals of the five-year cycle — translating broad commitments on employment, income distribution, and public services into concrete spending and construction priorities.

## Key Measures by Sector

### Education

Under the plan, the state will support the construction of ordinary senior high school teaching buildings, dormitories, and related facilities to increase student placement capacity. It will also back qualified localities in establishing new-type research universities and build a batch of high-level vocational undergraduate schools with distinctive specializations.

### Healthcare

In the health sector, priority support will go to county-level shared centers for ECG diagnosis, clinical testing, and medical imaging. The plan also targets improved service levels at key central township health centers and continued renovation of hospital wards to make the inpatient experience more comfortable.

### Elderly Care and Childcare

For elderly care, the focus is on renovating and upgrading outdated care institutions and updating equipment, improving capacity to care for disabled and dementia-affected elderly. On childcare, the state will push for full prefecture-level-city coverage of comprehensive childcare service centers and develop integrated childcare-kindergarten services, while encouraging employers to provide childcare — giving families more and better options.

## Employment and Social Security

The measures reported this week form part of a broader package that also encompasses employment and social security. Earlier reporting on the 15th Five-Year Plan indicated targets including maintaining a "considerable scale" of new urban employment alongside a suite of quantified indicators across development goals.

Specific employment and social security measures were not detailed in the CCTV broadcast summary captured, but the framing of the announcement as a comprehensive livelihood package suggests they will be elaborated in subsequent policy documents.

## Analysis: A Signal on Spending Priorities

The emphasis on county-level medical infrastructure and township health centers points to a continued drive to narrow the urban-rural gap in public services — a long-standing challenge as China balances growth between its prosperous coastal cities and its interior. The push for new-type research universities and high-level vocational undergraduate schools likewise reflects a dual goal: building elite scientific capacity while expanding practical, skills-oriented higher education.

The childcare provisions — particularly the encouragement of employer-provided care — signal an awareness that supporting working families requires policy beyond direct subsidy, extending into workplace arrangements and service infrastructure.

## What's Next

With 2026 framed as the opening year of the 15th Five-Year Plan, the NDRC measures are likely to be followed by sector-specific implementation plans and budget allocations. The key questions ahead are how the commitments will be funded at the local level, where fiscal pressure has constrained social spending, and whether the employment and social security components of the package will be detailed with the same specificity as the education, health, and care measures.

For now, the announcement confirms that livelihood improvement remains a central organizing theme of China's current development cycle — and that the state intends to be judged on the delivery of tangible services to ordinary citizens.
