# China's Han Zheng, Guterres Meet at COP31 in Antalya

Chinese Vice President Han Zheng met UN Secretary-General Antonio Guterres on the sidelines of the COP31 climate summit in Antalya, Turkey, on 10 November, using a meeting cast as a personal channel from President Xi Jinping to reaffirm Beijing's climate commitments and its backing for the Paris Agreement, according to [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/world/20261111/f2a808220b49435187c178a21320eb33/c.html).

The meeting took place during the COP31 Leaders' Summit, within the 31st Conference of the Parties to the UN Framework Convention on Climate Change, which is running in the Turkish Mediterranean city from 9 to 20 November under the presidency of Turkey's Murat Kurum.

## A special representative, not a vice premier

Han attended in his capacity as **Special Representative of President Xi Jinping** — a role that lent the encounter diplomatic weight beyond a routine ministerial exchange. Han, who served as a vice premier from 2018 to 2023, became Vice President of the People's Republic of China in March 2023. The official readout emphasized that Han conveyed Xi's cordial greetings to Guterres, and that Guterres asked Han to pass on his sincere greetings to the Chinese president in return.

The setting was not incidental. COP31 opened against the stark admission that this is the first conference held in the knowledge that the world will overshoot 1.5°C of warming — a backdrop that has thrust China's role as the world's dominant producer and installer of renewable energy into the center of the negotiations.

## What China put on the table

According to the Xinhua readout, Han said China "attaches great importance to the issue of climate change" and pointed to the 2035 Nationally Determined Contribution announced by Xi the previous year, which pledged that China would "strive to do better." Han added that China has now set 2030 targets for non-fossil fuel sources to reach 50% of electricity generation and for electricity to reach 35% of final energy consumption.

Han stressed that Beijing would "keep its promises and act resolutely," vowing that China will continue to provide "valuable stability and certainty for global climate governance." He also reaffirmed that China would "firmly uphold and fully implement" the UNFCCC and the Paris Agreement, expand South-South climate cooperation with developing countries, and actively support the UN's "Early Warnings for All" initiative.

The Chinese side also used the meeting to press a familiar negotiating point: that developing countries face shortages of funding, backward technology and insufficient capacity as they implement their own climate plans, and that the international community must help close those gaps. That framing aligns China with the G-77/China bloc and the BASIC group of Brazil, South Africa, India and China, which at the summit's opening pushed for progress on adaptation finance and for wealthy nations to honor their obligations, as detailed by the [IISD Earth Negotiations Bulletin](https://enb.iisd.org/antalya-un-climate-change-conference-cop31-daily-report-9nov2026).

## Guterres: China 'matches words with deeds'

Guterres's remarks, as relayed by Xinhua, were conspicuously warm. He described China as "an important pillar of multilateralism and a key supporter of the United Nations," and said that on climate change China "matches words with deeds, and its actual actions always exceed its commitments."

He praised China as the world's renewable energy leader and credited it with a "substantive contribution" to the decline in global greenhouse gas emissions. At what he called a difficult moment for global climate action, Guterres said the UN's efforts "rely heavily on China's contribution and support," and he expressed hope for closer coordination to send a positive signal from COP31 and to help reverse the upward trend in global temperatures.

## Why it matters

The exchange underscores a strategic interdependence at the heart of the UN climate process. China is the world's largest greenhouse gas emitter, but it is also the indispensable engine of the clean-energy transition — and the UNFCCC Secretariat, which has flagged funding shortfalls, depends on major economies to keep the multilateral machinery functioning. For Beijing, the meeting was an opportunity to burnish its credentials as a leader of the Global South and a defender of multilateralism at a time of geopolitical fragmentation.

It also places climate diplomacy squarely within Xi's personal diplomatic channel: by dispatching his special representative rather than a technical minister, Xi signaled that Beijing wants the Guterres relationship managed at the highest level.

## What to watch

COP31 is scheduled to run through 20 November, with the presidency aiming for a party-driven outcome and high-level summaries expected in the coming days. The key question is whether China's stated domestic targets — 50% non-fossil electricity generation and 35% electricity in final energy consumption by 2030 — will be matched by concrete commitments in the summit's final decisions on finance, adaptation and the phase-down of fossil fuels.

For now, the readout from Antalya is a message of continuity: Beijing wants to be seen as the stable, predictable anchor of global climate governance — and Guterres, facing a difficult summit, appears eager to reinforce that framing.
