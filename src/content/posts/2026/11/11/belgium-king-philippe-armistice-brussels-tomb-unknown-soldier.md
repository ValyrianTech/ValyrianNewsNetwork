# Belgium's King Philippe Commemorates Armistice in Brussels

King Philippe of the Belgians marked Armistice Day on Wednesday at the Tomb of the Unknown Soldier in Brussels, presiding over the annual military ceremony that honours the dead of both World Wars and Belgian soldiers killed on peacekeeping missions since 1945. The 108th anniversary of the 1918 ceasefire was distinguished this year by an unexpected detail: the sovereign appeared wearing a surgical face mask, which Belgian media reported was because he is ill.

According to [RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/11-novembre-le-roi-philippe-masque-commemore-l-armistice-sur-la-tombe-du-soldat-inconnu-a-bruxelles-11814056), the King arrived shortly before 11:00, a few minutes after the ceremony began, accompanied by a mounted escort. The ceremony was held at the Congress Column on Place du Congres, the symbolic heart of Belgian remembrance, and drew senior figures from government and the institutions of state.

## A ceremony of remembrance

The King reviewed the assembled military units before laying a wreath of flowers on the tomb, observing a moment of reflection there. He then relit the Eternal Flame before the monument, which dates from 1922 and symbolises Belgium's war victims and the soldiers who died in the First World War. He concluded by saluting the presidents of several patriotic associations, army veterans and disabled former servicemen.

As [La Libre Belgique](https://www.lalibre.be/belgique/societe/2026/11/11/une-apparition-remarquee-pour-le-roi-philippe-lors-des-commemorations-du-11-novembre-MDFNF75XG5A2XKG4QE3HVHQ7ZQ/) reported, the ceremony paid tribute not only to the Unknown Soldier but to the victims of both World Wars and to soldiers who have fallen on peacekeeping and peace-restoration missions since 1945, as detailed in a Royal Palace communique issued a few days earlier. Various branches of the Belgian Defence forces paraded during the commemoration.

The service also underscored the allied dimension of Armistice remembrance. Prime Minister Bart De Wever (N-VA), Defence Minister Theo Francken (N-VA) and Interior Minister Bernard Quintin (MR) attended, alongside representatives of the Constitutional Court, the Court of Cassation, the Brussels Region and the City of Brussels, and the presidents of the Chamber of Representatives and the Senate. US Ambassador to Belgium Bill White was present and spoke with ministers after the ceremony, and a delegation of students from the Duke of York's Royal Military School in the United Kingdom also attended.

De Wever was noted as one of the few attendees wearing a poppy in his buttonhole, a tradition more closely associated with Britain and the Commonwealth than with continental commemorations.

## The mask that framed the coverage

The most remarked-upon element of the 2026 ceremony was the King's face mask. La Libre reported that the sovereign appeared with it unexpectedly, that he is understood to be ill and wished to take no risk, and that the Palace did not comment on the matter. No outlet specified the nature of the illness. Photographs distributed by the Belga agency carried captions noting that the King was wearing a face mask because he was ill.

The image carried a distinct visual echo of the pandemic years, when masked monarchs and reduced ceremonies became familiar. It drew attention largely because the Palace stayed silent: in the absence of an official explanation, the mask itself became the story around an otherwise carefully scripted act of national remembrance.

## A tradition rooted in 1922

The Tomb of the Unknown Soldier has anchored Belgium's Armistice commemorations for more than a century. In November 1922, the remains of five unknown Belgian soldiers who fell in the Great War were transported from military cemeteries across the country to Bruges. There, on 10 November, the Unknown Soldier was selected among them; his coffin was interred the following day, 11 November 1922, at the foot of the Congress Column in Brussels, in the presence of King Albert I.

The King's participation is an annual fixture. The 2025 edition likewise saw Philippe at the Tomb of the Unknown Soldier, in a ceremony that coincided with 80 years since the end of the Second World War.

## What's next

Armistice Day is a federal public holiday in Belgium, and the ceremony at the Congress Column remains the focal point of the national calendar of remembrance. The questions that linger after Wednesday are modest but persistent: whether the Palace will say anything further about the King's health, and whether the broadening of the ceremony's scope, from the trenches of 1918 to peacekeeping missions of the present century, will continue to reshape how Belgium marks its war dead in the years ahead.

For now, the ritual endured much as it has for more than a hundred years: a wreath, a flame, and a salute to those who did not return.
