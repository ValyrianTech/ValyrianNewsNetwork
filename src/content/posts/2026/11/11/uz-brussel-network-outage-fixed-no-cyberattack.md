# UZ Brussel Outage Fixed After 24 Hours, No Cyberattack

A major computer network failure that disrupted electronic patient records at UZ Brussel and three affiliated hospitals was resolved on the morning of Wednesday 11 November, roughly 24 hours after it began. The University Hospital of Brussels, located in Jette, said there was no indication of a cyberattack and that no data breach had been detected, as [VRT NWS reported](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/11/netwerkproblemen-uz-brussel-na-24-uren-opgelost-geen-sprake-van/).

## What Happened

The outage began around 11:00 on Tuesday 10 November, when electronic patient records (EPD) could no longer be entered, retrieved or updated. Making new appointments and telephone contact also failed. UZ Brussel suspended all planned operations and consultations, and its emergency department was diverted to surrounding hospitals via the 112 emergency number.

At exactly 11:00 the following day — 24 hours to the minute after the problems started — UZ Brussel announced that the network was working again and that normal operations could resume. The timing coincided with Armistice Day, a Belgian public holiday on which no appointments had been scheduled, meaning already-hospitalised patients were monitored on paper until the systems came back online.

## A Shared Platform, a Shared Failure

The disruption did not stay confined to the Brussels campus. Three partner hospitals that share **PrimUZ**, the electronic patient-record platform developed at UZ Brussel, also lost access to their medical records and reverted to pen-and-paper record-keeping: AZ Monica in Antwerp, Heilig Hartziekenhuis in Lier and Sint-Andriesziekenhuis in Tielt.

According to [HLN's detailed follow-up](https://www.hln.be/brussel/netwerk-uz-brussel-hersteld-na-grote-it-storing-ook-problemen-in-tielt-en-lier-verholpen-geen-sprake-van-cyberaanval-of-datalek~aad79b49/), institutions running PrimUZ on their own or separate servers — including AZ Jan Palfijn in Ghent and Sint-Elisabethziekenhuis in Zottegem — experienced no disruption at all. That split points directly to a structural vulnerability: the hospitals that went down were the ones connected to UZ Brussel's central server.

By Wednesday afternoon the partner hospitals were progressively restarting. Sint-Andriesziekenhuis in Tielt was fully operational, while Heilig Hartziekenhuis in Lier completed internal testing before a full restart. "Everything is working normally again, all appointments and procedures go ahead as planned," Lier spokesperson Barbara Kempeneers said, adding that patients whose appointments were cancelled would be called to reschedule. AZ Monica, which had been able to disconnect from the affected systems in time, was conducting final tests with the aim of being fully operational by the afternoon.

## No Cyberattack, but No Explanation Either

UZ Brussel moved quickly to rule out the most feared scenario. "There is no question of a cyberattack and no data breach was established," the hospital said. Patient safety, it stressed, was guaranteed throughout and "was never in danger at any point," according to spokesperson Karolien Deprez, who noted that hospitalised patients were being followed up with pen and paper.

The hospital declined to say what did cause the failure. "A hospital is one of the most complex environments in terms of IT infrastructure, with extensive security measures and strongly integrated systems. That is why we cannot give further details on this," it said.

That silence is likely to sustain scrutiny. AZ Monica, one of the affected partner hospitals, spent more than a month recovering from a severe cyberattack earlier this year. [VRT NWS reported](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/02/17/az-monica-deurne-cyberaanval-opnieuw-digitaal-website-heropstart/) that the Antwerp hospital only returned to running almost all of its digital systems in mid-February, and that the Antwerp prosecutor's office was still investigating the attack. Against that backdrop, an unexplained 24-hour outage across a shared hospital network is unlikely to be accepted at face value by everyone.

## The Wider Knock-On Effects

The failure rippled outward. In Vilvoorde, the AZ Jan Portaels emergency department became overwhelmed by patients diverted from UZ Brussel, with a spokesperson saying the service "was full" as a result. The 24-hour disruption also highlighted how quickly a single IT fault can cascade across a region's healthcare capacity.

Separately, several Belgian health insurance funds reported database problems at the same time, but these were unrelated to the hospital outage. A software update to the Kruispuntdatabank (Crossroads Bank) temporarily made some files unavailable, meaning some benefit payments could be delayed by a few days. The socialist health fund Solidaris confirmed the issue and said benefits would be paid by Friday at the latest.

## What to Watch

The immediate crisis is over, but the incident leaves several open questions. Chief among them is the root cause: UZ Brussel has ruled out a cyberattack but has not explained what went wrong. The episode also exposes a concentration risk in Belgian hospital IT, with multiple institutions depending on one centrally hosted patient-record platform. Whether partner hospitals now reconsider more resilient or decentralised hosting arrangements is likely to be a live debate in the months ahead.

For now, the hospitals are back online and patients whose appointments were cancelled are being contacted to reschedule. But the 24-hour outage has served as a reminder of how deeply digital infrastructure now underpins the delivery of care — and how much depends on it working.
