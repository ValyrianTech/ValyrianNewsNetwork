# Meghan Stays Silent in Brussels as Harry Speaks Alone

Meghan, the Duchess of Sussex, withdrew from a planned joint address to the European Parliament in Brussels at the last moment on Tuesday, leaving her husband Prince Harry to deliver a solo keynote on children's online safety, [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/10/harry-en-meghan-europees-parlement/) reports. The decision came days after her estranged father, Thomas Markle Sr., 82, was arrested in Florida on federal charges of possessing child sexual abuse material (CSAM).

The visit to the Belgian capital was the couple's first joint working engagement in Europe since they relocated back to England. Meghan's appearance alongside Harry marked the first time she had been seen in public since the allegations against her father surfaced. She chose silence, reportedly because she did not feel "strong enough emotionally" to speak while her father's legal case is ongoing.

## A Plan Changed at the Last Minute

The Sussexes had travelled to Brussels at the invitation of the European Parliament's Intergroup on Children's Rights. The original programme called for a joint keynote of roughly fifteen minutes on the importance of digital safety for children. Meghan was due to introduce her husband before the assembled lawmakers and press.

Instead, Harry opened the **Lost Screen Memorial**, a travelling art installation commemorating children who died after online harm. After the address, the couple together opened an exhibition and visited a memorial installation for children who died as a result of online bullying. Meghan accompanied Harry for these segments, but did not speak.

According to [RTE News](https://www.rte.ie/news/europe/2026/1110/1598637-harry-meghan-brussels/), citing the Press Association, the withdrawal was attributed to Meghan not being "strong enough emotionally" to take the podium. The couple ignored questions from journalists; reporters had been briefed in advance that no questions would be taken, a strategy widely seen as protecting Meghan's silence from interfering with the US legal process.

## Harry's Message: Protection, Not Abstinence

Harry's speech steered clear of direct reference to his father-in-law, focusing instead on the broader cause that brought the couple to Brussels. "I'm not here as an expert or a politician. I'm here as a father. My wife, as a mother," he told the parliament, as reported by RTE.

He pressed lawmakers on why child safety online so quickly becomes complicated. "Why, when we ask whether it can keep a child safe, does everything suddenly become complicated?" he asked. "I am not asking for a world without technology. That isn't possible, it isn't what young people want, and it certainly isn't what I want for my own children."

"All children should be able to go online and discover something wonderful. A friend. An interest that becomes a passion. A place where they feel understood," he said. "They deserve to feel safe wherever they are. In a classroom. On a playground. At any time, no matter where they are."

The address reflected what Harry described as a lifelong disposition toward prevention. "My approach to life, and to the value of it, has perhaps unsurprisingly always been about prevention," he said. "Preventing harm before it happens, rather than waiting for tragedy before deciding something must change."

## The Charges Behind the Silence

The context for Meghan's withdrawal is a federal case that has gripped transatlantic media. According to the [US Department of Justice](https://www.justice.gov/usao-cdca/pr/thomas-wayne-markle-arrested-federal-complaint-charging-him-possessing-sexually), Thomas Wayne Markle was arrested on 6 November at a senior care facility in Lakeland, Florida, and charged with one count of possession of child pornography. Prosecutors allege he kept CSAM, including sexually explicit photographs of himself with prepubescent girls, in two Los Angeles storage units he had rented since 2008.

Agents seized approximately 78 items, including slides, film negatives and books containing suspected CSAM, according to an affidavit cited in the DOJ release. Two images allegedly depict Markle with a nude prepubescent girl; a third shows a nude prepubescent girl in what appears to be a Philippine motel room. First Assistant US Attorney Bill Essayli said the allegations are "extremely serious" and pledged to prosecute "to the fullest extent of federal law." FBI Director Kash Patel said Markle "allegedly targeted innocent children and sought to steal their innocence."

Per [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/07/thomas-markle-onder-elektronisch-toezicht/), a US court placed Markle under electronic monitoring at a Florida care home, ordered a psychiatric evaluation amid concerns over dementia, and restricted his travel to two districts. Markle, who suffered strokes and had a leg amputated last year, denies the accusations. If convicted, he faces a statutory maximum of 20 years in federal prison.

## A Family Rupture Years in the Making

Meghan definitively broke off contact with her father last year, according to BBC News, and has said she deliberately kept her children, Prince Archie and Princess Lilibet, away from him since 2019, when she first became aware of disturbing allegations. In a written statement, she said she hoped "justice will be done and that the protection of innocent children remains paramount," calling the alleged abuse "disgusting."

Reports indicate that Meghan's mother, Doria Ragland, Markle's ex-wife, to whom he was married from 1979 to 1987, is cooperating with the FBI investigation. It was reportedly Ragland who first alerted police in 2019, in a letter, to Markle's "disturbing" trips to Asia.

## Analysis: A Delicate Calibration

Meghan's decision to appear but not speak is a carefully calibrated response to an impossible situation. By travelling to Brussels, she honoured a commitment to a cause, child online safety, that now carries painful personal resonance. By staying silent, she avoids creating a public statement that could be seized upon in the US legal proceedings surrounding her father.

Royal commentator Pascale Mertens, quoted by VRT NWS, said the timing was "particularly bad" for the couple's European engagement. Yet the paradox of the moment is that Harry's message on protecting children online has rarely been more pointed or more personal, even as the couple worked to keep the focus squarely on policy rather than their private grief.

The European Parliament's intergroup on children's rights, for its part, secured a high-profile platform for an issue that has struggled for legislative urgency. Whether that attention translates into concrete policy momentum on digital safety remains to be seen.

## What to Watch

Several threads are set to develop in the coming weeks. The results of Markle's psychiatric evaluation will determine whether he can be formally interrogated and how the case proceeds. The FBI investigation continues, and there is currently no reported evidence of victims in the United States beyond the images themselves. Meghan has not indicated whether she will make a fuller public statement, and the couple's approach, presence without commentary, may evolve as the legal process unfolds.
