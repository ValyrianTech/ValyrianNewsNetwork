# China Exports Jump 35.2% in October on AI Trade Boom

China's exports rose 35.2% year-on-year in October to $412.3 billion, beating forecasts as demand for AI-related hardware — particularly integrated circuits and computing-power equipment — sustained rapid growth, according to customs data released Tuesday. The strong showing pushed the country's cumulative trade surplus past the $1 trillion mark for the second consecutive year, reaching $1.04 trillion for the January–October period, the fastest that milestone has ever been attained.

The data, from the General Administration of Customs, highlights how the global AI buildout has become the central engine of China's export machine — and how that reliance is intensifying friction with trading partners who want Beijing to rebalance toward domestic consumption.

## A price story, not a volume story

According to [Caixin Global](https://www.caixinglobal.com/2026-11-10/chinas-exports-jump-on-strong-ai-trade-102503130.html), October's export growth slowed by 1.1 percentage points from September's 36.3% pace but still exceeded the 33.6% average forecast from a survey of 11 domestic and international institutions. Imports rose 41.6% to $307.1 billion, accelerating by 10.6 percentage points and far exceeding forecasts, as the monthly trade surplus narrowed to $105.3 billion from September's record $133.5 billion.

Integrated circuits were the standout category, with export value surging 137.1% year-on-year to $39.59 billion, according to the customs breakdown reported by the [Global Times](https://www.globaltimes.cn/page/202611/1374002.shtml). Automobile exports climbed 37.6% to $19.69 billion, a sharp slowdown from September's 62% as a front-loading rush faded, while mobile phone exports rose 13.2% to $17.57 billion and rare-earth exports jumped 45% by value to $82.24 million.

Yet beneath the headline figure lies a critical nuance: overall export shipment volumes actually fell 2.5%, meaning most of the growth was driven by rising prices — particularly for AI chips and memory — rather than a proportional increase in physical goods shipped. That distinction matters for sustainability. If AI investment cools or chip prices normalize, the value surge could deflate quickly.

## Imports mirror the AI supply chain

The import surge tells a parallel story — one about the AI supply chain rather than a consumer recovery. "Imports were still dominated by AI trade," Tianchen Xu, senior economist at the Economist Intelligence Unit, told [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/10/china-trade-october-exports-imports-surplus-.html). High-tech product imports rose 72% by value, alongside sharp increases in critical minerals such as copper and in energy supplies, including crude oil, liquefied natural gas and coal.

In other words, China is importing components and materials to feed its AI-hardware export machine — not signalling that domestic consumer demand has meaningfully recovered. Retail sales grew a modest 1.1% in September, underscoring the persistent imbalance in the world's second-largest economy.

The strength in exports has allowed policymakers to continue relying on moderate support to meet the country's annual growth target of 4.5%–5%, even as household consumption stays weak. That dynamic deepens the global imbalance and fuels complaints from the EU, the U.S. and the International Monetary Fund, all urging Beijing to pivot to a consumption-driven growth model.

## The trillion-dollar milestone and its discontents

The cumulative surplus crossing $1 trillion a month earlier than in 2025 — when it first broke that threshold and ended the year at a record $1.2 trillion — has amplified calls for rebalancing. "China remains well on pace for a new record high for the trade surplus by year-end despite a miss in October," Lynn Song, chief economist for Greater China at ING Bank, told CNBC.

By destination, China's exports to the U.S. rose 32.9% in October by value, while imports from the U.S. grew nearly 35%, leaving a bilateral surplus of $32.7 billion. Exports to the EU rose 24.4% while imports fell more than 4%, widening the surplus to $33.4 billion.

## Headwinds gather

Analysts see momentum beginning to slow. "Momentum looks to have slowed," said Nguyen Hoang Nam, China economist at Capital Economics, estimating a seasonally adjusted 2% month-on-month fall in exports. Citi Research analyst Yuanliu Hu projects export growth could decelerate to 8% in 2027 as favorable base effects fade and global AI investment moderates.

Two policy risks loom. The U.S.–China trade truce is set to expire in January 2027, making next week's APEC leaders' summit in Shenzhen — where Presidents Xi Jinping and Donald Trump are due to meet for a third time this year — the next test. And EU auto import curbs take effect December 1, capping imported cars at 350,000 a year.

That cap "would barely contain China's export boom," argued Brad Setser, a senior fellow at the Council on Foreign Relations and a former U.S. Treasury official. In an [analysis](https://www.cfr.org/articles/europes-china-auto-deal) for the CFR, Setser noted the cap sits above the 300,000 hybrids China exported to Europe last year, though below the roughly 800,000 annualized pace of recent months. "Europe still is a long way from a coherent trade and industrial policy with respect to autos, let alone a coherent economic policy towards overall trade with China," he wrote, warning that the world has never encountered such elastic Chinese supply capacity.

## What to watch

The October figures confirm a structural reality: China's economy is leaning harder on external demand precisely as its trading partners lose patience with the resulting imbalances. Whether that arrangement holds depends on three variables — the durability of the global AI investment cycle, the fate of the U.S. truce in January, and whether Beijing is willing to shift its policy mix toward household consumption.

For now, the AI boom is doing the heavy lifting. But a growth model powered by rising chip prices rather than rising volumes is a fragile one — and the trade frictions it generates are only beginning to bite.
