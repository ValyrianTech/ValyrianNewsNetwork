# Boys, 15 and 9, Ran Antwerp Shop; Police Seal It

Antwerp police judicially sealed a shop on the Abdijstraat in the Kiel district after inspectors found it was being kept open by two brothers, aged 15 and 9, with no adult present, [VRT NWS reported](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/11/winkel-antwerpen-verzegeld-nadat-jongen-van-15-en-broertje-van-9/). The discovery came during a wider inspection of shops along the busy shopping street on Tuesday, in which officers also seized large quantities of suspected counterfeit goods from three other businesses.

The case has cast a spotlight on child-safeguarding and labour concerns in a neighbourhood already under sustained multi-agency scrutiny for illicit trade.

## What inspectors found

The checks were carried out by the team handling fraudulent businesses (*malafide handelszaken*) within the Antwerp police's Regio Zuid division. In one shop, officers found the 15-year-old serving customers, accompanied by his 9-year-old brother.

"When the police stepped into the shop, we saw that a minor was serving customers: a boy of 15," police spokesperson Wouters Bruyns said, according to VRT NWS. "Normally an adult operator or responsible person must be present in a shop, but that was not the case. The boy was moreover in the company of his brother of 9. That is of course not permitted."

Because no responsible adult was present, the shop was ordered to close and was judicially sealed, [according to a press release from Politiezone Antwerpen](https://www.politieantwerpen.be/nieuws/twee-kinderen-houden-zaak-open). A responsible person did turn up a short while later, police said, but the sealing had already been ordered.

## Counterfeit haul from three other shops

The inspections also targeted suspected counterfeiting. In three other shops, inspectors seized a range of branded goods including:

- 129 mobile-phone cases bearing various luxury brands;
- 131 counterfeit Apple and Samsung earphones;
- 37 rolls of fake residual-waste bags carrying the City of Antwerp logo;
- 264 phone back covers;
- 61 lip balms of the brand Rhode;
- six dolls of the Labubu brand;
- a Lemaire handbag; and
- an Essentials tracksuit.

"All the goods that have been seized will of course be further examined for authenticity," the police statement said. "It is the inspection services that are conducting and continuing this investigation."

The counterfeit city waste bags are notable: they represent not only brand counterfeiting but a form of fraud against the municipality itself.

## Part of a sustained crackdown on the Kiel

The Abdijstraat and the surrounding Kiel area have been the focus of a prolonged enforcement drive by the City of Antwerp, police and partner agencies aimed at improving liveability and safety and curbing fraudulent business practices.

In August, a multi-agency operation checked nine shops on the Kiel. In a clothing store on the Abdijstraat, police found €30,400 in cash and a suitcase containing 129 suspected counterfeit iPhones, opening two money-laundering files, [VRT NWS reported at the time](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/08/11/inbreuken-malafide-handelszaken-antwerpen-kiel-nagemaakte-iphone/).

The pattern has continued into the autumn. In October, Antwerp mayor Els van Doesburg (N-VA) ordered a fish shop in the Abdijstraat to close for three months after repeated breaches of public-safety, health and social legislation, [VRT NWS reported](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/10/30/viswinkel-abdijstraat-kiel-sluiting-burgemeester-gezondheid-veil/). Inspectors had found contaminated work surfaces, pests and evidence of illegal employment and possible economic exploitation of workers.

## Analysis: children at the centre of a wider problem

The immediate trigger for sealing the shop was the absence of any responsible adult. But the case points to how counterfeit trade, undeclared work and the use of minors can converge in the same small businesses.

Belgian rules set strict limits on the minimum age for work and require supervision of minors in the workplace. A 9-year-old working in a retail shop raises serious questions about where the children's parents or guardians were and who actually owns or manages the premises. No information has been released on whether the family or the operator faces prosecution for illegal child labour.

The judicial sealing typically places the matter under the supervision of the labour auditor (*arbeidsauditeur*), suggesting a judicial rather than merely administrative response. Whether that leads to charges over counterfeiting, child labour or social fraud is not yet public.

## What to watch

Several questions remain open. Who owns the shop, and where the minors' guardians were, has not been disclosed. The authenticity of the seized goods is still being tested, and no charges have been announced. What is clear is that the Kiel remains a priority for Antwerp's enforcement agencies, and that the latest inspection has added child welfare to a list of concerns already including counterfeit goods, cash laundering and undeclared labour.
