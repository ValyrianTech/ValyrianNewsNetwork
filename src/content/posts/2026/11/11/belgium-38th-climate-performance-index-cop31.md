# Belgium Slips to 38th in Climate Performance Index at COP31

Belgium has dropped one place to 38th in the 2027 Climate Change Performance Index (CCPI), earning an overall "low" rating, as the COP31 UN climate summit gets underway in Antalya, Turkey. The annual ranking, published by German NGOs Germanwatch and the NewClimate Institute with the Climate Action Network, places Belgium firmly among the weak-performing nations on climate action, according to [RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/cop31-la-belgique-vegete-parmi-les-pays-a-faible-performance-climatique-selon-des-ong-11813948).

The CCPI assesses 64 countries plus the European Union — together representing more than 90% of global greenhouse gas emissions — across roughly 14 indicators grouped into four weighted categories. The ranking draws on the analysis of some 500 energy and climate experts, many affiliated with NGOs in the Climate Action Network.

## A Mixed but Mediocre Scorecard

Belgium's performance is uneven. According to the [CCPI Belgium country profile](https://ccpi.org/country/bel/), the country earns a medium rating for greenhouse gas emissions (which carry a 40% weighting) and for energy use (20%). But it receives a low rating on renewable energy (20%) and climate policy (20%) — the two areas most directly shaped by government decision-making.

The overall verdict is that Belgium has lost ground: it stood 37th a year earlier. As RTBF put it, the country's climate action is once again judged to be weak.

For the sixth consecutive year, the top three places remain vacant because no country is deemed to pursue a climate policy ambitious enough to meet the 1.5°C goal. Denmark ranks 4th, followed by Lithuania and Luxembourg. The EU as a whole sits 19th, up one place.

The contrast with COP31's hosts is stark: Türkiye ranks 53rd, while Australia — presiding over the summit's negotiations — sits 54th. Most G20 economies score poorly, with the United Kingdom (7th) the only member faring comparatively well. The United States ranks 67th, second to last, with Iran at 68th.

## Flanders and a Weakened Energy Plan

The CCPI's sharpest criticism targets Belgium's policy architecture. Experts point to an unclear and incomplete long-term transition strategy and single out a lack of ambition from Flanders that weakens Belgium's National Energy and Climate Plan (NECP).

Belgium's federal system divides climate powers between the federal state and its three regions, and the CCPI notes that Flemish reluctance to raise ambition undermines national coherence. The experts also flagged slow building renovation, rising transport-sector emissions despite company-car electrification efforts, the absence of a fossil-fuel exit strategy framed around a just transition, and the too-slow deployment of renewable energy.

Perhaps most damning, Belgium lowered its renewables target in its updated NECP from 21.7% of gross final energy consumption by 2030 to 20.4%. The CCPI warned the move sent a detrimental signal to other countries and endangers attempts to increase energy sovereignty.

There are bright spots. The CCPI credits Belgium with a comprehensive policy framework aligned with international commitments, cooperation between federal and regional levels, a well-integrated electricity grid, a company-car electrification scheme, and carbon contracts for difference supporting industrial decarbonisation. The Belgian contributor to the index is Maud Buytaert of the Flemish environmental NGO Bond Beter Leefmilieu.

## Why It Matters

Belgium's ranking has hovered in the low band for years — 39th in the 2024 edition and 35th at COP29 in 2024 — never escaping the low overall category. The stagnation points to structural rather than cyclical problems.

Transport is a persistent weak point: mobility emissions represent more than a fifth of Belgium's total and continue to grow, driven by freight and increased kilometres travelled. Under EU effort-sharing rules, Belgium must cut non-ETS emissions by 47% by 2030 relative to 2005, while a 2023 Klimaatzaak court ruling obliges the country to pursue a 55% reduction. Flanders reportedly refuses to commit beyond 40%, setting up a gap between legal obligations and regional political will.

## What's Next

The CCPI's experts call on Belgium to adopt a clear long-term strategy for the energy transition and to increase ambition in upcoming talks on post-2030 reduction targets. At COP31, Belgium's positioning on a fossil-fuel phase-out roadmap will be closely watched.

With the country's ranking stalled and its renewables target weakened, the question is whether federal and regional governments can reconcile their competing ambitions — and whether Belgium can translate its comprehensive policy framework into the emissions cuts its courts and its climate commitments demand. For now, the NGOs' verdict is unambiguous: Belgium remains among Europe's climate laggards.
