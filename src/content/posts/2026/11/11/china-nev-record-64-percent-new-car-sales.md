# China NEVs Hit Record 64.3% of New Car Sales

New energy vehicles (NEVs) accounted for 64.3 percent of new car sales in China in October 2026, the highest monthly penetration rate on record, according to data released by the China Association of Automobile Manufacturers (CAAM) and [reported](https://www.news.cn/fortune/20261111/b5e9695ebc0f4b47977867d16ee1ee06/c.html) by Xinhua on 11 November. The figure cements battery-electric and plug-in hybrid models as the dominant powertrain in the world's largest auto market, and marks a rapid acceleration from just a year ago, when NEVs captured roughly half of monthly sales.

## A Milestone Built on Volume and a Shrinking Denominator

October's numbers reflect both the robust growth of the NEV sector and the contraction of the traditional-fuel market around it. NEV production reached 2.017 million units in October, up 13.8 percent year on year, while NEV sales hit 1.94 million units, a 13.1 percent annual increase.

The overall market told a different story. Total auto production in October was 3.063 million units and total sales 3.016 million units, up 2.5 percent and 2 percent month on month but slightly down year on year. Because NEV volume keeps climbing while the total market stays flat or shrinks, the NEV share rises faster than absolute growth alone would suggest. Fuel-car sales have fallen notably since April 2026, pressured by high fuel prices and shifting joint-venture strategies.

The cumulative picture reinforces the trend. For January through October 2026, NEV sales reached 14.411 million units, up 11.3 percent year on year, lifting the cumulative NEV share of new car sales to 54.8 percent. Total vehicle sales over the same period were 26.286 million units.

The trajectory through 2026 has been steep: NEV monthly share stood at about 56.9 percent in May, crossed 60 percent for the first time in July at 60.4 percent, edged up to 60.6 percent in August, and then jumped to October's 64.3 percent.

## Exports Emerge as the Growth Engine

While domestic demand cools, overseas sales have become the stabilizing force for the industry. Vehicle exports totaled 1.066 million units in October, up 60.2 percent year on year, with NEV exports reaching 606,000 units, a 1.4-fold (roughly 140 percent) increase. For the first ten months of 2026, cumulative vehicle exports hit 9.271 million units, of which NEV exports accounted for 4.612 million units, up approximately 130 percent year on year.

NEV exports have now represented more than half of all Chinese vehicle exports for several consecutive months. CAAM Deputy Secretary-General Chen Shihua framed the shift in structural terms in September 2026, saying that "for every two cars exported, one is a new energy vehicle," a change he described as China's auto exports switching to "NEV-led," opening global markets through the electrification technology route.

## Policy and Infrastructure Underpin Adoption

The rapid adoption rests on a policy and infrastructure foundation that is now being gradually recalibrated. From 1 January 2026 through the end of 2027, NEVs are subject to a half-rate purchase tax, capped at a 15,000 yuan reduction per passenger vehicle, ending the full exemption that applied in prior years. CAAM cited the year-end "policy switch window" as a factor motivating October's production push, as automakers moved to capture demand ahead of a change in purchase-tax treatment.

Charging infrastructure has expanded in parallel. As of end-June 2026, China's charging network totaled 23.057 million charging piles, up 43.2 percent year on year, with county-level coverage reaching 98.61 percent, the largest charging network in the world.

## Headwinds and the Road Ahead

Despite the record penetration rate, industry figures have flagged pressure in the months ahead. Chen Shihua noted earlier that because monthly sales from September to December 2025 were at relatively high levels, domestic production and sales in the final four months of 2026 still face considerable pressure. The year-on-year decline in total October sales underscores that the overall market is not expanding.

On the export side, Beijing is signaling a shift in emphasis. In September 2026, three ministries issued guidelines on overseas competition behavior and compliance, a move that points toward compliance-driven, higher-quality overseas expansion rather than pure scale. CAAM's start-of-year forecast projected NEV sales of about 19 million units for 2026, a 15.2 percent annual increase.

For now, the October milestone is unambiguous: two of every three new cars sold in China are electric or plug-in hybrid. What to watch next is whether that share keeps climbing on genuine volume growth, or, increasingly, on the continued contraction of the gasoline car it is displacing.
