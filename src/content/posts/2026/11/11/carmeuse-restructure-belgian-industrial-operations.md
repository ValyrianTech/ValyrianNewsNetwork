# Carmeuse to Restructure Belgian Industrial Operations

The Walloon industrial group Carmeuse has presented worker representatives with a project to transform its Belgian industrial network, with the measures envisaged for rollout progressively between March 2027 and March 2028. The lime and mineral-products giant, headquartered in Louvain-la-Neuve, insists the move is not a disengagement from Belgium, but the number of jobs affected and the exact sites concerned have not been specified.

According to [L'Avenir](https://www.lavenir.net/regions/2026/11/10/carmeuse-annonce-un-projet-de-transformation-de-son-activite-en-belgique-limpact-sur-lemploi-pas-encore-connu-EG6CCKE6QNDVLGOJE2LTRI37CA/), the company said in a statement published on Tuesday 10 November that the project responds to structural shifts affecting activity volumes and certain deposits and installations.

## What the company says

"This project responds to the structural evolutions affecting activity volumes, certain deposits and installations, while allowing the industrial set-up to be adapted to anticipated market needs," management said in the statement.

In a passage clearly aimed at heading off fears of an exit from Belgium, the company added: "It is not a disengagement from Belgium, but a transformation intended to preserve a more robust Belgian industrial base capable of continuing to invest."

Carmeuse stressed that no definitive implementation decision has been taken and that a consultation phase is now open with workers' representatives. That consultation is to examine the project, its terms, realistic alternatives and "solutions to limit the consequences for employment."

As [L'Echo](https://www.lecho.be/entreprises/industries-de-base/carmeuse-va-restructurer-ses-activites-industrielles-en-belgique/10699376.html) reported, the world leader in lime, based in Wallonia, wants to restructure its activities from 2027 in Wallonia itself — with no job numbers fixed yet.

## The sites in play

Carmeuse's main Belgian operating sites are concentrated in two provinces. In Liège province, the group operates at Engis and Wanze (Moha); in Namur province, at Andenne (Seilles), Fosses-la-Ville (Aisemont) and Couvin. The company has not indicated which of these locations could be affected.

The FGTB socialist union's permanent representative for the quarries sector, contacted by L'Avenir, said it was reserving the information for Carmeuse workers first and declined to comment immediately — a signal that the process is at an early and sensitive stage.

As [Sudinfo](https://www.sudinfo.be/id1224131/article/2026-11-11/carmeuse-prepare-une-reorganisation-de-ses-activites-en-belgique-des-2027-nous) noted, employment sits at the heart of the discussions, with the consultation under way with worker representatives.

## A major employer in a difficult climate

Carmeuse is one of Wallonia's flagship heavy-industry firms. Founded in 1860 and controlled by the Collinet family, the group produces lime, limestone and mineral products used in steelmaking, water and flue-gas treatment and soil improvement. Its Belgian operations sit in the traditional industrial heartland of Liège and Namur provinces.

The announcement lands in an already difficult period for Walloon manufacturing. It comes in the same news cycle as major job losses at GSK and the bankruptcy of Vynova Belgium, events that have amplified sensitivity to any new industrial restructuring in the region.

## The decarbonisation backdrop

Beyond market and geological pressures, the restructuring sits within a wider pattern of cost and portfolio adaptation across a capital-hungry group. According to [Forbes Belgique](https://www.forbes.be/fr/carmeuse-renforce-son-pole-europeen-le-nouveau-pari-industriel-de-la-famille-collinet/), Carmeuse has been expanding through acquisitions — Italy's Unicalce, Chile's Cementos Bío Bío and Argentina's Caleras San Juan — while needing to finance the decarbonisation of its kilns.

That is a heavy burden. Per the European Lime Association, roughly 69% of CO2 emissions from lime production come from the calcination chemical reaction itself, not merely from fuel combustion — meaning switching fuels alone cannot eliminate them. The group's LEOPARD project at Aisemont, using membrane-based CO2 capture with EU Innovation Fund support, targets more than 70,000 tonnes of CO2 avoided per year, with the group setting a 1 April 2026 start date. In October 2026, Carmeuse Europe's capital was reportedly raised to 299 million euros via a roughly 73 million euro cash contribution.

## Environmental friction

The company also faces organised opposition to quarry expansion — notably the contested Florennes limestone deposit, where the anti-quarry committee has lodged a sixth appeal before the Council of State. Carmeuse's reference to "certain deposits" being affected by structural evolutions may connect to such geological and permitting constraints, though the company has not drawn that link explicitly.

## What to watch

The only firm facts so far are that a transformation project exists, that it targets the Belgian industrial network, that implementation is envisaged between March 2027 and March 2028, and that the employment impact is undetermined with a consultation now open. No site closures or specific job numbers have been officially confirmed.

The key questions are which sites will be affected, whether the consultation triggers Belgium's so-called "Renault procedure" for mass layoffs, and how the environmental disputes over deposits interact with the company's stated rationale. For now, Carmeuse's framing of modernisation rather than withdrawal will be measured against the concrete measures that eventually emerge from the consultation.
