# Eurostar Strike Cancels, Delays Trains From Brussels

A surprise strike by Eurostar staff disrupted cross-Channel rail services on Wednesday morning, 25 November 2026, cancelling one train and delaying others departing from Brussels-Midi station while hitting the busy Paris-London route hardest, according to [RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/trafic-eurostar-perturbe-par-une-greve-surprise-un-train-deja-annule-au-depart-de-la-gare-du-midi-11820827).

The operator attributed the disruption to an "unexpected strike" by some of its staff, citing "une greve imprevue menee par certains membres de notre personnel sur le reseau Eurostar."

## What Was Affected

Between Paris and London, traffic was impacted "in both directions," Eurostar said on X, as reported by [Le Soir](https://www.lesoir.be/784790/article/2026-11-25/greve-surprise-chez-eurostar-plusieurs-trains-annules). Trains scheduled to depart London at 07:01 and 08:01 were cancelled, along with Paris departures at 07:37, 08:39 and 09:10.

For Belgium, [La Libre Eco](https://www.lalibre.be/economie/entreprises-startup/2026/11/25/greve-surprise-a-eurostar-nous-demandons-aux-clients-dont-le-train-est-annule-de-ne-pas-se-rendre-en-gare-BLJCRIGYVFGTPGNROWWACPWL74/) reported that two Brussels-London services were delayed - the 07:57 and 08:51 departures - while the train scheduled to leave Brussels-Midi at 11:56 was cancelled. Beyond its historic Paris-London link through the Channel Tunnel, Eurostar also serves Belgium, the Netherlands and Germany, and some of those connections showed delays on Wednesday morning.

## Company Advice and Compensation

Eurostar urged affected passengers not to travel to stations. "We ask customers whose train is cancelled not to come to the station, as they will not be able to travel," the company said in a statement to AFP. Those affected can change their trip to another date or obtain a full refund under the company's commercial policy, and Eurostar "apologises to customers for the inconvenience caused."

The operator also called on trade unions to "continue discussions directly with the company, which remains open and willing to continue a constructive dialogue," initially without publicly stating the reasons for the walkout.

## The Strike's Roots

While Eurostar did not publicly confirm the trigger, [Le Dauphine Libere](https://www.ledauphine.com/social/2026/11/25/la-circulation-des-eurostar-entre-paris-et-londres-tres-perturbee-en-raison-d-une-greve-surprise) reported, citing an internal source, that the strike coincided with ongoing salary negotiations between unions and the company - and, above all, with anger over a farewell party for the former CEO.

The event, reportedly scheduled for the evening of the strike day at a luxury hotel in London and expected to draw about 400 people, "makes many people grind their teeth internally," the source said. The apparent contrast between a lavish send-off and stalled pay talks appears to have inflamed tensions at a company already under scrutiny.

## Why It Matters

The timing - the morning peak - maximised passenger disruption, striking at one of Europe's flagship cross-Channel connections. Eurostar is a subsidiary 55.75%-owned by SNCF Voyageurs, and its expanded network now stretches across Belgium, the Netherlands and Germany, meaning a walkout in one market can ripple across several countries.

For Brussels travellers in particular, the disruption underscored the capital's dependence on a single operator for direct high-speed links to London. With no confirmed end to the action, uncertainty remains over whether further cancellations could follow later in the day.

## What to Watch

Passengers holding tickets for affected services should check Eurostar's website and rebooking options before travelling. The larger question is whether the salary dispute - and the internal resentment laid bare by the timing of the strike - will push the company and unions back to the table quickly, or extend the disruption into the coming days.
