# PBOC Floods Short End, Trims Mid End to Steady Month-End

China's central bank has injected liquidity through several channels to keep money-market conditions stable through the month-end, deploying a combination of large daily overnight reverse repos and a freshly re-activated seven-day operation. The move marks the first month-end funding test since the People's Bank of China re-anchored its primary policy rate from the seven-day reverse repo rate to the overnight rate on November 2, according to [Xinhua's Economic Information Daily](https://www.news.cn/fortune/20261125/26a5d53c816d489283b477aa221435f8/c.html).

The operations are aimed at absorbing seasonal and tax-period cash demand without signaling a broader easing pivot—a delicate balancing act that analysts say reflects a more surgical approach to monetary management.

## A Deliberate Split Between Short and Mid End

The week of November 23–27 presented the central bank with a heavy maturity wall. Window data cited by the outlet showed 379.5 billion yuan (about $53 billion) of overnight reverse repos and 1 trillion yuan ($140 billion) of one-year medium-term lending facility (MLF) loans maturing—a combined 1.38 trillion yuan draining from the system, even as banks built up cash buffers ahead of the month-end.

November 25 was the crunch point: the single most concentrated day of funding pressure, coinciding with a reserve-requirement day, the MLF maturity, and a weekly peak in government bond settlement. Yet as of November 24, the weighted DR001—the depository-institutions overnight repo rate that now serves as the PBOC's target—held steady at 1.27%, a sign of "reasonable and ample" liquidity.

The PBOC's response was visibly tiered. From November 16 to 24 it ran daily overnight reverse repos ranging from 37.95 billion to 76.75 billion yuan, with several operations above 50 billion, precisely topping up short-term cash. It then added a 300 billion yuan seven-day operation at the tax-period node on November 13, and on the evening of November 24 announced a 500 billion yuan seven-day reverse repo for execution the next day.

At the same time, the central bank shrank its mid-term footprint. On November 25 it conducted an 800 billion yuan one-year MLF against the 1 trillion yuan maturing—a 200 billion yuan net withdrawal. Combined with a reduced rollover of its outright reverse repos, November's medium-term tools drained a net 500 billion yuan, reversing four consecutive months of increased rollovers, [Cailianshe reported](https://www.cls.cn/detail/2543357).

## Why Re-anchor the Policy Rate?

To understand the strategy, it helps to recall what changed in early November. The PBOC had designated the seven-day reverse repo rate as its primary policy rate back in 2024, but the market's most active and benchmark-setting rate was already the overnight DR001. That created a maturity mismatch between the policy rate and the rate the central bank actually targeted.

At the 2026 Financial Street Forum, Governor Pan Gongsheng signaled the fix: the PBOC would switch its main open-market operation tenor to overnight, making the overnight repo rate the primary policy rate. The switch took effect November 2 with a 541 billion yuan overnight operation at 1.25%, and other tools were re-based on the overnight rate with unchanged spreads.

The early verdict is encouraging. Wang Qing, chief macro analyst at Orient Gold Credit, noted that since November 2 DR001's high was 1.3270% and its low 1.2591%—a monthly range of roughly 6.8 basis points, the narrowest since overnight reverse repos were introduced in late June. "This shows the new open-market operation framework has already demonstrated its effect in smoothing funding-rate volatility," he said.

## Reading the "Short-End Up, Mid-End Down" Signal

The seeming contradiction—flooding one end of the curve while draining another—is the story's analytical core. Dong Ximiao, chief economist at Zhaolian, argued it is not a tightening of policy orientation but an active adjustment of the liquidity maturity structure after the re-anchoring.

On the short end, sustained large overnight injections were meant to steady expectations during the framework's early days and to meet primary-dealer demand in full. On the mid end, trimming the six-month outright reverse repo and MLF withdrew excess liquidity accumulated earlier, preventing DR001 from falling too fast and curbing idle "empty-turning" funds and excessive bond-market leverage—while avoiding any misreading of the framework change as sustained easing.

Dong described the logic as "managing the floor with the short end and managing the pace with the mid end": flexible overnight tools absorb short-term shocks such as tax periods and payments, while mid-term tool space is preserved for year-end fiscal payments, government bond issuance, and supporting financing needs.

Ming Ming, chief economist at CITIC Securities, said the "MLF shrinking by 200 billion yuan plus a 500 billion yuan seven-day injection" combination was broadly in line with expectations, continuing the post-re-anchoring framework of refined short-end adjustment and flexible mid- to long-end contraction.

## The Year-End Test Ahead

The bigger stress test looms in December. China's fourth quarter is a peak government-bond financing season: including 550 billion yuan of unused local-government-debt quota now being deployed, Q4 government bond financing is expected to rise about 1.5 trillion yuan year-on-year, alongside 800 billion yuan of new policy-based financial instruments being disbursed—all of which need central bank liquidity support.

Wang Qing expects the PBOC to return to net injections via MLF and both tenors of outright reverse repos before year-end, keeping overnight and seven-day operations at high volume and potentially restarting 14-day reverse repos in mid-December.

For now, the message from Beijing is one of steadiness rather than stimulus. The deliberate contraction of mid-term tools while flooding the short end signals a "steady, slightly loose, no-big-stimulus" posture—curating the term structure of liquidity rather than expanding the aggregate aggressively.

Whether that surgical approach can hold the funding market steady through a heavy year-end issuance calendar will be the real measure of the re-anchored framework. Early signs suggest it is working; December will decide how well.
