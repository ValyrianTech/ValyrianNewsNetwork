# China TAO Touts Cultural Ties, Warns on Taiwan Independence

China's State Council Taiwan Affairs Office (TAO) said on November 25 that the popularity of mainland "new three cultural products" in Taiwan reflects a shared cultural identity that "no political manipulation can block," while in the same press conference warning that pursuing Taiwan independence "has no market and no way out."

Speaking at the TAO's regular press conference, spokesperson Zhang Han addressed both themes in response to reporters' questions, striking a dual message that paired cultural outreach with a firm political warning aimed at the island's governing party, according to [The Paper](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_34554855) and [Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/tw/20261125/e56df5d35024466fab63bb45defdb14c/c.html).

## The Cultural Pitch

Zhang told reporters that online novels, online games, and online dramas and films from the mainland — collectively framed as the "new three cultural products" (文化新三样) — have grown popular with audiences in Taiwan. She cast the trend as organic evidence of a shared Chinese heritage rather than a matter of policy.

"The reason compatriots on both sides of the strait can grieve and rejoice together over a novel, a game, or a drama is that the same Chinese cultural genes flow in our bloodline — no political manipulation can block this," Zhang said, as quoted by The Paper.

The framing is a recurring element of Beijing's messaging on cross-strait relations. By presenting mainland entertainment and literature as naturally appealing to Taiwanese audiences, the TAO seeks to counter efforts to emphasize a distinct Taiwanese cultural identity. The approach has been continuous: earlier in November 2026, the office signaled support for Taiwanese industry participation in mainland micro-drama production.

## The Political Warning

In the same session, Zhang restated the mainland's core position on sovereignty. "There is only one China in the world, and Taiwan is an inalienable part of China's territory," she said, according to Xinhua.

She described resolving the Taiwan question and achieving complete national reunification as the Communist Party's unswerving historical task, calling it a shared aspiration of all Chinese people and a necessary requirement for China's modernization and national rejuvenation.

Zhang called the Taiwan question "the core of China's core interests" and "the most important and most sensitive issue in China-US relations," saying that handling it properly is the key to stable bilateral ties. She said pursuing independence separatism "runs against the tide of the world and the trend of history, violates the nation's greater righteousness, and has no market and no way out."

Directing her remarks at the island's governing Democratic Progressive Party (DPP), Zhang urged the authorities to "recognize the situation, thoroughly abandon the 'Taiwan independence' delusion, and stop separatist activities — otherwise they will only suffer complete defeat."

## Analysis: One Press Conference, Two Audiences

The dual message reflects the TAO's established playbook of pairing soft cultural engagement with hard political red lines. The cultural-products remarks are aimed at ordinary Taiwanese audiences and signal continued openness to cross-strait exchange in the entertainment and publishing sectors. The independence warning, by contrast, restates a baseline that Beijing has held consistently and ties it explicitly to the China-US relationship.

For Beijing, elevating the Taiwan question to "the core of China's core interests" and the "most sensitive issue" in ties with Washington serves to frame US handling of the island as central to bilateral stability — an emphasis that has recurred throughout 2026. The latest statement does not appear to introduce new policy, but rather reinforces a position ahead of any further developments in cross-strait or US-China relations.

## What to Watch

Questions remain over how Taipei will respond. Neither the DPP leadership nor Taiwan's Mainland Affairs Council had publicly reacted to the specific remarks as of the press conference, and the two source reports — both from mainland state-aligned outlets — did not document any Taiwanese reaction. The full official transcript of the November 25 session had also not yet been posted on the TAO's website, which would provide the complete Q&A, including the exact reporter questions that prompted the two lines of response.

For now, the conference underlined a consistent strategy: present cultural affinity as a bridge, and independence as a dead end.
