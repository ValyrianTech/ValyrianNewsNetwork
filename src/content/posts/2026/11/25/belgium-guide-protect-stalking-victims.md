# Belgium Launches Guide to Protect Rising Stalking Victims

Belgian authorities have presented a new practical guide for victims of stalking as the number of cases handled by public prosecutors keeps climbing, [VRT NWS reported](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/25/gids-met-praktische-handvaten-voor-slachtoffers-van-stalking/). The guide, titled *Stalking: wat te doen?* ("Stalking: What to Do?"), was unveiled on 25 November, the International Day for the Elimination of Violence against Women, alongside 21 new proposals to strengthen victim protection.

## A steep rise, few convictions

The numbers behind the launch are stark. Stalking cases at the public prosecutor's offices (parketten) rose 35.5 percent over five years, reaching more than 30,000 last year. In more than four out of ten cases, the perpetrator is a current or former partner.

Yet remarkably few cases ever reach a courtroom. Between 2021 and 2025, 54 percent of stalking dossiers were dismissed without further action (geseponeerd), and only 9 percent led to a formal summons before a judge. As VRT NWS noted, the figures are not new, but they remain telling — highlighting a persistent gap between reporting and justice outcomes.

## What the guide offers

The new guide gives victims concrete, step-by-step information: how to file a police report, how to secure their home, phone and online accounts, and which other protective measures they can take. It is being distributed through public prosecutors' offices, the police, the Sexual Assault Care Centres (Zorgcentra na Seksueel Geweld), the Safe Houses (Veilige Huizen), and the Centres for General Welfare Work (CAW's).

## Twenty-one proposals for better protection

At the initiative of Flemish Justice Minister Zuhal Demir (N-VA), victims of stalking and their relatives spent the past year in talks with representatives of the police, the judiciary and social services. Together they drew up 21 proposals to better protect victims.

One of these — which requires federal competence — is that a contact ban (contactverbod) should remain in force when a perpetrator appeals. Currently, that protection lapses in principle during appeal proceedings.

Other proposals target the Flemish level. Flanders is examining, together with the judiciary, how the Victim Support Service (Dienst Slachtofferonthaal) can better support victims of stalking by a current or former partner.

"We must protect victims of stalking much better and, as a society, also take them seriously," Demir told Radio 1.

## The mobile alarm keeps expanding

The launch also builds on the steady expansion of Belgium's mobile stalking alarm — a discreet button that lets victims alert emergency services in a crisis. Nearly 350 victims now use the device, according to figures Federal Justice Minister Annelies Verlinden (CD&V) released for the international day.

The system has grown considerably: [VRT NWS reported](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/24/bijna-350-slachtoffers-mobiel-stalkingalarm/) that 349 victims were protected with an alarm as of 1 October 2026, up from 80 in March 2024. Since the project began in 2020, 871 people have received an alarm. The federal government decided [earlier this year](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/02/06/mobiele-stalkingalarmen-regering/) to buy 500 additional alarm buttons, and authorities are working to make the system compatible with iOS smartphones; it currently works only on Android.

The alarm is assigned by the public prosecutor's office on the basis of a risk analysis. A single press activates the 112 app and connects the victim directly to emergency services, whose calls are given priority so the police can intervene as quickly as possible to counter an escalation of violence.

"I am indeed glad that we can offer a mobile stalking alarm to more victims of stalking, because they are better protected that way," Verlinden told Radio 1. "Their call gets priority, so that the emergency services can intervene as quickly as possible and counter an escalation of the violence."

## The wider toll

The scale of the problem extends well beyond case files. Roughly 25,000 stalking reports are filed in Belgium each year — an average of 70 per day — and in 62 percent of cases the perpetrator is an ex-partner. About 81 percent of women stalked by an ex-partner also face physical violence, and on average 20 people a year are killed by their stalker, while 120 to 130 survive an attempted murder or manslaughter.

Evaluations of the alarm are encouraging: 89.6 percent of women who receive one say they feel significantly safer, and after an average of six months the device is often no longer needed. Police interventions occur on average within ten minutes.

## What to watch

The guide and the 21 proposals represent the latest step in a years-long, incremental policy build-up, from the 2019 pilot alarm to nationwide availability in 2024 and the expanded capacity approved this year. The key question now is implementation: how quickly the federal contact-ban reform can be addressed, how the Flemish-level support measures take shape, and whether the stark dismissal rate — more than half of all cases — will finally begin to fall.

For now, the message from ministers is one of taking victims seriously. As Demir put it, that means protecting them far better — and, as a society, treating their experience with the seriousness it deserves.
