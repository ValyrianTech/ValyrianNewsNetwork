# China Launches 6G Innovation Pilot Across Eight Regions

China's Ministry of Industry and Information Technology (MIIT) joined eight provincial and municipal governments on 24 November 2026 to launch a collaborative pilot program for 6G innovation and development, [Xinhua reported](https://www.news.cn/tech/20261124/f92bd4ce5cec4eee9ed5dbbcc82ca5a7/c.html) from the 2026 6G Development Conference in Shanghai. The initiative brings Beijing, Hebei, Shanghai, Jiangsu, Zhejiang, Anhui, Hubei, and Guangdong together under a "national coordination, differentiated local advancement, multi-party participation" model intended to build a unified nationwide framework for next-generation mobile communications.

## A Central-Local Governance Model

The pilot is notable less for any single technology than for how it is organized. Rather than issuing a purely top-down mandate, the MIIT established a formal central-local ("部省") collaboration in which provinces pursue specialized paths aligned with their own industrial strengths while the ministry retains coordination and oversight. The goal, according to Xinhua, is to form a nationwide "one chessboard" pattern for 6G innovation.

The foundation was laid months earlier. Per the [MIIT action plan](https://www.miit.gov.cn/jgsj/txs/wjfb/art/2026/art_46a074da52f54393b04f67cfb839eaaa.html) issued in June 2026, the program is grounded in the "15th Five-Year Plan" and China's "new-type whole-nation system," with guiding principles of coordinated advancement, adaptation to local conditions, innovation-driven development, and benchmark leadership.

## Four Pillars and a 2029 Target

The action plan sets out four areas of coordination: technology R&D, industrial R&D, application cultivation, and project layout. It calls for strengthening frontier 6G research—including converged systems combining communications with artificial intelligence, satellite internet, and wireless sensing—while deepening basic theory in information, coding, and electromagnetic fields. On the industrial side, it targets 6G base stations, core networks, transport networks, and specialized instruments, alongside new terminals, chips, operating systems, and commercial aerospace, with an aim to build local industry clusters.

Application development is to focus on potential 6G scenarios such as immersive communications, industrial manufacturing, the low-altitude economy, embodied intelligence, and smart oceans. By 2029, the plan states, the pilot should yield a batch of self-reliant 6G technical solutions, cultivate promising new business scenarios, and produce diverse new terminal products to support 6G commercial deployment.

## Selection Criteria and Guardrails

Eligibility was demanding. According to the action plan, pilot regions needed active technological innovation, a solid industrial base—at least one domestic-leading or internationally influential flagship enterprise and three or more organizations that have undertaken national 6G major projects—rich application scenarios built on strong 5G and 5G-A deployment, and robust policy and funding support. The eight selected regions are weighted toward China's most advanced eastern and southern economies, plus select central provinces.

The [MIIT approval letter](https://www.miit.gov.cn/jgsj/txs/wjfb/art/2026/art_ab6fba9058b848d0a1fa3cd7d3e91e2c.html) of 17 November 2026 attached firm conditions. Commercial 6G applications require a telecommunications business license, and firms may not explore commercial scenarios before obtaining one. Radio frequency use and equipment must comply with national radio management rules, including the necessary licenses and type approvals. The letter also requires provinces to standardize 6G news publicity with "internal and external differentiation" and strict review, consulting competent authorities before publishing news touching on national-level or industry-wide 6G development—a reminder that commercialization remains tightly supervised.

## International Posture

Alongside the pilot, the conference saw the IMT-2030 (6G) Promotion Group, together with international organizations, industry alliances, and domestic and foreign enterprises, release a Global 6G Development Declaration. It sets out five development concepts centered on native intelligence, green sustainability, security and trustworthiness, open cooperation, and innovation-driven empowerment.

"6G is currently at a critical stage where technology R&D is advancing in depth and standard development is fully underway," MIIT Chief Engineer Zhong Zhihong said, according to Xinhua, adding that global industry, academia, and research parties need to build consensus and cooperate closely. Wang Zhihong, head of the IMT-2030 (6G) Promotion Group and deputy president of the China Academy of Information and Communications Technology, said the group would follow core concepts of native intelligence, green sustainability, security and trustworthiness, innovation-driven development, and open cooperation.

## What to Watch

The pilot runs for an initial three years from approval, with possible extension after evaluation, and the MIIT will conduct periodic reviews under a dedicated management measure. The key questions ahead are whether the differentiated regional approaches translate into interoperable standards rather than fragmented efforts, and whether China's push to shape global 6G standards—paired with its emphasis on open cooperation—can coexist with the tight regulatory controls attached to the domestic rollout.
