# Pentagon Hires Conservative Firms to Audit Military Schools

WASHINGTON — The Defense Department is pushing ahead with an overhaul of the schools it runs for military families, awarding no-bid contracts to firms tied to the conservative education movement to audit curriculum across the system and pilot a "classical" physical education course at two U.S. campuses.

According to [The Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/defense-department-schools-curriculum-audit-classical-pe-852a3b03e9b2567c0063cc4a2e798720), the Department of Defense Education Activity (DoDEA) — which operates about 160 schools across 11 countries and educates roughly 70,000 children of service members — approved a $5.7 million curriculum audit contract in October without seeking competing bids. A separate contract of just over $100,000 went to a second firm to test the classical PE program.

## A System-Wide Audit Without Precedent

The curriculum contract was awarded to American Education Solutions (AES), founded by Jordan Adams, a Hillsdale College graduate who previously consulted with conservative-led school boards in Pennsylvania and Texas to overhaul their curricula. The Pentagon said the audit was issued in compliance with an executive order from President Donald Trump aimed at limiting how schools teach about race and gender.

The department said the review will examine "curriculum, instruction, and professional learning, helping ensure these areas remain rigorous, effective, and aligned with the needs of students and educators."

But two employees of the Pentagon's education division, speaking anonymously for fear of retribution, told the AP that a system-wide review by an outside consultant is without precedent. Typically, schools work with a national accreditor to evaluate and develop standards for subjects, they said.

On its website, AES advertises a curriculum adoption, analysis and development service and criticizes materials used in American schools as "content-light, employ pedagogical practices not supported by the science of learning, and eschew the patriotic civic role of public education." The firm also markets an audit to ensure schools "Do No Harm," describing the intellectual and character formation of children as "a serious responsibility for school systems."

AES did not answer questions from the AP and said the company does not "share work for past, current or pending clients without authorization."

## Greek-Inspired PE Pilot at Two Schools

The second contract, worth just over $100,000, went to Iliad Athletics to pilot a classical physical education course at Lejeune High School in North Carolina and Quantico Middle High School in Virginia. Inspired by traditional Greek athletics, the course will emphasize running, jumping, climbing, carrying and throwing rather than introducing students to team sports.

The program also includes a "character development" component that highlights a weekly virtue and examples of historical figures who embodied it.

As with the audit contract, Iliad's selection did not involve competitive bids. The Pentagon said the company was the only one that could meet its requirements at the time. Patrick Whalen, Iliad's CEO, said in an email he was not aware of any other programs like the one his company offers. Whalen previously worked in the office of Hillsdale College's president.

The Pentagon denied that political connections influenced either award. "The War Department always looks for solutions to benefit the warfighter, not any pre-selected entity," an official said in a statement. "Outside affiliations, investors, or political connections play absolutely no role in the Department's funding decisions."

## Ties to Hillsdale and the Broader Overhaul

Both contractors trace back to Hillsdale College, the conservative Christian school in Michigan that has become an influential producer of K-12 curriculum materials. AES founder Adams worked on Hillsdale's Trump-aligned 1776 Project, a free curriculum created in response to what the college described as "the weaponization of history." The material has been criticized for downplaying slavery and for describing the Jamestown colony as a failed communist project.

The contracts are the latest step in a transformation that [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2026/09/29/politics/pete-hegseth-push-remake-dodea-defense-departments-schools) reported has unfolded across the DoDEA system under Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth. The system is now led by Matthew Kirby, founder and former CEO of Liberty Classical Schools, an Atlanta-based classical charter network. Ret. Army Brig. Gen. Anthony Tata oversees the system as under secretary of defense for personnel and readiness.

Hegseth has a long-standing interest in classical education, particularly a version that incorporates Christian history and virtues. In his 2022 book *Battle for the American Mind*, he argued that a classical educational movement rooted in Christianity was the only way to give children a "fighting chance to save America."

Other changes have accompanied the leadership turnover. U.S. military academies now accept the Classic Learning Test (CLT), positioned as a classical alternative to the SAT. In September, defense schools announced 100 vouchers for students to take the CLT; this month, the agency said the test would be widely available as a college readiness assessment. This summer, DoDEA also launched a new online Western Civilization course using a textbook developed in partnership with Hillsdale that some scholars say presents a misleading portrait of history, framing Islam as a violent threat to Western civilization.

Earlier in the administration, defense schools removed books from libraries covering topics such as race, gender and democracy. The ACLU filed suit and was partially successful in overturning the bans at a handful of schools.

## Supporters and Critics Diverge

Supporters frame the moves as a return to traditional, patriotic instruction that reinforces critical thinking and communication skills. Critics question the lack of competitive bidding, the ideological alignment of the contractors with the administration, and the precedent of an external audit that bypasses the normal accreditation process.

Tom Brady, who directed DoDEA from 2014 to 2024, told CNN he was troubled by the direction. "The beauty of the history of DoDEA is that we provide a quality education to the children of the men and women who serve our nation," he said. "It didn't matter who was the president or the administration. So, I'm very distressed to learn, to think that they're using DoDEA as a test bed for whatever the current administration thinks it is."

Rep. Gil Cisneros, a California Democrat who served as under secretary of defense for personnel and readiness during the Biden administration, argued the changes are ideological rather than evidence-based. "The changes they're making, none of it is based on data," he told CNN. "It is just basically a philosophical change that the secretary wants to make because it aligns with his way of thinking."

[University Herald](https://www.universityherald.com/articles/80713/20261125/pentagon-overhauls-military-schools-conservative-curriculum-classical-education-programs.htm) reported that DoDEA students have consistently scored higher than the national average on federally mandated tests — a fact critics say undercuts the case for sweeping changes. The outlet noted that the department says it regularly reviews its academic programs and makes improvements when needed.

## What to Watch

The audit and PE pilot represent the most concrete spending yet behind Hegseth's vision for military schools. The open questions are how the audit's findings will be used and whether they will translate into system-wide curriculum changes across all 160 schools.

Also unresolved is the fate of the ACLU's litigation over removed books, which continues in federal court even as the Pentagon enforces what one internal memo described as "100% compliance" on keeping banned titles off shelves. For the families whose children attend these schools, the debate over what — and how — their children are taught is far from settled.
