# Greenpeace: Google Building Without Permit in Farciennes

Google is pressing ahead with construction of a data center in Farciennes even though its building permit was annulled in August, Greenpeace alleged on Wednesday, launching an urgent procedure with the Walloon administration to secure an immediate halt to the site. The accusation, [reported by RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/farciennes-google-poursuit-les-travaux-d-un-data-center-sans-permis-denonce-greenpeace-11820807), sharpens a dispute that pits one of the world's largest tech companies against local residents, environmental campaigners and a regional minister over water use in a drought-prone corner of Belgium.

The NGO said it based its claim on satellite imagery and reports from alerted citizens, and on Monday sent a letter to the Walloon administration requesting that the construction site be stopped at once.

## How the permit unravelled

The conflict has been building for two years. In its original permit, granted in 2024, Google planned three data centers at the Écopôle site, an industrial zone straddling Farciennes, Aiseau-Presles and Sambreville along the Sambre river in Hainaut province.

In 2025 the company amended its request, merging two of the three centers into a single, much larger building and switching the cooling design from air cooling to water cooling drawn from the Sambre. The Walloon administration approved the modified permit in April 2026, but a citizens' appeal followed a month later.

In August 2026 — as Belgium endured a historic drought — Walloon Minister for Territory, Infrastructure and Local Authorities François Desquesnes (Les Engagés) annulled the building permit following that appeal, citing the facility's water needs.

"The reality of climate change is upon us and must change our approach to this kind of case," Desquesnes said at the time, according to [RTL Info](https://www.rtl.be/actu/belgique/societe/google-souhaite-pomper-dans-la-sambre-pour-refroidir-son-futur-centre-cest-non/2026-08-15/article/797332). "I don't have enough visibility to guarantee that in the coming years the Region will be able to assume the water withdrawal requested by Google."

Google's first building at the site, which uses air cooling, was nearing completion by September 2026, while construction of the second building had already begun, [Sudinfo reported](https://www.sudinfo.be/id1198457/article/2026-09-25/google-farciennes-permis-refuse-eau-bruit-un-des-patrons-du-groupe-en-belgique) after speaking with Olivier Delcourt, Google's site lead operations manager.

## The water at the heart of the fight

Greenpeace argues the case is about far more than paperwork. It points to Google's existing data center at Saint-Ghislain, near Mons, which it says draws 1 billion litres of water a year for cooling and returns only about a quarter of what it takes — the rest evaporating in the cooling process. The NGO calls such facilities "entreprises potomanes," or water-guzzling companies.

"The climate crisis will only intensify in our regions," said Nadia Cornejo, spokesperson for Greenpeace Belgium. "In this context, the construction of energy-guzzling mastodons cannot slip under the radar. That Google continues to build even though its permit has been annulled gives an idea of the respect the multinational has for our regulations, our resources, the impact on our territories and on our citizens."

She added that the arrival of data centers "is a real societal debate and cannot remain confined to discreet agreements between industrialists and our administrations."

The project has drawn opposition from citizens and groups including "Stop Data Center Belgium," which have long questioned the environmental cost of hosting the cloud and AI infrastructure of global tech firms in the region.

## Google's legal counter-move

Google has not accepted the minister's decision. On 17 October 2026 the company filed an appeal for annulment and suspension with Belgium's Council of State against Desquesnes' ruling. On 16 November, several citizens and Greenpeace lodged a request to intervene in that procedure to put forward their own arguments.

A public hearing before the Council of State is scheduled for 7 January 2027. That ruling is likely to be decisive: it could settle whether construction must stop and whether the annulled permit retains any legal force in the interim.

The central factual question is whether the ongoing works are lawful while Google's appeal is pending — a point that may hinge on whether the company's challenge has suspensive effect. Google maintains it is contesting the annulment itself and has argued its case around jobs, noise and operations, while opponents focus on water consumption and the precautionary principle.

## What to watch

For now, the dispute sits at the intersection of two broader trends: Europe's push to build AI and cloud capacity, and growing pressure on water resources as droughts become more frequent. How Wallonia handles the Farciennes case could set a template for how other regions weigh the demands of the digital economy against local environmental limits.

Two dates matter in the coming weeks. Greenpeace's urgent request asks the Walloon administration to halt the site immediately, and the Council of State hearing on 7 January will test Google's legal position. Until then, the construction cranes at the Écopôle — and the question of who has the right to keep them moving — remain the focus of a debate that neither side shows signs of abandoning.
