# Thomas Markle Indicted on Child Sex Abuse Material Charges

A federal grand jury in Los Angeles on Tuesday indicted Thomas Wayne Markle — the 82-year-old estranged father of Meghan, Duchess of Sussex — on two counts of possessing child sexual abuse material, escalating a case that now stretches from California to the Philippines. According to [The Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/meghan-thomas-markle-child-abuse-charges-5d3a0fc3377e4538880cf0b8ce080fa7), Markle faces up to 20 years in prison on each count — a potential 40-year maximum — if convicted.

The charging document, filed by the U.S. Attorney's Office for the Central District of California, accuses Markle of knowingly possessing 35mm film negatives containing child sexual abuse material on two dates, October 30 and November 4, 2026. Prosecutors allege he knew the images contained such material on both occasions.

## Context: From a Florida Arrest to a Federal Indictment

Markle was arrested November 6 at a senior living facility in Lakeland, Florida, after the FBI said it recovered roughly 78 items — including slides, film negatives and books containing suspected child abuse images — from two Los Angeles storage units he had rented since 2008. As the [AP reported](https://apnews.com/article/meghan-markle-father-abuse-allegations-0bf10d1c7d915b82048c04bd9cca9fc5) at the time of the arrest, some of the seized images appeared to depict Markle himself with children.

The case traces back to the Philippines. Meghan's half-brother, Thomas Markle Jr., reportedly found the material while clearing out their father's home in Cebu City and reported it to Philippine police and the FBI. "I have supported, cared for him and helped him for years — but that stops now," Markle Jr. said, according to the AP. "I won't speak to my father ever again unless I'm called to give evidence against him."

## What the Indictment Alleges

Per the [BBC](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/crwy4egxn592o), the indictment alleges Markle possessed film negatives that included at least one image involving a prepubescent minor and a minor under 12 years old. Prosecutors said agents found two images depicting an adult man — identified by a witness as Markle — with an underage child, as well as a third image appearing to show a child in a room believed to be inside a Philippine motel chain.

The affidavit, prosecutors said, "describes additional photographs that appear to show Markle with children in various stages of undress." Bill Essayli, the top Los Angeles federal prosecutor, said the evidence "suggests Thomas Markle did not just collect images but also engaged in the sexual abuse of children."

Markle has denied wrongdoing. In a 90-minute competency interview with court-appointed psychiatrist Dr. Amie Gerodimos, he said, "I don't have any child porn. I have no pictures of naked kids," according to her report. He also blamed his son, saying, "My son hates me."

## Competency and Custody

The indictment follows a ruling last week that Markle is mentally competent to stand trial. According to the [AP](https://apnews.com/article/meghan-thomas-markle-charges-e02a684853914ae2581aa26b06fdc8d6), Gerodimos found that Markle understands the charges against him and can assist his lawyer, though she noted he was "malingering" — exaggerating — at times during the interview. The defense did not contest her findings.

Markle remains jailed in Florida. He was initially released to the care center with electronic monitoring after a brief court appearance in Tampa, but was detained again days later after authorities said he cut off his ankle monitor in the middle of the night. He is expected to be transferred to Los Angeles in the coming weeks, where he will face arraignment in federal court.

His health is likely to complicate detention and trial logistics. Markle uses a wheelchair and has a range of medical problems after suffering a stroke earlier this year that led to the amputation of his left leg, prompting his move back to the U.S. from the Philippines.

## A Parallel Case in the Philippines

The U.S. indictment is only one track. On Monday, November 23, the Philippine National Police's Women and Children Protection Center filed a formal criminal complaint with the Cebu province prosecutor's office requesting that Markle be charged with two counts of intent to distribute child sexual abuse material and one count of possession, [the BBC reported](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/ckg5ry8m49ggo).

The complaint followed an investigation into digital devices — including computers, phones, hard drives and old video tapes — that Markle Jr. handed over to police from his father's former Cebu home. Brig. Gen. Allen Rae Co, a Philippine National Police spokesperson, confirmed the investigation. The matter remains at the prosecutor-review stage and has not yet resulted in formal charges.

## The Royal Estrangement

The case has thrust a long-simmering family rupture back into public view. Meghan and her father have been estranged since 2018, when he did not attend her wedding to Prince Harry at Windsor Castle after posing for staged paparazzi photos arranged by a British tabloid. In a statement after the allegations first surfaced, Meghan said she was "deeply distressed" and that her thoughts were "with any children who may have been harmed."

She also referenced a 2019 letter containing "troubling and disturbing allegations" about her father that was sent to London police, saying she had been assured it was being investigated. "Any distance from my father and my choice to not allow him to have contact with our children was for one reason: to protect them," she said. Buckingham Palace has not commented and is not expected to.

## What's Next

The immediate questions are procedural: when Markle will be transferred to Los Angeles and arraigned on the indictment, and whether his defense will contest the charges or seek to leverage his age and health in detention and scheduling decisions. Meanwhile, Philippine prosecutors must decide whether to formally charge him, a step that could open a separate legal front.

Essayli said investigators have not identified victims or incidents in the United States, though he stressed the probe is ongoing. Whether that changes — and how the two countries' cases proceed in parallel — will define the next phase of a prosecution that has already become one of the most closely watched federal cases of the year.
