# US Plans Tariffs on 14 Trade Partners, 20% More on China

The Trump administration announced plans Wednesday to raise tariffs on 14 of its trading partners, including an additional 20% levy on Chinese goods and new duties on the European Union, Japan, South Korea and Taiwan, in a sweeping escalation of its trade policy that threatens to reignite global trade tensions.

The action, unveiled the day before Thanksgiving, stems from Section 301 investigations into what Washington calls "structural excess capacity and production" in manufacturing. According to [AP News](https://apnews.com/article/trump-tariffs-china-european-union-367083ea32f71fd7f70029ba7df66bf0), the new taxes are also intended to help replace the tariff revenues lost after the Supreme Court ruled in February that Trump could not declare an economic emergency to bypass Congress and impose across-the-board tariffs.

## Context: Rebuilding the Tariff Wall

The proposal is the latest chapter in a multi-step legal and political saga. Trump's sweeping "Liberation Day" reciprocal tariffs of April 2025 were struck down by the Supreme Court in a 6-3 decision early this year. The administration then briefly invoked a temporary global tariff under Section 122 before imposing 10% to 12.5% duties on 60 partners under Section 301, citing inadequate enforcement of forced-labor bans.

In March 2026, the Office of the U.S. Trade Representative self-initiated investigations into 16 economies over excess manufacturing capacity, gathering testimony from nearly 140 witnesses and more than 1,070 public comments. On Wednesday, it finalized determinations for 14 of them, deferring decisions on Mexico and Norway.

## The Proposed Rates

According to the [USTR's official press release](https://ustr.gov/about/policy-offices/press-office/press-releases/2026/november/ustr-makes-determinations-and-proposes-actions-14-section-301-investigations-relating-structural), the targeted economies are China, the European Union, Singapore, Switzerland, Indonesia, Malaysia, Cambodia, Thailand, South Korea, Vietnam, Taiwan, Bangladesh, Japan and India.

The proposed duties vary widely. China faces up to 20% on top of existing forced-labor Section 301 rates; combined with roughly 25% forced-technology-transfer tariffs from Trump's first term, total U.S. tariffs on some Chinese goods would reach about 45%. The EU, Japan, South Korea, Switzerland and Taiwan are proposed at 15%. Vietnam faces up to 35%, reducible to 20% with a reciprocal trade agreement; India up to 25% (or 17% with a deal); Singapore up to 20% (or 10% with a deal); and Bangladesh, Cambodia, Indonesia, Malaysia and Thailand up to 18%.

Critically, the new tariffs would not stack on top of separate Section 232 duties on automobiles, steel and aluminum, and USTR proposed a "broad exemption" for about $30 billion of Chinese goods in line with the U.S.-China truce, which is set to expire on January 10, 2027.

## Greer: 'More Like a Top Up'

U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer framed the measures as calibrated rather than confrontational. "For decades, many of our trading partners have adopted industrial strategies built on targeting manufacturing, wage and labor suppression, financial and other support, non-market economy inputs, and other market distorting practices that tilt the trade balance in their favor while eroding America's manufacturing base," Greer said in the release.

Speaking to [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/25/us-tariffs-trump-section-301.html), Greer described the net effect as modest. "In effect, it'll look more like a top up," he said. "When it all nets out, it really will just top up a few percentage points." He added that none of the duties would take effect earlier than "a couple months from now," with public comments and hearing requests due January 5, 2027, and USTR hearings beginning January 25, 2027.

China rejected the premise behind the investigations. "A tariff war or a trade war serves no party's interests," Foreign Ministry spokesperson Guo Jiakun said. "The so-called overcapacity is a false narrative, and China opposes using it as a pretext for political manipulation."

Separately, Brazil was singled out for extra punishment after trade talks stalled, with proposed higher duties on raw and refined sugar and a new 10% tariff on aircraft, a move that would hit planemaker Embraer.

## Analysis: Political Risk and Legal Strategy

The timing is striking. The announcement comes just weeks after Republicans lost both the House and the Senate in midterm elections, with about two-thirds of voters telling AP's Voter Poll that Trump's policies had made the economy worse. The administration appears willing to absorb political risk to pursue its protectionist agenda, betting that the "top-up" framing limits the blowback.

The move also reveals a legal strategy: constrained by the Supreme Court on emergency powers, the White House is rebuilding its tariff wall through Section 301. Critics argue the excess-capacity and forced-labor investigations are legal pretexts for reviving the struck-down tariffs, a claim at the center of an ongoing lawsuit. With Democrats now controlling Congress but Trump retaining veto power, legislative reversal is unlikely.

Diplomatically, the China measures come amid an apparent thaw. Trump met Xi Jinping in Shenzhen last week and plans to host him at the G20 summit in Florida next month, suggesting tariffs are being used as leverage within a broader stabilization effort rather than pure confrontation.

## What to Watch

The scope of the proposal, spanning major allies as well as rivals, raises the prospect of broad-based pushback from the EU, Japan and others. Key questions remain: whether effective rates will match the proposed maximums once netting against existing deals is calculated, whether the U.S.-China truce survives its January expiration, and how the courts rule on the pretext lawsuit. For now, the announcement signals that Washington's turn toward tariffs is far from over, even after a stinging electoral rebuke.
