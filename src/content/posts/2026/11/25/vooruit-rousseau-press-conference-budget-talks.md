# Vooruit's Rousseau Calls Press as Budget Talks Stall

Belgium's federal budget negotiations are deadlocked, and the question now hanging over Brussels is whether Vooruit — the only centre-left party in Prime Minister Bart De Wever's five-party coalition — will stay on board. Vooruit leader Conner Rousseau has called a press conference for 14:30 on Wednesday afternoon to announce his party's position after consulting its leadership, [VRT NWS reported](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/liveblog/blijft-vooruit-aan-boord-bij-begrotingsgesprekken-rousseau-roep~1793247532380/). He has said he will deliver a statement only and take no questions.

The outcome remains genuinely undecided: as of midday on 25 November, Rousseau had not yet spoken, and his party's decision — to remain, to set new conditions, or to step out — was still unknown.

## How the talks reached a standstill

Negotiations over the multi-year federal budget have been difficult for months but have been completely blocked for roughly a week and a half. In mid-November, De Wever presented a new budget table to his deputy prime ministers. Four of the five governing parties agreed to keep negotiating on that basis; Vooruit did not.

Vooruit's objection was that the proposal was "not fair enough." The party wants less severe cuts to healthcare, pensions and the wage-indexation mechanism, and larger contributions from people with large fortunes — an extra levy on millionaires. Since that refusal, there has been almost no substantive negotiation, and governing parties have traded attacks on social media.

The tensions have turned personal. As [VRT NWS detailed](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/23/het-verhaal-van-de-kern-en-het-begrotingsoverleg/), Les Engagés deputy prime minister Maxime Prévot publicly accused Vooruit's Frank Vandenbroucke of spreading "fake news" over an alleged index cut for teachers, while MR's David Clarinval claimed Vandenbroucke had demanded apologies from the other parties — a charge Vandenbroucke disputed, saying he had argued the opposite.

## Escalation to the party chairs

On Monday, after the core cabinet of deputy prime ministers failed to reach agreement, De Wever reported to King Filip and then summoned the chairs of the five coalition parties — N-VA, CD&V, MR, Les Engagés and Vooruit — to discuss the future of the talks and, by extension, of the government itself.

That meeting, on Tuesday, also produced no breakthrough. Afterwards, CD&V chair Sammy Mahdi said the group intended to reconvene over the weekend and that "the exercise is becoming broader and bigger." Asked whether the government was now seeking €13 billion rather than €10 billion, he replied: "It's indeed about that order of magnitude — you're well informed."

De Wever has set a hard deadline: a budget agreement must be reached before the end of the year, specifically before the "provisional twelfth-month" arrangements that allow the state to keep running without a budget. Without an agreement, he has warned, the government effectively cannot govern and would fall.

## Why the figure has grown

De Wever has explained that the rising target reflects the drawn-out negotiations. The Monitoring Committee estimated in March that an effort of €7.7 billion was needed; the government added €2.3 billion as a buffer, taking it to about €10 billion, and now expects that number is already outdated.

"The government is very seriously in trouble," De Wever said. "Then it's not so strange to call the party chairs, the guardians of the coalition, together. The question for them was: are you still willing to reach a solution? And if so, how?"

He framed walking away as an abdication of duty. "Running away from that responsibility is not an option," he said. "Walking out of a government in the hope that elections could improve your position — that should not be an option for people who regard this calling to the res publica as a serious calling. Prosperity and social security are at stake."

Some coalition partners have floated an even broader effort. Les Engagés chair Yvan Verougstraete suggested an honest effort of up to €14 billion to allow around €4 billion of investment in easing taxes on labour and preparing the country for the future. MR chair Georges-Louis Bouchez said that "given the current economic reality, the task could well be larger than initially suggested," adding that "no governing party that takes responsibility walks away."

## Rousseau's conditions

After Tuesday's meeting, Rousseau reiterated that he wants guarantees on an extra contribution from millionaires while protecting people's purchasing power — the index. He said he had "again put many questions on the table" that were not answered, and that he would therefore consult his party and provide clarity later.

The stakes are heightened by Vooruit's isolated position. Former Vooruit deputy prime minister Johan Vande Lanotte dismissed suggestions that the party wants elections. "Claiming that Vooruit wants elections is ridiculous," he said, arguing that Vandenbroucke "wants an agreement more than any other person" and that dropping him "would be senseless" since Vooruit is the only centre-left party in the government.

PS chair Paul Magnette, whose party sits in opposition, backed Vooruit's stance: "It's not for me to say whether joining this government was a mistake, but it's clear their position isn't easy as the only left-wing party in a very right-wing government."

## A possible replacement?

If Vooruit were to leave, attention could turn to Anders (formerly Open VLD). Its chair, Frédéric De Gucht, said he is "ready" to step in — but with conditions. "If we replace Vooruit, don't think I'll sit at the table with a blank cheque and just sign," he said. He acknowledged that a coalition without Vooruit but with Anders would have a razor-thin majority of only 76 of 150 seats.

Analysts remain cautious. VUB political scientist Robin Devroe called bringing the chairs together "a good move" but not a miracle cure, given that they represent sharply conflicting visions. VRT political analyst Ivan De Vadder questioned whether the chairs' meetings risk becoming "occupational therapy" to keep party leaders from campaigning.

## What to watch

The immediate focus is Rousseau's 14:30 statement: does Vooruit stay in, set firmer conditions, or step out? Beyond that, the parties are expected to reconvene over the weekend on what is likely a larger numerical basis, and an updated Monitoring Committee figure could confirm a target in the €13–14 billion range.

The overriding deadline remains the year-end. If no budget is passed before the provisional twelfth-month threshold, De Wever has warned, the government's existence ends — paving the way for early elections.
