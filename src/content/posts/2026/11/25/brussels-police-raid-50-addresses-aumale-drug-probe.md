# Brussels Police Raid 50 Addresses in Aumale Drug Probe

Belgian federal police carried out around 50 house searches at addresses in and around Anderlecht overnight, arresting approximately ten suspects in one of the largest anti-drug operations seen in the Brussels-Capital Region this year. The raids, which took place on the night of Tuesday 24 November, targeted a criminal organisation allegedly trafficking narcotics in the Aumale district — a metro station and park area long regarded as one of the capital's most intractable drug hotspots.

According to [VRT NWS](https://www.vrt.be/vrtnws/nl/2026/11/25/50-huiszoekingen-brussel-onderzoek-naar-drugs/), investigators from the Brussels Federal Judicial Police's drugs section conducted the searches as part of a judicial investigation and deployed considerable resources to strike a blow against the organisation. The suspects were expected to be brought before an investigating magistrate later on Wednesday for interrogation.

## Context: A District Under Pressure

The Aumale area, centred on the metro station and the adjacent park in Anderlecht, has for years been one of Brussels' most persistent drug nuisance zones. It was formally designated a drug hotspot in October 2024, and by this autumn residents were describing conditions in increasingly urgent terms — open dealing, crack use, discarded needles and a spate of shootings, including an August attack on the police station on the rue Démosthène.

Reporting by [V-Nieuws, citing BRUZZ](https://www.v-nieuws.be/aumale-ontspoort-bewoners-vluchten-voor-drugs-en-geweld/), captured the sense of deterioration: residents spoke of up to twenty drug users gathering near the metro lift and of families considering leaving the neighbourhood. Anderlecht mayor Fabrice Cumps (PS) cited 181 patrols over 31 action days since mid-May, resulting in 288 arrests, alongside requests for additional mobile cameras — measures many residents said were insufficient.

It is against that backdrop of public frustration and political pressure that the scale of Tuesday's operation becomes significant.

## A Pattern of Large-Scale Enforcement

The operation fits an established enforcement model in the capital. [Het Nieuwsblad](https://www.nieuwsblad.be/binnenland/50-huiszoekingen-bij-grootschalige-politieactie-tegen-drugsbende-aan-hotspot-aumale/165067015.html) reported that investigators raided dozens of addresses in and around Anderlecht, targeting a criminal organisation active at the Aumale hotspot, with around ten suspects taken into custody. The newspaper, like VRT, attributed the figures to sources close to the investigation rather than an official statement.

That model has been deployed before. In late November 2025, a large-scale anti-drug operation in Brussels led to 27 house searches and 15 people deprived of liberty after the interception of two pallets of marijuana shipped from the United States, as the [Brussels public prosecutor's office](https://www.om-mp.be/nl/artikel/grootschalige-antidrugoperatie-brussel-15-personen-opgepakt-27-huiszoekingen-uitgevoerd) detailed at the time. Seven people were charged with participation in a criminal organisation and five were placed under arrest warrants. Earlier, in March 2025, federal police raided the Aumale district itself, seizing 63 blocks of cocaine with a wholesale value of roughly €1.575 million. That earlier case is separate from this week's raids but illustrates how repeatedly the same neighbourhood has drawn major investigative resources.

## Official Silence and Open Questions

As of midday on Wednesday, neither the Brussels public prosecutor's office nor the federal police had issued a detailed public statement on the operation. Mayor Cumps, approached for comment, said the matter was judicial in nature and that communication was being handled by the prosecutor's office.

That silence leaves several questions unresolved. It remains unclear how many of the roughly ten detainees will be formally charged, and with what offences — participation in a criminal organisation and drug trafficking are the most likely counts. Nor is it yet known whether any cash, weapons or narcotics were seized during the searches, or whether the targeted network has international supply links of the kind seen in the 2025 case.

A further open question is whether the gang is connected to the violence that has unsettled the district, including the shootings and the August attack on the local police station. Investigators have not publicly drawn such a link.

## What to Watch

The coming days should bring clarity. The prosecutor's office is expected to confirm the number of arrests, set out the charges and disclose any seizures once the suspects have appeared before the investigating magistrate. Those details will indicate whether Tuesday's operation represents a decisive blow against the Aumale network or another round in a longer campaign.

For residents of Anderlecht, the deeper test is whether large-scale policing translates into lasting change on the ground. The repeated operations of recent years suggest that enforcement alone has struggled to dislodge the district's drug economy — a challenge that now sits squarely between the prosecutor's office, the federal police and a municipality under growing pressure to deliver visible results.
