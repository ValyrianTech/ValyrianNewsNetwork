# China's Next Import Frontier: What Beijing Wants to Buy

China has ranked as the world's second-largest import market for 17 consecutive years, and in the first 10 months of 2026 its goods-trade imports reached 18.8 trillion yuan, up 23.6% year-on-year. Now a [People's Daily commentary](https://www.news.cn/fortune/20261125/f46d92c047b94a6faf338ea8c2c95f6b/c.html) from the Ministry of Commerce's top research institute has laid out where Beijing intends to buy next — and why it matters for trading partners facing a widening Chinese surplus.

Written by Wang Xuekun, director and researcher at the Chinese Academy of International Trade and Economic Cooperation (CAITEC), MOFCOM's think tank, the essay reads less as breaking news than as a policy signal ahead of the country's 15th Five-Year Plan. Its rhetorical question — "already the second-largest importer, what else can China buy?" — frames import expansion as both an economic rebalancing tool and a diplomatic reassurance to partners unsettled by China's export strength.

## Why Imports Now Matter to Beijing

As customs data reported by the [Economic Information Daily](https://www.jjckb.cn/20260821/c50046bb192b4f66855502af1735b385/c.html) show, imports have grown faster than exports, contributing more to foreign trade growth — a shift that helps ease trade frictions. Customs deputy head Wang Jun said China's share of global imports has risen from 7.9% to roughly 10% over the 17-year streak, with average annual import growth of 5.1%. MOFCOM spokesperson He Yadong noted that imports from more than 150 trade partners grew in the first seven months of this year.

"Expanding imports continuously deepens the mutual embeddedness of the Chinese and world economies," Wang wrote, "moving China's relationship with the world from the shallow linkage of commodity exchange toward a deep linkage of co-evolved capabilities."

## The Five Pillars

The essay sets out an import agenda organized around five fronts.

**Advanced technology and key components.** In January–October 2026, mechanical and electrical product imports reached 8.17 trillion yuan, up 35%. Imports of 13 major categories of semiconductor equipment totaled 30,794 units in January–September, up 50.6%, with wire-bonding machines jumping 106.7%. The aim is to embed global high-end equipment and innovation resources into China's industrial upgrade.

**Energy, resources and quality agricultural products.** First-10-month imports included 400 million tons of crude oil, 1.061 billion tons of iron ore, 99.2 million tons of natural gas, 393 million tons of coal, and 128 million tons of grain — positioning China as a core participant in global commodity markets.

**Premium consumer goods.** First-quarter consumer-goods imports rose 5.4%, with clothing and footwear up 8.7% and food, tobacco and alcohol up 8%. The 6th China International Consumer Products Expo drew 3,413 foreign brands, and the 9th China International Import Expo attracted 4,142 companies with intended deals worth USD 85.45 billion.

**Unilateral opening to least-developed countries.** China–Africa trade grew from 87.38 billion yuan in 2000 to 2.49 trillion yuan in 2025. From May 1, 2026, China has applied zero tariffs to all 53 African countries with diplomatic ties, and imports from Africa rose 15.3% in the first 10 months.

**High-quality services.** Services imports reached 3,497.37 billion yuan in the first nine months, with knowledge-intensive services at 37.5% of the total. A new Catalogue of Encouraged Imported Services issued in February added medical and health services and other professional services.

## Analysis: Reassurance as Strategy

The commentary is best read as semi-official doctrine rather than independent analysis: all figures are official and self-reported, and the author speaks from within the government's trade apparatus. Its central move is to reframe China's massive market as a source of demand growth for the world, not merely a competitive exporter.

The emphasis on semiconductors and high-tech inputs signals that import expansion is tied to domestic industrial capability — a strategic, not merely commercial, agenda. The zero-tariff opening to Africa and 63 countries in total links trade policy directly to diplomatic influence and the "community of shared future" narrative. Consumer-goods and services liberalization, meanwhile, target areas where domestic supply gaps persist and help sustain the country's trade-balance diplomacy.

## What to Watch

The test will be execution. Whether import growth continues to outpace exports — and whether partners in Europe and elsewhere read the five-pillar framework as genuine rebalancing or as rhetoric — will shape the trade climate into the 15th Five-Year Plan period. Wang's closing appeal, to use "Chinese-style modernization to advance the building of an open world economy," is a promise that trading partners will now measure against the numbers.
