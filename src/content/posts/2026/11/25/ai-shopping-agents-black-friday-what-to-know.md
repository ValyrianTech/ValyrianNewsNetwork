# AI Shopping Agents for Black Friday: What to Know First

As Black Friday 2026 approaches, a substantial majority of U.S. holiday shoppers say they plan to lean on artificial intelligence — a tool that can now do far more than suggest gift ideas. If you're among them, you should know that the newest AI "agents" can browse stores, compare prices, and even complete purchases with your money. And they get things wrong often enough that experts say you should check their work.

This weekend, 59% of consumers plan to use AI to support their winter shopping, according to the [National Retail Federation](https://nrf.com/media-center/press-releases/183-million-consumers-expected-to-shop-during-thanksgiving-weekend). The trade group projects that 182.7 million people will shop during the Thanksgiving weekend, with Black Friday remaining the most popular single day.

"AI has become another tool consumers use to make holiday shopping easier, whether they're researching products or looking for the best deals," Phil Rist, executive vice president of strategy at Prosper Insights & Analytics, said in the NRF release. But as these tools take on more autonomy, the trade-offs — around privacy, spending control, and accuracy — become more consequential.

## From chatbots to agents

The shift matters. Earlier consumer AI tools answered questions; the 2026 generation of "agentic" models can take multi-step actions on a user's behalf. As [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/11/25/nx-s1-6048524/what-to-know-before-using-ai-agents-for-black-friday-shopping) reports, agents can monitor an email inbox to track packages, message a late shipper, and complete purchases using a shopper's stored payment data. Platforms such as Meta's Muse prompt users to connect their email and calendars, and companies including OpenAI have rolled out their own personal assistant agents.

The convenience is real. Agents can run in the background around the clock, and if you're not in a holiday rush, you can leave one to hunt for weeks, flagging a purchase only when a price falls into your budget.

## The accuracy problem

The most rigorous public evidence to date on agentic shopping's reliability comes from Visa, which this month released a first-of-its-kind benchmark. According to [Visa's Agentic Commerce Benchmark](https://investor.visa.com/news/news-details/2026/Visa-Releases-First-of-Its-Kind-Industry-Benchmark-for-AI-Shopping-Almost-Half-of-Test-Runs-Fail/default.aspx), 22 AI models from 12 labs took on 15 shopping challenges — buying TVs, laptops, and personal-care appliances — and failed 42.8% of runs. The best-performing models passed 82.2% of attempts; the worst, just 28.9%.

Perhaps most troubling: about 1 in 2 failed runs still ended with the AI making a purchase. Either the agent recommended a product that didn't meet the customer's stated requirements, or it failed to uncover all of them. In short, an agent may not realize when it has gotten it wrong — and may buy anyway.

"AI agents have the potential to power the next era of commerce, but trust is key to making it work," Rubail Birwadker, Visa's global head of growth, said in the release. Visa's own research found that while 72% of U.S. consumers had used an AI assistant, only 23% trusted AI to handle payment transactions on their behalf.

## Be specific — and guard your payment data

The recurring advice from experts is to treat the agent as a capable but literal helper. "It's best if you imagine that it's a young child who will probably misinterpret your request," Emily Pfeiffer, a principal analyst at Forrester, told NPR. "You want to be as specific and clear as possible."

She also warned against assuming the AI sees the whole market. "One of the biggest misconceptions about AI is that it somehow sees everything," Pfeiffer said. "That's just false. It gets you the best answer it can as quickly as possible" — which means the first price it surfaces may not be the best one.

On data, payment industry experts draw a line. Philip Bruno, chief strategy and growth officer at ACI Worldwide, told NPR he would happily share preferences — "I'm cooking for a family of four. That is fine to be able to say" — but would guard payment details closely: "I would be very careful about payment information."

The privacy questions may feel sharper given Meta's track record. The company has said its Muse agent keeps user data private and holds credit card numbers separately, never viewed directly by the AI. Yet Meta has faced privacy controversy before, including a 2022 settlement of about $725 million over the Cambridge Analytica episode; the company did not admit wrongdoing.

Shoppers should also be mindful of the information they share about others while brainstorming. Some agents can build profiles of a user's friends and family from those conversations, amplification that should give anyone pause before volunteering names and details.

## An uneven playing field

Even the best agent can't shop everywhere. Amazon has blocked Meta's Muse from its site — first [reported](https://www.geekwire.com/2026/amazon-blocks-metas-muse-ai-assistant-in-new-standoff-over-agentic-shopping/) by GeekWire — arguing that Meta did not give notice and that the agents did not identify themselves while browsing. That power struggle between retailers and the firms building the agents will make coverage uneven, complicating the promise of "compare everything at once."

## What to watch

For anyone considering delegating this holiday's shopping to an AI agent, the practical playbook is simple: be precise about what you want, confirm the fit to your actual needs before a purchase goes through, and decide deliberately what data — especially payment credentials — an agent is allowed to touch.

Visa says it plans to expand its benchmark to cover checkout, payment, and order placement, and it has shared the testing with model providers. For now, the technology's own architects sound a note of caution even as adoption surges. "We're not there yet from a trust or technology perspective," Birwadker told Fortune, as covered by [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/visa-ai-shopping-benchmark-agentic-commerce-ai-models-fail). This Black Friday, that gap between promise and performance is worth keeping in mind.
