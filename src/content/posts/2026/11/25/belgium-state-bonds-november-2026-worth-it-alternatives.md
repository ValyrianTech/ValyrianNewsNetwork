# New Belgian State Bonds: Worth It or Better Elsewhere?

Belgium's Federal Debt Agency opened subscriptions on Wednesday for three new State bonds, offering savers gross yields that range from 2.95% for a one-year term to a historically high 4.10% for ten years. The bonds, which will be issued on 4 December, give households a fresh opportunity to lock in returns — but financial experts warn the deals are not automatically the best option on the market.

Subscription runs through 3 December at placing banks, and closes a day earlier, on 2 December, for those subscribing directly through the Grands-Livres service, according to [RTL Info](https://www.rtl.be/actu/belgique/economie/une-nouvelle-campagne-de-bons-detat-sera-lancee-debut-decembre-voici-les/2026-11-23/article/807457). Interest is subject to a 30% withholding tax, leaving net returns of 2.065%, 2.52% and 2.87% across the three maturities.

## Three Durations, Three Betting Strategies

Unlike recent campaigns that offered only one- and ten-year maturities, this edition adds a five-year bond — a middle path between short-term safety and long-term yield. According to comparison site [YouSave.be](https://www.yousave.be/fr/blog/epargner/bon-detat-december-2026-taux-conditions-et-alternatives), the three bonds break down as follows:

| Duration | ISIN code | Gross rate | Net rate (after 30% tax) |
|----------|-----------|-----------|--------------------------|
| 1 year | BE3871316464 | 2.95% | 2.065% |
| 5 years | BE3871317470 | 3.60% | 2.52% |
| 10 years | BE3871318486 | 4.10% | 2.87% |

For the one-year bond, savers recover their full capital plus interest at maturity. The five- and ten-year bonds pay an annual coupon, taxed at 30%, with the invested capital returned at maturity.

## Why the Rates Are Unusually High

The 10-year rate is at a historically high level by the Debt Agency's own statistics, a reflection of the broader rising-rate environment. As [RTBF](https://www.rtbf.be/article/trois-nouveaux-bons-d-etat-ouverts-a-la-souscription-des-ce-mercredi-que-valent-ils-et-quelles-sont-les-alternatives-11820407) notes, this reflects a market in which the European Central Bank has been tightening policy. It is also the reason banks are competing aggressively for savers' money — and why the State's offer is only one of several options.

## The Alternatives: Banks Are Fighting Back

The key message from experts is that a State bond is safe and simple, but a determined saver can often do as well — or better — elsewhere.

**Savings accounts.** Traditional regulated savings accounts at the big banks still hold most Belgian household savings but pay precious little. "At the big banks, the classic accounts, where the majority of Belgians' savings are still held today, are at less than 1% and are actually more like 0.25 to 0.60% per year," said Nicolas Claeys, investment expert at TestAchats Invest, quoted by RTBF. Some smaller and online banks offer promotional rates that match or beat the one-year bond — Me Direct's "Essential" account offers about 2.20% if money stays a year, Santander's Vision Max was raised to 2.40% in mid-November, and Keytrade Bank's "High Fidelity" account rises to 2.25% from 1 December. The trade-off: savings-account rates, especially base rates, can change at any time, whereas the one-year bond's rate is locked for the full year.

**Term accounts and deposit certificates.** Banks compete hardest during State bond campaigns. Claeys points to VDK Bank and Argenta offering net rates of around 2.24%, and BNP Paribas Fortis's 18-month deposit certificate at 3.50% gross (2.45% net). ING and Europabank have offered term accounts at up to 4% gross, and Beobank on 25 November raised rates on five- to ten-year term accounts to as much as 4.10%. These products are covered up to €100,000 per depositor by the deposit guarantee fund — though early exit can carry penalties.

**Savings insurance (Branch 21).** These can offer higher returns but require an eight-year horizon; below that, a 30% withholding applies on redemption. Fees can significantly erode returns.

## The Risks Worth Weighing

The biggest caveat concerns liquidity. A State bond can be sold on the secondary market before maturity, but there is no guarantee of recovering the initial capital. "If you resell in a year or two, you may get back a little less or a little more. It will actually depend on how rates evolve," Claeys said.

The second is discipline. "You shouldn't take a 10-year bond just because the rate is higher if you know you might need your money in 3 or 4 years," he warned. And for the decade-long commitment, inflation is an open question: whether 2.87% net will outpace price rises over ten years is far from certain.

## What's Next

The September campaign raised just over €1.255 billion, offering one-year and ten-year bonds at net yields of 1.925% and 2.59%. Observers will watch whether the addition of a five-year maturity and a historically high 10-year rate draw more savers — and whether aggressive bank promotions, like Beobank's same-day rate hike, siphon demand away from the State. The subscription window closes on 3 December, with issuance on 4 December.
